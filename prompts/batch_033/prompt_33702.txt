I am preparing to teach Brecht's The Good Person of Szechwan in a course on philosophy and literature, and I want to read it against the situationist critique of virtue ethics: the line of argument, associated with John Doris and Gilbert Harman among others, that behavior is driven far more by features of the immediate situation than by stable global character traits, so a moral psychology built around virtues like benevolence rests on an empirically shaky foundation. The play looks as if it were written for this debate. Three gods come to earth looking for one good person, find Shen Te, and give her the means to open a tobacco shop; she then discovers that being good to everyone who asks leaves her unable to survive, and she invents a ruthless alter ego, Shui Ta, to handle the practical side of her life. I want a reading that takes both the play and the empirical critique seriously and does not reduce either to an illustration of the other.

In particular: which turns in the play most strongly dramatize the situationist thesis, and what exactly do they show — that Shen Te lacks a stable trait of benevolence, that the trait is real but swamped by circumstances, or that the economic setup makes the expression of benevolence self-undermining? Where does the play complicate or resist the situationist reading — for example, does Shen Te's goodness persist as a standing disposition even while she must act through Shui Ta, and does the play locate the problem in social and economic structure rather than in the psychology of situations? Does Brecht's Marxism, or his concept of gestus, change how we should map the play onto the empirical debate? And what does the play add to the philosophical argument that the situationist literature alone cannot capture? I am especially interested in the ending, where the gods seem willing to accept the split between Shen Te and Shui Ta: does the play treat the failure of goodness under pressure as a refutation of the possibility of virtue, as a demand for different circumstances, or as something else?
