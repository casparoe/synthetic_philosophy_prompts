I teach an intro epistemology unit for about 25 undergrads, one 75-minute session. Design a small-group game that makes them feel the regress problem before anyone says the word "regress." I want them to get forced into choosing between three bad-feeling exits: declaring something basic or obvious, looping back to a reason they already used, or refusing to stop. The rules should make each exit cost something visible to the group (points, in-game standing, whatever) so nobody can just quietly pick one and move on. Groups of 4-5 with a recorder, no real money. The claim they have to justify should start ordinary and local — like a campus policy or a claim about a class theyre in — not a classic skeptical scenario, so they dont immediately see where it's heading. The debrief afterwards should map what happened onto foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism, say what each view buys and gives up, and identify wich moves in the game correspond to wich view. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be honest about which feature of the actual philosophical positions the game cant reproduce. The idea should come out of what they do, not out of me explaining it afterwards.
