I am a PhD student in philosophy working on the foundations of rational choice and game theory, and I have been trying to mediate between two senior colleagues whose dispute about the backward induction paradox has become a fixture of our departmental seminars. One of them holds the orthodox view: backward induction yields the uniquely rational solution to the centipede game, and the so-called paradox merely reveals that our intuitions about mutually beneficial cooperation are unreliable in this setting. The other holds a revisionist view: the backward induction argument is unsound because it requires players to reason about what they would do at nodes that can only be reached if someone has already deviated from rationality, and this counterfactual reasoning cannot be grounded solely in the common-knowledge assumptions that are supposed to drive it.

I would like you to draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between these two positions ought to produce — a document both sides could sign. It should include the following:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides agree on. These should cover the formal structure of the centipede game, what the backward induction algorithm prescribes, what the standard experimental evidence shows about how human subjects actually play, and what the paradox is supposed to challenge. The agreed claims should be neither vacuous nor slanted; they should record genuine common ground.

2. A disagreements table with four columns: (a) the issue, (b) the orthodox position stated in wording the orthodox side would accept, (c) the revisionist position stated in wording the revisionist side would accept, and (d) whether the disagreement is substantive or partly verbal. Each side's position should be phrased so that its own proponents would recognise it as a fair characterisation.

3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that, if they were settled, at least one side would change its view. For each crux, state what sort of argument, formal result, or empirical evidence both sides would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction about what that investigation would show.

4. If any part of the dispute turns out to be verbal rather than substantive, identify it explicitly and explain why — for instance, if the two sides are using the phrase "common knowledge of rationality" in different senses, or if they disagree about what "a rational player would do at an unreached node" means without disagreeing about any underlying fact.

Please write the entire document in British English throughout.
