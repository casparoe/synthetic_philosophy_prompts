So I was reading this old book about puzzles and logic my uncle gave me, its from the 80s or something, and it has this thing called the MU-puzzle. You start with the string "MI" and you have four rules for changing strings. I wont list all the rules but basically you can add letters, double parts of the string, and do some replacements. The goal is to get from "MI" to "MU".

Anyway I got really into it and tried for like a week to find a sequence that gets you to MU. Couldn't do it. Then I read ahead and the book says the puzzle is actually impossible — you can prove it by "jumping out of the system" (the book calls it JOOTSing). You look at the number of I's in the string and notice that none of the rules ever let you get to a number of I's that's a multiple of 3, starting from MI which has one I. MU has zero I's, and zero IS a multiple of 3, so you can never reach it.

I told two friends about this at a bar and they got into this whole argument that I'm still thinking about.

Dave said JOOTSing is the whole point — the puzzle teaches you that some problems can't be solved from inside the system, and the real solution is to step outside and see the invariant. He said if you just kept trying rules forever you'd be missing the lesson entirely.

Maya pushed back hard. She said Dave was confusing "proving a puzzle is unsolvable" with "solving the puzzle." The puzzle as stated says "get from MI to MU" — that's the task. Proving it can't be done isn't completing the task, it's showing the task is impossible. That's a different thing. She said its like if someone asks you to bake a cake and you come back with a proof that the recipe has a contradiction — interesting, but there's no cake.

Dave said that's a terrible analogy because puzzles are about understanding, not about producing physical outputs. The MU-puzzle isn't a cooking challenge, it's a formal system. Understanding that MU is unreachable IS the solution — it's what the puzzle was designed to teach.

Maya said fine, but then the puzzle is badly posed. If the real goal was "determine whether MU is reachable from MI," the instructions should say that. By saying "get from MI to MU," the puzzle invites you to try to produce the string, and JOOTSing is a clever way of refusing to do what was asked while claiming the moral high ground.

Dave said she was being deliberately obtuse — no one thinks the point of a puzzle like this is to just mechanically apply rules until you happen to stumble on the answer. The point is to figure out what's going on. JOOTSing is figuring out what's going on.

Maya said she wasn't being obtuse, she was taking the instructions seriously. And theres a difference between a puzzle where you have to think outside the box to find a solution that exists, and a puzzle where the "solution" is realizing there is no solution. The first kind rewards creativity, the second kind just changes the subject.

At this point they were going in circles and I had to leave to catch my bus. I've been turning it over in my head since. Who's actually right here? Is JOOTSing a solution to the MU-puzzle or a refusal to engage with it on its own terms? And does Maya have a point that the puzzle is badly posed, or is Dave right that anyone reasonable understands what the puzzle is really asking?
