I'm putting together an application for a two-year postdoctoral fellowship, and I need a research proposal (about 1500 words). My half-formed idea is this: the debate over Taurek's claim that the numbers don't count in rescue cases has been conducted almost entirely under the assumption that we know the relevant moral facts with certainty. But if we take moral uncertainty seriously — the idea that we should be uncertain about which moral theory is correct and should take that uncertainty into account in our practical deliberations — then Taurek's position may face a distinctive problem. Specifically, even if Taurek is right that there is no requirement to save the many rather than the few when we are certain of our moral reasons, it seems that under moral uncertainty we may be forced to aggregate across persons anyway, because the expected moral value of saving the many (computed across the range of theories we have nonzero credence in) will almost always favor saving the larger group. If so, Taurek's anti-aggregation conclusion might be practically inert: it only applies in a condition of moral certainty that we are never actually in.
