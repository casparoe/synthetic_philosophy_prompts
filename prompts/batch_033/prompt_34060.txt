I'm a data analyst at a hospital system and I just got burned by Simpson's paradox in a way that's honestly embarrassing. For like six months I've been telling everyone our new post-discharge follow-up program cuts readmissions — the aggregate numbers were beautiful, p < 0.01, the whole thing. Then someone finally broke it down by patient severity and... it makes things worse within every single severity tier. The program wasn't helping; it was just being assigned disproportionately to healthier patients. I feel like my entire credibility is shot.

Anyway, my boss wants me to give a thirty-minute session to the clinical department heads — smart people, but most of them haven't thought about statistics since their one required course in med school — to explain what happened and why they should be suspicious of aggregate comparisons going forward. I don't want to just explain the paradox *at* them. I want to plan the session so the structure actually does the work.

What order do the pieces have to go in? I'm guessing I should start by showing them the two tables — the aggregate where the program looks great, and the disaggregated where it looks terrible — and let them sit in the confusion before I name anything. But what depends on what? Like, do they need conditional probability or can I stay in percentages the whole time? I want one clean carrying example — should I use our actual hospital data or is that too close to the bone and they'll get defensive? And what misconceptions should I expect? I can already hear one of the surgeons saying "so we should just always disaggregate everything" and I need to explain why that's not quite right either. What other wrong ideas are going to come up, and at what points in the session do I intercept each one? And what's one question I can ask at the end that'll tell me wheter they actually got it, versus just nodding along because they want to get to lunch?
