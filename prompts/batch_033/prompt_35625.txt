Please give me your current credence that the correct normative epistemology of uncertainty is imprecise probabilism. By that I mean the thesis that a rational agent's doxastic state is, at least in the epistemically interesting cases, a set of probability functions over the relevant propositions — a credal set — rather than a single precise probability function, where the set is not merely a convenient summary of a sharp probability distribution over possible objective chances or over one's own possible future credences. I am not asking whether imprecise models are useful as descriptive or bookkeeping devices; I am asking whether the set-valued representation is the right normative model of rational uncertainty.

I want a number, not a survey. Decompose the claim into the sub-questions your credence actually depends on, assigning each a rough probability and one line of justification. The following should be among them unless you can say why one of them does no work:

(a) That there are evidential situations in which no unique precise credence is rationally mandatory — cases of ambiguous or unsharp evidence of the kind Ellsberg-style examples are designed to elicit — so that some form of permissivism holds for those cases rather than uniqueness.

(b) That, conditional on there being such cases, the agent in such a case is best modeled as occupying a set-valued credal state, rather than the weaker view that many precise credences are each permissible while the agent always occupies exactly one.

(c) That imprecision cannot be replaced without loss by sharp higher-order uncertainty, such as a probability distribution over objective chances, over possible evidence, or over one's own possible future credences.

(d) That the standard objections to imprecise probabilism can be answered or shown not to be fatal: the dilation of credal sets under conditioning, in which new evidence expands rather than shrinks the set of probabilities; the lack of a unique decision rule and the charge that set-valued states are decision-theoretically incomplete; and the alleged diachronic incoherence or violation of the reflection principle.

(e) That the dispute between sharp and imprecise Bayesians is a substantive disagreement about rationality, not a largely verbal difference about the meaning of rational or belief state.

For each term, give your probability and one line of justification. Then show how the terms combine — state the dependencies and the combination rule you used — to yield your final credence in imprecise probabilism. Finally, identify the term you are least confident in, specify the evidence or argument that would move it most, and say how much your final number would change if that term were settled against you.
