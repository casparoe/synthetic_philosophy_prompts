I sit on my university's Academic Integrity Committee. We are revising how we handle undergraduate cheating. One proposal would replace most punitive hearings with a required program in academic character: workshops, reflective writing, and mentorship aimed at cultivating virtues such as intellectual honesty. A second faction, drawing on the situationist critique of virtue ethics, argues that conduct is far more responsive to local situational pressures — deadlines, grade distributions, proctoring, peer norms — than to stable character traits, and that character built in a classroom will not transfer to the situations where students actually cheat. They want the same funds spent on situational redesign: assessment formats that are harder to cheat on, restructured deadlines, and changes to grading practices. Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate before next month's meeting. It should recommend a concrete policy — the character program, the situational redesign, or a specified hybrid — with an implementation time frame, and it should engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side. End with a line beginning "Verdict:" stating your bottom-line recommendation in one short phrase.
