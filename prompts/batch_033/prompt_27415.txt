An online store tried out two checkout flows, O and N, and logged the following results:

Mobile:
O: 100 completed out of 2000 sessions (5.0%)
N: 200 completed out of 5000 sessions (4.0%)

Desktop:
O: 300 completed out of 2000 sessions (15.0%)
N: 2600 completed out of 20000 sessions (13.0%)

(a) Compute the overall completion rate for each flow.
(b) Compute the completion rates separately for mobile and desktop.
(c) Which flow should the store ship, and why? If the aggregate and subgroup comparisons seem to point in different directions, explain what's going on and say which comparison should guide the decision.

Here's the student's answer:

"Overall, O converted 400/4000 = 10% and N converted 2800/25000 = 11.2%, so N looks better in the aggregate. But when you split by device, O wins on mobile (5% vs 4%) and on desktop (15% vs 13%). So this is Simpson's paradox: the overall comparison flips because the N group is mostly desktop users, and desktop users convert at a higher rate no matter which flow they get, while O's group is half mobile. The right call is to ship O, because O wins in every subgroup. Simpson's paradox shows the overall comparison is confounded by device, so the subgroup comparison is the correct one. Whenever the aggregate and subgroup results disagree, you should always trust the subgroup results."
