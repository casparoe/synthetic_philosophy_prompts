I am preparing a position statement for a workshop on the philosophy of interpretability, and I would like a consistency audit of the commitments below before I circulate it. The commitments are:

(1) Realism. In at least some trained networks, there are internal states with representational content, and the network's behavior is causally organized around those contents. This is a fact about the network itself, not merely a useful way of describing it.

(2) Causal criterion. The best evidence that an interpretability method — for example, a sparse autoencoder — has recovered a real representation is that interventions on the purported feature, such as ablating it, steering along it, or clamping it, produce the behavioral changes one would expect if the network were using that feature, and do so robustly across a wide range of inputs rather than as an input-specific effect.

(3) Decodability skepticism. Linear probes can decode information from activations that the network does not causally use in producing its outputs. Decodability alone is therefore weak evidence that the decoded content figures in the network's own computation.

(4) Superposition. Networks can represent more features than they have dimensions. Individual neurons are often polysemantic, and features may live in distributed directions rather than being aligned with single units.

(5) Decompositional pluralism. The activation space of a network typically admits many different, equally legitimate factorizations into feature directions, and no single factorization is the uniquely correct one. Which basis one uses is in part a modeling choice.

(6) Content determinacy. For at least some directions or circuits, there is a fact of the matter about what they represent, even when our methods cannot recover it exactly.

I have been treating these as a coherent package: (3) and (4) explain why naive neuron-labeling fails, (2) supplies the causal discipline that (3) lacks, and (6) is what makes interpretability a discovery enterprise rather than curve-fitting. But I would like an audit before I present. Please go through the set and identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be; rank the genuine tensions by severity; for each, say what would have to be revised and what the revision would cost; and tell me which single change would do the most to restore consistency.
