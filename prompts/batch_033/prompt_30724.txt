so i'm going to be interviewing someone who worked extensively on iterated amplification and distillation as an alignment approach — think the kind of person who could give the definitive insider account of where IDA stands right now. my audience reads lesswrong and the alignment forum, so they know the basics: human overseer decomposes tasks, uses copies of a weaker model to handle subtasks, distills the amplified system back down, repeat. they've also seen the standarad objections.

what i want is 8–10 questions that go past the stuff that's been debated to death. i want questions where the answer isn't obvious and where the standard replies have internal tensions you can expose with a good follow-up.

specific things i want at least one question on:

- the decomposition problem. when an overseer breaks a task into subtasks, they're making choices about what counts as a natural decomposition. if those choices embed values or assumptions that aren't themselves aligned, does iterating just amplify the misalignment? and is there any way to detect this from inside the system, or do you need an external evaluation that the overseer can't provide?

- the distillation step. distillation approximates the amplified system's behavior, and in practice it doesn't perfectly reproduce it. what's the argument that alignment-relevant properties survive this approximation rather than being among the first things that get dropped? has anyone actualy tested this empirically with current models, or is this still at the level of "we hope"?

- the oversight gap. at some depth of iteration the system does things the human can't evaluate directly. the standard reply is that you check the process (the decomposition tree, the reasoning steps) not just the final output. but process-checking itself has limits when the process involves reasoning the overseer can't follow. at what depth does this actually break, and do we have any way to estimate that depth?

- the meta question: if IDA were failing to preserve alignment, what would we see first? what would the warning signs be, and would we be able to notice them before something catastrophic? i want them to engage with specific failure modes, not just say "we'd run careful experiments."

for each question i want a follow-up that anticipates their likely answer and presses on what's still unresolved. so if they say "we decompose recursively until subtasks are human-checkable," the follow-up should ask whether verifying the decomposition itself requires understanding the whole thing. if they say "we can use adversarial testing on the distilled model," the follow-up should ask whether adversarial testing catches alignment failures that don't manifest as obvious misbehavior on test distributions.

i want this to feel like it's making progress on actual cruxes. if there's a question where the honest answer is "we don't know and that's fine at this stage," i still want it framed pointedly enough that they can't just say "it's a research program not an objection" and dodge.
