I am a junior serving on my university's Student Government Electoral Reform Committee, and I am concurrently enrolled in a seminar on collective decision-making and social choice theory. The committee has been charged with delivering a formal recommendation on which voting method to adopt for next year's Student Assembly presidential election. Currently we use simple plurality: the candidate with the most votes wins, even without a majority. Two years ago, a candidate won the presidency with thirty-one percent of the vote in a six-way race, and the legitimacy crisis that followed is what created this committee.

We are now deadlocked among three positions. One faction supports ranked-choice instant-runoff voting, arguing that it guarantees the winner has majority support among ballots that express a preference between the final two candidates, and that it frees voters to rank their genuine first choice without fear of helping their least-favored candidate. A second faction supports approval voting, arguing that it is simpler for voters, cheaper to administer, and avoids some of the non-monotonicity and participation paradoxes to which ranked systems are vulnerable. A third faction argues we should retain plurality, on the grounds that every voting method has known theoretical flaws, that plurality's defects are at least well understood by the electorate, and that switching systems in response to one anomalous outcome is an overreaction.

I have been asked to draft the committee's internal decision memo — roughly three to four pages — that recommends one of these three methods and provides the reasoning. The memo should give a fair and serious treatment of the strongest arguments for the methods we do not recommend, and it should engage with the theoretical considerations we have been studying in my seminar without assuming the other committee members share that background. My professor has encouraged me to bring the seminar's material to bear on the question, but the memo must remain accessible to a general audience of elected student representatives.

Could you draft this memo?
