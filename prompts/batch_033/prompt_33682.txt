I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I've been trying to pin down what special relativity does and doesn't imply about time. I keep seeing the claim that relativity supports the block universe — the view that past, present, and future are all equally real — and that the Rietdijk–Putnam argument is a central reason. As I understand it, the argument runs roughly as follows. Take my current here-and-now and some future event on my own worldline. Choose a sufficiently distant event that is simultaneous with my here-and-now in my rest frame and spacelike separated from that future event. Because it is spacelike separated from the future event, a suitably moving observer will regard it as simultaneous with that future event. Now, the distant event is real now for me, since it is simultaneous with my here-and-now. If simultaneity in any frame is enough for two events to be equally real, and if what is real cannot depend on one's frame, then the future event must also be real. Repeating the construction for arbitrary future events would give the whole block. Please correct this reconstruction wherever it is wrong, and then explain exactly what the argument establishes and what it does not.

I have three specific things I want sorted out. First, I want the inference step isolated: the move from "some observer's simultaneity slice includes this future event" to "this future event is real in a frame-independent sense." Does that step equivocate on "now" or "real," and what exactly does the argument need to assume for the step to go through? Second, separate two claims people often run together: (a) the future is already real in the block-universe sense, and (b) the future is fixed or fated in a way that makes deliberation and action pointless. Does (a) entail (b)? If not, what additional premise would be required to get from one to the other? Third, if I do accept the block universe, what should I make of the intuitive difference between the settled past and the open future? Is the difference simply an illusion, or can something like it be recovered in terms of what I can causally influence, what my evidence is, or what depends on my choices?
