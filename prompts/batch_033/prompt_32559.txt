I am a PhD student in formal epistemology, and I have come to hold the following view: the lottery paradox and the preface paradox are not merely analogous puzzles; they are the same underlying problem about the structure of rational belief, and any proposed solution that handles one without handling the other is for that reason alone inadequate. In particular, I think attempts to dissolve the lottery paradox by appeal to probability thresholds fail because the preface paradox reproduces the same structure without any probabilistic apparatus, and attempts to dissolve the preface paradox by appeal to the norms of assertion fail because the lottery paradox reproduces the same structure without any authorial context.

I want to know what would count against this unification claim. What arguments, formal results, or edge cases should lead me to abandon it? Which defeaters would be decisive, and which merely awkward? I am especially interested in hearing from the perspective of someone who thinks the two paradoxes are genuinely distinct and require different treatments: what would have to be true for my view to be wrong, and what evidence or argument would show that it is? I would also like the response to say what would change its own mind about the matter — which argument or consideration would lead to a different verdict, or where its reasoning is most likely to fail.
