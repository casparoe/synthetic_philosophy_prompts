I am organising a small workshop on formal epistemology and the foundations of game theory, and two participants have agreed to replace their usual debate with an adversarial collaboration. The first defends the common prior assumption, sometimes called the Harsanyi doctrine, as a requirement of ideal rationality: any difference between the probabilities assigned by two rational agents must ultimately be traceable to a difference in their information, so that agents with the same information cannot rationally hold different priors. The second defends a permissivist position: the standard coherence arguments license any probabilistically coherent prior, rational agents may permissibly begin from different priors, and persistent disagreement between ideally rational agents is not in itself a failure of rationality. Please draft the document that these two could both sign.

The document should open with a numbered section of agreed claims. I expect these to include, though not to be limited to, a precise statement of what Aumann's agreement theorem does and does not establish, the sense in which that theorem is a conditional result rather than a proof that disagreement is irrational, the demanding character of its common-knowledge assumptions, and the points about Dutch book and accuracy arguments on which the two sides concur. It should then set out the residual disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own position, with the disagreements presented in a table whose columns give the issue, the common-prior camp's formulation, and the permissivist camp's formulation. Finally, the document should identify the cruxes — the sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one side — and, for each crux, describe the formal result, theorem, or concrete case that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I suspect the cruxes will include whether coherence arguments extend to uniqueness of prior, the normative status of imprecise credences, and how Aumann's result should be interpreted; include any others you judge more central, and if part of the dispute turns out to be verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why. Please write the document in British English.
