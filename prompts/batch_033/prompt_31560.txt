Two of my coursemates got into it over a news story last night, and I ended up having to referee. The story was that the Prime Minister authorised a drone strike on a terrorist's car knowing his twelve-year-old daughter was in it; the strike killed both of them, and it's since come out that it stopped an imminent attack. The arguement went like this. (1) Dan said the PM did the right thing, but his hands are dirty: he should feel awful about the girl, and we'd be right to distrust a leader who shrugged it off. (2) Mina said that's incoherent — if the act was right, guilt is misplaced, and if guilt is fitting, he did something wrong, in which case he shouldn't have done it. (3) Dan replied that morality isn't a balance sheet: the wrongness of killing the girl doesn't vanish just because it was outweighed; it leaves a remainder, which is why the PM owes us honesty, remorse, maybe even accepting some consequence. (4) Mina said that turns every moral trade-off into dirty hands — the dieter who skips the cake also leaves a remainder — so either the idea is trivial or Dan needs a line between ordinary trade-offs and the tragic ones, and he hasn't given one. (5) Dan said the line is that some acts aren't just dispreferred, they're normally forbidden in a way that defines what sort of person you are — torturing a suspect, killing a child — and crossing that line changes you even when the numbers come out right. (6) Mina said "changes you" is psychology, not ethics: the act was either permitted or not, and punishing someone for a permitted act is unjust, so the PM shouldn't resign or face consequences. (7) Dan said it isn't punishment for doing the right thing, it's marking the cost so the norm against killing children doesn't quietly erode. (8) Mina said that's a lovely ritual, but it selects for the wrong people: the leaders who do the awful necessary thing and then resign get replaced by ones who do it without the guilt. Could you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about what, where each of them overreaches, and whether there's a coherent position left at the end?
