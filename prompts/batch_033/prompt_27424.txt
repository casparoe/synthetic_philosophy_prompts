Daniel Dennett's intentional stance is often glossed as the view that one is justified in attributing beliefs and desires to a system whenever doing so is the most effective way of predicting its behaviour. The stance was originally motivated by systems far simpler than present-day language models, where the attributions were plainly instrumental. But the condition now appears to be met by large language models: treating them as though they hold beliefs about the world and desires about the conversation is often the most reliable available way to anticipate their outputs. Does the intentional stance therefore settle the question whether such systems literally have beliefs? If it does not settle that question, what exactly does the stance license, and what is left open? Please write your answer in British English.
