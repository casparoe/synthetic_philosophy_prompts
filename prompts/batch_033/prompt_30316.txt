so two people in my english class, jake and maria, got into it last week about whether the self is real. jake had watched some youtube thing about dennett and was going on about how the self is a "center of narrative gravity" — basically that the self isn't a real thing inside you, it's more like the protagonist of a story your brain tells. maria thought this was completly ridiculous and they went back and forth for like the whole lunch period.

here's roughly how it went:

maria: if the self is just a story, who's telling it? you need a self to do the storytelling in the first place.

jake: nah, the brain tells the story. the self is the character, not the author. like, your brain generates this narrative and the "you" is the main character in it.

maria: but i'm experiencing my life right now. i'm not a character in a book. when i stub my toe it hurts ME, not some fictional protagonist.

jake: right, but the feeling that there's a "me" doing the experiencing — that's part of the narrative too. the narrative includes a character who feels like an experiencer.

maria: that's circular. you're saying the self is an illusion, but an illusion needs someone to be fooled by it. who's being fooled?

jake: i don't think it works like that. think about a center of gravity — it's not a real physical thing, there's no actual point-mass there, but it's still useful to talk about it. the self is like that. a useful fiction.

maria: okay but a center of gravity doesn't think it exists. i do. the fiction itself is conscious of being a fiction, which makes no sense.

jake: the fiction isn't conscious. the brain is conscious. the brain generates both the consciousness and the character. the character — the self — is part of what the brain generates, not the thing doing the generating.

maria: so you're saying my brain is conscious but "i'm" not? that seems like word games. if my brain is conscious then something is conscious, and that something is me.

jake: i'm saying "you" is a useful label for a bunch of processes, not a single thing. there's no little homunculus in there. it's just neurons doing neuron stuff, and one of the things they do is create this story about a unified self.

maria: but then who is the story FOR? stories have audiences. if there's no self, there's nobody listening to the narrative, so what's the point of telling it?

jake: it's not FOR anyone. it's just what brains do to coordinate behavior across time. the narrative helps the organism plan and make decisions. no audience required.

maria: that still seems like you're sneaking in a self. "the organism plans" — plans for who? "coordinates behavior" — coordinates it into what? you keep using words that imply an agent and then saying there's no agent.

then the bell rang and they had to stop. i've been thinking about it since and i genuinely can't tell who's got the better argument. can you go through their exchange move by move and adjudicate? like, which points actually land, which ones are missing something important, and where (if anywhere) they're just talking past each other? also, is there a way to actually settle this debate or does it come down to what you mean by "real"?
