I'm a novelist, mostly literary fiction but I've been drifting toward speculative stuff lately. My last book did okay and now I'm trying to write a short story collection, and one of the pieces I'm stuck on is about wireheading — you know, the old thought experiment where you plug into a machine that directly stimulates your brain's reward centers so you feel perfect bliss all the time, but your life is actually going nowhere. I've got the setup: near-future, a startup called (tentatively) Eidolon has released a consumer device, the "Halo," that's basically a non-invasive neurostimulator you wear like a headband. It doesn't knock you out or anything — you can walk around, talk to people, do your job — but it constantly optimizes your subjective well-being. The twist I'm playing with is that the device doesn't just make you feel good; it gradually tampers with what you find rewarding, so you end up wanting exactly what you're already getting. The protagonist is a product manager at Eidolon who starts using the Halo to cope with a breakup and slowly realizes she's being rewritten from the inside.

I need the actual story — maybe 2,000-3,000 words. I want it to really sit with the philosophical problem: if the device makes you genuinely happy and you genuinely want the life your living, what's a argument that something has gone wrong? Like, is there a coherent objection that isn't just status quo bias or some puritanical reflex about "earning" your happiness? I don't want the story to preach — it should dramatize the question, not answer it. Can you write this for me? Give me something with scenes, dialogue, interiority, the works. And please don't let it turn into a morality tale where the device obviously breaks and everyone learns a lesson. I want the horror to be that it works perfectly, and the protagonist can't even trust her own objection to it because that objection might itself be the device talking her out of disabling it. Or maybe it isn't. That's the vertigo I'm after.
