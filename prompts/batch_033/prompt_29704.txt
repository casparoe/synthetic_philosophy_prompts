Analyze the July 20 plot of 1944 — Claus von Stauffenberg's attempt to assassinate Hitler at the Wolf's Lair and the military resistance's plan to seize control of the state afterward — through the lens of Aquinas's treatment of tyrannicide. I know the familiar story: the bomb in the briefcase, the failed explosion, the executions that night, the wider purge. What I want is a disciplined mapping. Run the episode through Aquinas's actual distinctions rather than a slogan: a tyrant by usurpation versus a legitimate ruler who governs tyrannically, tyranny to be tolerated versus tyranny to be resisted, action by public authority versus action by a private person, and the warning that removing a tyrant may install a worse one. Say what, by the lights of that framework, the conspirators faced, what it would have permitted or forbidden them to do, and whether the framework can even get a grip on a totalitarian state in which no public authority remains above the tyrant to appeal to. Where the text supports more than one reading, work through the readings rather than choosing the convenient one. Then run the analysis in the other direction: which features of the episode does Aquinas's account explain well — for instance, the conspirators' insistence that they acted on conscience and duty rather than private ambition, or the fear that a botched coup would make the regime harsher — and which features does it misdescribe or leave out, such as the genocidal character of the regime, the personal oath of loyalty, and the awkward fact that many of the plotters had served that regime for years. Be explicit about where the theory is silent or underdetermined, and close by saying whether the episode is better read as a confirmation of Aquinas's account, an anomaly for it, or a case it was never built to handle.
