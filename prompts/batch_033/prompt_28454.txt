ok so i'm teaching a philosophy of math course and we just hit skolem's paradox. you know the one: downward löwenheim-skolem says that if ZFC has a model at all, it has a countable model. but ZFC proves there are uncountable sets (cantor's theorem, the whole diagonal argument). so you've got a countable model that contains a set the model itself "thinks" is uncountable. what gives?

i want to give my students a taxonomy of the positions people actually take on this, as a handout. not a historical survey — i want a tree built from the logical space. what are the questions that genuinely split the positions, and what answers to those questions land you where?

i want the leaves to include at least: the deflationary response (it's just the internal/external distinction, no real paradox), the second-order response (first-order logic is too weak, that's the whole problem), the relativist/multiverse position (cardinality is model-relative, there's no absolute fact), and the view that it's a genuine problem for platonism about sets.

for each leaf, give me the name of the position (or say if it's unnamed), the one-sentence core claim, and the most awkward thing the position has to bite — the commitment its defenders squirm about.

flag any pair of leaves your tree can't cleanly separate, and mark any logically possible combination of answers that nobody seems to occupy, with a guess about why it's empty (incoherent? just unexplored?).

also — and this is important for how i teach it — tell me what would change your mind about how you've drawn the tree. which branch is shakiest, and what argument or consideration would make you redraw it?
