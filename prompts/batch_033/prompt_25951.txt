I've spent my career in the natural sciences, where the idea of progress is relatively unproblematic: we know more about the genome, about plate tectonics, about the cosmic microwave background than we did fifty years ago, and that knowledge accumulates in a roughly monotonic way. Since retiring, I've been reading philosophy seriously, and I keep running into a debate that I find both fascinating and slippery: does philosophy itself make progress? The answers seem to range from "of course, just look at the history" to "of course not, that's the whole point," with a great deal of sophisticated terrain in between. I'd like to get clearer on the logical geography.

Please construct a decision tree or taxonomy that sorts positions on whether philosophy makes progress. I want the tree to be built from the logical space rather than from a list of received labels: first identify the key questions on which positions genuinely come apart, then locate the actual positions within the space of possible answers, noting any combinations that are logically possible but unoccupied (and briefly diagnosing why — incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect). Positions the tree cannot cleanly separate should be flagged rather than papered over.

Here are the constraints I'd like you to work with:

1. The tree should have roughly eight to twelve leaves, covering the major live options in the contemporary debate. At minimum, include: some version of the view that philosophy converges on truths in a way analogous to science; the view that philosophy makes progress but of a fundamentally different kind (e.g., by refining questions or eliminating dead ends); the view that philosophical problems are perennial and insoluble, so talk of progress is misplaced; and the view that philosophy's value lies elsewhere entirely and progress is the wrong metric.

2. Each branching node should be a diagnostic question phrased so that someone can answer it without already knowing which position their answer leads to. The questions should capture the claims on which the positions genuinely divide, not surface-level associations. Avoid questions like "do you think philosophy makes progress?" — that's what we're trying to diagnose, not ask directly.

3. For each leaf, give: a short name for the position; one or two sentences stating its core commitment; and one sentence identifying its most awkward or difficult commitment — the thing a critic would press hardest.

4. Since I come from the sciences, I'm particularly interested in positions that draw analogies between philosophical and scientific progress, and in positions that explicitly reject such analogies. Where relevant, flag how a given leaf handles the comparison with science.

5. If there are pairs of positions that your tree cannot separate — because they agree on all the diagnostic questions you've chosen — please flag them explicitly and explain what further question would be needed to pull them apart.
