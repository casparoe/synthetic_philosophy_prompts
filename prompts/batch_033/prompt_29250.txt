I'm putting together a handout for a grad seminar on non-classical logic and I want a 'which view on contradictions do you actually hold?' quiz, the sort of thing where people get sorted by what they say about cases rather than by which label they like. Eight questions, each a short concrete case: the liar sentence, a statute that both permits and forbids the same act, a database that records a customer as both having paid and not paid, a heap case at the boundary, Russell's set, a time-travel story where someone both is and is not their own ancestor, a fictional sacred text issuing two flatly incompatible commands, and a pair of conflicting but equally careful measurements. Give each question two or three answer options, and make sure the options track live theoretical choices (both true and false, neither true nor false, simply false, true and we just have to live with the consequences, and so on) without using any theory names in the questions themselves. Then provide a scoring key in plain prose that maps answer patterns onto these leaves: classical logician, gappy or paracomplete view, paraconsistent non-dialetheist, dialetheist, and trivialist. Build the key from the space of answers first rather than from the labels, note any answer combination that no defended position occupies and say in a sentence whether it looks incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored, and flag any two leaves your questions cant really separate. After the key, add a paragraph on where you're least confident: which question is most likely to misclassify people, and what pattern of answers would lead you to redraw the boundaries. Keep the whole thing as continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, just the way you'd write an email.
