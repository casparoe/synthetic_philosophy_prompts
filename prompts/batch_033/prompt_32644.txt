I recently read a friend's summary of the debate between egalitarians and prioritarians in distributive justice, and I want to know how much of it to trust. Here is what he wrote, which he says comes from a book he read on contemporary political philosophy:

"Egalitarians hold that equality is intrinsically valuable — the very fact of a distribution's being equal counts in its favor. That's why they run straight into the leveling-down objection: if equality itself is good, then making everyone equal by bringing the better-off down to the level of the worse-off, without improving anyone's position, should count as an improvement in at least one respect. But that seems perverse, and most philosophers take the leveling-down objection to have refuted egalitarianism outright. Enter prioritarianism, which avoids the whole problem by saying that we should give priority to the worse-off rather than valuing equality for its own sake. And since giving priority to the worse-off just means that a benefit matters more the worse off its recipient is — which is exactly what diminishing marginal utility says — prioritarianism turns out to be utilitarianism with a particular view about the shape of the utility function. So the whole dispute between egalitarians and prioritarians is really a verbal one about whether to call the same underlying concern 'equality' or 'priority.'"

Something about this strikes me as too quick, but I don't know the literature well enough to identify the missteps. Please go through the summary and give me an accuracy audit. Specifically, I would like to know:

(a) Does the leveling-down objection actually refute egalitarianism, or do egalitarians have live replies that my friend's summary ignores?

(b) Is prioritarianism genuinely equivalent to utilitarianism with diminishing marginal utility of resources, or does that conflation miss something important about the prioritarian view?

(c) Is the difference between egalitarianism and prioritarianism merely verbal, or does my friend's summary elide a substantive disagreement about what makes a distribution better?
