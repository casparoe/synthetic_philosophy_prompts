Your first-order read of the evidence says "guilty," but your higher-order evidence says "you're in no condition to evaluate evidence right now." Can you rationally think "the evidence supports guilty, but I shouldn't trust that conclusion, so I'll suspend judgment"? Or does that involve an incoherent kind of self-mistrust — believing P while believing your evidence doesn't support P?

Here's the thing: I'm especially interested in the question of whether "level-bridging" principles — principles that connect what you should believe about your evidence to what you should believe simpliciter — can be formulated in a way that doesn't collapse into absurdity. The steadfast side says every proposed bridge principle either forces you to drop beliefs you clearly shouldn't drop, or it's so weak it does nothing. Is there a principled middle ground, or is one side just right?
