I'm a software engineer, and I've been reading up on the simulation argument and longtermism in my spare time. Something isn't squaring for me.

If Bostrom's simulation argument shows that we're almost certainly living in a computer simulation, then doesn't that pretty much gut the case for caring about the far future? The way I see it, a simulation is just a program, and programs get terminated all the time — the simulators could pull the plug whenever they want. Maybe they get bored, maybe their funding runs out, maybe they've already collected whatever data they're after. And even if the simulation runs for another million years, nothing we do inside it has any effect on base reality, so the only thing that could possibly matter is what happens at the simulator level. If that's right, then the whole longtermist project — worrying about existential risks centuries from now, trying to shape the trajectory of civilization over millennia — seems like a mistake. We should be trying to figure out how to communicate with the simulators or escape the simulation, not worrying about what happens inside it.

I'm not trying to be glib here — I genuinely want to understand whether this line of reasoning holds up. I'd appreciate it if the response could separate what's actually settled among people who work on these questions from what's genuinely contested, and not present a contested position as the consensus.
