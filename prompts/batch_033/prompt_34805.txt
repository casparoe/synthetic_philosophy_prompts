I'm a software engineer by trade, no formal philosophy background, but I've ended up down a rabbit hole on Occam's razor and why it works. I keep seeing people cite Solomonoff induction and Kolmogorov complexity as if they settle the whole thing: simpler hypotheses get higher prior probability because shorter programs are more likely under a universal prior, so Occam is basically a theorem. But the more I poke at it, the less clear I am about what's actually been shown and what's just hand-waving. For instance, the choice of reference machine only changes Kolmogorov complexity by a constant, but that constant can be arbitrarily large, so in any finite case it could swamp everything. And Solomonoff induction is uncomputable anyway, so what is it actually buying me as someone who trains models?

If I had a few months of evenings to spend on the conceptual side of this—not to write a paper, just to get my head straight—which two or three open questions or lines of work in the Solomonoff/Kolmogorov/Occam area should I prioritise, and why? Also flag the commonly repeated implications that are overrated or don't follow at all—e.g. that Solomonoff induction justifies Occam's razor, that compression equals intelligence, or that Kolmogorov complexity explains why simpler models generalise better.
