I'm a PhD student in political philosophy, and I'm preparing a paper on the leveling-down objection and its implications for the debate between prioritarians and egalitarians. I'd like you to write a dialogue between two characters:

- **Alex**, a prioritarian who holds that benefiting the worse off has greater moral weight — roughly, the moral value of a unit of well-being is a strictly increasing, concave function of the recipient's absolute well-being level — but who denies that equality is intrinsically valuable. Alex thinks the leveling-down objection shows that telic egalitarianism is untenable and that prioritarianism is the best way to capture what was right in egalitarian intuitions without the implausible implications.
- **Bryony**, a telic egalitarian who holds that inequality itself is non-instrumentally bad, and who thinks the leveling-down objection can be met — or at least that prioritarianism purchases immunity to the objection at the cost of abandoning the very concerns that make egalitarianism attractive in the first place.

The dialogue should be a genuine back-and-forth in which each character responds to the other's actual moves. Neither is a strawman; both are philosophically competent and aware of the relevant arguments in the literature. I want to see where the pressure points really are — where each view has to make concessions, and where the disagreement bottoms out in a clash of bedrock intuitions rather than a mistake one side is making.

Some issues I'd like to see them work through, though the dialogue should follow its own organic course rather than mechanically ticking through a checklist:

- The distinction between telic and deontic egalitarianism, and whether the leveling-down objection bites equally against both or whether the deontic egalitarian can sidestep it.
- The claim that prioritarianism cannot explain the intuitive objection to certain inegalitarian distributions — for instance, a case where two groups are both well off in absolute terms but one is much better off than the other, and there is no prioritarian-weighted reason to redistribute because the marginal utility of well-being has flattened for both.
- The pluralist egalitarian reply: that leveling down can be bad all things considered (because it reduces total well-being) while still being good in one respect (the respect of equality), and whether this move is principled or merely ad hoc.
- Whether the prioritarian's concave function is itself a way of smuggling in a concern for equality under a different name, and if so, whether that matters.

Please write the dialogue in British English. The characters should be roughly equally matched; the dialogue should not read as though one side is obviously winning. Please separate what is settled in the literature from what is genuinely contested among those working on the topic, and do not present a contested view as though it were the consensus.
