I have been trying to settle, on my own, whether I should take the claims of current science at something close to face value, and the thing that most troubles me is the pessimistic meta-induction: the pattern in which theories that were once extraordinarily successful were later discarded as false in central ways. I have read enough to see that people argue about this one historical case at a time — the caloric theory of heat, the luminiferous aether, phlogiston, Newtonian gravitation — and I cannot tell what would actually settle the dispute, because I have never seen a full statement of what a position would have to get right in the first place. So before I read any further, I want the desiderata: what must an adequate realist position about scientific knowledge deliver in order to survive the pessimistic meta-induction while still being genuinely realist — still holding that mature scientific theories are approximately true, or that their central terms typically refer to real things — rather than a disguised instrumentalism or a retreat to the view that science is merely useful?

Give me the requirements list. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say whether it is non-negotiable or merely the preference of one camp. Distinguish the things a position cannot collapse into one another — for example, a theory's being false and its central terms' failing to refer, or predictive success and truth. Be explicit about which historical episodes any adequate position must handle and why. Then tell me whether some subset of these requirements cannot be jointly satisfied given the actual history of science; that is the part I most want, because if the list itself is inconsistent, that is a result rather than a failure. End with a committed judgment: which single requirement does the most to eliminate the standard positions, and which position — full realism, selective realism, structural realism, instrumentalism, or something else — comes closest to meeting the full list in your view. Take a definite position and give your reasons; do not lay out the options and leave me to choose. I am not asking for citations or a reading list; I want the reasoning itself.
