I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I contribute to a magazine that runs a weekly feature based on reader-submitted summaries of recent research. The editors are considering replacing the current system—two senior editors select the strongest submissions—with an open vote: readers would upvote the summaries they consider most accurate and important, and the top-scoring pieces would be featured. One editor argues that with thousands of readers, the majority is almost certainly better than any small group of editors at identifying which summaries are accurate and important. Another editor dismisses this as a popularity contest. I can see something in both positions.

Please take the role of an expert in social epistemology and the mathematics of collective decision-making, and think carefully about the structure of this disagreement. Under what conditions does majority voting among a large group outperform a small group of experts at arriving at correct or well-founded judgments, and which of those conditions plausibly hold or fail here? I'd like a precise, point-by-point analysis rather than generic advice about crowdsourcing, ending with a clear assessment of whether the first editor's argument actually supports the proposed system.
