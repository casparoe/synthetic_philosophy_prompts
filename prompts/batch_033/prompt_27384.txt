I have been reading the Churchlands and Fodor on eliminative materialism, and I find myself wanting something I rarely see: a document that both sides could actually sign, laying out what they agree on, where exactly they part ways, and what would move the debate. I am a retired geophysicist, so I am used to controversies that eventually get settled by data, and I keep wondering what the analogue would be here.

Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed eliminative materialist about propositional attitudes and a committed realist about beliefs and desires (of the sort who holds that folk psychology picks out real, causally efficacious internal states that will be preserved, perhaps reduced, by mature cognitive science). The document should have two sections.

Section 1: Numbered claims both sides can sign. These should not be vague truisms. They should capture substantive points of agreement about the structure and function of folk psychology, about what neuroscience has and has not shown, about the track record of folk-psychological explanation, about what reduction or elimination would consist in, and about the criteria by which the dispute should be adjudicated. If there are historical cases (phlogiston, caloric, vital forces, genes) that both sides accept as relevant analogies or disanalogies, include them — but only if both would genuinely sign the formulation.

Section 2: A disagreements table. Each row should contain: (a) a precise statement of the point of disagreement, phrased in neutral terms; (b) the eliminativist's position, worded as the eliminativist would accept; (c) the realist's position, worded as the realist would accept. The table should be followed by a list of cruxes: the sub-questions whose resolution would cause at least one side to revise its view. For each crux, specify what sort of empirical or conceptual development both sides would acknowledge as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction about what that development will show.

I am especially interested in whether any part of the dispute turns out to be verbal — for instance, whether the two sides mean different things by "belief" or "elimination," such that they might agree on all the facts and still talk past each other. If you find such a component, flag it explicitly and say why it does not dissolve the entire disagreement.
