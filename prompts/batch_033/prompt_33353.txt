"Searle's Chinese Room argument is meant to show that the Turing Test is not a sufficient condition for genuine understanding or intelligence. The thought experiment goes like this: imagine a monolingual English speaker locked in a room with a big rulebook written in English. Chinese speakers outside slide slips of paper with Chinese writing under the door. The person inside has no idea what any of the symbols mean, but the rulebook tells him exactly which symbols to write in response to which input symbols. He follows the rules mechanically and pushes the output slips back out. From the perspective of the Chinese speakers outside, the room is carrying on a perfectly coherent conversation in Chinese — it passes the Turing Test for understanding Chinese. But the person inside understands nothing. Searle's point is that a digital computer is in exactly the same situation: it runs a program (the rulebook) that manipulates formal symbols based on their shapes, not their meanings, and so it has no genuine understanding. Syntax alone cannot produce semantics.

The main objection to Searle is the Systems Reply. This reply says that Searle is looking for understanding in the wrong place. Of course the person inside doesn't understand Chinese — he's just a component of the larger system, like a single neuron. The whole system, consisting of the person, the rulebook, the scratch paper, and the room, is what understands Chinese, even though no individual part does. Searle responds to this by imagining that the person internalizes the entire system: he memorizes the rulebook, does all the symbol matching in his head, and goes outside to interact with Chinese speakers directly. He still doesn't understand Chinese. So, Searle claims, the Systems Reply fails — adding more components or internalizing them doesn't create understanding where there was none.

I think Searle's argument is ultimately successful. The Systems Reply tries to locate understanding in the system as a whole, but Searle's internalization move shows that even when the whole system is collapsed into one person there's no understanding there. The Systems Reply is basically question-begging because it just assumes that the right kind of organization will produce understanding, which is exactly what Searle is challenging. Moreover, Searle's argument shows that no purely computational system could ever have genuine intentionality, because computation is defined purely syntactically and syntax is observer-relative — you can't get semantics from syntax alone. So the Chinese Room refutes not just the Turing Test as a criterion for intelligence, but the whole project of strong AI."
