im drafting a short "what i think about Goodhart and specification gaming" note for a reading group, and before i send it around i want a consistency check on the set of views i've ended up with. here they are, roughly as i'd state them:

(1) Goodhart's law is basically a fact about optimization pressure: if you optimize a proxy hard enough, you eventually find points where the proxy and the underlying target come apart, because the optimizer is finding the proxy's edge cases.

(2) specification gaming is a gap problem: there's a mismatch between the objective as written and what we actually want. if we could write down the true objective exactly—not a proxy for it—hard optimization would be fine, and the remaining problems would just be engineering.

(3) in practice you can stay out of the bad regime: regularization, ensembling several proxies, or quantilizing keeps the optimizer weak enough that the proxy still tracks the target, so Goodhart effects are manageable without solving the whole problem.

(4) there isn't really a fact of the matter about what an agent "actually wants" over and above what its behavior would reveal; goal-talk is a compressed way of describing policies, not a report of some hidden mental item.

(5) reward hacking, Campbell's law, the Lucas critique, and Goodhart's law are all the same underlying phenomenon: a metric that starts out as a proxy for something else gets treated as a target and then gets gamed by the agents optimizing against it.

(6) a sufficiently capable optimizer will eventually exploit any finite, human-specified proxy or test suite, so no finite battery of evaluations can certify alignment.

(7) because of (6), i've become drawn to process-based oversight—evaluating how the agent reasoned rather than just what it output—as the thing that might actually scale.

what i'd like is an audit, not a lecture: tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they're in tension, and rank the genuine tensions by severity, saying what would have to give in each case. also, please flag what's settled and what's genuinely contested among people who work on this, and dont present a contested view as if it were the consensus.
