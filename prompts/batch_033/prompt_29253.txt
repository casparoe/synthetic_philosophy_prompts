I keep noticing a pattern when people assign blame and credit, and I can't find the right word for it. Suppose a manager approves a project that will increase profits, and as a foreseeable but not really wanted side effect it will either damage or help the environment. When the side effect is damage, people say the manager harmed the environment intentionally. When it's help, far fewer people say the manager helped intentionally. Same structure, same foresight, just the valence of the side effect flipped. I've seen the same thing with a soldier's bullet hitting a civilian versus hitting an enemy, and with a drug that causes a nasty rash versus a nice side benefit. Is there an established name for this, and is it best understood as a discovery about the concept of intentional action, or about how people's moral judgments color what they report? If there's no exact name, what would you call it, and how do you separate it from things like moral luck or the halo effect?
