So I'm a retired physicist — cosmology was my field — and I've been spending my retirement reading philosophy, which my old colleagues find baffling but honestly it scratches the same itch. Lately I've been stuck on the doomsday argument. You know, the one where you're supposed to update toward thinking humanity won't last that long, because if there were going to be trillions of future humans, you probably wouldn't find yourself among the first hundred billion or so. The Bayesian in me can follow the math, but the Bayesian in me also feels like something is being pulled over my eyes.

Anyway, here's what I'd like: could you write a dialogue between two people arguing about this? One of them thinks the doomsday argument is basically right — maybe not airtight, but it should shift your credences nontrivially. The other thinks there's a fundamental mistake in it. And I want them to go at it properly. Not just trading slogans, but actually responding to each other. The skeptic should push on things like the reference class problem (who counts as "us"? why should we use all humans as the reference class?) and the self-sampling assumption, and the proponent should have real answers. I'd also love if the skeptic brought up the idea that if you accept the doomsday argument, you might have to accept some pretty weird conclusions about other things — like, does it imply we should be more worried about asteroid impacts than we already are? Stuff like that. The point is I want to feel like I'm listening to two smart people who actually disagree, not a staged debate where one side is just setting up the other's talking points.
