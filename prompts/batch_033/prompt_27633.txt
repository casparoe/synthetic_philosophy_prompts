I coordinate a philosophical working group on population ethics and the aggregation of harms. For the better part of a year we have been stuck on what everyone in the group now simply calls 'the Dust Specks Problem.' As you will know, the problem asks whether some sufficiently large number of very mild discomforts — a dust speck in the eye, say, for each of billions or trillions of distinct people — could together be worse than one person's intense, prolonged torture. Two of our most tenacious participants have come to represent the opposing poles of the discussion. The first defends straightforward aggregation: harms are additive across persons, and there is always some finite number of dust specks that would outweigh the torture, even if that number is astronomically large and practically unreachable. The second defends a lexical-threshold view: beyond a certain severity, no amount of mere mild discomfort can outweigh a harm of the worse kind, so the torture is worse than any number of dust specks whatever.

Both have agreed — somewhat to everyone's surprise — to attempt an adversarial collaboration. They have asked me to draft the joint document for them to negotiate and sign, and I would be grateful for a carefully worked-out version.

The document should have the following structure. First, a numbered section of claims both sides accept: about how the problem is best formulated, about what is and what is not at stake in it, about which considerations both regard as genuinely relevant (and which they agree are distractions), and about what each acknowledges as the strongest argument on the other side. Second, a disagreements table with four columns: the point of disagreement, the formulation each side would accept for its own view, the crux or sub-question that separates them, and — where possible — a test case or argument both would agree bears on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Third, a brief closing note on whether any part of the dispute proved to be verbal rather than substantive, and if so, which part and why. Please write this as a fair, precise document that both could in principle sign; do not adjudicate. Use British English throughout.
