ok so i've been trying to get my head around evidential cooperation in large worlds (the idea that in a big universe you should act like you're cooperating with all your copies or near-copies, because your choice is evidence about theirs). i thought i had it until i tried to actually use it, and now i think it might eat itself. here's the argument that's got me stuck:

(1) if ECL is correct, then when i decide whether to do X or Y, i should consider not just the direct effects of my action but also the effects of all the agents who are similar enough to me making the same choice, since my choice provides evidence about theirs.
(2) in a sufficiently large universe, for any choice X vs Y, there are astronomically many agents similar enough to me facing the same choice.
(3) so for any choice, the stakes are astronomical.
(4) but if every choice has astronomical stakes, then the difference in expected value between X and Y depends almost entirely on my estimate of how many copies i have and how similar they are — not on the direct effects of X and Y themselves.
(5) those estimates are extremely fragile: tiny changes in my cosmology or my similarity metric flip the sign of the expected value difference.
(6) a decision theory that makes every choice depend on fragile cosmological estimates rather than on the actual consequences of the options is not a usable decision theory.
(7) so ECL is either false or practically useless.

i want to resist this conclusion because ECL seems to do real work — it explains why you should cooperate in one-shot prisoner's dilemmas with your copies, and it might even ground something like Kantian ethics in decision theory. but i can't see which step to reject. is the problem with (2) — maybe the universe isn't that large or copies aren't that common? or is it with (4) — maybe there's a way to factor out the copy-count so it cancels? or is the whole setup smuggling in something about "similar enough" that needs to be cashed out before the argument even gets off the ground?

tell me which step fails and what giving it up costs. keep it under 400 words and in american english.
