I keep seeing the Tool AI vs. agentic AI debate go in circles, and I'm tired of it. If you had to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between someone who thinks we should only build AI as non-agentic tools and someone who thinks agentic AI is the right paradigm (or at least inevitable enough that we should stop arguing about it), what would that document look like?

I want it structured as: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign — starting with what they actually agree on about what "agent" means, about the technical lay of the land, about where alignment difficulty comes from; (2) a disagreements table where each row is a precise claim both sides would accept as a fair characterization of their dispute; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions that, if answered, would actually move someone; (4) for each crux, the test or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction. If you find that some of their fight is just about words, flag that and say why.

Some specific things I want them to grapple with: whether corrigibility can be maintained in agentic systems without it being a patch on a fundamentally unsafe architecture, whether the tool/agent distinction is even stable under optimization pressure, and whether mesa-optimizers basically make the whole distinction moot. But don't let those constrain them — they may have better cruxes.

I want the real thing, not a diplomatic press release. Push them to say something substantive.
