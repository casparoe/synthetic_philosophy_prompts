My dad died a couple years ago, and I've noticed something since then that I can't quite put my finger on, and I'm wondering if philosophy or cognitive science has a name for it.

It's this: I'll be in some situation — could be anything, fixing a sink, choosing between two job offers, whatever — and I'll just know what he would have said. Not in a vague "he would've wanted me to be happy" way. I mean I can hear his actual voice, his phrasing, even the particular way he'd pause before making a dry joke about it. And here's the weird part: my sister reports the exact same thing, independently. We'll compare notes and she'll say "I knew Dad would've said X" and it'll be word-for-word what I had in my head. It's not like we're remembering a specific conversation we both witnessed — these are novel situations he never faced.

It feels like there's a low-resolution copy of him still running, distributed across the people who knew him well. Not a ghost or anything supernatural — I'm a materialist. But it does feel like his self, his particular way of being a person, hasn't fully stopped existing. It's been parcelled out among us. And the more I think about it, the more this seems like it might be true of everyone all the time, not just the dead — that who I am exists partly in other people's heads, and their selves exist partly in mine, and the boundaries between selves are a lot blurrier than we pretend.

I've tried googling around but I just get grief counselling stuff or vague talk about "living on in memory." I'm after something more precise. Is there an established term or concept in philosophy of mind for this — the idea that a self or consciousness can be genuinely distributed across multiple brains, not just metaphorically? And if there isn't a single term, what are the closest concepts and how do they differ from what I'm describing? I'm less interested in the label itself than in understanding exactly what this phenomenon is and where its edges are — like, at what point does the distributed version stop being "him" in any meaningful sense? And does anyone actually defend the stronger claim that this distributed thing is literally the same self, rather than just a representation of it?
