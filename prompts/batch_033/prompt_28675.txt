we were doing bradley's regress in my metaphysics class and the prof kept using the glue analogy — you know, a relation is like glue that sticks two things together, but then you need more glue to stick the glue to the things, and off you go into the regress. the conclusion was that relations can't be what unifies a proposition (or a fact, or an object-with-property) because you'd need infinitely many of them. but the more i think about it the more the glue picture seems to be doing all the argumentative work. glue is a *thing* that needs its own attachment to what it joins, but is that a feature of relations or just a feature of glue? i want a proper audit of the analogy: what corresponds to what, where the mapping breaks, which disanalogies matter for the argument and which don't, and whether the regress conclusion survives once you stop picturing relations as a kind of metaphysical adhesive. and if the glue analogy is smuggling in the conclusion, is there a better analogy that doesn't?
