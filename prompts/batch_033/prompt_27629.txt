Okay so I've gotten pretty convinced by the Hofstadter and Sander view that analogy-making is the core of cognition — not just one tool in the toolkit but the thing that thinking basically is. The idea that categorization is analogical, that every concept is built by mapping new situations onto old ones, that even the most abstract reasoning is just layers of analogy all the way down. I buy it. But I want to know what would count against it. Not the obvious stuff like "what about logical deduction" — I want the serious defeaters. What experimental results, what cognitive phenomena, what arguments from linguistics or neuroscience should actually make me worried? For instance, if it turned out that some kinds of reasoning really are rule-governed in a way that can't be cashed out as analogical mapping, or if there are cognitive processes that develop before analogy-making does and don't depend on it — would those count? I want the defeaters ranked from "this kills the view" to "this is a bad Tuesday but you patch it," and I want to know whether defenders of the view would actually conceed these as counting against them or whether they'd reinterpret them. Also: has the view been stated in a way that makes it unfalsifiable, and if so, is that a problem? Like, is it an empirical claim that could be wrong, or is it more of a framework you adopt, and does that distinction matter here?
