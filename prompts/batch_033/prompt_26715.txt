I've been thinking about the line between medical treatment and human enhancement — things like whether taking modafinil to focus better is fundamentally different from taking an antidepressant for diagnosed depression, or whether gene editing to prevent Huntington's disease is in a different moral category than editing for taller height. I'd like to build a decision tree that takes someone step by step from their most basic intuitions about this distinction to a named position.

The tree should begin with the most fundamental question: whether there is any real, non-arbitrary difference between therapy and enhancement at all. From there, each branch should split on a concrete claim or case where the major positions genuinely come apart. Every node should be phrased as a question someone can answer without already knowing which position it leads to — the point is to sort people by what they believe about the cases, not by which label they prefer.

The leaves should include at least these positions: the view that the distinction is empty and should be abandoned; the view that normal species functioning provides the right anchor; the view that disease concepts, even if partly socially constructed, do the necessary work; the welfarist view that only outcomes for well-being matter; and the bioconservative view that enhancements are presumptively suspect regardless of how the line is drawn.

Please flag any pairs of positions your tree cannot separate, and note any logically possible combinations of answers that no one seems to defend — and briefly say whether that is because the position is incoherent, deeply unattractive, or simply unexplored.
