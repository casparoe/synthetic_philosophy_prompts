My neighbourhood association runs an annual fundraising campaign for the local park. The target is £20,000, and roughly 2,000 households are asked for £20 each. If the target is met, the park stays well-maintained; if we fall well short, it deteriorates. I know my individual £20 is vanishingly unlikely to be the deciding contribution that tips the total over the threshold. But I also know I am not special: roughly two thousand other people are in my position, looking at the same numbers and asking themselves the same question. If I decide not to give because my £20 doesn't matter, and everyone sufficiently similar to me reasons the same way, we collectively guarantee the park's decline. Does the fact that I cannot causally control what my neighbours do settle the question of whether I should contribute? Or should my decision take into account that it provides evidence about what people like me will do, even though we never interact? Please answer in British English, and keep your response to no more than 400 words.
