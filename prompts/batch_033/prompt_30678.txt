A friend who teaches South Asian philosophy walked me into this and now I can't find the exit. He was defending karma as a theory of moral desert, and his version says:

(1) The serious suffering in a person's life is the ripening of a wrong that person did, in this life or a previous one.
(2) If someone's present suffering is the ripening of their own past wrong, then that suffering is deserved.
(3) If a person deserves their suffering, then they have no valid complaint against it.
(4) If a person has no valid complaint against their suffering, then third parties have no obligation to relieve it; at best, helping them is supererogatory.
(5) So on karma-as-desert, we have no obligation to relieve poverty, sickness, or oppression, since the people suffering those things deserve them.
(6) But we plainly do have obligations to relieve those things. A moral view that made the suffering of the poor none of our business would be monstrous.
(7) Therefore karma is either false as a theory of moral desert, or it is not a theory of desert at all.

I'd rather keep (6) and keep some version of karma than swallow the conclusion, so something has to give. Which step do you reject? Explain why that step looked so plausible, and itemize what rejecting it costs. Keep it under 500 words.
