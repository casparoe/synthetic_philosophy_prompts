I'm in an upper-level undergraduate seminar on contemporary theories of rights, and we've just spent two weeks on Nozick's discussion of animals in chapter three of *Anarchy, State, and Utopia*. For my final project I need to design and run a thirty-minute in-class activity for about fifteen students. The activity should make participants feel the force of what I take to be the central unresolved tension in Nozick's view. He grants that animals have rights — they are side constraints on our action, not merely loci of utility to be traded off — but he also holds that these constraints can be overridden when the human benefit at stake is sufficiently significant. The difficulty, which Nozick himself flags, is that once you allow a right to be overridden for "significant" benefits, you need a principled account of the threshold, and he never supplies one. He even says he is uncertain whether the pleasure of eating meat clears it.

I want the activity to force students to make a series of concrete decisions about tradeoffs between animal harm and human benefit, where the structure of the decisions gradually reveals that the significance threshold is doing nearly all the normative work and that Nozick gives us no resources to set it non-arbitrarily. By the end, students should see that the view faces a dilemma: either it needs a theory of significance that Nozick does not provide, at which point the override condition is unprincipled, or it collapses into a form of weighted utilitarianism, at which point it loses the distinctive character of rights as side constraints.

Design the activity: the setup, the rules, any roles students are assigned, any materials I would need to prepare, a minute-by-minute timeline for the thirty minutes, and a detailed debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto Nozick's concepts — side constraints, override conditions, the significance threshold, the comparison with utilitarianism, and his own stated uncertainty about eating meat. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how I should handle it, and identify which feature of Nozick's view the activity structurally cannot reproduce and I will need to supply in discussion afterwards.
