I cover tech and ideas for a living, and I've started to suspect that a lot of the hand-wringing about "responsibility gaps" with autonomous systems is basically right: when a self-driving car or a battlefield drone does harm, the operator didn't intend it, the company built a learning system whose behavior nobody fully specified, and the machine isn't the kind of thing that can be answerable for what it did. So there's no one left who deserves blame in the ordinary sense. I want you to push back on that position — what would count against it? Not just the standard "we can always sue someone" line, but the best cases, arguments, and developments that should make me think the gap is smaller than I've been assuming, or that whole framing is off. Rank them from decisive to merely awkward, and tell me straight whether the position has been insulated to the point of unfalsifiability — and if it has, whether that's a problem for a claim like this.
