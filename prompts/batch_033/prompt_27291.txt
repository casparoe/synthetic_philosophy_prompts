I've been trying to work through Benatar's asymmetry argument for antinatalism, and I've hit a problem I can't resolve. The asymmetry, as I understand it, involves four claims: (1) the presence of pain is bad; (2) the presence of pleasure is good; (3) the absence of pain is good, even if no one exists to experience that absence; and (4) the absence of pleasure is not bad, provided there is no one who is deprived of it. From this, Benatar concludes that coming into existence is always a harm. But here is my difficulty. If we accept claim (3) — that the absence of pain is good even when no one exists — then for any possible person we refrain from creating, we secure the good of their absent pain. But for any possible person we do create, we secure the good of their pleasure and we also incur the bad of their pain. Now consider a possible person whose life would contain vastly more pleasure than pain. Creating them seems to yield a large net good (pleasure minus pain), while not creating them yields only the good of absent pain. And since claim (3) does not scale with the severity of the pain that would have occurred — the absence of a mild headache and the absence of a lifetime of agony both count as good in the non-existence scenario — the good of absent pain looks like a fixed or at least capped benefit, while the net pleasure of a flourishing life could be enormous. So the asymmetry seems to recommend creating happy people, not refraining. Benatar obviously thinks otherwise. Where does my reasoning go wrong?
