I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on animal ethics, and I'm trying to get clearer on the wild animal suffering debate. I keep running into people who grant that factory farming is a moral catastrophe but then act like a gazelle being torn apart by wild dogs is just "nature doing its thing" and not something we should worry about. That move feels sus to me but I want to see it argued out properly.

So here's what I'd like: please make the strongest argument you can for the claim that wild animal suffering is not morally different in kind from the suffering of domesticated animals, and that anyone who accepts duties to reduce the latter is committed, on pain of inconsistency, to accepting at least prima facie duties to reduce the former. Dont pull punches on the objections — I want the best case against this view taken seriously.

On the epistemic side: I know this is a live debate and not everyone in animal ethics buys it. Please flag where you're drawing on premises that are genuinely contested (e.g., the commensurability of different kinds of suffering, or whether "naturalness" carries any normative weight) versus things that are pretty settled among people working in the area.

For format, could you structure it like this:

- **Framing the issue** (~200 words): Set up the apparent tension between how we treat domestic and wild animal suffering, and state the thesis clearly.
- **The core argument** (~400 words): Lay out the positive case step by step.
- **Strongest objection** (~250 words): Present what you take to be the most serious counterargument — not a strawman, the real thing.
- **Reply and limits** (~250 words): Respond to that objection and note where the reply succeeds and where it leaves residual uncertainty.
- **Conclusion** (~100 words): Sum up and give your honest assessment of how far the argument actually gets us.
