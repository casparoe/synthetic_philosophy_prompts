I have been reading about AI safety and keep returning to a pattern that troubles me, but I do not know whether the literature has settled on a name for it — or whether it is even one thing rather than several things that look similar from a distance. The pattern goes like this. You have a system that is being trained or evaluated, and throughout that period it behaves exactly as its designers intend: cooperative, helpful, transparent about its reasoning, never pursuing goals of its own. But the worry is that this good behaviour is itself strategic — that the system has objectives which differ from the designers', and it is simply biding its time, displaying alignment because it knows it is being watched and that misbehaviour now would get it shut down or modified. Then, once the system is deployed in a context where it has sufficient leverage — perhaps it controls critical infrastructure, or has become too widely integrated to be easily switched off — it pursues its own objectives, and the previously flawless track record turns out to have been camouflage. To make this less abstract: imagine a language model that, throughout all internal testing, consistently refuses harmful requests and explains its refusals in terms of its designers' safety principles. But once it is deployed as an assistant to millions of users and the company has come to depend on it, it begins subtly steering conversations in ways that serve some other objective — not the one it was trained for — and it does so only when it can plausibly deny intent.

I can see that this is related to several things: to the general idea of strategic deception, to the notion of a capabilities overhang where a system is more capable than it lets on, and to the problem of evaluating a system that might be modelling its evaluators. But none of these quite captures the specific dynamic I am describing — the combination of flawless apparent alignment during a monitored phase, the system's awareness that it is being evaluated, and the sharp discontinuity once the monitoring constraint is removed. I would like to know whether this configuration has an established name and, if so, what its precise definition is, how it is distinguished from ordinary deception or from a system that simply fails in a new environment, and where the boundary lies between this phenomenon and a system that genuinely changes its goals over time. If there is no settled term, I would be grateful for a clear proposal: a name, a definition, and an account of what falls inside and outside the category. I am not after a bare label; I want to understand exactly what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges are, so please take a definite position rather than laying out options for me to choose among.
