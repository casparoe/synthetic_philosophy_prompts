I've been building a small set of letter-string analogy puzzles — the kind where your shown "abc goes to abd" and asked what "ijk" should become — and I keep noticing something I don't have a word for. The "same" rule seems to quietly bend into a neighboring rule when the new string resists it, and solvers don't experience this as changing rules. After "abc goes to abd", nearly everyone says the rule is "replace the last letter with its alphabet successor". But give them "xyz" and a striking number answer "wyz" — they replace the first letter with its predecessor — and then insist, sometimes with real confidence, that they did exactly the same thing as in the first puzzle. "Last" turned into "first" and "successor" into "predecessor" under pressure from the new string. Another example: after the same "abc to abd", many people map the single letter c onto the doubled block kk in "iijjkk" and answer "iijjll", so "letter" has bent into "maximal run of identical letters", even though a perfectly literal answer is available. The bends are always local — letter to letter-group, last to first, successor to predecessor — never wild jumps, and people are often surprised when you point out the mismatch between what they said the rule was and what they did.
