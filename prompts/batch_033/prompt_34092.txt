Please write a short story that explores the repugnant conclusion in population axiology. The story should center on a character or institution faced with a concrete choice between two possible futures: one in which a moderate number of beings lead genuinely flourishing lives, and another in which an astronomically larger number of beings lead lives that are only barely worth living—lives of mild contentment punctuated by quiet dissatisfaction, yet still preferable to nonexistence. The story need not argue for a resolution to the philosophical puzzle; instead, it should dramatize the moral vertigo of realizing that a widely held principle of impartial beneficence—that we ought to bring about the world with the greatest total sum of well-being—might demand choosing the second future over the first.
