I've been teaching an undergraduate seminar on irrationality and the emotions, and last week we spent two sessions on self-deception. The students split roughly into three camps: those who think the so-called paradox of self-deception is genuine and probably unsolvable, so self-deception in the strict sense simply doesn't occur; those who think there is a genuine puzzle but that partitioned-mind or deflationary accounts resolve it; and those who regard the whole thing as a conceptual confusion and believe we should set it aside in favor of studying the empirical psychology of motivated reasoning directly. I found myself unable to settle on a view with much confidence, and I'd like to use your answer as a kind of benchmark when I teach the material again next year.

So here is what I would like from you. Please give me your credence — a number between 0 and 1 — that self-deception, understood as an intentional project in which a person deliberately brings themselves to believe something they currently know or confidently believe to be false, is psychologically possible in ordinary adult human beings. But I do not just want the number: I want the decomposition. What are the key sub-questions your credence rides on? For each, give a rough probability and a sentence or two of justification. Then show how they combine to yield your final number. Finally, tell me which sub-question your credence is most sensitive to — that is, which one, if you changed your mind about it, would shift your final number the most, and what kind of evidence or argument could change your mind about it.

To be clear about what I am asking: I am not asking whether people engage in motivated reasoning, confirmation bias, or wishful thinking. Those are plainly real. The question is specifically about the strong, paradox-generating sense of self-deception — the case where someone knows P, wants not-P to be true, and somehow manages to bring themselves to sincerely believe not-P, in something like the way you might deceive another person but with yourself as the target. If you think that description is incoherent and no possible psychological process could realize it, your credence should be near zero; if you think it happens routinely, near one. And if you think the description needs to be refined before the question has a determinate answer, tell me how you would refine it and give your credence for the refined version.
