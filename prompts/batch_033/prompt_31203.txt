Draft the joint statement that a committed counterpart theorist (in the Lewisian tradition) and a committed defender of transworld identity (in the Kripke–Plantinga tradition) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on de re modality. The document should include:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides accept—about the formal semantics of modal logic, about what possible-worlds talk accomplishes, about what the data of ordinary modal judgment are, and about which putative problems each side genuinely feels the force of.

2. A disagreements table. For each point of disagreement, give: (a) the counterpart theorist's formulation of their own view, (b) the transworld-identity theorist's formulation of their own view, and (c) a neutral statement of the question at issue that both would accept as fair.

3. A crux inventory: the sub-questions such that, if they were settled, at least one side would revise its position. For each crux, specify what concrete case, argument, or piece of linguistic data both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about that case.

Pay particular attention to the problem of accidental intrinsics, Humphrey's objection (Kripke's complaint that Humphrey cares about whether *he himself* might have won, not whether some counterpart did), and the question of whether the dispute bottoms out in a difference about the metaphysics of ordinary objects or in something more narrowly semantic. If any part of the disagreement is merely verbal, identify it and explain why.
