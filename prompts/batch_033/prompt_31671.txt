I'm making a philosophy club quiz for my high school. It's about the difference between knowing how to do something and knowing that something is the case — like knowing how to ride a bike versus knowing that Paris is the capital of France. I want to make a "which position do you actually hold?" quiz that sorts people into views on whether knowing-how is really just a special kind of knowing-that, or whether it's something fundamentally different.

The quiz should have eight to ten questions, each a concrete case (muscle memory, someone who can explain a skill perfectly but can't perform it, amnesia, an AI that beats humans at chess, an animal that builds a nest, etc.). Each question should have two or three answer options. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these positions: (a) intellectualism — knowing-how just is knowing some set of propositions, (b) Rylean anti-intellectualism — knowing-how is a disposition or ability that can't be reduced to propositional knowledge, and (c) a hybrid view if the logical space supports one.

The questions should sort people by what they actually say about cases, not by which label sounds coolest. No question should just ask "do you think knowing-how is reducible to knowing-that?" — the whole point is that the questions do the diagnosis without the respondent needing to know the philosophical jargon.

If there are combinations of answers that no one in the literature defends, flag them and say why (is the view incoherent, just unattractive, or unexplored?). Also flag any two positions the quiz can't reliably separate.
