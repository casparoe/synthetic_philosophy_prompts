Consider a philosopher who has spent years developing and defending a minority position in a contested area of philosophy. Judged from the inside, the arguments strike her as decisive, and she assigns the position a credence of 0.8. She also accepts, as an outside-view matter, that only about one in five similarly situated minority positions is eventually vindicated. What is your credence that her epistemically rational all-things-considered credence should fall below 0.5 — that is, that the outside view should dominate? Decompose your estimate into at least these components: (a) the probability that first-person philosophical reasoning is truth-tracking enough to justify a large departure from the one-in-five base rate; (b) the probability that philosophy differs from ordinary forecasting in ways that make the outside view substantially less applicable; and (c) the probability that there is a defensible first-person/third-person asymmetry allowing her rationally to keep a high credence for herself while expecting most similarly situated others to be wrong. Give a number for each component, explain how you combined them, and identify the component to which your final credence is most sensitive and what evidence would move it most.
