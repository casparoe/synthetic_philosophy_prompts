I work in formal semantics and keep encountering theories of implicature — neo-Gricean, relevance-theoretic, grammatical — that each claim to account for the data better than the others. Before I evaluate any of them, I want the specification they should all be measured against. What must an adequate theory of conversational implicature do? Give me the desiderata with the motivating case or consideration behind each. Include at least: the standard diagnostics (calculability, cancelability, non-detachability, reinforceability), the distinctions a theory must draw (conventional versus conversational, particularized versus generalized, what is said versus what is implicated), the embedding facts (why implicatures seem to disappear under negation, in antecedents of conditionals, and in downward-entailing environments more generally), and the acquisition and processing data that any theory must now reckon with. For each desideratum, indicate whether it is non-negotiable or whether a theory could plausibly argue that it is not a genuine requirement. Finally, flag any subsets of the desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable — if, for instance, a theory that respects the embedding data must give up on cancelability as a diagnostic, say so. My goal is to see whether the desiderata themselves force a theoretical choice, or whether there is a coherent position that satisfies all of them.
