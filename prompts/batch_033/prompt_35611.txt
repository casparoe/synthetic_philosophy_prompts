I've been reading debates about whether what we see is shaped by what we believe, and I keep noticing a recurring argumentative impasse that I don't have a name for. One side offers an example: a radiologist looking at a scan immediately sees a tumor that a novice would miss, or an experienced birder spots a warbler in a thicket where I would see only leaves. The claim is that what you know changes what you literally see. The skeptic replies: no, both parties have the same visual experience — the expert simply knows what to do with it; the difference is in judgment or interpretation, downstream of perception. The first side then says: but how could you possibly tell those apart from the inside? And the debate stalls there, with both sides appealing to the same examples but describing them in incompatible ways.

I've seen this exact shape in arguments about the Müller-Lyer illusion (knowing the lines are equal doesn't make them look equal — but does that show perception is encapsulated, or only that one particular kind of knowledge fails to penetrate?), about the duck-rabbit (does flipping between interpretations count as a change in perception or just in how you categorize what you see?), and about the infamous dress photograph from a few years ago. Each time, the disagreement seems to turn on whether there is a principled way to distinguish a change in perceptual experience itself from a change in how you judge or label that experience — and whether the examples being offered actually demonstrate one rather than the other.

Is there an established term for this specific argumentative impasse? I'm aware of the broader debate about whether perception is theory-laden or cognitively penetrable, but what I'm asking about is narrower: the recurring move where one side offers a phenomenon as evidence for penetration, the other side redescribes it as post-perceptual, and then the first side challenges the very distinction between the two. Does this cycle have a name, and more importantly, are there accepted criteria for breaking the stalemate — for determining whether a given example really shows cognitive effects on perception versus on judgment? If there is no established name for the impasse itself, how would you characterize it and what would you call the move?
