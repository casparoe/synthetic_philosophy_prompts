I've been going to this philosophy club at school and we've been stuck on accuracy-first epistemology for weeks now. The basic idea as I understand it is that you can score how close a set of beliefs or credences is to the truth—kind of like a weather forecaster getting a Brier score—and then say that rational rules (dont have contradictory beliefs, update on evidence, be coherent, etc.) are rational because they improve expected accuracy. I think that's neat, but a freind keeps saying it's too narrow: understanding, coherence, explanatory power, maybe even intellectual humility, seem epistemically good in a way that isnt just "they get you more true beliefs." Like, two people could have exactly the same number of true beliefs and equally accurate credences, but one has a tight explanation of how a bunch of facts hang together and the other just has scattered trivia. The accuracy-firster has to say theyre epistemically equal, and my friend thinks that's obviously wrong.

Have them actually respond to each other's points instead of talking past each other. Include a moment where they discuss whether strictly proper scoring rules and accuracy-dominance arguments just assume veritism from the start, and whether the pluralist's extra values can be shown to be good because they eventually improve accuracy. Set it at a study session, maybe with a whiteboard. Dont resolve it—leave the disagreement sharp. I want to show my club that both sides are smarter than we thought.
