my school has a philosophy club and this month we're doing a project where we write short stories based on some weird idea instead of essays, which is honestly way better. i got assigned origin essentialism — basically the question of whether you could have come from a different sperm and egg. like, if your parents had conceived you on a different night, a different sperm wouldve won, and the kid would have different genes and maybe a different personality, but it would still be their kid and they might even give it the same name. is that person you? most philosophers (i think, from what our teacher said) say no: your particular origin is essential to being you, so that kid would be your sibling, not you, and you just never would have existed. others say thats overblown and what matters is something about your life or your parents or whatever.

could you write a short story that gets at this? i want the main character to be a teenager who gets obsessed with the idea after learning about meiosis in bio class. they find out their parents actually did IVF and there was another embryo made at the same time that got frozen and later donated, so somewhere out there is a kid who is their full genetic sibling, just born years later. the character keeps having this half-dream where they meet the person who would have existed if the clinic had transfered the other embryo that day — same room, same parents, same name, but it's not them. the story should make the reader really feel why someone would say 'that's not me, i was never born in that timeline' without turning into a lecture. also if you think the 'no' answer is wrong, feel free to lean the story the other way, but pick a side and commit.
