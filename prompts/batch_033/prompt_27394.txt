I've been chewing on something for a while now, and I'd like to know whether it has a name in philosophy or statistics or wherever it lives. I don't have any formal training in this area — I work in logistics — but I read a fair amount on my own, and this puzzle keeps showing up in my thinking.

Here it is. A few months ago, my wife and I were trying to decide whether to send our daughter to a particular summer program. We weren't sure whether she'd get in — it's competitive — but we were also unsure whether the program was even worth the cost if she did get in. A friend who knows the program well told us, "If she gets in, the science track is much stronger than the arts track." That's a conditional: it tells us something about the world under the assumption that she gets in. And here's what I noticed: after hearing it, I felt slightly more confident that she would get in. Not because the friend said anything about her chances — the statement was purely about what the program is like conditional on admission. But the fact that the friend was willing to state the conditional, to engage with the "she gets in" scenario at all, seemed to carry some information. At the same time, I couldn't articulate why. If I try to be strict about it, the friend told me P(strong science | admission) is high, and that shouldn't touch P(admission). And yet.

I started noticing the same structure everywhere. A doctor says, "If this is bacterial, the antibiotic will clear it up in three days." A financial advisor says, "If interest rates rise next quarter, your adjustable-rate mortgage payment will go up by about two hundred dollars." A mechanic says, "If the noise is coming from the transmission, the repair is going to be expensive." In every case, I'm given information about what follows from some uncertain proposition, and I find myself unsure whether I should also be adjusting my belief in the proposition itself. Sometimes it feels like I should — the fact that someone bothered to state the conditional seems like evidence that the antecedent is live. Other times it feels like a mistake, like I'm double-counting or letting the salience of a scenario infect my probability judgment.

I've tried to work this out on my own with Bayes' rule, but I keep going in circles. The standard machinery tells me how to update on learning that some event occurred. It doesn't tell me how to update on learning that a conditional probability has a particular value. So my questions are: does this problem have an established name? Has it been carved up clearly — what exactly the puzzle is, what the competing solutions are, where they disagree at the edges? And is there a consensus about the right way to handle it, or is it still an open question? I'd appreciate an answer in American English, and I'm less interested in the label itself than in understanding the shape of the problem and the main approaches people have taken to it.
