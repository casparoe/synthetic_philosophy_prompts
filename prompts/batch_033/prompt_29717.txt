I am a high school student, and I have been arguing with a friend about an incident on a Minecraft server we both use. One player spent roughly a hundred hours building a large structure; another player, who knew that the server rules forbid griefing, destroyed it and took the rare materials. The server could in principle be restored from a backup taken the day before, and the player who lost the build was upset but has since rebuilt a smaller version. My friend says the victim suffered a real harm, of the same general kind as having a physical possession vandalised, just much smaller. I am unsure: nothing in the physical world was damaged, and the loss was, in some sense, reversible.

Please give me your actual credence — a number between 0 and 1 — for the following proposition: the victim suffered a genuine prudential harm, meaning a real setback to their well-being, of the same kind as (though far smaller than) the harm of having a physical possession vandalised, and not merely a breach of the server rules or an unpleasant experience. I do not want 'it depends on your theory of harm'; I want your all-things-considered number, and then the decomposition that shows where the theoretical dependence enters.

Please decompose the number into at least these terms, each with a rough probability and a sentence or two of justification:

1. That virtual objects, such as a Minecraft build, can be genuine bearers of value or legitimate property, rather than patterns whose value is only a matter of emotional attachment.
2. That the correct account of harm counts the destruction of such an object as a prudential harm in its own right, not merely because the victim felt upset, and even when the loss is in principle reversible from a backup, conditional on the first term being true.
3. That the context — the server's explicit rules, the builder's reasonable expectation of persistence, and the griefer's knowledge of both — is enough for the act to count as a harm rather than an accepted risk of playing the game.

Show how these terms combine to yield your final number. Then tell me which single term your credence is most sensitive to: if you had to change one of the three probabilities, which change would move the final number most? My own credence is around 0.5 and my friend's is around 0.9; say where you think the disagreement between us most likely lives, in terms of the decomposition.

Finally, tell me what would change your mind: which argument, additional fact about the case, or philosophical consideration would most shift your credence, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. Please write the whole answer in British English.
