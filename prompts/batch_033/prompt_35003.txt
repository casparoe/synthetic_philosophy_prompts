I'm a team lead at a small startup (about 12 people) and we've been going back and forth for weeks on how to make decisions. We want to finally write down a formal process — like, when do we vote, when does the lead (me) just decide, when do we need full consensus, and what counts as a veto. But here's the snag: we don't have a process for deciding on the process. If I just say "here's the process, I decided it," that's me unilaterally imposing something, which is exactly the kind of thing we might want to rule out. If we vote on which process to adopt, what voting threshold do we use? We haven't agreed on that yet either — and if I pick the threshold, we're back to unilateral imposition. My co-founder laughed and said we need a meta-process, and then a meta-meta-process, and it's turtles all the way down. He was joking, but I've been thinking about it and it actually seems like there's something genuinely weird going on — like we're trying to pull ourselves up by our own bootstraps. Is there a principled way out of this, or is there something fundamentally broken about trying to design a decision-making system from within the system itself? I feel like this can't be a new problem but I dont know where to even start thinking about it.
