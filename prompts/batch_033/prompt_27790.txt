I keep coming back to Andy Weir's short story "The Egg" (and the Kurzgesagt video of it), and I'm trying to pin down what it's actually doing. I know Schrödinger had this whole Vedanta-inspired view that the apparent seperateness of individual minds is an illusion, and that underneath there's really only one consciousness looking out through all the eyes at once. The story looks like it dramatizes that: a guy dies, meets God, and learns he's already been every human being who ever lived, and once he's lived every one of those lives he'll be ready to become God himself. But the more I sit with it, the more it seems like the story quietly swaps Schrödinger's view for something else. As I understand Schrödinger, the claim isn't that one person sequentially reincarnates through all of history, getting morally wiser with each pass; it's that all the apparently separate centers of experience are already the very same subject, all at once, so the separation is the illusion, not the unity. "The Egg" turns that into a story about succession and maturation, where the point of all those lives is for the protagonist to grow up into God. So I want a careful reading of "The Egg" through Schrödinger's Vedantic unity-of-consciousness idea: where does the story genuinely capture the view, where does it replace it with something closer to a single soul running through a curriculum, and does that replacement maybe reveal something the philosophical view can't easily say in narrative form? I'd like a reading that takes both the story and Schrödinger seriously, and is willing to say where each one resists the other, not just a "the story got the philosophy wrong" verdict.
