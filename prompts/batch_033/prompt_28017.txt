I'm preparing a seminar on Orwell's views about language and political thought, and I want to read *Nineteen Eighty-Four* against the arguments Orwell makes in his essay 'Politics and the English Language'. The essay claims, roughly, that linguistic decay and political decay form a feedback loop: slovenly language makes clear thought impossible, and unclear thought enables bad politics. But the novel seems to tell a different causal story: the Party deliberately engineers Newspeak as an instrument of domination, and the narrowing of language follows from political power rather than the other way round. I'd like a careful reading that maps this difference. Where does the novel dramatise the essay's thesis, where does it complicate or reverse it, and where does it show something neither account quite captures — for instance, in Winston's attempts to think against the grain of a language that is being systematically pruned? Does the novel, taken as a whole, vindicate the essay, undermine it, or simply operate at a different level of analysis? And are there scenes or details — Syme's enthusiasm for the dictionary, the appendix on the principles of Newspeak, the fragments of doggerel verse Winston half-remembers — that matter to this question in ways they are rarely asked to bear?
