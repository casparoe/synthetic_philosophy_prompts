I've been going down a rabbit hole on Pascal's mugging and I think I'm missing something. The way I understand it: a mugger says "give me your wallet or I'll torture an astronomically huge number of people" and since the expected disutility is enormous even with a tiny probability, expected utility theory says you should comply. But that's obviously the wrong answer. So doesn't this just show that expected utility maximization is a bad norm? Like, if a decision theory can be hijacked by anyone who can utter a big number, why would anyone in EA or longtermism still use expected value reasoning as their core framework? It seems like the mugging is a reductio of the whole approach, not just a quirkly edge case.
