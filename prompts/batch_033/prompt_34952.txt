I've been reading the epistemology of echo chambers and epistemic bubbles for a couple years now and I feel like the whole conversation has hit a wall. Everyone agrees on the Nguyen distinction — bubbles are about missing information, chambers are about active distrust of outside sources — and everyone agrees both are bad for knowledge. But then the debate just fragments. Some people say the distinction collapses under pressure because a well-functioning chamber ends up looking like a bubble (you're just missing the information that would correct your distrust). Others say the distinction is fine conceptually but the interesting question is about remedies, and there the literature gives contradictory advice: breaking a bubble just requires exposure to diverse sources, but breaking a chamber requires trust repair, and no one agrees on what trust repair actually means in a mediatized environment. And then there's a third group saying the whole framing is outdated because it assumes a pre-platform epistemic landscape where "inside" and "outside" are stable, whereas on TikTok or YouTube the algorithm is constantly reconfiguring who's inside and who's out, so the distinction can't even get a grip.

I'm starting to think this isn't one debate but three debates sharing a vocabulary, and the appearance of a single stalled conversation is an illusion. Is that right? If so, what are the three debates actually about, and do any of them have a path forward? And if I'm wrong and it's one debate, what kind of disagreement is it — verbal, factual, about starting intuitions, about what counts as a solution? Either way, give me a diagnosis, not a literature review. I've already read the literature; I want to know what's structually keeping it stuck.
