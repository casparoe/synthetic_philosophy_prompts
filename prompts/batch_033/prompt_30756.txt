I am putting together the slurs unit for an advanced graduate seminar in the philosophy of language, and I want the centerpiece to be a single comparative exercise rather than a survey. The students will already have worked through the core data: the way a slur's derogatory force seems to survive embedding under negation, questions, conditionals, and modals; the way a speaker can reject the descriptive claim — for example, He is not a kraut, he is Belgian — while the offense remains; the wide variation in intensity across different slurs and different targets; the existence of reclaimed, affiliative, or otherwise non-hostile in-group uses; and the felt difference between using a slur and merely mentioning or quoting it.

Assume the five main competing approaches are: (a) truth-conditional content accounts, on which the slur contributes a negative evaluation of the target group to what is said; (b) presuppositional accounts, on which the derogation is a presupposition that projects; (c) expressivist accounts, on which the slur conventionally expresses contempt or endorsement of a derogating perspective; (d) prohibitionist or use-conditional accounts, on which the offensiveness is a matter of a conventional norm against using the word; and (e) deflationary pragmatic accounts, on which the slur is truth-conditionally just a plain group-denoting term and all derogation is supplied by speaker meaning or conversational mechanisms.

Please rank these five approaches into tiers (top, middle, bottom, with ties allowed) by overall explanatory coverage of the data above. For each approach, identify the diagnostic it handles best and the one that most strains it. Then tell me which single diagnostic should carry the most weight in the ranking and why: in particular, say whether any approach earns its tier mostly on the strength of that one diagnostic, and whether that should make us trust the ranking or discount it. I am less interested in a survey of the views than in the comparative structure — where the real fault lines lie, and which data a defender of each approach would most want to explain away.
