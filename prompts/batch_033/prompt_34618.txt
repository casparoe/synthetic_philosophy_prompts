here are the cases and my gut reactions:

**1. classic money pump.** agent has cyclic preferences: a ≻ b ≻ c ≻ a. i offer them b for a plus a penny, they accept. then c for b plus a penny, they accept. then a for c plus a penny, they accept. they're back where they started, three pence poorer. my verdict: this agent is irrational. the preferences themselves are the problem, not just that they got taken advantage of.

**2. pump that never fires.** same agent, same cyclic preferences, but no one ever actually offers them the trades. they just sit there with a ≻ b ≻ c ≻ a. my verdict: still irrational. the irrationality is in the preference structure, not in getting exploited. being lucky enough that no one pumps you doesn't make your preferences okay.

**3. the resolute agent.** same cyclic preferences, but the agent foresees the pump coming and adopts a plan: they'll stick with a and refuse all trades. they execute the plan perfectly and never budge. my verdict: rational. they recognised a flaw in their own preferences and acted to avoid being exploited by it. that seems like exactly what a rational agent should do.

**4. shifting preferences (no synchronic cycle).** agent prefers a to b on monday, b to c on tuesday, c to a on wednesday. at no single time do they have a cyclic preference ranking — each day's preferences are perfectly transitive. but across the week, if you offered them the right trades on the right days, you could still pump them. my verdict: not irrational, or at least not in the same way. there's nothing wrong with changing your mind across time.

**5. sophisticated refuser.** agent has cyclic preferences, is offered the first trade (b for a plus a penny), and refuses because they can see the whole sequence coming. they don't adopt a plan in advance like the resolute agent; they just reason through each choice as it comes, anticipate where it leads, and stop. my verdict: rational. maybe slightly less praiseworthy than the resolute agent? i'm not sure, but definitely not irrational.

**6. zero-fee cycle.** agent has a ≻ b ≻ c ≻ a and is offered each trade for free — no money, no cost. they cycle a → b → c → a → b → ... endlessly. my verdict: irrational. the fact that they're not losing money doesn't make it fine to have no stable endpoint. something is still wrong with them.

**7. incommensurable goods.** agent prefers a beethoven symphony to a mozart symphony, the mozart to a haydn, but the haydn to the beethoven. these aren't commodities you can trade; they're aesthetic experiences the agent can only have one of on a given evening. no one is pumping them; they just have these pairwise preferences. my verdict: i want to say this isn't irrational, or at least it's a different kind of thing from the money cases. but i can't articulate why, and i'm worried i'm just making a special exception because i like the example.
