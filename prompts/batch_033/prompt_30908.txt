I am a PhD student in political theory drafting a qualifying paper on the boundary problem: the question of who, prior to any democratic procedure, properly constitutes the demos whose collective decisions are legitimate. I want the specification before I evaluate the usual proposals. Write the desiderata that any adequate account of the demos must satisfy, where adequacy means able to guide and appraise actually existing democratic institutions rather than merely ideal theory. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it binding, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp (cosmopolitan, statist, all-subjected, all-affected, and so on). I especially need a clear treatment of five points. First, non-circularity: a principle for constituting the demos cannot presuppose a demos already empowered to constitute itself, yet an externally imposed standard seems to violate the democratic idea that a people settles its own boundaries — so are these two desiderata jointly satisfiable, and if not, which one gives way? Second, extensional adequacy across the hard cases: long-term resident noncitizens, emigrants, future persons affected by present policies, children, persons with severe cognitive disabilities, and outsiders subject to a state's border or climate coercion. Third, the distinctions between being affected, being subjected, being coerced, and having a stake — which of these, if any, should inclusion track, and does inclusion come in degrees? Fourth, non-gerrymanderability: the criterion must not allow boundaries to be drawn so as to predetermine outcomes. Fifth, identify the single requirement that does the most work to exclude the leading candidate principles — all-affected interests, all-subjected persons, the coercion principle, the stakeholder principle, and the view that the demos is simply given by existing states — and then give a brief scorecard of those principles against the full list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scorecard.
