I'm a PhD candidate in philosophy of science putting together the position statement for my dissertation proposal defense, and I'd like an outside audit of my views before I have to defend them in front of my committee. I work on the interpretation of probability, and I've ended up with the following eight commitments. (1) I'm a Bayesian about rational credence: degrees of belief should be precise, satisfy the probability calculus, and update by conditionalization. (2) I'm a subjectivist about priors: any coherent prior is rationally permissible, and there are no further constraints on an agent's initial degrees of belief. (3) I think probability statements about coins, atoms, and laboratory setups are best interpreted as objective single-case propensities — mind-independent tendencies of a particular token arrangement to produce a given outcome on a particular trial. (4) I think long-run frequencies are evidence about propensities, not what probability is; a coin would still have its one-half propensity if it were never tossed. (5) I think deterministic systems can have well-defined, nontrivial probabilities — statistical mechanics is my central example — and I don't think these probabilities are merely epistemic measures of our ignorance. (6) I take quantum mechanics to provide the clearest case of objective chance, with the Born rule giving single-case probabilities for measurement outcomes. (7) I don't think there is any objective chance of a scientific theory being true; the credences I assign to theories are subjective degrees of support, not estimates of some hidden chance. (8) I accept the standard chance–credence norm: when you know the objective chance of an outcome, your rational credence in that outcome should equal that chance.

Please act as an expert in the philosophy of probability and formal epistemology, and think carefully before you diagnose anything. I'd like you to start by mapping out what each commitment commits me to and which of them interact, then go through the set and tell me whether it hangs together. For every pair or cluster that is in tension, rank the tensions by severity, say exactly what has to give, and describe the cheapest repair and what it costs. Be sure to distinguish real tensions from apparent ones that dissolve once the relevant terms are kept straight — I don't want to overcorrect. Finally, tell me which single revision would do the most to make the overall position coherent, and what accepting it would cost me.
