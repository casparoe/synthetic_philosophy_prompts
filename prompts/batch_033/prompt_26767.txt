I am taking a course this term on the semantics of conditionals, and I have been keeping a list of the verdicts our professor has given on particular examples. I would like to work out what theory she is actually running, because her examples look carefully chosen and I suspect they are meant to eliminate some of the views we read at the beginning of the course.

Here are the cases, with the background facts as she stated them:

(1) Before we learned who sent a letter to the dean's office, but while we knew that the letter had in fact been sent, she called "If Alice didn't send the letter, someone else did" true.

(2) After we learned that Alice was in fact the sender, and that no one else was prepared to send the letter, she called "If Alice hadn't sent the letter, someone else would have" false.

(3) The sprinkler system was off all evening, and no waterproof tarp was on the lawn. She called "If the sprinklers had been on, the lawn would have been wet" true.

(4) Same sprinkler system, same evening. She called "If the sprinklers had been on and a waterproof tarp had covered the lawn, the lawn would have been wet" false.

She has now told us that next week she will discuss a fair coin that sat untouched on her desk all afternoon and that could only land heads or tails if tossed. I want to predict what she will say about:

(i) "If the coin had been tossed, it would have landed heads."
(ii) "If the coin had been tossed, it would not have landed heads."
(iii) "Either if the coin had been tossed it would have landed heads, or if the coin had been tossed it would not have landed heads."

My questions are these. What verdict should I predict for each of (i) through (iii), and how confident should I be? Which theory or family of theories best fits her four earlier verdicts? In particular, do (1) through (4) rule out the material conditional and the strict conditional, and if so, which cases do the work? And if the coin case leaves two theories still in play — say, a Stalnaker-style selection-function semantics and a Lewis-style variably strict semantics for counterfactuals — which further example would cleanly separate them? I lean toward a unified truth-conditional account because she freely calls indicative conditionals true or false, but I am not sure how much weight that should carry.
