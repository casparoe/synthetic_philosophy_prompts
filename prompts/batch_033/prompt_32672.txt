Write a dialogue between a proponent of the all-affected principle of democratic inclusion and a proponent of the all-subjected principle, in which they work through the implications of their views for a concrete case: the decision by a wealthy state to set carbon emission targets that will affect the global climate. Each should press the other on the hardest cases for their own view — for instance, the all-affected theorist must explain why distant strangers whose weather patterns shift slightly should have a say in domestic energy policy, and the all-subjected theorist must explain why the circle of the subjected is not itself a product of the very political decisions whose legitimacy is at stake. The dialogue should not resolve into a tidy compromise, but each character should refine their position in response to the other's challenges.
