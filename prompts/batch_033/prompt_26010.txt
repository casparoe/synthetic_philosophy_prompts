I've been reading Popper and I'm struggling with what seems like a destructive implication of falsificationism. As I understand it, Popper argues that scientific theories can never be verified or confirmed inductively; they can only be falsified. But if that's right, then at any given moment, every theory that has not yet been falsified appears to have exactly the same epistemic status: it's an unfalsified conjecture. There would seem to be no principled way to say that well-tested theories like general relativity are better supported, or more rational to believe, than unfalsified claims made by astrology or parapsychology. Doesn't falsificationism therefore collapse into a kind of radical skepticism, where the history of science is just a graveyard of refuted guesses and the survivors are all equally baseless? If Popper has a way around this, I'm not seeing it, and I'd appreciate help understanding where my reasoning goes wrong. In your response, please separate out what is genuinely contested among philosophers of science from what is settled or widely agreed upon.
