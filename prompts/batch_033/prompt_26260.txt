I'm a journalist covering cognitive science and philosophy of mind, and I recently came across a popular science article that offered the following summary of Daniel Dennett's view of the self:

"Dennett argues that the self is an illusion — there is no real thing inside us that is the 'self.' Just as a center of gravity isn't a physical object but a mathematical point we find useful for calculations, the self is merely a convenient story our brains tell to make sense of the stream of experiences. It's a socially constructed narrative we spin about ourselves, and different cultures construct different kinds of selves. So the idea that each of us has a unified, persisting self that is the author of our actions is, according to Dennett, a pre-scientific myth we should abandon."

I know Dennett was a careful and nuanced philosopher, and I suspect this summary, while engaging, may flatten or distort his position in ways that matter. Could you go through it and tell me which parts a scholar familiar with Dennett's work would accept as fair, which parts would make them wince, and whether the overall conclusion the article draws — that we should abandon the idea of a persisting self — follows from Dennett's actual view?
