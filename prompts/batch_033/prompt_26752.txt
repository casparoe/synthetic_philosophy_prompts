I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology, and I've been working through the literature on imprecise probabilities. Write a dialogue between two equally sophisticated researchers at a conference — one who holds that rational credences are always sharp (point-valued probabilities), and one who holds that imprecise credences, represented by sets of probability functions, are sometimes rationally required, not merely permitted. The dialogue should be a genuine back-and-forth: each character should respond to the other's specific arguments rather than delivering monologues, and neither side should be a strawman. The conversation should engage, at some point, with the problem of dilation, with the challenge of making decisions when one's credal state is imprecise, and with the charge that insisting on sharp credences in the face of sparse or ambiguous evidence amounts to demanding arbitrary precision.
