A couple weeks ago I was at a friend's birthday party and ended up cornered by two people who'd gotten into a thing about the Sleeping Beauty problem. I'd read the Wikipedia page once so I knew the basic setup — coin flip Sunday night, waking Monday for sure, waking Tuesday only if tails, amnesia each time so she never knows what day it is — but I'd never seen people actually go at it in person.

The first guy, call him Dan, was a thirder: when Beauty wakes up she should be 1/3 sure the coin was heads. The second, Mira, was a halfer: 1/2. Dan's opening move was the betting argument: if Beauty bets a dollar on heads at even odds every time she wakes, she loses money in the long run, so 1/2 can't be right. Mira said that's a decision-theoretic point, not a pure credence point — the fact that her bets are counted twice on tails means the optimal betting odds and the correct credence come apart here. Dan said that's a dodge: if you're genuinely 50-50 on heads, you should be willing to bet at 1:1 odds, period. Mira said no, because you know the setup makes your bets asymmetric — you'll be offered the bet twice when you're wrong, and rational betting already has to account for that without changing your credence.

Then Mira brought up the "no new information" point: Beauty knew on Sunday she'd be woken, so waking up teaches her nothing she didn't already know, so her credence should stay at 1/2. Dan said she does learn something: she learns that "today is an awakening day" — which is true with probability 1 on heads and 2/3 on tails, if we think of it as randomly sampling one of the three possible awakenings. Mira said that's exactly what's at issue: you can't just assume the three awakenings are equiprobably for the agent, that's the whole question. Dan said fine, but then consider the variant where tails means a million awakenings — are you really going to tell me Beauty should still be 1/2 on heads when she wakes up, knowing that if it's tails she's having one of a million indistinguishable experiences? Mira said yes, and that's the bullet she bites, and it's not crazy: the coin was fair, and nothing she experiences distinguishes the million-awakening world from the one-awakening world in a way that licenses a shift away from 1/2.

At that point I had to leave, but I've been turning it over since. Can you go through their exchange and adjudicate move by move? Who's right about what, which arguments land and which don't, and is there anything important both of them are missing?
