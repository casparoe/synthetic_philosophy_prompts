I've noticed something about my own thinking that I'm trying to get clearer on, and I'm wondering whether there is an established name for the hypothesis I keep circling toward.

When I'm working through a difficult problem, I often have the experience of grasping a thought before I can put it into words. The thought has a kind of internal coherence and structure, and I can tell whether a particular sentence captures it well or badly, but the thought itself does not seem to be in English, or in any natural language. I will sometimes try out several different phrasings until one clicks, and when it does, I recognize it as what I already had in mind. The same thing happens when I read a sentence that articulates something I had been turning over inarticulately: there is a shock of recognition, as if the words matched a thought that was already there, fully formed but wordless.

I know that some people report that they think entirely in inner speech — a more or less constant internal monologue in their native language. That is not quite what I am describing here, or at least it does not feel like the whole story. What I am after is the idea that there might be a representational system underlying both that kind of inner speech and my own more wordless style of thinking — something more fundamental than natural language, in which thoughts are actually composed, and which natural language translates rather than constitutes. Is there a name for this hypothesis? And, if there is, how exactly does it relate to the claim that some people think in inner speech: are these competing accounts of the same phenomenon, or are they about different levels of explanation? I am less interested in a bare label than in understanding where the boundaries fall between these ideas and what each is actually committed to.
