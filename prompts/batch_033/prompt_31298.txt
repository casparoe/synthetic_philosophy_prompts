"Compatibilists and incompatibilists disagree about whether free will is compatible with determinism, which is the idea that everything that happens is the inevitable result of prior causes. Incompatibilists say that if determinism is true, nobody ever could have done otherwise than they actually did, so there's no free will and no moral responsibility. Compatibilists respond by redefining free will: they say free will just means acting on your own desires without external coercion. So for a compatibilist, even if your desires themselves are determined, you're free as long as nobody is holding a gun to your head. This compatibilist move is basically a semantic trick — they've changed the subject from what people actually mean by free will to something weaker. And most people's pre-theoretical intuition is firmly incompatibilist, which is why experimental philosophy studies consistently show that ordinary folks reject compatibilism. So incompatibilism is clearly the default view, and compatibilism is a revisionary position that needs to do a lot of work to justify itself."
