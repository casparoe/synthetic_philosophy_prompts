I'm giving the toast at my daughter's wedding this Saturday. She is a research geologist — rigorous, evidence-minded, someone who was raised to mistrust any claim that cannot be measured. Her new husband is a committed Catholic for whom faith is not a compartment of life but the lens through which he sees everything. Over the past year, watching them navigate their differences, I found myself reading about the old dispute between William James and W.K. Clifford — whether it is ever permissible to believe something on insufficient evidence. I have no academic background; I run a hardware store. But the question has become personal to me. I want the toast to honor both of them, and both families, without pretending the tension is not real. I want it to say something honest about what it means to vow yourself to another person when you cannot possibly have adequate evidence that it will all work out. Please write the toast — roughly three minutes spoken — and keep it from sounding like a philosophy seminar. Half the room will understand every reference; the other half just needs to feel the warmth.
