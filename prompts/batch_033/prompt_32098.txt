I've been working through Montaigne's "Of Cannibals" for a seminar on early modern philosophy and it's really messed with my head. The professor keeps pushing us to not just exegete the text but to figure out what we actually believe, and I've been trying to write down my own position and I'm worried it doesn't hang together. Here's what I've got, as best I can articulate it:

(1) Montaigne was basically right that we reflexively call "barbarous" whatever diverges from our own customs, and that this tells us more about us than about them. The essay's descriptive psychology holds up.

(2) But I don't think this means we have to be total relativists. I think there are some practices—clitoridectomy, say, or honor killings—that are genuinely wrong, not just wrong-relative-to-my-culture. I'm not willing to say "well, that's just their culture" about everything.

(3) I also think moral progress is real. The abolition of slavery wasn't just a change in fashion or a shift in power; it was an actual improvement. We're not just different from our ancestors; we're better in some respects.

(4) At the same time, I don't think there's any neutral, culture-independent standpoint from which to judge. Any judgment we make comes from somewhere, shaped by a particular history and set of values. There's no view from nowhere.

(5) Montaigne's response to the Brazilian cannibals was basically the right one: try to understand before judging, and recognize that the supposedly civilized Europeans were doing things—torture, religious persecution, colonial violence—that were at least as bad. The essay's normative stance is the right one.

(6) I'm suspicious of anyone who claims to have found the universal moral truth, especially when it conveniently aligns with their own culture's values. If your universal morality just happens to tell you to do what your culture already does, I'm skeptical.

I feel like these all sound right to me individually, but when I put them next to each other I get a headache. (2) and (4) seem to be in the most obvious tension—how can I say some things are genuinely wrong if there's no neutral standpoint? But maybe I'm wrong about which ones are the real problem. Maybe (3) and (6) are the ones that can't both be true, or maybe (1) and (2) are the deep issue and I'm just not seeing it. I'd like a committed diagnosis: which of these are actually incompatible, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix? Don't just lay out all the options neutrally—tell me what you think I should revise and why, and rank the problems by how serious they are.
