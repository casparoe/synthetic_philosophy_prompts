I've been reading about consciousness on my own for about two years now — not in any formal course, just following arguments online, working through some books, and discussing them with a few friends who share the interest. One thing that has increasingly bothered me is the debate about whether conceivability is a reliable guide to genuine possibility. From what I can gather, the back-and-forth goes roughly like this: one side says that we can clearly conceive of a being physically identical to a human but lacking any conscious experience — a philosophical zombie — and that this act of conceiving shows that consciousness cannot be identical to or reducible to physical processes. The other side replies that conceivability is an unreliable test: people once thought they could conceive of a scenario in which the Morning Star and the Evening Star were distinct celestial bodies, or one in which water was not composed of H₂O, but those turned out to be metaphysical impossibilities that merely seemed conceivable because the conceiver was missing relevant information. The first side then responds that there is a special kind of conceivability — call it ideal conceivability, or conceivability on full rational reflection — that does track possibility, and that the zombie scenario passes this stricter test because it involves no empirical ignorance, only a conceptual distinction between physical structure and felt experience. At this point, as far as I can tell, the argument simply loops. Neither side has shifted ground in decades, and yet both sides strike me as articulate, careful, and sincere.

What I want to know is: what kind of disagreement is this, really? Is it a substantive metaphysical disagreement that could in principle be resolved by some new argument or discovery, even if we do not yet know what it would look like? Is it a verbal dispute about what the word 'conceivable' means, where the two sides are using different standards and talking past each other? Or is it the kind of impasse where the two sides simply have different starting intuitions about what counts as evidence for metaphysical possibility, and neither can argue for those intuitions without begging the question? I have a suspicion — and I offer it only as a hypothesis — that the whole thing turns on a hidden disagreement about the relationship between imagination and modal knowledge, but I am not confident enough to press that. I would like a diagnosis of the structure of the impasse, grounded in what each side actually says about the zombie argument and the link between conceivability and possibility, and some sense of what, if anything, would count as progress. And if the honest answer is that nothing would move it, I would like to know why, and whether that tells us something about the limits of the method itself.
