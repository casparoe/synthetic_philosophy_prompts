For some time I have been reading rationalist-adjacent discussions in which the common prior assumption is treated either as an obvious coherence requirement or as an obviously question-begging modeling choice, and I would like to stop going in circles. Before I read another defense or dismissal, I want the specification: what would an adequate theory of the common prior assumption — and of when rational agents may permissibly disagree — have to deliver?

Write the desiderata. For each requirement, give the case, result, or argument that makes it a requirement, and mark it as non-negotiable or as a preference of one camp. Then identify which subsets of the desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied — I expect Aumann's agreement theorem to make at least one combination inconsistent — and say which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the standard positions. Do not bother with a scorecard or a survey of the literature; the criteria and their tension points are what I want.

Make sure the list engages at least the following pressures: the agreement result — that agents who share a prior and have common knowledge of each other's posterior probabilities cannot stably disagree — and the question of which premise an account is forced to reject; the distinction between a common prior as a normative constraint on rational belief, a modeling convention, and an empirical claim about real agents; the doctrine that differences in belief should be explained by differences in information rather than by different priors; the no-trade result and what it commits an account to saying about speculative trade; the question of whether a shared prior must also be a unique rational prior, or whether many priors are permissible provided they are shared; the fact that actual agents disagree persistently, which any account must either explain away or accommodate; and the question of whether disagreement must wash out as shared evidence accumulates. Where these pressures pull against one another — for example, preserving stable rational disagreement while retaining common knowledge of posterior probabilities — spell out the conflict explicitly, because that is the part I most want to see.
