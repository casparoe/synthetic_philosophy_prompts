I'm a grad student in epistemology, and I keep running into a fourth move in the regress debate and I can't tell whether it has a name or is just a vibe. The standard three I know: foundationalism (the chain stops at something self-justified or otherwise specially justified), coherentism (it loops back on itself), infinitism (it goes on forever and that's fine). But the people I'm reading make a different move. They say the chain doesn't stop at a justified belief and doesn't loop or trail off; it stops at a commitment that isn't the kind of thing that could be justified or unjustified, because it's part of what makes justification possible in the first place. Like the commitment that your memory is broadly trustworthy, or that the world didn't pop into existence five minutes ago with all its apparent traces — these aren't things you'd normally offer evidence for, but they aren't mere assumptions either; the demand for a reason is supposed to be out of place. Its not 'I can't give a reason for it' but 'the demand for a reason doesnt apply here.' And they're not relativists — they think this background is mostly fixed for us, not up for grabs. Is there an established name for this exact position, and how does it differ from foundationalism (which also stops the chain) and from coherentism (which also denies that justification bottoms out in a single belief)? I'd rather you commit: tell me which label you'd actually use and why, what the nearest named positions are and how this thing differs from each, and where the view stops being coherent or collapses into one of the standard three.
