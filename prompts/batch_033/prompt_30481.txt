I keep going in circles on this and I want to see two people actually fight it out. The thing is: I exist because one specific sperm got to one specific egg. If my parents had conceived an hour later, or a different sperm had won, the kid would of had a different set of genes and grown up into a different person. Is that kid me? One side of me says obviously not — that's like a sibling who never got born, and I just never would have existed. But then I think: that makes my existence so brittle that any tiny change before my conception erases me and swaps in someone else. And that feels too strict, because when I imagine my life going differntly — different job, different city, even different childhood — I still say 'that's me.' So why would the starting cells be the one thing I can't swap out? I want a dialogue between two people arguing this. One holds that a different sperm and egg means a different person, period. The other holds that this makes 'me' so fragile it's basically a useless idea, and what makes me me is the ongoing person, not the original blueprint. Both should be smart and neither should be a pushover; they need to respond to each other's actual points, not just take turns. Give me exactly five numbered exchanges, each person's turn a sentence or two, no intro and no outro.
