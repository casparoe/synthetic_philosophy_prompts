I've been following the scientific realism debate for years, and I'm struck by how little the core dispute around the pessimistic meta-induction has moved since Laudan's 1981 paper. The realist says the empirical success of our best theories would be a miracle unless those theories are approximately true. The anti-realist points to the historical record: phlogiston theory, caloric, the ether — all empirically successful by the standards of their day, all radically false by our lights. So by induction, our current successful theories are probably false too. Realists have responded with structural realism (the mathematical structure gets preserved across theory change), with selective realism (only the working parts are approximately true), and by challenging the historical cases. Anti-realists have replied to those replies. And around it goes. My working hypothesis is that this isn't really an empirical disagreement about the historical record — it's a disagreement about what the burden of proof is in an inference to the best explanation, and about whether the realist's no-miracles argument and the anti-realist's induction are even answering the same question. But I'm not confident about that diagnosis. What kind of disagreement is this, at bottom? A factual one about the history of science? A methodological one about how to weigh evidence? A semantic one about "approximate truth"? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that would actually break the impasse, or is this a debate where the two sides are fated to talk past each other indefinitely?
