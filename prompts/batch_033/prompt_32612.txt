Here's something I can't get straight. In AI alignment, people contrast "approval-directed" agents (which try to do what a human would approve of) with "goal-directed" agents (which try to bring about some fixed state of the world). But the obvious move is to say an approval-directed agent just *has* a goal — namely, earning approval — which collapses the distinction. The standard reply is that an approval-directed agent is supposed to be uncertain about what's good and treat the human's judgment as giving evidence, whereas a goal-directed agent knows what it wants and treats the human as part of the environment to be optimized. But here's where I get stuck: couldn't you build a goal-directed agent whose goal is specified in terms of the human's latent values, and give it uncertainty about what those values are? That seems to walk and talk like an approval-directed agent but be built on a goal-directed architecture. So I'm asking you to compare the two concepts and help me see whether there's a genuine architectural difference, or whether it's just a difference in emphasis and rhetoric. If the distinction is real, what does each one commit you to about the nature of human values? And if it collapses, what was doing the intuitive work that made people think it was a real distinction in the first place? American English, please.
