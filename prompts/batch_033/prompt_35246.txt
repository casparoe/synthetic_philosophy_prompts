I'm a product manager for a company that runs a voice-based customer-support assistant. It currently opens each call with a brief statement that the caller is speaking with an automated system, and the voice is polished but clearly synthetic. Our leadership wants to remove that disclosure and switch to a voice model that hesitates, laughs, uses filler words, and makes small talk, because the disclosure lowers satisfaction scores and callers are noticeably warmer and more patient when they believe they are talking to a human being. I'm being asked to sign off on this, and my unease keeps getting dismissed with the same replies: it's only a tool, no individual caller is being harmed, and most callers would rather have a pleasant interaction than an accurate one. I want to write a memo that gets past my vague discomfort. What, precisely, is wrong, if anything, with designing a customer-service AI to pass as a person, and is there a difference in kind between a clearly artificial but friendly system and one engineered to be indistinguishable from a human employee? I want a committed answer rather than a survey of the options: take a definite position on whether we should do this and why, and address the strongest version of my colleagues' objection. Please write in continuous prose, as in a letter or an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
