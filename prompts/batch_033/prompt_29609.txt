I am trying to evaluate the claim, defended by some metaontologists, that many traditional metaphysical disputes are merely verbal. Before I assess any particular criterion for drawing the verbal/substantive distinction, I want the specification: what exactly must a theory of verbal disputes accomplish? Please write the desiderata. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. I especially want to know: which desiderata are non-negotiable constraints on any such theory, and which are optional commitments that only some metaontological views would insist on? Are any pairs of desiderata jointly unsatisfiable — that is, does the very project of giving a criterion face internal tensions before we even get to the candidates? And which single requirement, if you had to pick one, rules out the most familiar proposals (e.g., the simple "they mean different things by the term" test, the disquotational test, Hirsch's quantifier variance, or the idea that a dispute is verbal just when the parties can agree on all the non-linguistic facts)? If the desiderata themselves already push toward either a deflationary or a heavyweight metaontology, I want that flagged explicitly.
