I keep seeing the problem of logical omniscience treated as a flaw to be engineered around, but I have never seen a clear statement of what a successful engineering-around is supposed to deliver. Before I evaluate any more proposals, write me the specification: what must a formal model of belief do to count as an adequate representation of a logically bounded agent? I want the desiderata themselves, each with the case or phenomenon that forces it, and each marked as non-negotiable or as a preference of one camp. In particular, I want to know which closure principles must go, which easy inferences must still go through, and which distinctions (explicit vs. implicit belief, not having considered a proposition vs. not being able to derive it, inconsistency vs. incoherence) a model cannot collapse. Then flag any subsets of the desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most to rule out the standard approaches.

Please structure your answer in these sections, in this order:

1. The phenomena (about 200 words): the core cases an adequate model must capture.
2. The desiderata (about 500 words): named items, each with its motivating case and its status as non-negotiable or camp-specific.
3. Tensions (about 250 words): which subsets of desiderata look jointly unsatisfiable, and why.
4. The load-bearing requirement (about 150 words): which single desideratum excludes the most familiar approaches, and why.
5. Scorecard (about 250 words, table or bullets): how possible-worlds models, syntactic models, awareness models, and impossible-worlds models fare against the list.
