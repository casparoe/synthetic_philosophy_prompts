I read this explainer and I'm not sure whether to trust it because it makes some pretty strong claims. Here's the summary I read:

"The Löbian obstacle is basically an extension of Gödel's incompleteness theorems. Gödel showed that no consistent formal system can prove its own consistency. Löb's theorem takes this further: it shows that a formal system can never prove that its own proofs are reliable. That is, a system can't prove 'if I prove P, then P is true' for any nontrivial P. This means that any AI built on formal logic will inevitably be paralyzed by self-doubt — it can never trust its own reasoning, because to trust itself it would need to prove it's trustworthy, which Löb's theorem says is impossible. That's why people say the Löbian obstacle is a fundamental barrier to building self-modifying AI: the AI would need to verify that its modifications won't break its reasoning, but it can't even verify its current reasoning, so it would just freeze up."
