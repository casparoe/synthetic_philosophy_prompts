I've been reading about the Letter Spirit approach to letterforms, which holds that a lowercase a in any typeface is an a not because of any fixed shape but because it plays a certain role in the alphabet — it has an abstract identity, a letter-spirit, that survives changes of style. What puzzles me is this. Take a canonical lowercase a and gradually morph it through a sequence of intermediate forms into a canonical lowercase o. At no individual step does the form stop being an a and become an o; the change is continuous, and adjacent forms are nearly identical. Doesn't the existence of such a continuum show that there is no determinate letter-spirit after all — that categories like a and o are merely fuzzy family-resemblance clusters, and that talk of the essence of a letterform rests on a mistake?
