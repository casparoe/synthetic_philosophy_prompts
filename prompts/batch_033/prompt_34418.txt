I'm trying to work out what I think about individual contributions to collective harms, and my gut reactions to cases keep pulling me in different directions. Here are the verdicts I find myself with:

- I skip voting in a national election. I don't think this is seriously wrong. My vote had no chance of changing the outcome, and my time was honestly better spent on something else.

- I buy a factory-farmed chicken for dinner. I think this is wrong. Yes, one chicken is a rounding error in the supply chain, and the causal link between my purchase and any particular animal's suffering is probabilistic at best. But I still think it's wrong.

- I take one extra long-haul flight for a vacation. I feel this is wrong, but noticeably less wrong than buying the chicken, even though the climate harm might be objectively larger. Something about the causal chain being stretched across millions of emitters makes the wrongness feel more attenuated to me.

- I'm one of a hundred soldiers in a firing squad, each firing a rifle. I'm told truthfully that only one rifle has a live round; the rest are blanks. I fire. The prisoner dies. I think I did something clearly wrong, even if it turns out my rifle had the blank. I participated in a killing.

- I witness a group assaulting someone. I could call the police, but realistically they won't arrive in time to help. I don't call. I think I acted wrongly — I should have called.

- I don't recycle a single aluminum can. This feels trivial, not wrong. It's one can.

- I join a consumer boycott of a company that uses child labor. The company sells millions of units a year; my individual purchasing decision is insignificant. I think I should boycott, and I think it would be wrong not to. But I can't explain why this is different from the voting case.

What principle — or small set of principles — best captures these verdicts? I'm particularly interested in whether there's a clean way to draw the line that separates the voting case (where inefficacy seems to excuse) from the boycott and chicken cases (where it doesn't) without simply gerrymandering the principle to fit my intuitions. And if no tidy principle gets all of them, which of my verdicts should I give up?

Please flag how confident you are in what you propose and single out the point you're least sure of.
