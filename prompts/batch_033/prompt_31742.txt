I am trying to work out whether my commitments about the concept of disease are jointly tenable, and I would appreciate an audit. Here they are:

(1) I hold a naturalist view: to call a condition a disease is to say that it involves a biological dysfunction — a failure of some part or process to perform its evolutionarily or statistically typical function. Disease ascriptions are, in principle, empirical claims answerable to biological facts, not value judgments.

(2) I think the same analysis of disease should apply to both physical and mental disorders. There is no deep conceptual divide between diabetes and major depression in terms of what makes something a disease.

(3) I think the DSM's removal of homosexuality as a disorder was correct. I also think, as a historical matter, that this change was driven primarily by shifting social norms and political advocacy rather than by new biological or physiological evidence.

(4) I think we were right not to classify pregnancy as a disease, even though pregnancy involves substantial departures from a woman's typical physiological state — hormonal changes, immune modulation, metabolic shifts, and so on.

(5) I think the proper method for determining what is and is not a disease is empirical investigation into biological function. Conceptual analysis and normative argument have at most a peripheral role in this enterprise.

Please go through these and identify which pairs are in tension and how severely. For each pair in tension, say what the cheapest revision would be. Distinguish genuine tensions from merely apparent ones that dissolve on closer inspection. Then tell me which single revision would resolve the most tensions at the lowest cost.
