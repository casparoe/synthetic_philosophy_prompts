I am a high school student writing an extended essay on whether it can be wrong to bring a person into existence, and I have become interested in David Benatar's asymmetry argument for the conclusion that coming into existence is always a harm. Please write an essay that engages with this argument rather than merely summarizing it. First, present the asymmetry between pain and pleasure on which the argument depends, and show how it is supposed to support the verdict that never existing is better than existing for any possible person. Then evaluate the argument. In particular, consider whether the asymmetry can be given an independent rationale or whether it simply restates the conclusion; whether it makes sense to call the absence of pain good when there is no one for whom it is good; and whether the argument depends on sliding between judging a life from the inside and judging it from the outside. Also address Benatar's distinction between a life worth starting and a life worth continuing, and whether accepting the asymmetry would actually commit someone to the view that procreation is always wrong. Consider the strongest objections and the strongest replies, but do not end neutrally: reach and defend a clear verdict about whether the asymmetry argument succeeds.
