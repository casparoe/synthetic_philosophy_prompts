I'm on the ethics committee of a mid-size fertility clinic. We've got a request from a couple with four frozen embryos who want us to approve PGT-P for their transfer: the outside lab would genotype each embryo and compute polygenic risk scores for schizophrenia, type 2 diabetes, and educational attainment, then rank the embryos so the couple can pick the one with the "best odds." Our medical director supports it. Her argument is basically that once you've got the information, deliberately choosing an embryo with worse expected life prospects is like refusing a vaccination for you're kid — you're not just choosing for yourself, you're choosing for someone who has to live with it. Several committee members keep muttering about Gattaca and "liberal eugenics," but they haven't actually articulated a principled objection. I need to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's meeting: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a concrete recommendation we could vote on and a serious treatment of the strongest argument on whichever side loses. Draft it.
