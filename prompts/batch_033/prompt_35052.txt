I came to philosophy late, after a career in the natural sciences, so I want two things at once: the mathematics should be exactly right and the poem should work as a poem, not merely a formal exercise. You are a poet with a working knowledge of decision theory and the history of probability. Write a villanelle about the St. Petersburg paradox.

The villanelle should have two refrains. One refrain should speak for the expected-value calculation: a fair coin is tossed until it first lands heads; the payoff is $2 if the first toss is heads and doubles with each additional toss the game requires; the sum of probability times payoff comes out as 1 + 1 + 1 + ..., with no upper bound. The other refrain should speak for the actual player, who will pay only a modest finite sum for this gamble. The refrains should be worded so that a specialist immediately recognizes the two horns of the paradox, but they should also work for a reader who has never heard of it.

Use the form deliberately. The alternation of the refrains should enact the way the paradox keeps reasserting itself: a concave utility function such as the logarithm makes the expected utility finite, but a modified game with a fast enough payoff schedule reopens the gap unless utility is bounded; a finite bankroll caps the largest payoffs, but that is a concession about what games can really be offered, not a solution to the idealized problem. The final couplet should bring the two refrains together without reconciling them, leaving the tension between the arithmetic and the reasonable refusal intact.

Before drafting, think through the paradox carefully in plain prose: why the expected value is genuinely infinite, why reasonable people nonetheless refuse to pay large sums, and what a logarithmic utility function does and does not accomplish. Then let the poem carry that thought implicitly rather than explaining it. Do not name-drop terms like expected utility or Bernoulli unless the poem has earned them.

The response should consist of the poem itself, in American English, with no surrounding commentary.
