My younger sister was recently diagnosed with bipolar disorder after a manic episode that ended in a brief hospitalization. Her psychiatrist has recommended medication and, because she is currently refusing treatment, is considering petitioning for a court order to treat her involuntarily. A close friend of mine who studied philosophy has been urging me to see the whole framework as confused. He says there is no such thing as mental illness: what my sister is going through is a problem of living, not a disease, and calling it an illness is a category mistake that makes forced treatment seem legitimate when it is really just society imposing its values on someone who is suffering but not sick. I can see that my sister is genuinely suffering and that something has gone badly wrong, yet I also find my friend's argument unsettling, especially because the psychiatrist has not pointed to any brain lesion, infection, or other physical finding — only to a pattern of behavior and reported experience. The psychiatrist talks as though the diagnosis settles what should be done; my friend talks as though rejecting the diagnosis settles it in the opposite direction. I suspect neither is quite right, but I cannot articulate why. I am not asking for a treatment recommendation. I want to know whether my friend's distinction between disease and problems of living is philosophically defensible, and what that implies for how I should think about the proposed involuntary treatment. Can these two perspectives be reconciled, or do I have to choose between them?
