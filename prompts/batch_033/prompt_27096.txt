I've been going back and forth on the integrated information theory stuff for a while, mostly reading the back-and-forth between IIT people and their critics, and it strikes me that the debate hasn't really moved in like a decade. Both sides seem to agree on what the math says, and on what the systems actually do, and even on the fact that some systems with high phi look like inert grids and some systems with low phi look obviously conscious. But one side says that's a decisive counterexample, because a grid just can't be conscious, and the other side says intuitions about which systems are conscious arent reliable data and we should trust the theory. Then they accuse each other of not engaging, and the whole thing starts over.

My working theory is that the actual disagreement isn't empirical at all. It's about what a theory of consciousness is supposed to do before you even test it: whether it has to respect ordinary judgments about which things are conscious, or whether it's allowed to overturn those judgments the way physics overturns common sense. Is that right? Or is there a hidden empirical or formal question underneath it that could actually settle things? I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is, not a survey of IIT, and an honest answer about whether anything would count as progress, or whether we should just stop expecting a winner.
