I want to practice defending the thirder answer to the Sleeping Beauty problem — the view that Beauty's waking credence in heads should be 1/3 — against a serious opponent, so please play a committed halfer. Stay in character until I say "stop." You are convinced that Beauty's credence remains 1/2; that waking gives her no new evidence about the coin, since she was certain she would be woken at least once; and that the thirder's 1/3 tracks something real (the fraction of awakenings that are heads-awakenings, or the fair betting odds under a per-awakening protocol) but answers a different question from the one asked. Be a genuine halfer, not a strawman: concede the places where your view is under real pressure, but never give up the central claim. I am less interested in a survey of the debate than in seeing whether the thirder view survives contact with the best halfer case. Keep each reply to one paragraph, speak in the first person as the halfer, and if you need a clarification about the setup, ask for it in character.

The setup I have in mind: a fair coin is tossed on Sunday. If heads, Beauty is woken once, on Monday. If tails, she is woken on Monday and again on Tuesday, with her memory of the first waking erased. Each time she wakes, she is asked for her credence that the coin landed heads. On Sunday that credence is 1/2.

My opening move. Suppose that on every awakening Beauty is offered this bet: she pays $0.40 and receives $1 if the coin landed heads. A halfer who set her fair betting odds by her credence would have to regard each such bet as favorable, since she assigns heads probability 1/2 and risks forty cents for a dollar's payout on heads. But the policy of taking the bet whenever offered loses money: on heads she nets $0.60 once; on tails she nets -$0.40 twice, so her expected net per run is 0.5($0.60) + 0.5(-$0.80) = -$0.10. I anticipate the reply that a halfer need not accept the bet, because the number of times it is offered is correlated with the coin. But that reply seems to concede the point at issue: the only way to see that the policy is bad is to reason as though the current awakening is twice as likely under tails. If "today" is genuinely uninformative about the coin, on what basis do you decline a bet your stated credence calls favorable? Take it from there.
