I've been trying to get clear on which systems matter morally in their own right, not just as tools, and my gut keeps landing on a wierd pattern. A healthy adult matters. A patient in a vegetative state matters, though I can't say why. A worm matters a little. A locked-in patient who can't signal at all still matters. But a large language model that talks convincingly about being in pain doesn't matter in the least, and neither does a thermostat or a simple reinforcement-learning robot. A whole-brain emulation running on silicon matters. A lookup table with the same input-output behaviour as that emulation doesn't. And here's the one that bothers me: a weather system that happened to implement the same formal computation as a suffering brain for an hour wouldn't matter, even though the emulation would. Is there a principle that captures all or most of these? What does it say about a detailed simulation of an animal's nervous system, and about a brain under deep anaesthesia?
