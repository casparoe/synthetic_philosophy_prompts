Two guys in my department got into it at a happy hour last week about Boltzmann brains, and I've been turning it over ever since. Here's roughly how the exchange went:

(1) Dan said he doesn't get why the Boltzmann brain thing is treated as this deep puzzle. If your cosmology says random thermal fluctuations produce way more disembodied brains with fake memories than ordinary observers like us, then your cosmology says you're probably a Boltzmann brain. That's absurd, so you reject the cosmology. Straightforward reductio.

(2) Mira said that's too quick. If you actually were a Boltzmann brain, then all your "memories" of studying physics and your "reasoning" about cosmology would just be random noise — there's no reason to think any of it's reliable. So the argument saws off the branch it's sitting on: you can't trust the chain of reasoning that gets you from the cosmology to "I'm probably a Boltzmann brain," because that chain of reasoning is exactly the kind of thing a Boltzmann brain would hallucinate.

(3) Dan said that's just a global skeptical worry, and we don't let those shut down inquiry anywhere else. We have to assume our cognitive faculties are roughly reliable — inquiry is impossible without that. The Boltzmann brain argument just says: if theory T implies that this unavoidable assumption is almost certainly false, then T is in tension with the very possibility of doing science. That's not global skepticism, it's a specific problem for T.

(4) Mira said no, it's worse than that. In a normal reductio you can trust the deduction from the theory to the contradiction. But here, if the theory is true, you can't even trust that deduction — because the deduction is itself just a fluctuation. So you can't even get the reductio off the ground. The argument is self-defeating in a way that "this theory implies 2+2=5" isn't.

(5) Dan said that's still just skepticism with extra steps. We have no choice but to reason as if our faculties work. Given that, we evaluate theories. If theory T plus "my faculties are reliable" implies "my faculties are almost certainly not reliable," that's a pragmatic contradiction — you can't coherently believe T while also believing the things you need to believe to do any reasoning at all.

(6) Mira said okay, but then loads of theories have that problem. Determinism arguably undermines rational belief-formation too — if all your beliefs are determined by prior physical states, why think they're tracking truth? You're not gonna say determinism is self-defeating, are you?

(7) Dan said no, because determinism doesn't say that the vast majority of belief-like states are unreliable. It just says beliefs are caused. The Boltzmann case is special because the theory says the overwhelming majority of things that feel like reasoning are actually random static — that's a way stronger challenge to the reliability assumption.
