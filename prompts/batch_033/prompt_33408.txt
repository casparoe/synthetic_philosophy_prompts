So I've been arguing with people on the internet (I know, I know) about whether race is socially constructed or biologically real, and I've noticed something weird that I can't stop thinking about. Both sides have their set of go-to facts and neither side ever budges. The construction people always bring up how racial categories shift across time and place — like how the Irish and Italians weren't considered white a hundred years ago in America, or how Brazil has a totally different system where your race can basicaly change depending on your income or who you're talking to. The "race is real" people always bring up genetic clustering studies, or how forensic anthropologists can identify someone's race from a skeleton, or the fact that race predicts health outcomes even when you control for socioeconomic stuff. I've read enough of these arguments — honestly spent way too many late nights going through philosophy forums and blog posts and the occasional article — that I can script the whole exchange in advance. And I've noticed the exact same pattern in debates about whether gender is socially constructed: same set of facts getting recycled, same stalemate, same predictable moves. It's been the same debate for what feels like decades and nobody ever seems to change their mind.

What I want to understand is: what's actually going on underneath this stalemate? Is it just a verbal disagreement where people mean different things by "real" or "constructed" and if they'd just clarify their terms they'd mostly agree? Or is it a substantive disagreement about facts that could in principle be settled by more science? Or is it deeper than that — like, different starting assumptions about what even counts as a "kind" in the world that you can't argue someone into or out of? And here's the part I really care about: given whatever diagnosis you come up with, is there anything that could actually move this debate forward, or is it the kind of thing where everyone should just accept that it's not going to resolve and move on? I'm not asking who's right — I'm asking what kind of fight this even is.
