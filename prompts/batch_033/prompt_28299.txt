I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so forgive me if this is undergraduate-level confusion. I'm working on a book where the protagonist has a kind of episodic amnesia — she wakes up each day with no memory of the previous one, and the question of whether she's "the same person" across days is central to the plot. (Working title: _The Inventory_.) So I started reading Descartes, thinking the cogito would give me a clean starting point for thinking about selfhood, and now I've gotten myself tangled in it in a way that's making me suspicious of the whole thing.

Here's the argument I keep coming back to, and it feels like it proves the cogito is either trivial or cheating:

(1) Descartes says: I can doubt everything — the external world, my body, even mathematical truths if there's an evil demon — except that I am thinking. From "I am thinking" I can infer with certainty "I exist."

(2) But what is this "I"? If it's just whatever is picked out by the act of thinking — a bare indexical, like the "it" in "it is raining" — then the conclusion is just "the thing doing this thinking exists," which is barely more than "this thinking is occuring."

(3) If that's all the cogito proves, then by exactly the same reasoning, "I am walking" proves "I exist" — because the "I" there is also just whatever is doing the walking. Sure, I might be dreaming I'm walking, but even in the dream, the dream-walker exists qua dream-character. The existence claim is just as thin.

(4) And "I seem to see a candle" proves "I exist" just as well as "I think I see a candle." The thinking doesn't add anything special if the "I" is doing no metaphysical work.

(5) So either the cogito proves something so thin it applies to any verb you plug in (making it unremarkable — congrats, you've proven that walking-occurs entails a walker-exists, which is grammar not metaphysics), or Descartes is smuggling in a thick notion of the self — a persisting substance — that isn't licensed by the premise.

(6) If the latter, the whole project of rebuilding knowledge from the cogito outward fails at step one, because the foundation isn't clean.

I feel like there must be a mistake here because this argument is too easy and I'm not a philosopher. But I've been turning it over for weeks and I can't find the exit. Which step is wrong, and what do I have to give up to reject it? I'm especially interested in what this means for my amnesiac protagonist — if the cogito can't deliver a persisting self, what can?
