I serve on the board of the Ellison Literary Foundation, which awards a mid-career fellowship carrying a substantial stipend and a yearlong residency. This year's strongest candidate by a clear margin is a novelist whose last two books have reshaped the landscape of American fiction—formally audacious, critically revered, widely taught. The candidate has also been credibly accused, in multiple published accounts, of predatory behavior toward younger writers over more than a decade. The foundation's guidelines are silent on matters of personal conduct. The board is split: several members argue that the fellowship is an award for literary achievement and that excluding someone for extraliterary behavior sets a dangerous precedent; others argue that a fellowship is not a book review—it confers prestige, money, and institutional endorsement on a person, not a text—and that bestowing it would signal the foundation is willing to overlook serious harm. I have been asked to draft a decision memo for the board, recommending a course of action with reasons. The memo should engage the strongest arguments on both sides honestly, reach a clear recommendation, and be something the board can actually vote on. Please write the answer in American English.
