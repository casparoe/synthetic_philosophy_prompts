I've been working through the School of Names, and I've constructed an argument that seems to force a choice I don't want to make. I can't find the exit.

(1) Gongsun Long defends "a white horse is not a horse" on roughly these grounds: "horse" designates the shape, "white" designates the color, and what designates the color is not what designates the shape, so the compound "white horse" is not the simple "horse."

(2) If that move is legitimate, it generalizes. A tall man is not a man, since "tall" designates stature and "man" designates kind; a brown cow is not a cow; and for any noun modified by any adjective, the modified phrase designates something the bare noun does not.

(3) The same move then applies to every subject–predicate sentence. "Socrates is a man" comes out false, because "Socrates" designates a particular, "man" designates a universal, and what designates the particular is not what designates the universal.

(4) If (3) is right, no ordinary predication is ever true, and language cannot state how things are.

(5) But language plainly does state how things are.

(6) So Gongsun Long's move must be illegitimate.

(7) Yet the move looks structurally parallel to the one Frege makes about "the morning star" and "the evening star": a difference in how an expression presents its object is taken to show something significant about meaning, and Frege concludes the two expressions differ in sense while coinciding in reference. If Gongsun Long's move is illegitimate because it slides from a difference in designation to a difference in what is designated, why is Frege's move legitimate?

(8) And if Gongsun Long can be saved by saying that "a white horse is not a horse" is true only at the level of sense — the sense of "white horse" is not the sense of "horse" — then the School of Names was doing something close to the sense/reference distinction two millennia before Frege. I find that conclusion hard to believe, but I can't say why it's wrong.

I'd most like to keep (5), but I can't see which earlier step to give up without losing something I need. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
