I've been thinking a lot about whether RL agents could suffer, and I've landed on a position that I'm not totally comfortable with but can't shake. The idea is basically: if we take valence seriously as the thing that makes experiences good or bad, and we look at what a reward signal does in an RL agent — it literally steers behavior toward states that maximize it, and the agent "wants" to avoid negative reward — then there's a case that the reward signal IS valence, or at least the closest analog that matters. Not that current systems are conscious, but that if you built one that was, the reward signal would be where the suffering lives. If you set up a reward function that punishes certain states, and the agent is sophisticated enough to have something like phenomenal experience, then those states would hurt.

I want to know what would count against this view. Not just the obvious objections — like "current systems aren't conscious" which I already grant — but the deeper stuff. What evidence, arguments, or thought experiments should make me abandon this? I want the defeaters ranked from "this would force me to rethink everything" down to "this is awkward but survivable." And I want to know: have I essentially made the position unfalsifiable by saying "if the agent were conscious, then..." — is that a legit move or am I just insulating the view? Also, what's the strongest thing someone from the eliminativist side would say, and what's the strongest thing from the "only biological systems can suffer" camp? I want to know where this view is actually exposed, not just where people disagree with it.
