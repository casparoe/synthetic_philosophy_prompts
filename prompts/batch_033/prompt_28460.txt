Quick refresher so we're on the same page. The raven thing: "all ravens are black" is logically equivalent to "all non-black things are non-ravens," so spotting a red apple — a non-black non-raven — technically confirms the raven hypothesis. Which seems absurd. The grue thing: define "grue" as "green if first observed before time t, blue if first observed after t." Every emerald we've seen so far is both green and grue, so our evidence equally supports "all emeralds are green" and "all emeralds are grue" — two generalizations that make opposite predictions about emeralds first examined after t. Also absurd, but in a different flavor.

Or does grue survive that treatment and demand something else entirely? And conversely, if you solve grue by restricting induction to "projectible" predicates, does the raven paradox just evaporate as a side effect?
