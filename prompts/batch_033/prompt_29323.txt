I'm taking a course on distributive justice this term, and we recently covered the leveling-down objection to egalitarianism alongside prioritarianism as an alternative. As I understand it, the leveling-down objection says that if you think equality is intrinsically valuable, you are committed to the strange conclusion that making everyone worse off could be an improvement, provided it reduces inequality. Prioritarianism is meant to avoid this: it says we should give extra weight to benefits for the worse off, but it does not treat equality itself as a good.

What I cannot get clear on is whether there is any real daylight between these views in practice. Every policy trade-off I can construct — taxing the rich to fund transfers to the poor, prioritising healthcare for the disadvantaged, and so on — seems to come out the same way on both views: help the worse off. If prioritarianism and egalitarianism are extensionally equivalent across all realistic cases, then is not prioritarianism simply egalitarianism with the branding changed to dodge an awkward objection? And if so, why do philosophers treat them as distinct positions in the literature rather than one view with a defensive patch?
