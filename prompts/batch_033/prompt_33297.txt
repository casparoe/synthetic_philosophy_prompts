I am a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, and I would like a move that recurs in our reading group audited. One of the more senior students argues as follows. Darwin inverted the usual order of explanation: the appearance of design in organisms — the vertebrate eye, echolocation, the immune system — does not require a designing mind that understands the design. Natural selection produces competence without any agent comprehending what is built. To make this vivid, the student leans on an analogy: for explanatory purposes, natural selection is a designer, specifically an engineer without foresight, goals, or blueprints. The analogy is then made to carry real weight. The student claims that Turing's inversion is the same move transposed to intelligent behaviour, and that just as we no longer need a comprehending designer behind the eye, we should not demand a comprehending inner agent behind intelligent performance, whether in a Turing machine, a trained network, or a person. The conclusion drawn is that exhibited competence exhausts the explanatory target, and that asking whether a system genuinely understands is the modern counterpart of asking who designed the eye.

I would like to know how far this analogy actually holds. Please provide three things. First, a correspondence table for the analogy that natural selection is a designer: what corresponds to what, including the designer's goals, prototypes, evaluation of candidate designs, knowledge of the problem, choice of materials, and foresight. Second, a sorting of the disanalogies: which features of a designer does the analogy quietly import that natural selection lacks — foresight, representation of a goal, evaluation of unbuilt alternatives, global rather than local optimization, sensitivity to future environments — and which of those are load-bearing for the further step from Darwin to Turing, rather than harmless shorthand. Third, an assessment of whether the conclusion survives the audit: once the disanalogies are explicit, is Darwin's inversion genuinely the same kind of move as Turing's, or does the transfer depend on the designer analogy doing more work than it can support? If there is a better analogy for what natural selection does, say what it is and whether it helps or hurts the conclusion.
