I'm a philosophy grad student but my work is mostly in metaphysics, and I've been noticing this thing in moral life that I can't quite pin down. Here's the pattern: someone does the right thing, but the way they got there — the deliberative route they took — seems to mess with the act itself. Like, imagine someone's partner is drowning, and instead of just diving in, they pause to think "ok, what does the Greatest Happiness Principle say about saving a loved one vs a stranger?" Even if they conclude "yes, save the partner" and do it, the pause itself feels wrong, almost like a betrayal. But it's not just emergencies. I've noticed it in smaller ways too — a friend who you can tell is running through some internal checklist of duties before they do you a favor, and by the time they do it, the favor feels weirdly transactional or hollow.

I'm not talking about cases where the person reasons badly, or where their deliberation leads to the wrong action. It's specifically cases where the reasoning is correct and the action is right, but something about having to reason your way there at all undercuts the whole thing.
