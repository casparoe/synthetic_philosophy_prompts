I'm a software engineer, and I've been offered a spot in a clinical trial for a gradual neural replacement procedure. The idea is simple: over about five years, a device would replace my biological neurons one at a time with synthetic equivalents that replicate the exact input-output behavior of each neuron. At no point is brain function interrupted — the synthetic neurons interface with the biological ones in real time. The procedure is fully reversible at any stage. The pitch is immunity to neurodegenerative disease and eventual optional uploading. I'm trying to decide whether to consent, and I keep hitting the same question: would I still be me at the end? If replacing one neuron makes no detectable difference to my thoughts, memories, or personality, I should be the same person after any single swap. But if that's true for each step, then after all neurons have been replaced, I should still be me. Yet the conclusion feels wrong — it seems like the original biological me would be gone, replaced by something that merely believes it's me. I recognize this resembles a sorites paradox, but I'm not sure whether that means the reasoning is flawed or whether I should just accept the conclusion. I'd like help thinking through this, taking the structure of the argument seriously rather than getting generic advice or being told to trust my gut. Please write your answer in American English.
