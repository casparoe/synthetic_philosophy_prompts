I'm a novelist applying for a six-month research residency that funds the background work for a fiction project. My next novel follows a woman who must choose between two fundamentally different lives — staying in the rural community where she grew up to care for aging parents and raise her own children near family, or moving to a city where she has been offered a surgical residency she has wanted since she was seventeen. I want the novel to take the structure of the choice seriously as a philosophical problem rather than treating it as a plot device that gets resolved by a dramatic revelation or a convenient accident.

I have been reading the philosophical literature on incommensurable values and hard choices — Raz, Chang, Anderson, and others — and my half-formed idea is this: the philosophical debate has focused almost entirely on whether incommensurability is real and what it implies for the structure of rational choice, but it has said remarkably little about what it is like to inhabit a hard choice. I mean the phenomenology of facing options that cannot be ranked on a common scale, the temporal experience of deliberation when reasons run out, the way people construct or discover reasons after the fact to make sense of a choice that at the time felt underdetermined by any deliberation they could do. Novelists know something about this texture that philosophers have not yet theorized, and I suspect that bringing the two traditions into contact — philosophical analysis of value structure and narrative attention to lived experience — would yield a sharper account of what hard choices are and how people actually navigate them.

I need a short research proposal (maximum 800 words) that I can submit with my application. It should state a precise research question, explain why the existing philosophical positions leave it open, describe the approach I would take and why that approach rather than the obvious alternatives, say what a deliverable would look like and what would count as the project failing, and give a realistic scope for six months. Do not include a bibliography — I will add citations myself. The proposal should be written for a committee that includes both philosophers and artists, so it needs to be philosophically rigorous but accessible to non-specialists.

Before you write the proposal, please do the following. First, take a moment to think through the main positions in the incommensurability literature — the parity view, the incomparability view, the view that hard choices are cases of equality or vagueness, and any others you think relevant — and identify exactly which gap my half-formed idea is pointing at. Be specific: what do the existing views say about the experience of choosing, and what do they leave untouched? Second, brainstorm two or three possible research questions that could anchor the project, and pick the one that is both genuinely open and tractable in six months. Then write the proposal. The committee will be reading for clarity, originality, and feasibility — they want to fund projects that will actually produce something, not just gesture at big ideas.
