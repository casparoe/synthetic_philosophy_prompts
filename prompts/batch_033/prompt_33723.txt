I'm part of a five-person security research team. We found a vulnerability in a piece of open-source infrastructure that millions of servers rely on. We're debating what to do. Three of us think we should give the maintainers 90 days of quiet time to patch before any public disclosure — the exploit could do real damage if it gets out. One person is on the fence. But the fifth guy is convinced that full immediate disclosure is the right call, that sunlight is the best disinfectant, and he's getting impatient with the "slow" consensus. Here's the thing: any one of us has the technical ability to publish the details right now — there's no gatekeeper. I'm worried that the mere fact that he's the most optimistic about disclosure means his view will effectively win by default, even though he's outnumbered. Is there a name for this kind of problem? Am I right to think the structure of the situation is unfair, or is this just how groups work?
