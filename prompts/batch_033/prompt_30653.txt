My verdicts:

2. Against a total stranger on the other side of the world, no communication. I defect, and I think that's just the rational play.
3. Against a perfect copy of me made ten minutes ago, same memories. I cooperate, same as the twin case.
4. Against a stranger whose action is causally wired to mine by a machine — if I cooperate it makes them cooperate, if I defect it makes them defect. I cooperate, but it feels like a different reason, not the we're-in-this-together thing.
5. Against a stranger where the payoffs are almost symmetric but not quite: if we both cooperate, I get $10 and they get $9. Here the pull to cooperate basically vanishes, even though a dollar shouldnt matter. That bothers me.
6. The case I cant stop going back and forth on: a one-shot PD against a stranger I've never met, no causal link between us, but I know for a fact they're a perfectly rational agent with exactly the same information about the game, and they know the same about me. Half the time I think superrationality says we both cooperate, since we'd both arrive at the same answer and cooperation is better than defection. The other half I think that's magical thinking and I should defect like in case 2.

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? What does it say about case 6, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop?
