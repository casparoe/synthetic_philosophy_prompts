ok so ive been chewing on this two envelope thing for a while now — not a philosophy person, just someone who read about it and now cant stop thinking about it. the basic idea is theres two envelopes, one has twice the money as the other, you pick one and open it and see some amount, and then the math says you should switch because the expected value of the other envelope is higher. but then once you switch, the exact same reasoning says you should switch back. and it just goes in circles forever. drives me nuts.

i want to write a villanelle about it. the reason i want a villanelle specifically is that the form is already built around two lines that keep returning — the refrains chase each other through the whole poem and then finally land together in the last two lines. thats exactly what the paradox does. the two refrains should be the two voices of the paradox: one is the argument for switching, the other is the realization that the argument applies equally to the envelope you just switched to. and as the poem goes on the refrains should start to feel like the same thing said two different ways, until they meet at the end and you realize the whole thing was a kind of trap.

the poem shouldnt try to resolve the paradox by explaining that the prior distribution is improper or whatever. it should just make you feel the dizziness of it. someone who knows the paradox should recognize it immediately, and someone who doesnt should still feel like theyre being pulled in two directions at once.

also i think theres a way the villanelle form itself can do the work of showing why the paradox is unsolvable without fixing the prior — the refrains keep returning no matter what you do, the same way the expected value calculation keeps returning no matter which envelope youre holding. but i dont know how to say that exactly, you probably know better than me.
