i've spent way too long trying to figure out who actually deserves what — the raise, the praise, the bigger share — and every rule i try falls apart on some case: the person who works twice as hard but has half the talent, the one who got lucky once and coasted, the kid who inherited a head start and then worked hard from there. before i read another defense or attack of meritocracy, i want the spec for the thing it's supposed to track. what would a theory of desert have to get right to be worth taking seriously? give me the list of requirements — the cases it has to handle, the distinctions it cant blur (deserving an outcome vs deserving a fair shot, desert vs entitlement vs need, backward-looking vs forward-looking), the stubborn facts about how talent, effort, luck and upbringing interact that it has to respect — and for each one, the case or consideration that forces it. then tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's hangup, flag any that cant be satisfied together, and say which single requirement does the most damage to the standard positions, with a short scorecard of how those positions fare. i want a committed answer: pick the requirements youd defend and the view that survives them, and argue for it; dont just lay out options.
