I keep coming back to the Sleeping Beauty problem and I've started to suspect the whole argument is stuck for structural reasons rather than because anyone's being thick. Quick setup, in case it matters: Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday and a fair coin is tossed. She's woken Monday. If the coin came up tails she's given a memory-wiping drug and woken again Tuesday, so on any waking she can't tell whether it's Monday or Tuesday, or whether the toss was heads or tails. The question is what credence she ought to have in heads when she wakes. Halfers say a half, because she knew the coin was fair and, they claim, she's learnt nothing new that bears on the toss. Thirders say a third, because there are three indistinguishable wakings she might be in and only one of them is a heads-waking; the betting and frequency arguments seem to point that way too.

Everyone knows everyone else's arguments by now. The 'no new information' argument, the Dutch book argument, the long-run frequency argument, the variants where there are lots of tails-wakings, the bit where the answer shifts if she's told it's Monday. None of it moves anyone. My working theory is that the two camps arent actually answering the same question — that 'her credence in heads' is ambiguous between something like 'the chance the coin landed heads' and 'the chance this waking is a heads-waking', and the debate is really a fight over which question Beauty is being asked. But I'm not sure, because both camps are convinced they're answering the same question as the other lot.

I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, substantive, a difference about what counts as evidence, a difference about reference classes for self-locating belief, or a sign the question is ill-posed — and an honest answer about what would count as progress: a sharper question, a theorem, a new variant, or the admission that neither side can be budged. Dont give me a survey of the positions; give me the structure of the impasse.
