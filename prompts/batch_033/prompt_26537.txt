Ok so I spent my career in experimental physics and now that I'm retired I've been diving into philosophy, especially the stuff at the boundary with cosmology. I've been reading around the Boltzmann brain problem and I've sort of landed on a set of views, but I want a consistency check before I present them to my reading group. I'm not sure where the tensions are exactly, and I don't want to walk in with something that falls apart on contact.

Here's roughly where I am:

(1) The Boltzmann brain problem is a real epistemic problem. If a cosmological model implies that the overwhelming majority of observers with experiences like mine are random thermal fluctuations, that model is in serious trouble.

(2) Anthropic reasoning is fine, but it has to be done properly: you condition on all your evidence, not just on "I am an observer." So I don't think the problem is that I should believe I'm a Boltzmann brain. My coherent stream of experiences screens that off.

(3) But I don't think (2) dissolves the problem. There's still a confirmation-theoretic issue: a theory that says most observers with evidence E are Boltzmann brains is disconfirmed when you find yourself having evidence E and not being a Boltzmann brain. The numbers still matter.

(4) The right way out is to prefer cosmological models where ordinary observers outnumber Boltzmann brains by a huge margin, or where Boltzmann brains don't arise. That's a legitimate theoretical dessideratum.

(5) I think the Doomsday Argument is wrong. The fact that I'm born roughly 60 billion humans into the species doesn't license any inference about how many more will come.

(6) The measure problem in eternal inflation is a separate technical headache and we shouldn't let it get tangled up with the Boltzmann brain issue, even though they interact.

I've got nagging feeling that (2), (3) and (5) are pulling against each other, and maybe (1) and (2) are in tension too. But I can't quite put my finger on it. Could you go through these and flag which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and rank the tensions by severity? For each genuine tension, tell me what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs.

Also, please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this — I don't want to be told that some controversial move is the consensus view when it isn't.
