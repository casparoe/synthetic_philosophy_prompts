I'm taking an upper-division epistemology seminar, and next week we begin a unit on higher-order evidence and epistemic akrasia. Before I work through the readings, I want to know what an adequate theory in this area would have to accomplish. Write the desiderata: what must a theory of how higher-order evidence bears on rational belief get right, and what should we ask of it?

Please make each desideratum concrete by giving the case or argument that motivates it. I have in mind things like: cases in which a careful piece of first-order reasoning is undermined by evidence that you are sleep-deprived, cognitively impaired, or generally unreliable in the relevant domain; cases in which the higher-order evidence is misleading and your first-order reasoning is actually fine; the distinction between evidence that undercuts your first-order reasoning and evidence that bears directly on the target proposition; the distinction between a belief state that is irrational and one that is merely mistaken or unfortunate; and the structural "levels" problem — whether a single belief can coherently answer both to first-order and to higher-order evidence at once. For each desideratum, say whether it is a non-negotiable demand on any adequate theory or a preference of one camp. I'm aware, for instance, that some approaches influenced by the peer-disagreement literature treat higher-order evidence as requiring substantial revision of one's first-order attitude, while others treat a ban on epistemic akrasia — an enkratic requirement — as a fixed point; these need not be the only options, and please don't assume either camp is right. I most want the part where you assess whether some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable: can a theory both treat higher-order evidence as fully evidential and still forbid rational akrasia, or does acknowledging the force of higher-order evidence force you to allow at least some cases in which it is rational to believe p while believing that your evidence does not support p? If a trade-off is forced, say which desideratum should give way and why.

Please separate what is settled among people who work on this from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Write in American English.
