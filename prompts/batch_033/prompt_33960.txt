I'm putting together the theory chapter of my dissertation on collective intentionality, and I've landed on a set of commitments that I genuinely like but have a nagging feeling don't all hang together. Before my advisor finds the problem and makes me rewrite the whole thing, I want a consistency audit. Here's roughly where I am:

(1) I'm a reductionist about shared intention in something like Bratman's sense. When you and I intend to paint the fence together, there's no group-level subject doing the intending; it's just that I intend that we paint it, you intend that we paint it, we each intend that this happen by way of meshing subplans, and all of this is out in the open between us.

(2) But I also find Gilbert persuasive that joint commitments create obligations. If we've jointly committed to walk home together and I get a better offer on the way, I can't just walk off: you have a claim against me. And this isn't just a moral obligation or an ordinary promise — it's a distinctive kind of non-moral directed obligation that I can't unilateraly dissolve.

(3) I don't think full common knowledge is required for joint action. Skilled dyads — two people carrying a couch down a tight stairwell, a pair of dancers improvising — seem to be acting together through continuous mutual responsiveness, without running the kind of iterated higher-order beliefs that (1) requires.

(4) At the same time, I'm moved by Searle's line that some basic forms of collective intentionality are primitive and developmentally early. Toddlers building a tower together, and maybe some nonhuman animals, seem to share intentions without having the conceptual resources to form the iterated attitudes in (1); the "we" seems to come first, with individual intentions as a later abstraction.

(5) Finally, I'm a realist about group agents. A corporation or a court can have intentions and beliefs in a literal sense, not just as shorthand, and these aren't reducible to what any of the members happen to think.

So that's the package. I can't tell where the real strain is. Please go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look that way, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what the cheapest fix is. I want to know where the load-bearing conflicts are, not a list of every possible quibble.
