I've been having this recurring argument with a coworker during lunch breaks and neither of us knows enough philosophy to know if we're reinventing the wheel. It started when our company was deciding wether to switch to a slightly more expensive health insurance plan that would cover a rare treatment one employee needs. The cost per employee would've been like three dollars a month. Most people were fine with it, but a few argued that you can't impose even a tiny cost on everyone just to benefit one person — it's a matter of principle. I found myself agreeing with both sides somehow. Then I realized this same tension shows up everywhere. We all think you should divert a runaway trolley from five people to one person. That's aggregating: the five together outweigh the one. But if you ask people whether we should tax everyone a penny to save one person from dying of a treatable disease, a lot of people get squeamish, even though the math is the same — many tiny burdens vs one huge benefit. And it's not just about money. If you could prevent one person from going blind by causing a million people to have slightly blurrier vision for an hour, almost nobody thinks that's okay. But if it's preventing five people from going blind, some people start to waver. So it seems like we're happy to aggregate when the harms on both sides are of the same type and comparable magnitude (five deaths vs one death), but we resist when the magnitudes are wildly different, even if the total "amount" of harm is larger on one side.

I guess my question is: is there a name for this specific issue — not the general question of whether aggregation is ever justified, but the puzzle about what makes harms "eligible" to be aggregated against each other? And relatedly, is there a name for the view that you can only aggregate claims that are of the same kind and roughly similar magnitude? I've heard the phrase "aggregation problem" but I think that's about something broader, like whether you can add up claims across persons at all. What I'm describing feels like a sub-problem about the preconditions for aggregation, and I'd love to know if philosophers have carved it out as a distinct issue, and if so what the main positions are.
