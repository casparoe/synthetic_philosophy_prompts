Write an essay on whether the Principal Principle has genuine exceptions. The Principal Principle says your credence in an outcome should equal its objective chance once you know what that chance is — that's basically what makes chance chance. But there are some famous puzzle cases: what if you've seen the future in a crystal ball? What if the chance itself depends on how things actually turn out (the undermining future problem)? Lewis built in an admissibility clause to handle the crystal ball stuff, but the real fight is over whether that clause can be made precise without just defining admissibility as "whatever makes the principle work." Weigh whether these cases show the principle needs to be abandoned, restricted, or whether they just show we need to be more careful about what objective chance is in the first place. Reach a conclusion and defend it — don't just say it depends on your metaphysics of chance.
