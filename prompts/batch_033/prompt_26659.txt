Since a spatially infinite universe would contain infinitely many agents, any act that has some nonzero probability of producing a benefit will, in expectation, produce that benefit infinitely many times — and likewise for harms. Doesn't it follow that all actions have the same infinite or undefined expected value, and that ethical reasoning therefore collapses into triviality? If so, why do philosophers treat this as a technical puzzle for consequentialism rather than as a straightforward reductio that shows the whole framework is ill-defined? In your answer, please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people working on infinite ethics, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
