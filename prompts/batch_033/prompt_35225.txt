I've been reworking a lecture on Zeno's paradoxes of motion, and I keep being bothered by the way the standard modern response is treated as a single, uniform solution. The usual move — granting that an infinite series of decreasing intervals can sum to a finite total, so that traversing infinitely many stages need not take infinitely long — seems to me to do very different amounts of work against the different paradoxes. It speaks fairly directly to the Dichotomy and to Achilles and the Tortoise, both of which can be framed as summation problems. But the Arrow appears to raise a different difficulty about what is true of a moving body at an instant, and the Stadium seems to depend on assumptions about indivisible minima and relative motion that the limit-based response does not touch. I want to know whether that impression survives scrutiny.
