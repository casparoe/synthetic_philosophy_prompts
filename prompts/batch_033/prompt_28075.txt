I'm on the board of a small effective-altruism-adjacent foundation, and we're having a bit of an identity crisis. We use the importance-tractability-neglectedness framework to evaluate causes — you know, the standard ITN thing — but we've never actually sat down and formalized how we weight the three factors against each other. We just go case by case and trust our guts, which worked fine until we hit a proposal that split the board right down the middle.

Looking back at our last five decisions:

1. We funded a malaria vaccine development project. Our assessment: high importance (malaria kills half a million people a year, mostly kids), medium tractability (vaccine R&D is hard but there are promising candidates), medium neglectedness (global health gets tons of money overall, but vaccine R&D for neglected diseases has specific gaps).

2. We declined a climate change mitigation proposal focused on U.S. federal policy advocacy. Very high importance, but low tractability (the political gridlock is real and our grant was tiny relative to the forces at play), and low neglectedness — this space is absolutely swarming with funders.

3. We funded an AI alignment research group at a non-elite university. High importance, medium tractability, high neglectedness — most alignment funding flows to top-ten departments, and there's talent elsewhere that nobody's supporting.

4. We declined a deworming scale-up in a region already saturated by other major funders. Medium importance, very high tractability (we know deworming works and it's cheap), but very low neglectedness — our marginal dollar would literally be displacing other peoples' dollars at this point.

5. We funded a project on improving institutional decision-making around global catastrophic risks. High importance, medium tractability (it's hard to measure but the theory of change is plausible), very high neglectedness — almost nobody works on this.

So here's the new proposal that's causing trouble: a pandemic preparedness project focused on strengthening surveillance systems in middle-income countries. Our analysis: high importance (the next pandemic could be worse than COVID), medium-high tractability (we have concrete interventions that demonstratably work), but low neglectedness — post-COVID, this space has gotten a ton of attention and funding, from governments and philanthropies alike.

Half the board says it's an obvious yes: importance and tractability are solid, and neglectedness shouldn't be a dealbreaker when the stakes are literally millions of lives. The other half says it's a clear no: the space is crowded, our marginal dollar would do more good somewhere more neglected, and we'd basically be free-riding on the attention the cause is already getting.

I'm less interested in what you personally think about pandemic funding than in what we should decide if we want to be consistent with our own precedents. Which past case is the closest analog? And if our precedents genuinely underdetermine this — which I suspect they do — what principle would we be adopting with each choice? Can you articulate the different implicit weighting schemes that are consistent with our first five decisions, and tell me what further hypothetical case would cleanly distinguish them?
