I started a company a couple years ago and I keep getting the same advice from older founders: that I should deliberately get myself to believe things that aren't quite true, or at least get myself into states that aren't rational, because it'll make me more credible. I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about this by going through cases, and I want to know what principle, if any, is behind my gut calls.

Here are my verdicts:

- The stranded hiker. I'm lost and a driver will only rescue me if I promise to pay him a lot once I'm safe. We both know that once I'm safe I'll have no reason to pay, so a plain promise won't work. If I could take a pill that would make me the sort of person who keeps promises even when it costs me, I think it would be rational to take it, even though later I'll pay out money for no benefit. Fine.

- The sales call. Before a big pitch, I can sometimes talk myself into genuinely believing my product is better than it is, and I sell better when I do. I think this is probably rational, or at least fine, though it makes me uncomfortable.

- The mugging. If someone's threatening me and I could take a pill that makes me genuinely furious and willing to fight a guy with a knife, which might scare him off, I think that's not rational. The state might get me killed and I can't control what it does once it's there.

- The pure belief bet. Someone offers me alot of money if, by Friday, I genuinely believe my company is worth far more than it is, and they'll pay me before I have to act on the belief in any way. I don't think I can just decide to believe it, and I don't think it's rational to try. This one feels completely different from the hiker, but I can't say why.

- The locked account. I moved some savings into an account with a withdrawal penalty so my future self can't blow it. That's obviously rational, but it doesn't feel like irrationality at all, just tying my own hands.

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? What seperates the hiker and the sales call from the mugging and the belief bet? And what would the principle say about a case I keep going back and forth on: I could take a pill that makes me forget a humiliating failure so I'm more confident in meetings, where the confidence itself is the only thing that changes. Also tell me which of my verdicts the principle forces me to give up, if any.
