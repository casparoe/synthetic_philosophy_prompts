i'm a phd student in moral philosophy, and a friend in the department who works on blame sent me his one-paragraph gloss of the standing-to-blame literature to sanity-check before he puts it in a handbook chapter. here's the gloss:

"The debate about standing to blame is about whether a hypocrite has the right to blame. The standard view says blame is a speech-act-like practice that requires authority, and someone who commits the very wrong she is blaming others for forfeits that authority, so her blame isn't just rude, it's void. The person being blamed is then entitled to dismiss the criticism entirely, since a blamer without standing has no right to make the accusation at all. This is usually presented as a point about the blamer rather than about whether the target really did wrong, which is why critics say the whole view is an elaborate way of letting wrongdoers off the hook."

I have a nagging sense that something's off — i remember someone in a seminar saying the target's blameworthiness is supposed to be untouched by all this — but i can't tell which parts are the mainstream view and which are his own spin. Could you go through it sentence by sentence and tell me what a defender of a standing condition would accept, what they'd wince at, and whether the final sentence follows from anything the defender actually says?
