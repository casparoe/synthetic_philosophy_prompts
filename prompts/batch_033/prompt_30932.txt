I'm a high school student, and my philosophy club is running a mock adversarial collaboration. I've been asked to produce the joint statement for two positions on the meaning of life. The first position, drawn from Schlick's essay on the topic, holds, roughly, that the meaning of a life is not a goal the life aims at but a quality of present activity: meaningfulness is found in doing things for their own sake — what Schlick calls play, as opposed to work pursued only for the sake of some further end — and the question of the meaning of life is thereby dissolved rather than answered. The second position holds that a life's meaning consists at least partly in the pursuit and achievement of worthwhile ends across time — commitments, projects, and a coherent narrative — so that a life organized only around present-centered activity would be missing something.

Write the document the two camps could both sign after a good-faith collaboration. I want: a numbered list of claims both accept, stated non-vacuously; the precise formulations of where they still differ, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; the genuine cruxes; and, for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I suspect one crux is whether goal-directed activity can carry playlike value, and another is whether the value of a completed project is exhausted by the quality of the moments that compose it, but don't let my guesses constrain you. If you think part of the dispute is only verbal, say which part and why. Structure the result as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table that includes, for each disagreement, each side's preferred wording, the crux underneath it, and what would count as evidence.
