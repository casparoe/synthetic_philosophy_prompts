I've noticed a problem that keeps coming up when I try to model uncertain evidence, and I wonder whether it has an established label—or a cluster of related labels.

Here's the situation. I glance at a table across the room and see something that might be a coffee mug. Before looking, my credence that there's a mug on the table is, say, 0.3. After the glance, I'm more confident—maybe 0.85—but I'm not certain. Standard Bayesian conditionalization tells me to conditionalize on my total evidence, but my total evidence isn't "there's a mug"; it's something more like "I seemed to see a mug" or "I had a visual experience as of a mug." If I conditionalize on that instead, I need to know P(mug | seemed to see mug), which just relocates the difficulty. So I try the alternative: directly set P(mug) to 0.85 and propagate the change through the rest of my credences. But there are indefinitely many ways to do that propagation. Which other propositions should change, and by how much? Should P(there's a spoon on the table) drop? What about P(the lighting was dim)? Different choices yield different posterior distributions.

I gather that Jeffrey conditionalization handles this by holding fixed the probabilities conditional on the mug and on not-mug—the "rigidity" condition. But two difficulties remain. First, why is rigidity the uniquely rational constraint, as opposed to some other pattern of propagation? Second, and more practically, which partition am I supposed to Jeffrey-conditionalize over? My visual experience bears on "mug on table" but also on "ceramic object on table," "white cylinder on table," "my spouse left their mug out again"—these partition the space differently, and the choice of partition changes the result. There doesn't seem to be a principle that picks one out.

Is there an established name for this tangle—the underdetermination of the posterior given uncertain evidence, the reliance on a partition that itself seems arbitrary, and the question of what justifies rigidity? Are these considered one problem or several? If the literature treats them separately, I'd like to know which name goes with which issue, and how they fit together. If, on the other hand, I'm describing something that doesn't have a settled label, I'd welcome a coinage and a definition that marks its boundaries.
