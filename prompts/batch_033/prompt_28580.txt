Scientists use thought experiments all the time — Galileo dropping balls from the Leaning Tower (which he probably never actually did), Einstein imagining himself riding a light beam, Schrödinger's cat. And these aren't just teaching tools; they're treated as genuine arguments that can shift scientific consensus. But here's what bugs me: you're learning something about the physical world without making any new observations. What epistemology does that practice presuppose? Specifically, what view of knowledge makes it possible to gain new empirical knowledge from an armchair? And here's the tension I want you to address: if thought experiments really do yield knowledge, why can't I just do all of physics from my couch? The practice seems to treat them as powerful but also somehow constrained — what's doing the constraining? Give me a committed reconstruction of the implicit epistemology here, and tell me whether the practice is coherent or whether scientists are basically running two incompatible theories of knowledge at once.
