Deterministic chaos is deterministic by definition — the governing equations have unique solutions given exact initial conditions, and no randomness enters the dynamics. So the unpredictability we associate with chaotic systems must be purely epistemic: it reflects our inability to specify initial conditions with infinite precision, not anything ontological about the system itself. But if that is all there is to it, why is chaos theory standardly described as having revealed something about the world, rather than merely something about the limits of our measuring instruments? Would a Laplacian demon who knows all initial conditions with perfect precision find chaotic systems perfectly predictable — and if so, doesn't it follow that the unpredictability of chaos is not a genuine feature of nature, but only of our epistemic situation?
