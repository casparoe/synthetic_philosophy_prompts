I've been reading about futarchy and I keep coming across the slogan "vote on values, bet on beliefs". It was used to shut down an objection I made on a forum: I had argued that choosing the metric of national welfare is itself an empirical matter, because you have to predict how it will be measured, gamed, and affected by the policies under consideration. The reply was simply "vote on values, bet on beliefs". I take it the sentence is meant to divide the labour between voters and prediction markets, but I suspect it bundles several different claims together. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan might be making — about whether values and beliefs can be cleanly separated, about what the betting is supposed to contribute, and about where democratic legitimacy comes from — and say for each whether it is fairly harmless, genuinely controversial, or just false? Most importantly, which of those readings would actually answer my original objection, and which would leave it standing?
