I'm running a reading group on Taurek's 'Should the Numbers Count?' and I want to start with a sorting quiz that places people into positions on the debate before they recognise the labels. Could you build a decision tree where each branch is a yes/no question about a concrete rescue case, and the leaves are named positions in the debate?

The positions I want covered: straightforward aggregation (always save the many), Taurek's coin-flip view, weighted lottery, Scanlon-style contractualism, and Kamm's pairwise comparison view. If there are other coherent positions that fall out of the logical space, include them and name them if they have names.

The questions should use short dilemma cases — lifeboat scenarios, triage, that sort of thing — and should be answerable by someone who's never read the literature. No question should mention Taurek, aggregation, or any other term of art. The point is to sort people by what they'd actually do or endorse in cases, not by which philosopher they recognise.

Also flag: any pairs of positions the tree can't cleanly seperate (and what extra question would be needed), and any logically possible combinations of answers that no one defends, with a brief note on why — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Aim for about six questions. The audience is grad students in philosophy but not ethics specialists. And don't just give me a list of questions — I want the full tree with branching logic and a scoring key if it's a quiz rather than a pure flowchart.
