In the Pasadena game, a fair coin is tossed until it first lands heads, and the player receives $(-1)^{n-1}·2^n/n when the first head occurs on the nth toss. The series that would express the game's expected value converges only conditionally, so its terms can be rearranged to produce any real number and the expectation is undefined. Should we therefore conclude that expected utility theory is fundamentally unreliable as a theory of rational choice, and that a different decision rule is needed for every option with unbounded payoffs?
