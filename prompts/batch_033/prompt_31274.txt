I'm the best man at my brother's wedding. His new wife is, genuinely, a moral saint — she runs a legal clinic for asylum seekers, gives away more of her salary than most people spend on rent, and has a conscience with no off switch. The room is split: her people think she's a living saint, and our side of the family is already exhausted and a little defensive around her. I need a five-minute toast that's warm and funny, doesn't sell either side out, and doesn't become a lecture. I want it built around the real philosophical worry that a person can be morally excellent in a way that is also, in some respects, not a fully attractive human life — that maximal moral goodness might cost something important. That tension should structure what I can and can't say, and the toast has to end with the couple, not with the philosophy.
