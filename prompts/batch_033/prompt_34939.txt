I'm a science journalist and I've been sitting in on a philosophy of science conference (don't ask). One of the speakers keeps invoking "The history of science is a graveyard of theories" like it's a mic drop. The realists in the room roll their eyes and counter that some parts of old theories get retained, or that we're converging on the truth, or whatever. But it got me thinking—what is that slogan actually claiming? It seems like it could be doing a bunch of different things: a boring truism ("past scientists were wrong about some stuff"), a genuine inductive argument ("our current theories will probably be overturned too"), or something even stronger about underdetermination. Can you lay out the distinct theses someone might be expressing when they say that line, and say which of them actually threaten scientific realism and which are compatible with it? I want to know if the realist should be losing sleep or if the slogan is mostly rhetorical.
