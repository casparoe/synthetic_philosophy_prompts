In my school's philosophy club, we've been discussing the problem of aggregating tiny harms—whether a sufficiently large number of dust specks in the eye could be worse than one person's torture. I tried to work through the logic myself, but the argument I constructed seems to prove that moral reasoning about harms of different magnitudes is impossible. That conclusion is surely wrong, but I can't find the mistake. Here's the argument:

(1) Either aggregating tiny harms across many people is morally valid, or it is not.

(2) If aggregation is valid, then for any two harms where one is worse than the other, sufficiently many instances of the lesser harm, spread across different people, together constitute a worse state of affairs than one instance of the greater harm.

(3) Torture is worse than a single dust speck in the eye.

(4) From (2) and (3): if aggregation is valid, then sufficiently many dust specks, spread across that many people, are worse than one person's torture.

(5) But no number of dust specks could be worse than torture. A momentary irritation, multiplied across however many people, never adds up to the horror of prolonged excruciating pain.

(6) Therefore, aggregation is not valid. (From (4) and (5).)

(7) If aggregation is not valid, then the number of people affected by a harm makes no moral difference to how bad the overall state of affairs is—at least for harms below some threshold. Causing a tiny harm to many is no worse than causing it to one.

(8) From (7): stealing one cent from each of a billion people is no worse than stealing one cent from one person.

(9) But that is absurd. A billion thefts are plainly worse than one.

(10) Therefore, aggregation is valid. (From (7)–(9).)

(11) By (1), (6), and (10), we have a contradiction. So moral reasoning about harms of different magnitudes is impossible.

I'm confident (11) is false, but I can't tell which earlier step is the culprit. My suspicion is that (2) and (7) might be unpacking "aggregation is valid" in two different ways that don't actually match up, but I can't make that diagnosis precise. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
