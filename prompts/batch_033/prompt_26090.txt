I've been reading about the distinction between doing harm and merely allowing harm — the killing versus letting die debate — for a couple of years now, mostly through online discussions, popular philosophy books, and whatever papers I can find without a university login. What strikes me is that after decades of argument, the two sides seem to have settled into positions that neither advance nor retreat. One side says there is a deep moral difference between, say, a doctor who injects a lethal drug and a doctor who withholds life support, even when the outcome and the intention are the same. The other side says this is a distinction without a moral difference — that our intuition about a difference is tracking something else, like the salience of the action or our aversion to being the causal agent, and that these are not morally relevant factors. Both sides can point to cases where the other side's view yields uncomfortable conclusions. Both sides have refined their views to handle the counterexamples. And yet nobody seems to change their mind.

I am trying to understand what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it a disagreement about a factual matter — for instance, about how human moral psychology actually works, such that if we understood it better one side would collapse? Is it a disagreement about which starting intuitions to trust, where neither side can argue for its starting point without begging the question? Is it a verbal dispute about what doing means, or about what counts as a moral reason? Or are the two sides answering subtly different questions — one asking about the structure of moral responsibility and the other asking about which actions a decent person would feel differently about? My own suspicion, for what it is worth, is that the impasse persists because each side is answering a different question under the same heading, but I have not been able to articulate what those two questions are with enough precision to satisfy myself.

I want a diagnosis of the structure of the impasse, supported by what each side actually says, not a verdict on who is right. And given your diagnosis, I want to know what — if anything — could count as progress: a new kind of thought experiment, an empirical finding from psychology, a conceptual distinction that has not been drawn clearly enough, or perhaps an acknowledgment that the question as currently framed is ill-posed and should be replaced with something else.
