I'm a second-year PhD student and I have to put together my dissertation prospectus this term, and the area I keep getting pulled back into is the metaphysics of dispositions — specifically the mess around finks and masks. The basic problem as I understand it: the simple counterfactual analysis says x is disposed to manifest M in response to stimulus S iff, were S to occur, x would M. Finks (things that destroy the disposition when the stimulus occurs) and masks (things that block the manifestation while the disposition stays in place) are usually treated as two different kinds of counterexample to that analysis. But when I try to sort actual cases into the two boxes, the sorting depends a lot on how I describe the causal story and how finely I individuate the stimulus and background conditions — and I've started to suspect the fink/mask distinction isn't tracking two kinds of worldly interference so much as two ways I'm regimenting the case. My half-formed idea: once you fix a precise way of specifying the stimulus and background, the two classes collapse into eachother, and the real work is being done by the regimentation, not by anything in the dispositions themselves.
