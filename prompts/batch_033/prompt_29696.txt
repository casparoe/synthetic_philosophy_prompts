I hold the view that there is no coherent way to define "low impact" or "avoiding side effects" for an AI system without at least implicitly specifying what the system is supposed to achieve — that is, without solving the value specification problem. The core thought is that a side effect is a change to the world that is not among the intended changes, and "intended" is defined relative to a goal or value function. Any putatively goal-agnostic impact measure — for example, one that penalizes the agent for reducing its ability to achieve a wide range of possible goals, or for changing the reachability of future states relative to a baseline — must still choose which features of the world to preserve, which baseline to compare against, which possible goals to average over, and how to weight them. Each such choice either smuggles in values or leaves the measure exploitable, inert, or useless. I want to know what would count against this view, not merely as a criticism but as evidence that should move someone who holds it. Which formal results, constructed measures, or arguments about the structure of side effects would be decisive, and which would be merely awkward? What does my view predict about the behavior of proposed impact measures that the rival view — that goal-agnostic low impact is definable — would not predict, and how well do the measures actually proposed fare against those predictions? I would be moved most by an explicit construction: a formally specified impact measure that provably tracks side effects across a wide range of environments without encoding a goal, and that cannot be gamed into either paralysis or catastrophic action. I also want an honest assessment of whether the view has been insulated: have I defined "side effect" and "full specification" in a way that no possible measure could satisfy, and if so, is that a problem for a view of this kind, or a feature of it? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which of them a defender of my view should concede as counting against it.
