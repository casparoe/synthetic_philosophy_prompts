I'm a software engineer at a startup building an internal tool that ranks feature proposals by expected value. We keep running into a problem: someone says "if we build this, there's a 0.001% chance it goes viral and we make a billion dollars," and the EV formula says we should take it seriously, but obviously that's insane. I've heard this called Pascal's mugging and I know there's a philosophical literature on it, but I don't have time to read it and I need something I can actually use.

I want a decision procedure — a checklist or flowchart in words — that my team can run when a proposal involves a tiny probability of a massive payoff. What questions should we ask, in what order? At what point do we reject the proposal? Which steps are judgment calls where we can't just compute an answer? And what kinds of Pascal's mugging cases does the procedure handle versus which ones does it implicitly punt on?

Run it on two concrete examples: (1) a feature with a 0.001% estimated chance of 10x-ing our revenue, and (2) a security patch with a 0.0001% chance of preventing a data breach that would kill the company. I want to see where the procedure gives different answers for these and why.

One other thing: if the procedure would tell me to reject the security patch, say so upfront. I need to know whether I'm signing up to ignore tail risks I actually care about.
