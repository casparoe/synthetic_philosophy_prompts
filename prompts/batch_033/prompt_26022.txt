ok so i'm a grad student in phil of mind and i've been trying to figure out whether my views actually fit together or whether i've been patchworking stuff from different authors without noticing the seams. here's where i'm at:

(1) i'm an eliminative materialist about propositional attitudes — beliefs, desires, hopes, fears, the whole folk-psychological ontology. they're theoretical posits of a bad theory and neuroscience won't vindicate them any more than physics vindicated phlogiston.

(2) i'm a physicalist. whatever exists is physical or constituted by physical stuff. no spooky dualism.

(3) i think phenomenal consciousness is real. there's something it's like to smell coffee or to be in pain, and that's not an illusion and it's not going to be eliminated by mature neuroscience. i'm a realist about qualia.

(4) i think we should keep using belief/desire talk in everyday life and in the social sciences. it's got enormous predictive power and we'd be crippled without it, even if it doesn't carve nature at the joints.

(5) i think the representational theory of mind is roughly correct — cognition involves the manipulation of internal states that have semantic content, that represent features of the world. this is how we should do cognitive science.

(6) i think moral responsibility requires that agents literally have beliefs and desires (not just that we usefully talk as if they do). so if eliminativism is true, then nobody is morally responsible in the sense that would justify retributive punishment.

i've been going back and forth on whether this is a coherent package. (1) and (3) feel like they might be in tension — if folk psychology is a failed theory, why should i trust the deliverances of introspection about qualia? but maybe that's a different faculty. (1) and (5) worry me more — representational states with semantic content sounds an awful lot like beliefs. and then there's the whole thing with (4) and (6): if i'm using belief talk instrumentally in everyday life, do i also get to use responsibility talk instrumentally, and does that undercut the eliminativist point?

could you go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, how bad each tension is, and what the cheapest revision would be? i want to know if there's a single change that would fix most of the problems, or if i need to rethink the whole thing. rank the tensions by severity rather than just listing them all as equally bad.
