You are a professional metaphysician preparing to moderate a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who defends mereological universalism as her answer to the special composition question: she holds that for any plurality of objects whatever, however scattered or gerrymandered, there is a further object they compose. Write eight to ten questions that go beyond asking her to restate the view. Include at least one question on each of the following, each with a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give: (1) the charge that universalism is ontologically profligate, and the standard reply that the fusion of some things is nothing over and above those things; (2) the argument from vagueness, including whether it proves too much and whether it rests on a contestable premise about the impossibility of vague existence; and (3) whether universalism genuinely answers the special composition question or instead dissolves it, and what, if anything, remains of that question once composition is unrestricted. End with one question about what evidence or consideration could make her abandon universalism. Think through the dialectic carefully before writing, and press where the literature presses rather than asking generic or biographical questions.
