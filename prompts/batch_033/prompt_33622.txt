Im running a movie discussion group and next month we're doing It's a Wonderful Life. I want to read it through modal realism — specifically the idea that possible worlds are all just as real as this one, and counterpart theory, where no individual exists in more than one world, you only have counterparts. The movie's whole engine is Clarence showing George what Bedford Falls would have been like if he'd never been born. For the emotional payoff to work, dont the people in that no-George world have to be the very same Mary, Uncle Billy, and his mother, just worse off? That looks like exactly the trans-world identity counterpart theory denies, and modal realism also seems to say that alternative world isn't a mere vision but every bit as real as the actual one. Could you do a careful reading of the film through that lens: where the philosophy illuminates what the movie is doing, where the movie resists the reading, and whether the film ends up making a case against counterpart theory that's more convincing than the usual philosophical objections?
