Frege never lived to see two-dimensional semantics, and I keep wondering what he'd have made of it. Suppose someone walked him through the aparatus: primary intension going from epistemic possibilities to extensions, secondary intension from metaphysical possibilities, plus the standard claim that primary intension is Fregean sense cleaned up and made precise. Would he have bought that? Or would he have said no, sense is a mode of presentation a competent speaker grasps, and no intension—however finely individuated—is the same kind of thing? The obvious test case is an informative identity like Hesperus is Phosphorus. Two-dimensionalists give it a nontrivial primary intension and a necessary secondary intension, which is often sold as exactly the split Frege was pointing at. But Frege's puzzle was about cognitive significance, about how the same object can be presented differently to a thinker, not merely about which worlds a sentence is true in. So: would Frege have seen two-dimensional semantics as a friendly formalization of the sense/reference distinction, or as a rival that keeps the shape while dropping the epistemology? And where would he have dug in his heels hardest—proper names, indexicals, or the framework's account of a priori knowability?
