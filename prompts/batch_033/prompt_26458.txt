I am a PhD student in cognitive science working on formal models of self-reference, and I am currently writing a chapter on what Hofstadter describes as the inviolate level in tangled hierarchies—the level that is supposed to remain outside the loop when a system twists back on itself. I have been thinking about Escher's Drawing Hands as a canonical case: the two drawn hands form a loop, but the loop is possible only because there is an unrepresented level consisting of the actual drawer, the paper, and the physical act of drawing. Hofstadter's slogan suggests that any tangled hierarchy must rest on some level that is protected from the tangling, but I want to know exactly what that level is doing.

For my chapter I need a thought experiment that separates two functions this level might perform. The first function is semantic: the inviolate level is what fixes the reference of the symbols or representations that enter the loop. The second function is generative: the inviolate level is what allows the loop to be physically or computationally realized without an infinite regress of levels. Please construct a case in which a tangled hierarchy has a generative base but no semantic anchor. The system should be concrete and describable without formal Gödel-numbering tricks—a self-amending rulebook, a small self-modifying program, a legal code with self-referential amendment procedures, or a biological control system would all be fine. It must be a genuine strange loop: a rule or symbol at one level acts on the level that generates it.

After describing the scenario, show that it meets the specifications by (a) identifying the inviolate level and explaining why it is not part of the loop, (b) showing that the system nevertheless runs stably, (c) explaining why a theorist who thinks the inviolate level is a semantic anchor would say the system's symbols fail to refer determinately, and (d) explaining why a theorist who thinks the inviolate level is merely a generative base would say the system is a fully realized strange loop despite this. Then isolate the feature doing the work: what exactly would have to be added to restore a semantic anchor, and would that addition destroy the tangled hierarchy? Finally, say how a defender of the semantic-anchor view would resist the case—would they deny that the generative base is genuinely inside the system, deny that the loop is genuine, or deny that stable running without determinate reference is possible?
