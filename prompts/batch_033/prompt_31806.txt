im the student rep on my schools conduct board and we have a case that nobody can agree on. two juniors, Dan and Chris, skipped class last week and threw rocks off the pedestrian bridge that goes over the road next to the school, basically a dare to see who could hit a car. they threw at the same time, same kind of rock, same everything. neither one was more careful or more reckless than the other, neither has any record, and they both admitted it. Dan's rock went through a windshield, the driver swerved into a pole and broke her arm, and her parents are already calling the school. Chris's rock landed on the shoulder and did zero damage. our handbook says discipline should be proportionate to the harm caused, and half the board wants to expel Dan and give Chris a few days of detention, because a real person is in the hospital and the school has to answer for that. the other half says that's crazy: they did the same thing with the same mindset, the only difference is where a rock happened to land, and we shouldn't punish someone for a coin flip.

I keep going back and forth. it feels wrong to treat them the same because the driver is hurt and the outcome has to count for something; but it also feels wrong to give one kid a worse punishment when the only thing separating him from the other is luck. my philosophy teacher said this is basically the problem of moral luck, but she refused to tell me the answer. i need to circulate a short written opinion before our vote on thursday, and the board will actually act on it. it should recommend a concrete decision — same punishment or different, and what specifically — and it has to honestly deal with the strongest argument for the side im recommending against. no fence-sitting.
