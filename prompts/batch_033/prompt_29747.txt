I'm a high school student, and our philosophy club has spent most of this year returning to the experience machine thought experiment without ever getting anywhere. The setup we use is the standard Nozick-style one: a machine that can give you a life of experiences indistinguishable from the real thing — real-seeming achievements, friendships, discoveries — while your body floats in a tank; you can't tell from the inside that anything is illusory, and the machine is guaranteed to keep working for your whole life. Every discussion produces the same two camps. One says plugging in is rational, or at least not obviously irrational, because what matters is how a life goes from the inside, and the machine matches any real life on exactly that score. The other says they would not plug in, because the tank life would not actually be lived: the friendships and achievements would be fake, and that matters even if it never feels different. Both sides agree on the facts of the setup, and both usually concede the other side's strongest points, yet nobody moves. I've begun to suspect the two camps are not answering the same question — that one is answering a question about well-being or happiness and the other is answering a question about authenticity or contact with reality, with the phrase "good life" doing too much work. But I can also see other explanations: maybe it's a bedrock clash of intuitions, or maybe some hidden empirical fact about how we actually evaluate lives is driving one side. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself, not a verdict on whether to plug in.
