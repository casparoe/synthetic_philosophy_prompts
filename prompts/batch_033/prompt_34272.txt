I've been trying to get clear on what I actually think about the problem of evil, and my gut reactions to different cases are all over the place. I'd like you to help me figure out if there's a principle (or a small set of them) that captures my verdicts, and if not, which ones I should reconsider.

Here are the cases:

- A six-year-old dying of leukemia. I can't see how this is compatible with a loving God. This is the hardest one for me.

- The evolutionary process — millions of years of predation, disease, starvation before any humans showed up. This also seems like a serious problem, maybe even worse than the child case in a way, since none of the standard free-will or soul-making moves can reach back that far.

- Someone who goes through a brutal addiction, hits bottom, and then becomes a counselor who helps hundreds of others. I don't think this suffering counts against God. The good that came from it seems to redeem it.

- The Holocaust. Clearly incompatible. No amount of post-hoc good could justify that.

- A deer that dies slowly and alone in a forest fire, and no one ever knows. This bothers me a lot — no observer, no moral lesson, nothing.

- Someone who freely chooses to harm others and causes real suffering. I don't think this kind of evil counts against God. Free will seems like a genuine answer here.

- A child born with a condition that causes constant pain and dies in infancy, no chance for growth or meaning. Feels like the leukemia case — can't square it.

- The pain of childbirth. Doesn't bother me at all as a theological problem. It's pain with a point, and it's temporary.

- The sheer scale of it — billions of sentient beings in pain right now. Even if each individual case could be explained away, the quantity seems impossible to justify.

There seems to be a pattern here about whether the suffering enables a greater good that couldn't be had without it. But then why does the evolutionary case bother me so much? If complex life is the good that evolution's suffering buys, shouldn't my verdict on it match my verdict on the addiction case? Or am I smuggling in the assumption that God could have made complex life some other way, without the predation? And I'm not sure whether the scale worry is a separate principle or just an intensifier on the others.

What principle or principles best fit these verdicts? Which ones do I probably have to give up if I want consistency? And what does the resulting view say about a case I haven't mentioned: a universe where sentient life never evolves and there's no suffering at all — is that a better world than ours, by the lights of my own verdicts?
