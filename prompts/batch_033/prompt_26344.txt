ok so I've been going back and forth on many-worlds for like a year and I think I've finally settled on a cluster of views but I have this nagging sense that they might not all fit together and I'd rather find out now than when my advisor asks me about it. here's roughly where I am:

(1) I'm an Everettian — the wavefunction is the whole ontology, it always evovles unitarily, measurement is just decoherence selecting a basis, and all branches are equally real. no collapse, no hidden variables, no extra structure.

(2) I think the Born rule isn't just a phenomenological add-on; probabilty in MWI is genuinely meaningful. It's not that I'm uncertain about which branch I'll be in — I know they all happen. But it still makes sense to say a measurement outcome is "more probable" in some non-epistemic sense.

(3) I find the Deutsch-Wallace decision-theoretic argument roughly persuasive: a rational agent who cares about their successors across branches should weight their welfare by the branch amplitudes when making decisions under branching. so the Born rule falls out of rationality plus the physics.

(4) I think self-locating uncertainty in MWI is incoherent — before a measurement, asking "which branch will I be in" is like asking which H2O molecule in a glass of water is "the water". all post-measurement versions of me are equally me, and there's no further fact about which one I am.

(5) Nevertheless, I think I should bet in accordance with the Born rule — if you offer me a bet that pays $100 on spin-up and costs $1 on spin-down, with unequal amplitudes, I should take it or decline it based on the expected value computed with Born weights. this isn't just a pragmatic "might as well" thing, it's genuinely what rationaliy requires.

(6) Relatedly, I don't think quantum suicide gives you immortality. the copies of me that keep surviving in ever-rarer branches don't get to feel smug about it, because the measure of those branches is vanishingly small and that matters normatively — it's not just about whether *some* successor survives, it's about how much of your future welfare is at stake.

(7) As a background commitment, I'm a Humean about chance: chances aren't fundamental properties, they're whatever the Best System of the Humean mosaic says they are. I think this should actually make MWI easier to square with probabilty, not harder, because in MWI the mosaic just is the branching structure and the Born weights are the simplest summary of what happens across it.

So I guess my question is: are these seven things consistent? I especially worry about (4) and (5) — if I don't have self-locating uncertainty, what does it even *mean* to say I should bet one way rather than another? But maybe the Deutsch-Wallace argument already assumes (4) and works fine with it, I'm not actually sure how that part goes. And then (7) feels like it should help but I can't articulate exactly how, and I worry it might actually undermine the normativity in (5) — if chances are just summaries, where does the "should" in "you should bet this way" come from? Can you go through the pairs that are in tension, tell me which ones are real problems and which are just apparent, roughly how severe each is, and what the cheapest fix would be if I have to revise something?
