Next month I am giving a colloquium in a joint statistics and philosophy of science seminar, defending a subjectivist interpretation of probability. My thesis is that probability statements are ultimately statements about the degrees of belief, or credences, of an ideally rational agent; there are no mind-independent chances that would make a single-case claim such as the probability that this patient responds to this drug true or false. In the talk I defend three claims: first, that single-case probabilities cannot be identified with long-run frequencies without a reference-class problem, and cannot be identified with physical propensities without leaving the numerical values unexplained; second, that the Dutch book and accuracy-dominance arguments show that rational credences must satisfy the probability axioms; third, that objectivity re-enters through constraints on priors — regularity, symmetry, exchangeability, and calibration — together with convergence theorems showing that priors wash out under shared evidence.

The audience will be mostly applied statisticians trained in frequentist methods, who think of probability as long-run frequency and care about coverage, error rates, calibration, and reproducibility; two or three philosophers of science; and one senior faculty member who defends single-case propensities. The Q&A will run about twenty minutes, and each reply must fit in two minutes or less.

Prepare my Q&A prep sheet as structured data. Return a JSON object with the following fields:

- summary: a two- or three-sentence description of the overall shape of the Q&A and the line I should hold throughout.
- objections: an array of seven to nine objects, ordered from most to least threatening, where threat is the combination of how likely the objection is from this audience and how much damage it does if pressed. Each object has these fields:
  - order: the integer position in the ordering.
  - objection: the objection phrased the way that specific audience member would actually put it, in their vocabulary, so that a frequentist speaks of coverage and long-run error rates rather than credences, and the propensity theorist speaks of features of the world.
  - source: a short label for which kind of audience member raises it.
  - likelihood: one of high, medium, or low.
  - damage: one of high, medium, or low.
  - reply: the two-minute reply I should give.
  - weak_point: where that reply is softest and what a persistent questioner would say next.
  - concede: what I should concede up front to that objection.
  - hold: what I should not concede.
- question_i_hope_nobody_asks: the single question I should fear, phrased as it would actually be asked.
- why_it_is_dangerous: two or three sentences on why that question is the dangerous one, and the best available response if it comes.
- reply_that_must_land: the one reply in the whole Q&A that has to succeed for the position to survive, and why.
