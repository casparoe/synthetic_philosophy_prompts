I have followed the experimental-philosophy debate over the Knobe effect for some time, and I keep having the same reaction I have to many disputes on rationalist blogs and forums: the two sides are mostly arguing past each other, and neither has said in advance what evidence would change its mind. I want the document that would fix that.

Background. The Knobe effect is the following asymmetry. In the standard design, subjects read about a corporate executive who approves a new initiative solely because it will increase profits and who explicitly says he does not care about a side effect the initiative will have on the environment. In one condition the side effect is harmful; in the other it is beneficial. The executive's stated attitude, the causal structure, and the decision procedure are otherwise the same. The initiative goes ahead, the side effect occurs, and subjects are asked whether the executive intentionally brought about that side effect. A large majority say yes in the harm condition, and a large majority say no in the help condition.

Two positions about what this shows have hardened. The conceptual view holds that the asymmetry reflects the folk concept of intentional action: ordinary judgments of whether someone acted intentionally are sensitive to the normative status of the side effect, so moral or normative evaluation is a genuine input to the concept rather than a contaminant. The deflationary view holds that the asymmetry is a performance or pragmatic artifact. Deflationists disagree among themselves about the mechanism—some favor blame, some a general negativity bias, some conversational implicatures, some the fact that only the harm vignette involves accepting an environmental cost—but they agree that the effect does not reveal the structure of the concept of intentional action.

Write the joint statement these two camps could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. It should contain:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides accept about the effect itself, about which features of the design are and are not essential, and about the constraints any adequate account must satisfy. The claims should be substantive rather than vacuous, and neither side should feel that the other has smuggled in a conclusion.

2. A precise statement of the residual disagreement, with each side's own position phrased so that its proponents would accept the wording.

3. A disagreements table. For each crux—a sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side—give a concrete experiment, manipulation, or argument both sides agree bears on that crux, and record each side's prediction about it in advance.

4. A short note on whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal, and why.
