In contemporary epistemology, the Meno problem—why knowledge is more valuable than mere true belief—has generated a tangle of proposed solutions, but I rarely see anyone lay out the full specification of what a solution must actually deliver before arguing for their own. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of the value of knowledge must explain, which cases it must get right, which distinctions it must respect, and which results from epistemology it must be consistent with. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. Flag which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are commitments of particular camps (e.g., virtue epistemologists, process reliabilists, knowledge-firsters). Identify any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most work to exclude the most-discussed proposals. If you take the swamping problem to be the central challenge, show why by locating it within the broader list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring contenders.
