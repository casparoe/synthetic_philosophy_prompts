A Bongard problem, for readers unfamiliar with the term, consists of two panels: a left panel with six figures that all exemplify some rule, and a right panel with six figures that do not. The solver's task is to induce the rule. The philosophical puzzle is that any finite set of positive and negative examples underdetermines the rule: infinitely many rules are consistent with the data, yet human solvers reliably converge on one — or at least on a narrow range. The question is what guides that convergence.
