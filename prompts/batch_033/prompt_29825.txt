I was at a philosophy meetup last week where two people got into a long argument about the Sleeping Beauty problem, and they asked me to settle it afterwards. I have read some of the literature and I still find it genuinely hard to adjudicate, so I am turning to you.

For context: the standard setup is that Beauty goes to sleep Sunday night. A fair coin is tossed. If it lands heads, she is woken Monday and the experiment ends. If tails, she is woken Monday, given a drug that erases her memory of the waking, and woken again Tuesday. She knows the protocol in advance. Each time she wakes, she is asked: what is your credence that the coin landed heads?

The argument went roughly like this. Anna opened by defending the halfer position. She said Beauty's credence on Sunday is obviously one half. When she wakes, she learns nothing new — she knew all along she would be woken at least once, and every waking feels exactly the same. Since no new evidence has been acquired, her credence should not shift. It stays at one half. Ben countered that this misses the self-locating character of the question. When Beauty wakes, she gains the information "I am awake now," and in the tails world there are twice as many "nows" as in the heads world. That is new evidence, and it shifts her credence to one third. He added a betting argument: if Beauty bets on heads at even odds every time she wakes, she loses money in expectation, which shows that one half cannot be right. Anna replied that the betting argument is question-begging because it assumes each waking gets an independent bet. If instead Beauty places one bet that pays out once per coin-toss rather than once per waking, the fair odds revert to even. The betting argument does not decide between halfing and thirding — it just reveals which question you think you are answering. Ben came back with what he called the technicolour argument. Suppose the experimenters show Beauty a different colour each waking — say red on Monday-heads, blue on Monday-tails, green on Tuesday-tails. When she sees red, she knows it is heads. When she sees blue or green, she knows it is tails. But the crucial point: even before she sees a colour, she knows that after seeing it she will assign credence zero or one to heads. If she now assigns one half, then she expects her future credence to be zero half the time and one the other half. If she assigns one third, then she expects her future credence to be zero with probability two thirds and one with probability one third. Which better reflects what she actually knows now? Ben said one third does a better job of anticipating her own future updates. Anna pushed back: the reflection principle is not sacrosanct in cases of forgetting. Beauty knows she will have her memory wiped — of course her future self will disagree with her present self. That is not a paradox; it is just what amnesia does. The real question is what credences are warranted given her current evidence, not whether those credences cohere across memory wipes.

At that point they turned to me and said I had been quiet too long. I said I needed to think about it and promised to get back to them.

Please adjudicate their argument move by move. For each exchange, say who has the better of it on that specific point, and why. Then give an overall assessment: is one of them more right, or are they talking past each other in a way that makes the debate less substantive than it appears? I would also appreciate it if you could flag any arguments neither of them made that you think would have shifted the balance.

A few requests on format: please write in continuous prose — no headings, no bullet points, no bold or tables — the way you would write a letter. And please use British English throughout.
