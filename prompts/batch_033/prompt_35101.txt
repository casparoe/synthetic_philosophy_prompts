My friend said there's a famous essay by Susan Wolf that shows this kind of person — what she calls a moral saint — would have to be tedious and humorless, with no real interests or hobbies of their own, because every decision get's run through the filter of whether it's the most moral thing they could be doing, and they could never do anything just because it's fun. He said Wolf's conclusion is that we shouldn't try to be moral saints and that morality is basically overrated as a guide to living. I read the essay ages ago and remember it being more careful, but I can't say exactly how. Is his summary fair? Also, for the novel: does she think a maximally good person is actually boring, or just that we'd probably find them boring, and does she treat those as different things?
