I've been reading Dennett on and off for years, and I keep coming back to the same frustration. The Cartesian theater critique — the idea that there's no central place in the brain where "it all comes together" for consciousness — seems absolutely devastating to me. Like, once you see it, you can't unsee it. And yet, thirty-plus years after Consciousness Explained, the debate seems completely stuck.

Here's what I mean. On one side, you have people (Dennett and his followers, some enactivists, some philosophers of cognitive science) who say that the whole idea of a "unified field of consciousness" or a "place where experience happens" is a leftover from dualism that we need to purge. On the other side, you have a huge number of neuroscientists and philosophers who don't exactly say "there is a Cartesian theater" — nobody literally believes in a tiny homunculus watching a screen — but who talk in ways that seem to presuppose it. They talk about "binding" as if there's a moment when seperate features get glued together into a single representation that is then "presented to consciousness." They talk about "conscious access" as if there's a gateway through which some representations pass into the light. Global workspace theory, which is probably the dominant neuroscientific approach to consciousness right now, has this whole architecture of a central workspace where information gets "broadcast" — and I've read Dennett's responses to GWT, and the GWT people's responses back, and it feels like they're just talking past each other.

What I can't figure out is what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it a factual disagreement about brain architecture that could be settled by more data? Is it a disagreement about what counts as an explanation of consciousness — about what the phenomenon is that needs explaining? Is it a verbal dispute about what "consciousness" means, where Dennett is trying to explain something different from what the GWT people are trying to explain? Or is it something else entirely — maybe a disagreement about what it would take to "explain" consciousness at all, where the two sides have different stopping conditions for when an explanation is good enough?

I've heard people say that Dennett "explained consciousness away" rather than explaining it, and I've heard Dennett say that the people who accuse him of this are clinging to a phantom. But I don't think either side has ever really articulated what the other side would have to show to change their minds. The debate just sort of cycles.

So: diagnose this. What kind of disagreement is the Cartesian theater / multiple drafts debate, at bottom? Is it substantive, verbal, methodological, or something else? And given your diagnosis, what would actually move it — a specific empirical result, a conceptual distinction, a reframing of the question, or is this just one of those debates where the participants have different fundamental intuitions and nobody should expect resolution?

I'd like you to take the role of a professional philosopher of mind who's followed this literature closely. Please think carefully — maybe start by brainstorming the different possible ways of characterizing the disagreement, then narrow down to your best diagnosis with reasons, and give me an honest answer about whether progress is possible.
