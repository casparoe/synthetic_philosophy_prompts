I'm in an upper-division epistemology course, and we've been working through the literature on epistemic closure and Nozick's tracking theory. I find myself stuck on a meta-question about the debate itself.

The basic clash is familiar: Nozick's tracking theory denies that knowledge is closed under known entailment, because sensitivity to the truth can fail across an entailment — I can know P and know that P entails Q, yet fail to know Q if my belief that Q would persist even if Q were false. This is how Nozick handles the skeptic without conceding that we don't know we have hands. The cost, of course, is giving up closure. Closure defenders argue that this cost is unacceptable — that it makes knowledge too fragile for ordinary reasoning, that it licenses abominable conjunctions like knowing you have hands while failing to know you're not a handless brain in a vat, and that we should look elsewhere for a response to skepticism.

What puzzles me is that after decades of back-and-forth — sensitivity versus safety, tracking versus relevant alternatives, the zebra case, the lottery puzzle, various counterexamples to both sides — the debate seems to have reached a kind of equilibrium where neither camp moves. Both sides acknowledge the pull of the other's central intuition. So I want to ask: what kind of disagreement is this, at bottom? Is it a substantive dispute about the nature of knowledge, or a clash of bedrock intuitions that neither side can argue for? Is it a disagreement about what an epistemological theory is supposed to do — block radical skepticism versus systematize our ordinary knowledge ascriptions? Could it be a merely verbal dispute about the word knows, or are the two sides answering different questions under a single heading? I'd like a diagnosis that engages with the actual arguments on both sides, and an honest assessment of what, if anything, would count as progress — a new kind of case, a formal result, a sharper conceptual distinction — or whether we should expect the impasse to persist. Please write your answer in American English.
