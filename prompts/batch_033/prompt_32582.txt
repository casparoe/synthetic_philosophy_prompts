The absent-minded driver problem and the Sleeping Beauty problem are both decision-theoretic puzzles that turn on self-locating uncertainty—an agent's uncertainty about their temporal position within a branching or sequential structure. Yet they are standardly treated in separate literatures, with Sleeping Beauty framed primarily as a puzzle about credence assignment and the absent-minded driver as a puzzle about the rationality of mixed strategies under imperfect recall. I would like a systematic comparison of the two problems. Specifically: do the standard positions on one problem commit one to corresponding positions on the other? For instance, does the thirder view on Sleeping Beauty have a natural analogue in the absent-minded driver case, and does the case for or against the use of mixed strategies in the driver problem carry any implications for the halfer/thirder debate? More broadly, are these two problems best understood as distinct manifestations of a single underlying puzzle about self-locating belief and rational choice, or do the structural differences between them—such as the presence or absence of memory loss, and the role of planning versus updating—justify treating them as separate phenomena?
