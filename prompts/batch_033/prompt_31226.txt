Rudolf Carnap died in 1970, several decades before "AI alignment" became a recognized research program. Reconstruct, as precisely as you can, the position Carnap would have taken on the central questions of contemporary AI alignment, arguing from his documented philosophical commitments rather than from anything he said about machine intelligence.

Take the following commitments as the relevant starting points: (i) his distinction between theoretical questions, which are settled by evidence and logic, and practical decisions, which are settled by choice or attitude; (ii) his method of explication, which replaces vague pre-theoretic concepts with precise substitutes chosen for similarity, exactness, fruitfulness, and simplicity; (iii) his view that value judgments express practical attitudes rather than assert facts; (iv) his principle of tolerance, according to which there is no uniquely correct linguistic framework and the choice among frameworks is a practical matter; and (v) his left-wing political sympathies and his conviction that scientific methods should be brought to bear on social and economic planning.

Address at least the following questions, and say explicitly where the commitments underdetermine an answer.

1. Would Carnap have accepted the standard framing of alignment as the theoretical problem of learning or inferring human values and encoding them in an objective function, or would he have reclassified most of it as a practical problem of choosing a goal? What theoretical residue, if any, would he have left to alignment research?

2. How would Carnap have applied explication to the notion of a reward model or a formal specification of human intent? Which of his criteria for a good explicatum would do the most work, and what would he identify as the irreducible cost of replacing the ordinary concept with any formal substitute?

3. Would Carnap have regarded the empirical aggregation of revealed human preferences, or the training of a model to discover what humans really want, as a category mistake, a legitimate heuristic, or a disguised exercise of practical choice? What follows from his non-cognitivism about values for the project of value learning?

4. How would the principle of tolerance extend to the choice of an AI's goal architecture? Would Carnap have been a pluralist about aligned systems, and what constraints, if any, would he have placed on the frameworks that may be chosen?

5. Given his politics, would Carnap have seen alignment primarily as a scientific, engineering, or political problem, and what would he have regarded as the characteristic failure modes of leaving it to either market competition or centralized planning?

End by stating the single claim about AI alignment you think Carnap would have defended most firmly, and the one point on which his own commitments would have left him most visibly unsettled.
