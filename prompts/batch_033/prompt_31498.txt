I spent my career in a lab doing spectroscopy and now that I'm retired I've been reading my way through decision theory, and the thing I can't shake is evidential cooperation in large worlds. Before I read any more of it, give me the spec: what does a theory of ECL actually have to deliver to count as adequate? Not a survey of positions — the desiderata. Tie each requirement to a concrete case or consideration, mark which are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's taste, and flag any subset that can't be jointly satisfied. Then, briefly, say which single requirement does the most to eliminate the usual contenders (plain evidential decision theory, causal decision theory, functional decision theory).

Here's the kind of thing I'd expect to show up, but push back if I'm wrong about whether some of these are genuine requirements. Newcomb's problem, since that's the original standoff between evidential and causal reasoning and any theory of ECL is going to have to take a side or explain why it's a false choice. The twin prisoners' dilemma, where two copies of the same algorithm reason about each other with no causal channel between them. The hitchhiker case where someone helps you only if they predict you'll keep a promise later, when keeping it no longer pays. The reference-class problem: which other agents count as your evidential peers in a large world — a theory that lets you cooperate with anyone logically correlated with you is too loose, and one that lets you cooperate with no one just begs the question. The large-world dependence worry: why should the size of the universe change whether I should vote or keep a promise, and if it does, can the theory say that without making everyday choices hostage to cosmology. The correlation-causation distinction, which has to be respected without simply asserting that only causal consequences matter. Finiteness: if the view needs exact algorithmic similarity or sums over infinitely many copies, it's mathematics, not a decison procedure — and I suspect this one is in tension with taking large worlds seriously at all. And the self-locating part: if I'm one of many copies I need to know which one I am before "acting for the group" even makes sense, so the theory has to say how it plugs into anthropic reasoning rather than dodge it.
