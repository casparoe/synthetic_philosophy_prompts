I've been building game engines for about a decade—mostly RPGs and immersive sims, the kind where you really want the player to feel like they're inside the world. Lately I've been reading about this thing called illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. From what I gather, it says that when we talk about "what it's like" to see red or feel pain, we're not describing a real inner glow or private quality—we're just reporting on a kind of introspective representation that our brain generates, and we mistake the representation for a real property. There's no actual phenomenal consciousness, just the illusion of it.

The thing is, I keep thinking about this in terms of how a game engine works. The player sees a health bar on screen and feels like they "know" their character's health in a direct, qualitative way—the red sliver *feels like* low health. But inside the engine, there's no red sliver. There's just a float variable `currentHealth` and some code that scales a rectangle on the UI layer. The feeling the player has is the UI representation itself, not a perception of some real "health-stuff" in the game world. And the UI isn't lying exactly—it's tracking something real (the variable)—but the way it presents that information has properties (color, position, urgency of the pulse animation) that don't belong to the underlying thing being tracked.

So I want to take this seriously as a translation exercise. I'm not a philosopher, I'm a self-taught programmer, so I want you to restate the core claims of illusionism about phenomenal consciousness in the vocabulary I actually think in—game engines, rendering pipelines, UI layers, game state vs. player-facing representation, and so on. Be as faithful as you can. Don't just use my vocabulary as a cute metaphor; actually try to carry the claims over and see whether they land.

Then I want the residue report. Tell me:

- Which claims translate cleanly, without distortion.
- Which claims you had to stretch or fudge to make them fit my vocabulary, and exactly where the metaphor strains.
- Which claims have no counterpart at all in how game engines work—things the philosophers are saying that simply can't be expressed in terms of state machines, UI layers, and rendering.
- And finally, your honest take: does the stuff that doesn't translate point to a limitation in *my* framework (i.e., the game-engine way of thinking is missing something real), or does it point to the philosophers saying things that are less clear than they think they are?
