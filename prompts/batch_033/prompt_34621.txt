Here's an argument I've been chewing on that seems to prove way too much, but I can't figure out which premise to give up.

(1) The classic money pump: if at a given time you prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A, a trader can take you in a circle — trade B for A plus a fee, then C for B plus a fee, then A for C plus a fee — and you end up where you started, poorer. This reveals intransitive preferences as irrational.

(2) Now consider someone whose preferences change because they learn something. Say at 2pm I prefer the chicken sandwich to the tuna wrap; then I read a sign saying the tuna was caught this morning while the chicken's been sitting out since yesterday; now, at 2:01pm, I prefer the tuna wrap to the chicken sandwich.

(3) If we look at the set of my preferences across that one-minute interval, we find both "chicken > tuna" (at t1) and "tuna > chicken" (at t2). That's an intransitive preference relation when viewed across time.

(4) If intransitivity is irrational, as the money pump suggests, then I've been irrational. But updating your preferences in light of new evidence is about as rational as it gets.

(5) So the money pump argument either applies across time — in which case it condemns rational belief-updating, which is absurd — or it only applies to preferences held at a single moment. But what exactly does "at a single moment" mean? If I can hold "chicken > tuna" in one moment and "tuna > chicken" a minute later without irrationality, why can't I hold "A > B" and "B > C" at t1 and "C > A" at t2 without irrationality, so long as I never hold all three at once? And if that's allowed, can't I just avoid the money pump by never having all three preferences simultaneously, even while my preferences are intransitive across a slightly longer stretch of time?

(6) That would mean the money pump argument doesn't actually rule out intransitive preferences — it just rules out having them all at the exact same instant, which almost nobody does anyway, since we can only entertain a few comparisons at a time.

I want to save the money pump as a genuine constraint. It feels like it's getting at something true. But once I start prying at the "synchronic vs. diachronic" distinction, I worry the whole thing dissolves into the trivial demand that you can't hold contradictory attitudes in a single moment of reflection — which isn't a constraint on preferences at all, just a constraint on consciousness.
