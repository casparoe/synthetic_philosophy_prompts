I hold a paracomplete theory of truth: the Liar sentence is neither true nor false, and this is a genuine semantic status rather than a failure to express a proposition, a context shift, or an ambiguity. I want a map of what would count against this view — not the textbook objections I can already recite, but the defeaters a careful defender of the gap theory would themselves concede as counting against it, ranked from potentially fatal to merely awkward. In particular: what exactly does the strengthened Liar, the sentence that says of itself that it is not true, show, and is there any reply that does not simply relocate the problem into the metalanguage or into the meaning of neither? Which formal or logical results — about bivalence, excluded middle, negation, or fixed-point constructions — genuinely put pressure on the view, and which are red herrings? I would personally find a successful revenge argument the most damaging; tell me whether that is right or whether some collapse argument is worse. Finally, say honestly whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability by its replies to these objections, and if so whether that is a vice or just the usual condition of a theory of truth.
