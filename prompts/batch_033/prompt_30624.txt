Question. State precisely what Bell's theorem rules out. In doing so, distinguish local causality, determinism, and hidden variables. Explain why the experimentally observed violation of Bell inequalities does not show that quantum mechanics permits faster-than-light signaling. Finally, identify which assumption in Bell's derivation a superdeterminist would reject.

Student answer. Bell's theorem shows that no deterministic hidden-variable theory can reproduce the predictions of quantum mechanics. The proof requires determinism, since the hidden variables λ are taken to determine the outcomes A(a, λ) and B(b, λ) uniquely; without that, the inequalities cannot be derived. It also requires realism, understood as the claim that particles have definite properties before measurement. Since quantum mechanics violates the Bell inequalities, at least one of these assumptions must fail. The standard diagnosis is that locality fails: the measurement on one side affects the outcome on the other side, which is why quantum mechanics predicts superluminal signaling between the two wings of the experiment. Bell's theorem therefore shows that Einstein was wrong to regard quantum mechanics as incomplete.
