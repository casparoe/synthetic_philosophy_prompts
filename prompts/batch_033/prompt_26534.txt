I've been going down a bit of a rabbit hole reading econ blogs and watching some YouTube lectures on microeconomic theory — I'm a software engineer by trade, so this is pretty far from my day job, but I find it weirdly fascinating. Anyway, I keep running into this idea of "revealed preference" and I feel like I'm either missing something fundamental or there's a bait-and-switch happening that nobody wants to admit to.

Here's how I understand it: the basic move is that you can't just ask people what they want because people lie, or they don't know, or their stated preferences are cheap talk. So instead you look at what they actually choose when there are real tradeoffs involved. If I say I prefer apples to oranges but I consistently buy oranges when they're the same price, my behavior "reveals" that I actually prefer oranges, whatever I tell myself. So far so good — that's basically just taking choices seriously as evidence.

But then economists seem to go a step further and treat preferences as *nothing over and above* consistent choice patterns. And that's where I get stuck. Because if preferences just *are* whatever you choose, then there's no room for the idea that someone could choose something they don't prefer. Take addiction: I smoke cigarettes, I wish I didn't, I've tried to quit and failed. Under revealed preference, you have to say I *do* prefer smoking — my "true" preference is just whatever my behavior consistently reveals. The whole distinction between what I want and what I end up doing collapses. And the standard response I've seen — "well, you have a preference for immediate gratification that outweighs your preference for long-term health" — feels like it's just redescribing the behavior in preference language after the fact. You can always cook up some set of preferences that rationalizes any choice pattern, which makes the whole thing seem unfalsifiable.

So my genuine question is: why do economists treat revealed preference as if it's a substantive insight about human motivation rather than just a stipulative definition that rules out akrasia by fiat? It seems like they smuggled in a pretty heavy metaphysical claim — that there is no gap between choice and preference — and then act like it fell out of the methodology. Am I misunderstanding what the theory is even trying to do?
