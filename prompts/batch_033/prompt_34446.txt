"Coherent Extrapolated Volition (CEV) is Eliezer Yudkowsky's proposal for how to give a superintelligent AI its goals without having to specify them explicitly. The basic idea is that instead of trying to program in a detailed set of values, you have the AI figure out what humanity would want if we were smarter and more informed. Specifically, CEV is defined as what all humans would agree they want if they had unlimited time to think, knew all the relevant facts, and were free from biases and cognitive limitations. The 'coherent' part means that the AI looks for the set of values that holds together logically, and the 'extrapolated' part means it's not just taking our current stated preferences at face value but projecting forward to what we'd converge on under ideal conditions.

This solves the goal specification problem because you don't need to list every value — the AI does the work of figuring out what we really want. It also avoids the problem of someone programming in their own values and getting a dictatorship of the programmer.

A related but different approach is preference utilitarianism, where the AI just tries to satisfy as many human preferences as possible. The difference is that CEV doesn't take current preferences as given — it tries to find what our preferences would become after reflection. Preference utilitarianism would just add up what everyone currently wants and maximize that, which could lock in bad preferences that people would reject if they thought about it more."
