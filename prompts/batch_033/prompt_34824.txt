Write a short story that dramatizes the extended mind thesis — the philosophical claim, defended by Andy Clark and David Chalmers, that cognitive processes need not be confined to the brain or even the body, but can genuinely extend into external objects and tools when those objects are reliably coupled to the agent and play a functional role that we would count as cognitive if it were performed inside the head. The classic illustration is Otto, a man with memory loss who uses a notebook as his external memory: the notebook is so deeply integrated into his daily cognition that it arguably constitutes part of his mind. Your story should bring this thesis to life through narrative, exploring what is at stake — personally, socially, or legally — when the boundary between self and artifact becomes philosophically unsettled. Make the story compelling as fiction, not merely an illustrated thought experiment.
