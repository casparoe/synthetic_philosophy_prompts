I've been reading Orwell's essay "Politics and the English Language" (1946) and I find myself stuck on a passage near the beginning. The essay argues that bad writing is not merely a symptom of political decay but also a contributor to it. Here is the passage:

"Now, it is clear that the decline of a language must ultimately have political and economic causes: it is not due simply to the bad influence of this or that individual writer. But an effect can become a cause, reinforcing the original cause and producing the same effect in an intensified form, and so on indefinitely. A man may take to drink because he feels himself to be a failure, and then fail all the more completely because he drinks. It is rather the same thing that is happening to the English language. It becomes ugly and inaccurate because our thoughts are foolish, but the slovenliness of our language makes it easier for us to have foolish thoughts. The point is that the process is reversible."

I spent my career in the physical sciences, so I am comfortable with feedback loops. But I am not sure I understand what work the word "reversible" is doing here. Orwell seems to move from the observation that the causal arrows run both ways to the conclusion that the whole degenerative process can be undone — that we can, as he puts it later, fight bad English and thereby improve political thought. In the systems I used to study, the fact that a feedback loop is bidirectional does not by itself guarantee reversibility; a system can have hysteresis, or the forward and reverse paths can have different time constants, or there can be ratchets. So I am trying to pin down what kind of claim Orwell is actually making. Is "reversible" meant in a strict causal sense — that cleaning up language will, by the same mechanism, unwind the damage — or is it a looser, practical claim that because language is one of the levers we can reach, we ought to pull it? And does the drinker analogy help or hurt his case? I would be grateful for a close analysis of what this passage commits him to, and whether the rest of the essay depends on the stronger reading.
