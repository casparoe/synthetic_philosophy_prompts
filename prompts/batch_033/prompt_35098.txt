I teach a seminar on pragmatism — about 18 undergrads, mostly philosophy majors but a few from poli sci, one 80-minute session. We're about to hit Dewey's claim that democracy isn't just a decision procedure, it's a form of experimental inquiry: you've got a problematic situation, you form hypotheses, you test them in action, you learn from the consequences and revise, and the whole thing only works if everyone affected can participate in the inquiry. I can lecture this and they'll nod along, but I want them to feel why it's actually different from "democracy is voting" and what it costs to actually do it.

Design an in-class activity where the group has to solve a collective problem that can only be cracked through something like experimental inquiry — pooling partial information, forming hypotheses, testing them, revising based on results. The activity should be structured so that if they just vote on what to do or defer to whoever talks loudest, they fail or at least do worse than groups that actually inquire. I want them to notice the structure of what they're doing before I name it.

Details: 80 minutes total including setup and debrief, so the activity itself should run maybe 40-50 minutes. I can print materials. No real stakes needed but there should be some metric of success so they can see whether they did well or poorly. The debrief should map specific moments in the activity onto Dewey's concepts — the problematic situation, hypothesis, experimental testing, consequences as feedback, the role of free communication and diversity of standpoint, warranted assertability vs. certainty. Also tell me what predictably goes wrong when you run this (who dominates, what corners get cut, which groups never get to the insight) and how to handle it in the debrief rather than by intervening during the activity. Be honest about what feature of Dewey's view the activity can't capture — I'm guessing it's something about the open-endedness of real social inquiry or the fact that real problems don't have a known answer the facilitator is hiding.
