write a villanelle from the point of view of an illusionist about phenomenal consciousness. the two refrains should be something like "there is nothing it is like to be me" and "and yet i cannot shake the sense there is." the form should do philosophical work: the way the refrains keep returning, alternating and then meeting in the final two lines, should mirror how the illusion of consciousness keeps reasserting itself even after youve accepted the theory. someone who knows frankishs position should recognize the tension between the theoretical claim (phenomenal properties are an introspective misrepresentation) and the lived experience (the misrepresentation is impossible to shake). it should be accurate enough that a philosopher of mind wouldn't wince, and good enough as a poem that someone who doesnt know the view would still find it unsettling.
