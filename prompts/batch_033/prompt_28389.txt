So I got into it last night with a friend at a bar who’s been reading some Nietzsche, and he kept dropping that line from the Genealogy about there being no doer behind the deed — y’know, the lightning flash thing, where the doer is just a fiction we tack onto the action afterwards. His take was that since there’s no real subject standing behind an action pulling the levers, praise and blame are totally incoherent, full stop. I pushed back, but honestly I found myself flailing because I don’t actually know how many different things Nietzsche (or people who quote that line) might be trying to say. It seems like the slogan could be making a linguistic point, a metaphysical point, a psychological point, or even a political point about how we get people to police themselves — and I doubt they all lead to “blame makes no sense.”

So I’m heading back to that bar next week and I want to come prepared. Can you take that slogan — roughly “no doer behind the deed, the doer’s a fiction we add later” — and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be expressing? I need a menu of readings from the radical to the trivial, each with a quick verdict on whether it’s defensible, and then a clear answer on which ones would actually support my friend’s no-blame conclusion and which ones wouldn’t survive three sips of a beer.
