Write a sonnet in the voice of Johannes de Silentio about Abraham's journey to Mount Moriah. The octave should build the ethical indictment: Abraham is no tragic hero — Agamemnon and Jephthah could speak, could be wept for, could find rest in the universal, but Abraham's act has no such mediation. The volta should turn at the moment the knife is raised: the teleological suspension of the ethical, where the ethical is not canceled but placed in a higher relation to the absolute, a relation that cannot be spoken. The closing couplet should capture the paradox of Abraham's silence — that he cannot speak not because speech is refused but because speech belongs to the universal, and his act is a private relation to God that language would falsify. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader of Fear and Trembling would recognize the distinction between the knight of infinite resignation and the knight of faith, and would see the absurd — the movement of faith by virtue of the absurd — as the poem's operative concept, not a buzzword.
