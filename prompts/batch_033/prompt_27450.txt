I'm a journalist working on a explainer about rules that refer to themselves — the game Nomic, a constitution clause that sets its own amendment procedure, a bylaw that says it can never be repealed, that kind of thing. I want to give readers a flowchart that sorts them by what they actually think about self-amending rules, not by which theory they've heard of.

Could you build me a decision tree like that? The trunk can be something like: "When a rule changes itself, what is actually happening?" Every branch should be a concrete yes/no case or claim where the positions genuinely come apart, not a question about what label someone likes.

Use at least these cases somewhere in the tree: (1) Nomic's rule that lets players change the rules — does it let them change the rule-changing rule itself? (2) A constitution with a clause saying any article, including this one, may be amended by two-thirds — can two-thirds amend that very clause to require unanimity, and if they do, was the vote valid? (3) A bylaw reading "this bylaw may never be repealed" — can a majority validly repeal it?

The leaves should cover roughly these positions: self-amendment is just incoherent because every system needs a fixed hierarchy outside itself; there's an ultimate rule (a grundnorm or rule of recognition) that the system's own procedures can't touch; self-amendment only works if you carefully seperate the act from the rule it changes; self-amendment works but only as a two-step process where the new rule comes into existence after the old one has applied; and a fully reflexive view where a system can amend its own amendment rules, entrenchment and all. Add leaves I'm missing if you think of them, and mark any that no one really defends as "unnamed."

Where the tree can't tell two positions apart, just flag it instead of inventing a fake distinction. And if a combination of answers leads to no leaf, say why — is it incoherent, just unappealing, or unexplored?

Keep it readable for a general ideas audience: six to eight yes/no questions, five to eight leaves. No bibliography or footnotes, I just want the tree and the logic for how it sorts people.
