I run a small online shop that sells refurbished expresso machines. Out of nowhere a guy offered to buy my entire remaining stock at my listed prices, no haggling and no questions about condition. My instinct was to say yes — I've been sitting on this inventory for months and the cash would help. But my co-founder says we should refuse. His reasoning: the buyer clearly thinks he can resell these for more than we're charging, which means he knows something we don't about what they're worth, and anyone who's eager to buy at your listed price is giving you information that your price is too low. If that were right, though, no one could ever sell anything to anyone, since every buyer's willingness to pay would just be evidence that the seller is getting ripped off. I can't tell if he's onto something or if there's a flaw in the principle. What are the conditions under which his reasoning actually holds, do they hold in my situation, and should I take the offer or walk away?
