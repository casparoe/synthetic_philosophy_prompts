I'm preparing a handout for a graduate seminar on the ethics of blame. I'd like a decision tree that maps the logical space of views on when someone has the *standing* to blame. The tree should start from a single question — roughly, whether a correct moral judgment is sufficient for standing, or whether facts about the blamer can defeat it — and branch through successive distinctions until each path reaches a named position.

Please use the following distinctions as branch points, in whatever order you think best captures the logical structure of the debate:

1. Whether standing can be defeated at all, or whether anyone who correctly identifies a wrong thereby has standing to blame.
2. If standing can be defeated, whether the defeating factor is the blamer's own moral record (hypocrisy, complicity) or the blamer's relationship to the wrongdoer, the victim, or the moral community.
3. Within moral-record views, whether hypocrisy (having committed the same kind of wrong) and complicity (being implicated in this very wrong) both defeat standing, or only one of them does.
4. Within relational views, whether the relevant relationship is to the victim, to the wrongdoer, or to the shared moral community.

Each leaf should carry the name of the position — "Universalism," "Hypocrisy Account," "Complicity Account," "Victim-Centered View," "Communal View," or whatever label best captures it — or be marked "unnamed" if the position is logically possible but no one in the literature defends it. For each leaf, add one sentence identifying its most awkward commitment.

Additionally, please flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate: for instance, the tree will distinguish hypocrisy from complicity as grounds for losing standing, but it will not distinguish different accounts of why hypocrisy defeats standing (fairness-based, warrant-based, commitment-based). And note any logically possible combinations of answers that remain unoccupied, with a brief diagnosis of whether the position is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.
