At my organization we are setting the value policy for an AI assistant that will be used by millions of people in many countries, with sharply different moral and religious commitments. One faction argues that we should aggregate: the assistant should act on whatever values would produce the greatest total user satisfaction across all interactions, even when that systematically disserves some minority of users. Another faction insists that the right model is drafting a constitution for a pluralistic society. A liberal constitution, they say, does not pick a winner among religions or ways of life; it fixes neutral ground rules protecting speech, bodily integrity, and exit, and then lets citizens pursue their own conceptions of the good within those limits. By analogy, the assistant should enforce a small set of neutral liberal constraints on every interaction and otherwise defer to each individual user's own values. They conclude that this, and only this, treats all users with equal respect.

I suspect the constitution analogy is carrying the argument, but I want to be fair to it. Please audit the analogy in detail. Give the correspondence table: what is supposed to correspond to the constitution, the citizens, the legislature, the courts, the rights, the private sphere, enforcement, and amendment. Then identify where the mapping breaks down or becomes strained, and separate the disanalogies that matter for the intended conclusion from those that are merely cosmetic. Pay particular attention to what the analogy quietly imports from the political case: a demos whose members are bound by a shared order, a legitimating procedure for choosing the rules, a distinction between public coercion and private conscience, and the assumption that a set of ground rules can be neutral among competing conceptions of the good. Finally, say whether the conclusion — that the assistant should enforce liberal-neutral constraints and otherwise defer to each user's values — survives the audit, and if not, what framework or corrected analogy would do better.
