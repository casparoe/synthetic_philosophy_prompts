I'm running a reading group on vagueness this fall — just six of us, mostly grad students, and we all have strong but totally unarticulated views on this stuff. Before we crack open any papers I want to hand out a sorting quiz, like a "which theory of vagueness do you actually hold?" thing, so we know what we're each bringing to the table. I'm picturing eight or nine questions, each built around a concrete case. The leaves I want: epistemicism, supervaluationism, degree theory (fuzzy logic), and contextualism. If you think nihilism about vagueness earns its own leaf, add it, but only if the questions actually pull it apart from the others.

The diagnostic pressure points I care about: whether bivalence holds for borderline statements, whether there's a sharp-but-unknowable boundary vs. no boundary vs. many admissible ones, whether higher-order vagueness is a real phenomenon or just an artifact of the model, and whether the sorites is solved by rejecting a premise or by messing with classical logic.

Each question should have answer options that map onto the theories without naming them. Include a scoring key. Flag any two positions your quiz can't reliably separate — I'm especially worried that supervaluationism and epistemicism might blur together on some questions if the wording isn't tight. Also note any logically possible answer patterns that have zero defenders and say why you think that is (incoherent? just nobody's bothered?).

And vary the cases please — don't give me five heap problems. Throw in a legal case where a vague statute is being litigated, or a dispute about whether someone's really "bald," or a case about identity over time where vagueness is doing the work.
