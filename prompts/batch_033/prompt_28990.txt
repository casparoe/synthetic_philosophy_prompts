I am a software engineer who designs event-sourced systems, and I have no formal background in philosophy, only a habit of reading the occasional popular article on my own. The systems I work with do not store the current state of a record and overwrite it. They keep an append-only log of every change, and the current state is derived by replaying the log up to a point, usually with cached snapshots or projections so that clients can read the state as it stood at some sequence number. Lately I have been trying to understand the debate between presentism and eternalism in the philosophy of time, and I keep reaching for the distinction I use every day. My rough thought is that the presentist sounds like someone who says that only the latest projection really exists, because the earlier events have already been consumed to produce it, while the eternalist sounds like someone who says that the whole log exists and the present is just a read position within it. But I suspect this flattens the debate.

I would like you to restate both positions, and any nearby positions you think the distinction forces us to notice, in the vocabulary of event sourcing: events, log order, append point, replay, snapshots, projections, and read positions. Be as faithful as you can, and do not just map words onto words. Then give me a residue report. Which parts of the debate translate cleanly, which parts you had to distort, and which parts have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? I am especially unsure about two things. First, my log grows, so it only contains events that have already happened; a completed log would be a different picture, and I cannot tell whether that gives me a three-way distinction rather than a two-way one. Second, the debate seems to care about whether the present is objectively special and whether the future is open in a way the past is not, and I do not know whether those claims survive the translation or simply become invisible to it. If they do not survive, is that because my vocabulary is missing something, or because those claims are less clear than they look once the image of a moving now is set aside? I am not asking for a survey of authors or a reading list; I want the translation itself.
