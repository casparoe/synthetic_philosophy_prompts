I've been trying to nail down my views on anthropic reasoning for a while now, and I'd like a consistency check before I commit to writing them up. Here's where I've landed:

(1) The self-sampling assumption (SSA) is the correct principle for reasoning under indexical uncertainty: you should reason as if you are randomly sampled from the set of all observers in your reference class.

(2) The presumptuous philosopher thought experiment is a successful reductio of the self-indication assumption (SIA) — the conclusion that we should be overwhelmingly confident in a theory that posits vastly more observers, on no evidence beyond the theory itself, is absurd.

(3) In Sleeping Beauty, the correct credence upon waking is 1/3 that the coin landed heads.

(4) The doomsday argument is structurally valid — the Bayesian shift is real — but its practical force is small because our prior over possible futures is not uniform in the way the simple version of the argument assumes, and we have independent reasons to assign non-negligible probability to long futures.

(5) In the incubator gedanken (a coin is flipped; if heads, one observer is created in room A; if tails, two observers are created, one in A and one in B; you wake up in room A), the probability that the coin landed heads is 1/3.

I'm worried that (3) and (5) might be in tension with (1), and I'm not sure whether (4) is doing real work or is just a way of having my cake and eating it too. Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what the least costly revision would be. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad.
