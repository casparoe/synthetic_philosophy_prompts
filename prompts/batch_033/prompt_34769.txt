I'm presenting a paper at a department colloquium next month. My thesis is that the standard consequentialist toolkit cannot capture what is morally distinctive about extreme suffering, but not for the reason usually given. The usual argument for suffering-focused ethics claims that suffering is lexically worse than happiness — that no amount of happiness can outweigh even a single pinprick of suffering. I reject that claim, along with the familiar objections to it. Instead, I argue that the reason to prevent a being from undergoing extreme suffering is a reason that cannot be discharged by providing a compensating benefit to a different being, even if the benefit is very large. This is not a lexical claim about the relative weight of values but a structural claim about the direction of reasons: the duty to prevent extreme suffering is a duty owed to the one who would suffer, and it cannot be traded away for benefits to others. I call this the directed-reasons argument for suffering-focused ethics.

The argument proceeds in three stages. First, I argue that the directedness of the reason to prevent suffering is phenomenologically grounded: extreme suffering is not merely a bad state of the world but an experience that involves a subject's whole perspective being held hostage, and the moral response this demands is directed at that subject, not at the world's aggregate value. Second, I argue that standard consequentialist attempts to capture this by assigning higher weights to suffering fail because weighting does not alter the structure of reasons — a weighted reason to prevent suffering is still a reason to produce a state of affairs, and it can still be satisfied by producing a different, better state of affairs. Third, I argue that the directed-reasons framework yields the practical implications of suffering-focused ethics — prioritising the worst-off, rejecting trade-offs between extreme suffering and moderate benefits to others — without the implausible implications of lexical views, such as the claim that one pinprick outweighs any amount of happiness.

The audience is a philosophy department with the following members, all of whom will be in the room:
