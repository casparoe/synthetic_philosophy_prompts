In my reporting on the science of consciousness, I’ve kept returning to Daniel Dennett’s proposal that the self is a “center of narrative gravity”—a purely abstract, fictional entity generated by our linguistic and narrative practices, not a substantial inner thing. I’m struggling to see how this picture leaves room for moral responsibility. If the self is a fiction, then attributing praise, blame, or desert to a person looks like attributing those properties to a fiction. Doesn’t Dennett’s view entail that moral responsibility is a kind of category mistake, that when we hold someone accountable we are simply punishing and praising a story we tell ourselves? Or can a “fictional” self be robustly held to account, and if so, what exactly carries the responsibility?
