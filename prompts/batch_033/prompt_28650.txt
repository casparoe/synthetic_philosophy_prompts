I've been teaching a grad seminar on anthropic reasoning this term, and working through the standard cases has made me realize my own gut verdicts don't actually hang together. I'd rather not just pick the self-sampling assumption or the self-indication assumption by fiat; I want to know what principle, if any, captures the way I actually react, and which of my reactions I'd have to abandon. Here's where I land before I start trying to be consistent.

In the God's coin-toss case with one observer on heads and two on tails, I wake up in a white room with no way to tell which person I am, and I want to say the coin is still 50/50. Waking up doesn't feel like evidence, since I'd wake up either way. But change the setup so tails creates a billion observers, and my gut flips: I now feel heads is basically ruled out, because a hypothesis on which a billion observers exist makes my own existence vastly more likely than one on which exactly one does. Then there's the presumptious philosopher case: two cosmological theories fit all the data equally well and are equally simple; the only difference is that one says the universe contains 200 billion observers and the other says 200 trillion. The philosopher says we should strongly favor the bigger number, just because our existence is more likely if there are more of us. My gut says that's nuts — the fact that I exist can't be evidence for a theory that says more people exist. And yet I find the doomsday argument genuinely hard to resist: if I reason as if I'm a random draw from all humans who will ever live, then finding myself around the 60-billionth human strongly favors a total of 100 billion over 100 trillion, and I cant shake the feeling that there's something to that.

So I seem to be running the self-sampling assumption in the one-vs-two coin toss, the doomsday case, and the presumptuous philosopher case, but running the self-indication assumption in the one-vs-a-billion coin toss. Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? If not, which verdict should I drop, and why? And what would the principle you settle on say about a case I've been avoiding: a presumptuous-philosopher setup where the two theories differ by only a factor of two, 200 billion versus 400 billion — and about a one-vs-ten coin toss? If I stay anti-presumptuous in the factor-of-two case, does that force me to give up the billion-observer verdict, or is there a way to have both without just drawing an arbitrary line somewhere?
