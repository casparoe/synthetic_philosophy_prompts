I'm a PhD student in philosophy of law. I'm drafting a working paper that uses Bostock v. Clayton County (2020) as a test case for the supposed opposition between textualism and purposivism. I know the doctrinal facts: the Supreme Court held 6-3 that Title VII's prohibition on employment discrimination because of sex covers discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation and gender identity, with Justice Gorsuch writing for the majority. I want the conceptual mapping, not the case law.

Analyze the decision through the textualism/purposivism debate. Specifically: (1) State the version of textualism and the version of purposivism that actually do work in the standard debate, and say what each would require an interpreter to do with a statute whose drafters likely did not anticipate the application. (2) Walk through the majority's actual moves — its treatment of ordinary meaning, but-for causation, the individual-level rather than group-level reading, and its insistence that it is enforcing what the statute says rather than what anyone expected — and assess for each whether it is genuinely textualist, covertly purposivist, or neither. (3) Then reverse direction: what does this episode show about the contrast itself? Does Bostock confirm that textualism and purposivism are rival methods, show that the rivalry is overdrawn because the two converge at the point of application, or show that each borrows from the other when the text underdetermines the result? Reach a definite conclusion about which of these the case best supports, and identify the strongest consideration on the other side.

Begin with a one-sentence bottom line, then the analysis. Maximum 1,000 words.
