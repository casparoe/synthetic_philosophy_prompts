I'm a journalist working on a longread about spectrum arguments and whether "better than" is transitive when you're comparing whole human lives. Ive been corresponding with a philosopher — she's a postdoc in axiology, not a public figure, so I'm trying to nail down what view she actually holds before I describe her position in the piece.

She's given me verdicts on a series of pairwise comparisons. The first spectrum was straightforward: lives that differ only in the intensity of a single bad thing (chronic pain), moving from excruciating to barely noticeable in ten steps. She said each step was an improvement, and she also said the first life was worse than the last. No drama there — transitivity holds fine.

Then I gave her a spectrum that varies two things at once: the depth of a person's relationships and the length of their life. Here's what she said. (1) 10 years of extraordinarily deep relationships is better than 20 years of very deep relationships. (2) 20 years of very deep relationships is better than 35 years of moderately deep relationships. (3) 35 years of moderately deep relationships is better than 55 years of somewhat shallow relationships. (4) 55 years of somewhat shallow relationships is better than 80 years of quite shallow relationships. So far so good. But then I asked her directly: is 10 years of extraordinarily deep relationships better than 80 years of quite shallow relationships? And she said no — the 80-year life of quite shallow relationships is better. She didn't blink when I pointed out this gives us A > B > C > D > E and also E > A. She said "better than" just isn't always transitive when you're comparing across multiple dimensions, and shes fine with that.

Now here's the new trio I haven't sent her yet. I want to know what she'll say before I email her, so I can write up her view accurately. Life X: 40 years of moderately deep relationships. Life Y: 30 years of very deep relationships. Life Z: 50 years of relationships at a depth that's exactly halfway between "somewhat shallow" and "moderately deep" — call it "middling." What will she say about X vs. Y, Y vs. Z, and X vs. Z? Are there multiple consistent rules that fit her existing verdicts but diverge on this new trio? If so, which rule do you think she's actually running, and what's the single further case that would most cleanly tell them apart? And for my own thinking about how to frame her view in the piece: what's the strongest consideration that could make you change your mind about which rule she's committed to? Where in your reasoning are you least confident?
