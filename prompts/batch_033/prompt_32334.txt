A few years ago I received a psychiatric diagnosis, and I've noticed something strange ever since. The diagnosis itself — just having the label — changed how I thought about my own behavior. Things I used to shrug off as quirks suddenly felt like symptoms. I started describing myself differently to other people, and they started treating me differently too. It made me wonder whether the label was capturing something that was already there, or whether it was partly creating the very thing it was supposed to name.

I've been reading about this in my spare time, and I came across the idea that psychiatric categories can "loop" — that when you classify people, the classification feeds back into their lives and changes them, which in turn changes what the category picks out. I gather this is sometimes called the looping effect. Meanwhile, the DSM — the manual psychiatrists use — is built around the idea that we can refine diagnostic criteria over time, get better reliability, and move closer to capturing the real disorders underneath. Committees revise it every few years, adding and dropping categories and adjusting thresholds. But if the looping effect is real, I don't understand what the DSM project is actually presupposing about what mental disorders are. It seems to treat them as stable targets we can zero in on, like mapping a coastline. But if the coastline shifts every time you draw a map of it, what are you really doing?

I want to understand what the practice of diagnostic classification in psychiatry — the whole DSM-style enterprise — implicitly assumes about the nature of mental disorders. Does it commit to the idea that disorders are natural kinds, or could it be agnostic? What views of mental disorder does it rule out? Are there places where the practice is compatible with several different philosophical pictures, where the practice itself doesn't actually force a choice? And if we took the looping effect seriously, what would psychiatric diagnosis have to look like — what would need to change, and what could stay the same?

I'd like the response in two parts. First, a prose discussion that walks through the implicit theory. Second, a structured summary as a JSON object with the following fields: "core_presuppositions" (an array of claims the DSM project seems committed to, whether or not it states them explicitly), "views_ruled_out" (an array of views about the nature of mental disorder that the practice cannot accommodate), "underdetermined_choices" (an array of places where the practice is compatible with multiple philosophical views and doesn't force a decision), "looping_vulnerability" (an assessment of whether and how the practice is vulnerable to the looping effect), and "redesigned_practice" (a description of what psychiatric diagnosis would look like if it were built from the ground up to take looping effects seriously).
