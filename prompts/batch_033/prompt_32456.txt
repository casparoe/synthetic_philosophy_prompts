The debate about the norm of assertion — knowledge, truth, belief, reasonable belief — has been running for over twenty years and I can't see that anyone's moved. We all know the cases: lottery propositions, Moore-paradoxical assertions, the person who blurts out something true on terrible evidence. Every theory handles some of these and struggles with others, and the counterexample/countermove cycle just keeps spinning. I'm starting to think the problem isn't that we haven't found the right norm but that the question is badly posed. Here's my working hypothesis: the disagreement is really about what kind of activity assertion is — an epistemic act, a social act, a cooperative speech act — and each candidate norm captures something real but for a different one of those. So the sides aren't disagreeing about a single thing; they're answering different questions under one heading. I want a diagnosis, not a verdict on which norm is correct. Is the debate stalled because it's ill-framed in this way, because it's genuinely hard and the data underdetermine the theory, or because there's a hidden empirical disagreement about what people actually do when they assert?
