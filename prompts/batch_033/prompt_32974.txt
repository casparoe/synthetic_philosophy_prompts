I keep noticing a configuration in reasoning about threats, and I want to know whether it has an established name. A threat is supposed to work by making someone believe you will do something, but the thing threatened would be costly or even self-destructive, so if the moment ever came you would have every reason not to do it. To get around this, the threatener deliberately gives up the power to choose later: the retaliation is made automatic, or entrusted to a machine, or a bridge is burned so that backing out is no longer possible. Two instances: a state that installs an automatic response system precisely so that no official can rescind the order, and a driver in a game of chicken who conspicuously disables the steering. The strange feature is that the very step that makes the threat credible is also what makes catastrophe more likely, because it removes the last point at which a person could stop it. I can see that this is somewhere near brinkmanship and commitment tactics, but those seem to be ways of signaling resolve while leaving someone in control. The thing I am after is the specific structure in which credibility is bought by making the final choice impossible. Does that structure have its own name, and how is it distinguished from a bluff, a commitment device, and brinkmanship? If it has no established name, what should it be called and defined?
