I serve on the standards committee of a professional accreditation body. We design requirements—examinations, supervised practice hours, continuing education—meant to signal practitioner competence to the public. We have no systematic way to distinguish a requirement that actually screens for quality from one that merely imposes costs every candidate must bear. Please write a decision procedure, grounded in costly signalling theory and the handicap principle, that our committee can use to evaluate any proposed accreditation requirement. The procedure should be a sequence of questions with criteria for what counts as a sufficient answer, clear stop points, and explicit flags for where judgement cannot be eliminated. Run it on three proposals: (1) a 2,000-hour supervised practice requirement; (2) a difficult closed-book examination with a low pass rate; (3) a substantial accreditation fee. Include at least one case where the procedure returns an answer the committee will find unwelcome, and state what the procedure cannot handle. After the prose, supply a structured summary as a JSON object with fields for each step: the question, the criterion for yes, the criterion for no, whether the step requires judgement, and the next action. Please write in British English. Also indicate which empirical finding or theoretical challenge would most alter the procedure's recommendations—where the reasoning is most vulnerable.
