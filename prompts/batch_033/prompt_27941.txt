I'm a novelist, not a philosopher — my last book was literary fiction about a family running a motel, so this is new terrain — but my current project has a protagonist who's a decision theorist and I've fallen down a rabbit hole trying to get her right. I've been reading about the Allais paradox, Ellsberg, framing effects, all of it, and I've ended up with a set of views I'd like to run past someone who actually knows this stuff before I put words in her mouth. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) The Allais paradox isn't just a quirk — it shows that the independence axiom is genuinely false as a normative standard. People aren't making a mistake when they prefer the sure million in the first pair and the 10% shot at five million in the second. The axiom is asking something unreasonable.

(2) But I don't think the same about the Ellsberg paradox. Ambiguity aversion seems rational to me — if you don't know the composition of the urn, preferring to bet on the one where you do know the odds isn't a bug, it's a feature. I don't think Ellsberg needs to be "debunked" the way some people try to debunk Allais.

(3) I take money pump / Dutch book arguments seriously. If a pattern of choices means someone can extract resources from you for free, that seems like a real problem for whatever principle generates that pattern. I'm not willing to just shrug at exploitability.

(4) Framing effects — like the Asian disease problem where people flip from risk-averse to risk-seeking just because the options are described as lives saved versus lives lost — those I think are irrational. That's a mistake, and we should correct it when we catch ourselves doing it.

(5) I'm attracted to regret theory as a way of making sense of Allais without abandoning the whole expected utility framework. The idea that people minimize anticipated regret rather than maximize expected utility seems to capture something real.

(6) I think completeness — the idea that a rational agent must be able to compare any two options — is too demanding. There are pairs of options where it's fine to say "I can't rank these."

I'm trying to figure out if this is a coherent package or if I'm smuggling in contradictions. Which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix? I genuinely don't know where the problem is, so don't pull punches.
