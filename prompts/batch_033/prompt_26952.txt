I teach a course on classical Chinese ethics, and the unit on Mencius and Xunzi keeps colliding, in my mind, with the institution in which I teach. The school district where my university is located runs, at the elementary level, a social-emotional learning curriculum built around the idea that empathy, self-control, and conflict-resolution skills can be cultivated in every child, given the right environment and the right adult modeling. The same district also suspends kindergartners for disruptive behavior, maintains a zero-tolerance discipline code under which some students are removed from the classroom for long stretches, and in its policy language treats certain students as unable to benefit from the ordinary classroom. Alongside the restorative-justice circles in the high schools runs an exclusionary discipline pipeline that seems to take it for granted that some children cannot be reached by cultivation. I keep wanting to put these two strands side by side and ask what the district is committed to about human nature. Mencius, as I teach the text, holds that the tendencies toward compassion, shame, deference, and a sense of right and wrong are present in everyone as incipient sprouts, and that bad conduct results from a failure to nourish them in a supportive environment. Xunzi holds, by contrast, that our inborn material is unruly and self-seeking, and that whatever goodness we display is an artifact imposed through teachers, ritual, and explicit norms that redirect us against our initial grain.

What theory of human nature does this institutional pattern presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view as precisely as you can: is it Mencian, Xunzian, an unstable mixture of the two, or something else that neither text would recognize? Distinguish what the coexistence of the restorative and exclusionary strands strictly presupposes from what it merely happens to fit with, and identify where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, in the sense that more than one view of human nature would license the same policy. Then say what would actually have to change in the district's practices if it adopted one of the two positions consistently: what would a consistently Mencian discipline code look like, and what would a consistently Xunzian one look like? Write for someone who has read both texts but has not thought about school discipline in these terms before.
