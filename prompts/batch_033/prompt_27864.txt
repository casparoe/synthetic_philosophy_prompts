ok so I'm putting together a paper on joint action and I need a sanity check before I lock myself into views that don't cohere. here's what I've got:

(a) Bratman's account of shared intention is basically right. shared intention just is each person intending to do their part, each believing the other will do theirs, and all of this being common knowledge. no group agents, no spooky we-intentions, just interlocking individual attitudes.

(b) Gilbert's whole plural subject thing is wrong. there's no irreducible "we" hovering over the individuals.

(c) same for Searle. I don't buy that you need a primitive we-intention that can't be analyzed into I-intentions plus common knowledge.

(d) but here's the problem. two people carry a table together, but one has a gun to their head. the coerced guy intends to do his part (he wants to stay alive), he believes the other will do theirs, and all of this is common knowledge. Bratman's conditions are satisfied. but I want to say this isn't genuine joint action, or at least there's something central missing. if you can coerce someone into satisfying all the conditions, the conditions aren't sufficient.

(e) so I think we need a normative element: in genuine joint action, each party is obligated to the other and each is entitled to rebuke the other if they bail. the coerced guy has no such entitlement against the guy with the gun, so it's not joint action. BUT I think you can add this normative stuff without going full Gilbert — it's just a normative relation between individuals, not a commitment that constitutes a plural subject.

my question is whether this package hangs together. (a) and (d)/(e) seem like they're tugging in opposite directions — if Bratman's sufficient conditions aren't sufficient, in what sense is the account "basically right"? and (b) plus (e) worries me: I say Gilbert is wrong, then I add exactly the kind of reciprocal obligations and entitlements she builds her account around. am I just a Gilbertian who's in denial? also, does (c) stay innocent here, or does adding normative relations drag we-intentions back in somehow?

rank the tensions by severity, say what has to give for each, and tell me if there's a single revision that cleans up the most. don't give me a lit review, i just want to know if i'm tying myself in knots.
