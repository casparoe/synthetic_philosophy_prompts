Hey, I'm a PhD student in decision theory and I've got a kind of embarrassing problem that I think might actually be philosophically interesting. I keep cycling between three different note-taking / productivity setups for my research. Call them A, B, and C. Every couple months I get fed up with whichever one I'm on, convince myself one of the others will finally fix my workflow, and switch. Each switch costs me something — subscription fees, time migrating notes, buying new apps. And I've now gone A → B → C → A at least twice, so I'm definitely worse off than if I'd just picked one and stuck with it.

I know this looks exactly like a money pump: I'm paying to go around in a circle and ending up back where I started. The standard line is that this kind of cyclical preference is irrational and that's why you shouldn't have it. But here's the thing — when I'm about to switch, it genuinely feels like the right move. My preferences seem to genuinely shift depending on what I'm currently using and what's annoying me about it right now. So I'm not sure the "just don't have cyclical preferences" advice actually helps me, because at each step my preference feels stable and well-grounded in that moment.

So my question: am I actually being irrational? Or is there a defensible way to have preferences that shift over time without being vulnerable to the money pump charge? Like, does the money pump argument actually force diachronic stability of preferences, or are there ways to resist it that would let me off the hook? I'm trying to figure out if I should just pick one and use willpower, or if my behavior is maybe not as dumb as it looks.
