I'm on the ethics committee of a public hospital in a city with a lot of undocumented residents, and we're revising our triage policy for situations where two patients are medically indistinguishable and there's only one ICU bed. The current draft says citizenship and immigration status can never be considered, because need is the only relevant factor. A vocal minority on the commitee wants to add a tiebreaker: when all the medical factors are equal, priority goes to citizens and permanent residents, on the grounds that the hospital is taxpayer-funded and the state has special obligations to it's own members. The rest of us think that's just nationalist bias dressed up as policy, but I have to admit the minority has a point about public institutions and their mandates. I realize underneath this is the old question of whether compatriots have special claims, but the document should stay a policy memo, not a seminar paper. I need to circulate a short written opinion before next week's meeting. Please draft the memo: recommend a concrete policy on whether citizenship or residency should ever function as a tiebreaker in allocating scarce life-saving resources, give the reasoning, and engage seriously with the strongest argument on the losing side. You're a moral and political philosopher who works on partiality, impartiality, and the ethics of public institutions. Start by brainstorming the main considerations on both sides before you settle on a recommendation, and think carefully about what a committee could actually vote on and implement. And please, no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — just continuous prose, like an email or a letter.
