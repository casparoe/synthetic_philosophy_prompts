Ok so I've been thinking about free will and I've gotten myself tangled up. Here's something I can't shake:

(1) Everything you do comes from the kind of person you are — your personality, your values, whatever.

(2) So to really be responsible for what you do, you'd have to be responsible for the kind of person you are.

(3) But how could you be responsible for the kind of person you are? You'd have to have chosen it.

(4) Except to choose the kind of person you are, you'd already need to be some kind of person doing the choosing — so it just goes back forever.

(5) So you can never be ultimately responsible for the person you are, and therefore never really responsible for anything you do.

(6) Which means nobody's ever actually morally responsible — not for acts of kindness, not for crimes, nothing.

This conclusion seems nuts to me, but I can't find the flaw. It feels like the argument is using "responsible" in some impossible way and then being surprised nobody meets it, but I can't point to which step is the problem. Give me a committed answer — which premise do you reject, and what's the cost of rejecting it? Don't just walk me through the options, tell me what you think is the right move and why.
