In my philosophy of biology reading group, someone dismissed a discussion of whether natural selection explains adaptation by saying, "Darwinism is not a testable scientific theory, but a metaphysical research programme," and added that Popper had already made the point. I suspect this one-liner is doing more work than it can bear, but I cannot say exactly what it is claiming. I want a menu of the distinct precise theses the sentence could express: for instance, that "fitness" and "adaptation" are defined by survival, so the central principle is a tautology; that the theory is unfalsifiable in principle; that it is testable in principle but not in practice; that it is not a single theory at all but a framework for generating testable theories; that "metaphysical" here means meaningless; that it means non-empirical but rationally assessable; and so on. For each reading, say whether it is trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false, and whether it is something Popper actually held at some stage or only a loose paraphrase of his view. Then say which reading, if any, would actually license treating the question "does natural selection explain adaptation?" as ill-posed or settled, and which readings leave that question entirely open.
