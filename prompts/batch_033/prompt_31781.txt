I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I am preparing a long essay on the ethics of rescue and triage — specifically, on whether the fact that a course of action would save more people should, by itself, settle what one ought to do. Before I interview philosophers on all sides of this, I want the specification rather than the sales pitch: what must a defensible account of whether the numbers count actually deliver?

Write the desiderata. Which cases does any adequate theory have to get right — the bare one-versus-five rescue among strangers; the known victim set against a larger statistical group; unequal probabilities of survival among those who could be saved; special obligations when a friend or child is among the smaller number; the contrast between not saving the many and actively harming the few; enormous disparities in numbers, where the smaller side seems almost beneath notice — and what is the consideration each case is meant to force? Which distinctions may such a theory not collapse: counting a person's interests versus counting persons; intrapersonal versus interpersonal aggregation; ex ante justification in terms of each person's chance versus ex post evaluation of who is actually saved; equal moral status versus an equal say in a coin toss? Which formal constraints from decision theory — transitivity, dominance, independence, continuity — is the theory bound by, and which of them become negotiable when lives rather than gambles are at stake?

Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are only the preferences of particular camps. I especially want to know whether some subset of the list is jointly unsatisfiable — whether, for example, equal respect for each person, a prohibition on interpersonal aggregation, and a complete transitive ordering of options cannot all be had at once, and what has to give. Then tell me, briefly, which single requirement does the most work in excluding the standard positions, and where the remaining live options diverge. I do not need a reading list; I need the criteria and the tensions between them.
