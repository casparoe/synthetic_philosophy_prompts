I've been reading around the Taurek debate for a while now, the one about whether you're required to save the greater number when you can save either one stranger or five strangers. And I'm struck by how little the debate has moved. On one side you've got the view that of course you save the five: five deaths are worse than one, and anyone who thinks otherwise has let a clever argument override something obvious. On the other side you've got the Taurek-style view that the numbers don't aggregate like that: each of the five has a claim no stronger than the one's claim, so from the point of view of any individual involved there's nothing that makes the five's side more important; you may save the one, or flip a coin to be fair to everyone.

What's weird to me is that both sides seem to have fully absorbed each other's arguments and neither has budged. The critics say the anti-aggregation view gives you absurd results — you wouldn't save five from a year of torture over saving one from a hangnail — and the anti-aggregation people say those results only seem absurd because we're smuggling in the very assumption at issue, namely that harms sum across persons. Then the other side says that's not an argument, it's just refusing to accept the intuition. And round it goes.

I have a working theory but I don't trust it. My theory is that this isn't really one disagreement: the two sides are answering different questions under the same heading. One side is asking what makes an outcome better, the other is asking what you owe to each person considered one at a time, and those questions just don't have to line up. But I can also see the case that its a substantive disagreement about whether there's any such thing as impersonal value over and above individual claims, or a bedrock intuition about aggregation that neither side can argue for.

Id like a diagnosis, not a verdict. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Verbal, substantive, different questions, clashing bedrock intuitions, a hidden empirical or formal question? And given that diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a distinction, a new case, a theorem, a change of question — or is this just a permanent feature of the terrain?
