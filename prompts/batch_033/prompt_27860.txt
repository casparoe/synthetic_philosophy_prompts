I'm a graduate student in astrophysics, and I keep hearing a one-liner that seems to me to be doing far more argumentative work than it can bear. In a seminar on the Fermi paradox, a colleague dismissed decades of null results from SETI with the remark that "absence of evidence is not evidence of absence." His suggestion was that the Great Silence gives us little or no reason to lower our credence in the existence of extraterrestrial technological civilizations — perhaps no reason at all.

I suspect the slogan could be unpacked into several quite different claims, and I don't want to attack a straw man. Please respond as an expert philosopher of science and formal epistemologist. Start by brainstorming the distinct things someone might mean by this slogan in the SETI context, and then lay out the strongest readings as a numbered menu, ordered from the most trivial to the most substantive. For each reading, give me: (i) the precise thesis being asserted; (ii) a verdict on whether it is a tautology, a plausible-but-empirical claim, or false as stated; and (iii) an explanation of whether that reading actually supports treating the null results as carrying no evidential weight. Where it helps, be explicit about the formal structure — likelihood ratios, priors, and the difference between a search that could realistically have detected a signal and one that could not. End by telling me which reading or readings my colleague's argument needs in order to work, and whether those readings survive scrutiny.
