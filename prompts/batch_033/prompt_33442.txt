I'm giving the opening remarks at a workshop on psychiatric diagnosis reform next month. The audience is a mix: about half are clinicians and researchers who've worked with the DSM/ICD for decades, and the other half are service users and advocates, some of whom have quite strong views about the harm diagnostic labels have done them. I've got eight minutes.

The thing I want to get across — without using the word 'looping' or mentioning Ian Hacking or sounding like I'm giving a philosophy lecture — is the basic idea that psychiatric classifications aren't like classifying rocks. When you classify a person, the person notices, and that changes how they think about themselves, which changes the presentation, which changes what the classification picks up. And this isn't just a measurment problem you can fix with better instruments; it's structural. The categories keep moving.

I want the clinicians to feel this as a genuine challenge to what they're doing, not an attack. And I want the service users to feel heard without being patronised. Can you write the speech? Please just give me the speech itself, no preamble or commentary.
