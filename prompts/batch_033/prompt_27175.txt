I'm a retired condensed-matter physicist. For the past two years I've been reading philosophy of social science — Watkins, Mandelbaum, Elster, Pettit, Sawyer, the usual entry points — and I keep finding myself translating the central debate into the conceptual vocabulary I spent my career using. The debate is methodological individualism versus holism: roughly, whether social facts (norms, institutions, group-level regularities, structural constraints) must be explained in terms of individual actions and attitudes, or whether they possess autonomous explanatory power that cannot be reduced without loss.

The shape of this debate is immediately familiar to me. It sounds like arguments we had in statistical mechanics about whether thermodynamic quantities are "nothing but" statistical summaries of underlying microstates, or whether they pick out real, causally efficacious macro-level structures that constrain and govern the micro. It sounds like debates about emergence, about whether the arrow of explanation always points downward, about multiple realizability and coarse-graining and whether you can recover macro-regularities from micro-dynamics without already building the macro into your boundary conditions. But I have learned enough philosophy by now to know that an analogy that feels natural to an outsider can be deeply misleading, and I want to know how far this one actually goes.

Please approach this as a professional philosopher of social science who also has genuine working fluency in the conceptual machinery of statistical mechanics and complex systems. Take your time and think carefully before writing; I would prefer a thorough treatment of a few central issues to a survey that skims everything.

Your task: restate the individualism/holism debate in the vocabulary I know — microstates and macrostates, coarse-graining, supervenience and multiple realizability, phase transitions and critical phenomena, universality classes, the renormalization group, effective field theories, downward causation (understood as macro-level constraints on micro-dynamics), equilibrium and non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, ergodicity, and the relationship between a system's parts and its collective variables. I am fully fluent in this vocabulary; do not simplify it or explain the physics to me. For each major position in the philosophical debate — reductionist individualism, non-reductive or supervenience individualism, explanatory holism, and any form of ontological holism that claims social structures are causally autonomous — give the best translation you can manage into this framework.

Then give me the residue report. Which claims translate cleanly, and which translate only with distortion (and where exactly does the distortion enter)? Which claims have no expression at all in the statistical-mechanical vocabulary? And the question I most want answered: is the residue a sign that the philosophical debate is tracking something real — features of the social world that the physics vocabulary was never designed to capture — or is it a sign that some of the philosophical claims were less precise than they appeared, and the translation attempt exposes that?

I am not looking for an analogy. I am looking for a systematic translation whose value lies as much in what it fails to carry as in what it carries.
