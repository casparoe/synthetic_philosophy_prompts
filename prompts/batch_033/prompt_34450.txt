Last spring I found a fledgling robin on the sidewalk with a broken wing. I took it in, fed it, and after a few weeks it could fly again and I released it. I felt good about this — it seemed like the obviously right thing to do. But since then I've fallen into reading about wild animal suffering online, and now I'm not so sure.

The thing that's bothering me is this: that bird almost certainly went on to have a normal wild life, which means raising multiple broods a year, most of whose chicks die within weeks — from starvation, predation, parasites, exposure. If I hadn't intervened, that one bird would have died, but the dozens or hundreds of offspring it will produce over its life would never have existed to suffer and die. And the same goes for those offspring's offspring, and so on. By saving one bird, I may have set in motion a cascade of suffering that ripples out for generations.

But that line of thinking seems to lead somewhere very strange. If I shouldn't have saved the robin, then shouldn't I also think it's wrong to put up bird feeders, or plant flowers that attract pollinators, or do anything that helps wild animals survive and reproduce? And if helping individual animals is wrong because of the suffering their descendants will experience, then what about predator control, or vaccination programs for wildlife — are those good because they prevent suffering, or bad because they enable more lives that will eventually suffer?

I'm not an academic and I don't have any training in philosophy. I'm just someone with a backyard and a conscience, trying to figure out whether the kindness I showed that bird was actually cruelty in disguise. What should I make of this?
