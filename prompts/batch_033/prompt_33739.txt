I've been reading through the moral uncertainty literature — MacAskill, Bykvist, and Ord, and the surrounding debates — and I keep running into the same frustration. Everyone seems to have a preferred approach: maximize expected choice-worthiness, or use a "my favorite theory" heuristic, or adopt a parliamentary model, or something else. But the arguments for and against these approaches often assume different standards of success. One paper will critique a view for failing to handle intertheoretic comparisons; another will dismiss that problem as a bullet worth biting; a third will say the whole enterprise is misguided because the correct moral theory already tells you how to handle uncertainty about itself. I want to step back and get clear on the criteria before I evaluate any particular theory.

What would a theory of decision-making under moral uncertainty actually have to get right? I'm looking for a list of desiderata: the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must handle correctly, the formal constraints it must respect, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the results from neighboring fields — decision theory, metaethics, normative ethics — it must be consistent with. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. Then flag which requirements are non-negotiable, meaning any adequate theory must satisfy them, and which are preferences of particular camps that a theory could reasonably reject. Most importantly, I want to know whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable. If there's a tension between, say, avoiding fanaticism about low-credence extreme theories and respecting the formal structure of expected utility theory, or between requiring intertheoretic comparability and accommodating theories that deny cardinal structure, or between being action-guiding and respecting the possibility that the correct moral theory instructs you to ignore moral uncertainty — I want that spelled out. If the list itself turns out to contain an impossibility result, that's the part I most need to understand. I'm not asking for a scorecard of existing theories against the list; I can do that myself once I have the criteria. What I want is the specification. What is the job description for a theory in this area, and does the job description itself contain contradictions?
