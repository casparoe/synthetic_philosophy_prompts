Consider the following practice. The dominant tools are strictly proper scoring rules such as the Brier score and the logarithmic scoring rule. These rules have a well-known formal property: in expectation, a forecaster maximizes her score precisely by reporting her true credences, whatever they happen to be. Some platforms also report a decomposition of the Brier score into calibration, resolution, and a base-rate uncertainty term, and they praise forecasters separately for being well calibrated and for making discriminating forecasts. At the same time, the platforms do not, by and large, score the explanatory quality of a forecaster's rationale, her grasp of the underlying mechanisms, or whether her credences were justified by her evidence, except insofar as those things show up in the accuracy of the numbers she reports.
