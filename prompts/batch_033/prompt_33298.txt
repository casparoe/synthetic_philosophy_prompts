So I got into a long argument last night with a friend about whether you can check that a source of information is reliable without already relying on some other source. He's a reliabilist — he thinks a true belief counts as knowledge if it's produced by a reliable process — and he gave me this gas gauge case. I wrote it up after he left, and now the conclusion feels way too strong, but I can't find the exact step to blame.

Roxanne's gas gauge is in fact perfectly reliable, but she has never verified this. She's never measured the tank by hand, never compared the gauge to another one, nothing. She just reads it.

(1) When the gauge reads "F," Roxanne forms the belief that the tank is full, and since the gauge is reliable and she's reading it normally, that belief counts as knowledge.
(2) She also knows the gauge reads "F" — she can see the needle.
(3) From "the gauge reads F" and "the tank is full," she can deduce that the gauge read correctly on this occasion.
(4) Deduction from known premises yields knowledge, so she knows the gauge read correctly on this occasion.
(5) She does this over many fill-ups: each time she reads the gauge, forms the corresponding belief about the tank, and deduces the gauge got that one right.
(6) From all those cases she infers that the gauge is reliable.
(7) So Roxanne can come to know her gauge is reliable purely by using the gauge — no independent check at all.

Then my friend said the same move works for any source that happens to be reliable. If a horoscope column were somehow reliable about your day, you could read it, form the beliefs it predicts, and then combine each prediction with the matching belief to deduce it got that one right, until you'd bootstrapped your way to knowing the horoscope is reliable. Same for a Magic 8-Ball, same for a hunch.

I told him that's absurd. You can't earn the right to trust a source by trusting it a bunch and keeping score with the very beliefs the source gave you. But I couldn't say exactly which numbered step fails, or what rejecting it would cost. I'd like to keep (4) and (6) if I can — deduction and track-record evidence both seem non-negotiable — but I also cant live with (7) or the horoscope version. Where does this go wrong, if it does? Which step do I actually reject, and what does that choice cost me?
