I run a tiny newsletter where every month I make a "which position do you actually hold?" style quiz for my friends about something I've been reading too much about, and this month I've gone down a rabbit hole on moral luck: the fact that two people can do the exact same careless thing, but one of them hits a kid on a bike and the other just drives home, and we treat them completely differently even though the only difference is luck. I've been piecing the debate together from Wikipedia and reddit arguments and a few blog posts, and I can see their are a bunch of distinct positions people end up at, but I don't trust myself to write questions that don't just ask people which position they like.

So I'd like you to write a "what do you actually think about moral luck?" sorting quiz. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete little scenario with two or three answer choices, and a scoring key at the end that sorts people by how they answered the cases, not by which label they like. The cases should include at least: two drunk drivers where one hits someone and the other doesn't; two people who try to kill someone and one succeeds; a person in Nazi Germany who gets the chance to be a hero versus someone in a quiet suburb who never gets that chance; a surgeon whose identical mistake kills one patient and spares another; and a person born with a short temper versus someone born calm. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least: (a) the view that luck never changes how much blame or praise someone deserves; (b) the view that outcomes really do change blame even with identical intentions; (c) the view that outcomes shouldn't change blame but the unlucky person should feel a special kind of regret; (d) the view that circumstances and temperament you didn't choose change how much blame or praise applies; and (e) the view that once you see how much is luck, blame and praise themselves start to look shaky. If some combination of answers has no position attached, say so and briefly say whether it looks incoherent or just empty. Also flag any two positions your questions can't actually pull apart.

Please write it all in American English, and at the very end, after the scoring key, add a last line beginning "Summary:" that gives a one-sentence summary of what the scoring key sorts people into.
