I'm part of an advanced reading group on metaethics, and we've spent the last several weeks working through the evolutionary debunking literature. We've covered the main debunking arguments — Street's Darwinian dilemma, Joyce's genealogy-based argument, and narrower debunking arguments targeting specific classes of moral judgments — as well as the major realist responses. Next week we're doing a synthesis session, and I've been asked to prepare a tiered ranking of the realist responses to evolutionary debunking arguments, ordered by how well they hold up under sustained dialectical pressure.

Specifically, I'd like you to rank the following six responses into tiers (S, A, B, C, D, or F, where S is strongest and F is weakest) based on how effectively they defuse the core challenge that evolutionary debunking arguments pose to robust moral realism — the view that there are mind-independent moral truths that we can have knowledge of. Here are the responses:

1. The third-factor response: our moral beliefs and the moral truths are correlated because both are explained by a common third factor (e.g., the value of survival or flourishing).
2. The pre-established harmony response: natural selection happened to favor cognitive mechanisms that track independent moral truths, perhaps because true moral beliefs were fitness-enhancing in the environment of evolutionary adaptedness.
3. The companions-in-guilt response: if evolutionary debunking undermines moral realism, it also undermines mathematical or logical realism, since our mathematical and logical faculties were also shaped by evolution; but we don't (and shouldn't) abandon mathematical realism, so the debunking argument proves too much.
4. The Moorean response: our confidence in first-order moral truths (e.g., that torture is wrong) is more secure than any premise in the debunking argument, so we should reject the debunking argument rather than abandon moral realism.
5. The "evolution tracks the truth" response: evolutionary pressures did in fact favor creatures who grasp genuine moral truths, because acting on true moral beliefs confers survival advantages — so there is no gap between evolutionary influence and truth-tracking.
6. The rationalist response: moral beliefs are not primarily the product of evolutionary forces but of autonomous rational reflection, which can correct whatever evolutionary distortions exist; evolution may have given us the raw materials, but reason does the real normative work.

Please give each response a tier and a brief justification for its placement. I'm looking for a committed assessment — don't hedge by saying each has merits and demerits. Take a definite stand on which responses are genuinely strong and which collapse under scrutiny, and explain your reasoning. Please write your answer in American English.
