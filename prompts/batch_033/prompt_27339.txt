One of my officemates has been working through Scanlon-style contractualist ideas about when the numbers count, and over the last few months I've kept a list of her verdicts on cases because I can't figure out what rule she's actually running. So far:

The transplant case: a surgeon could quietly kill a healthy patient and harvest his organs to save five patients who will otherwise die. She says wrong, and she says it stays wrong no matter how many people you'd save.

The standard trolley: a runaway trolley is heading toward five workers, and you can throw a switch to divert it onto a side track where one worker is standing. She says permissible, though she admits it feel bad.

The footbridge: same five workers, but you can stop the trolley by pushing a large man off a footbridge onto the tracks, killing him. She says wrong.

The rescue case: you're in a boat and can either reach one person stranded on an island or five people stranded on another island, but not both. She says save the five; she thinks flipping a coin would be perverse.

The broadcast case: a technician is being painfully electrocuted in a broadcast booth, and you could cut the live feed so someone can pull him out, but millions of viewers would miss the final minutes of a match. She says you obviously have to save the technician; the viewers many small frustrations don't add up to a reason that outweighs his serious pain.

Next week she's going to be asked about the loop case: the trolley is heading toward five workers, but the side track loops back onto the main line before the five, and there is one very heavy worker standing on it. If you throw the switch, the trolley hits him, kills him, and his body stops the trolley before it loops back and hits the five. So his death is part of the mechanism that saves them.

What do you think she'll say about the loop case? I'm less interested in the bare verdict than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her track record just as well and what each would predict about the loop case, your best guess and why, and if her track record genuinely underdetermines it, which further case would settle it.
