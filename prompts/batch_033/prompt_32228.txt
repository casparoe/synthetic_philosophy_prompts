I recently read a popular philosophy article that summarized the Gödelian anti-mechanist argument — the line of reasoning associated with J.R. Lucas and Roger Penrose — as follows:

"Gödel's incompleteness theorems prove that for any consistent formal system, there are true mathematical statements that the system cannot prove. Lucas and Penrose showed that this refutes the idea that the human mind is a computer. Here is the reasoning: if the mind were a computer program, it would be equivalent to some formal system. By Gödel's theorem, that system would be unable to prove its own Gödel sentence — a statement that, from outside the system, we can plainly see is true. Since we can see the truth of that sentence while the system cannot, the human mind must transcend any formal system. It follows that the mind is not computational, and artificial intelligence that genuinely matches human thought is impossible in principle. This result has been widely accepted as a decisive objection to the computational theory of mind."
