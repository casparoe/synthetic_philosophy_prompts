I am a novelist, and I am at work on a book in which the central character—a father in late middle age—defends a demanding set of views about individual responsibility for large collective problems to his adult children over a series of dinners. In drafting his position I have discovered that I cannot tell whether the views I have given him are jointly tenable, because I hold them myself, or nearly do. Before I let the character speak them with confidence, I would like an audit of the position.

The position, as I would write it into his mouth:

(1) No individual act of mine—one flight, one purchase of factory-farmed meat, one vote—makes a causal difference to the outcome that concerns me. The atmosphere is not changed by my single flight; the number of animals raised is not changed by my single purchase; no election is decided by my single ballot.

(2) I nonetheless believe that I ought to vote.

(3) I also believe that I ought to avoid factory-farmed meat, even though I accept that my own abstention does not reduce the number of animals raised or killed.

(4) But I do not believe that I am obligated to forgo flying for holidays, even though I accept that my own flight does not change the climate.

(5) I think there is a moral difference between participating in a practice and causing an outcome: if the outcome would occur whether or not I act, I am not causally responsible for it, but I may still bear a different kind of responsibility for participating in the practice that produces it.

(6) I think that the fact that things would be much better if everyone, or enough people, refrained from a given act can make an individual performance of that act wrong, even when no single act makes a difference.

Please go through these commitments and tell me whether they hang together. For each pair or subset that is in tension, say how severe the tension is and what would have to be revised to remove it; for any pair that merely appears to be in tension, say why the appearance is misleading. I am not asking for a general lecture on the inefficacy objection; I want an audit of this particular position, and I would rather you rank the problems by severity than list them all as equally serious.
