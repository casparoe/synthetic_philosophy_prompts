Okay so here's a case I keep trying to build and failing at. With Foucault, the usual story is that power and knowledge feed each other: the more an institution watches you, the more it knows about you, and the more it knows, the better it can sort you, time you, rank you, fix you. The prison, the exam, the case file, all of that. I want a thought experiment that runs the other way: discipline gets more intense, but the institution ends up knowing less and less about the actual people under it. And I don't mean it just stops caring, or the records get lost, or the staff are lazy — I mean the very techniques that tighten control are what eat away at the knowledge.

Rules: realistic setting (a school, a hospital, a warehouse, an office — not a prison, no sci-fi), the scenario itself should be one paragraph, and the knowledge loss has to fall out of how the techniques work, not from me declaring it. The control has to be real control too, not rules nobody follows.

Then after the scenario, walk me through it the way Foucault would: what counts as the discipline here, what kind of knowledge was getting produced before, what kind stops getting produced, and why the two moved in opposite directions. Then be straight with me about whether this actually breaks the power/knowledge link, or whether a Foucauldian would just say the institution starts producing a differnt kind of knowledge (about compliance, say, instead of about persons) so the link holds but the content shifts. Which one feature of the case is doing the real work, and what happens if you take it out? And finish with the strongest move a defender of the hard power/knowledge reading could make to save the link in this case.
