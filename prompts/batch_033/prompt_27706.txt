I am a graduate student writing a paper on well-being, and I have been trying to state hedonism precisely enough to defend it or reject it. The view sounds simple: a life goes well to the extent that it is pleasant and not painful. But when I try to say what 'pleasure' refers to, my examples pull apart. The relief when a headache finally lifts is a felt change in the body. The satisfaction of finishing a proof is not located anywhere in particular, and it can be intense even while I am physically uncomfortable. The pleasure of hearing that a disliked colleague has failed feels ugly, and I half-wish I did not feel it, yet it is undeniably something I want to linger in. The calm after meditating seems pleasant precisely because striving has stopped rather than because any positive sensation arrived. A warm bath, a good conversation, a revenge fantasy: each is pleasant in what feels like a different way, and I cannot find the single thread running through all of them.

I know Mill distinguishes higher and lower pleasures, but that seems to be about ranking pleasures once we already know they are pleasures; it does not tell me what makes them pleasures in the first place. If I fall back on a functional definition — pleasure is whatever a person wants to continue or is glad to be having — then the view no longer seems to be about a feeling at all, and hedonism starts to look like desire-satisfaction in disguise. I am looking for the established name, if there is one, for this specific difficulty. More important than the label, I want the carving: is this one problem or several, how exactly does it differ from nearby worries such as the paradox of hedonism or the experience-machine objection, is it fatal to hedonism as such or only to a narrow version of it, and if there is no established name, what would you call it and where would you draw its boundaries?
