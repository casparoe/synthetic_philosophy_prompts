I've been working on a PhD in philosophy of mind, and my advisor recently had me read Hofstadter's *Gödel, Escher, Bach* — not the usual reading for a mainstream philosophy of mind course, but she thought it might shake up some of my assumptions. I've been going back and forth with a friend in the program about whether Hofstadter's notion of "tangled hierarchies" and the "inviolate level" actually contributes something real to the hard problem of consciousness, or whether it's just a beautiful metaphor that doesn't carry its weight when you try to cash it out.

Here's the core of the disagreement. Hofstadter argues that some systems — paradigmatically, conscious minds — involve a kind of self-reference where the "top" level feeds back into the "bottom" level in a way that makes it impossible to reduce the phenomenon to the bottom level without destroying it. That's the inviolate level. Escher's *Drawing Hands*, where two hands each draw the other into existence, is his visual metaphor for this. One hand seems to be the "creator" and the other the "created," but the relationship is circular — push the explanation down a level and you lose the thing you're trying to explain. My friend Sam thinks this is all confused. He says any apparent tangled hierarchy can be untangled by being more careful about levels of description. The Drawing Hands seem paradoxical only because we're failing to distinguish between the hands *as depicted* and the hands *as depictions*. Once you separate those, the loop dissolves. Similarly, he thinks appeals to tangled hierarchies in consciousness are just failures to specify what exactly is supposed to be looping into what, and that when you get precise, the mystery evaporates — or at least becomes an ordinary scientific problem rather than a deep metaphysical one.

I find myself pulled in both directions. The idea that consciousness might be essentially self-referential, and that this explains why it won't yield to reductive explanation, feels intuitively right. But I also worry that this is exactly the kind of intuition that sounds deep and turns out to be empty.
