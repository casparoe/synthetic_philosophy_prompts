So I've been running a small rationalist-adjacent reading group on the Liar, and after about a year the dialetheist and the gap-theory person are still talking past each other. I finally got them to agree to do an actual adversarial collaboration, and I want you to draft the joint statement they'd sign.

The two camps, as precisely as I can state them:

(a) The dialetheist (roughly Priest-line): the Liar is both true and false; we should accept some true contradictions and use a paraconsistent logic so explosion doesn't follow.
(b) The paracomplete/gap theorist (roughly Kripke/Field-line): the Liar is neither true nor false; truth has gaps, and we need a partial logic where the T-schema or intersubstitutivity holds without bivalence.

Both already reject naive truth plus classical logic, so dont spend the document rearguing that.

I want the statement to have:

1. A numbered agreed-claims section: what both can sign about the Liar, the T-schema, and the constraints any solution has to meet. Make these non-vacuous — no 'the Liar is puzzling' filler. Get into what the T-schema commits you to and which classical moves both give up.
2. A disagreements table: the exact residual disagreement, phrased so each side would accept its own wording. I especially want the row on how 'not true' behaves — whether it's the internal negation meaning 'false', or an external 'fails to be true' that includes the gap — and whether that distinction can be expressed without a revenge paradox.
3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. Cover the strengthened/revenge Liar and Curry at minimum. For each crux, give a concrete argument or test both sides accept as bearing on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance.
4. A short note on which parts of their year-long fight, if any, were just verbal — where they were using 'true' or 'not' differently and not actually disagreeing.

Im less interested in a verdict on who's right than in a document both of them could sign without wincing. If you think the two positions are closer than they look, say so, but don't dissolve the disagreement into words unless it really is words.
