I was listening to a philosophy podcast and the host summed up Quine's indeterminacy of translation like this:

"Quine showed that translation is radically indeterminate. Picture a linguist visiting a newly discovered tribe. A rabbit scurries past and a native shouts 'gavagai!' The linguist naturally translates this as 'rabbit' — but Quine argues she can never be sure. 'Gavagai' might mean 'undetached rabbit part,' or 'temporal rabbit stage,' or even 'there is rabbit-stuff over there.' No amount of pointing, questioning, or living with the tribe will ever settle the matter, because all these possibilities are consistent with every observation the linguist could make. So Quine's point is that meaning is a myth: there's simply no fact about what anyone's words mean, and all translation is ultimately guesswork."

I remember studying Quine briefly in undergrad and something about this summary felt off, but I can't put my finger on what. The host presented it as a kind of skeptical bombshell — that Quine basically proved communication doesn't really work. Is this a fair summary? If not, walk me through what a Quinean would accept, what they'd push back on, and whether the 'meaning is a myth' conclusion actually follows from the parts that are accurate.
