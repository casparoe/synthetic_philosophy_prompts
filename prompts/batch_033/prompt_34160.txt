I've spent the last few months reading about causation on my own — not for any course, just following my curiosity — and I've become preoccupied with a view I first encountered in Al-Ghazālī's work from the eleventh century. I came across it almost by accident, in a footnote to something else I was reading, and it has been quietly rearranging my assumptions ever since. As I understand it, his position is that there are no genuine causal powers in created things at all. When we say fire burns cotton, what actually happens is that God directly creates the burning at the moment of contact, and He does so with such perfect regularity that we mistake the sequence for a necessary connection. The fire itself does nothing; it is merely the occasion for God's action, never the cause. Al-Ghazālī seems to have thought that this was required to preserve divine omnipotence: if fire really had the power to burn, then God would not be the sole agent in the universe, and miracles — where the usual sequences break down — would be metaphysically impossible rather than merely unusual. I find this line of reasoning surprisingly difficult to dismiss, and I notice that versions of it keep surfacing in my own thinking about what causation even is.

I want to know what would count against this view. I'm not asking for a historical rebuttal from within Islamic theology — I'm asking what evidence, arguments, observations, or conceptual considerations should move someone who finds occasionalism plausible. What would the world have to look like for occasionalism to be false? Are there things we already know about the world that sit uncomfortably with it? I'd like the defeaters sorted by how decisive they are, from fatal to merely awkward, and I want to know which of them a committed occasionalist would concede as genuine problems and which they would dismiss as begging the question. I'm also curious whether the view has been insulated by qualifications to the point where nothing could count against it, and if so, whether that's a problem for a metaphysical position of this kind. Please write your answer in American English.
