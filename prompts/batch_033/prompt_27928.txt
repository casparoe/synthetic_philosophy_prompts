I work as a software engineer and have been reading about the frame problem in AI on my own. As I understand it, the problem is this: an agent that plans by reasoning about the effects of its actions must track not only what each action changes but also everything it leaves unchanged, and that second list is open-ended — stop hand-coding "this action doesn't affect that" axioms and the system either fails to infer the obvious or drowns in irrelevant consequences. I've seen a range of proposed fixes (circumscription, default logic, successor-state axioms, the event calculus, plus the claims that the whole thing is an artifact of one particular formalism and that the real problem is relevance rather than representation). Before I read another one, I want the spec. What must an adequate solution to the frame problem actually deliver, for an agent that has to act in a changing world rather than a toy blocks-world demo? Give me the desiderata — the cases it has to handle, the distinctions it can't collapse, the constraints it can't stipulate away — with the consideration behind each, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's preferences. Then flag any pair of desiderata that looks jointly unsatisfiable, and say which single requirement eliminates the most popular proposed solutions.
