A friend of mine has thought a lot about what we owe each other, and over the years I've asked him about a bunch of specific cases — whether the action in question is morally required or just goes beyond the call of duty. He's always been firm and consistent. Here's what he's said:

- Jumping into a shallow pond to save a drowning child, ruining an expensive suit: obligatory.
- Donating a kidney to a stranger: supererogatory.
- Calling 911 after witnessing a serious car crash with injuries: obligatory.
- Volunteering every Saturday at a soup kitchen for a full year: supererogatory.
- Donating a $10,000 inheritance to malaria prevention instead of taking a long-planned vacation: supererogatory.
- Yelling a warning to a driver who is about to back over a child they clearly can't see: obligatory.
- Pulling someone back from the edge of a subway platform as a train is approaching: obligatory.
- Spending your two-week vacation building houses for the homeless instead of relaxing: supererogatory.

Now here's a case I haven't asked him about. You're hiking alone on a remote trail and come across a stranger who has fallen and badly broken their leg. You're the only person around, and there's no cell service. Carrying them down the mountain would take several hours of exhausting physical labor and carries a real risk of injuring yourself — a twisted ankle, a back strain, or worse on the uneven terrain. The stranger will almost certainly die of exposure if left overnight. There's no middle option: either you carry them down, or you leave them and go for help, in which case they won't survive.

What would my friend say about this — obligatory or supererogatory? I'm less interested in the bare answer than in the rule he seems to be running, the other rules that fit his track record just as well, and which of those rules would give different verdicts on this new case. If I wanted to settle which rule he's really committed to, what further case would do it?
