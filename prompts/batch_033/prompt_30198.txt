I've ended up at this view after a lot of thinking and I don't really have anyone to bounce it off. The view is: the only thing that's bad in itself is suffering (including craving, since craving is a kind of suffering). And the only thing that's good in itself is the absence of suffering. Everything else — pleasure, achievement, relationships, art, knowledge — is only instrumentally good, meaning it's good because it reduces or prevents suffering. I didn't get this from Buddhism or Schopenhauer or anyone, I just sort of arrived at it by noticing that whenever I think something matters, if I ask "why does it matter?" enough times, the chain always ends at suffering.

But I want to know if I'm wrong, and I want to know what being wrong would look like. So: what would count against this view? What kinds of cases, thought experiments, or arguments should make me abandon it or at least seriously doubt it? I want them ranked from "this would basically refute it" to "this is awkward but surviveable." And I want to know: have I set things up so that nothing could count against it? Like, can I always just say "well that thing you're pointing to is bad *because* it involves suffering in some subtle way"? If the view is unfalsifiable, is that a problem for a view about what ultimately matters, or is that just how these things work?
