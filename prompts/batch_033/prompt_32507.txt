I help run a co-working space with about 120 members, and we've got this whole unwritten rulebook that's built up over five years — who gets to use the phone booths for what, how long you can hog the good desk by the window, whether you need to clear your stuff at night or can leave it, how the coffee kitty works. Every few months someone gets burned by one of these rules they didn't know about, and then we have a meeting where half the room says "we need to write this down so it's clear" and the other half says "it's working fine, don't bureaucratize it, you'll just make it rigid." I'm the one who has to decide.

I read something about David Lewis's account of convention — that a convention isn't just any old rule people follow, it's a solution to a coordination problem where everyone goes along because they expect everyone else to, and the specific rule is sort of arbitrary (everyone could have settled on a different one and it would've worked fine). That feels like it should have something to say about when you formalize a norm and when you leave it alone, but I don't know how to actually use it.

Can you turn Lewis's account into a decision procedure I can run when someone proposes codifying one of our unwritten norms? I want a set of questions to ask in order, with clear stopping points: where the procedure says "leave it unwritten," where it says "codify it," and where it says "this isn't a convention in Lewis's sense, it's something else, and you need a different framework." The procedure should be usable by someone who's not a philosopher — I can observe what people do and talk to maybe five or six members, but I'm not running a survey. Also, flag the places where the procedure requires a judgment call that the theory itself can't settle.

Then run the procedure on two of our actual norms: (1) the unwritten rule that you don't take calls in the main room, you go to a phone booth, and (2) the unwritten rule that the person who's been a member longest gets first dibs on the window desk when it opens up. For each one, tell me where the procedure stops and what it says.

Oh, and be honest: if the procedure would tell me to do something I'm not gonna like — like telling me to leave something unwritten even though it's causing problems — don't soften it. I'd rather know that's what the view actually says.
