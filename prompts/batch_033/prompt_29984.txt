I'm preparing a unit on functionalism and multiple realizability for an upper-level undergraduate philosophy of mind course, and I want to end it with a sorting instrument: a which-position-do-you-actually-hold decision tree that classifies students by their commitments rather than by the labels they can recite. My students reliably learn the names — identity theory, role functionalism, realizer functionalism, eliminativism, and so on — but they don't see how the positions come apart on cases, and they miss how multiple realizability cuts across the usual physicalist/dualist divide. I'd like the instrument to be built from the logical space rather than from a list of positions: first work out the diagnostic questions and the combinations of answers they permit, then locate the actual positions as leaves in that space, and flag any unoccupied combinations with a one-line diagnosis of why no one is there (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored).

The questions should be phrased so a student can answer them without already knowing which position the answer leads to, and the following cases must appear among the branch points: gradual replacement of a person's neurons by silicon chips that preserve the same input-output profile; the inverted spectrum; the China brain, understood as the whole population of a country wired together to reproduce a person's functional organization; and an alien species whose pain-related behavior matches ours but whose underlying chemistry is completely different. The leaves should include at least these positions: type physicalism/identity theory, role functionalism, realizer functionalism, eliminative materialism, nonreductive physicalism, anomalous monism, and a property-dualist leaf. If your tree cannot genuinely separate two of these leaves, say so explicitly rather than inserting a fake question. No bibliography or citations are wanted; the instrument should stand on its cases and claims alone.

Please return the instrument as a JSON object with this schema. nodes: an array of objects, each with id, text (the question or claim in non-technical language), and answers (an array of objects each with text and goes_to, where goes_to gives the id of the next node or leaf). leaves: an array of objects with id, name, and diagnosis (one sentence stating the view's most awkward commitment or consequence). unoccupied_combinations: an array of objects with answers (a combination of branch answers no actual position occupies) and diagnosis (why: incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored). separability_flags: an array of objects with pair (two leaf names) and why_the_tree_cannot_separate_them.
