I run a philosophy club at my high school, with about fifteen members aged sixteen to seventeen, and I have a single one-hour session to fill. I would like a structured activity that makes participants feel the force of testimonial injustice: the wrong done when a person is assigned less credibility than they deserve because of who they are rather than because of what they say, so that they are harmed specifically in their capacity as a knower, not merely ignored or made to feel unwelcome. I do not want this introduced by a lecture, and I would prefer that the group not hear the term until the debrief.

Please design the activity in enough detail that I could run it: the roles or rules, the materials I would need, a timeline for the hour, and a debrief that maps particular moments in the activity onto the ideas of a credibility deficit, identity-based prejudice, and the difference between being disbelieved and being systematically discounted. The activity should be arranged so that participants run into the phenomenon themselves before it is named — for example, by having to rely on one another's reports or expertise under conditions that quietly distribute credibility unevenly. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, such as the group treating it as a game to be won or the debrief collapsing into "we should just listen more," and how I should handle those failures. Finally, be explicit about which feature of testimonial injustice the activity cannot reproduce and what I will have to supply in the debrief myself.
