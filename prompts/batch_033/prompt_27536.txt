Please explain a conceptual issue in J. L. Schellenberg's divine hiddenness argument. The argument's force is often thought to depend on the premise that a perfectly loving God would never permit a capable person to remain nonresistantly unaware of God's existence. I want a close explanation of the move from the earlier idea of reasonable or inculpable nonbelief to the later idea of nonresistant nonbelief: what work does the notion of nonresistance do, and what exactly is the content of the awareness that God is supposed to ensure? In particular, assess whether the argument is vulnerable to the objection that someone might enjoy a positively meaningful personal relationship with God while lacking explicit belief that God exists, or while believing only in a de re or unformulated way. If that objection works, explain whether it targets the link between love and openness to relationship or only the psychological claim that all nonbelievers are resisting. If it does not work, explain which part of Schellenberg's reasoning rules it out. Please say how confident you are in your analysis and single out the point you are least sure of.
