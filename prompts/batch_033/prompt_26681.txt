I'm a cog sci PhD student working on how people segment scenes into objects, and I keep stumbling into philosophical debates about composition. I read this pop philosophy book (dont remember the title, it was a friend's) that had a chapter on the special composition question, and it summarized things like this:

"The special composition question asks when a bunch of parts count as a single object. Nihilists say never — there are no composite objects, just simples arranged object-wise. But Van Inwagen pointed out that this is absurd because it would mean you and I don't exist, so he modified nihilism to say that the only composite objects are living organisms. Universalists say that any collection of parts composes something, no matter how scattered — so there's an object made of your nose and the Eiffel Tower. The moderate view, which is what common sense endorses, is that parts compose a whole when they're bonded together in the right way, like the parts of a table or a rock."

I'm pretty sure some of this is off. Like, I know Van Inwagen isn't usually called a nihilist, and the "bonded together" thing feels like it's glossing over a lot of work on what the moderate actually needs to say. But I don't know the metaphysics literature well enough to sort out exactly which bits are accurate, which are misleading, and what a defender of each view would actually object to. Also, the book later says the special composition question is "basically the same issue" as the Ship of Theseus — is that right? I feel like one is about synchronic composition and the other is about identity over time, but maybe they collapse into each other in a way I'm not seeing.
