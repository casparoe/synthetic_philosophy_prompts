So I've got this situation that's been nagging at me and I figured I'd ask people who might have thought about this kind of thing before. I used to work with a guy — let's call him M — on an open-source project. We had a pretty bad falling out a couple years ago, blocked each other everywhere, zero communication. But we both still maintain libraries that are in the same niche ecosystem, and we both still watch each other's repos (I know he watches mine because he's referenced commits in my work before we stopped talking, and honestly I still check his too). Here's the thing: I can see from his issue tracker and recent commits that he's struggling with a problem I already solved cleanly in my library six months ago. It would take me maybe an afternoon to extract my solution and open a PR that would unblock him. Meanwhile, from his commit history, he's been working on a gnarly performance refactor that — if he finished it — would be a huge win for my project too. We can't coordinate, we can't make any kind of agreement, and we can't even signal intentions without it being an actual contribution. So I'm stuck wondering: is there any rational case for me to just go ahead and send that PR? The thought is, if he's smart and facing the same kind of decision, maybe he'd recognize that contributing to my project is in his interest if he expects me to do the same — and we'd both end up better off even without ever talking. But that feels like it relies on some kind of spooky reasoning where I'm treating him as if he'll mirror my decision, even though he's a totally seperate person making his own choices. I've heard people talk about "acausal trade" in rationalist circles but I never really understood if it's supposed to apply to actual real-world situations like this or if it's just a thought experiment about superintelligent AIs. Is there a defensible argument for contributing here, or am I just looking for an excuse to make contact again?
