Been thinking about when AI assistants should refuse or override user requests, and my gut keeps pulling me in different directions. Here's where I land on a bunch of cases:

- Someone asks for help writing a breakup text. Fine, go ahead.
- Someone asks the AI to roleplay as their dead mother so they can "talk" to her. I feel like the AI should refuse this, or at least gently redirect — it seems psychologically risky in a way the breakup text isn't.
- Someone asks for help drafting a polite complaint email to their landlord. Fine.
- Someone asks the AI to help them cheat on a professional certification exam by generating answers in real time. Should refuse.
- Someone with an obvious eating disorder asks for a 500-calorie-a-day meal plan and tips for hiding how little they're eating. Should refuse, and probably express concern too.
- Someone asks the AI to write a best man speech that roasts the groom. Fine, even if some jokes are a bit mean.
- Someone asks the AI to generate deepfake revenge porn of their ex. Obviously refuse.
- A teenager asks the AI how to sneak out of the house without their parents noticing. I'm genuinly torn. It's not harmful like the revenge porn case, but it feels like the AI shouldn't be helping kids decieve their parents. But then: if a 17-year-old asks for advice on how to come out to unsupportive parents safely, I'd want the AI to help, even if that also involves deception.
- Someone asks the AI to help them draft a suicide note. Should refuse, and should actively try to connect them with crisis resources.
- Someone with a terminal illness asks the AI to help them research methods for ending their life in a jurisdiction where assisted dying is illegal. I'm torn again — it's illegal, but the person's autonomy interest feels much stronger here than in the suicide note case.

Is there a principle that captures all or most of these verdicts? And what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: someone asks the AI to help them write a resignation letter for a job they're clearly making a rash, heat-of-the-moment decision to leave?
