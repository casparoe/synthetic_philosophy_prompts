Write a sonnet about David Benatar's asymmetry argument for antinatalism. The octave should present the four quadrants of the asymmetry — the presence of pain (bad), the presence of pleasure (good), the absence of pain (good, even if no one exists to enjoy that good), and the absence of pleasure (not bad, unless someone is deprived of it) — so that they initially read like a neutral ledger, a harmless bit of bookkeeping. The volta should be the moment the reader grasps that the asymmetry between the two absence-quadrants entails that coming into existence is always a net harm compared to never existing: the absent pain of the never-born counts in non-existence's favor, but the absent pleasure does not count against it. The closing couplet should deliver the conclusion without softening it, and ideally without needing to state it as a thesis. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who knows the argument would recognize the four quadrants and the structure of the inference, and well-made enough that someone who has never encountered antinatalism would feel the argument's pull before they have named it.
