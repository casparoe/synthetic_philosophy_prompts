Political fact-checking outlets — PolitiFact is the best-known example — rate the truth of politicians' statements on multi-point scales: "true," "mostly true," "half true," "mostly false," "false," and "pants on fire." A claim about an unemployment statistic, a historical event, or the text of a piece of legislation can receive a rating; a claim about what policy would be best, what values a society should prioritize, or what a nation's identity demands does not. The practice thus draws a sharp operational line between factual claims (checkable, ratable) and normative or interpretive claims (not checkable, not rated). Moreover, the ratings are presented as neutral findings — the fact-checker claims no political authority beyond having done the research.

Reconstruct the implicit theory of political truth and political speech that this practice presupposes. Address in particular the following challenge. Hannah Arendt argued that factual truth occupies a uniquely fragile position in politics: unlike rational or philosophical truth, factual truth is contingent — it could have been otherwise — and therefore carries no inner necessity that compels assent. Precisely because facts are established by witnesses and records rather than by logical demonstration, they can be undone by lies, and more radically by organized lying that does not merely assert falsehoods but works to dissolve the shared reality in which facts can be checked at all. On Arendt's account, the modern political lie aims not just to deceive but to make the distinction between fact and fiction itself lose its grip.

Does the practice of fact-checking presuppose a picture of political truth that Arendt's analysis rules out? Or can fact-checking be understood as a bulwark against exactly the erosion she describes — and if so, under what conditions does it actually work? If Arendt is right about the nature of the threat, what would have to change about the practice of fact-checking for it to address the modern political lie rather than a tamer, more traditional kind of falsehood?
