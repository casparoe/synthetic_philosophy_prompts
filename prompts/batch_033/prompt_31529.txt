So I've been going back and forth on this for like a week now, rereading the old LessWrong threads on Parfit's hitchhiker and Kavka's toxin puzzle, and I feel like I keep hitting the same wall. The basic idea of "rational irrationality" — that it can be instrumentally rational to make yourself genuinely irrational about something — seems either obviously correct or obviously incoherent depending on how I squint at it, and I can't tell which intuition is tracking the real structure of the problem.

Here's the setup for anyone who hasn't spent way too much time thinking about this: Parfit's hitchhiker is you, dying of thirst in the desert. A hitchhiker happens by with water, and you promise to pay him $1000 once you reach town. But you both know that once you're saved, you'll have no selfish reason to pay. The hitchhiker won't give you water unless he believes you actually will pay — which means you need to somehow be the kind of person who follows through on threats or promises even when they're no longer in your interest. The claim is that it's rational to make yourself irrational (to genuinely become someone who pays even when it's not in your interest) because that irrational disposition is what gets you the water. But is this really a case of "irrationality," or is it just ordinary precommitment — and if it's precommitment, what's doing the precommitting, given that you can't just decide to sincerely believe something you know is false? And then there's the related worry: if you deliberately engineer an irrational disposition in yourself for rational reasons, is the resulting disposition actually irrational in the relevant sense, or does the rational origin somehow "infect" the whole thing and make it not count as genuine irrationality? I've seen people argue that once you're doing it on purpose, you're not really being irrational, you're just executing a clever strategy — and other people say no, look, the individual action (paying when you don't have to) is straightforwardly against your interests, that's what irrationality *means* in this context, and the fact that you got there by a smart route doesn't retroactively make the action rational.
