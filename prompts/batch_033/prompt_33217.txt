I recently spent a long evening arguing with a friend about how one ought to act under moral uncertainty, and by the end of it I found myself defending a view I had not previously stated in so many words. I have come to think that the correct approach is to maximize expected choiceworthiness: assign each live moral theory a subjective probability, represent what each theory recommends on some common scale of choiceworthiness, and choose the option with the highest expectation. My friend pressed two objections especially hard. First, he argued that the whole apparatus depends on intertheoretic value comparisons for which there is no fact of the matter, so any trade rate between utilitarian value and deontological constraint, for example, is arbitrary and the results of the calculation are artefacts of the scale. Second, he argued that the view invites fanaticism, since a minuscule probability assigned to a theory according to which the stakes are astronomically high will dominate every practical decision, and we have no principled way of excluding such theories from consideration.

I still think something close to this position is right, but I want to know what would count against it, not merely what critics have already said. I would like a map of the view's exposure: which observations, possible cases, formal results, or commitments would have to be true for it to be false or badly damaged? Which of these would a defender of expected choiceworthiness themselves concede as counting against the view, and which would they dismiss as difficulties for everyone rather than difficulties for this position in particular? I would also like to know what the view predicts about actual decisions that its main rivals, such as acting on one's preferred theory or adopting a precautionary rule, do not, and whether a failure of those predictions would count against it. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and give an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that should trouble me. I would personally find most persuasive a defeater that shows the view cannot even be stated without presupposing one of the theories in dispute. Please write the answer in British English.
