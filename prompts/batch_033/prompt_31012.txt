I'm a student in a seminar on the philosophy and formal foundations of AI safety. I've come to hold that ascription universality is the right target for a safe question-answering system: roughly, that relative to our epistemic state, the system's explicit answers epistemically dominate those of any relevantly simpler computation, so that no simpler computation is systematically better informed than the system on the questions we care about. What would count against this view? I'd like a map of its exposure: which observations, possible systems, or formal results would be decisive defeaters, and which would be merely awkward? Please give an honest treatment of the places where the definition itself is doing the work — the choice of which computations count as simpler, the way beliefs are ascribed to a system in the first place, and whether epistemic dominance is the right notion as opposed to, say, mere consistency between hidden cognition and stated output. Which of these would a committed defender concede as genuine problems, and which would they dismiss as fixable by refinement? Finally, say whether the view risks being insulated by qualifications, and whether that would itself be a problem for a target of this kind.
