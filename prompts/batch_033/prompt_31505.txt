I've gotten pretty convinced that the inside view is underrated. You know the distinction—inside view is when you build a forecast from the details of your specific situation, outside view is when you look at base rates for the reference class. The standard advice (from Kahneman and Tetlock and everyone) is that the outside view is usually more accurate and people are overconfident about their inside view. But I've started thinking this cuts the wrong way. When you actually know a domain deeply, the reference class is almost always too coarse—it lumps together cases that are genuinely different in ways the base rate can't capture. The outside view treats every startup in year three with X revenue as the same, but the founder knows why theirs is different. Not just *feels* it's different—actually knows things the reference class can't encode. What would count against this position? I want the evidence or arguments that should make me update, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. And I want to know whether the position, as stated, is too slippery to be tested, and if so whether that's a problem for a view about when to trust inside views.
