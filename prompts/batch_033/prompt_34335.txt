For a piece I'm working on, I've been reading about the relationship between relativity and the metaphysics of time, and I've encountered a version of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument that I find both elegant and unsettling. The argument seems to derive a radical metaphysical conclusion — that the universe is a static four-dimensional block, with past, present, and future all equally real — from the physics of special relativity alone. But the conclusion feels too strong, and I suspect the reasoning hides a misstep. I've laid out the argument below as I understand it, and I'd like to know which premise or step you would reject, and what that rejection costs.

(1) According to special relativity, there is no absolute, frame-independent notion of simultaneity. Two events that are simultaneous in one reference frame may occur at different times in another.

(2) For any two spacelike-separated events — events far enough apart that no signal could travel between them at or below the speed of light — there exists some inertial reference frame in which they are simultaneous.

(3) Take any event in my future: for instance, my typing the last word of this article tomorrow morning. Relative to my current reference frame, that event is in the future. But there exists a distant observer, moving at an appropriate velocity relative to me, for whom that very event is simultaneous with their current "now" — the event lies on their simultaneity plane.

(4) If an event is simultaneous with an observer's present experience, then that event is real. It cannot be the case that the observer's present is real while something simultaneous with their present is unreal.

(5) Therefore, tomorrow's typing event is already real — not merely will be real, but exists with the same ontological status as events occurring right now.

(6) By parity of reasoning, the same holds for any event in anyone's past or future: for any event, there is some possible observer for whom it is simultaneous with their present. Hence all events in spacetime are equally real.

(7) Conclusion: the distinction between past, present, and future is not a feature of reality itself but a perspective-dependent illusion. The universe is a four-dimensional block in which time does not pass.

I'm reluctant to accept (7), but I'm not sure where the argument goes wrong. I find myself wanting to keep (1) and (2), since they seem like well-established physics. I also feel the pull of (4) — if something is happening "now" for you, it seems odd to say it does not exist. But the conclusion strikes me as extracting much more metaphysics from relativity than the theory can be expected to deliver. Which premise should I give up, and what does that choice commit me to?
