I keep noticing a pattern in things I read about pain, and I don't have a word for it. I'm hoping someone can tell me whether there's an established concept — or if there isn't one, help me carve out what exactly the phenomenon is.

Here's what I mean. In several quite different contexts, people seem to report having pain without suffering from it. They can locate the pain, describe its intensity and quality, but they don't find it unpleasant, don't want it to stop, and don't try to escape whatever is causing it. The clearest cases I've read about are neurological: patients with damage to a particular brain region who, when pricked with a pin, will say something like "yes, that hurts — it's sharp and located right there" but they don't flinch, don't withdraw, and when asked whether it bothers them they seem not to understand the question. Then there are the things patients on morphine sometimes say: "the pain is still there, I just don't care about it anymore." And I've also come across descriptions of experienced meditators, and occasionally of people in states of extreme focus — soldiers wounded in combat, athletes mid-game — who report being aware of a painful injury but not being troubled by it in the moment.

What strikes me as philosophically important is this: if the sensation and the suffering can come apart, then the badness of pain doesn't seem to be built into the sensation itself. It's something extra, something that normally rides along with the sensation but can in principle be detached. That seems like it should matter for a lot of things — for theories of well-being, for how we think about the experiences of non-human animals, for what medicine is actually targeting when it treats pain.

So my question: does this dissociation have an established name in philosophy or in the sciences? If there are several nearby concepts, what are they, and how does what I'm describing relate to each of them? And if the phenomenon is real, what exactly is the "something extra" that turns bare pain-sensation into something aversive? I should say upfront that I'm not just after a label — I want to understand what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, and where its edges are.
