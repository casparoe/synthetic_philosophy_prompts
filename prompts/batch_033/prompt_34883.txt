Ok so I'm in this grad seminar on contemporary ethics and we've been going deep on the aggregation debate — you know, Taurek vs. Kamm vs. Parfit, the whole "should the numbers count" thing. The professor keeps pressing us to figure out what we actually think, not just what sounds clever in discussion, and I realized I'm honestly not sure where I land. Like I say I'd save the five, but then I read this one case about iterated rescues and suddenly I'm not so sure. And my friend in the class keeps insisting that if you really think each person matters equally, you have to flip a coin, which sounds insane but I can't find the flaw in his arguement.

So I had this idea to make a sorting quiz for my study group — like a "which position on aggregation do you actually hold?" thing that we could all take before the next session. But I want it to actually work, not just be one of those quizzes where you can guess the philosopher by question two. The questions should be concrete rescue scenarios where you have to pick what to do, and the scoring key should map your pattern of answers onto the real positions in the literature.

The positions I want as leaves: pure aggregation (always save the many, no exceptions), Taurek's coin-flip egalitarianism (each person's claim is separate so you flip a coin), weighted lottery (give each person a chance proportional to their group size), and maybe a threshold view where numbers count but only past some minimum difference. If there are other logical positions that my questions uncover but nobody defends — like, idk, "always save the few" or something — flag those too and say why they're empty.

Can you write the quiz? Like 8-10 questions, each a specific scenario with multiple choice answers, and then a scoring key. The scenarios should vary the numbers, whether you know the people, whether the choice repeats, whether the harm is the same kind, etc. And no questions that are just "do you agree with Taurek?" — the whole point is that the cases sort you without you knowing which philosopher your siding with.
