I'm giving a department seminar next month defending the truth norm of assertion — the view that the rule is "assert p only if p is true" — to a department that's basically ground zero for the knowledge norm. Most of them work in epistemology, several cite Williamson like scripture, one of the senior people has published defenses of the knowledge norm in top journals. They're collegial but they think the truth norm is obviously wrong and they'll come loaded for bear.

I need a Q&A prep sheet tailored to *this* audience, not a generic one. Here's what I want:

**Section 1: What to concede in the talk itself** (about half a page). Stuff I should grant up front so we don't waste Q&A time on things I already agree with — e.g., I'm not denying that knowledge matters epistemically, I'm not saying justification is irrelevant to the ethics of assertion, etc.

**Section 2: The objections, in order** (the bulk — maybe 7-8 objections, a paragraph or two each). Order them by roughly when they'll come up in Q&A (first objections first). For each: (a) how they'll actually phrase it, in their vocabulary, with the commitments that make it salient to them; (b) my two-minute reply; (c) an honest note on where the reply is weakest or what I'm hoping they won't press.

**Section 3: The question I'm dreading** (a paragraph). The one objection that really exposes a soft spot in the truth norm that I don't have a clean answer for, and what I should say if someone asks it.

My argument sketch, so you know what the objections will be targeting: I'm going to argue that (i) the knowledge norm can't explain why we criticize false-but-well-justified assertions — the lottery case cuts both ways and they need to stop acting like it doesn't; (ii) the truth norm handles Moore-paradoxical assertions more cleanly because the problem is the falsity of the embedded belief report or the truth-aim mismatch, not a knowledge failure; (iii) the knowledge norm overgenerates — it rules too many ordinary assertions out of bounds, like when someone asserts something true on the basis of a Gettiered justification and we don't actually blame them; and (iv) if assertion is the external face of belief, and belief constitutively aims at truth (not knowledge), then the constitutive norm of assertion should be truth too.
