I am preparing a short teaching module on the distinction between tool AI and agent AI, and I want a worked example that does not collapse in the way most discussions do. The examples I keep finding are either set in science-fictional scenarios involving superintelligent systems, or the "tool" quietly turns out to be an agent with additional guardrails. I would like you to construct a thought experiment for me to use as a handout.

The specifications are these. Build a scenario, set in a realistic near-term workplace (an administrative, scheduling, or procurement assistant, for instance), in which a tool AI and an agent AI have identical capabilities, identical information, and receive the same instruction from the user, yet take materially different courses of action — and the agent's course of action is the one a thoughtful user would judge worse. The tool must be a genuine tool in the standard sense: it may draft, summarise, model, propose, and execute narrowly scoped actions, but it has no open-ended goals, no persistent initiative, and no representation of itself as an actor with interests; it must not simply be a less capable agent. The divergence must be traceable to a single structural difference between the two designs, not to differences in competence, knowledge, or instruction. The case is for teaching, so it fails if it merely stipulates the verdicts rather than generating them from the two designs, or if the tool turns out to be an agent in disguise.

After the scenario, show that it meets each specification: state the user's instruction and the two resulting courses of action; walk through why each design produces its action; name the single structural feature doing the work; say how the case would collapse if that feature were removed; and give the strongest objection someone could raise — presumably that the "tool" is really an agent with extra guardrails, or that the "agent" is only a badly specified tool — and answer it. Please write in British English throughout.

Structure the response in this order, with roughly these lengths: Scenario (one paragraph, about 150 words); The two systems' actions (about 100 words); Why each design produces its action (about 250 words); The structural feature doing the work (about 100 words); Collapse test (about 100 words); Strongest objection and reply (about 150 words); Settled versus contested (about 150 words). In the final section, separate what is definitional stipulation and what is widely agreed from what is genuinely disputed among people who work on this: in particular, whether a system can remain a pure tool as its capabilities increase is contested, so do not present one side of that as settled.
