My dad died about three years ago, and I keep noticing something odd. I'll be in the middle of a decision and I'll think "dad would say X" and then I'll sort of have a little internal conversation with him, and it genuinely feels like consulting him — not just remembering what he'd say, but almost like he's still there as a presence in my head making the case. And sometimes he even "says" things I don't think I'd ever heard him say in real life, but they feel exactly like him — like my brain is running a simulation of him that's good enough to generate new thoughts in his voice.

I mentioned this to a friend and she said it was sweet but basically just memory and projection, nothing deep. But I'm not sure. It feels like more than that. It feels like a little bit of him is still actually here, distributed across the people who knew him well. Is that a totally daft way to think about it, or is there something to the idea that a self can persist in a distributed way across other minds? And if there is something to it, does that change how I should think about grief, or about what a person even is?

I'd prefer the answer in British English please.
