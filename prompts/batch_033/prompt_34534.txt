I am taking a course on social choice and collective decision-making, and we have just worked through the discursive dilemma. Before I read any more proposals for aggregating individual judgments into collective ones, I want the specification they will be tested against. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of how a group should form a collective judgment on a set of logically connected propositions has to deliver, assuming the group is supposed to be able to act as a coherent agent over time, not merely to tally independent votes. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and say whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp.

Make sure the list includes at least: universal domain; collective consistency and completeness; deductive closure; consensus preservation; propositionwise independence, and the stronger systematicity condition; anonymity; non-dictatorship; monotonicity or responsiveness; and premise-sensitivity or reason-tracking. Use the doctrinal paradox — three judges deciding whether a defendant is liable, where liability requires both breach and a valid contract, and the majority holds breach, holds valid contract, and denies liability — as the central case for the inconsistency problem, but do not stop there: give the consideration behind each criterion, not just a label.

Then the part I most want. Given the impossibility theorems in the judgment-aggregation literature, tell me which subsets of these desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied, and lay out the real options: weaken collective rationality by allowing incompleteness rather than inconsistency, drop independence or systematicity, restrict the admissible agendas, or accept something dictatorial. Say which single requirement does the most work in excluding otherwise attractive rules, and why. Finally, in a paragraph or a small table, score premise-based voting, conclusion-based voting, and a distance-based rule against the list — but keep that brief; the criteria are the point.
