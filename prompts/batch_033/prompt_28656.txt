I've been turning over a pattern I keep running into, and I'm hoping someone can tell me whether it has an established name — and if it doesn't, what it should be called.

The pattern goes like this. Someone is offered a thing they badly want, but getting it requires being able to make a promise, or form a genuine intention, to do something later that, when later arrives, they will have no reason at all to do — and everyone in the situation knows this. A driver won't stop unless you can convince him you'll pay after the ride, even though he knows that once you're out of the car you'd have no reason to hand over the money. A billionaire will pay you tomorrow morning if you can sincerely intend tonight to make yourself sick tomorrow afternoon, and the money gets paid whether or not you actually follow through. Faking the intention doesn't help, because the other party — or the setup itself — can tell the differnce between a real intention and a plan you'll drop the moment it stops paying. So the only route to the benefit seems to be to actually become the kind of person who will do the later thing: to make your future self act against its own reasons, out of stubborness or honor or some disposition you can't switch off.

What's strange to me is that this move looks rational and irrational at once. It's rational because it's the only way to get what you want. But you're also deliberately paying to acquire a disposition to act against your own interests later, which sounds like a definition of being irrational. I know about commitment devices, but those usually work through external scaffolding — contracts, reputation, burning bridges, making the bad option impossible or costly. Here there's nothing external; the enforcement is entirely inside your own head, and what you're installing isn't a constraint on your options but a change in what you'll find yourself doing. So: does this configuration have a name of its own, or is it just a special case of something broader? If it has a name, I want more than the label — I want it carved off from the neighboring things, especially ordinary self-control and the reverse of weakness of will. If it doesn't have a name, coin one and define it with clear edges. Please answer as exactly five numbered items, a sentence or two each, no introduction or conclusion.
