I've been reading about Molinism lately and I keep running into an argument that seems to show the view self-destructs, which is weird because the whole point of the view was to avoid exactly this outcome. I'd be grateful if you could tell me which step to reject.

(1) Suppose Molinism is true: before creating anything, God has middle knowledge, which includes counterfactuals of creaturely freedom — for every possible free creature and every possible circumstance, God knows what that creature would freely choose in that circumstance.

(2) When God decides to create, he surveys the feasible worlds — the worlds he can actualize, given which counterfactuals of freedom happen to be true — and actualizes one of them, knowing in advance what every creature in that world would freely do.

(3) Once God actualizes a world, everything you will do is already settled by two things: the circumstances God placed you in, and the counterfactual of freedom that was true before creation. Since the counterfactual says that in those circumstances you would do A, and those circumstances now obtain, your doing A is already settled.

(4) If your doing A was already settled before you existed, then there is no possible course of events in which you do otherwise.

(5) If there is no possible course of events in which you do otherwise, then you could not have done otherwise.

(6) If you could not have done otherwise, you did not act freely.

(7) So on Molinism, no creature ever acts freely.

(8) But Molinism was introduced precisely to show that God's providence and foreknowledge are compatible with genuine creaturely freedom. So Molinism refutes itself.

I have a feeling something slips around (4) or (5), but I can't articulate why its a slip rather than a valid inference — once the counterfactual is true and the circumstances obtain, what room is left for an alternative possibility? If I had to pick, I'd rather keep (1) and the motivation in (8) than give up Molinism, but I want to know what I'm actually paying for that. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
