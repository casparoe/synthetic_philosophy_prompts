So I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about trading one kind of thing for another, and my gut reactions don't add up, and I want to know if there's a principle that gets them or if I should just accept that I'm inconsistent.

Here are my verdicts on some cases.

If my friend asks me to skip lunch with him so I can take a $40 tutoring gig, I'd do it and not feel bad at all. It's just lunch, we hang out all the time.

If it's my best friend's birthday dinner, and the same kind of gig pays $200, I still wouldn't skip it. And it's not that I'd do it for $500 either. If someone asked "how much money would make you miss it," the question itself feels kind of wrong, like their forcing me to compare things that don't go on the same scale.

When people play "would you do it for a million dollars," like would I quit the basketball team for a million, I never know what to say. Its not that I'd say no, and its not that I'd say yes. It feels like the question has no answer, not like the answer is just hard to find.

Last month I had to choose between finishing a huge history project and going to my little sister's school play. That one felt hard in a specific way. It wasn't that I couldn't tell which was better. It was more like whichever I picked, something real would be lost, and the other thing couldn't make up for it.

But I also think some trade-offs obviously have a right answer. My mom asked whether I'd rather get an extra hour of sleep or do the extra credit that could bump my grade, and I said the grade, duh. Like, that's not a hard choice.

And here's the part that bugs me: I said you can't put a price on friendship, but I skip hanging out with one of my friends like twice a month to tutor, and I'm literally doing it for money. I don't think that's wrong. So maybe I do put a price on it, or maybe those are different somehow.

Is there one principle that captures all of these, or close? If there is, what does it say about a case I keep going back and forth on: skipping my grandma's 80th birthday for a one-time $500 gig that I don't strictly need. And if there isn't a principle that gets all my verdicts, which ones do I have to give up and why?
