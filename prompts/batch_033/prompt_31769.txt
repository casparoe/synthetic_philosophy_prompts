I'm on the editorial board of my school's student newspaper, and before Thursday's meeting I need to circulate a written opinion about whether we should run a piece on a claim in the student-body presidential election. One candidate, Maya, has been telling voters that the administration is "quietly planning to eliminate the music program next year." Our reporting found no evidence for this: the proposed budget keeps the program fully funded, the principal says there is no such plan, and Maya has produced no document supporting the claim. The claim is working—it is the most-repeated line of her campaign, and many students now believe it. The board is split. Some members want a prominent front-page fact-check, on the ground that the claim is a false factual assertion and a newspaper's job is to report what is true, not to treat every assertion as one more opinion. Others want us to stay out of the election entirely: they say campaigns always exaggerate, that the claim expresses a real and reasonable fear about arts funding, and that correcting one candidate mid-election would be taking sides. Our faculty advisor has framed the disagreement in terms of Hannah Arendt's writing on lying in politics—her distinction between factual truth and opinion, and her worry that when factual assertions are treated as just another partisan position, the shared factual ground that political argument needs gets damaged. The candidates' debate is Friday and voting opens Monday, so we have to decide this week. I lean toward publishing something, but I'm not sure whether it should be a front-page fact-check, a short news item, or an unsigned editorial, and I want the memo to take the losing side's strongest argument seriously rather than dismiss it. Please draft the short memo I will circulate. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame, explain how that course handles the Arendtian concern, and address the strongest consideration for the option you reject. Begin with the memo itself, with no preface or commentary.
