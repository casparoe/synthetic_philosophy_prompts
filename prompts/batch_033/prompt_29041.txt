I recently started as an operations manager at a small manufacturing firm. On my first week, I noticed that every morning a senior technician spends about forty-five minutes walking the factory floor, recording temperature and humidity from a dozen analog gauges into a paper logbook — even though the same data is captured digitally by our climate control system. I proposed eliminating the manual logging. Several longtime employees objected, but when I pressed them, no one could give me a concrete reason. The closest thing to an argument I got was from a foreman who said: "I don't know why we do it either, but I've learned not to scrap a practice until I understand what problem it was solving." I find this maddening. It looks like pure institutional inertia, and I'm tempted to just issue the change. Am I being unreasonable?
