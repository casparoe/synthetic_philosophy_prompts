I keep running into this analogy in philosophy of mind: consciousness is like the steam whistle on a train — the brain does all the causal work, and conscious experience is just a byproduct, like the whistle that makes noise but doesnt actually drive the wheels. (I think it goes back to Huxley.) The conclusion people draw is epiphenomenalism: consciousness exists but has no causal power. But then I hear about the paradox of phenomenal judgment — if consciousness has no causal power, how are we even forming judgments about it and writing papers about it? And doesn't that undermine epiphenomenalism itself? I want you to audit the whistle analogy: what corresponds to what, where it breaks, what it quietly imports, and whether the epiphenomenalist conclusion survives the disanalogies — especially once the paradox of phenomenal judgment is on the table. And tell me how confident you are and what point you're shakiest on.
