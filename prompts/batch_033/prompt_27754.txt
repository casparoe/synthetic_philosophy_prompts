I'm a novelist, about halfway through a book centered on a woman with a diagnosis that's contested — the kind of thing where half the doctors say it's a real disease and the other half say it's somatization or whatever. The novel isn't a polemic; I want it to actually sit inside the philosophical problem of what makes something a disease in the first place. I've read a bit of Boorse and some of the disability-studies responses, so I get the basic naturalism-vs-normativism split, but I'm worried I'm working with the undergraduate version of the debate. What I need isn't a survey — it's a prioritization. Among the open questions in that debate right now, which two or three are the ones where progress would most change how we should think about contested illnesses? I'm especially interested in the fault lines where the philosophical dispute would actually alter how a doctor treats a patient or how a diagnostic manual gets written — as opposed to disputes that matter for journals but cash out the same in practice. And please flag where the consensus among people who work on this would suprise a smart lay reader, because those are the scenes I want to write. Separate what's settled from what's contested; I don't want to write a novel that treats one side of a live debate as obviously correct.
