I have a recurring argument with my cousin that has started to feel less like a disagreement about the topic at hand and more like a disagreement about arguing itself. Recently we were discussing whether a mutual friend should be trusted with a spare key to our apartment. I put it this way: if someone has always returned borrowed things promptly, then they can be trusted with a key; this friend has always returned borrowed things promptly; so this friend can be trusted with a key. My cousin said he accepts both starting claims — he agrees with the general claim and with the particular fact about our friend — but he still will not accept the conclusion. When I asked why not, he said he simply does not see how the two starting claims force the conclusion. So I laid out the rule I was using: from a claim of the form "if this, then that" together with "this," you are entitled to move to "that." He said he accepts that rule too. But then he said that accepting the rule just gives us a third starting claim, and he still does not see how the three of them force the conclusion unless we add yet another rule saying that when you have the first two claims plus the rule, you may move to the conclusion — and so on. Every time I supplied the missing piece, he placed it back among the starting claims and asked what forces the next step. Eventually I gave up in frustration.

He insists he is not being stubborn for its own sake; he says he genuinely cannot see why someone who accepts the starting claims is required to accept the conclusion, and that I keep helping myself to an extra step without saying so. Part of me believes he must be making a simple mistake or playing a kind of game. But I cannot say exactly what the mistake is, and whenever I try to corner him, the same thing happens one level up. What is really going on here? Is there a way to present the original argument that would satisfy him, or is he raising a genuine point about what it means to be entitled to draw a conclusion? And if he is making a mistake, what exactly is it?
