I'm giving a seminar next month to the epistemology group of a philosophy department whose members mostly work in formal epistemology and Bayesian decision theory. I'll be defending the claim that higher-order evidence—evidence about the quality of one's own reasoning, such as learning that one is sleep-deprived, biased, or in disagreement with an epistemic peer—can make it rational to be epistemically akratic: to believe a proposition while believing that one ought not to believe it. My argument, in brief, is that rationality is fundamentally a matter of responding correctly to evidence at each level, that first-order and higher-order evidence can pull in opposite directions, and that in such cases the enkratic requirement is the rational requirement that gives way rather than a fixed point.

The topic became personal for me after I served on a jury last year and only afterward learned how severely sleep-deprived I had been throughout the trial; the more I tried to reconstruct what I should have believed when we returned the verdict, the less the standard anti-akrasia picture seemed to describe my situation.

The audience will be friendly to the idea that higher-order evidence matters. Several of them work on peer disagreement and accept conciliatory views, but they insist that conciliation never requires akrasia. They treat the enkratic requirement as close to a fixed point: they hold that it is always irrational to believe a proposition while believing that one ought not to believe it, and they tend to model rationality as a set of coherence constraints on credences rather than as responsiveness to evidence. They will resist the idea that any evidence could license an akratic state.

Please draft my Q&A prep sheet. I need the eight or so objections I am actually likely to receive from this group, ordered by how damaging they are, each phrased the way a formal epistemologist would put it, with a roughly two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is soft. Include what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and the question I am hoping nobody asks. Please write the prep sheet in American English.
