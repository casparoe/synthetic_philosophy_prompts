I'm a novelist — literary fiction, midlist, you know the deal — and at my writing group last week we got into it about whether we should still be reading and discussing this one writer after some pretty grim stuff came out about him. Someone shut the whole thing down with 'you have to separate the art from the artist,' and everyone nodded like that settled it. But I've been turning it over since and I realize I genuinely don't know what claim is being made. When a reader says it they usually mean something like 'don't let the biography spoil your experience of the work,' but when a critic says it they might mean something about where meaning resides, and when a publisher says it they definitly mean 'keep buying the books.' I want to go back next week with a clear map. Can you lay out the distinct precise theses hiding inside 'separate the art from the artist,' from the trivial to the radical, and say for each: is it obviously true, substantive but defensible, or probably false? And which reading would actually do the work my friend was trying to make it do — namely, settling whether we should keep reading and discussing the guy's novels in a group that includes people who feel genuinely hurt by what he did?
