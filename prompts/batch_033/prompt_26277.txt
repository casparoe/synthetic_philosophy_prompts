The group is mostly ML grad students with a couple philosophers and one cognitive science postdoc mixed in. The topic is Bongard problems — those little grids where you get six boxes on the left, six on the right, and you have to figure out the rule that separates them. The group read the Hofstadter chapter from Gödel, Escher, Bach but that's it, so they basically think of Bongard problems as charming brain-teasers rather than as a genuine challenge to theories of induction and concept learning.

I've got about forty-five minutes and I want them to come away understanding why Bongard problems are philosophically and computationally deep — specifically, why inducing a concept from half a dozen positive and negative examples is a hard problem in a way that isn't just "well you need more data." What has to come first in the session? What depends on what?
