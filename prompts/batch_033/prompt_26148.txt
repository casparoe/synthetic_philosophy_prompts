I've been reading about epiphenomenalism and I'm stuck on the paradox of phenomenal judgment. The basic argument seems to be: if phenomenal consciousness is epiphenomenal, then our judgments about our own conscious states (like "I'm in pain right now") can't be caused by those conscious states. But then why would those judgments be accurate? The standard reply is that the neural correlates cause both the phenomenal state and the judgment, so there's a common-cause explanation for the correlation. But here's what I don't get: even if a common cause explains why my judgment "I'm in pain" correlates with actually being in pain, it doesn't seem to explain why the judgment is *about* pain — why it has that intentional content rather than just being, say, a judgment about C-fibers firing. Can you explain whether this "aboutness" objection is actually distinct from the original paradox, and whether the epiphenomenalist has any good moves here? I feel like I'm missing something.
