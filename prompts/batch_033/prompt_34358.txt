I've spent the last couple of years reading about the hard problem of consciousness on my own — Chalmers, Dennett, Frankish, Goff, and a lot of online discussion — and I keep noticing that the two sides talk past each other. One side says there is an explanatory gap between physical processes and subjective experience; the other says the gap is an illusion generated by our cognitive architecture. I want to see what would happen if a defender of each position sat down and tried to produce an adversarial-collaboration document.

Draft that document for them. The two positions are: (1) The hard problem is real and distinct from the easy problems — explaining why and how physical processes are accompanied by subjective experience requires something beyond our current physical framework, whether that is a new fundamental principle, an expanded ontology, or a revision of what we count as explanation. (2) The hard problem is a cognitive illusion — once all the easy problems about access, reportability, behavioral integration, and the functional role of conscious states are solved, there is no further fact about consciousness left unexplained; the sense that something remains is a trick of introspection.

The document should include: a numbered list of claims both sides can sign (about what the easy problems cover, about what introspection does and does not reveal, about what neuroscience has and has not explained); a disagreements table with each side's position stated in wording its own defenders would accept; a list of cruxes — sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and for each crux, a concrete test, case, or line of argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I am especially interested in whether any part of their disagreement turns out to be verbal rather than substantive, and if so, which part.
