The way I understand IIT is roughly this: a system is conscious to the extent that it has integrated information. You start with the system's full cause-effect repertoire — the set of all possible past and future states it can transition through — and then you try to partition it. You cut the system into parts, and you ask: how much information is lost? If you can decompose the system into independent modules without losing much information, Φ is low. If every partition destroys a lot of information about the system's causal dynamics, Φ is high. Consciousness just IS high Φ. That's the claim. So a photodiode has basically zero Φ (it's just one element), a digital camera sensor has low Φ (the pixels are causally independent of each other — you can split the grid and lose nothing), and a brain has high Φ because the neurons are all tangled up in ways that resist clean decomposition. The theory also has this "exclusion" postulate — only the maximum-Φ system within a set of overlapping systems is the conscious one — and five axioms: intrinsic existence, composition, information, integration, and exclusion.
