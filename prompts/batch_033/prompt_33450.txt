I've been reading the behavioral genetics literature lately and I'm struck by how people who agree on the formal definition of heritability — h² is the proportion of phenotypic variance in a population attributable to genetic variance — still disagree sharply about what a high estimate actually licenses. One side says: if h² for a trait is high in a given population, then the environmental variation present in that population isn't doing much explanatory work; this constrains purely environmental theories and tells you where to look for causes of individual differences. The other side says: h² tells you about variance partitioning under one set of environmental conditions and says nothing about what would happen if you changed those conditions, nothing about individual causation, and nothing about group differences — and since it is so routinely misinterpreted along exactly these lines, the concept may do more harm than good.

I'd like you to draft the joint statement that a thoughtful representative of each camp could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Include: (1) claims both can agree on, stated precisely; (2) the exact disagreements, each phrased so its own side would endorse the wording; (3) the cruxes — sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, the kind of evidence or argument both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction. If any part of the dispute is merely verbal, say so and explain.

Please begin the response with the joint statement itself, with no preface or commentary.
