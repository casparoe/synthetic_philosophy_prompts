I keep noticing this pattern and I wonder if philosophy or psychology has a name for it. Sometimes it feels like my brain is editing my experience after the fact. Like, I'll mishear what someone said, but a split-second later the "corrected" version pops into my head, and I genuinely can't tell whether I consciously heard the wrong word and then my memory got rewritten, or whether my brain simply delayed my conscious experience until it had sorted out the right interpretation. The same thing happens with sudden realizations — I'll have a moment where I "remember" something that happened earlier in the day, but the memory feels so vivid I'm not sure if I actually experienced it at the time and forgot, or if my brain is constructing it now and backdating the timestamp.

It seems like there are two fundamentally differnt things that could be going on: either (a) I had the original experience and it got overwritten, or (b) I never had the original experience — my brain just made me think I did, after the fact. And from the inside, I can't tell the difference. Is there an established term for this distinction? And does the distinction even matter, or are these two descriptions just different ways of telling the same story?
