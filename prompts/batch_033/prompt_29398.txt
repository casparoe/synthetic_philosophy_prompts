I was reading a piece on a philosophy blog that summarized David Chalmers's recent work on the meta-problem of consciousness. Here is the relevant passage:

"Chalmers distinguishes the 'hard problem' of consciousness — explaining why physical processing gives rise to subjective experience at all — from what he calls the 'meta-problem': explaining why we are so strongly disposed to believe there is a hard problem in the first place. The meta-problem asks where our sense of an inner mental life comes from, and Chalmers argues it is empirically tractable: cognitive science can investigate the mechanisms that generate our intuitions about qualia, the explanatory gap, and the conceivability of zombies. The crucial point is that if we can fully explain, in physical terms, why humans cannot help but think consciousness is non-physical, then we have removed the only reason we had for taking the hard problem seriously. Chalmers himself has acknowledged this, which is why his recent turn toward the meta-problem is widely read as a major concession to illusionists like Dennett and Frankish — he has effectively admitted that the hard problem may dissolve once we understand our own cognitive architecture."
