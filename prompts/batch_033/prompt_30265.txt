I've spent the past few months reading about AI alignment through blog posts and online forums rather than academic work, and I've gradually assembled a position on a proposal I keep encountering called iterated amplification and distillation. As I understand it, the idea is to take a task too hard for one person to supervise directly, break it into subtasks simple enough that a human can reliably judge whether each was done well, train a model on those human judgments, and then compress the resulting system into a smaller, faster model by having it imitate the larger one; you repeat this process to climb toward systems that are more capable than any individual human. I'm writing out the package of views I've ended up with, and I'd like to know whether it hangs together before I commit to it.

Here is the package:

(1) Human values cannot be written down as a specification, but a careful human can reliably recognize better and worse behavior on a concrete, narrowly scoped subtask, so piece-by-piece human evaluation can serve as the ground for alignment.

(2) If a hard task is decomposed thoughtfully by a human and each subtask is carried out by a model trained on human evaluations of that subtask, then the composed system as a whole is aligned, because alignment is preserved under composition.

(3) Distilling a large, slow model into a small, fast one is safe as long as the student is trained to imitate the teacher on a broad and diverse distribution of examples; the student inherits the teacher's competence and values on anything near that distribution.

(4) At the same time, I think current models show that a system can perform well on every case you test and still fail or drift on genuinely novel inputs, because imitation does not reliably transfer far outside the training distribution.

(5) I also think iterated amplification and distillation is the most promising route I know of to building systems that are both more capable than any individual human and aligned.

I honestly can't tell which parts of this are carrying the load or where the strain is. Could you go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look as though they are, and rank the genuine tensions by severity? For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would cost.
