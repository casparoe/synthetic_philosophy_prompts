I want to understand whether her present self is rationally required to defer to that future self. This seems to connect to Van Fraassen's Reflection principle — roughly, the idea that your current credence in a proposition should equal the weighted average of the credences you expect to have later, so that if you're sure you'll believe P at 0.9 tomorrow, you should believe it at 0.9 today.

So here's what I'd like: an essay on whether Reflection is a real constraint on rational belief. Also keep two questions apart that I keep conflating: whether she should already believe what her future self will believe, and whether she should take steps now to constrain or outsource her decisions to that future self. Don't end on a fence. Reach a conclusion about how much of the principle survives, and say explicitly what it implies about my historian. I don't need footnotes or citations, just the argument.
