I've been working through some material on factored cognition, and I keep coming back to a worry about the HCH (Humans Consulting HCH) idealization that I can't quite shake. As I understand it, HCH is defined recursively: a human at the top can decompose a problem and delegate subproblems to subagents, each of which is itself an HCH, and the recursion bottoms out in subproblems small enough for a single human to answer directly in a short amount of time. The usual motivation is that this gives a model of how a system could do far more total cognitive work than any individual human while remaining aligned, because every local step is just a human judgment.

But here is what confuses me. Since every subagent in the tree is the same human — same knowledge, same reasoning patterns, same values — the whole structure looks as though it should collapse into that one human simply thinking about the problem for a very long time. A parallel tree of copies of me does not seem to add any capability that serial me lacks, because no node ever does anything I could not do myself. I can see that the tree offers parallelism, but parallelism by itself seems to be only a speedup, and a speedup does not seem to justify treating HCH as a fundamentally different kind of system. So why is HCH treated as an interesting idealization of aligned superintelligence rather than just a person given arbitrarily much time? What exactly does the recursion buy, and where does my collapse picture go wrong?
