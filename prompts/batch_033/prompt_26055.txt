I have been thinking about how much of what I take myself to know comes from other people. Almost everything, really — history, science, geography, news of current events. But when I examine what is actually happening when someone tells me something, it seems clear that testimony is not a genuine source of knowledge in its own right. It is simply one person reporting what they claim to know. If I cannot verify the claim through my own senses or my own reasoning, then I do not actually know it — I am merely taking someone's word for it. It seems to follow that the only intellectually honest stance is to treat unverified testimony as hearsay and to restrict real knowledge to what I have confirmed for myself. Yet philosophers seem to resist this conclusion. What am I missing?
