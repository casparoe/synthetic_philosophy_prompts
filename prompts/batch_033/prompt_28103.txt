I'm taking an elective called "Freedom and Responsibility" this semester, and our teacher has an unusual teaching style: she never tells us what theory of free will she endorses. Instead, each week she presents a detailed scenario and then gives her verdict on whether the person in it is morally responsible for what they did. We are supposed to work out the underlying principle ourselves, and at the end of the term we each have to submit a paper identifying her theory and defending or criticizing it. I have been keeping careful notes. So far, her verdicts are:

1. A bank customer hands over her wallet because someone is holding a gun to her head. Verdict: not responsible.
2. A woman in a fully deterministic universe weighs the pros and cons of two job offers and chooses one after careful deliberation. Verdict: responsible.
3. A normally gentle man develops a brain lesion that causes aggressive outbursts; during one such outburst he assaults a stranger. Verdict: not responsible.
4. A man decides on his own to lie to his business partner; unbeknownst to him, a device implanted in his brain would have forced him to lie if he had shown any sign of telling the truth, but the device never activates. Verdict: responsible.
5. A person raised from infancy in an isolated cult, who has never encountered alternative viewpoints, acts on beliefs instilled by the cult's leader and harms someone. Verdict: not responsible.
6. In an indeterministic universe, a person's decision is the direct result of a random quantum event, bearing no connection to her character, values, or deliberation. Verdict: not responsible.

Next week's case, which she previewed at the end of class, is this: a person was raised in a cult just like in case 5, but she left the cult at age twenty-five. Now, at age thirty-five, after a decade of exposure to the outside world, diverse perspectives, and many opportunities to reconsider her beliefs, she commits a crime motivated by beliefs she still holds from her cult upbringing. She has encountered arguments against those beliefs and has rejected them.

I need to predict what verdict our teacher will give on this new case. More importantly, I need to identify which theory or theories of free will and moral responsibility best fit her track record so far, say what each candidate theory predicts about the new case, and give my best guess with reasons. If two theories both fit all six earlier cases but diverge on the new one, I would also like to know what further case would cleanly tell them apart — something I could suggest to her as a follow-up.
