(a) **Bald-faced lying** — you assert something you know is false, and the listener also knows you know it's false, but you say it anyway (like a politician saying something obviously untrue in a press conference, where everyone in the room knows it's nonsense).

(b) **Ordinary lying** — you assert something you believe false, and the listener trusts you and believes it.

(c) **Misleading by implicature** — you say something strictly true, but you know the listener will draw a false conclusion from it, and you're counting on that (the classic "he's not in the office today" when you know he's working from home but the caller will think he's out sick).

(d) **Paltering** — you use a true statement to create a false impression, like when a negotiator says "we've had offers as high as $X" which is technically true but that offer was from three years ago and isn't on the table anymore.

(e) **Lying by omission** — you stay silent when you know that your silence will lead someone to maintain a false belief they already have, and you could easily correct it without any real cost.
