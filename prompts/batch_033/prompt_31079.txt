Last month a colleague invoked it to dismiss a junior researcher's question about whether the wavefunction is a real physical field or merely a bookkeeping device for assigning probabilities; the colleague said this was precisely the sort of question the slogan correctly rules out. I would like to know what, exactly, that sentence is claiming, because it seems to me that it can mean several quite different things depending on how it is pressed.

Please act as an expert philosopher of physics explaining this to a newcomer who wants precision rather than a party line. Think carefully, and begin by brainstorming the distinct precise theses that "shut up and calculate" could be used to express, from the obviously true to the radical or clearly false. Then address the use my colleague made of it: which of these readings would genuinely support dismissing the question of whether the wavefunction is real, which readings would merely change the subject or impose a sociological norm, and which readings would beg the question by smuggling in a particular interpretation — for instance, a deflationary or Copenhagen-like one — under the guise of neutrality.
