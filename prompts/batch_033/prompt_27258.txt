I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on free will and moral responsibility, and I am setting the final exam. I would like one essay question, to be answered in 60 minutes, on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument that cannot be answered well merely by reciting the argument's familiar steps. The question should force students to do three things: explain why the Basic Argument is a regress argument about self-creation rather than a bare complaint that one did not create oneself; explain why the argument is not a version of the consequence argument and does not stand or fall with determinism, since the regress would persist even if one's choices were undetermined; and identify the premise a compatibilist should reject, then assess whether rejecting it leaves compatibilism with a conception of responsibility robust enough to ground ordinary blame and resentment, or only a deflated, merely causal notion of an action's being one's own. Please write the question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 marks. The marking scheme should name the specific moves that separate an A from a B answer and should flag common ways students misstate the argument—for example, conflating it with the claim that determinism rules out alternative possibilities, or reading the premise that responsibility for an action requires responsibility for the way one is as the obviously false demand that one be literally self-created. Because the underlying issue is genuinely contestable, the model answer should say which single objection or alternative consideration would be most likely to change its verdict, and the marking scheme should specify what a student would need to argue to earn comparable credit for a contrary conclusion.
