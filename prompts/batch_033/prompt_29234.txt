So I just got back from arguing with a friend for hours about horror movies, and we kept getting stuck on the same thing: when you know the monster isnt real, is the fear you feel during the movie actual fear, or some kind of pretend fear? Write a dialogue between two people who are both sharp and charitable about each other's view. One holds that the emotion is genuine and of the same kind as ordinary fear or sadness — it just gets aimed at a thought or an imagined thing, and you don't need to believe something exists to be moved by it. The other holds that it's only a make-believe or quasi-emotion, like a kid playing monsters, and that calling it real fear papers over a genuine difference. Have them actually respond to each other's points instead of taking turns, and make them push on the awkward cases: why we don't run out of the theater if it's real fear, why the tears feel involuntary if it's only pretend, why we can feel relieved when a fictional character escapes. Make each side get forced to sharpen or concede something at least once. And write the whole thing in American English.
