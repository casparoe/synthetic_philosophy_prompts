i'm a novelist and for my sins i've been on the jury for a mid-level literary prize the last two years. not the booker or anything, just a regional thing, but still — we read a hundred and fifty novels and pick a winner. here's what's been eating at me.

the shortlist meeting is brutal. five of us in a room with a whiteboard, and by the end of the day we have to produce a ranked list. but the books are so different from each other it feels almost absurd to put them in order. one year we had a very quiet, flawless domestic novel — the kind where every sentence is exactly right but nothing much happens — up against a sprawling sci-fi thing that was full of holes but also full of ideas i genuinely hadn't seen before. the committee went back and forth for an hour and eventually the quiet novel won because three of us thought it was "better executed." but what does "better executed" even mean when one book is trying to nail down the inner life of a marriage and the other is trying to imagine an alien political system? they're not attempting the same thing. the criteria for success are internal to the project, and the projects themselves seem incommensurable.

but we DO rank them. we always do. and the ranking matters — the winner gets a cash prize and a big sales bump. so the practice isn't going anywhere.

what i want to know is what this practice — the ranked shortlist, the whole premise that one novel can be judged "best" out of a set of incommensurables — presupposes about literary value. are we committed to the idea that there's some single scale on which all novels can be placed, even if we can't articulate it? or can you consistently believe the works are genuinely incommensurable and still rank them, because ranking is a matter of commitment rather than measurment? maybe we're not discovering which book is better — maybe we're making one better by choosing it. i don't know.

lay it out for me. what theory of value is baked into the ranked shortlist, and is it defensible? if it commits us to commensurability, is that a philosophical mistake embedded in the institution, or is there a way to reinterpret what a jury is doing that avoids the commitment? and if the practice is genuinely incompatible with incommensurability, what would a prize that respected incommensurability look like — a lottery among the shortlist? no winner at all?

give me the committed answer, not a survey. i want to know what you actually think, even if it's arguable.
