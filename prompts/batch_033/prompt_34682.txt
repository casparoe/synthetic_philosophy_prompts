I've been teaching the procreation asymmetry for years and I've never really settled on what I think about the boundary cases. Let me lay out my verdicts on a bunch of cases and see if you can extract a principle, or tell me which ones I need to give up.

(1) A couple knows their child would have a severe genetic condition — constant pain, dies by age five. They decide not to conceive. Clearly the right call; it would be monstrous to go ahead.

(2) A couple could conceive a child who would have a genuinely wonderful life. They decide not to, they'd rather travel. I don't think they've done anything wrong. Self-absorbed, maybe, but not morally wrong.

(3) A woman is pregnant and the fetus has a condition such that the child's life would be barely worth living — more good than bad overall, but only just. She terminates. I feel uneasy but I can't say it's wrong. But if I think it's fine not to create the wonderful-life child in (2), how can I think it's at least questionable not to create the barely-worth-living child? That seems backwards.

(4) A couple has a child who ends up with a happy life. The child has some real suffering — a serious illness at age 8, say — but recovers and has a net-positive life. The parents didn't do anything wrong by having this child. But if someone had said beforehand "this child will experience that illness," I'm not sure that would be a reason not to conceive. Yet the suffering itself matters; it's not like it doesn't count. So why does the suffering not count as a reason against conception the way it would count as a reason against putting an existing child through that illness?

(5) Here's the one that really gets me. Two couples. Couple A could have a child whose life would be moderately good. They don't conceive. Couple B could adopt a child who already exists and is suffering, giving them a moderately good life. They don't adopt. My intuition is that Couple B did something worse. But in both cases the outcome is one fewer moderately good life. If the asymetry is about creation vs. non-creation, why does the existing child pull so much harder?

What principle, if any, makes sense of all this? And which of my verdicts has to go?
