I'm a PhD student in political theory, and I keep comming back to Fukuyama's end of history thesis when I read about the war in Ukraine. I want a proper case analysis of the 2022 full-scale invasion through that lens, not the lazy reading where Fukuyama was wrong because wars still happen. I mean the actual thesis: that the big ideological alternatives to liberal democracy exhausted themselves, that what remains is the slow, uneven working-out of liberal democratic norms, and that the deeper driver underneath it all is the demand for equal recognition. Take the invasion as the episode. What would the thesis lead you to expect about the players and their self-understandings — Putin and the Russian regime, the Ukrainian side, the western/NATO response, and the way each side frames what the fight is really about? Where does the thesis do genuine explanatory work on the episode, and where does it misdescribe it or just go silent? Then run it the other way: is the invasion an anomaly for the thesis, a partial confirmation, or a case the thesis never claimed to cover in the first place? And be explicit about which version of the thesis you're testing at each point — the strong claim that liberal democracy has no live ideological rival, versus the weaker normative claim that it remains the only broadly legitimate endpoint — because I think people slide between them without noticing, and the episode probably bears on them differently.
