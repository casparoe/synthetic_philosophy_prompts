I've become pretty convinced that the Moloch / multipolar trap framework is the best single explanation for why so many of our institutions and systems produce outcomes that literally nobody wants. The basic idea: you've got multiple agents each responding rationally to local incentives, and the Nash equilibrium of that game is terrible for everyone — but no one can unilaterally deviate without getting crushed. Think arms races, beauty contests in hiring, the attention economy making everyone miserable, safety shortcuts in competitive industries, etc. The claim isn't that every bad outcome is a trap, but that this dynamic is pervasive and underappreciated relative to explanations that appeal to individual malice or stupidity.

I want to know what would count against this view. Not just "here's why it's wrong" from someone who never bought it, but a honest map of its exposure: what evidence or arguments should make me downgrade my credence, sorted from "this would basically kill the view" to "this would be awkward but recoverable." What would Moloch-as-dominant-explanation predict that its rivals wouldn't, and has any of that failed to show up? Are there cases where the framework has been tried and the trap didn't materialize when it should have? I also want to know whether the view, as it's usually articulated, has been qualified in ways that insulate it from counterevidence — and if so, whether that's a legit move or a problem. Basically: is this a falsifiable empirical claim about the world, a unfalsifiable interpretive lens, or something in between, and what follows from that?
