So I'm a philosophy PhD student working on moral responsibility and I've been deep in the Dennett/Caruso debate on punishment and just deserts for months now, and I've hit a wall. The debate feels totally stuck and I can't figure out why.

Quick sketch of the positions for context: Dennett's a compatibilist who thinks our ordinary practices of holding each other responsible — including punishment — don't require libertarian free will and are justified by their role in a broader system of social regulation. He's got this whole thing about how "just deserts" isn't some metaphysical claim about ultimate origination, it's about being an appropriate target of reactive attitudes within a practice that works. Caruso's a free will skeptic who thinks that once you give up libertarian free will (and maybe even if you don't), retributive punishment has no justification, and we should replace the whole apparatus of blame and desert with a public health model — quarantine dangerous people if needed, but drop the moral condemnation.

Here's what's driving me nuts: they agree on basically all the factual questions. Both accept determinism (or at least that we lack the kind of free will that would ground basic desert). Both agree the brain does what brains do. Both want a humane criminal justice system. And yet Dennett thinks we should keep talking about desert and moral responsibility, and Caruso thinks those concepts are part of the problem and need to be retired. After reading their whole exchange I genuinely cannot tell you what the remaining disagreement is made of.

My working hypothesis — and I'm not confident about this — is that the disagreement is mostly about which things you're allowed to treat as fixed points. Dennett seems to treat our ordinary practices of praising, blaming, and holding responsible as data that any theory has to accommodate; Caruso treats those very practices as the thing that needs explaining away once we accept determinism. If that's right, then the debate is stalled because they're not disagreeing about facts or arguments, they're disagreeing about what counts as the explanandum. But I could be wrong — maybe there's a hidden empirical bet in there somewhere, or maybe it's a verbal dispute about "desert" that could be dissolved with enough conceptual hygiene.

So: diagnose this. What kind of disagreement is it actually — verbal, substantive, different standards of evidence, bedrock intuitions, something else? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress? A new distinction, an experiment (what would it even look like?), a reframing of the question? Or is this just one of those debates where the impasse is the conclusion?
