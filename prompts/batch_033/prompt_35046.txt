I'm a novelist preparing a craft lecture for an MFA residency, and I've been wrestling with Tolstoy's *What Is Art?* — a book that, as you probably know, concludes that most of what we call art (including nearly all of Tolstoy's own novels) is counterfeit. I want to give my students a practical instrument for locating their own work within Tolstoy's framework, not as an endorsement of his view but as a way of making his categories vivid enough to argue with.

I'd like you to build a decision tree that takes a particular work of art — say a novel, a short story, or a film — and classifies it according to the taxonomy implicit in Tolstoy's argument. The tree should have at least four leaves: counterfeit art (works that fail Tolstoy's definition of art entirely, being mere imitation, decoration, or entertainment aimed at pleasure rather than at infecting an audience with the artist's feeling), bad but genuine art (works that sincerely transmit feeling but transmit feelings Tolstoy would condemn — for instance, feelings that reinforce class pride, nationalism, or sensuality divorced from any universal human concern), good universal art (works that transmit simple, accessible feelings anyone can share, such as joy, sorrow, or wonder at the natural world), and good religious art (works that transmit the feeling of human brotherhood and the recognition that we are all children of a single source — what Tolstoy called the religious consciousness of his time, though he meant something broader than any creed).

Each branching node should be a concrete diagnostic question about the work — about the artist's process, the work's effect on an audience, or the nature of the feeling it transmits — phrased so that a writer could answer it honestly without already knowing where the tree will place them. Please flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate (I suspect the line between universal and religious art may blur in practice), and identify any logically possible combinations of answers that do not correspond to a named category in Tolstoy's scheme, with a brief note on whether Tolstoy simply overlooked the combination or whether it is ruled out by one of his deeper commitments. Keep the entire response to no more than 500 words.
