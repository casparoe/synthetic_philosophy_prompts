A friend whose into the rationalist scene has been trying to convince me that geometric rationality is the right way to think about everything, and I've been reading up on Kelly betting to figure out what he means. The usual story, as I understand it, is that when you face a sequence of favorable bets, you shouldn't maximize expected wealth; you should maximize expected log wealth — bet the Kelly fraction — because that maximizes the long-run growth rate of your bankroll and avoids ruin. So far that's fine.

But then the argument seems to slide from 'this is the best policy for repeated bets' to 'this is a general norm of rationality,' and that's where I get lost. Isn't maximizing expected log wealth just expected utility theory with logarithmic utility? If it is, then Kelly can only be normative for people who actually have log utility, which almost nobody does. So geometric rationality would just be one risk preference dressed up as a deep principle, not a rule everyone should follow. I can't tell if there's a sleight of hand here or if I'm missing a step that makes Kelly privileged independently of anyone's utility function. What am I missing?
