Ok so I've been reading around in moral philosophy — I was a geologist before I retired, so this is all pretty new to me — and I've sort of settled into a set of views about supererogation (doing more than duty requires). Here's where I am:

(1) I think there really are supererogatory acts — things like donating a kidney to a stranger, or running into a burning building for someone you don't know. They're morally good, they're praiseworthy, but you're not a bad person if you don't do them.

(2) I also think morality, at its core, is about what we owe to each other — the stuff you can legitimately be blamed for skipping.

(3) I don't think there's a sharp line between duty and supererogation. It feels like a gradient. Giving ten percent of your income might be supererogatory, giving nothing is wrong, and somewhere in between it shades from one to the other.

(4) I think a moral theory ought to be action-guiding — if it shrugs and says "do what you want" too often, it's not doing its job.

(5) And I'm drawn to the idea that the best moral theory is the one whose universal acceptance would make the world go best.

I'm writing this up for a discussion group and I want to know if these hang together before I embarass myself. Which of these are actually in tension, which just look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix? Rank the problems by severity.
