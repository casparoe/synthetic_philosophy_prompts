ok so i'm in this philosophy of AI seminar and we've been going back and forth on the whole agents vs simulators vs something-else thing for like three weeks and i feel like we're totaly stuck. the simulator camp (you know, the idea that LLMs are just doing role-play, simulating characters from the training distribution, no persistent goals or beliefs) keeps pointing to the architecture — it's next-token prediction, there's no reward channel during deployment, the thing doesn't have preferences it's trying to satisfy. the agent camp says sure but look at what they do: they plan, they pursue subgoals, they model other agents, they act coherently over long horizons. if it walks like an agent and talks like an agent, at some point the architecture stops mattering. and then there's the third group saying both sides are trapped in old categories and LLMs are genuinely a new ontological kind.

and more importantly — given whatever diagnosis you give, what would actually move this debate? a new distinction, a different framing, or should i just accept that people with different temperaments are gonna line up differently and there's no resolution to be had?
