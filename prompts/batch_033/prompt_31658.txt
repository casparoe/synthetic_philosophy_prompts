I'm a software engineer and I've never taken a philosophy class, but a friend who's into Jainism has been telling me about anekāntavāda and syādvāda. As I understand it, anekāntavāda is the view that reality is many-sided, so no single perspective captures the whole of a thing, and syādvāda is the habit of qualifying every claim with something like in some respect — which leads to a sevenfold scheme where a statement can be affirmed, denied, affirmed and denied, called indescribable, and so on. This hit me hard because it sounds alot like debugging a distributed system: two services can each have a locally true account of what happened, and neither log is the truth, and adding more observability sometimes just gives you more partial views rather than one clean answer. SQL NULLs and three-valued logic feel like a cousin of this too.

Could you write a short story that dramatizes anekāntavāda rather than explaining it? I'd love it if the story made a reader feel what it's like to hold several apparently conflicting accounts as all partially true, instead of turning into a lecture. If you can work the sevenfold bit in — a character trying to classify something and ending up with is, is not, both, and neither — even better. Maybe set it around a small team of engineers trying to diagnose an outage that keeps getting misclassified because every monitoring tool reports a different, partially right story; or pick whatever setting works. Under about 1,200 words would be great.
