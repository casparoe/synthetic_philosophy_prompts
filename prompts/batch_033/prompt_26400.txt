I'm a graduate student in philosophy, and I've been working through the illusionist literature on phenomenal consciousness — Keith Frankish, Derk Pereboom's earlier work, and some of the back-and-forth with realists like Galen Strawson and David Chalmers. I find the view intriguing, but I'm stuck on what seems like a basic conceptual point that I can't get any of the illusionist responses to address squarely.

The heart of my worry: when illusionists say that phenomenal consciousness is an "illusion," they seem to be using the term in two different ways at once, and the view only looks plausible because of the slippage between them. On one reading, an illusion is a perceptual or introspective state that misrepresents its object — the way the Müller-Lyer lines look unequal. On that reading, illusions are themselves conscious experiences: there is something it is like to see the lines as unequal. But then illusionism about phenomenal consciousness would be claiming that there is a conscious experience (the illusion) that misrepresents us as having conscious experiences — and that seems to just be realism about phenomenal consciousness with a false belief tacked on. On the other reading, "illusion" is being used in a deflated or purely theoretical sense to mean something like: our cognitive systems generate a representation with false content, but there is nothing it is like to host this representation. On that reading, the view avoids self-refutation, but then I do not see what makes it an illusion rather than just a false belief or a theoretical error. An illusion in the ordinary sense is precisely a state with phenomenal character that misrepresents — that is what distinguishes it from merely being wrong about something.

So the dilemma seems to be: either the illusion is phenomenally conscious, in which case illusionism collapses into realism; or it is not, in which case "illusion" is just a rhetorically loaded synonym for "false belief" and the view has not earned its name or its interest. I have seen Frankish gesture at a middle path where the illusion is something like a non-conscious representational state that nevertheless has enough of the functional profile of a perceptual illusion to merit the term, but I can not get a clear grip on what that middle path would amount to — what features would a state need to have, short of phenomenology, to count as an illusion rather than a mere false belief, and do those features actually obtain in the case of introspection?

I would be grateful if someone could untangle this dilemma — and I would especially appreciate it if the answer could separate what is genuinely settled in the debate (if anything) from what remains actively contested among people who know the topic, rather than presenting one side's position as the obvious truth.
