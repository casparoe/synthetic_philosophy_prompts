My department has a working group on population ethics that's been going in circles on the procreation asymmetry for two semesters. The two camps: one side thinks the asymmetry is a genuine moral datum that any adequate population axiology has to accomodate — roughly, that there's a strong reason not to bring a miserable person into existence but no corresponding reason to bring a happy person into existence, and that this isn't just some quirk of our psychology we can debunk away. The other side thinks the asymmetry collapses under pressure: it's either a scope confusion between person-affecting and impersonal reasons, or it entails antinatalism and should be rejected as a reductio, or it can't even be stated without smuggling in an equivocation on "good for." Both sides have read the same papers and are sick of talking past each other.

I want you to draft the adversarial-collaboration document they should have produced by now. It should have:

- A numbered list of claims both sides can sign — about the structure of the asymmetry, about what it does and doesn't commit one to, about what the key test cases are that any account has to handle.
- A disagreements table: the precise points where they still differ, with each side's view stated in wording that side would actually endorse (not a caricature).
- The cruxes: the sub-questions where settling them would actually move at least one side. For each crux, include a concrete case or thought experiment that both sides agree bears on it, and state what each side predicts about that case.
- If any part of the disagreement is merely verbal, flag it and say why.

Be as precise as you can about the formulations. The goal is a document both sides could put their names to without feeling misrepresented, even if neither has changed their mind. Don't adjudicate — just map the terrain.
