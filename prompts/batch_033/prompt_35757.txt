The debate between causal, evidential, and updateless decision theory has been running in the rationalist community for well over a decade. The canonical thought experiments — Newcomb's problem, the smoking lesion, counterfactual mugging, the psychopath button — are thoroughly mapped, and each theory has its stock of intuitive wins and its stock of embarrassing losses. Nobody moves.

I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is, not a verdict on which theory is right. My working hypothesis is that CDT, EDT, and UDT are each the correct answer to a different question, and the appearance of a single dispute comes from using "rational decision" as if it picked out one thing. But I can imagine alternatives: perhaps there is a hidden empirical question about what kinds of decision problems agents in our world actually face; perhaps the disagreement is about which counterfactuals are the right ones to condition on; perhaps it is a bedrock-intuition clash about whether rationality is fundamentally about controlling the world or about holding accurate beliefs.

Engage with what each side actually says — not a caricature — and give me your best diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement, together with what would count as progress given that diagnosis, or an argument that none should be expected.
