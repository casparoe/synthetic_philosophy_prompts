I'm a law clerk for a federal judge known for unusually thoughtful sentencing opinions. She's never stated her sentencing philosophy in so many words, but her decisions across cases are strikingly consistent. I've pulled five representative sentences from her docket over the past three years:

1. A 23-year-old with no prior record robbed a convenience store at gunpoint. No one was physically hurt, and $1,200 was taken. She sentenced him to 5 years.

2. A physician who, over six years, sexually assaulted sedated patients — seven victims came forward. She sentenced him to 15 years.

3. A mother who forgot her infant in a parked car on a hot day; the child died. She was distracted by a work emergency, had no history of neglect, and was devastated afterward. The judge gave 3 years, with 18 months suspended.

4. A city council member who accepted $350,000 in bribes to steer public contracts. She sentenced him to 6 years.

5. A 20-year-old who set fire to a parked car during a street disturbance; first offense, acted impulsively in a group. She sentenced him to 2 years in a young-offender facility.

Now a new case is on her docket. A man, now 52, killed a rival drug dealer during a confrontation 22 years ago. He fled, assumed a new identity, and has since lived an entirely law-abiding life — steady job, married, raised two children, extensive community volunteering, no further offenses. He was recently identified through a DNA database match and arrested. He has confessed fully, expressed deep remorse, and a psychological evaluation concludes he poses no danger of reoffending. The prosecution wants life with parole eligibility after 15 years; the defense argues his 22 years of demonstrated reform warrant a substantially reduced sentence.

Please work through this as an expert on sentencing theory. Start by identifying at least two distinct sentencing principles that are each consistent with all five of her prior sentences. For each principle, explain what it would prescribe for the new case and why. Then give your best prediction about which principle she is actually using, what sentence she'll likely hand down, and your reasons for preferring that reading of her record. Finally, describe a further realistic case — not a contrived philosophical puzzle — that would cleanly distinguish the principles you've identified, and say what each would predict for it.
