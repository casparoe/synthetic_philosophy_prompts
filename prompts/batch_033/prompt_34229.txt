The central charge, as I understand it, is that the empirical record—studies like Darley and Batson's Good Samaritan experiment, Milgram's obedience work, and the broader literature on the power of situational factors—shows that putatively stable character traits don't actually predict behaviour across contexts with anything like the consistency that virtue ethics requires. If that's right, then virtue ethics is prescribing the cultivation of something that doesn't, as a matter of psychological fact, exist in the way the theory needs it to.

My seminar has been unusually civil about this, but I can tell two of my classmates hold genuinely opposed views and have never quite gone head-to-head on it. I'd like to see what that conversation would actually sound like if both sides were fully pressed.
