I've been trying to work out what I actually think about multiple realizability — the idea that the same mental state can be realized in very different physical stuff — and my gut verdicts keep pointing in different directions. Here they are.

A normal human who stubs their toe and has C-fibers firing in the usual way: pain, no question.

A Martian whose brain is made of some silicon-based goo rather than neurons, but which has exactly the same functional organization as a human brain — same sensory inputs, same internal causal transitions, same behavioral outputs and dispositions: I'd say it feels pain, no hesitation.

An octopus with completely different neurochemistry but the same overall functional organization as a human pain system: also pain.

A human whose C-fibers fire but the usual functional role is missing — the firing isn't hooked up to tissue damage, wincing, avoidance, or memory in the normal way because of some wierd rewiring: I want to say that's not pain, or at least not the ordinary kind.

A computer program that implements the same functional organization as a human pain system, step by step, on a normal digital computer: I feel like it doesn't hurt, or at least I'm much less confident than with the Martian.

The entire population of China wired together with radios so that the whole system implements the same functional organization as a human brain for an hour: no pain at all, that seems obvious to me.

But I can't see a clean difference between the Martian goo case and the China case. Both are just the same functional organization in a different substrate. So why do I say yes to one and no to the other? Is there a principle that captures all or most of these verdicts? If so, what does it say about a creature built from lab-engineered biological cells that implement the same functional organization but share no evolutionary history with us? And which of my verdicts does the principle force me to give up?
