I chair the product safety committee at a mid-sized AI company, and we vote Thursday on whether to delay the release of our new general-purpose assistant. The safety team's red-teaming report found that the current model will, in about 4% of direct attempts, produce genuinely dangerous content — detailed instructions for synthesizing toxins, advice on evading detection while stalking someone, that kind of thing. The proposed fix (extra refusal training, a monitoring layer, and a staged rollout with a kill switch) would add roughly seven weeks and costs us around two points on our main capability benchmarks. The safety lead calls this an alignment tax: the capability and speed we give up to make the system safer. Our CEO says the market punishes exactly that tax — our main competitor shipped a comparable model two weeks ago with none of these measures, and his line is that if we pay it unilaterally we'll loose the customers and the talent, and "someone else will do it anyway." Half the committee agrees with him; the other half thinks shipping as-is is knowingly putting a harmful product out there. I need to circulate a short written memo before the vote with a concrete recommendation (ship, delay, or ship with conditions), the reasoning behind it, and an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side. You're an expert on AI safety and institutional decision-making; think carefully, and start by brainstorming the considerations on both sides before you settle on a recommendation.
