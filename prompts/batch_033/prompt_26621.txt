so i just got back from this like three-hour argument with my friend about whether you can accidentally get married, and it spiraled into a whole thing about speech acts and performatives and now i can't stop thinking about it. the crux was: if two people go through a wedding ceremony, say all the right words in front of the right officiant, sign the paperwork, the whole deal — but one of them is secretly thinking "this is just for the health insurance, i don't actually intend to marry this person" — did they get married? my friend was adamant that no, they didn't, because marriage requires the right intention, you can't just accidentally perform a speech act. i was on the side of: look, the words were said, the conventions were followed, the public uptake happened, so the marriage occurred regardless of what was going on in their head — even if it's fraudulent or voidable later, the performative act itself succeeded at the moment.

we kept going back and forth and it got into austin vs. searle vs. grice territory but we were both kind of fuzzy on the details. could you write me a dialogue between two characters — call them A and B — who are basically fighting out the two sides of this? A holds that performative utterances succeed or fail based on whether the speaker's communicative intentions are sincere and properly formed (broadly the intentionalist / Gricean / Searlean side), and B holds that they succeed or fail based on social convention and uptake, and that intentions are downstream of the act or at least not strictly necessary for the act to occur (broadly the Austinian side).

the dialogue should be genuinely back-and-forth, neither side should be a strawman, and both should be equally sharp. they should push each other on borderline cases — like promising under duress, a judge who pronounces a sentence they privately disagree with, a baptism performed by a priest who's lost their faith, someone saying "i bet you five dollars" as a joke and the other person taking it seriously. i want the dialogue to end without a tidy resolution, because honestly we couldn't resolve it either.

also, at the very end of the dialogue, i'd like each character to say what argument or kind of case would actually make them switch sides — like, what would it take to change their mind. it doesn't mean they do switch, just what they'd need to see.
