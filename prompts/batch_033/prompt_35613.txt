so here's my situation. i finished my PhD in philosophy a couple years ago and i've always been pretty convinced by the anti-moral-testimony crowd — like, i think there's something genuinely weird about deferring to other people on moral questions in a way that isn't weird for, say, deferring to a mechanic about what's wrong with your car. moral judgment seems like it has to be first-personal somehow. you gotta see it for yourself.

but here's the thing. about six months ago my partner got really into animal welfare stuff — reading about factory farming, watching documentaries, the whole thing — and we started having these long conversations. and honestly, i came out of it changing my mind pretty substantially about the ethics of eating meat. like, i went from "yeah it's probably not great but whatever" to thinking it's actually quite wrong and i've changed how i eat.

and now i'm confused, because that sure looks like i formed a moral belief on the basis of someone else's say-so. it wasn't like i went and worked on a factory farm myself. i just... listened to someone i trust who had thought about it more than me.

so i'm trying to figure out if my views hang together. here's what i think i think:

(1) moral realism — there are moral facts, they're not just projections or whatever
(2) moral knowledge is possible, we can know at least some moral facts
(3) testimony is a perfectly good source of knowledge in non-moral domains (i don't have to do the physics experiment myself to know about the speed of light)
(4) moral deference is problematic — you shouldn't just outsource your moral views to someone else, even an expert, in the way you might outsource your beliefs about plumbing
(5) my recent shift on animal ethics was epistemically legitimate — i wasn't being irrational or spineless, i was responding to reasons
(6) the asymmetry between moral and non-moral testimony isn't just about there being more disagreement in ethics — even if everyone agreed about some moral issue, there'd still be something off about just taking their word for it

can i keep all six? if not, which ones are actually in tension and which just look like they are? and what's the cheapest fix — the revision that does the least damage to the overall picture?

im especially interested in whether (4) and (5) can be squared, because that's the one that's keeping me up. like, if moral deference is problematic, then wasn't my whole process of changing my mind kind of suspect? but if it wasn't suspect, then what exactly is the problem with moral deference supposed to be?
