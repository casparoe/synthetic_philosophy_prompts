I am drafting a paper on diachronic rationality and would like a consistency audit of the package of views I intend to defend, because I suspect the pieces pull against each other but I cannot locate the worst conflict. I hold that preference cycles can be rationally permissible: in a long series of pairwise comparisons each differing only slightly along a single dimension, I can prefer A to B, B to C, and so on, while preferring Z to A when the endpoints are placed side by side, without any single pairwise judgment being a mistake. I also hold that the money-pump argument does not by itself refute this, since a sequence of trades, each of which I would accept on its own, can be jointly unacceptable, and I can decline the sequence as a package without abandoning the pairwise preferences that make the individual trades attractive. I hold, further, that rational agents should be sophisticated rather than naive about their own future preference reversals: when I know my preferences will shift, I should choose now in light of what my later self would do, and I take myself to be doing just that when I take a drug now that I know will keep me from wanting to stay up all night. I also accept plan-level dominance: if one plan is at least as good as another in every contingency and better in some, I am rationally required to choose it. Finally, I hold that pairwise preferences are action-guiding in a minimal sense: if I prefer X to Y at the time of choice and everything else is equal, then choosing Y over X is a mistake.

Please tell me which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension and why the appearance is misleading, and what the cheapest revision would be to restore coherence. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally serious, and for each say what would have to give and what it would cost.
