I'm a PhD student trying to get my head around On Certainty, and a friend in my reading group built an argument I can't shake. Here's roughly how it goes.

(1) Hinge propositions are the certainties that make doubt and evidence possible — e.g., that there is an external world, or that the earth has been around for more than a few minutes.
(2) Whatever makes doubt and evidence possible can't itself be supported by evidence, because that would be circular.
(3) So hinges are held without evidence.
(4) But a belief held without evidence is arbitrary.
(5) So the foundations of all inquiry are arbitrary.
(6) If the foundations of inquiry are arbitrary, then no framework of inquiry is more rationally justified than any other — at best, each is internally coherent.
(7) So hinge epistemology implies that, say, a scientific worldview and a worldview built on a sacred oracle or a conspiracy theory are on an epistemic par.

That conclusion seems crazy, but my friend says its just what Wittgenstein's view commits you to.
