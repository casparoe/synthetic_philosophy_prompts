so i'm on this internal tools governance comittee at work — i'm a backend engineer, not a philosopher, but somehow i ended up as the person who has to figure out our voting system for technical RFCs. we've got about 60 engineers across four teams and right now decisions get made by whoever has the most stamina in a githib issue thread, which is obviously terrible.

we agreed we want ranked-choice voting for cross-team technical decisions (stuff like standardizing on a build tool, adopting a new database, that kind of thing). but then i started reading about *which* ranked-choice method and fell into the arrow's impossibility theorem hole and now i can't unsee it. every method breaks something.

from what i understand: condorcet methods can give you cycles where A beats B, B beats C, C beats A. borda count is super vulnerable to strategic voting (you bury your real competition at the bottom). instant-runoff can eliminate someone who's actually the consensus second choice for everyone. approval voting isn't even ranked — you lose all the preference intensity info. and arrow says you literally cannot have a system that satisfies unrestricted domain, pareto, IIA, and non-dictatorship all at once.

so we're going to violate something no matter what. fine. but i have to write a memo by wednesday recommending one specific method for our use case, and i need it to actually be defensible when the other committee members (who are also engineers, not philosophers) push back.

what i need:
- pick one method and recommend it concretely
- explain the tradeoff you're making — what arrow condition are we violating, and why is that the least bad violation for *our* context (60 voters, technical decisions, people who will absolutely try to game the system if they can)
- the strongest objection to your recommended method, stated fairly, with your response
- keep it readable for engineers — you can use technical terms but define them
- bottom line: what should we implement

and please actually commit to something. i don't need a survey of options, i need a recommendation i can defend in a room full of people who will spot the loophole in whatever i propose.
