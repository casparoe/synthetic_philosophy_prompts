I've been going back and forth on whether my moral judgments about promising actually follow any general principle, or whether I'm a particularist in practice. Here are my gut calls, as close as I can get to honest.

1. I promised a colleague I'd read her grant proposal by Thursday. Wednesday night my kid spikes a fever and I spend the evening at urgent care. I don't read it. Verdict: fine, though I owe her a note and a new time.

2. Same promise, same deadline, but Wednesday night I get invited to a show I really want to see and I go instead. Verdict: not fine.

3. I promised a friend I'd help him move on Saturday. Friday night he gets drunk and unloads on me for not also offering my truck. I don't show up Saturday. Verdict: fine.

4. I promised my mother I'd call every Sunday. I'm in a cabin with no service one weekend and skip it. Verdict: fine.

5. I promised my mother I'd call every Sunday. One week I just don't feel like talking and skip it. Verdict: not fine, though small.

6. I promised my grandfather on his deathbed that I'd scatter his ashes at the cove where he proposed to my grandmother. He's dead now, no one else knows about the promise or cares, and the cove is a three-hour drive. I keep putting it off for months. Verdict: still not fine, and I can't say why on the principle I'm about to state.

The principle I keep reaching for is something like: a promise binds you to the extent the promisee still relys on it or would be hurt by its breaking. That handles 1 through 5 pretty well, but it seems to say 6 is no big deal, which I don't believe.

So: is there a principle that gets all six without turning into a gerrymandered mess, and if not which verdict should I drop? Or is this exactly the kind of case where generalism has to add epicycles and particularism is the more honest description of what I'm doing? And whatever the best principle ends up being, what does it say about a case I haven't settled: I promised a friend I'd attend her PhD defense, but she has since told me she genuinely doesn't care whether I'm there and will have plenty of people—do I still have any reason to go?
