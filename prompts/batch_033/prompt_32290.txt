Kavka's toxin puzzle has generated a substantial literature since the early 1980s, with proposed responses falling into broad camps: those that treat the puzzle as genuine and conclude that you cannot win the prize, those that show how an agent can win (through self-binding, higher-order intentions, or decision-theoretic revision), and those that challenge the framing by questioning the relationship between intention and belief that generates the puzzle in the first place. Suppose you're advising a PhD student who wants to spend three years working on the philosophy of intention and practical rationality, with the toxin puzzle as a central case study. Which of the unresolved questions in this literature would you recommend they prioritize, and in what order? I want the reasoning behind the ranking: which questions are upstream of others, which are tractable with the tools of contemporary philosophy of action, and which — despite being unresolved — would not change very much downstream if they were answered.
