alright so i'm trying to get my head around aesthetic testimony and i've written down where i currently stand because i want to know if the whole thing actually holds together or if i'm papering over cracks. here goes:

(a) i accept the acquaintance principle — roughly, that you can't genuinely know or judge a work's aesthetic character without having experienced it first-hand yourself. seeing a photo of the painting or hearing a recording of the piece counts as acquaintance; reading about it doesn't.

(b) i'm an optimist about testimony for thin descriptive aesthetic properties: if someone tells me the symphony is in d minor or the painting is mostly ochre, i can take their word for it and be justified. but i'm a pessimist about testimony for thick evaluative properties — if someone says the performance was "devastating" or the prose is "flabby," i don't think i can acquire a justified aesthetic belief just on their say-so.

(c) reading a long, careful piece of art criticism — the kind that describes the work in granular detail and walks you through its structure and effects — can give me justified aesthetic beliefs about a work i've never encountered. not just factual ones about its dimensions or subject matter, but genuinely aesthetic beliefs, like that a certain passage is emotionally manipulative or that the composition holds a productive tension.

(d) i'm a realist about aesthetic properties. "elegant," "saccharine," "rhythmically inventive" — these pick out real features of objects, not just projections of my responses or the responses of my community.

(e) moral testimony is fine. i think you can justifiably form moral beliefs on someone else's say-so — e.g., that a certain practice is exploitative — without needing to work through the moral reasoning yourself. but aesthetic testimony is different and much more limited, and i want to hold that there's a principled asymmetry here.
