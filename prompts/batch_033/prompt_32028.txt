I'm presenting a paper at the next APA Eastern defending Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: the claim that no one can be ultimately morally responsible for their actions, because to be responsible for what you do you must be responsible for the way you are, and that requirement generates an infinite regress. My angle is that the regress problem bites even under compatibilist accounts of free will — in particular, reasons-responsiveness theories — because the question of whether you are responsible for having the receptive-to-reasons makeup you in fact have is independent of determinism. The audience will be roughly half compatibilists (mostly of the Fischer/Ravizza sort) who think Strawson sets the conditions for responsibility absurdly high and that "ultimate" responsibility is a needlessly hyperbolic standard, and half libertarians and hard incompatibilists who are sympathetic to the conclusion but worry that the argument's reliance on the notion of being causa sui is obscure, question-begging, or less rigorous than the luck-based arguments they favor. Prepare my Q&A prep sheet: the objections I will actually get, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased as this particular audience would phrase them. For each, give a concise two-minute reply and identify where that reply is weakest. Include the question I am most hoping nobody asks.
