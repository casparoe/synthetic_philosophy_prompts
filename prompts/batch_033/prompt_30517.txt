I'm presenting a defense of Williamson's anti-luminosity argument at a rationalist meetup next month. My thesis: no phenomenal condition is luminous — for any mental state you might think you can introspect infallibly (feeling cold, seeming to see red, being in pain), there are borderline cases where you satisfy the condition but cannot know you satisfy it, because knowledge requires a margin for error that introspective indiscriminability cannot supply. The audience is longtime LessWrong readers who accept Bayesian epistemology, treat self-knowledge as mostly unproblematic, and care about whether an agent can know its own beliefs for reasons that bear on decision theory and AI alignment. I know what academic epistemologists object to; I do not know what this crowd will say. Give me exactly five objections I should expect, ordered from most to least damaging, each phrased the way a sequences reader would put it. For each, give a one- or two-sentence reply and a one-sentence note on where that reply is weakest. No introduction, no conclusion — just the five numbered items.
