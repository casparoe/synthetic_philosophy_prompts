I write novels for a living, and at a residency last month I watched two friends argue about an AI company that put out a novel written entirely by a model, with "written by AI" printed right on the jacket. Priya called it a counterfeit person. Her line: we don't just ban passing counterfeit money, we ban making it — that's Dennett's point — and a system built to pass for a novelist's work is the same kind of thing even if it's labeled. Tom said the harm is all in the deception, and a labeled book deceives nobody, so it's just a tool, like a ghostwriter or a drum machine. Priya shot back that labels get stripped, and once convincing fake authors exist, the background trust that there's a person with a reputation behind a book is gone for everyone. Tom said that's a slippery slope; you don't ban cameras because someone might take a misleading photo. Priya said a camera doesn't write in your voice, and a model has no skin in the game — you can't hold it responsible when the book lies. Tom said the publisher is responsible, same as with any ghostwritten book. It got heated there and I lost the thread. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right about what, what both of them are missing, and especially whether the counterfeit-money analogy actually does the work Priya wants and whether Tom's tool point survives her trust point? I genuinly can't sort it out.
