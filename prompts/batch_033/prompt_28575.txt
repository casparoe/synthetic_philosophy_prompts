I keep coming back to a frustration with the satisficing and bounded rationality literature: the term gets used for a stopping rule, for a decision criterion, for a family of heuristics, and for a claim about what rationality requires under limited computational resources, and these senses slide into one another without anyone laying out the full job description first. Before I read any further, I want the specification. What must an adequate theory of satisficing actually get right? Write the desiderata, each with the case or consideration that makes it a requirement. Cover at least the following terrain and tell me which items are genuine requirements and which are just the tastes of one research program: distinguishing satisficing from maximizing with search costs added back in; the setting and revision of aspiration levels, including what happens after repeated failure or easy success; the difference between satisficing as a criterion for accepting an option and satisficing as a description of how search unfolds; whether a satisficing model must be behaviorally distinguishable from an optimizing model with enough extra parameters, or whether outcome-equivalence to optimization is acceptable; how the theory handles lexicographic and other non-compensatory choice rules; and what it says about why satisficing can be rational rather than merely a bias induced by cognitive limitations.

For each requirement, mark it negotiable or non-negotiable, and say which single requirement does the most work in eliminating candidate theories. Then, if any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable—for example, if process realism about bounded computation and equivalence to some optimizing model cannot both be had, or if a mandatory aspiration-adjustment rule conflicts with empirical findings about how people actually search—say so plainly, because that is the part I most want to see. Close with a brief scorecard of how Simon-style aspiration-level models, search-cost and rational-inattention treatments, and the fast-and-frugal heuristics program fare against the list, but keep the scorecard short; the criteria themselves are the deliverable.
