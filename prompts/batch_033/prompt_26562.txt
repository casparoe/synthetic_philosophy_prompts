I'm presenting a paper at a philosophy of science research seminar next month. My thesis is that the pessimistic meta-induction succeeds against standard convergent realism but fails against a properly formulated version of selective realism — one that restricts realist commitment to those theoretical posits that are indispensable to novel predictive success and that have survived a specific kind of cross-theoretic robustness check. I argue that the historical record Laudan and others appeal to actually supports selective realism once we apply the right criterion for which parts of past theories were genuinely doing predictive work.

The seminar has about fifteen regulars. I need to prepare for Q&A from:

- Two faculty who are committed anti-realists in the Laudan tradition: they think the historical record of abandoned theoretical posits is simply too extensive for any realist extraction procedure to work, and they will press me on whether my criterion is not just post hoc.
- A structural realist in the Worrall mould who thinks I am conceding too much to the pessimistic meta-induction by restricting realism to posits rather than structure, and who will argue that structure is all that survives theory change anyway, so why not go all the way.
- An entity realist in the Hacking–Cartwright tradition who will object that my criterion is too demanding and that we can be realists about entities we manipulate without needing my robustness condition.
- Several agnostic faculty and graduate students who are sympathetic to realism but worried about the PMI and will ask clarifying questions about how my criterion actually applies to specific historical cases — the phlogiston-to-oxygen transition, the caloric-to-kinetic theory transition, the Fresnel-to-Maxwell transition.
- One philosopher of biology who thinks the whole debate is distorted by focusing on physics and will ask whether my criterion even makes sense for evolutionary or molecular biology.

Draft my Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I will actually get, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased roughly as these people would put them. For each, give me a reply I can deliver in about two minutes and an honest note on where the reply is weakest. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks. Also: tell me which two objections have the most force and what I should consider conceding in advance to take the wind out of them. Finally, say how confident you are in your assessment of each objection's force and which point in your analysis you are least sure of.
