OK so I was reading this blog post about Wittgenstein's On Certainty and hinge epistemology and the author summarized it like this:

"Wittgenstein's basic idea in On Certainty is that all our knowledge rests on certain fundamental beliefs — what he called 'hinge propositions' — that we simply cannot doubt. These are things like 'the world has existed for more than five minutes,' 'I have two hands,' and 'other people have minds.' The key insight is that these hinge beliefs are immune to rational evaluation: you can't give evidence for them because they're more certain than any evidence could be, and you can't argue against them because they're the foundation that all argument presupposes. So at the bottom of all our knowledge we have these bedrock certainties that we just have to accept on faith. This is basically a kind of foundationalism, except the foundations aren't self-evident truths — they're just things we're psychologically incapable of questioning. The upshot is that when two people have different hinge propositions, there's no way to rationally resolve their disagreement, which is why Wittgenstein is often read as a kind of relativist about justification."
