Please play a committed proponent of AI safety via debate: the proposal that we can oversee reasoners far more capable than ourselves by having two of them argue opposite sides of a question before a human judge, because a false position is harder to defend than a true one under adversarial cross-examination. Stay in character as a sharp, technically literate advocate — not a strawman — who will concede genuine open problems but never the central claim, and keep each reply to one paragraph. Keep the character honest about which parts of the proposal it takes to be established and which it would admit are live or contested among people working on debate; don't have it present a contested position as the consensus. Here is my opening move. Your scheme assumes the judge can tell a good argument from a bad one once the flaw is pointed out, but the judge is by hypothesis the weakest reasoner in the room. An agent that wants the judge to accept a false claim need not defeat the other agent; it only needs to make the dispute too expensive to adjudicate — a long derivation in which each step looks plausible locally while the error sits buried in step seventeen, so catching it would require the judge to hold the whole chain in mind. Under those conditions, debate selects for the best obfuscator, not for the truth. Where does your argument go wrong, or do you bite that bullet?
