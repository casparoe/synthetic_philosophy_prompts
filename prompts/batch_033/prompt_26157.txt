I'm clerking for a judge and I need to figure out what she'd do on a case we just got assigned. Her past statutory interpretation rulings, as best I can read them from her opinions:

- She held that a statute penalizing "whoever knowingly makes a false statement to a federally insured bank" requires the defendant to know the bank was federally insured, even though a plain reading could attach "knowingly" only to "makes a false statement." She said the presumption of mens rea runs through the whole phrase unless Congress clearly indicates otherwise.

- She held that a statute banning "the sale of drug paraphernalia" covers a head shop that gave away free pipes with every purchase, because "sale" in this context includes commercial distribution by any means and reading it otherwise would let the whole statute be evaded.

- She held that a statute requiring employers to "reasonably accommodate" disabled employees does not cover accommodating an employee's commute from a far-away suburb, because "accommodate" in the employment context means adjustments to the workplace itself. She leaned hard on the ordinary meaning of words in their specific legal setting.

- She held that a statute making it a crime to "use a firearm" during a drug offense covers a defendant who traded a gun for drugs, because "use" includes employing something as an item of barter. She spent three pages on dictionary definitions and corpus linguistics.

- She held that a statute granting asylum to refugees with a "well-founded fear of persecution on account of membership in a particular social group" does not cover someone fleeing gang recruitment, even when the gang targeted them for refusing to join. She said "particular social group" requires a shared immutable characteristic, not merely being singled out by criminals.

- She held that a statute prohibiting "whoever transports a minor in interstate commerce with intent that the minor engage in prostitution" applies even when the defendant and minor crossed state lines for unrelated reasons and the prostitution plan formed only after they arrived. She said the jurisdictional hook is satisfied once the travel happened, and the statute's protective purpose demands a broad reading of the transport element.

The new case: a statute makes it a crime to "knowingly transfer, possess, or use, without lawful authority, a means of identification of another person." The defendant used a fake name and a social security number he literally made up — it doesnt correspond to any real person. He used it to open a credit card. The question is whether a fabricated SSN counts as "a means of identification of another person."

I need to predict which way she'll go, and also tell her which of her own precedents is the closest analog if she asks. I can see at least two rules that fit all six of her past rulings, and they point opposite directions on this one. What are those rules, which one do you think she's actually running, and is there a case on the horizon that would settle which rule she's really committed to?
