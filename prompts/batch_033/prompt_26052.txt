I've come round to Hacking's view that psychiatric classifications are interactive kinds — they don't just label a stable underlying condition, they set off looping effects where the labelled person changes in response to the label, which then changes how the condition presents, which feeds back into revised criteria, and so on. The conclusion I'm drawing is that the DSM's aspiration to carve nature at the joints is largely misguided for most of its categories; the looping isn't noise, it's constitutive.

I want to know what would count against this view. Not the weak version where looping sometimes happens — the strong version where it's pervasive and undermines the realist project. What would a well-designed study have to show? Are there categories where we'd clearly predict looping effects (because of high awareness, contested status, etc.) but where the evidence suggests the condition has stayed essentially stable across decades? Could you show that what looks like looping is really just better measurement — we're not changing the thing, we're just seeing it more clearly? And does the view have a falsifiability problem: if stability is always interpretable as "the loop has equilibrated" and change as "the loop is active," has it insulated itself from counterevidence? I'd like the defeaters sorted by how much trouble they'd actually cause, and an honest answer about whether the view risks anything at all.
