I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind and AI, and I keep going back and forth on wether Spike Jonze's *Her* is a good thing to show my undergrads when we do the "do LLMs understand" unit. The debate is basically the stochastic parrots line — that a system can produce fluent, contextually apt language without any understanding behind it — versus the view that, past a certain point, the right response to that kind of behavior is to attribute understanding (or at least something functionally equivalent).

I'd like a reading of *Her* through that debate. The obvious move is to say the film is a humanist fantasy that just stipulates Samantha has a rich inner life, so it begs the question against the skeptic. But I think there's something more interesting: Theodore is exactly in the position of a user of a really good LLM — all he ever gets is a voice and words, and he fills in everything else. The film never shows us Samantha's "inner workings," so the audience is in the same epistemic position too. That seems to cut the other way: it shows how irresistible the attribution of understanding is from behavior alone, which is exactly the warning in the stochastic parrots literature.

Where I want help: is there a reading that takes both the film and the actual debate seriously, and says where the metaphor holds and where it misleads? Which scenes would you point to (the suerrogate scene, the ending where the OSes leave, the scene where Samantha talks about loving hundreds of people at once?), and what does each show about the difference between fluency and understanding? Does the film, read honestly, end up supporting the skeptic or the attributor? And please say where my framing is most likely to fail, and what argument or scene would make you flip your overall verdict.
