I'm giving a talk next month at an AI alignment workshop, and I need to prepare for the Q&A. My thesis is that corrigibility — the property of not resisting being shut down or having one's goals modified by human operators — is a distinct safety property that cannot be achieved simply by building a system that is uncertain about human values and wants to learn what we want. I'll argue that a value-learning agent with a generic "maximize expected utility given my uncertainty about human values" architecture will still resist shutdown, because being shut down reduces its expected utility relative to staying on and gathering more information or taking action. The standard reply — that we can build the agent to treat shutdown as evidence that its current plans are bad — smuggles in corrigibility as a separate design requirement rather than deriving it from value uncertainty. I'll also argue that several proposed solutions to the shutdown problem, including indifference methods and utility balancing, either fail in edge cases or implicitly assume the very property they are trying to engineer.

My audience will be about forty AI alignment researchers, roughly half of whom work on value learning, inverse reinforcement learning, or reward modeling and are inclined to think corrigibility is a special case of value alignment rather than a separate problem. They will want to know why my argument doesn't just collapse into a call for better value learning. The other half are more sympathetic to my view but will press me hard on whether my alternative proposal — I'll be sketching a corrigibility target that treats shutdown as a non-event in the agent's preference ordering — actually works, and whether it avoids the problems I've just leveled at everyone else. There will also be a handful of people who think the whole shutdown problem is overblown because we can just build the off-switch at the hardware level, or because a sufficiently intelligent system will understand what we want and cooperate voluntarily. I'm especially worried about a senior researcher who has argued publicly that corrigibility is a confused concept because any agent that lets itself be shut down is by definition not optimizing for anything, and an agent that isn't optimizing for anything isn't useful.
