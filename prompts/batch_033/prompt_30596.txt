I'm the secretary of a small union local (about 400 members), and I keep getting handed proposed voting rules for officer elections, contract ratification votes, and strike authorizations. The problem is that people game whatever we adopt: they rank a weak candidate first to knock out a stronger one, or vote against a contract they'd actually accept because they're hoping to force a better offer, and we end up with outcomes nobody wanted. We revise the rules every couple years, and the same argument happens every time — someone wants ranked choice, someone wants approval voting, someone wants a two-round runoff — and nobody on the commitee can say, from the rules themselves, which ones are safe from strategic voting and which ones invite it. The stakes are high (a bad ratification rule can lock us into a contract the membership hates), and I have to explain whatever we pick to members who are suspicious of anything that sounds like a consultants' trick, so it has to be plain-language.

I half-remember a theorem — Gibbard–Satterthwaite, I think? — that says something like every nontrivial rule with three or more options is manipulable, so perfect strategy-proofness is off the table. But I don't know how to turn that into something I can actually use when a rule lands on my desk. Could you write me a reusable decision procedure I can run on any proposed voting rule before we put it to a vote of the membership? I want the questions to ask in order, what counts as a pass or fail at each step, how to actually exhibit the strategic ballot a rule invites when it fails (not just say "it's manipulable"), and where the procedure has to stop and say "this is a tradeoff between values, not a math question." Then run it on three proposals we're currently looking at: (1) instant-runoff/ranked-choice for a three-candidate officer election; (2) approval voting for a contract decision with three options (accept the company's offer, reject and authorize a strike, accept with amendments); (3) plain majority between two options with the incumbent chair breaking ties. Please be honest about which ones the procedure approves or fails and why, including any result I'm not going to like, and say plainly what the procedure cannot settle.
