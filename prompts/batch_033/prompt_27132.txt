I am the parliamentarian for a professional association of about four hundred members. Our bylaws are ordinary except in one respect: they contain a rule governing their own amendment — roughly, a clause providing that the bylaws may be amended by a two-thirds vote of members present at a properly noticed meeting. A few times a year, a member proposes an amendment that changes that very amendment rule, or declares some provision permanent and unrepealable, or takes effect retroactively so as to validate something the board did without authority at the time. The chair expects me to rule on whether such proposals are in order before they reach a vote, in writing, and to be consistent from case to case. I have no legal training and no research staff, I usually have a day to rule, and my rulings can be challenged at the next meeting; the chair signs them and does not want surprises.

Please write me a reusable decision procedure for ruling on the formal validity of a proposed amendment in a self-amending rule system like ours. Ground it in what is actually understood about Nomic and the paradoxes of legal self-reference — especially the difference between a proposal's being valid under the rules as they currently stand and its being valid under the rules it would create, the way an amendment rule can apply to itself, and the limits of a rule that tries to bind future rule-makers. But translate this into a checklist I can run without the theory: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a finding of out of order or valid as to form, and where it hands the decision to the board or the membership because the question is one of judgment or values rather than mechanics. Mark explicitly which steps are mechanical and which are judgment calls wearing a mechanical costume.

Then run the procedure on three proposals: (1) a proposal to raise the amendment threshold from two-thirds to three-quarters of members present; (2) a proposal declaring that the article in question, together with the amendment rule itself, shall never be amended or repealed; (3) a proposal to ratify, effective as of the start of the fiscal year, a budget the board passed without a quorum. Include at least one case where the procedure gives an answer the membership will not like, and say plainly what the procedure cannot settle.

If this request rests on a confused premise — for instance, if in a self-amending system there is no neutral procedure for judging whether a rule-change is valid without already relying on the very rules under challenge, so that a fixed checklist would only push the circularity one step back — tell me that directly instead of giving me a procedure that pretends the problem away.

Keep the response under 1,200 words, and begin with the procedure itself rather than with framing.
