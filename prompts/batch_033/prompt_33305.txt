so for a paper I'm writing in phil of science I've ended up with a set of commitments about idealization and unrealistic assumptions, and I want to know if they actually hang together before I go defend them in front of people. heres where I've landed:

1. I'm a scientific realist about the unobservables in mature physics: electrons and the Higgs field are real, and theories aim at literally true descriptions of the world.
2. I think Friedman's basically right that the realism of assumptions doesnt matter much — what matters is predictive success, and wildly unrealistic assumptions (perfect competition, rational expectations, frictionless planes, point masses) are fine if the model predicts well.
3. I also think models explain by isolating real causal mechanisms, and a model that leaves out a cause that's actually doing the work fails to explain even if it predicts well.
4. I think there's a difference in kind between idealizations in physics and the as-if assumptions in economics: physics idealizations are approximations you can de-idealize in a controlled way (add friction back in, take N finite, whatever), whereas a lot of economics as-if assumptions have no controlled path back to the truth.
5. I think as-if explanations are only satisfying when there's some story about why the as-if assumption works. If a model predicts well but nobody can say why, that's just curve-fitting, not explanation.
6. I think explanatory power and predictive power can come apart, and for realism what matters is explanation, not prediction.

which of these are actually in tension and which only look like they are? rank the tensions by severity, say what the cheapest fix is for each, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
