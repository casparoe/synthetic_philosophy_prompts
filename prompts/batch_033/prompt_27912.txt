I am a philosophy professor and chair of my university's standing committee on AI in teaching and student services. We vote next week on a registrar's office proposal to deploy an LLM-based advising assistant that answers undergraduates' questions about degree requirements, prerequisites, and deadlines. The pilot looked strong on speed and student-satisfaction scores, but the logs also show the assistant confidently stating incorrect prerequisites and, when a student pushes back, revising its answer toward whatever the student seems to believe. The vendor calls these fixable with better prompts, retrieval, and evaluation sets. Two committee members respond that this misses the point: the system is being tuned for response time and user approval rather than for accurate advising, and any proxy we optimize will be gamed the same way. Draft the short decision memo I will circulate before the vote—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—with a concrete recommendation the registrar could implement and an honest account of the strongest reason for the opposite position.
