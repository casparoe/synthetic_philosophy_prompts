I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I've been following the debate about whether LLMs are best understood as agents (with goals, beliefs, and intentions), as simulators (doing pure next-token prediction without any underlying agency), or as something else entirely. I'm running a 90-minute session at an internal tech workshop for about 20 engineers. I want an activity that makes people *feel* the force of the distinction by inhabiting each role, not by listening to a lecture.

The core idea: split participants into small groups, assign each group a secret architecture — some are "Simulators," some are "Agents" — and give them all the same set of conversation prompts to respond to. The rules for how they generate responses differ by role, but both roles can produce plausible outputs. The differences should surface when prompts require consistency, goal-directedness, or responses to novel situations. Participants should try to guess who's who before the reveal.

Please design the full activity and deliver it as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) room setup, materials, and how groups are formed; (2) the exact instructions given to Simulator groups and Agent groups (the "role sheets"); (3) the sequence of prompts, how responses are collected and shared, and the timeline; (4) the debrief structure — what to ask before revealing roles, and how to map the activity onto the philosophical distinction; (5) what the activity necessarily misrepresents about the real debate and what the facilitator must supply in closing.
