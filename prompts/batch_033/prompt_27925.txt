In the machine learning community where I work, the argument over whether large language models "really" understand language or have beliefs has been running for years, and nobody seems to move. Both sides are looking at the same models, the same benchmarks, and the same failure cases, yet one side insists these systems genuinely represent facts about the world and can be said to hold beliefs in at least a thin sense, while the other side insists that talk of belief or understanding is a category error and that the models are just doing next-token prediction, so any mental vocabulary is loose metaphor. My working hypothesis is that the two camps are answering different questions under the same word: one is making an empirical claim about what is inside the model, while the other is making a claim about which explanatory or predictive strategy is appropriate for the system — roughly, whether attributing a belief is a report on internal machinery or a stance you adopt toward a system (I've seen this called the intentional stance). Diagnose this impasse. Is it a merely verbal dispute, a substantive empirical disagreement in disguise, a difference in standards of evidence, a difference in bedrock intuitions, or something else? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a specific kind of measurement, a conceptual distinction, or an argument that the question is ill-posed?
