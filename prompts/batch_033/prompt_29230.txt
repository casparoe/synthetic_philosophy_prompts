I have come to hold a version of the position associated with Taurek about rescue cases. Take a case in which you can save either one stranger or five strangers, with no special obligations, no differences in need, and no other morally relevant asymmetry. I am now convinced that you are not required to save the five. The fact that there are more of them does not by itself give you a reason to prefer the greater number, because each person's loss is borne entirely by that person, and the five losses do not add up to a larger loss that anyone undergoes. On my version of the view, the correct procedure is to give each imperilled person an equal chance of being saved — say, a fair coin between the one and the five — and the fact that it would be permissible to save the five does not make it required. I find the underlying thought hard to shake: each of the five stands to lose a life, and the one stands to lose a life as well, so no one of the five can point to a loss or a claim that the one does not also have.

What I want is a map of what would count against this view, not a survey of what critics happen to have said. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. For each, say what observation, case, argument, or result elsewhere in moral theory would have to be compelling for the view to give way, and whether a committed defender would concede that it counts against the position or would dismiss it, and on what grounds. I am especially interested in: non-rescue trade-offs (a scarce drug; a large aggregate of small benefits to many against a great benefit to one); threshold cases (one versus thousands, or one versus a vast future population); whether the coin-flip conclusion is required by the anti-aggregation premise or is a separable further commitment that could be dropped while keeping the core of the view; and whether the view can explain the widespread conviction that a rescuer who saves the one and lets the five die has not merely chosen differently but failed to respond to something. I would personally be most moved by a demonstration that the view is committed to something unacceptable outside rescue cases, or that it collapses into a rival position once "equal chance" is made precise.
