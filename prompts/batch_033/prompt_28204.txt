I'm a journalist working on a long-form piece about the "hard problem" of consciousness—the question of why and how physical processes in the brain are accompanied by subjective, phenomenal experience. Before I assess the major proposals (from panpsychism to illusionism to mysterianism to various forms of physicalism), I want to get clear on what a solution would actually have to achieve.

Please lay out the desiderata for an adequate theory that addresses the hard problem. What phenomena must it explain? Which cases must it get right? Which distinctions must it respect? Which empirical findings must it accommodate? For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it.

Then flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied—that is where the real tension lies. Finally, distinguish the non-negotiable requirements (a theory that fails them simply hasn't addressed the hard problem) from those that reflect the commitments of particular philosophical camps rather than the problem itself.
