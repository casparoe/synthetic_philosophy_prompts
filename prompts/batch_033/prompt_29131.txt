I'm giving a short speech at the memorial for my aunt, who was a philosopher of Ubuntu and spent her career arguing that personhood is constituted relationally — that 'a person is a person through other persons.' The audience is a mix: her colleagues from the philosophy department, her neighbours from the village where she grew up (some of whom taught her these ideas before she ever read academic accounts of them), and younger family members who just knew her as a beloved aunt. I need to say something true to her convictions without turning it into a seminar. The hard bit: if she was right about what a person is, then what does it mean to speak about her now that she's gone — and what kind of 'we' is left behind? Write the speech itself, with no preface or commentary, in British English.
