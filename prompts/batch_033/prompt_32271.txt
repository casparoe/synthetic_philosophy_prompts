I work as a software engineer, and I have a regular dinner group — mostly other engineers, one lawyer, one high school teacher — where a handful of topics keep resurfacing. One of them has been running for over a year now and we are completely gridlocked. The question is whether a work of art can be genuinely good art if the artist who made it did morally terrible things. The concrete cases get recycled: Polanski's films, Wagner's music, Caravaggio's paintings, more recently certain musicians and comedians. The two positions are clear. One side says the moral defect of the artist is a defect of the work — it's not a separate fact about the biography, it's something that infects the experience of the work itself, and if you continue to hold the work up as good you're implicitly treating the moral wrong as irrelevant. The other side says aesthetic value is a property of the artifact, not the artist's character, and if we couldn't separate them we'd have to purge most of the canon, which is absurd.

Here's the thing: I don't think this is a factual disagreement. Nobody disputes the historical facts about any of the artists. Nobody thinks the moral wrongs are ambiguous. We've tried hypotheticals — what if we discovered tomorrow that Shakespeare tortured animals? — but they don't move anyone, because each side simply absorbs the hypothetical into its existing framework. I've started to suspect we aren't actually disagreeing about a single question at all. Maybe one side is making a claim about the ethics of attention and cultural endorsement, and the other is making a claim about aesthetic evaluation, and the two claims don't actually contradict each other in the way they sound like they do. But I'm not confident in that diagnosis, and my friends are tired of me trying to meta-analyze the argument instead of taking a side.

So I want a diagnosis: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it a substantive disagreement about one question, a verbal dispute about what "good art" means, or two different conversations running under the same heading? Give me reasons for your diagnosis, and then tell me whether anything could actually resolve it — a distinction we haven't drawn, a way of reframing the question, or an argument that it's genuinely irresolvable and we should stop expecting convergence.
