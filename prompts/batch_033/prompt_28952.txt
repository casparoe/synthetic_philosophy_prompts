I chair the mortality and morbidity review committee at a teaching hospital. Each month we review cases in which a patient died or was seriously harmed after someone failed to act: a nurse did not recheck an abnormal potassium level, a resident did not call an attending, a monitoring system did not flag a drug interaction, a physician did not offer a low-yield test. Our finding about whether an omission caused the outcome determines whether a case becomes an incident report, triggers remediation or training, or is referred for legal review, and the chief medical officer signs every report. The committee members are working clinicians with no philosophical training and meet for ninety minutes, so the procedure has to be usable cold, without background reading.

Please write a decision procedure, grounded in the philosophy of causation by omission and negative causation, that my committee could actually run. It should be an ordered sequence of questions; for each question, say what counts as a sufficient answer, where a no or a yes sends the user, where the procedure ends in "not a cause," and where it must end in "a human judgment is required." Be explicit about which philosophical distinctions you are preserving in the checklist — for example, but-for dependence, the idea that an omission is not an event, and the role of a relevant duty or default — and which distinctions a checklist cannot capture. Then run the procedure on three cases: (1) a nurse forgets to recheck an abnormal potassium level and the patient has a fatal arrest; (2) a physician does not offer a low-yield screening test that would have detected a fatal cancer with only a modest probability; (3) an entire night shift fails to notice a slow deterioration in vital signs that no policy required them to monitor at that interval. Make sure at least one case yields an answer the committee will not like, and say plainly what the procedure cannot settle.

After the procedure, provide a brief structured summary in the form of a table with these columns: "Step", "Question to ask", "What counts as a sufficient yes", "Where a no or yes sends the user", and "Judgment call?"
