I've been reading about the iterated prisoner's dilemma and the evolution of cooperation, and I keep coming back to one idea: that cooperation doesn't require altruism or a central authority—it can emerge from pure self-interest, provided the shadow of the future is long enough that the gains from ongoing cooperation outweigh the temptation to defect today. Could you write a short story that dramatizes this threshold? I'd like a setting where the shadow of the future is uncertain or borderline, so that it isn't obvious whether cooperation will take hold. The story should show the gradual, almost accidental emergence of a cooperative norm—through trial and error, imitation, or selective retention—rather than through characters consciously reasoning their way to a Nash equilibrium. The answer should be in American English.
