I keep hearing about the AI alignment problem and I'm a bit confused about what the actual hard part is. As a software engineer, the way I see it, you build a system, you specify what you want it to do, and it does it. If it doesn't, you debug. So isn't the alignment problem just: make the AI do what the user asks it to do? Like, I get that current LLMs sometimes ignore instructions or hallucinate, but that seems like a regular engineering problem — better training, better prompting, guardrails, etc. Why do people talk about it like it's this deep philosophical thing about "human values"? If I tell the AI "make me money" and it robs a bank, that's not a values problem, that's just bad specification — I should have said "make me money legally." So why isnt the whole thing just a matter of getting better at specifying what we want?
