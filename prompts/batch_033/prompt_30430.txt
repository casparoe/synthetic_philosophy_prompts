Consider the July Crisis of 1914—the assassination at Sarajevo, Austria-Hungary's ultimatum, Russian mobilization, German war plans, and the cascade of declarations of war—as a case study for the debate between covering-law and narrative explanation in the philosophy of history. I know the broad facts; what I want is the explanatory structure. What would a covering-law explanation of the outbreak have to look like, and where does it strain? What would a narrative explanation foreground instead: contingency, the order and timing of decisions, the actors' reasons? Is the narrative best read as a sketch that hidden laws would eventually complete, or as an irreducible form of historical explanation? Then run the case in the other direction: does July 1914 confirm covering-law models, refute them, or expose their limits? Please separate what is settled in this debate from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
