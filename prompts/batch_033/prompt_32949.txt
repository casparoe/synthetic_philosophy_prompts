A weird thing happened in my ethics seminar last week, and I've been stewing about it. We were discussing a hypothetical where someone's spouse is drowning alongside a stranger, and they can only save one. I said I'd save my wife, obviously. The professor pushed me: "But can you justify that to the stranger's family?" I started trying to construct an argument — like, special obligations arise from relationships, or maybe a rule that permits partiality leads to better overall outcomes — and then I stopped mid-sentence because I felt gross. Not because I couldn't finish the argument, but because the whole project of *arguing* for it felt like a betrayal. Like, if my wife knew I saved her after running through a cost-benefit analysis or a moral principle, she'd be hurt. But the professor seemed to think that if I can't articulate the justification, I'm just being sentimental. Am I missing something, or is there something wrong with the demand for a justification here?
