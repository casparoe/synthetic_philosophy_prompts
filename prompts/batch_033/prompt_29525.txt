I've been keeping a record of a friend's judgments about how much moral weight different animals should carry, and I want to predict her ruling on a new case before I ask her. Her verdicts so far: humans and chimpanzees very high, with chimpanzees just below humans; dolphins, elephants, and sperm whales high, with elephants and sperm whales below chimpanzees; capuchin monkeys above cows; ravens above sheep and roughly level with capuchins; mice and frogs low, with frogs lowest. When pressed, she has said she distrusts raw brain size as a guide, because a cow's brain is much larger than a capuchin's and a sheep's much larger than a raven's, and she doubts that difference tracks anything morally important.

The new case is an octopus: a brain small in absolute terms and organized very differently from a mammal's, with no cortex to speak of and most of its neurons distributed through its arms, paired with unusually flexible behavior for an invertebrate — learning, problem-solving, and object manipulation.

What should I predict she will say about the octopus, and what rule is she implicitly running? I can see at least two rules that fit her record so far, and they appear to point in different directions here. Which fits best, what does each predict for the octopus, and what further case would most cleanly separate them?
