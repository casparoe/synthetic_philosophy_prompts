ok so i've been going back and forth on the internal vs external reasons debate and i want you to write an essay that actually takes a side. not one of those "on the one hand, on the other hand, and in conclusion the question is complicated" things. i want you to weigh the main arguments and then defend a verdict.

here's the structure i want:

**What's at stake** (~300 words): lay out the distinction between internal and external reasons as clearly as you can, using the Williams-style formulation. say why it matters — what hangs on whether external reasons exist. don't just define terms; show me why someone should care about this debate.

**The case for internalism** (~500 words): give the strongest arguments for the view that all reasons are internal. the explanatory argument, the argument from deliberation, whatever you think carries the most weight. don't just list them — develop the best one or two in enough detail that someone could feel their force.

**The case for external reasons** (~500 words): same thing but for the other side. what are the best things an externalist can say? the phenomenology of obligation, the "sociopath" objection, the argument from moral error, whatever you find most compelling.

**Where internalism goes wrong** (~400 words): here's where you start taking sides. identify the specific move or committment in the internalist picture that you think fails. be precise — not "internalism is just unintuitive" but something like "the internalist's account of what counts as a sound deliberative route is either too restrictive to capture ordinary reasons-talk or too permissive to exclude anything."

**Your positive view** (~400 words): sketch the alternative. what kind of externalism, or what hybrid, or what dissolution of the debate, do you actually endorse? and how does it handle the internalist's best objection?

**One hard case** (~300 words): pick the single hardest case for your view and show how you handle it. don't pick a straw man — pick the case that genuinely makes you nervous, and then do your best with it.

keep the whole thing under 2500 words. and please don't spend the first paragraph telling me that the debate goes back to Hume or Kant — jump straight into the substance.
