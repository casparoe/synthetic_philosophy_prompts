Write me an argumentative essay defending the following position: in a nearly just state, civil disobedience is better understood as a mechanism for raising the enforcement costs of an unjust law than as an appeal to the majority's sense of justice. Dont just restate the familiar Rawlsian picture and call it wrong; actually engage with the thought that civil disobedience is a communicative act, and with the worry that the cost-raising view cannot distinguish civil disobedience from coercion or blackmail by any group with enough leverage. I want you to argue that the distinction can be drawn, but that it has to be drawn at the level of the content of the law being protested and the kind of justification the law claims for itself, rather than by the public, communicative manner of the act. If your argument forces you to reclassify some paradigm cases as not civil disobedience, say so rather than papering over it. As you go, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present a contested reading as the consensus.
