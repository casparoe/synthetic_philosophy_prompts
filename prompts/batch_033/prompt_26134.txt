I spent my career in geophysics where we dealt with low-probability high-impact events all the time — earthquake risk, asteroid impacts, that sort of thing — and the standard move was always to multiply probability by cost and compare. So when I started reading philosophy and came across the Pascal's mugging / expected value fanaticism stuff, I was surprised that people think this is a deep problem rather than just a fact about how decision-making works.

But I've read enough now to see that it's not trivial. The basic worry: if you assign any nonzero probability to arbitrarily large payoffs, expected value calculations can be hijacked by fantastical scenarios. A mugger says "give me your wallet or I'll simulate a trillion suffering minds" and if you assign that claim even 10^-20 probability, the expected disvalue swamps everything.

So here's what I want: give me your actual credence that some form of fanaticism is the *correct* verdict — that is, that we really should be willing to sacrifice a lot of certain value for astronomically tiny chances of astronomically large payoffs, and that our reluctance to do so is a bug in our psychology rather than a feature of correct decision theory. I want a number between 0 and 1.

But more importantly, decompose it. What sub-questions does this credence depend on? For each, give a rough probability and a sentence of justification. Then tell me how they combine to your overall number, and which sub-question you're least confident about — the one where new evidence or a new argument would shift your credence the most.

I'll seed the decomposition with a few candidate sub-questions but you should modify, add, or remove as you see fit: (a) Does the problem go away once we have a good theory of logical uncertainty or bounded rationality? (b) Are there principled reasons to discount extremely small probabilities — not just pragmatic ones about our cognitive limits? (c) Is the real issue not about probability but about the metaphysics of value — e.g. whether value is bounded or whether infinite or astronimical value claims are even coherent? (d) Does a satisfactory decision theory need to handle these cases at all, or can we just say it's a problem for ideal agents that doesn't bear on what humans should do?
