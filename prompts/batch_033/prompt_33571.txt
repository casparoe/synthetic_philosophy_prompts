ok so for my school's ethics bowl team we've been prepping on just war theory and the moral equality of combatants thing has been driving me kind of nuts. like i've been reading some stuff online (stanford encyclopedia, some youtube debates, etc.) and i've sort of cobbled together what i think, but i'm worried it doesn't actually hold together when you look at it closely. here's my list:

(1) i basically buy the traditional view that soldiers on both sides of a war are morally equal in the sense that they're permitted to fight and target each other. individual soldiers usually don't have enough information to know whether their side's cause is just, they've been trained to obey, and the consequences of refusing are huge.

(2) but i also think there are edge cases where this breaks down — like if you're a Nazi soldier fighting for literal genocide, you should know better. i don't think moral equality covers that.

(3) i think the political leaders who start unjust wars bear the primary moral responsibility, not the rank-and-file soldiers. like the blame should flow upward.

(4) at the same time, i do think being a soldier is a choice (at least in countries with volunteer armies), and when you sign up you take on some responsibility for what you might be asked to do. you can't just say "i was following orders" and wash your hands completely.

(5) relatedly, i think moral equality makes way more sense for conscripts who had no choice than for professional soldiers who enlisted voluntarily.

(6) and i think even if you're on the just side, there are absolute limits — deliberately targeting civilians is never okay no matter what.
