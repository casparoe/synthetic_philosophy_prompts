I've been getting into accuracy-first epistemology — the Joyce/Pettigrew program where epistemic norms are supposed to fall out of the single goal of minimizing inaccuracy, measured by proper scoring rules. The idea that probabilism, conditionalization, and even the principle of indifference can be derived from accuracy arguments is really elegant. But I'm aware I've mostly read the people who are building the framework, not the people who think it's a wrong turn. So tell me honestly: what would count against this whole approach? Not just the objections that get a paragraph in the SEP entry and a reply — I want the stuff that should actually make me lose confidence. Which specific commitments does the view make that could turn out false? Are there formal results that would be genuinely awkward rather than just needing a patch? Has the framework been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so is that a problem for a program of this kind? And is there a live rival that does the same explanatory work — grounding epistemic norms in something thinner than practical rationality, without the baggage? Rank the defeaters from "this probably kills the project" to "accuracy-firsters can shrug this off."
