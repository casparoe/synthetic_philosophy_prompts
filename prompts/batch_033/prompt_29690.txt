For our summer issue on "The Unknowable," I'm commissioning a piece of short fiction. I'd like you to write a story that engages the philosophical problem at the core of Zhuangzi's butterfly dream — the parable in which Zhuang Zhou, waking from a dream of being a butterfly, finds he cannot determine whether he is a man who dreamed he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming he is a man. The distinctive move in the parable, which I'd like the story to capture, is not simply "what if this is all a dream?" but the symmetry: two perspectives, each of which would explain the other as merely dreamed, with no vantage outside both from which to adjudicate.

Do not retell the ancient parable in modern dress. Invent a new, present-day situation in which a character confronts a structurally similar uncertainty — one that is not idle but bears on a consequential choice. Let the narrative carry the philosophy rather than unpacking it in exposition. Maximum 800 words.
