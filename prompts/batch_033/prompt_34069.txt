You are a professor of philosophy of mind, writing for a sophisticated interdisciplinary audience. Please think carefully about the following question and write a well-structured essay in response.

The digger wasp *Sphex ichneumoneus*, studied by the naturalist Jean-Henri Fabre in the nineteenth century and later taken up by Dean Wooldridge and Daniel Dennett, exhibits a behavior that has become a philosophical parable. When the wasp returns to its burrow with a paralyzed cricket, it leaves the cricket at the threshold, enters the burrow to inspect it, re-emerges, and then drags the cricket inside. If, while the wasp is inside inspecting, an experimenter nudges the cricket a few inches away, the wasp will reposition the cricket at the threshold and then — rather than simply dragging it straight in — go back inside to inspect the burrow all over again. It will repeat this loop indefinitely, never seeming to notice that the inspection step has become superfluous. The wasp's behavior is perfectly adaptive in the normal case, but perfectly rigid in the face of a minor perturbation: it cannot "step back" and revise its script.

This phenomenon has been given the name "sphexishness," and it has been used to raise an uncomfortable question: to what extent are humans sphexish? We readily acknowledge that much of our behavior runs on automatic — habits, biases, and heuristics that operate below the level of conscious deliberation. But some thinkers have gone further, suggesting that even our most cherished exercises of reason and choice may be, in principle, no more flexible than the wasp's provisioning routine — just more elaborately nested and harder to see through.

Write an essay that evaluates the claim that sphexishness poses a genuine challenge to human free will. Consider the strongest arguments on both sides. On one hand, the sphexishness critique suggests that if our behavior is the output of fixed algorithms (even very complex ones), we may be no freer than the wasp, just more complicated. On the other hand, humans demonstrably possess capacities — metacognition, long-term planning, the ability to notice and override automatic patterns — that seem to break the sphexish mold. Is the wasp a disturbing mirror, or a false analogy? Your essay should reach a definite conclusion and justify it, while treating the opposing view with intellectual charity.
