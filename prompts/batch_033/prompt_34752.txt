I keep running into the same tangle in arguments about animal minds, and I don't have a word for it. The people I argue with are perfectly happy to say that awareness comes in degrees — a dog has more of it than a mouse, a mouse more than a bee, a bee more than... something. But then when the conversation turns to what we owe the animal, the same people switch to on/off talk: either a thing 'counts' morally or it doesn't. And there's a third wrinkle: they'll happily say a thermostat 'detects' temperature and a fly 'reacts' to being swatted, but if I ask whether the thermostat is at the bottom of the same scale as the dog or off the scale entirely, they act like I've changed the subject. So here's my question. Is there an established name for the specific view that moral weight should track degree of awareness all the way down, so the thermostat sits on the same scale as the dog, just at a negligable point — as opposed to the weaker view that awareness is graded but moral weight stays basically on/off? A bare name won't do me much good: I want the nearest concepts and how my view differs from each. And please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
