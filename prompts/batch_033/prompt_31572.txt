Right, I'm drafting the meta-rules for a Nomic-style game and a friend says my validity rules can't all be true at once, but he wont tell me which one he means. Here's what I'm committed to.

(1) A rule is valid only if it was adopted in the way the rules in force at the time required; no amount of everyone going along with it can make an invalid rule valid.
(2) A rule adopted under the current procedure can validly change that very procedure, for example moving amendments from majority to unanimity.
(3) Once a rule has been validly adopted it stays valid until it is validly repealed or amended; repealing the rule that authorised its adoption doesn't by itself undo the rules adopted under it.
(4) If a rule was adopted by the wrong majority but everyone has treated it as binding for several turns, I'd let it stand rather than unravel the game.
(5) If the players adopt, by the correct procedure, a rule that declares itself invalid, I'd say the adoption was valid and the rule has exactly the effect it states: it is invalid.

Do these hang together? Go through the pairs, say which are genuinely in tension and which only look like they are, rank the tensions by severity, and tell me the cheapest fix for each. I'd rather sort this before we start then rewrite the rulebook mid-game.
