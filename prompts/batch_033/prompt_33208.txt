In my biology class we've been studying the handicap principle — the idea that some animal signals are honest because they're costly to produce, so only high-quality individuals can afford them. My teacher has been giving us cases and ruling on whether each counts as a costly signal in the Zahavian sense. Here are her verdicts so far:

Peacock's tail: yes. It's a handicap that only healthy males can sustain, and it serves no function beyond attracting mates.
Gazelle stotting — jumping high when a predator approaches: yes. It wastes energy and honestly demonstrates that the gazelle is fit enough to escape.
The bright warning coloration of a poison dart frog: no. That's aposematism, not a signal of individual quality — it works through predator learning, not through differential cost.
A stag's antlers: yes. They're costly to grow and are used in display to signal fighting ability.
The long tail of a male widowbird: yes. A classic costly signal of mate quality.
Courtship feeding in birds, where a male brings food to a female: no. It's a direct material benefit, not a signal of quality through waste.

Next week we're discussing a new case: a species of reef fish where males build elaborate nests from algae and shell fragments. Larger, more complex nests attract more females, and building a big nest is energetically demanding — healthier males build bigger ones. But the nest also directly protects the eggs from predators after spawning.

I'm less interested in the yes-or-no answer itself than in figuring out what rule my teacher is actually running. I can see at least two principles that fit all her verdicts so far. One says a costly signal must involve genuine waste — the cost can't serve a practical function beyond signaling. The other says the cost just has to be differentially affordable by high-quality individuals, even if the costly trait also happens to be useful. The fish nest case would split them. Which rule do you think she's using, what would each rule predict about the nest, and what further case would settle the question if this one doesn't?
