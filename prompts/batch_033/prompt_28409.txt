I'm writing up my views on the ethics of consent for a seminar and I'd like a consistency check before I present. Here are the commitments I currently hold:

(a) Consent is a normative power. When validly given, it changes the moral status of an act by waiving a right the consent-giver holds. It does not merely provide evidence that the act is welcome or beneficial.

(b) Deception invalidates consent only when it concerns facts that are material to the decision — that is, facts such that the consent-giver would not have consented had they known the truth. Deception about facts that would not have changed the person's decision does not invalidate consent.

(c) A threat can invalidate consent even when the threatened action is one the threatener has a perfect right to carry out. For example, "sign this contract or I will end our friendship" can invalidate consent, even though ending a friendship is not independently wrongful.

(d) Consent given in exchange for payment is valid even when the payment is for something the person would never do without it, and even when the person's economic circumstances make the offer extremely difficult to refuse. Offers, unlike threats, expand the options available to the agent and therefore do not undermine voluntariness.

(e) A person who consents while mildly intoxicated — sufficiently to be somewhat disinhibited but still capable of understanding the situation and communicating a decision — gives valid consent.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. For each genuine tension, explain what would have to give to resolve it and at what cost, and then tell me which single revision would do the most to clean up the overall view. Please write your response in continuous prose, the way you would write an email — no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
