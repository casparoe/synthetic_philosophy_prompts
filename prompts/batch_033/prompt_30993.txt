I've been working through Quine's gavagai example and find myself puzzled about what it is supposed to establish. A field linguist hears a native utter gavagai whenever a rabbit appears, and no amount of observation seems to settle whether the native means rabbit, undetached rabbit part, or brief rabbit stage. This looks like a straightforward case of underdetermination by the available evidence — an epistemic limitation that more data or a longer stay might in principle overcome. If that is all the example shows, why does Quine insist that the indeterminacy of translation is a distinct and deeper thesis, one that would persist even with all possible behavioral evidence and that implies there is no fact of the matter about which translation is correct? Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among those who work on Quine, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus.
