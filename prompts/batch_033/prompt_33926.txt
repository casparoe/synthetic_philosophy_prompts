In discussions of personal identity around mind uploading and cryonics, I keep encountering a move that goes something like this: we have no trouble accepting that a road extends through space with spatial parts — the northern stretch is no less the road than the southern stretch, and no one thinks the road is "wholly present" at each mile marker. A person, the perdurantist says, is just the same kind of thing extended through time rather than space: a space-time worm with temporal parts, not a substance wholly present at each moment. The analogy is meant to make perdurantism feel like the default view and endurantism like a weird prejudice in favor of the present moment.

I want to audit this analogy properly. Lay out the correspondence table: what plays the role of the road, the mile markers, the northern and southern stretches, the spatial parts, and the "wholly present" intuition in the temporal case. Then tell me where the mapping breaks down — what features of spatial extension the analogy quietly imports that time does not share, and what features of temporal persistence the analogy obscures. In particular, I want to know whether the analogy is smuggling in a B-theory of time by treating temporal positions as directly analogous to spatial positions in a way that begs the question against the endurantist who is also an A-theorist. And finally: does the conclusion that perdurantism is the natural default survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy doing all the work?
