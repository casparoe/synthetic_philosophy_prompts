So I've been running this little philosophy discussion group at my local library — nothing fancy, just a bunch of us working through existentialist stuff on our own. Two of the regulars, Maria and Dave, have been going back and forth for months about Sartre's ideas on freedom and bad faith, and I honestly think they've been talking past each other more than they realize.

Maria is pretty convinced by Sartre. She thinks we really are radically free in the way he describes — that even when we feel trapped by circumstances, we're still choosing how to respond, and pretending otherwise is bad faith. Dave thinks this is way overblown. He's coming from more of a psychology angle and thinks a lot of what Sartre calls "bad faith" is just ordinary cognitive biases, self-deception that's well-studied, and that our freedom is massively constrained by our biology, upbringing, and social situation in ways Sartre just hand-waves away.

I want them to produce an adversarial collaboration document — the kind of thing where both sides actually sit down and figure out what they can agree on, where exactly they part ways, and what would actually change someone's mind. I'm hoping the process of writing it will force them to stop caricaturing each other's views.

Can you draft that document for them? Here's what I'd like:

A numbered "Agreed Claims" section — stuff both Maria and Dave could sign off on, about what Sartre actually says, about how human psychology works, about what needs explaining. These shouldn't be watered-down mush; they should be substantive claims where there's genuine overlap.

Then a disagreements table. For each row: the exact point of disagreement, stated neutrally; how Maria would state her own position (in wording she'd accept); how Dave would state his (in wording he'd accept); and what the crux is — the sub-question that, if settled, would actually move one of them.

Finally, for each crux, a concrete test or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it, plus what each of them predicts that evidence would show.

One thing: please seperate what's genuinely settled among people who know this stuff from what's still contested. I don't want the document to accidentally present a fringe reading of Sartre as if it's the consensus, or vice versa.
