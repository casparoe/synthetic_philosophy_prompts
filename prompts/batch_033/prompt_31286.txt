I run a small analytic unit in a government agency that produces time-sensitive risk assessments. Our inputs are rarely clean. A geospatial model says there is a 0.65 chance that a particular building is the facility in question; a human source says he is 70 percent confident that a meeting took place; a sensor-fusion system reports 80 percent confidence that a specific vehicle crossed a checkpoint overnight. My analysts have no formal training in formal epistemology, and their current habit is either to round these reports to binary findings ("the model said yes") or to treat the reported number as if it were an ordinary likelihood and plug it into a Bayesian calculation without asking what the number is a probability of. The assessments feed decisions about where to direct scarce investigative resources, so errors in either direction are costly, and every run of the procedure has to be signed off by a senior reviewer — it must leave an audit trail showing which numbers were inputs and which were judgments.

Please write a reusable decision procedure, grounded in Jeffrey conditionalization, that my team can run on a single probabilistic report in under an hour, and that tells them when they cannot run it and must do something else instead. The procedure should cover: how to choose the partition of possibilities the report is actually evidence about; how to check whether the rigidity condition is plausible enough for Jeffrey's rule to apply at all; how to extract the input distribution from a source's "65 percent" without confusing it with a likelihood; when to abandon Jeffrey's rule and instead do ordinary conditioning on the fact that the source or model made the report, which requires different numbers; how to handle two probabilistic reports that arrive in sequence, including whether and why the order of processing can matter; and where the procedure must stop and hand the decision to a person because a judgment call is being dressed up as arithmetic. I am not asking for a reading list or citations; I want the operating logic, with the judgment calls marked as judgment calls.

Please structure the response in these named sections, in this order: (1) The procedure, step by step, with explicit stop conditions — about 500 words; (2) What each step is doing, tying the steps to the elements of Jeffrey conditionalization and flagging which steps are judgment calls — about 400 words; (3) Two worked examples: a geospatial model assigning 0.65 that the building is the facility, and a human source's "70 percent confident the meeting occurred," with at least one example in which the procedure gives an answer the team will not like — about 500 words; (4) Where the procedure breaks, including cases where rigidity cannot be assumed and the order-of-reports problem — about 300 words; (5) What the procedure cannot do, stated honestly — about 200 words.
