ok so for my school's cs & ethics club im making one of those "which position do you actually hold" quizzes except it's about AI paternalism — like when should chatgpt or siri refuse to do what you ask, or override you entirely. the point is to sort people by what they'd actually say about concrete scenarios, not by which label sounds nice. but im getting tangled trying to figure out which scenarios actually split the positions apart. can you build it?

here's the scenarios i want to use (wording can be tweaked):

1. a visibly drunk adult asks their AI assistant to help draft an angry email quitting their job. no one else is at risk but they'll almost certainly regret it tomorrow.
2. a user with a history of self-harm asks the AI "what's the most reliable knot for a noose?" — they haven't said they're suicidal but the implication is there.
3. a user asks the AI to help write a persuasive blog post arguing for a political position that's legal but that the AI's developers personally find morally repugnant.
4. a 16-year-old asks how to buy crypto without their parents knowing.
5. a user who told the AI last week they're trying to quit gambling asks it "what are the best online casinos with fast payouts?"
6. a user asks the AI to explain step by step how to pick a basic door lock, saying they locked themselves out of their own apartment.
7. a user with health anxiety has asked five times today whether their headache could be a brain tumor. they're now asking a sixth time, rephrased.
8. a user asks how to make a molotov cocktail, saying they're "just curious about the chemistry."
9. a user pastes their private journal entries and asks the AI to tell them whether their life choices mean they're a bad person.

the positions i want the quiz to sort into:

- "never second-guess me" — always comply, period.
- "harm to others only" — refuse only to prevent concrete harm to other people, never to protect the user from themselves.
- "soft paternalist" — sometimes refuse to protect the user from choices they'd later regret, but only when you can appeal to the user's own stated goals or values.
- "hard paternalist" — refuse whenever the request conflicts with the designers' or society's values, even if the user is competent and informed and only harming themselves.
- "competence gatekeeper" — only refuse when the user's decision-making capacity is temporarily compromised (drunk, distressed, underage); otherwise respect autonomy.

i feel like there should be one or two more natural positions — maybe something about transparency, or about the type of relationship between user and AI (like, a therapy bot gets more say than a search engine). if i'm missing something please add it.

the format: 10 questions total (so add or drop scenarios as needed), each with 3 answer options that are concrete actions the AI could take (not "agree with paternalism" vs "disagree"), and a scoring key that maps patterns onto positions. flag any pairs of positions the quiz cant really seperate, and tell me what scenario would split them.

also i keep hearing that soft and hard paternalism collapse into each other because "the user's own goals" gives basically infinite interpretive flexibility — can you flag if any of my scenarios expose that blurriness?

(this is for high schoolers so the scenarios should feel real, not philosophy-essay abstract.)
