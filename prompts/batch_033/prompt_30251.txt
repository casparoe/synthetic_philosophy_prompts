Kurt Gödel held that the human mind is not a finite machine, a view he connected to his incompleteness theorems, and he argued in letters to his mother that a personal afterlife is rationally to be expected: a rationally ordered world would not give human beings capacities for personal development and relationship only to let those capacities go almost entirely unrealized. Suppose Gödel were alive today and encountered the claim that mind uploading—scanning the structure of a person's brain and running the resulting computation on a digital substrate—could secure personal survival after biological death. On the basis of his stated commitments, how would he most likely have assessed this claim? Would he have regarded a successful upload as the continuation of the same person, as a mere simulation, or as something else, and would he have seen uploading as relevant to the hope for an afterlife? Explain the reasoning he might have offered, and flag any points where extrapolating from his views becomes underdetermined.
