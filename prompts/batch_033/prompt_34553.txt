Here's what I want. I've been teaching the slurs literature off and on for years, and it drives me nuts that the two camps talk past each other. So: draft the joint statement that a semantic theorist of slurs and a pragmatic theorist of slurs could both sign at the end of a genuine adversarial collaboration. By "semantic theorist" I mean someone who thinks the derogatory ingredient of a slur is in what the word itself encodes — its at-issue content, a presupposition, a conventional implicature, whatever, as long as it's conventionally tied to the word. By "pragmatic theorist" I mean someone who thinks the offensiveness is not encoded content at all but lives in the norms around using the word — a taboo, an expression of contempt, an implicature — so the slur and its neutral counterpart can have the same semantics and still differ in use. Don't use any actual slur in the document: write "S" for the slur and "N" for its neutral counterpart, and pick a couple of canonical examples to reason about without writing them out.

The document should have four parts. (1) A numbered section of agreed claims: the data both sides can sign. Cover at least: co-extension with N (be careful here — some semantic theories deny full truth-conditional equivalence, so put that claim in a form both can actually sign, or flag it as a first-order disagreement if it really is one); the way the offensiveness survives negation, questions, conditionals, and modal embedding; the difference between using S and mentioning "S"; in-group reclamation of the same string; slurs with no obvious N; and what a child or second-language learner can know about S before knowing exactly who it targets. These have to be non-vacuous — the point is to pin down exactly which data is shared before the fight starts. (2) A disagreements table with three columns: the issue, how the semantic theorist would phrase their own view, how the pragmatic theorist would phrase their own view. Each side has to be able to sign their own column without wincing. (3) A cruxes section: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux give a concrete test — a sentence, a minimal pair, an embedding, a speaker-judgment task — that both sides agree would bear on it, and write down in advance each side's prediction, including the result each side says would be a genuine problem for them. I'm thinking at least the projection-under-embedding crux, the mention/use crux, the reclamation crux, and the "John said I'm S" mixed-report crux, but add more if you think there are better ones. (4) A short paragraph saying whether any part of this fight is just verbal — a fight about the word "semantic" or "meaning" rather than about the phenomenon — and if so, isolate it and restate the non-verbal remainder cleanly.

Dont hand-wave that last part; it's the one I care most about.
