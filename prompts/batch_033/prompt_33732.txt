Some people think this is a real, deep moral phenomenon; others think it's confused and that the whole idea of "justified wrongdoing" doesn't make sense.

I want you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two people:

The wrongness isn't cancelled by the justification; it persists.

- **Clean-hands theorist**: believes that either the action is justified all-things-considered (in which case it wasn't wrong, and guilt is misplaced), or it was wrong (in which case the leader shouldn't have done it). The idea of an action being both justified and genuinely wrong is a conceptual confusion — maybe it conflates the tragic with the immoral, or it smuggles in a deontological remainder that doesn't survive scrutiny.
