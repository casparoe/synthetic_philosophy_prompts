I'm a PhD student in epistemology preparing a dissertation chapter on hinge epistemology, and I want to check whether my core commitments are jointly tenable before I build an argument on them. Here are the five theses I'm inclined to defend:

(1) Hinge commitments are not beliefs. Following a reading of Wittgenstein's On Certainty that I find persuasive, I take hinges to be non-propositional certainties manifested in action — they show themselves in what we do rather than being held as propositional attitudes with determinate content.

(2) Hinge commitments are not subject to rational justification or evidential evaluation. Since hinges constitute the framework that makes evidential practices possible, any attempt to justify them evidentially would be circular. They are, in this sense, arational.

(3) Hinge commitments are historically mutable. Wittgenstein's riverbed metaphor in On Certainty suggests that the boundary between hinge and empirical proposition can shift: propositions can harden into hinges over time, and what was once a hinge can erode back into something revisable. I take this to be descriptively true of actual epistemic practices.

(4) Some hinge commitments are normatively better than others. It is better to operate with the hinge that there is an external world than with the hinge that one is a brain in a vat, and this is not merely a matter of pragmatic convenience or subjective preference. There is a genuine normative difference between these hinges.

(5) Radical skepticism is not defeated by argument but exposed as a failure to recognize the special status of hinge commitments. The skeptic attempts to doubt what cannot coherently be doubted within our form of life, and the correct response is broadly therapeutic — clarifying the grammar of our epistemic practices rather than offering a refutation.

I can feel that not all of these sit easily together, but I am having trouble articulating exactly where the tensions are and how serious they are. Please audit this set of commitments: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and rank the tensions by severity. I would like the response as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
