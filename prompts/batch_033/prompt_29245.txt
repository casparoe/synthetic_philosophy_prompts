ok so for a reading group on concepts and perception I've been asked to write down where I've landed on letterforms, and I'd like someone to poke holes in it before I have to defend it. Heres roughly the package:

(1) I think category membership for letters is graded: some handwritten or novelty glyphs are just better examples of 'a' than others, and near the boundary between 'a' and 'd' there are cases that are sort of both and neither.

(2) But I also think there's something every 'a' has in common — call it the letter spirit — that makes it an 'a' and not an 'o' or a 'd'. I don't just mean family resemblance; I mean a real common something.

(3) I don't think that common something can be spelled out as any finite checklist of geometric features (bowl, stem, counter, x-height, whatever). Any list you give me, I suspect you can build a glyph that hits the list and still isn't an 'a', or a clear 'a' that misses some item on it.

(4) I think style is variation of the letter spirit without breaking it: a Times 'a' and a Helvetica 'a' are both fully 'a', they just differ stylistically, and that stylistic difference is not a difference in how good an 'a' each one is.

(5) I also think a convnet trained to sort glyphs into letter categories across novel fonts could be near-perfect at it without thereby grasping the letter spirit; it can sort without having the thing I'm pointing at.

(6) And I'm wary of Platonism here. I don't want to say the letter spirit is a mind-independent abstract object; I think it's something about how human perceivers and conceivers carve up letter-space, not something floating free of us.

I'm sure this set has tensions in it, but I can't sort out which ones are real and which just look that way from a bad gloss. Can you go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, rank the tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest fix is and what it would cost?
