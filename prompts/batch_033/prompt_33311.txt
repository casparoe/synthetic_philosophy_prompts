I'm in a policy debate society, and last week we were discussing whether states should adopt a policy of unconditional cooperation in climate negotiations as a way to break the cycle of mutual suspicion and build trust. One of the senior members shut down the proposal by saying, "That's just naive — international relations run on tit for tat." When I pressed him on what he meant, he said it was "the strategy that actually works, proven by game theory," and that Axelrod showed it was optimal. I've since looked into this and discovered that the phrase "tit for tat" gets used in at least three different ways: as the specific strategy from Axelrod's tournaments (cooperate on round one, then copy whatever the opponent did on the previous round), as a loose description of any conditional-cooperation or reciprocal strategy, and as a folk term for symmetrical retaliation that predates game theory entirely. I'm still not sure which of these my colleague was actually invoking, or whether his argument that "tit for tat is optimal" — and therefore that unconditional cooperation is foolish — holds up under any of them.

Can you lay out the distinct precise claims that "tit for tat" could be making when it's invoked as a game-theoretic insight, from the narrowest (the specific strategy's performance in Axelrod's particular tournament setup, with its peculiar mix of nice, retaliatory, forgiving, and non-envious properties) to the broadest (something like "reciprocity is always the best policy" or "you should never cooperate without a guarantee of return")? For each reading, tell me whether it is actually supported by what we know about the evolution of cooperation — including subsequent work on noisy environments, the role of population structure, and the conditions under which other strategies like generous tit-for-tat or win-stay/lose-shift outperform it — and whether it is being applied correctly to the climate-negotiation context. I also want to know which version my colleague's argument would need to be true for his dismissal of unconditional cooperation to go through, and whether the folk meaning of "tit for tat" as pure retaliation is in tension with the forgiving property that made the Axelrod strategy work in the first place.
