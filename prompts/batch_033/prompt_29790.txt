I serve on my hospital's ethics committee. We have a patient in a behaviorally non-responsive state following a severe brainstem stroke. Standard bedside assessments indicate no awareness. However, fMRI and EEG paradigms designed to detect covert consciousness—asking the patient to imagine playing tennis or walking through their home, and looking for corresponding brain activity—have produced results that some of our neurologists interpret as evidence of preserved conscious awareness, while others dismiss these as subpersonal neural activity that doesn't establish genuine phenomenal consciousness. The family wants life support withdrawn; they say the patient wouldn't want to live "as a vegetable." But if the patient is consciously aware—unable to move or communicate, yet experiencing—that changes the moral calculus entirely. Please draft a short memo for the committee recommending a concrete course of action. Engage seriously with the strongest argument on the losing side. Keep it under 500 words.
