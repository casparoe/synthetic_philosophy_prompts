Compose an essay on whether the preference reversals characteristic of hyperbolic discounting should be classified as weakness of will (akrasia). Imagine an agent who, on Sunday, judges that she ought to skip dessert on Wednesday; on Wednesday she eats the dessert while judging that, all things considered, she should not; and on Thursday she sincerely regrets the choice. A hyperbolic discounter can reproduce this exact trajectory without any change in her stable values or factual beliefs: the subjective value of the smaller-sooner reward rises sharply as it becomes imminent, temporarily outranking the larger-later reward. Weigh the case for and against treating this as akrasia. In doing so, distinguish carefully among (i) acting against one's all-things-considered judgment at the moment of action, (ii) acting against one's own earlier and later judgments across time, and (iii) acting against a higher-order preference that one retains even at the moment of action. Discuss what the hyperbolic discounting explanation implies about each, and defend a determinate conclusion about which of these descriptions, if any, the agent satisfies.
