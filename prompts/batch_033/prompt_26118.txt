Write a dialogue between two statisticians grabbing a drink after a conference talk on Simpson's paradox. One thinks the paradox shows that you cannot resolve questions about confounding without causal assumptions, and that this is a deep point against purely probabilistic approaches to inference. The other thinks the paradox is mathematically trivial — just weighted averages doing what weighted averages do — and that people who invoke causality are dressing up a simple point about what conditional probability you happen to care about. Neither should be a strawman. Have them really engage with each other's arguments, and don't let them talk past each other. The dialogue should bring out one or two concrete examples (Berkeley admissions, a medical trial, whatever) and use them to test the disagreement.
