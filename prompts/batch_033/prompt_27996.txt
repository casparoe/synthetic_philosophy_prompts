I sit on a clinical ethics committee with a colleague whose votes on preimplantation genetic diagnosis are consistent and firmly held, and I want to anticipate how she will vote on a proposal at our next meeting. Her record is this: she approved selecting against Tay-Sachs in a couple who were both carriers; approved selecting against trisomy 21; approved selecting against a Huntington's expansion; approved hearing parents selecting an embryo without a congenital deafness variant; approved selecting against a high-penetrance BRCA1 variant; and she opposed a Deaf couple who wanted to select an embryo carrying the same deafness variant so that the child would share their language and community. She also opposed sex selection for family balancing, but her stated reason was that it would entrench sexist family structures, not that the resulting child would be worse off. The new proposal is different in kind from anything she has voted on: a healthy couple with several healthy embryos asks the committee to approve ranking the embryos by a polygenic score for educational attainment and transferring the one with the highest expected score. I am less interested in her actual vote than in the principle behind it. The two obvious candidates are procreative beneficence, roughly the view that reproducers should select, among the possible children they could have, the one expected to have the best life, and a treatment–enhancement line that permits selection only to avoid disease or disability. Do her earlier votes force one of these principles, or do both fit the record, or does some third principle fit just as well? What does each candidate predict about the polygenic-score proposal? Which of her earlier cases is the closest analog, and does it pull one way or the other? Give your best prediction of her vote and the reasons for preferring it, state what principle a vote each way would commit her to, and specify the further case the committee has not yet seen that would cleanly separate the leading principles and show which one she is actually running.
