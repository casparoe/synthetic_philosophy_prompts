I've been reading a lot of LessWrong- and EA-adjacent stuff about wild animal suffering and digital minds, and I keep running into a pattern I dont have a name for. People will happily say that sentience (or moral status, or whatever it is that makes a thing matter) isn't binary, its a matter of degree: a pig matters more than a shrimp, a shrimp more than a bee, a bee more than a thermostat, and the thermostat is very close to zero but maybe not exactly zero. Fine. But then, two paragraphs later, the same people talk as if the only question is whether something is "in" or "out." Either they treat anything with nonzero expected moral weight as fully mattering (because any chance of sentience means you should count it), or they pick a threshold somewhere between insect and shrimp and treat everything below it as zero for practical purposes. The gradient they just insisted on quietly collapses back into a cutoff, and I've seen careful people do this while fully aware they're doing it.

Is there an established name for this — either for the graded-status view itself, or for the collapse-back-into-a-binary move? And what would a view look like that actually keeps the grading all the way down, without either exploding into "everything above zero counts equally" or bottoming out at "below the line is zero"? I'm not after a bare label; I want the carving: how this relates to the problem of marginal cases, to sorites/vagueness worries about where the line is, and to expected-value reasoning about uncertain sentience, and where exactly the described move differs from each of those.
