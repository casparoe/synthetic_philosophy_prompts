Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed advocate of the gene's-eye view of selection and a committed advocate of multi-level selection theory. The statement should reflect the state of the debate as it stands after decades of exchange — not the caricatures each side sometimes attributes to the other, but the considered positions of researchers who have engaged seriously with the opposing framework. Structure the document as follows:

(1) A numbered list of claims both parties can sign. These should cover: the formal relationships between kin selection and group selection models; what the Price equation decomposition shows and what it leaves open; the empirical conditions under which group structure demonstrably affects evolutionary outcomes; the criteria both sides accept for identifying a level of selection; and any agreed-upon examples where one framework has guided research more fruitfully than the other.

(2) A disagreements table with three columns: the gene's-eye advocate's formulation, the multi-level advocate's formulation, and an analysis of where exactly the formulations part ways. Each side's column should contain wording that side would endorse without reservation. The table should include at minimum the following rows: whether between-group selection in nature is typically strong enough to be evolutionarily significant without special conditions; whether the gene's-eye practice of averaging fitness effects across social contexts obscures causally relevant structure; which framework gives the more perspicuous account of the major evolutionary transitions (such as the origins of multicellularity, eusociality, and chromosomal organization); and whether the concept of a "vehicle" of selection picks out a causally distinct role or merely redescribes gene-level dynamics.

(3) A section identifying the cruxes — the sub-questions whose resolution would cause at least one party to substantially revise their view — and, for each crux, a description of a kind of empirical or theoretical result that both sides agree would bear on it, together with each side's prediction about what such an investigation would find.

Ensure that the agreed-claims section is neither vacuous nor question-begging; the real work is in finding formulations that genuinely narrow the space of disagreement while remaining precise enough that both specialists would sign them.
