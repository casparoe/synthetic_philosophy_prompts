I'm taking a graduate epistemology seminar this term and I keep getting stuck on the dogmatism paradox. The version that bugs me is this: if I genuinely know that p, then I seem to be in a position to know that any evidence against p is misleading — since if p is true, evidence against p is by definition evidence for something false. But then knowledge would let me dismiss counterevidence without ever looking at it, which feels irrational and also seems to license being wrong forever with a clear conscience. The standard moves I've seen either reject closure (expensive), or insist I have to actually examine the evidence before dismissing it (but why, if I already know it's misleading?), or bite the bullet and say knowledge really is that stubborn. None of them satisfy me.
