I've gotten into a debate with a historian friend about counterfactual history. He keeps using this analogy: history is like a river. The current — deep economic and demographic forces — determines where its going. Specific events and individuals are just ripples on the surface. His conclusion is that the big outcomes (the industrial revolution, the rise of the nation-state, the fall of Rome) were basically inevitable, and counterfactuals that turn on individual decisions are just parlor games. I'm a retired physicist and this analogy strikes me as smuggling in a lot — rivers do change course from small perturbations, for one thing. But I'd like a proper audit: what maps onto what in this analogy, where it breaks, what it quietly imports, and whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. Maybe also what a better analogy would be, if there is one.
