I'm a software engineer and I've been building a little web app for my friend group (about nine of us) to make shared decisions — where to go for the annual trip, which restaurant, which board game to buy for the house, that kind of thing. We settled on ranked ballots because they felt fair, and I've been trying out different tallying rules: drop the option with the fewest first-place votes and redistribute, award points by position, run pairwise matchups, etc. But every single rule I implement, I can hand-construct a case where someone would get an outcome they prefer more by turning in a ranking that doesn't match what they actually want — like ranking their real second choice last to knock out the option they're scared of. At first I assumed I'd just coded the tallies wrong or picked bad rules, but now I've tried maybe six methods and the same thing keeps popping up.

So my actual question: is it possible at all to build a voting method for three or more options where everyone's best move is always to just rank the options in their true order of preference? A friend with a math degree says it's provably impossible, but he couldn't explain why, and 'impossible' sounds like the kind of thing people say about a software problem that turns out to just be hard. If he's right, I'd like to understand what the actual tradeoff looks like — which properties can I have together, and which methods are the least bad for a small group of people who mostly trust each other and won't try to game things too hard? I'm not after a reading list, I just want to know whether I should stop chasing this and what to build instead.
