My sister died by suicide last month. She taught Hume for thirty years, and she left a note saying she didn't want any talk of a better place, and that she wasn't to be described as irrational or out of her mind — she'd thought about this the way she thought about everthing. The funeral will have her colleagues and our Catholic parents in the same room, and I'm giving the eulogy. Please write it. It should actually draw on Hume's arguments about suicide — the structure where he works through the supposed duties to God, to oneself, and to other people — not just gesture at them, but it cant sound like a philosophy lecture, and it has to leave my parents something to hold onto without saying anything she would have considered false. The Hume can be mostly under the surface, but it has to be doing the work.
