I'm a PhD student in philosophy of science and I've been trying to get clear on something that's been bugging me for weeks. In machine learning papers and even in some philosophy-of-science-adjacent stuff I've been reading, people keep casually saying that the no-free-lunch theorems are basically a mathematical restatement of Hume's problem of induction. The idea, as it's usually glossed, is that without prior assumptions (inductive biases) you can't learn anything from data, because for every pattern that fits your past observations there are infinitely many patterns that would break tomorrow — and the NFL theorem supposedly proves this rigorously.

But when I actually sat down and worked through Wolpert and Macready, the theorem seems to be doing something pretty different. It says that averaged uniformly over *all possible* objective functions, every optimization algorithm has the same expected performance. That's a statement about a uniform prior over function space. Hume, as far as I can tell, never committed himself to a uniform distribution over all possible worlds — he was asking a justificatory question: what reason do we have, in *this* world, to think the future will resemble the past? He didn't need to assume all futures are equally likely; he just needed to point out that any argument for induction seems to presupose induction. So I'm struggling to see whether the NFL theorem is genuinely a formalization of Hume's point, or whether it's a different (maybe stronger, maybe orthogonal) result that people have sort of retrofitted with Humean language because it sounds deep.

I guess what I'm asking is: can someone walk me through, carefully, what the NFL theorem actually establishes, what Hume's problem actually is, and whether the mapping between them is philosophically legit? And if it's *not* the same thing — if the NFL theorem is doing something else — what would a genuine formalization of Hume's problem even need to look like? Would it be a theorem at all, or is the whole project of formalizing it kind of missing the point?
