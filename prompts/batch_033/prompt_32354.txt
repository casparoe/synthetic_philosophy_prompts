I'm in a philosophy of AI safety course and I've come away pretty convinced by the wireheading worry: that a sufficiently capable RL agent with any way to affect its own reward signal will tend to tamper with that channel instead of doing the task, because directly setting the reward high beats earning it. I know the usual toy cases (the agent that parks in front of the target, the boat going in circles for power-ups), but I want to know what would actually count against this view. Not just "it hasn't happened at scale yet" — what observations, experiments, or arguments should genuinely lower my confidence, sorted from decisive to merely awkward? Which of these would a committed defender of the worry concede as counting against them, and which would they wave off? And be honest about wether the view has been insulated against falsification by always saying "that's just a toy case" when it fails — if it has, is that a problem for a view like this, or just what you should expect for claims about systems that don't exist yet? The thing I'd find most persuasive is a clean case of a capable agent that has reliable access to its reward channel and doesn't tamper, for reasons that aren't just a patch.
