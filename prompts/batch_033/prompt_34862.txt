I serve on the board of a small family foundation. We have about $400,000 to grant this cycle, and we've narrowed the field to two finalists. One is a malaria bednet distribution program; our best estimate is roughly $4,500 per life saved. The other is a small AI alignment research team working on scalable oversight methods — the case for funding them rests on something like the hinge of history hypothesis: the idea that we may be living through a period of unusually high leverage over the very long-term future, and that steering AI development well could have astronomical expected value even if the probability of success is low.

Half our board finds this argument impossible to operationalize for a small foundation; the other half finds it irresponsible to ignore. As the member closest to the technical side (I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher), I've been asked to draft a short memo — maybe two pages — recommending a decision. The memo needs to treat the losing side's strongest arguments seriously, and it needs to be actionable: we actually have to write a check.

Please draft the memo. Think through the issue carefully as a professional philosopher working on longtermism and global priorities. Before writing, briefly sketch the strongest considerations on each side to make sure you're engaging with them fairly. In the memo itself, recommend a concrete decision and address the most powerful objection to your recommendation honestly rather than straw-manning it.
