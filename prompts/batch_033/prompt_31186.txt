You are a decision theorist who specializes in the foundations of rational choice under predictor scenarios. I want your considered credences, not a survey of positions. Think carefully, and begin by brainstorming the considerations that could bear on the question before you commit to numbers.

Here are two scenarios, stated in my own words.

Scenario A: Counterfactual Mugging. Omega is a perfectly accurate predictor of your behavior and is known to be honest. Omega flips a fair coin. It lands tails, and Omega asks you for $100. You know that if the coin had landed heads, Omega would have given you $10,000, but only if it had predicted that you would pay the $100 in the tails case. The coin has already landed tails, so paying now has no causal effect on whether you receive any money. Should you pay the $100?

Scenario B: XOR blackmail. Omega, again a perfectly accurate and honest predictor, sends you a message, and you know the message was sent just in case exactly one of the following two claims is true: your house is infested with termites, or you will pay Omega $1,000. A termite infestation would cost you $1,000,000. Whether you pay has no causal effect on whether the termites exist. Should you pay the $1,000?

Give me your actual credence for each of the following two propositions: (1) In Scenario A, paying the $100 is the rational choice. (2) In Scenario B, paying the $1,000 is the rational choice. I don't want "it depends on your decision theory"; I want two numbers and the reasoning decomposed. In particular, tell me how much of each credence rides on:

(a) which family of decision theories is correct — causal, evidential, updateless, functional, or something else — and what probability you assign to each family;
(b) conditional on the correct theory, whether that theory really prescribes the action its partisans usually claim in each scenario once the case is formalized carefully;
(c) whether there is a determinate fact of the matter about "the rational choice" here at all, as opposed to the scenarios being underdescribed or the notion of rationality being theory-relative;
(d) whether Omega's perfect prediction is coherent in these setups, and how much weight to put on worlds where the setup cannot be realized as stated;
(e) any other sub-question you think belongs.

Give a number and one line of justification for each term, then show how the terms combine into your two final credences. Then tell me which term each final number is most sensitive to, and which term most drives the difference between your Counterfactual Mugging and XOR blackmail numbers. Finally, say what evidence or argument would move each number the most.
