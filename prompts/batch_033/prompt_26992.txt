Okay so I'm a novelist, mostly literary fiction with a few essays, and I host a tiny podcast about craft and the mechanics of thinking. Next month I'm interviewing a guest who defends the Hofstadter–Sander line from Surfaces and Essences: analogy isn't a special literary device but the core of all cognition, concepts are categories stretched by analogy and held together by word labels, literal and figurative language differ only in degree, and the essence of a concept is really a caricature, not a definition. My listeners are mostly writers who half-believe this already and will want me to push, not just nod along.

Draft me eight to ten interview questions that go past the elevator pitch. I want at least one question on each of the following, and for each, give me the follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give so I can keep the pressure on:

- the falsifiability worry: if every act of categorization is an analogy, the claim starts to sound unfalsifiable. His stock reply will probably be that literal meaning is just frozen analogy, so push on whether a frozen analogy is still doing explanatory work now or whether thats just the history of the word, not the mechanism.
- the grounding worry: analogy seems to presuppose similarity, and similarity is cheap, anything is like anything in some respect. If he says similarity is produced by the mapping rather than given in advance, ask what constrains the mapping and whether there's a non-analogical residue (perceptual, spatial, causal) doing the real work.
- the label question: the book leans hard on the idea that a word is what lets a category hold together across wildly different cases. Ask whether the real engine is lexical or social rather than analogical, and whether prelinguistic creatures or wordless thought can have concepts on his view.
- the normativity of metaphor: if creative metaphor and mundane categorization are the same mechanism differing only in degree, what distinguishes a good fresh metaphor from a dead cliché? If he reaches for aptness or how much a mapping reveals, ask what aptness is and whether that norm is itself explained by analogy or is a separate evaluative faculty being smuggled in.
- the logic and math case: analogy is content-sensitive and fallible, but modus ponens looks topic-neutral and exceptionless. If he says logic is the limiting case with zero slippage, ask whether a limiting case with no slippage is still an analogy in any ordinary sense, and what a non-analogical mental process would even look like on his view, i.e. what would falsify the thesis.
- the machine-learning point: he'll likely say LLMs and vector-space similarity are already doing something like this. Ask what the analogy claim adds beyond the empirical claim that transformers learn similarity structure from co-occurrence statistics, and what a real implementation of the theory would have to do that GPT-style models don't.

One more thing: no generic craft questions about where ideas come from. Wherever it's natural, tie the pushback to the experience of writing, revising, or reading a bad metaphor, so it sounds like a novelist asking and not a grad student. And dont write out the guest's answers, just the questions plus the follow-ups I'll use if he gives the standard reply.
