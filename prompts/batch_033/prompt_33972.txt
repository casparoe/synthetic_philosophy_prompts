The problem of logical omniscience is usually posed as a defect of possible-worlds and Bayesian models: they represent agents as certain of every logical truth and closed under logical consequence, which no finite reasoner is. Before I evaluate the proposed fixes, I want the specification of what a fix must accomplish. Write the desiderata for a theory of the beliefs or credences of a logically bounded agent. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are commitments of a particular research program rather than constraints on any adequate theory. Address at least the following: distinguishing failure to draw an inference from believing the negation of its conclusion; preserving some modest closure, such as modus ponens or conjunction elimination, without reinstating full classical closure; making sense of how an agent can rationally learn a logical truth or update on discovering that one of their beliefs entails another; stating how much of probabilism survives once logical omniscience is dropped, including whether credences over logically equivalent propositions must coincide and whether tautologies must receive credence one; and avoiding a theory so permissive that any pattern of belief, however incoherent, counts as rational. If some subset of these requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, identify it and explain why.
