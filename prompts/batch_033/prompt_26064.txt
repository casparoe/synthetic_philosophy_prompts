I've been working through Hofstadter's Ant Fugue — the dialogue where Aunt Hillary the ant colony supposedly has a mind, and the anteater defends this against various objections. Here's what I can't settle. Compare Hofstadter's claim that the colony-level patterns constitute a mind with the standard neuroscientific claim that a human brain's neural activity constitutes a mind. In the brain case, the neurons are physically contiguous, they signal eachother directly across synapses, and nobody thinks individual neurons have little minds of their own. In the ant colony, the "signals" are ants running around leaving pheromone trails, the parts are themselves organisms with some minimal cognition, and the whole thing is spread out over square meters. Are these differences just implementation details — so Hofstadter is basically a functionalist making the same point as any neuroscientist who says "mind is what the brain does"? Or is he making a fundamentally different kind of claim, and if so, what hangs on it? I keep feeling like there's a sleight of hand somewhere but I can't quite put my finger on it.
