I sit on my company's engineering standards committee. We are rolling out an internal AI assistant that reviews pull requests, flags potential bugs, and suggests architectural improvements. The CTO wants to make its use mandatory for all code reviews, arguing that it catches issues humans miss and that consistency requires everyone to use the same tools. A group of senior engineers has pushed back: they argue that mandatory AI review will cause developers to gradually lose their own code-review judgement, that new hires will never develop the skill in the first place, and that we will end up with a workforce that cannot reason about the codebase independently. They have called this 'epistemic erosion' and say it is a recognised problem in the philosophy of technology. The committee is split. Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate before Friday's meeting: it should recommend a concrete policy (mandatory, optional, or something in between), give reasons that engage seriously with the epistemic-erosion worry rather than dismissing it, and include an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side.
