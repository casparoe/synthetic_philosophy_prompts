i'm putting together a little reading packet on animal signaling and want a poem to open the section on costly signaling. write a villanelle that gets the handicap principle right — nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain. the two refrains should be some version of 'the cost is not a flaw, it's the proof' and 'what costs you nothing, anyone can fake' — tweak the wording so it scans, but keep them as the two halves of the argument. the form should do the actual work: the refrains keep returning and passing each other because cost and honesty are mutually entailed in the theory, and the last two lines should finally put them side by side. use the opening tercet to set up the puzzle (a gazelle stotting in front of a predator, a peacock dragging that tail), and let the middle stanzas work out that the waste is only informative because its priced differently for different signalers — a healthy gazelle can afford to stot and still outrun the lion, a sick one can't; the tail is a burden a well-fed peacock can carry and a worm-ridden one cant. don't let it drift into the lazy version where any old cost makes a signal honest. someone who knows zahavi's work should recognize the view and not wince, and someone who doesn't should still get a poem that does the explaining.
