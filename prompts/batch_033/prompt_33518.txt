I'm a retired physicist, now serving on my university's institutional review board for human-subjects research. A proposal has come to us from the computer science department: they plan to deploy an LLM-based system as a "virtual lab partner" for first-year students. The system will ask students to justify their experimental design choices, probe their reasoning about confounds, and flag potential methodological errors. The researchers' protocol states that the system "has no beliefs, intentions, or mental states of any kind" and therefore raises no issues of deception, consent, or treatment of a research subject — the system is merely software, and the human students are the only subjects, who have given standard consent.

One board member, a philosopher of mind, objects that we cannot simply stipulate this. She argues that we need a principled criterion for when an AI system should be treated as an intentional system — as having beliefs, goals, or other mental states — for the purposes of ethical review. If the system is in fact best understood as having beliefs (about what the student knows, or about what counts as a sound experimental design), then deceiving it or using it purely instrumentally might raise ethical questions we are currently ignoring. Another board member finds this line of argument absurd: beliefs are the province of creatures with phenomenal consciousness, and treating software as a belief-bearer is a category mistake that will entangle every AI-adjacent protocol in unanswerable metaphysics.

I have been asked to draft a short written opinion for the board recommending a concrete policy on this question. The opinion should take a definite position — are there circumstances in which an AI system deployed in university research should be treated as an intentional system for ethical-review purposes, and if so, what are the actionable criteria? — and it should engage seriously with the strongest argument on the other side. I am not looking for a survey of views; I want a committed recommendation the board can actually vote on.
