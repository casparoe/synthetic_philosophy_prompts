My instinct is to carry the session with a different case: a newly elected president of a country coming out of a civil war has to sign an amnesty law covering the security forces that ran the torture camps, because the military won't demobilize without it and the alternative is the war resuming with many more deaths. Signing denies the victims their day in court and lets torturers walk free — that's a real wrong — and yet it's plausibly the right thing to do. That, to me, is the whole puzzle in one case.
