What do you think Hume would have made of money pump arguments? I'm thinking specifically about the tension between his bundle theory of the self and the diachronic demands that money pumps seem to impose. Like, if the self is just a bundle of perceptions with no real unity across time, then why should I-Hume-now care that I-Hume-tomorrow is gonna get fleeced? The money pump argument against intransitive preferences seems to presuppose exactly the kind of unified agent across time that Hume spent a whole book arguing doesn't exist. But then again, Hume was super pragmatic — he basically said yeah the self is a fiction but we gotta live as if it's real. So maybe he'd just shrug and say, sure, avoid intransitive preferences because you'll lose money, and that's reason enough. Where do you think he'd actually land? And what part of his philosophy — the Treatise stuff on personal identity, or the more practical skepticism of the Enquiry — would do the most work in getting him there?
