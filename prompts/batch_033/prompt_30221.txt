I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I've been arguing on and off with a friend who did graduate work in epistemology. She keeps insisting that the interesting question isn't whether a fuel gauge happens to be right, but how anyone could come to know that their own information sources are reliable without going in a circle; to me it sounds like the question of whether a test suite could ever certify its own correctness, but she keeps bringing it back to concrete cases. I've been collecting her verdicts on a set of those cases.

Here is what she has said so far. (A) You look at the fuel gauge in an unfamiliar car; it reads Full; you have no prior independent evidence about whether this particular gauge is accurate. She says you still know the tank is full. (B) Over many fill-ups, you keep a log of pairs like the gauge said Full and the tank was full, where your only reason for writing down that the tank was full on each occasion is the gauge reading itself, and you then conclude that this gauge is reliable. She says you do not know that. (C) You check the gauge against a dipstick on many independent occasions and then conclude that the gauge is reliable. She says you do know that.

Next week's case is this: you look at the gauge, it reads Full, and you come to know that the tank is full in the way she allows in (A); then you infer the weaker claim that the gauge is not currently misreading the tank — that is, it is not the case that it reads Full while the tank is empty. I want you to predict whether she will say you know that weaker claim. I can see at least two rules that fit (A), (B), and (C) and that come apart on exactly this inference, so please spell out those rules, say what each one predicts about the new case, give your best guess about her verdict with the reasons for preferring one rule over the other, and name one further case that would settle which rule she is running if she hedges. Also tell me how confident you are in your prediction and which part you are least sure about.
