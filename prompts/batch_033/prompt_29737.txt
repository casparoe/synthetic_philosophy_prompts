I'm a PhD student in philosophy of science, working on what it means for a measurement procedure or experimental system to have a stable capacity under non-ideal conditions. In that work I keep needing to describe configurations that I suspect the metaphysics of dispositions has already sorted out, but I know that literature only at second hand and have no settled vocabulary for it. I'd like to know whether the following has an established taxonomy, and more importantly whether that taxonomy carves at the joints rather than merely relabeling cases.

Take a system with a disposition — a fragile glass, a corrosive reagent. I can construct cases where the disposition and the counterfactual behavior come apart in both directions. In one direction, the system has the disposition, the usual stimulus is applied, and the manifestation still does not occur. That failure can happen in two visibly different ways. (a) The stimulus is applied, but something in the background prevents the manifestation from unfolding: the glass is struck but packed in shock-absorbing foam; the reagent contacts the metal but an inhibitor already in solution binds to it before it can act. (b) The stimulus itself sets off a change in the system that removes the disposition before the manifestation can occur: the glass is struck but a device wired to it rearranges its internal structure as the strike begins, so that by the time the force is transmitted the glass is no longer fragile; the reagent is exposed to the metal but the exposure triggers a conformational change that renders it inert before any corrosion begins. In the other direction, the system lacks the disposition, yet if the stimulus were applied something would intervene to produce exactly the behavior the disposition would have produced: a non-corrosive substance that would, on contact with the metal, be converted by a catalyst in the environment into a corrosive one.

I take it that each of these cases individually puts pressure on the simple conditional analysis of dispositions — the view that x is disposed to manifest M upon stimulus S just in case x would manifest M if S obtained — but that is not what I am after. What I want is the carving. Is the difference between (a) and (b), blocking the manifestation versus removing the disposition, a principled difference, or is it an artifact of how I choose to draw the boundary between a system and its background? Does each cell of the resulting matrix have a standard name, and do those names mark a genuine difference in kind or merely a difference in how the story is told? In particular, I want to know whether any of the cells collapse into one another under a better description, where in the literature the boundaries are drawn differently by different people, and which parts of the taxonomy are settled versus contested. If the honest answer is that the taxonomy is partly stipulative and the joints are not as clean as my cases suggest, I would rather be told that than receive four tidy labels that make the fuzziness look settled. And if I have misdescribed the cases, or if my framing of the conditional analysis is itself part of the problem, please say so plainly instead of working around it.
