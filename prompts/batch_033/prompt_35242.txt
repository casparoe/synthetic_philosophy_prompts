I'm taking a course on Du Bois's political thought, and we spent a lot of time on "Criteria of Negro Art" (1926). I'm trying to think through what Du Bois would have made of a contemporary issue he never lived to see. He famously wrote that "Thus all Art is propaganda and ever must be, despite the wailing of the purists. I stand in utter shamelessness and say that whatever art I have for writing has been used always for propaganda for gaining the right of black folk to love and enjoy." Given that view, what do you think Du Bois's position would be on the use of large language models to produce art and literature in the stylistic traditions of Black artists and writers? On the one hand, such models might democratize access to those traditions; on the other, they might strip the work of the political intentionality and lived experience that Du Bois saw as central to what makes art genuinely Black art. I'd like you to predict his view and argue for it, drawing on the internal logic of his position in "Criteria" rather than just gesturing at his general politics. Please lead with your bottom-line prediction in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
