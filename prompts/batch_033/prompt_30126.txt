In a discussion about the sea-battle puzzle for future contingents, a colleague kept deflating every version of the problem with the slogan "whatever will be, will be," as if that sentence both captured the whole puzzle and resolved it in favor of a settled future. I suspect the one slogan is being asked to do several distinct jobs at once. Disambiguate "whatever will be, will be" into the precise theses it could be used to assert — for instance, a bare logical truth about the future tense, a principle of bivalence applied to statements about the future, a fatalistic claim that deliberation and precaution are impotent, a deterministic claim about the causal fixity of what is to come, and any others you can identify — and for each say whether it is a trivial tautology, a substantive and defensible thesis, or false, and whether that reading would actually settle the sea-battle question or license giving up on deliberation. Conclude by saying which reading the slogan's use in that argument would need in order to do the work it was being made to do.
