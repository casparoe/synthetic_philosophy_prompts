I'm running a small upper-level undergrad seminar called "Minds and Machines," 14 students, 90-minute sessions. We're about to hit the chapters in Gödel, Escher, Bach where Hofstadter builds the analogy between a formal system that can't prove its own Gödel sentence (but we can see it's true from outside) and a mind that becomes aware of itself — the "strange loop" idea. I want the students to feel the force of that inside/outside jump before we name it, not just hear me lecture about it. So I need a runnable classroom activity.

Design an activity around a small formal string-rewriting system. You can use a variant of the MIU puzzle from GEB: start with the string MI, and the rules are (1) if a string ends in I you may append a U; (2) from Mx you may derive Mxx; (3) anywhere III appears you may replace it with U; (4) any UU may be dropped. The target is MU. Students work in pairs or small groups, grinding out derivations, trying to reach MU. They should get stuck. Then, through some structured prompt from the facilitator (but no lecture), they should discover the invariant themselves — the thing about the number of I's that shows MU is unreachable. The moment they step outside the system and see why it can't be done is the core of the activity. Give me rules, materials (whiteboard? handouts?), a 90-minute timeline, and a debrief that maps that moment to Gödel's move: the system can't prove the sentence, but they can see it's true. Then extend to the self-aware mind part — and be honest about wich part of the analogy the activity actually reproduces and which part it can't, and what the facilitator has to supply.

Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it. I'm worried someone will have already done the puzzle and blurt out the invariant, or that the connection to self-awareness will feel like a total non-sequitur to students who just spent an hour manipulating strings. How do I handle that? And is there a way to set up the activity so that even if somebody knows the puzzle, the debrief still lands?
