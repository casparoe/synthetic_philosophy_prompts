I've been trying to understand Wittgenstein on religion, mostly from reading summaries and blog posts online — I'm not a philosophy student or anything, just got curious after a friend mentioned him. From what I gather, he says that religious belief isn't like scientific belief. You don't treat "God exists" as a hypothesis you check against evidence the way you'd check whether it's going to rain. But here's what confuses me and what I keep going back and forth on. If religious statements aren't making factual claims that could be true or false, then aren't they basically just emotional expressions or lifestyle choices? Like, if I say "I believe in God" and you say "I believe the universe is a meaningless accident," and neither of us is making a factual claim, then aren't we just reporting different feelings? And if that's the case, why did Wittgenstein seem to take religion so seriously — he wasn't exactly a believer himself but from everything I've read he clearly thought religious language was deep and important, not just a mistake or a confusion. He even said something about how he understood religious belief, or at least that he wasn't dismissive of it in the way a lot of atheist philosophers are.

So I feel like either I'm missing something, or he's trying to have it both ways: saying religious claims aren't factual but also treating them as more than just feelings. Which is it?
