Computational complexity theory is often invoked in philosophical debates about the limits of cognition, yet its canonical hardness results are worst-case results. Since many NP-hard problems turn out to be easy on the instances that arise in practice, and since any finite search space can in principle be exhausted by a sufficiently patient reasoner, why should these results be thought to constrain what human or machine intelligence can actually do? Aren't the alleged limits just artifacts of focusing on contrived worst cases rather than the problems people genuinely face?
