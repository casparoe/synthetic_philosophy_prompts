Take the James Frey thing from the mid-2000s — A Million Little Pieces, Oprah's Book Club, the Smoking Gun reporting that large chunks of the memoir were invented or inflated, Oprah initially defending him and then having him back on to confront him — and analyze it through Dennett's claim that the self is a center of narrative gravity. I know the basic story, so don't recap it. What I want is the careful mapping: on Dennett's view the self is an abstraction we posit to make sense of a person's story, not an object sitting somewhere in the head, and we are all confabulating our autobiographies to some degree anyway. So was the outrage just a category mistake, or is there a difference in kind between the routine self-narrative we all spin and what Frey did? Is Frey's published narrator a second center of gravity, and if so, which self were readers actually attached to? Then run it the other way: what does the scandal show about the theory — does it reveal that the narrative-gravity view is missing a truthfulness or accountability condition, or does it actually predict the sense of betrayal people felt, since they'd computed a Frey from false coordinates? Where does the theory go silent on the parts of the episode that mattered most, and is the whole mess a confirmation of Dennett's view, an anomaly for it, or a case it never anticipated?
