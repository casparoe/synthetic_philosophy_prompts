I've been turning over a puzzle that keeps coming up when people argue about whether you can compare futures that contain infinitely many people — or even say whether one such future is good, bad, or neutral overall — and I've realized the thing I'm actually stuck on might have a name, but I dont just want a label, I want to know what the phenomenon is and where its edges are. Here's the setup. Take a future where every person's life is either clearly good or clearly bad; say each person is at +1 or -1, and there are infinitely many of each. At first glance you'd call that future neutral overall, since there are as many happy people as miserable ones. But someone I was arguing with said you can't even say that, because you can line up the happy people and the miserable people one-to-one with nobody left over, which is supposed to show there are exactly as many of each. Maybe that just shows 'as many as' is a weird idea here. But then they offered a fix: forget pairing people up; just go through the future in some order and keep a running total. If the total climbs without bound, the future is good overall; if it plunges, it's bad; if it hovers around zero, it's neutral.

That fix is where I got stuck. The running-total method is completely hostage to the order you count the people in. If I list them as +1, +1, -1, +1, +1, -1, and so on, the total climbs without bound, so the future looks infinitely good. If I list them as -1, -1, +1, -1, -1, +1, and so on, it plunges, so the same future looks infinitely bad. If I alternate +1, -1, +1, -1, the total just bounces around and never settles, so it looks roughly neutral. Same future, same people, same lives — the only thing that changed is the order I happened to count them in, and nothing about the future itself picks out one order as the right one. So a method that was supposed to rescue comparisons of infinite futures can be made to give any verdict you want.

Is there an established name for this exact problem — not the whole issue of whether infinite outcomes can be compared, and not the pairing puzzle I started with, but specifically the fact that the running-total comparison depends on the order of summation? It feels adjacent to a few ideas I half-remember, but I dont want to guess at them. What are the nearest concepts, how is this different from each, and if there is no name for it, what would you call it? I'd also want to know where the boundary cases are: does the problem disappear if all the levels have the same sign, or if there's a natural spatial order to the people, or if you only compare finite changes? I care about the carving, not just the label.
