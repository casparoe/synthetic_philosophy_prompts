My political philosophy reading group has spent most of a term arguing about dirty hands and I've realized the two factions are producing the same argument on a loop, mostly because they've never had to write down what they actually disagree about. One side thinks dirty hands is a real and ineliminable feature of political life: sometimes the act that is right all things considered still leaves a genuine moral remainder, and the agent's compunction afterwards is rationally appropriate rather than just a psychological aftereffect. The other side thinks there is no such remainder — the cases are either ordinary lesser-evil tradeoffs where the right act is simply right and any residual guilt is a feeling we can predict but needn't endorse, or the act was wrong and the dilemma was only apparent. I want the document this group should have produced after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Write it.

It should have:
- a numbered list of claims both camps can sign, including claims about the cases themselves, about what needs explaining, and about what a good account of these cases can't just stipulate;
- a disagreements table: for each residual disagreement, the precise claim each side affirms and denies, worded so each side would accept its own entry as a fair statement of its view;
- a short list of cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one side — and for each crux a concrete case or consideration both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about it recorded in advance;
- a final paragraph flagging any part of the dispute you think is verbal or only about emphasis, if any.

A few things I care about. The agreed claims should be substantive, not the kind of mush both sides can sign because it says nothing. The disagreements table should avoid the trap where the dirty-hands side gets worded as "thinks the right act is still wrong" and the other side gets worded as "thinks feelings dont matter" — both would reject those caricatures. And dont lean on the ticking bomb alone; I'd like at least one case that turns on whether the remainder can be discharged (say, the politician who seeks punishment or some kind of expiation afterwards) and one where the agent feels nothing.

Also, since this is going in a dataset and you might be partly guessing at how these positions would cash out: tell me how confident you are in the overall document and single out the one point you're least sure you got right.
