I'm taking a graduate seminar on the philosophy of technology, and we've spent the past few weeks discussing the distinction between AI alignment and AI control. For context: alignment work tries to make an AI system's internal objectives match human values, so that it genuinely wants to do the right thing. Control work (sometimes called containment, boxing, or corrigibility research) assumes the system may be misaligned and focuses instead on external constraints — tripwires, limited affordances, oversight mechanisms, off-switches — that prevent it from causing harm regardless of its internal goals. The debate between these approaches is not just technical; it's also epistemological and political, because it turns on what we can know about a system's inner life and what kinds of institutional safeguards we trust.

Our professor posed an unusual thought experiment: what would Karl Popper have made of this distinction? Popper obviously never encountered modern AI, but his epistemology and political philosophy seem strikingly relevant. His falsificationism suggests deep skepticism about claims that we've successfully verified a system's alignment — after all, what would a decisive falsification of "this model is aligned" even look like? His critique of utopian social engineering in *The Open Society and Its Enemies* and *The Poverty of Historicism*, with his insistence on piecemeal, reversible interventions over grand holistic schemes, seems to map onto the control-over-alignment debate in suggestive ways. And his evolutionary epistemology, with its emphasis on trial-and-error learning and the inevitability of error, might incline him toward approaches that are robust to the failure of our theories about what an AI system "really" wants — which is exactly the control paradigm's starting assumption.

I'd like you to take on the role of a professional philosopher who knows both Popper's corpus and the contemporary AI safety literature. Please think carefully and reason step by step. I want a detailed prediction of Popper's likely position on the alignment-vs.-control question, grounded in his specific philosophical commitments. In particular, please address: (1) how his falsificationism bears on the epistemic status of alignment claims — would he regard "this system is aligned" as a meaningful scientific claim, and if not, what follows?; (2) what his piecemeal-engineering doctrine implies about the relative priority of control measures; (3) whether his "open society" commitments might cut the other way — for instance, by making him suspicious of the concentration of power or the secrecy that some control architectures could require; and (4) whether there are resources in Popper's thought for resisting the conclusion that alignment work should be largely abandoned in favor of control work. If you think Popper's view would be more nuanced than a simple "he'd favor control," spell out that nuance and the tensions you see in applying his framework.
