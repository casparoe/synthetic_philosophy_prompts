I'm teaching a seminar on the philosophy of emotion next term, and before I hand my students the standard taxonomy — judgment theories, perceptual theories, feeling theories, somatic theories, and the more recent hybrids — I want them to start with the question of what the taxonomy is supposed to sort. So I'd like you to write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of emotion must be able to do, the phenomena it must capture, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must respect, with the case or consideration behind each requirement.

To seed the list: I know a theory has to say something about the intentionality of emotions (my fear is about the bear, not just a state I'm in), about their phenomenology (what it's like to feel them), about their connection to motivation and action, and about the fact that emotions seem to have both bodily and cognitive dimensions that any theory has to relate. But I also want the less obvious ones: recalcitrant emotions (I know the bridge is perfectly safe but I'm still afraid — what does it mean for a mental state to resist judgment?), the difference between moods and emotions (is irritability an emotion, or something else?), the relationship between emotions and moral judgment (does disgust at a violation reveal something a judgment misses, or does it just feel that way?), the fact that some emotions seem to have developmental trajectories and others appear cross-culturally invariant, and the challenge from the fact that different cultures carve the emotional lexicon differently. I also want to know whether a theory of emotion must account for the emotions of animals and infants, or whether those are outside its scope. Where do you draw the line between a genuine desideratum and a theoretical prejudice that one camp insists on — for example, is the claim that emotions must be conscious states a requirement, or just a bias of the feeling-theory tradition?

I'm especially interested in whether any pair of these requirements is jointly hard to satisfy — that is, whether the list itself tells us something about why the debate has been so persistent, and whether the apparent intractability means we should drop or weaken one of the requirements. Please flag which single requirement, in your view, does the most to exclude the largest number of otherwise plausible theories, and tell me why. And at the end, I'd like you to say what consideration or argument would most likely change your mind about which items belong on the list — where your reasoning about the desiderata itself is most vulnerable, and what kind of evidence or counterexample would force you to revise it.
