So I've been teaching Harry Frankfurt's "Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility" for like the fifteenth time this semester, and I keep thinking — someone should really write a good piece of fiction that captures what's actually at stake in these cases. You know the setup: Jones is deliberating about whether to do something terrible, and unbeknownst to him, Black has implanted a device that'll make Jones do it if he shows any sign of deciding not to. But Jones goes ahead and decides to do it on his own, so Black never intervenes. Frankfurt says: Jones is morally responsible even though he couldn't have done otherwise. The principle of alternate possibilities is false.

I'd like you to write a short story that brings a Frankfurt-style case to life. Don't just rehash the Jones/Black scenario — invent your own characters and situation, make it feel lived-in. The story should make the reader feel the pull of the intuition that the protagonist is responsible, despite the counterfactual intervener lurking in the background. Maybe explore what it's like from the inside — the phenomenology of making a choice when (unbeknownst to you) you actually couldn't have done otherwise. What does that feel like? Does it matter that you don't know?

Feel free to play with genre — could be literary fiction, sci-fi, whatever works. And don't feel like you have to resolve the philosophical question neatly. A good story probably leaves the reader unsettled rather than convinced.
