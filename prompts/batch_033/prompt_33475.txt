I'm writing a one-page "where I stand" document for our AI alignment teams reading group on whose values an aligned AI should serve, and I'd like a consistency check before I circulate it. You're a philosopher who works on social choice theory and liberal political philosophy, so I'd trust you to catch tensions I've missed. Please think carefully: start by reconstructing, in a sentence each, what you take to be the standard motivation for each of my commitments, then go through them pairwise, say which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look like they are, rank the tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what the cheapest fix would be. Don't rubber-stamp it — I'd rather hear the problems now than in the meeting.

Here's where I've landed. (1) An aligned AI should be neutral between reasonable conceptions of the good: it shouldn't take sides on what makes a life worth living, and its operations should be justifiable to people who disagree with me about that. (2) But I'm not neutral about preferences that are themselves about subordinating or dominating other people; I think an AI should flatly refuse to help someone pursue a way of life that requires controlling someone else, even if the preference is deeply held and autonomous. (3) I think the best evidence about what someone actually values is their revealed preferences — what they choose and do — not their stated preferences, because people systematically say what's socially expected rather than what they'd actually stand behind. (4) At the same time I'm convinced revealed preferences can't just be taken at face value: adaptive preferences mean people in bad circumstances come to want less than they'd want under better conditions, and choices get shaped by advertising and manipulation, so actual choice can reflect circumstances rather than values. (5) I'm moved by the impossibility results in social choice theory: there's no neutral, non-dictatorial way to aggregate individual rankings into a single social ranking, so any AI that claims to serve "humanity as a whole" is implicitly choosing a weighting rather than discovering one. (6) Nevertheless I want the AI to be for everyone, and I think the test of a good alignment scheme is that it could be accepted by any reasonable person, not just by people who share my politics. (7) I'm inclined to respond to the aggregation problem by giving up on a single aggregate ranking and instead having the AI enforce individual rights as side-constraints while staying neutral otherwise. (8) I also think the AI should be Pareto-respecting: if everyone prefers option A to option B, it shouldn't choose B.

Where am I fooling myself?
