I am a retired physical chemist who has spent the last few years reading philosophy of economics and welfare, and I am preparing a short proposal for a small independent-research grant. My working idea is that discussions of interpersonal utility comparisons have been hobbled by an unexamined analogy with physical measurement: both defenders and critics tend to assume that a legitimate comparison must be backed by something like a common cardinal scale with specified invariance conditions. I want to ask whether the comparisons actually required by distributive and policy reasoning could instead be grounded in weaker structures—domain-specific comparisons, or pairwise judgments that need not be complete or transitive—and whether that weaker grounding would be enough to do the normative work. Please turn this into a proposal organized as exactly five numbered sections, each one or two sentences and with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the question stated precisely; (2) why the existing positions leave it open; (3) the approach I would take, month by month, and why it is preferable to the obvious alternatives; (4) what a successful result would look like and what would count as failure; (5) a realistic scope for the time available. Do not include citations or a bibliography; I will add those myself.
