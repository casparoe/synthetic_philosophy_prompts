"According to the Buddha, the self is an illusion. What you think of as your self is really just a bundle of constantly changing mental states, so there is no persisting person over time. That is why Buddhists say there is ultimately no one who suffers, no one who is reborn, and no one who attains enlightenment—and why they reject personal identity as a Western illusion."

I am inclined to accept the opening claim and the talk of a bundle, but the slide to "no persisting person over time" and then to "no one who suffers" feels as if it runs several distinct claims together, and "mental states" sounds more Humean than Buddhist. I have also come across the claim that the Buddha never actually said that there is no self. I do not know the tradition well enough to locate the errors myself.
