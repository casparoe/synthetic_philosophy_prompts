For an upper-level undergraduate seminar on value theory and aggregation, draft a final exam essay question on the torture-versus-dust-specks thought experiment. The question should not be answerable by summarising the thought experiment and reporting one's intuition. It must force students to engage with the underlying structural problem: that plausible principles of aggregation, when combined with the transitivity of betterness, seem to force a conclusion (that enough dust specks are worse than torture) that many find unacceptable. The question should require students to critically evaluate at least one specific strategy for blocking that conclusion — for instance, rejecting transitivity, defending a lexical threshold or discontinuity for severe harms, or challenging the assumption that tiny burdens can be aggregated across persons in the way the puzzle requires. Provide a model answer of roughly 500–600 words and a marking scheme that distinguishes an A-range answer from a B-range answer by naming the specific argumentative moves each must contain. The marking scheme should also flag one common mistake that even well-prepared students make and explain how it should affect the grade.
