I have read enough rationalist-blog and forum discussions of the centipede game to suspect that the backward-induction camp and the rational-cooperation camp are frequently arguing past one another, especially about off-path beliefs and about what a rational player may infer from a move that should not have occurred. Please draft the document that a well-run adversarial collaboration between those two camps would have produced; the point is to find formulations that are neither vacuous nor slanted, and to separate genuine disagreements from verbal ones.

The two positions are: (i) the Unravelling camp, which holds that in a finite centipede game with common knowledge of rationality, the first player should take immediately, and that passing reveals irrationality or a failure of common knowledge; and (ii) the Cooperation camp, which holds that passing can be consistent with rationality once one allows for realistic uncertainty about the other player, and that the backward-induction conclusion depends on a contestable account of how beliefs survive surprise.

The document should contain:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims both camps can sign. Include what the formal structure of the centipede game is, what standard backward induction actually establishes and does not establish, what experimental play does and does not show, and any distinctions among prediction, rational permissibility, and rational requirement that the two camps need in order to talk about the same question.

2. A disagreements table. For each residual disagreement, give the neutral formulation of the question; the Unravelling camp's position stated in wording its own members would accept; the Cooperation camp's position stated in wording its own members would accept; and an explicit note on whether the disagreement is substantive or merely verbal.

3. A cruxes section, in the spirit of a double-crux write-up. For each crux — a sub-question whose settlement would move at least one camp — state the argument, case, or experiment both camps agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance.

Make the document pin down in particular where the two camps differ about off-path beliefs, about what a rational player is entitled to infer from a deviation, and about whether common knowledge of rationality forces immediate taking or instead collapses once a surprise move is observed.

Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this material, and do not present a contested reading of the formal results as the consensus view. Keep the entire document to no more than 1,000 words, and write in British English.
