I've been going back and forth on the Sleeping Beauty problem for way too long and I want someone to just lay out the arguments clearly and take a side. For anyone who doesn't know it: Beauty goes to sleep Sunday, a fair coin is flipped. Heads: she wakes Monday and that's it. Tails: she wakes Monday, gets her memory wiped, wakes Tuesday. So on tails she's awakened twice. When she finds herself awake during the experiment, she knows the setup but not what day it is. What should her credence in Heads be — 1/2 or 1/3?

Write an essay that reaches a firm conclusion. But here's the thing: I want the essay to take seriously a line of argument I don't see explored enough. The standard arguments for thirding tend to run through betting: if Beauty bets on Heads at even odds each time she wakes, she loses money in the long run, which suggests her credence on waking should be 1/3. The standard halfing response is that the betting setup is rigged — on tails she gets to bet twice, so her "effective" credence should be different from what you'd naively read off the bets.

Here's what I want you to grapple with. Suppose we accept there's something funny about linking credence to betting when the number of bets depends on the outcome. But now consider this variant: Beauty bets once, on Monday. On Tuesday (if there is one) she's just interviewed, no bet. What should she be willing to bet on Monday morning when she doesn't know whether it's Monday or Tuesday? And now add a twist: on Monday, after she states her credence but before she bets, we tell her "today is Monday." Does her credence shift? If she was a thirder before, she should become a halfer upon learning it's Monday (since P(Heads|Monday) = 1/2 by Bayes). If she was a halfer already, learning it's Monday shouldn't change anything — but doesn't that mean she learned nothing, even though she was just told which day it is?

I want the essay to work through this and related puzzles and end up somewhere definite. Don't just catalogue positions. If you think the whole debate rests on an ambiguity in what "credence" means in self-locating contexts, say that and defend it. If you think one side is clearly right and the other is making a subtle mistake, say what the mistake is. I'd rather read a wrong but clear conclusion than a careful survey.
