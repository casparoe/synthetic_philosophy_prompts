You're a game theorist who's actually taught this material to people who find proofs unconvincing, not just someone who can recite the definitions. I teach a mixed undergrad/grad game theory seminar, around 18 people, one 80-minute session. I want an in-class game where students feel, before any definitions are given, that a strategy can be strictly dominated by a mixture of two other strategies even when neither of those two dominates it alone, and that iterating this kind of deletion eliminates options that pure-strategy elimination leaves alive. Design the whole thing: rules, materials, a points system that doesnt need real money, and a debrief that maps what happened to strict dominance by mixed strategies and to the fact that the set of survivors under iterated strict dominance is order-independent even though students will guess it isnt. The key is that they should run into the gap themselves: someone tries to defend a strategy as 'not dominated by anything I can see' and then gets shown a lottery over their own alternatives that beats it in every state. Before settling on a game, brainstorm two or three candidate payoff matrices and say briefly why you rejected the others, and think through the debrief carefully. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and which feature of the idea the game can't get across so I'll have to supply it myself.
