I'm a philosophy grad student working through some metaphysics of dispositions, and I keep tripping over a pattern I don't have a clean word for. Take a fragile glass. I'd normally cash out "fragile" as something like: if you dropped it on concrete, it would shatter. Now suppose the same glass is wrapped in a thick layer of foam, so when I drop it on concrete it doesn't shatter. It would be crazy to conclude the glass isn't fragile — the disposition is still there, something just kept it from showing up. But now tweak the case: suppose the glass is rigged to a device that, the instant it's dropped, rearranges its internal structure so it's tough by the time it hits the concrete. A moment before the drop I'd still say it was fragile, but at the moment the manifesting conditions arrive, it isn't anymore. So the conditinal "if dropped on concrete, it would shatter" is false in both cases, but for what feel like very different reasons: in one case the disposition is still present but blocked, and in the other it disappeared exactly when it was needed.

I'm trying to figure out whether this pair of failure modes has an established name, and whether they're really as different as they feel. I'm also curious about the mirror image: a thing that isn't fragile, but the act of striking it somehow makes it fragile just in time to shatter, so it "manifests" a disposition it never had before the stimulus. I'm not after just a label — I want the carving. Does the distinction survive cases where the blocker is internal to the object rather than external? Does it matter that the interference is triggered by the very same stimulus that's supposed to trigger the manifestation? And when cases get messy, what's the principle for telling the two apart?
