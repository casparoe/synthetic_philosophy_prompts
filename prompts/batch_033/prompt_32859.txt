Two friends of mine got into it at a party last weekend about whether you can get aesthetic knowledge from other people's testimony. I wasn't part of the argument but I was listening, and honestly they both made good points. Can you go through their exchange and tell me who's right about what?

Here's roughly how it went:

Marcus started by saying he didn't think you can really know a movie is good just from reading reviews — you have to watch it yourself. Elena said that's weird, because if she trusts a critic and the critic says it's great, she's justified in believing it's great, the same way she believes things on testimony in every other domain.

Marcus said aesthetic judgments are about how something strikes you. If a critic says a film is moving, that tells you how it struck him, not how it'll strike you. You can't subcontract your own sensibility. Elena replied that lots of testimony works that way — if a doctor prescribes a drug based on population studies rather than your specific biology, you still know it'll probably work. If critics have been reliable for your tastes, their recommendation is evidence you'll like it.

Marcus said that's a different thing. Predicting you'll like something isn't the same as the aesthetic judgment "this is a good film," which is a normative claim. You can't be justified in that normative claim without engaging with the work. Elena said that distinction collapses: "good film" just means something like "viewers with properly functioning sensibilities would respond positively," and if that's what it means, testimony about it is perfectly fine.

Marcus said that's circular — who has properly functioning sensibilities? The critic? How do you know that without developing your own by actually watching films? Elena said it's no different from trusting any expert: you calibrate over time, you don't need to run the physics experiment yourself to know the result.

Marcus said physics facts are observer-independent, but aesthetic facts essentially involve the observer's response, so you can't outsource them. Elena came back with: "So if you've never seen a Kurosawa film, and ten friends whose taste you trust all say Seven Samurai is a masterpiece, you're not justified in believing it's a great film until you watch it yourself?"

Marcus said: you're justified in believing your friends liked it, and justified in predicting you'll like it, but the aesthetic judgment itself — that it's a great film — requires your own engagement, because it's the kind of thing that can't be taken on someone else's word by its nature.

Then someone spilled a drink and the conversation moved on. But I want to know: who's right about what? Go through the exchange move by move and adjudicate — rate each point, say where someone's on solid ground and where they're off, and give me a bottom-line verdict on who's more right overall. Keep it to about 400 words.
