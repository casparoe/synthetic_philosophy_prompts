I'm a software engineer — no philosophy background, I live in git and distributed systems. I've been trying to wrap my head around this debate about advance directives. The scenario: I sign a living will at 45 saying "if I get severe dementia, no life-prolonging treatment." Then at 75 I actually have dementia, I seem content, and when someone tries to withhold antibiotics for my pneumonia I say "no, I want to live." The question is whose preference wins, early-me or current-me? And underneath that: is current-me even the same person as early-me, or did the dementia fork the repo?

I want you to restate the whole debate in version-control vocabulary. I mean commits (decisions at a point in time), branches, HEAD, tags, annotated tags, merge conflicts, rebase vs. merge, forced pushes, forks, the reflog — the whole toolbox. The arguments for the authority of the earlier self, the arguments for the authority of the present self, the identity question. Translate each position as faithfully as you can.

Then the residue report. What maps cleanly? What did you have to stretch or distort? What has no counterpart at all in git? And here's the thing I actually care about: when something doesn't translate, is that because my vocabulary is impoverished and missing a real distinction, or because the philosophical claim was never as precise as it sounded?
