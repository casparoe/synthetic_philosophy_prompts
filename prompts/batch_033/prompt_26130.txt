so in my gov class we were talking about voter turnout and i fell into this rabbit hole. i wrote out this argument that seems to show voting is never rational, but the conclusion is obviously insane because then democracy just... doesn't work? but i can't find the flaw. here's the argument:

(1) an action is rational only if its expected benefits are greater than its expected costs
(2) the benefit of your preferred candidate winning is huge, call it B
(3) but you only get B if your vote is the deciding vote — if the election would be tied without you
(4) in any election with more than a few thousand people, the probability your vote is decisive is astronomically small, like 1 in 10 million or worse
(5) so the expected benefit of voting is B × (basicaly zero) = basicaly zero
(6) the costs of voting — driving to the polling place, waiting in line, reading up on candidates — are small but definitely not zero
(7) so voting has negative expected value: it's irrational
(8) if voting is irrational for each individual, then democracy as a system is asking people to do something irrational, which is a pretty big problem for democratic theory
(9) but democracy isn't obviously bankrupt, so something's wrong with the argument
