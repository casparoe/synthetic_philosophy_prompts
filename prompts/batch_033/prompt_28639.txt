I just got put on my neighborhood council's committee that has to recommend whether to support a ban on a plasticizer that leaches out of food packaging, and I keep coming back to how my friend Dana applies the precautionary principle, because she's weirdly consistant about it. Her track record: she wanted a pesticide suspended when lab studies showed it disoriented bees at field-realistic doses even though the field data was mixed; she wanted a food dye pulled from shelves when rats got bladder tumors at high doses but the human data showed nothing; she was fine keeping a vaccine in use that carries a one-in-a-hundred-thousand chance of a serious autoimmune reaction, because the disease it prevents is common and severe; she wanted a flame retardant phased out of couch foam because it shows up in household dust and animal studies found effects on thyroid development at low doses, even though the alternatives cost more; and she thought a new imaging contrast agent that accumulates in tissue with unknown long-term effects should only be used when a scan is actually necessary.

The plasticizer in front of me: it leaches into food at very low levels, a couple of animal studies find mild and fully reversible changes in liver enzyme levels at high exposures, nobody knows wether the low-dose exposure does anything, and its main benefit is cheaper and more durable packaging.

What would Dana say about the plasticizer, and which of her earlier calls is the closest analog? More importantly, what rule is she actually running, what other rules fit her record just as well and would give a different answer on this new case, and what further case would cleanly settle which rule she holds?
