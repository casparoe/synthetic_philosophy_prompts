I run an upper-level undergraduate seminar in formal epistemology with about 16 students. We have 80 minutes. Design a classroom activity that makes students feel the force of the common prior assumption — specifically, the claim that if rational agents share a common prior and have common knowledge of each other's posterior probabilities, they cannot "agree to disagree." The activity should put students in a situation where they receive different private information, form probability judgments, and then have to reconcile those judgments publicly. The rules should be arranged so that they discover for themselves what happens when they do (and do not) share a common prior, before the concept is named. Include: the setup and materials needed, the sequence of rounds with precise instructions for the facilitator, a payoff scheme that does not require real money, a structured debrief that maps what happened in each round onto the formal idea, and an honest note on which features of the theorem the activity cannot reproduce. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it.
