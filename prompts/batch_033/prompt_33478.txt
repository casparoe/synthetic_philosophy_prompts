Play Kierkegaard. It's 1846, right after Two Ages came out. I'm a time traveler, and I've come to describe something to you: "social media," especially a platform called Twitter. Anyone can publish anything instantly to a vast audience. An algorithm decides what spreads based on what gets the strongest reactions. People form mobs, denounce each other, take sides, express outrage — there's constant moral fervor, constant conflict. You can ask me questions about the mechanics. Stay in character. Here's my opening: you wrote that the present age is essentially passionless, that people drift in reflection without deciding, and that the public is a phantom — an abstraction no one can be held responsible for. But what I'm describing sounds like the opposite — it's nothing but passion, decision, contradiction. People are constantly taking stands, condemning, praising, fighting. So either your diagnosis of the present age was wrong, or this thing I'm describing doesn't fit your categories. Which is it?
