I'd like you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers who have been arguing for years about Bernard Williams's integrity objection to impartial consequentialism. One side (call them the integrity camp) thinks Williams basically showed that act-consequentialism cannot make sense of the commitments and relationships that give a life its point, and that the one-thought-too-many complaint is fatal to any view on which the permissibilty of, say, a husband saving his wife depends on an impartial calculation. The other side (call them the impartialist camp) is a sophisticated consequentialist who thinks Williams was attacking a strawman, or at most a bad decision procedure, and that a criterion-of-rightness consequentialism can absorb the whole objection without becoming alienating.

The document should be the thing these two could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want it to include:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims. These shouldn't be vacuous ("both sides care about ethics") — they should be substantive claims about Williams's examples, about the difference between criteria of rightness and decision procedures, about what early act-consequentialism got wrong, and about what the husband case does and doesn't show. Both parties have to be able to sign each one without wincing.

2. A disagreements table. For each row, give the exact formulation of the disagreement in neutral terms, but also give each side's own preferred wording of their position, so the integrity camp writes their own line and the impartialist camp writes theirs. Make sure the two wordings are actually answering the same question and not just talking past each other.

3. A cruxes section: the sub-questions such that, if they were settled, at least one side would change their view. For each crux, give a concrete case or argument both sides agree would bear on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts that case or argument will show.

4. Finally, say wether any part of the dispute is merely verbal (e.g., over the word "consequentialism" or "reason") and, if so, which part and why.

Work through the actual material, don't just gesture at it: the Jim-and-the-captives case, the husband who stops to deliberate about whether to save his wife, the idea of ground projects, whether a consequentialism can be self-effacing without collapsing, and whether the impartialist can say the husband does the right thing and shouldn't have had the justificatory thought at all. Don't cite anything and don't give a reading list; the model this is for can't look anything up, so no bibliographies or page numbers. And don't just summarize Williams — the interesting part is the exact wording of the residual disagreement and the cruxes.

Write it casual, like a slightly frazzled reading-group organizer would email. A typo or two is fine.
