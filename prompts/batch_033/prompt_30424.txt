I work on the safety team at an AI lab. We review research proposals for new training setups and architectures, and one of our evaluation criteria is whether the system implements factored cognition — the idea that sophisticated reasoning can be produced by composing many short, bounded-context cognitive operations rather than relying on a single long sequential process. We treat HCH (Humans Consulting HCH) as our reference idealization for what factored cognition looks like in the limit. I need a reusable evaluation procedure my team can apply to a proposal: a set of questions to ask in order, what counts as a passing answer to each, where the procedure stops and flags a proposal as not satisfying factored cognition, and where it hands off to judgment because the idealization itself doesn't settle the matter. The procedure should be usable by someone who understands the broad idea but hasn't worked through the formal details — no more than a page, something we can run in a thirty-minute meeting. Then apply it to two proposals: (1) a chain-of-thought system where the model generates a long reasoning trace before answering, and (2) a debate setup where two models cross-examine each other's claims with a human judge. Flag the cases the procedure cannot decide and say what additional information would be needed. Please begin with the procedure itself, with no preface or commentary.
