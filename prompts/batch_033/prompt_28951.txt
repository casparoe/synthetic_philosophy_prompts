Please argue that the standard move in the theory of well-being — from the view that pleasure is a distinctive felt quality shared by all pleasant experiences to the view that pleasure is a propositional attitude of taking pleasure in states of affairs — does not solve the heterogeneity problem but merely relocates it. Show that this attitude is itself realized by a heterogeneous collection of psychological states, and that the usual attempts to unify it (by appeal to its formal object, to its functional role, or to a felt quality it silently reintroduces) either fail or forfeit the advantage the attitudinal view was meant to secure over its sensory predecessor. In the course of the argument, distinguish the claim that all tokens of the attitude share a single formal object from the claim that they form a single psychological kind, and close by stating exactly how strong a conclusion you take the relocation objection to establish.
