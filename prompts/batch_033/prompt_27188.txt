Indirect normativity gets much of its appeal from a division of labor: rather than trying to state our values directly, we specify a procedure of idealization or reflection and treat its output as authoritative. That division of labor, however, hides a cluster of unresolved questions about what the procedure is, whose values enter it, and why its output should bind anyone. A research initiative plans to fund two years of conceptual work on these foundations, at roughly the scale of three or four philosophers, with the explicit aim of settling the questions whose answers would most change how the rest of the field proceeds — not producing a survey of positions or an implementation. You sit on the committee that must set the agenda.

Give the three or four questions you would fund first, ranked, and defend the ranking. Your answer should not read as a list of interesting topics. For each question, explain what makes it prior to the others rather than downstream of them; how far a small conceptual program could realistically get in two years and what a decisive result would look like; and whether the question is comparatively neglected or already crowded. Where the criteria of importance, tractability, neglectedness, and dependence among questions pull in different directions, say how you resolve the conflict. Engage at least briefly with candidate questions such as: how to specify idealization conditions without quietly presupposing a substantive value theory; whether the deliverances of an idealized process have authority over the unidealized agents whose values are its input; whether convergence of idealized deliberation should be assumed, argued for, or abandoned; and how the framework should handle persistent disagreement among the inputs to the process. You need not choose from that list, but the ranking should show enough grip on the field's internal fault lines that a different committee member could not have produced it by shuffling familiar keywords. Finally, identify one development — conceptual or technical — that would most cause you to reorder the list, and say why.
