In my formal epistemology seminar we have been working through the Judy Benjamin problem, and I have reconstructed an argument from the discussion that I cannot find an exit from. Judy is dropped into a region partitioned into Red and Blue territory, and each territory is partitioned into Headquarters Company and Second Company; she begins with a uniform prior over the four cells. She receives a radio message telling her that if she is in Red territory, the odds are 3 to 1 that she is in Headquarters Company.

(1) When a piece of information is fully captured by a constraint on the posterior credence function, the rational update is the minimal revision of the prior that satisfies that constraint.
(2) The radio message is fully captured by the constraint P(H | R) = 3/4.
(3) The standard way to make minimal revision precise is to minimize relative entropy between prior and posterior subject to the constraint.
(4) This rule does not leave P(R) fixed: it pushes Judy's credence in being in Red territory below one-half, even though the message says nothing about whether she is in Red or Blue territory.
(5) A message that concerns only a conditional should not by itself move the probability of the conditional's antecedent; Judy's credence in R should remain exactly where it was.
(6) Therefore the minimal-revision rule, and with it the constraint-based generalization of conditionalization, must be rejected.

The conclusion strikes me as too strong, but I cannot see which premise is doing the work. Which step should go, and what does each way out cost? I would also like to know what would most likely change your mind about the diagnosis, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
