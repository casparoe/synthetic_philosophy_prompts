ok so i've been trying to nail down my views on peer disagreement for a while and i want a consistency check before i write them up for a post. here's roughly where i am:

(1) i think aumann's agreement theorem basically shows that if two people are genuine bayesians with common priors and common knowledge of each other's posteriors, they can't agree to disagree — their posteriors have to converge. and i think this has real normative bite, not just a cute formal result.

(2) i'm a conciliationist in the broad sense: when i learn that someone i consider roughly my epistemic peer disagrees with me on a question we've both thought about, i should update substantially toward their view. not necessarily split the difference exactly, but move a lot.

(3) i think the uniqueness thesis is false. there are cases where the same total evidence rationally permits more than one doxastic attitude. permissivism seems right to me, at least for some domains — maybe not everywhere, but it's not universally ruled out.

(4) i think that on some topics — the kind where i've spent hundreds of hours and the other person read one blog post — i'm rationally permitted to not update much, because we're not actually peers on that topic even if we're peers in general intelligence or rationality-hygiene. peerhood is domain-specific.

(5) i think the "right reasons" view in the epistemology of disagreement is wrong: the fact that i believe p on the basis of good evidence doesn't let me dismiss your disagreement, because you also think you're basing your belief on good evidence, and the whole problem is that we can't both be right.

(6) in practice, i notice i almost never actually update much when people disagree with me, and i'm suspicious of my own ability to identify who counts as a peer — i worry i'm just gerrymandering peerhood to protect my existing views. i think this is a real problem but i'm not sure how to fix it, and i'm not sure whether it's a problem with my psychology or a problem with conciliationism as a norm.

ok so: go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like they are. rank the tensions by severity, and for each real tension say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs. and please tell me what argument or consideration would most likely change your mind about the overall verdict — like where your reasoning is shakiest and what you'd need to see to flip.
