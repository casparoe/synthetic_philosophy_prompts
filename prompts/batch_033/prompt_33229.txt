ok so i've been trying to figure out what i actually think about moral responsibility, and my intuitions on cases are kind of a mess. i want to know if there's a principle that captures them or if i need to start overturning verdicts.

here are the cases and my gut reactions:

1. **normal bad actor**: someone with a totally ordinary upbringing, no trauma, no mental illness. at 35 they embezzle from their company because they want a nicer car. i hold them fully responsible, no hesitation.

2. **brain tumor**: someone develops a tumor that (verifiably) causes severe impulsivity and aggression. they assault a stranger. pre-tumor they were known as gentle. i don't hold them responsible at all. this feels like something happening *to* them, not something they did.

3. **abusive childhood**: someone was beaten and neglected throughout childhood, never got therapy or support, and at 25 commits a violent robbery. i hold them responsible, but less than case 1 — maybe 60%. not zero, but definitely mitigated.

4. **cult upbringing**: someone was born into an isolated cult, never exposed to outside norms, and at 20 participates in a ritual that outsiders would call assault. they genuinely believed it was morally required. i'm torn. i lean toward not holding them responsible, but it feels different from the tumor case in a way i can't articulate.

5. **the trained manipulator**: someone is a skilled emotional manipulator who exploits their partner for years, causing severe psychological harm. normal childhood by all external measures. but — here's the twist — you later learn their parent was a master manipulator who systematically trained them from age 4 to be this way, rewarding manipulation and punishing empathy. i still want to hold them responsible. but i can't explain why this is different from the cult case, where i let them off.

6. **addiction and circumstance**: someone with a genetic predisposition to addiction grows up in a neighborhood where opioid use is rampant, starts using at 14, and by 30 is stealing to fund their habit. i hold them somewhat responsible but way less than case 1. maybe 30%.

7. **the philosopher's puzzle**: a perfectly ordinary person sees a wallet on the ground, no one is watching, and takes it. they could have done otherwise in that moment. i hold them responsible. but i also believe (when i'm in a certain mood) that their choice was fully determined by genes + environment + whatever quantum randomness, none of which they chose. strawson's basic argument says this is incoherent — that you can't be ultimately responsible for what you do unless you're responsible for who you are, which you can't be. and yet i still blame the wallet thief.

so: is there a principle (or small set of principles) that captures these verdicts? which ones do i have to give up if i want consistency? and what does the principle say about a case i haven't listed — say, someone who voluntarily takes a drug they know causes aggression, and then assaults someone while under its influence?
