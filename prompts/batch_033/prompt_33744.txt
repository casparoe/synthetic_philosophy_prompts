So I've been working on this book manuscript for like two years now, and last month I wrote the preface — you know, the standard "all errors are my own" bit — and then I couldn't stop thinking about it. I genuinely believe every claim in the book, but I also genuinely believe there's at least one mistake in there somewhere. That's the preface paradox, and it turns out its basically the same structure as the lottery paradox (you believe each ticket will lose, but you also believe one will win), and once I noticed that I fell into a rabbit hole reading the epistemology literature.

Here's what's driving me nuts. The paradoxes have been around since the 60s — Kyburg for the lottery in 1961, Makinson for the preface in 1965 — and the literature is enormous. But as far as I can tell, the same positions have been staked out for decades and nobody budges. You've got the people who say the lesson is that rational belief isn't closed under conjunction. You've got the ones who say no, the lesson is that high probability isn't sufficient for rational belief (so the Lockean thesis is false). You've got the people who say the preface and lottery cases are actually different in kind and shouldn't be treated together. You've got the contextualists. Every paper I read starts by summarizing these positions, argues for one of them, and then the next paper does the same thing for a different one. Nobody moves.

I want a diagnosis, not another run through the options. What kind of disagreement is this, really? Is it a substantive disagreement about the nature of belief, or is it a verbal dispute about what "rational belief" means, or are the different camps actually asking different questions — some about what we should accept for practical reasoning, some about the logic of graded credence, some about the norms of assertion — and talking past each other? And if it is substantive, what would actually count as progress? A formal result, an experiment (can you even run experiments on this?), a conceptual distinction everyone's missing? Or is the impasse a sign that "rational belief" is a broken concept and we should just work with credences?

I'm not looking for a survey of the positions — I've read enough of those. I want the diagnosis of the debate structure itself. And while you're at it, tell me what's actually settled in this literature and what's genuinely contested, because I get the sense that some of the "disagreements" are just people talking past each other and some are real.
