I'm a novelist, and the book I'm drafting centres on a neuroscientist who ends up convinced that phenomenal consciousness — the felt what-it's-likeness of things — is epiphenomenal: caused by the brain but doing no causal work, contributing nothing to behaviour, speech, or judgement. The snag I keep hitting (and that she hits) is that the conviction itself is a phenomenal judgement: a judgement about how things feel, which on her view is produced by the physical story alone and not by the feelings themselves. If the feelings play no role in her coming to judge that feelings play no role, why should she — or my reader — trust the judgement? It seems to saw off the branch it's sitting on, and I don't want the novel to fudge that.

So before I write the resolution, I want the full spec: the desiderata for any adequate response to the paradox of phenomenal judgement. What must a solution deliver? For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, not just a slogan. Mark which are non-negotiable for any serious answer and which are really preferences of particular camps (physicalists, dualists, illusionists, whoever). And flag any pairs you suspect can't be jointly satisfied — thats the part I most want, because if the list is internally inconsistent, that itself is a plot point. Keep citations out of it; I'll chase the literature down myself.

Please write the answer in British English. And at the end, tell me how confident you are in the overall list and which single requirement you're least sure belongs on it.
