I'm a novelist, about four books into a series where my characters keep ending up in situations where they'd all be better off if they cooperated but each one has a reason to defect — prisoner's dilemma type stuff, but also more complicated multi-player coordination problems. I want my characters to be smart in a consistent way, not just doing whatever the plot needs. I've been reading about superrationality (Hofstadter's idea, where you assume the other players are reasoning the same way you are and you all arrive at the same answer), and I want to use that as the baseline for how my viewpoint characters think in these situations.

So what I need is a proceedure I can run each time I'm plotting a scene with a coordination dilemma. It should be something I can actually use — I'm not a philosopher, I'm a novelist with a deadline, and I need to get through it in maybe twenty minutes. The procedure should tell me: given the setup (who knows what, what the payoffs are, whether the characters can communicate), what would a superrational agent do? And if the answer is indeterminate or depends on something the theory doesn't settle, I need to know that too, so I can make a conscious choice as the author rather than accidentally having my characters be inconsistent.

The procedure should handle at least: one-shot PD, iterated PD where the number of rounds is known, and a case where there are more than two players and the symmetry is broken somehow (different stakes for different players).

For the response, give me:

1. **The procedure** — a step-by-step checklist or flowchart in words, about 400 words. Each step should be a question I ask about the scene, with what to do depending on the answer. Flag the steps where I'm going to need to make an authorial judgment call rather than the theory giving me a clean answer.

2. **Worked example 1** — run the procedure on a two-character one-shot prisoner's dilemma where the characters are strangers who will never meet again but both are smart and know the other is smart. About 300 words.

3. **Worked example 2** — run it on a three-character situation where two of them have a history together and the third doesn't, and the payoffs are asymmetric. About 300 words.

4. **What the procedure can't do** — cases where superrationality goes silent or gives answers that would make for a terrible novel, and what I should do as a writer in those cases. About 200 words.
