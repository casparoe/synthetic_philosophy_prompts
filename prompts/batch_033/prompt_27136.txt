I've been trying to get my anthropics intuitions straight and I keep going in circles. Here are my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases. I want to know whether there's one principle that captures all of them (or close), and if not, which of my verdicts has to go. Please take a definite position—dont just lay out SSA and SIA and tell me it depends. I want the principle you'd actually commit to, stated precisely, which of my verdicts it forces me to drop, and what it predicts about the extra case at the end.

Case 1, the coin toss. God flips a fair coin. Heads: he creates one person. Tails: he creates a million people. I wake up, know the setup, and have no other information. My considered view is 50/50 heads. I won't pretend there's no pull toward tails—there'd be a million of me—but the coin was fair and I'd exist either way, so I don't see how my existence is evidence. 50/50.

Case 2, the presumptuous philosopher. Two cosmological theories fit all the data equally well. Theory A says the universe will contain 200 billion observers; theory B says 200 trillion. Someone tells me I should be much more confident in B, just because B predicts vastly more observers and I'm one of them. I think that's nuts. Fitting the data equally should leave me where I was; the observer count shouldn't move me by itself.

Case 3, the incubator, room 1. God flips a fair coin. Heads: one person, in room 1. Tails: two people, one in room 1 and one in room 2. I wake up and see I'm in room 1. I think heads is 2/3 here. Under tails, there was a 50/50 chance I'd have been the room-2 person, and I'm not; heads guaranteed I'd be in room 1.

Case 4, the incubator, blue room. God flips a fair coin. Heads: one person in a blue room. Tails: one person in a blue room and one in a red room. I wake up and see I'm in the blue room. I think heads is 1/2 here, not 2/3. A blue-room person exists on both outcomes, so being in a blue room doesn't discriminate.

I realize cases 3 and 4 look structurally identical when I write them down, and yet I want to answer them differently. That's the part that's bugging me.

Case 5, doomsday. I also find the doomsday argument fairly gripping. If I learned I'm among the first 5% of all humans who will ever live, I'd raise my credence that the species ends relatively soon rather than going on for eons. Not a knockdown argument, but it feels like real force.

So: is there one principle that gets all five, or close? If something has to give, tell me which verdict and why. And what does the principle you'd commit to say about this further case: God flips a fair coin; heads means one person in a blue room, tails means one person in a blue room and two in red rooms; I wake up in the blue room. What should I think about heads?
