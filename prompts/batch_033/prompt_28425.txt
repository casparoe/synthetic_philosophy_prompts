I've been reading about weakness of will — akrasia — and the way philosophers and behavioral economists have tried to model it. The picture from hyperbolic discounting is especially vivid: your discount function is steep in the short run and flatter in the long run, so from the perspective of Monday, the choice between exercising on Wednesday and skipping it looks clear (exercise, obviously), but when Wednesday actually arrives, the proximity of the cost looms large and you find yourself on the couch. What's philosophically interesting is that this isn't just a failure of execution — it seems to involve a kind of internal division, a self that makes plans and a self that un-makes them, each with its own temporally indexed reasons. Some people talk about this as a problem of self-control; others think it raises deep questions about whether the planning self and the choosing self are even the same agent in any robust sense.

I'd like you to write a sonnet about this — about the relationship between weakness of will and hyperbolic discounting. The poem's structure should do philosophical work. Use the octave to give voice to the far-sighted, planning self: the one who can see clearly, from a distance, what the right course of action is and who makes resolutions with genuine conviction. Then use the volta — the turn at line nine — to enact the moment hyperbolic discounting kicks in, when the delayed cost suddenly becomes present and the resolution collapses. The sestet should capture the phenomenology of giving in: not ignorance (you know exactly what you're doing) but a kind of temporal myopia that feels, from the inside, like rationality itself. The closing couplet should say something about what this cycle implies for personal identity — whether the self who resolves and the self who caves are genuinely the same person, or whether the shape of the discount curve carves the agent into a succession of selves, each sovereign in its own moment. Someone familiar with the philosophical literature on akrasia and hyperbolic discounting should recognize the phenomenon, and someone unfamiliar should still find it a good poem.
