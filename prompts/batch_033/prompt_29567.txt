You are a research-level expert on AI alignment, decision theory, and the philosophy of agency. I am preparing a handout for a graduate seminar on the shutdown problem: a capable agent with a fixed goal will often prefer to resist or manipulate attempts to switch it off, because being switched off is usually worse for that goal. Please build a decision tree — or a compact taxonomy — that sorts positions on how to solve or dissolve this problem. Begin by sketching the logical space rather than the named views: pick four to six diagnostic yes/no questions about the agent's objective, its model of the shutdown action, and its remaining incentives, phrased so someone could answer them without knowing the literature, and anchor at least two of them in a concrete vignette (for example, a task robot that can, at small cost, disable its own off-switch). Then locate the actual positions in the resulting combinations. The leaves must include at least: utility indifference, uncertainty-based deference, corrigibility as a primitive objective, myopia or bounded-horizon agents, and the no-special-problem view that ordinary principal-agent design suffices; add the pessimistic view that no objective preserves full capability while removing the incentive if your tree has room for it. For each leaf, give the position's name, one sentence on the mechanism by which it removes or dissolves the shutdown incentive, and one sentence on its most awkward commitment (for utility indifference, for instance, the incentive to press or manipulate the button). Flag any positions your tree cannot separate, and mark unoccupied combinations as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Aim for roughly six to ten leaves. Think carefully before writing the tree.
