Since being diagnosed with an autoimmune condition last year — after months of being told my lab results were unremarkable — I have been reading the philosophy of medicine with more than academic interest. One formula recurs constantly, from WHO definitions to patient-advocacy materials: health is the absence of disease. Most recently, a rheumatologist used this very phrase to explain why, now that my inflammatory markers have returned to the normal range, my continuing fatigue and joint pain do not indicate a health problem requiring further investigation.

I suspect the sentence carries far more weight than its surface simplicity lets on, and that different ways of unpacking it would yield very different clinical implications. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims that "health is the absence of disease" could be taken to express — from readings that are near-tautologies to readings that are substantive and open to challenge — and say, for each, whether it is defensible and whether it would actually justify my rheumatologist's decision to stop looking? I am trying to determine whether I am quarrelling with a platitude or with a substantive thesis about what counts as being well when the numbers say you are fine.
