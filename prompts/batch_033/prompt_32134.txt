A friend and I have spent the past several months arguing about whether equality itself matters, or only how badly off people are. I hold the prioritarian view: a benefit matters more the worse off the person who receives it, but equality as such has no value, and this is exactly why the leveling-down objection is decisive — making someone worse off and no one better off is in no respect an improvement, even when it narrows a gap. My friend holds the telic egalitarian view: equality is valuable in itself, so leveling down can be good in one respect while bad overall, and prioritarianism's concave weighting of well-being merely redescribes the concern for the worse off without explaining its comparative character.

We want to stop talking past each other. Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between these two positions — a document we could both sign. Include: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides can accept, including about the leveling-down objection itself, about the priority owed to the worse off, and about what any adequate view in this area must explain; (2) a disagreements table stating the residual disagreements precisely, each side's view worded as its own proponent would word it; (3) the cruxes — I suspect these include whether the leveling-down objection is decisive or question-begging, and whether the value of a distribution is fully determined by weighted individual well-being levels or also by relations between levels, but identify whatever sub-questions you think would actually move one of us; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If you think part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why.
