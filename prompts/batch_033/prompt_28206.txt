I'm part of a philosophy of physics reading group that has spent the last semester working through the substantivalism–relationism debate about the nature of spacetime. We have two members who have become the de facto standard-bearers for each side: one defends a sophisticated substantivalism (spacetime is a real entity, but not every feature of the mathematical manifold corresponds to something physically real — the metric field is what carries the ontology, and diffeomorphism invariance handles the hole argument), while the other defends a modern relationism inspired by Mach and developed through the shape-dynamics and best-matching tradition (only relational quantities are real; the appearance of absolute space is an artifact of treating a particular configuration as a privileged reference frame). After months of arguing, they have agreed to produce an adversarial-collaboration document that captures what they have actually settled and what remains open.

Please draft that document. It should include: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign — these should be substantive, not just truisms, and should reflect genuine convergence after months of discussion; (2) a disagreements table where each row states the precise point of disagreement, with the substantivalist's position and the relationist's position each phrased in wording its own side would accept without flinching; (3) a section identifying the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, the kind of argument, thought experiment, or (if applicable) empirical evidence both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction or assessment recorded in advance. The Newton bucket thought experiment should feature somewhere in the document, but it should go well beyond it — inertia, the hole argument, the status of gauge freedom, the interpretation of general relativity, and the question of what counts as a genuine observable should all be addressed. The goal is a document that neither party feels misrepresents them, and that makes the residual disagreement precise enough that someone outside the debate could see what would actually need to change for one side to convince the other.
