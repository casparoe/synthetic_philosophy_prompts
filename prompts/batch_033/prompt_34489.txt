I have been reading the secondary literature on Wittgenstein's On Certainty, the set of remarks written near the end of his life in response to G. E. Moore's attempt to refute skepticism by holding up his hands and claiming to know them, and I am struck by how entrenched its central interpretive dispute has become. Wittgenstein appears to say that certain basic commitments — that one has hands, that the earth existed long before one was born, that one has never been far from the earth's surface, that one's name is what it is — stand fast for us in a way that ordinary empirical claims do not; yet he also appears to say that these commitments are not items of knowledge, because where doubt cannot get a grip, talk of knowing and not-knowing loses its point. In his metaphor, they are the hinges on which our enquiries turn. On one side of the resulting debate are commentators who take the hinges to be non-epistemic: not propositions, not beliefs, not truth-evaluable, but something more like animal certainties or ways of acting that make our practices of giving and asking for reasons possible. On the other side are commentators who take the hinges to be propositional commitments that are epistemically evaluable after all: we hold them, and we are rationally entitled to accept them without evidence, since any attempt to justify them would already presuppose the framework they constitute. Each side can marshal the same passages, and each side's account of why the other side misreads the text is, to its own satisfaction, complete.

I want a diagnosis of why this debate has stalled, not a verdict about which reading is right. Is the disagreement really about what Wittgenstein meant, or is it a dispute about the nature of belief, knowledge, or rational entitlement that gets projected onto the text? Are the two camps answering different questions — one about the psychological or practical underpinnings of enquiry, the other about its normative structure — under a single heading? Or is there a hidden shared assumption, perhaps a dichotomy between the epistemic and the non-epistemic that both sides presuppose, whose rejection would dissolve the impasse? And given your diagnosis, tell me concretely what would count as progress: a distinction, a reformulation of the question, or the recognition that the debate as posed cannot be settled — and why.
