I have recently joined an astrobiology working group charged with writing the target-definition section for a proposed life-detection instrument, and I have been struck by how faithfully the definition-of-life debate's decades-old standoff reproduces itself in our weekly meetings, with no one moving. One camp holds that life is fundamentally a metabolic phenomenon: a bounded, far-from-equilibrium system that actively maintains itself against environmental perturbation. For this camp, the relevant test is self-maintaining organization, and a metabolizing but non-replicating vesicle is a serious candidate for life. The other camp holds that life is fundamentally an evolutionary phenomenon: a population of entities that reproduce with heritable variation and thereby expose themselves to natural selection. For this camp, metabolism without replication is not life, while replication with variation is close to sufficient even for entities that are metabolically inert for most of their cycle. A third, deflationary camp insists that 'life' is not a natural kind but a cluster concept or a pragmatic category, and that the search for the definition is itself the mistake. The same borderline cases are recycled — viruses and viroids, endospores, obligate intracellular parasites, plasmids and transposable elements, minimal synthetic cells — and each side draws the line in a different place without, as far as I can tell, any new argument appearing.

My working hypothesis is that the two main camps are answering different questions — one about the organization of individual systems, the other about the history of populations — and that the word 'alive' is being asked to do two jobs at once; but I am unsure whether that diagnosis is too deflationary. I do not want a survey of definitions and I do not want a verdict about which criterion is correct. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself: Is it substantive or verbal? Are the sides disagreeing about facts, about which cases count as data, about standards of explanation, or about bedrock intuitions? Is there a hidden factual or formal question doing the work? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a decisive experiment, a change of question — or should we expect none?
