I work on the ethics review board at a mid-size AI lab. We're setting up a process to review new models and systems before deployment, and one of the things we're supposed to flag is whether a system's internal computations are the kind that could ground moral patienthood. The problem is that nobody on the board agrees on what that even means. Some people think only biological neurons count. Some think it's about information integration. Some think we should look for specific computational features like a global workspace or metacognition. And there's an extra headache: even if we agree on what computation matters, we have to figure out whether the computation is actually happening "in" the system in the right way. Like, if the relevant computation is distributed across eight different servers that don't share a memory bus, or if it's implemented in a weird way by a transformer doing in-context learning rather than having persistent recurrent states, does it still count? If the thing we care about is partly implemented by the human prompting the model, where do we draw the boundary?

I need a decision procedure my team can actually run on a system we're reviewing. It should be a set of questions to ask in order, with conditions for what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a "no," and where it hands off to a judgment call because the question is philosophical rather than empirical. The procedure has to be usable by people who aren't philosophers — my board is engineers, a lawyer, and one person from policy. We have maybe a week per system, and we're not going to have access to every detail of the architecture. It has to be honest about where the procedure is just pushing the hard philosophical question into a checkbox.

Then run it on two hypotheticals: (1) a large language model fine-tuned with RL that shows consistent self-reports of suffering when given certain prompts, and (2) a simulated insect brain running in a virtual environment, where the simulation is at the level of individual neurons and the behavior is indistinguishible from a real insect's.
