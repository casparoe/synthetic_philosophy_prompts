I have come to hold a lexical-threshold form of negative utilitarianism: there is some threshold of intensity such that suffering at or above that threshold cannot be outweighed or compensated by any amount of well-being, or by any amount of suffering below the threshold, no matter how large. I want a map of what would count against this view. Which arguments, formal results, possible cases, or implications should lead me to abandon it outright, and which should merely force me to qualify it? I am especially interested in whether the threshold can be located non-arbitrarily, whether the discontinuity between suffering just below and just above the threshold can be defended, and whether the view has unacceptable consequences in population ethics or in trade-offs between a single intense suffering and a very large aggregate of mild suffering. Please distinguish decisive defeaters from merely awkward ones, say which of these a committed defender of the view should themselves concede as counting against it, and assess whether the position can be stated so as to avoid the problems or whether it has been insulated against them in a way that is itself a defect. Please write the answer in British English.
