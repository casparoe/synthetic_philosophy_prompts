For a seminar paper on Moore's paradox, I want the specification before I commit to any theory. Write the desiderata that an adequate account of Moorean absurdity must satisfy. Cover at minimum: the commissive form ("p, but I don't believe that p"), the omissive form ("p, but I believe that not-p"), the first-person present-tense restriction, the asymmetry with the converse ("I believe that p, but not-p"), and the question whether the absurdity attaches to belief, to assertion, or to both. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and flag whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. Then, in a paragraph, identify the single desideratum that does the most to exclude the leading accounts — the Hintikka-style doxastic-logic approach, the Gricean implicature story, and the knowledge-norm-of-assertion view — and explain why.
