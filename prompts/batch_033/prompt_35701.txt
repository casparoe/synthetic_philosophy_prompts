Goodhart's law gets thrown around a lot where I work — I do program evaluation for a nonprofit. The version I keep hearing is that once a measure becomes a target it stops being a good measure. But I genuinely don't understand the mechanism. If test scores and actual learning are correlated in the student population, that correlation is just a fact about the data, right? It doesn't know whether anyone is being paid. So how does my deciding to tie teacher pay to test scores change a statistical fact about the data? Is Goodhart's law a causal claim about how people respond to incentives, or is it just a fancy way of saying people cheat? And if its just people cheating, why do people talk about it like its a law rather than a mundane monitoring problem?
