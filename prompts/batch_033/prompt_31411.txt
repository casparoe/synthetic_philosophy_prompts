I've been going back and forth on whether to go vegetarian, and the thing that keeps tripping me up isn't about nutrition or taste or convenience. It's about whether my choice actually does anything. If I stop eating meat, the same number of animals will still be raised and killed — my individual demand is just too small for any farm or slaughterhouse to notice, let alone to adjust their production downward. The same worry comes up when I think about flying less because of climate change, or whether to bother voting, or whether to recycle. In each case, the harm — or the benefit — only happens when huge numbers of people act together, and my own contribution seems to round to zero. But if I take that seriously, it seems to let everyone off the hook. If everyone thinks their action doesn't matter, then nothing changes and the harm just continues. And yet I can't pretend my one burger makes a difference just to feel better about myself. I'm stuck. Is there a way to think about this that doesn't collapse into either pretending my individual action matters or admitting it doesn't?
