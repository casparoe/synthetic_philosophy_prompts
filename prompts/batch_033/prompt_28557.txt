Write a dialogue between two equally competent moral philosophers who disagree about whether there is a morally significant difference between lying and misleading. One holds that lying is in principle more seriously wrong: the liar asserts what is false and thereby vouches for the truth of the assertion, whereas the misleader merely allows the hearer to draw a false conclusion, so the hearer bears part of the responsibility for the resulting belief. The other holds that this distinction cannot bear the moral weight placed on it: the misleader often engineers the false inference as deliberately as any liar, exploits the hearer's reasonable reliance on conversational norms at least as cynically, and can target the hearer's particular epistemic vulnerabilities with just as much precision. The dialogue should contain genuine back-and-forth, with each character responding to the other's actual arguments. Have them examine whether the distinction can be drawn at the level of what is said versus what is conversationally implicated, and whether a difference in the mode of communication can justify a difference in moral assessment. Each character should grant the other's strongest points where warranted and be forced to concede at least one significant point while preserving their core position. End without a tidy resolution, but with each view sharpened by the exchange. Do not include citations, named authors, or references to specific texts.
