I'm drafting a position statement for a workshop on hinge epistemology in the tradition of Wittgenstein's On Certainty, and I'd like a consistency audit of the commitments I find myself endorsing before I circulate it. Here is the set:

(1) I accept a closure principle for knowledge — if I know that p and know that p entails q, then I am in a position to know that q — and I take myself to know many ordinary empirical propositions, such as that I have hands and that I am sitting at a desk.
(2) I think the hinge response to radical skepticism is broadly right: there are commitments — that there is an external world, that there are other minds, that the earth has existed for a long time, that my name is what I take it to be — that are not held on evidence and cannot be given non-circular support, yet whose doubt is not a live option within ordinary inquiry.
(3) I think hinge commitments are propositional and truth-evaluable, but they are not beliefs and are not the kind of thing that is either justified or unjustified, known or unknown; they are the scaffolding that makes belief, doubt, and justification possible.
(4) I also think we are rationally entitled to trust our hinges: there is a legitimate non-evidential warrant, of the kind often called entitlement, because the cognitive projects those hinges make possible could not get started without them.
(5) About beliefs in general, I am an evidentialist: a belief is justified only if it is supported by adequate evidence.
(6) I think radical skeptical scenarios, such as the brain-in-a-vat, are fully intelligible: they describe coherent possibilities that our evidence cannot rule out, which is exactly why they exert philosophical pressure.

I want an audit rather than a lecture. Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each genuine tension is; rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious. For each genuine tension, state the least costly revision and what it would cost; for each apparent tension, say briefly why it dissolves. I can't tell where the set goes wrong, and I don't want to bias the audit toward any one commitment. Please also separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people working on hinge epistemology, and do not present a contested reading — for example, about what hinges are or about whether closure must be given up — as if it were the consensus.

Format: exactly five numbered findings, ordered from most to least severe, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Each finding should name the pair of commitments at issue.
