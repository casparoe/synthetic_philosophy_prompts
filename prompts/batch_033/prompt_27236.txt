Last night I had a long argument with a friend about whether ordinary objects persist by enduring — being wholly present at each moment at which they exist — or by perduring, that is, by being extended through time and having distinct temporal parts at different times. We kept trading arguments, but we came away unsure which of them do any independent work once the question is stated precisely. I would like you to tier the main arguments for perdurantism over endurantism by how much genuine force they carry, using something like four tiers, from close to decisive down to mostly rhetorical or question-begging. Consider at least the problem of temporary intrinsics, the argument from change, coincidence puzzles such as the statue and the lump, and the argument from special relativity; add any other argument you take to be in the same league. For each argument, say in a sentence or two why it lands where it does. I am especially interested in three things: which arguments collapse into restatements of the problem of temporary intrinsics; which arguments only work against an uncharitable version of endurantism and fail once endurantists are allowed to index properties to times or adopt adverbialism; and which arguments, if any, would still favor perdurantism even if the endurantist handles change and coincidence. Where it matters, note whether an argument supports perdurantism specifically or only some broader four-dimensionalism, such as stage theory.
