I'm trying to work out what I actually think about discounting the future, and my gut reactions are pulling in different directions. Here are the cases, with my verdicts.

- My own future wellbeing: a hundred quid of benefit to me tomorrow feels worth slightly more than the same benefit to me in a year, but only slightly — the later one is maybe 98% as important. So I do have a bit of pure time preference, but not much.
- A stranger's future wellbeing: same choice, but the benefit goes to someone I've never met. I'm indifferent between tomorrow and a year from now. Pure time preference seems to vanish when it's not me.
- Lives: I'd save a life today rather than a life in a hundred years if I had to choose, but not because the later life matters less. It's because I'm less sure my intervention will actually reach them through all that future noise.
- Money: a pound today to someone poor is worth more than a pound in a hundred years to someone who will be much richer. That feels right even though it's still a real person in the future.
- And the awkward one: I'd take £100 today over £110 in a week, but I'd take £110 in a year and a week over £100 in a year. I dont think those can both be rational, but both are how I'd actually choose.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? And what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on — whether its okay to discount the future wellbeing of non-human animals just because it's later?

Please give me exactly five candidate principles, each in a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. For each candidate, say which of my verdicts it keeps, which it overturns, and what it predicts for the animals case.
