I cover science for a living, and I've been dipping into philosophy of science on and off for years, and there's one thing that genuinely baffles me: the demarcation problem. You know — what makes science science, as opposed to pseudoscience or just non-science. Popper kicks it off with falsificationism in the 30s, and within a couple decades everyone's pointing out that astrology makes falsifiable claims (and astrologers just ignore the failures) while plenty of respectable science doesn't look falsifiable at all. Then Kuhn says paradigms and normal science, then Lakatos tries to patch things with research programmes, then Feyerabend says there's no method and we should stop pretending, then Laudan argues the whole question is a waste of time because "science" isn't a natural kind worth defining. And yet here we are, decades later, and people are still writing papers about it, still using "pseudoscience" as a term of abuse in public debates about creationism or homeopathy or whatever, and the philosophers don't seem any closer to agreeing than they were in 1970.

So here's what I want to know: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? My hunch — and I'm a journalist, not a philosopher, so take it as a hypothesis — is that the debate isn't really a conceptual one. It's a status fight. "Science" is the most prestigious label in modern culture, so of course everyone wants their thing to count as science and their opponents' thing not to. The conceptual apparatus is just window dressing for a sociological turf war. But I could be wrong — maybe there's a genuine conceptual puzzle here that's just really hard, or maybe the two sides are using "science" to mean different things and nobody's noticed.

I want a diagnosis, not a survey of positions. Tell me what's actually keeping this thing stuck: is it a verbal dispute, a clash of intuitions that can't be adjudicated, a hidden empirical question, or something else? And then tell me what, if anything, would count as real progress — a distinction that could break the logjam, a different question we should be asking instead, or an argument for why we should just drop it. Also: tell me what would change your mind about your own diagnosis. Where's your reasoning most likely to fail, and what argument or evidence would push you to a different read of the situation?
