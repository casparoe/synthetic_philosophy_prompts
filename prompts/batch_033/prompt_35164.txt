Malcolm and Dennett both set themselves against what is usually called the received view of dreaming: the view that a dream is a determinate stream of conscious experience unfolding in real time during sleep and later retrieved, perhaps imperfectly, from memory. Yet they draw almost opposite conclusions from this shared target. Compare Malcolm's claim that dreams are not experiences at all with Dennett's cassette theory, on which dreams are experiences whose content and temporal profile are composed at, or around, the moment of awakening.

Be specific about the following. First, the role that verificationist or epistemic scruples play in each position: which questions does each philosopher treat as answerable, unanswerable, or simply ill-posed, and do they draw the line in the same place? Second, what each philosopher means by "experience" — in particular, whether Malcolm's denial and Dennett's affirmation are even about the same thing, or whether the disagreement is partly verbal. Third, the status of the dream report: for Malcolm the waking telling is central, while for Dennett the report reflects a real but mislocated episode of presentation; explain how each handles the apparent fact that dreamers can, on waking, recount long and coherent narratives. Fourth, the temporal question — whether it makes sense to ask how long a dream lasted or at what point in the night it occurred — and how each position answers it.

Finally, take a stand on this: does Dennett's cassette theory really preserve a robust sense in which dreams are experiences, or is it a stable intermediate position only because it retains the word "experience" while abandoning most of what the received view meant by it? Is there any consideration that would push Dennett all the way to Malcolm, or Malcolm all the way to Dennett? Where exactly is the load-bearing disagreement?
