I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I serve on the program committee for this year's national science writers' conference. We have 14 panel slots and received 71 proposals — nearly all of them serious and well-constructed. The committee is deadlocked on how to choose, and I've been asked to draft the decision memo.

One faction wants us to build a multi-attribute scoring rubric — originality, diversity of voices, likely audience draw, speaker track record, topical balance across disciplines — score every proposal, and then solve for the optimal slate. They argue that anything less abdicates our responsibility to the attendees and to the field: we owe them the best program we can produce, and "best" means comparing all candidates.

The other faction says this is a fantasy. We are twelve volunteers with day jobs, the conference is in three months, and the proposals are not commensurable in the way the rubric pretends. They want us to set minimum thresholds for each criterion, read proposals in randomized batches, and accept the first ones that clear all thresholds until the slots are filled — with a small reserve at the end for obvious gaps we notice (e.g., no climate panel, no early-career voices). They acknowledge this means some excellent proposals will be left out that would have survived an exhaustive ranking. They say the alternative is paralysis or, worse, a rushed pseudo-rigorous process that claims to optimize but in practice just ratifies whoever argues loudest in the room.

I need to write the memo. It should recommend one approach (or a specific hybrid), give the reasons, and address the strongest objection to the recommendation honestly. The memo should be something the committee can actually vote on — concrete enough to implement.

After the memo, append a structured summary as a JSON object with these fields:

- "recommendation": a one-sentence statement of the recommended selection method.
- "decisive_consideration": the single consideration that most favors the recommendation (one to two sentences).
- "strongest_objection": the strongest argument against the recommendation, stated fairly (one to two sentences).
- "why_objection_fails": why, despite that objection, the recommendation stands (one to two sentences).
- "implementation_steps": an array of strings, each a concrete next step for the committee, in chronological order.
- "philosophical_commitment": a one-sentence identification of the underlying philosophical issue the dispute turns on (name the concept).

Please write in American English.
