Fitch's paradox of knowability purports to show that the principle that if a proposition is true, then it is possible to know it entails, under apparently minimal assumptions about knowledge, that every true proposition is in fact known. Please describe the most important implications of this result for semantic anti-realism and for epistemic or verificationist accounts of truth. In doing so, address which commitments the derivation actually pressures—for example, the knowability principle itself, the closure of knowability under conjunction, or the logic in which the derivation is carried out—and indicate which standard responses are thought to preserve an anti-realist outlook and at what cost. Throughout, distinguish carefully between what is settled, such as the formal validity of the derivation under the stated assumptions, and what is genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present any contested interpretation of the paradox's philosophical upshot as the consensus view.
