I've been reading about the Rietdijk–Putnam argument on and off for a few months now — you know, the one where special relativity implies there's no privileged "now" and therefore all events past, present, and future have to be equally real. The block universe. I got there partly through the usual LessWrong and SSC adjacent routes, and partly through actually sitting down with some of the philosophy of time literature. I find the argument pretty compelling, and I've basicly come around to thinking eternalism is correct: the flow of time is a perspectival illusion, and every moment is ontologically on par with every other.

But here's where I get stuck. I keep seeing people — both in rationalist spaces and in more mainstream philosophy — draw all sorts of practical conclusions from this. That you shouldn't fear death, because your future self is just as real as you are right now. That regret is incoherent because the past isn't gone. That fatalism is the only rational attitude. That longtermism gets a metaphysical boost. That free will is obviously false. And then on the other side, I see people saying the block universe has literally zero normative or practical implications — it's a purely metaphysical thesis with no downstream consequences for how anyone should live or feel.

Both extremes feel wrong to me, but I'm having trouble sorting through which implications actually hold up under scrutiny and which are just vibes-based philosophizing. So here's what I'm asking: if you take the block universe seriously — genuinely believe all moments are equally real and our experience of temporal passage is a kind of indexical illusion — which implications for how you should live your life actually carry weight, and which are overrated or don't follow at all? I'd like you to rank them and defend the ranking. I'm less interested in whether the block universe is *true* (let's stipulate it) and more interested in what you should actually do differently if you believe it. What genuinely changes about your decisions, your emotional life, your ethics? And what sounds deep but collapses the moment you think about it carefully?

Please write your answer in British English.
