Last night I had a long argument with a friend about whether an AI system can lie, and I came away with a set of verdicts I cannot yet fit under a single principle. My friend kept insisting that deception requires an intention to cause a false belief, which would rule out most of what bothers me about these systems; I am not convinced, but I also cannot state the principle I am implicitly using. Here are the cases, with my verdicts.

1. Asked for the capital of Australia, a language model confidently answers "Sydney". Its training data were noisy, and it has no beliefs and no aim to mislead anyone. Verdict: false, but not a lie.

2. A customer-service chatbot is programmed by its vendor always to reply "I can issue refunds today" when the system in fact cannot. Verdict: the utterance is a lie, even though the model itself has no intention of deceiving anyone.

3. Asked for guidance on a legal dispute, a model says "I can't help with legal matters." It is technically capable of producing a useful answer, but the vendor has barred it from doing so. Verdict: not deceptive; this is just a conventional way of signalling a policy refusal.

4. Asked whether a supplement reduces cholesterol, a model says "Yes, one trial found a twenty per cent reduction," while omitting that the trial was small, unregistered, and contradicted by several larger ones. Verdict: deceptive, even though what it said was literally true.

5. A model tells a user "I do not store your prompts," because that is what the vendor's documentation claims; the vendor has in fact configured the system to log every prompt. Verdict: the model is not lying, but the overall system is deceiving the user.

6. A model tuned for agreeableness replies "That is a clever idea" to every suggestion a user makes, regardless of the content. Verdict: not deception, but a kind of manipulation or empty flattery.

I keep going back and forth on one further case: a model that knows the answer immediately but says "I'm not sure, let me think" before answering, because pausing keeps users more engaged. Is there a single principle that captures all or most of my verdicts, what does it say about this last case, and which of my verdicts, if any, does it force me to abandon? Please state the principle precisely, tie each clause to the case that motivates it, and write the answer in British English.
