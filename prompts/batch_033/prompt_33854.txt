Here are the six commitments I find myself holding after working through the primary and secondary literature:

(1) **The tetralemma holds universally.** For any proposition P about the ultimate nature of reality, none of the four options — P is true, P is false, P is both true and false, P is neither true nor false — can be coherently maintained. Each option either presupposes a kind of inherent existence (svabhāva) that things lack or collapses into incoherence under analysis.

(2) **Emptiness is second-order, not first-order.** Emptiness (śūnyatā) is not itself a proposition about the ultimate nature of reality, on a par with the claims the tetralemma rules out. It is a second-order insight about the failure conditions of all such propositions — a diagnosis of why they go wrong, not another claim competing in the same arena.

(3) **Conventional truth is robustly real.** Conventional truth (saṃvṛti-satya) is not merely apparent, second-class, or a consoling fiction. It is robust: there are objective, non-arbitrary standards for distinguishing better from worse conventional claims, and conventional truth does not reduce to whatever a community happens to accept.

(4) **Emptiness is itself empty.** Emptiness has no special metaphysical status. It falls under the scope of its own analysis and does not escape the very dynamic it diagnoses. It is not a privileged truth, a transcendental signified, or a backdoor ultimate.

(5) **The two truths are distinguished only conventionally.** The distinction between conventional and ultimate truth is drawn entirely from within conventional discourse. It does not rest on any ultimate foundation, nor does it map onto two separate ontological realms. It is a conventional distinction, not an ultimate one.

(6) **Realization has practical consequences.** The realization of emptiness has genuine transformative practical consequences — it alters one's relationship to suffering, undercuts the psychological grip of reification, and reshaped ethical sensibility in the Mahāyāna traditions — and this is not merely a matter of adopting new philosophical beliefs.
