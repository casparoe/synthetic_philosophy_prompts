I'm a few months into my dissertation on hedonism and well-being, and I keep running into the same wall: the heterogeneity problem. You know the one — if hedonism says pleasure is the only intrinsic good, then it better tell us what pleasure *is*. But pleasures are all over the place. The pleasure of a cold drink on a hot day, the pleasure of finally understanding a proof, the pleasure of someone you love saying they're proud of you, the pleasure of scratching an itch. It's not obvious these have any one thing in common. Some philosophers (like Feldman) go attitudinal — pleasure is a propositional attitude toward states of affairs. Others (like Crisp) go for a distinctive felt quality, a hedonic tone that all pleasures share. Then there are the neuroscientists who want to point at dopamine or mu-opioid activity, and the folks who say we should just give up on a unified account and be pluralists. I've been going in circles reading this stuff and I think I need to step back and get the lay of the land properly.

Before I commit to any view, I want to get clear on what the theory actually has to do. So: write me the desiderata. What must an adequate theory of pleasure — one that solves or dissolves the heterogeneity problem — deliver? For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that motivates it. Which requirements are non-negotiable, and which are just commitments of particular camps that a theory could reasonably reject? And here's what I'm really after: are there pairs of desiderata that look like they can't be jointly satisfied? If the list itself contains a tension, I want to know. You can skip the scorecard of existing views — I know the lay of the land — but if you think one particular requirement does the most work in ruling out candidates, flag it. Also, I'm especially interested in whether the heterogeneity problem itself is best understood as a demand for a single common property, or whether that's already begging the question against the pluralist — so if that framing shows up in how you structure the desiderata, say so.
