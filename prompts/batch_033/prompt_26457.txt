Write an essay on the absent-minded driver problem. Set out the decision problem — the road network, the payoffs, the driver's imperfect recall — and explain why the ex ante optimal strategy (choose CONTINUE with probability p at whatever intersection you find yourself at) comes apart from the reasoning the driver would engage in upon actually reaching an intersection. Assess whether this divergence reveals a genuine paradox for decision theory, or whether the ex ante perspective is the correct one and the 'at the intersection' reasoning is confused. The essay should engage with the idea that the driver's inability to distinguish X from Y creates a kind of self-locating uncertainty that standard decision theory may not handle well, and it should reach and justify a clear conclusion.
