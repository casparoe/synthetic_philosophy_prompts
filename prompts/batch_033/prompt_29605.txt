I'm a software engineer at a mid-size company, no formal philosophy background, and my stats is one undergrad class I've mostly forgotten. My job lately is to sit in the weekly metrics review and either bless or block shipping a feature based on the numbers. The thing that keeps biting me is this: sometimes the overall number says a change is good but every breakdown says it's bad, and sometimes it's the reverse. Our data scientist calls this Simpson's paradox, and then the room splits into 'trust the aggregate' and 'trust the segments' and we end up deciding by whoever talks loudest. I need a decision procedure I can actually run in the 20 minutes before one of these meetings — a checklist with the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure says stop and don't ship, and where it says hand this off to the data scientist or to a human because it's a judgment call rather than something a formula can settle. Base it on what the philosophy/statistics people actually know about Simpson's paradox, not just 'always break things down.' I especially want it to tell me when the aggregate is the honest number and when the subgroup is, and how I'm supposed to know which breakdown is even the right one to look at. Then run it on three cases: (1) an A/B test where a new checkout flow lifts overall conversion but is down on iOS, Android, and desktop seperately — do we ship? (2) a before/after dashboard where a change raised activation in every acquisition channel but the overall activation rate is flat because the channel mix shifted — do we keep the change? (3) a test where the overall metric is flat but there's a big lift among users who clicked a new widget, and the PM wants to ship on that subgroup story — does the procedure let us? Make one of these come out 'don't ship' even though the number someone in the room is excited about looks good. Also be honest about where the procedure will misfire or can't settle the question. And at the end, tell me how confident you are in what you're claiming and single out the point you're least sure about.
