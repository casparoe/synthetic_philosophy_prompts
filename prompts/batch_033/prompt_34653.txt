Okay, I need a second opinion on a meta-question about a debate I've been stuck in. I work on animal consciousness — the distribution question, basically: which animals are conscious and what's the bar for saying so. And I've been watching the same arguments go back and forth for, I don't know, fifteen years now, between what I'll call the liberal camp (consciousness is widespread: insects, maybe all motile animals) and the conservative camp (mammals and birds, maybe cephalopods on a good day).

Here's what's driving me nuts. Both sides agree on essentially all the empirical facts. They agree about what we know about bee learning, fish nociception, the neural correlates of consciousness in humans, the evolutionary history of the relevant structures. They even agree that we don't have a direct window into animal experience and probably never will. And yet the disagreement persists and nobody moves. The same papers get cited, the same moves get made, the same impasse.

So my question is: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it a disagreement about what counts as evidence for consciousness — a methodological dispute about inference to the best explanation? Is it a disagreement about the prior probability that consciousness is widespread vs. rare, which probably traces back to different metaphysical pictures of what consciousness even is? Or is it something weirder — a disagreement about where to set the "reasonable doubt" threshold in a domain where certainty is impossible, which is basically a practical or ethical choice dressed up as a factual one?

I have my own hypothesis (I think it's mostly the threshold thing, and the methodological talk is downstream), but I want an honest diagnosis from someone not in the trenches. And crucially: given your diagnosis, is there anything that could actually move this debate? A new empirical result? A conceptual distinction? Or is this the kind of thing where we should just admit the question as posed doesnt have a determinate answer and start asking different questions instead?
