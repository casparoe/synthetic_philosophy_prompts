I'd like you to construct a decision problem to the following specifications.

The two theories at issue are standard expected utility theory (EU) and risk-weighted expected utility theory (REU), as developed by Lara Buchak. I want a case in which these two theories rank two available options differently, and the REU ranking is the one that matches ordinary intuition — that is, the option that REU favours is the one a reasonable person would actually choose.

The case must satisfy all of the following constraints:

1. It must not be a variant of the Allais paradox, the Ellsberg paradox, or any other familiar pattern of violation of the independence axiom or the sure-thing principle. The divergence should arise from a structural feature you have not seen discussed in the standard literature on these paradoxes.
2. The stakes must be ordinary — money, health, career prospects, or some combination of these — with no exotic goods and no life-or-death stakes that would make risk attitudes hard to interpret.
3. There must be no more than three states of the world.
4. The divergence between EU and REU must be driven by the shape of the risk function (the weighting of outcomes by their rank in the ordering) rather than by an unusual or contrived probability assignment. You should specify the risk function you are using and justify its shape briefly.

After presenting the scenario, do the following in order:

(a) State each option's expected utility and risk-weighted expected utility, showing your calculations in full and making clear which risk function you have adopted.
(b) Identify the structural feature of the case that makes the two theories come apart, and explain why this feature is not simply a repackaging of the common-pattern violations excluded by constraint 1.
(c) Remove or alter that feature and show what happens: do the two theories now agree, or do they still diverge? If they still diverge, what does that tell us about the robustness of the case?
(d) Explain how a defender of standard EU theory would resist the intuition that the REU-favoured option is the correct choice. Assess whether that resistance is plausible in this instance.

Finally, state how confident you are in the case you have constructed, and single out the point you are least sure of — whether about the calculations, the psychological plausibility of the claimed intuition, or the claim that the case is genuinely novel relative to the existing literature.
