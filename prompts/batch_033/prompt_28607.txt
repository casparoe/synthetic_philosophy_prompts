So I run this small philosophy podcast mostly for the rationalist-adjacent crowd, and I've managed to line up a guest who's one of the main defenders of updateless decision theory — not Wei Dai himself but someone who's published a fair bit defending UDT against the usual CDT and EDT camps. I want to skip the standard podcast fluff and really get into the technical objections, but in a way that's still followable for listeners who've read the sequences without necessarily working through the formal arguments.

Could you draft me eight to ten interview questions? Here's what I want covered:

— At least two questions on the logical-counterfactual problem. Everyone mentions it but I want to push past "it's hard" into what the actual candidate solutions are and why none of them have fully landed. Include follow-ups that anticipate the "we can use something like Pearl-style causal graphs extended to logical space" reply — what's the specific thing that breaks when you try to actually do that?

— At least one question on the relationship between UDT and functional decision theory. The guest has written that they're distinct theories, not just rebranding, so I want a follow-up that presses on what concrete decision problem comes out differently between them. If they can't name one, I want that awkwardness on the record.

— A question on the "updateless" part specifically: why shouldn't I update on the fact that I find myself in a particular branch or observing particular evidence? This feels like it cuts against basic Bayesianism and I want the guest to defend why abandoning updating isn't as crazy as it sounds — and then a follow-up asking whether there's any situation where an updateless agent does worse than an updating one, and what that says about the theory.

— A question about algorithm individuation: what counts as "the same decision algorithm" across different possible worlds? This seems like it could be gerrymandered to get whatever answer you want, and I want the guest to say where the boundaries actually are and whether there's a principled answer or just intuition.

— A question about whether UDT actually gives different practical recommendations from EDT or CDT for any decision a human being will realistically face, or whether this is all angels on pinheads. If the answer is "it matters for AI alignment," fine, but then I want them to say whether it matters for humans at all.

— And finally, a question about what evidence would actually make the guest abandon UDT. I don't want the "I'd update if I saw a better theory" dodge — I want to know what empirical finding or thought-experiment result would count as a falsification, something where they'd go "well, if that were true, UDT is in trouble."

Make the questions conversational — the way I'd actually say them into a mic — and please write the whole thing in British English. I've been reading LessWrong since 2009 so don't pull punches on the technical vocab; my listeners can handle it. And if the guest has a standard deflection for any of these, bake the follow-up right into the question so he can't just sidestep and move on.
