In epistemology, call a condition "luminous" if, whenever it obtains, you're in a position to know that it obtains — you could know it just by reflecting, with no new evidence. Timothy Williamson's anti-luminosity argument is meant to show that no non-trivial condition is luminous: roughly, take a slow change from clearly cold to clearly warm, note that there's no sharp cutoff, and argue that if you knew "I'm cold" at one moment, a margin-for-error requirement on knowledge would force you to be cold at the next moment too, which by induction is absurd. The same machinery is often taken to sink the KK principle — the idea that knowing p puts you in a position to know that you know p.
