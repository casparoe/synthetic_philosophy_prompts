The standard utilitarian replies tend to fall into two camps: either they deny that separateness has the moral weight critics claim it does, or they argue that properly specified utilitarian principles (like rule-utilitarianism or two-level utilitarianism) don't actually license the troubling trade-offs. But I suspect both sides are talking past each other because they are using "separateness" to mean at least two different things — roughly, one metaphysical (about the boundaries between selves) and one normative (about what kinds of trade-offs those boundaries rule out).
