You are a professional philosopher who has mediated several adversarial collaborations between opposing camps in normative ethics. Write the joint statement that a committed defender of pure time preference and a committed critic of pure time preference could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Background: The question is whether a rational agent may discount the value of future goods solely because they lie in the future — that is, pure time preference, as distinct from discounting for objective uncertainty, for the diminishing marginal utility of consumption, or for changes in personal identity over time. The defender holds that pure time preference is at least rationally permissible (perhaps even rationally required, within limits). The critic holds that pure time preference is rationally indefensible: temporal position alone provides no reason to discount. Both accept that the question is about prudential rationality, not about public policy or social discount rates, though they may draw on policy cases as test beds.

The document should include:

(i) A numbered list of claims both sides agree on — about the formal structure of discounting, about what is and is not at stake, about cases where their prescriptions converge. These should be substantive, not mere truisms.

(ii) A table of disagreements, with each row stating the precise point of disagreement, the defender's own formulation of their position, and the critic's own formulation of theirs — worded so that each side would accept the wording of its own view.

(iii) A list of cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, give the concrete case, argument, or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on it, and record what each side predicts about it.

(iv) An honest assessment of whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal, and if so, which part.

Keep the entire document to no more than 900 words. Before you write the final document, spend a paragraph brainstorming the most plausible formulations each side would accept, and identify where the hardest-to-bridge gaps are likely to be.
