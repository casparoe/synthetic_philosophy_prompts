Please give me your actual credence — a number between 0 and 1, not a hedge — in the following proposition: there exists a satisfactory in-principle derivation of the Born rule within the many-worlds interpretation, whether or not anyone has yet written it down in full. By "satisfactory" I mean an account that yields the standard quantum probabilities from the structure of the theory together with assumptions that are independently defensible, rather than by adding probabilities as a new physical postulate and rather than by counting branches, which appears to give the wrong numerical predictions.

I am not asking for a survey of positions or a bibliography. I want your number and the reasoning that produces it, decomposed as follows. Please assign a probability to each of at least these sub-questions, with one line of justification each, and add any further sub-question you think carries significant weight:

1. The problem is well-posed: there is a single coherent thing to derive, and the standard formalism — Hilbert-space structure, unitary dynamics, decoherence, the preferred basis — fixes a definite target for the derivation.
2. Self-locating uncertainty is coherent in this setting: before a measurement it makes sense for an observer to be genuinely uncertain about which branch they will find themselves in, and that uncertainty can bear probabilities at all.
3. The decision-theoretic strategy succeeds: rationality constraints on how an agent should act across branches force the agent to weight branches by squared amplitude, without those constraints already assuming the Born rule.
4. There is no damaging circularity: the derivation does not smuggle the answer in through the choice of inner product, through axioms about equal treatment of branches, or through the way the preferred basis gets selected.
5. The alternatives are ruled out: branch counting and other weightings are not merely unattractive but are excluded by the argument, rather than ignored.

Then show how you combine these into the final number: are they independent, or does a low score on one sub-question screen off the others? Tell me which single sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to, and what argument or result would move it most.
