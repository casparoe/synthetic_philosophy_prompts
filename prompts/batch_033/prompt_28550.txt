I'm working on a feature about free will and moral responsibility — the angle is whether our everyday practices of blame and praise can survive what we're learning from neuroscience and psychology. I've been going round in cirlces for weeks and I'd like someone to tell me if my current position even hangs together.

Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm pretty convinced by Strawson's line in "Freedom and Resentment" that the reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, indignation, all that — are the real foundation of moral responsibility. You don't need to prove free will first and then earn the right to feel resentful; the practices come first, and philosophical justification comes after, if at all.

(2) That said, I can't shake the sense that when you trace any person's actions back through their causal history, the whole notion of "ultimate" responsibility just evaporates. Nobody chose their genes, their parents, their early environment, the cultural moment they were born into. At the limit, it seems like everything you are and do traces to factors outside your control.

(3) I think we can and should modulate our reactive attitudes based on what we learn about a person's psychology and circumstances. If someone acts badly because of a brain tumour or a psychotic episode, we rightly withhold resentment. This suggests the reactive attitudes aren't just brute facts — they're sensitive to reasons.

(4) I hold that moral responsibility is fundamentally a social practice, and it doesn't require libertarian free will. Compatibilism seems obviously right to me on that narrow point.

(5) But I also think there's something genuinely disturbing about the sheer scale of luck involved in shaping who we become — and this seems to erode desert in a way that our ordinary blaming practices don't properly acknowledge. I'm not sure this is just a theoretical worry; I think it should make us less confident in our blame practices.

So that's the set. I need someone to go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest fix is. Rank the problems by severity rather than just listing everything as equally bad. And for each genuine tension, tell me what I'd have to give up and what that would cost.
