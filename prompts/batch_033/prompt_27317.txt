ok so i've been trying to figure out when moral compromise is actually a good thing vs when its just selling out, and ive ended up with this set of views that i want someone to stress-test:

(1) if two people have really different values, they can sometimes both win by trading compromises — like i care way more about X than Y and you care way more about Y than X, so we each give on our low-priority thing and both come out ahead by our own standards. this seems like the same logic as gains from trade in economics except applied to morality.

(2) but there are some things you just shouldnt compromise on, period. like if someone wanted me to help cover up a murder in exchange for them donating to a cause i care about, that's just off the table no matter how big the donation.

(3) the trade model only works if everyone whos affected by the deal is actually part of the negotiation. you cant trade away things that screw over people who arent in the room.

(4) when a compromise does make both sides genuinly better off by their own lights, thats a real moral good — its not just "well we had to settle for less." the compromise itself has positive value.

(5) moral values arent the kind of thing you can put on a single scale and weigh against each other. there isnt a common currency. so you cant just do a utilitarian math problem to decide when to compromise.

do these hang together? i feel like theres at least one tension in here that i'm not seeing, and maybe one that looks like a tension but isnt. which one would i have to give up first, and what would that cost me?
