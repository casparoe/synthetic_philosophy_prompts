I've been going down a rabbit hole on Kelly betting and geometric growth after a friend kept insisting that maximizing expected wealth is fine as long as you can repeat the bet, and I want a consistency check before I write up where I've landed for a decision theory reading group. Here's roughly my position:

(1) For one-shot decisions under risk, expected utility theory is the whole story: you have a utility function over final wealth, concave for basically everyone, and you maximize expected utility.

(2) For repeated multiplicative bets, the Kelly criterion is the right rule. It maximizes the long-run growth rate, and in the limit it almost surely ends up ahead of any fixed-fraction strategy that bets a different fraction of wealth.

(3) But Kelly isn't a competitor to expected utility theory. It just is expected utility theory with a log utility function. The log isn't magic.

(4) Still, I think the ergodicity-economics people are onto something real: for an individual who lives one path through time, the time-average growth rate is the right object to care about, not the ensemble-average expected wealth. Maximizing expected wealth can blow you up in repeated settings even when every bet has positive expectation.

(5) Fractional Kelly is defensible, not just a psychological crutch: it's the optimal policy for a power-utility agent who's more risk-averse than log, trading some growth for lower variance and shallower drawdowns.

(6) But for a one-shot bet I also don't think you should use Kelly at all; you should just maximize your actual utility function, whatever it is.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how severe each real tension is. For the real ones, what has to give and what's the cheapest fix? I'd rather find out now than have the reading group find out for me.
