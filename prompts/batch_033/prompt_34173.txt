I am writing the final exam for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on distributive justice, and I want one essay question that does real work. Too many students can state the leveling-down objection and still fail to see what prioritarianism has to give up in order to escape it, so the question should not be answerable by reciting the distinction between telic egalitarianism and prioritarianism. Please write the question, a model answer of roughly 800 words, and a marking scheme for a 25-mark essay.

The question should be built around this crux: prioritarianism avoids the leveling-down objection only by denying that equality is valuable in itself, yet that very denial seems to threaten prioritarianism's claim to capture what is distinctively objectionable about inequality—for instance, the thought that it can be unfair for one person to be worse off than another through no fault of their own, even when no feasible transfer would raise the worse-off person's welfare. The question should therefore require students to (i) state the objection while distinguishing a leveled-down outcome's being better in one respect from its being better all things considered, (ii) identify the precise premise of telic egalitarianism that prioritarianism rejects, (iii) assess whether prioritarianism can explain that comparative unfairness complaint without smuggling back in the idea it had to abandon, and (iv) reach a reasoned verdict on whether the objection establishes prioritarianism or only shows that egalitarians must refine their view. The model answer should model that four-part structure in continuous prose rather than as a list, and the marking scheme should name the specific moves that separate an A answer from a B answer, the errors that should cap an answer at a B or lower, and, for each common error, a one-line note on the misunderstanding it reflects.
