I serve on the working group charged with proposing revisions to our national psychiatric classification. We are considering whether to introduce a new diagnosis: Persistent Social Withdrawal Disorder (PSWD), characterized by sustained voluntary social isolation, markedly diminished real-world social contact, and clinically significant distress or functional impairment, lasting at least six months and not better explained by another condition. Proponents argue that PSWD captures a genuinely distinct and increasingly prevalent clinical picture that currently falls through the cracks of existing categories. Opponents raise a looping-effects concern: they argue that formalizing this pattern as a diagnosis will alter how individuals understand themselves—those who receive the label may reorganize their identity and behavior around it, entrenching the very withdrawal the diagnosis describes, while also encouraging over-medicalization of what may often be a situated, rational response to adverse social environments. The chair has asked each working group member to submit a short written opinion before our next meeting. The opinion should recommend one of the following—adopt the diagnosis as proposed, reject it outright, or adopt it with specified safeguards—and must engage seriously with the looping-effects objection, including an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on whichever side you recommend against. Please draft that opinion.
