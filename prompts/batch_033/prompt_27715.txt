Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers of physics about the quantum suicide thought experiment and the doctrine of quantum immortality. The set-up under discussion is this: an experimenter is wired to a device that kills them unless a quantum measurement yields a specified result, and the trial is repeated many times. Both characters grant the many-worlds interpretation for the sake of argument, so the dispute is not about whether many-worlds is true but about what the experiment shows if it is.

One character defends the quantum-immortality conclusion: the experimenter should expect to observe themselves surviving trial after trial, because every run produces branches containing a surviving observer and only branches containing an observer are available to first-person experience; on this view, observing one's own continued survival is a genuine empirical test that favours many-worlds over collapse interpretations. The other character rejects this conclusion: they hold that rational self-locating credence under many-worlds should track the Born weights, or some other well-motivated branch measure, rather than the bare existence of branches, so a low-amplitude survival branch does not warrant near-certainty of survival, and the experiment is at best an elaborate selection effect.

Neither character should be a strawman. The critic should state the defender's position in its strongest form before attacking it, and should answer the defender's charge that they are smuggling in a single-world or collapse-based conception of personal identity across branches. The defender should answer the critic's central point that there being some branch in which a survivor exists is not the same as that branch being the branch one should expect to inhabit. Each speech should respond to the previous one, not merely restate a prepared position. Aim for roughly 1,200 words.

If the task as I have framed it rests on a confusion — for example, if the two positions cannot be made coherent, or if I have misdescribed what the Born rule or a branch measure commits one to — say so plainly and reframe the task rather than producing a dialogue that politely complies with a muddle. Write in British English.
