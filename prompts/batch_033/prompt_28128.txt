I am the registrar at a mid-sized museum of natural history and ethnography. Several times a week I must decide whether the items in front of me should be recorded as one object or as a collection of distinct objects in the collections database. The choice controls accession records, insurance lines, loan units, and deaccession, and it can become a legal and ethical problem when provenance, donor restrictions, or repatriation claims track the record boundary. My staff have no philosophy training, a routine decision should take under an hour, and unusual cases can be escalated.

Please write me a reusable decision procedure for this, grounded in the special composition question — the question of when several things compose a further thing — and in the main families of answers to it: nihilism (composition never occurs; there are only simples), universalism (any plurality whatsoever composes something), life-based views (composition occurs exactly when the parts are caught up in a single life), and contact or fastening views (composition occurs when the parts are suitably joined). The procedure should ask its questions in order, say what counts as a sufficient answer to each, say where it stops with "record as a collection," where it stops with "record as a single object," and where it hands the decision to me or the collections committee because the matter is one of institutional convention or values rather than fact. Where the main philosophical answers would disagree, the procedure should say so explicitly rather than silently choosing one.

Then run the procedure on three cases: (1) a fossil skeleton excavated as several hundred disarticulated bones from a single individual; (2) a ceremonial mask whose carved wooden base, feathers, and beads arrived separately and are assembled only for display; (3) a cache of two dozen near-identical stone tools found together in one pit. Make sure at least one worked case shows the procedure returning an answer the registrar will not like, and end by saying what the procedure cannot settle.
