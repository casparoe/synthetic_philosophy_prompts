I've been reading Popper on Darwinism and I keep going back and forth on whether he's being consistent with his own philosophy of science. On the one hand you have the Popper everyone knows: a theory is scientific only if its falsifiable, and if it can be bent to fit any observation it isn't really empirical. On the other hand he says natural selection is almost tautological—the worry being that survival of the fittest ends up defining fitness by survival and so explains nothing—and then later he backs off and says Darwinism is better understood as a metaphysical research programme than as a directly testable scientific theory. Compare the two Poppers. Is the Darwinism stuff a consistent application of the demarcation criterion, a special exemption, or just a change of mind? And whichever it is, does it show that the falsifiability criterion is a bad fit for historical sciences like evolutionary biology? I want the comparison to stay close to what he actually wrote, not the cartoon Popper, and to say whether the tension is real or only apparent.
