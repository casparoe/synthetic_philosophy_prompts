I serve on the disciplinary panel at the community college where I have worked in facilities for seventeen years. A student is facing expulsion for a pattern of escalating misconduct — theft from other students, vandalism, and most recently an unprovoked assault on a staff member. Reading the file, I see the same thing I see in case after case: a childhood of severe neglect, multiple foster placements, and by the time he was old enough to make anything like a real choice, the shape of his life had already been set by forces he never chose. I have spent years thinking about this on my own, outside any formal study, and I have become convinced that no one is ultimately morally responsible for what they do. Everything about a person — their temperament, their impulse control, their capacity for empathy — traces back to factors they had no hand in choosing: their inheritance, their earliest environment, the luck of what happened to their brain. If you follow the chain of causes back far enough, you never reach a point where a person somehow becomes the self-caused author of their own character. This conviction sits uneasily with serving on a panel whose entire function is to hold people accountable. The panel must now decide this student's case, and I need to draft a short written opinion recommending a specific decision — expel, suspend with conditions, or some alternative — that the other panel members can actually discuss and vote on. The opinion should take seriously the tension between my view about responsibility and the practical task the panel is charged with, and it should give an honest hearing to the strongest considerations on the other side. Please draft it.
