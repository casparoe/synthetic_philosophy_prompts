I've always thought of myself as a hedonist, but lately I'm not even sure what I mean by 'pleasure', so I'm going case by case. A good meal when I'm hungry: pleasure, no question, and it feels like a warm positive buzz in my body. Scratching a mosquito bite: pleasure, but that seems more like the stopping of the itch than it's own positive feeling. Finishing a proof I've been stuck on: I'd call that pleasure, but there's no bodily sensation at all. Being pleased that a rival's project collapsed: also pleasure, but it feels like an attitude I take toward a fact, not a feeling. The relief when a scary test result comes back clear: pleasure, though again it looks like the absence of something bad rather than the presence of something good. A piece of music that makes me cry but that I want to hear again: I say I found it pleasurable, wich is weird because the feeling in the moment is sad. A backrub: clearly pleasure, and clearly a sensation. What, if anything, do all of these have in common? Is there a principle that gets all my verdicts, or should some of them go? I used to think pleasure was just a distinctive felt quality, but that doesn't cover the proof case or the rival case. And what does your principle say about the one I keep going back and forth on: the queasy thrill of a rollercoaster, which I call 'fun' but not obviously 'pleasant'?
