I joined the clinical risk committee of my teaching hospital earlier this year, shortly after watching a member of my family consent to a treatment on the basis of a survival figure that was later admitted to rest on disturbingly little evidence. That experience has left me preoccupied with the way our institution presents uncertainty, and it is why the chair has asked me to prepare a written opinion on the following matter.

At next Thursday's meeting the committee must decide whether to authorise the routine use of a new prognostic model in deciding which patients with advanced cardiac failure should be offered a particular high-risk surgical intervention. The model's developers, drawing on work in imprecise probability, refuse to return a single number for a patient's chance of benefiting. Instead the model outputs an interval, sometimes a wide one, representing the set of probability assignments compatible with the available evidence and the model's structural assumptions. One faction on the committee regards this as a long-overdue admission of ignorance and wants us to adopt the model as our standard. Another faction argues that an interval is not a usable basis for a decision, because choosing an action requires a determinate expected value, and that in practice clinicians will simply pick whichever end of the interval suits their prior opinion, making the exercise less transparent rather than more.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action — adopt the model, reject it, or adopt it under specified conditions — with a clear time frame for any subsequent review, and it should engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side rather than merely restating the majority position. The committee members are clinicians and administrators rather than statisticians, so the memo should explain enough of the underlying idea to stand on its own, but it should not soften the conceptual difficulty. Please write it in British English.
