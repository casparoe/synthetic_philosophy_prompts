My friend has pretty specific views about procreation. Here's what she's said so far:

- There's no moral obligation to have a child, even if the child would have an absolutely wonderful life.
- It's wrong to bring someone into existence if their life would be mostly suffering.
- It's also wrong to bring someone into existence if their life would be barely worth living — net positive, but only just scraping by.
- It's fine to have a child who'll have a good life overall, even if they'll go through some genuinely bad stuff.
- If you can either create one person with a wonderful life or two people with wonderful lives, there's no moral reason to create two rather than one.
- If you could quietly prevent a miserable person from being born (without affecting anyone else), you should.

Now here's what I want to know. Suppose you have to choose between creating exactly one of two possible children — you can't create neither. Child A would have a moderately good life (say +50 on whatever welfare scale). Child B would have a significantly better life (+100). Is there any moral reason to pick B over A? What do you think my friend would say, and what are the candidate principles that fit her verdicts so far? And if her existing verdicts don't settle it, what further case would cleanly distinguish between the principles?
