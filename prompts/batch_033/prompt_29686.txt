2. **The boss's threat.** An employer tells an employee they'll be fired unless they have sex. Verdict: consent invalid. Paradigm coercion — a threat that worsens the person's options relative to the baseline they're entitled to.

3. **Blackout drunk.** Pat is slurring, stumbling, and later remembers nothing of the night. Pat said yes to sex. Verdict: consent invalid. Pat lacked the capacity to understand what they were agreeing to.

4. **The undisclosed marriage.** During a casual hookup, Sam is married but says nothing about it. The other person, Riley, assumes Sam is single and doesn't ask. Verdict: I feel this is seriously wrong, and I lean toward saying consent is invalid — but I'm not confident, and this is the case I keep going back and forth on.

5. **The résumé puff.** On a first date, Morgan says they're a lawyer when they're actually a paralegal. They sleep together. Verdict: consent valid. It's embellishment, not the kind of deception that undermines sexual autonomy — or so I want to say.

6. **The guilt trip.** During an argument about whether to have sex, one partner says, "If you really loved me you'd do this." The other partner, worn down, agrees. Verdict: this is manipulative and wrong, but I think the consent is still valid — barely. It's pressure, but not the kind that removes the ability to say no.

7. **The tuition offer.** A wealthy older person offers to pay a struggling college student's full tuition in exchange for a sexual relationship. The student, after thinking it over, accepts. Verdict: consent valid. It's an offer, not a threat — it expands options rather than contracting them — even though it exploits a vulnerability.

8. **Stealthing.** Two people agree to sex with a condom. Midway through, one removes it without the other's knowledge or consent. Verdict: consent invalid. The condition under which consent was given has been secretly violated.
