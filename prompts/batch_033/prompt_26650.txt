In population ethics, I have been collecting my own verdicts about infinite populations, and I would like to know whether any single principle captures them. Consider these cases. If a universe contains infinitely many people all living good lives, and I can bring about one additional good life, I do not think the universe is made better. If a universe contains infinitely many people all in agony, and I can end the agony of one of them, I do think the universe is made better, even though the total amount of pain remains infinite. If a universe contains infinitely many happy people and one person being tortured, I think it is worse than a universe without the torture, even though total welfare is infinite either way. But I have no settled judgment about whether a universe with infinitely many happy and infinitely many miserable people is better than, worse than, or equal to a universe with only infinitely many happy people. I do, however, think a universe in which every person is happy is better than one in which every person is miserable, even if both populations are infinite. Is there a coherent principle of aggregation that yields exactly these verdicts? State it precisely, show how it handles each case, and tell me which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate.
