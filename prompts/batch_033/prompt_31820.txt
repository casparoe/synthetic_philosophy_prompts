I've been thinking about the problem of divine hiddenness — why God, if he exists, doesn't make his existence more obvious — and I've settled into a set of views that I'm fairly comfortable with. But I want to check whether they actually hang together, because I've been living with them long enough that I might be missing a conflict. Here's what I think:

(a) God exists, is perfectly loving, and desires a personal relationship with every human being.

(b) There are sincere, non-resistant seekers — people who honestly want to know whether God exists, who search with an open mind, and who come up empty. Some of them would enter into a relationship with God if they became convinced he was real.

(c) For a relationship with God to be authentic, it must be freely chosen. God cannot compel belief or love without undermining the very thing that makes the relationship valuable.

(d) Divine hiddenness — the fact that God's existence is not overwhelmingly obvious — is explained by (c): God remains partially hidden in order to preserve our freedom to respond to him.

(e) Hiddenness isn't just a concession to freedom; it plays a positive role. Wrestling with uncertainty builds virtues — patience, humility, trust — that would not develop if God's existence were simply handed to us.

I'd like an audit of these five commitments. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how serious is each tension? If something has to give, what's the cheapest revision and what does it cost?
