For years I've been teaching the debate about whether an artwork's moral character bears on its aesthetic value, and I've noticed something frustrating. The standard positions are all well worked out: autonomism holds that moral and aesthetic evaluation are independent; ethicism holds that a moral defect in a work counts as an aesthetic defect, at least pro tanto; moderate moralism holds that moral defects can be aesthetic defects but need not be, depending on the work; and immoralism holds that moral defects can sometimes function as aesthetic merits. The canonical examples — Triumph of the Will, Lolita, the films of Leni Riefenstahl, the paintings of Gauguin — have been traded back and forth until they are worn smooth. Each side can point to cases that seem to support it, and each side has a story about why the other side's cases do not cut the way they appear to. Yet nobody moves.

My working hypothesis is that the impasse traces to a deeper disagreement about what aesthetic value is: roughly, whether it is a matter of the experience the work affords a properly situated audience (in which case moral revulsion could straightforwardly diminish it) or a matter of the work's achievement relative to its constitutive aims (in which case moral defects might be irrelevant, or even part of the achievement). But I am not confident that this diagnosis is right, and I would like a proper one.

Diagnose this impasse. What kind of disagreement is it, at bottom — verbal, factual, about bedrock intuitions, about what counts as evidence for an aesthetic claim, about the metaphysics of aesthetic properties? Are the two sides even talking about the same thing when they say "good art"? If you think part of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why, and identify the term or terms whose disambiguation would dissolve it. And given your diagnosis, what would actually move the debate: a new conceptual distinction, a different kind of case, an empirical finding, a shift in the question — or should we expect the stalemate to persist, and if so, why?
