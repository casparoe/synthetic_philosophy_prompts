I've been stuck on a line of reasoning for a while now, and I can't tell where it goes wrong. Here it is:

(1) Take the statement "I will eat an apple tomorrow." Either that statement is true right now, or it's false right now. There's no third option — that's just the law of excluded middle.

(2) If it's true right now, then it's already a settled fact that I will eat an apple tomorrow. And if it's already a settled fact, nothing I do between now and then can make it false — because if I ended up not eating an apple, then the statement would have been false all along, not true.

(3) If it's false right now, then by the same reasoning, it's already a settled fact that I won't eat an apple, and nothing I do can change that.

(4) Either way, what I will do tomorrow is already settled. And the same reasoning applies to every future event — what I'll have for breakfast, who I'll marry, whether a war will break out. The whole future is already fixed.

(5) But that can't be right. I deliberate about what to eat. I weigh options. I change my mind. It feels like the future is genuinely open, not a book that's already been written.

(6) So something in the argument must be false. But the only way out I can see is to reject (1) — to say that statements about the future aren't yet true or false. But that means giving up a basic law of logic. Is that the cost? Or is there a flaw somewhere else in the reasoning that I'm missing?
