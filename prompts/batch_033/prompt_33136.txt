Question: In a study of a particular population, the heritability of a trait is estimated at 0.6. Explain what this estimate does and does not tell us. Your answer must address at least two common misinterpretations of heritability.

Student's answer: A heritability of 0.6 means that 60 percent of the variation in the trait among individuals in this population is associated with genetic differences among them, and the estimate is specific to this population and the range of environments it inhabits. It follows that, for any particular individual in the population, 60 percent of that individual's trait is caused by genes and the remaining 40 percent by the environment. The estimate also licenses an inference about group differences: if two groups drawn from this population differ in the trait, most of that difference is likely to be genetic, because genetic differences account for more of the total variation than environmental differences do.
