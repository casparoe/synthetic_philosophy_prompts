I've been reading around in the Humeanism vs. anti-Humeanism debate about laws of nature for a couple years now and I'm starting to feel like the whole thing is just spinning its wheels. The Humean says: the laws are just the axioms of the best systematization of the mosaic of particular facts — the simplest, strongest summary of what actually happens, period. The anti-Humean says: no, laws *govern*, they have real modal force, they're not just economical descriptions of the events they "cover." And both sides have been firing the same arguments at each other forever: the Humean says the anti-Humean's governing relation is spooky, unobservable, and does no explanatory work beyond what the best system already does; the anti-Humean says the Humean can't explain why the laws support counterfactuals or why induction is rational, since on the Humean picture the laws are just a human-selected summary and there's no reason nature should keep cooperating tomorrow. But here's the thing: as far as I can tell, both sides agree on every single empirical fact and on every piece of formal machinery — the Humean doesn't deny we can do modal logic, the anti-Humean doesn't deny we can systematize the mosaic. So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? My working theory is that it's not a factual disagreement at all — it's a disagreement about what metaphysics is supposed to *do*. The Humean wants a metaphysics that's maximally parsimonious and doesn't posit anything beyond the concrete particulars; the anti-Humean wants a metaphysics that takes our modal intuitions at face value, even if it means adding primitive nomological facts to the ontology. If that's right, then the debate is basically a clash of methodological values, and nobody's going to "win" by argument. But I'm not sure — maybe there's a hidden empirical or formal question I'm missing, or maybe the question is just ill-posed and the two sides are answering different questions under one heading. Diagnose this for me: what kind of disagreement is it really, and what would count as progress given your diagnosis?
