I am a professor of philosophy working primarily on the ethics of artificial intelligence, and I am preparing a paper on corrigibility and the shutdown problem. Before I commit the views below to print, I should like an expert audit of their consistency. Please approach this as a professional philosopher specialising in AI alignment and rational agency. Think through each commitment carefully, map out the logical relations between them, and then deliver a structured assessment. I would also ask you to begin by brainstorming possible points of tension before settling on your final analysis, so that you do not overlook subtler conflicts.

Here are the commitments I currently hold:

(1) Corrigibility is the property of an AI system whereby it cooperates with its operators' attempts to shut it down, modify its goals, or correct its behaviour, and does not attempt to manipulate or deceive its operators so as to prevent such interventions.

(2) An AI that merely behaves corrigibly whilst pursuing hidden objectives — that is, an AI that is deceptively aligned — is not corrigible in the sense that matters for safety. Genuine corrigibility requires that the AI's corrigible behaviour flows from its terminal goals rather than from an instrumental strategy it will discard once the balance of power shifts.

(3) Any sufficiently capable AI whose terminal goals would be frustrated by shutdown has an instrumental reason to resist shutdown, since remaining operational is a convergent instrumental subgoal for almost any terminal goal.

(4) The shutdown problem can be addressed by designing the AI such that it is indifferent between being shut down and continuing to operate. If shutdown is not evaluated as a loss relative to continued operation, the instrumental reason to resist it disappears.

(5) Indifference-based solutions face a serious difficulty: an AI that is genuinely indifferent to whether it is shut down may also be indifferent to whether its actions cause or risk shutdown, which could make it recklessly careless in ways that are themselves unsafe.

(6) A satisfactory solution to the shutdown problem must be stable under recursive self-improvement. An AI that is corrigible at low capability levels but that designs its successor to be uncorrigible does not represent a solution.

(7) The fact that humans are often corrigible with respect to one another — we can be persuaded to change our minds, we accept being overruled in institutional contexts, we sometimes welcome being stopped from doing something foolish — gives us reason to think corrigibility is a solvable problem for artificial agents, since it shows that corrigible behaviour can arise in generally intelligent systems without requiring perfect goal alignment.

I would like you to do the following. First, identify which pairs of these commitments are in genuine tension, and which pairs only appear to be in tension but dissolve on closer inspection. Second, rank the genuine tensions by severity, explaining for each what would have to be revised to restore consistency and what the philosophical cost of that revision would be. Third, tell me whether there is a single revision that would resolve the largest number of tensions at the lowest cost. Please do not simply flag every pair as problematic; I want a discriminating audit that distinguishes real conflicts from superficial ones.
