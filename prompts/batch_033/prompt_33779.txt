Adopt the role of a philosopher of medicine who specializes in the conceptual foundations of health and disease, and think through the following case carefully; precision matters more than length, and it is fine to begin by brainstorming the strongest version of each position before committing to an analysis.

Analyze the 1973 removal of homosexuality from the American Psychiatric Association's Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders as a test case for the dispute between naturalism and normativism about disease.

The episode, as I understand it: for decades the APA classified homosexuality as a mental disorder. After sustained protest by gay-rights activists and internal disagreement among psychiatrists, the APA voted in 1973 to remove it from the manual. A narrower diagnosis covering individuals distressed by their same-sex orientation remained in place for some years before being dropped as well. I am not asking you to relitigate the outcome; I want to know what the episode shows about the concept of disease itself.

Structure your answer around the following:
(1) State the naturalist and normativist positions precisely enough to apply them, including what each takes to make a disease attribution true.
(2) Reconstruct what each position entails about the pre-1973 classification and about the 1973 change. In particular, would a naturalist have to say the old classification was simply a scientific error? What evidence, if any, changed around 1973 that would count as new biological evidence on the naturalist view?
(3) Assess what each position explains well and what it explains poorly about the episode. Be candid about the awkward features: the biology of homosexuality did not change in 1973; what changed was organized pressure and professional politics. Does that favor normativism, or does it show only something about how institutions apply an otherwise value-free concept?
(4) Say what the episode does to each view: does it refute, merely complicate, or leave untouched the naturalist's claim that disease is a value-free biological notion? Does it leave the normativist with any residual problem — for example, explaining why the narrower distress-based diagnosis was retained, or explaining in what sense the earlier classification could be called a mistake rather than merely a different value judgment?
(5) Conclude with what the case genuinely establishes about the nature of disease categories, and what it cannot establish.
