An economist I know keeps pushing the idea that we should legalize kidney sales, and his whole argument is an analogy with dangerous jobs. His line is that if we let people work as loggers or crab fishermen, risking life and limb and using their body for pay, then we have no principled reason to stop someone from selling a spare kidney. Both, he says, are just ways of trading bodily risk and bodily capacity for money, so banning one while allowing the other is paternalistic and inconsistent. I'm supposed to respond to him at a seminar and I don't want to just give my gut reaction. Can you audit the analogy properly: what maps onto what, where it breaks, which of the breaks actually matter for his conclusion about legalization, and whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table? I'd rather have the strongest version of his analogy checked than a strawman.
