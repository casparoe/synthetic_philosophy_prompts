I'm about to start a unit on consciousness, and I keep running into the same frustrating move in both directions: people argue about whether phenomenal consciousness and access consciousness come apart by pointing at a single paradigm — blindsight, backward masking, change blindness, the partial-report paradigm, no-report binocular rivalry — and reading it as decisive, while quietly helping themselves to exactly the distinction the paradigm was supposed to settle. Before I read any more of that back-and-forth, I want the full specification on the table. Write the desiderata that any adequate account of the relationship between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness must meet.

For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it — an empirical finding the account must respect or a conceptual distinction it cannot collapse — and say why it is a requirement rather than a preference. I particularly want the list to handle: the fact that report is itself a task with its own memory and attention demands, so evidence for phenomenality is always filtered through access; the apparent mismatch between the felt richness of experience and the sparse contents that survive full report; and the question of what it even means for a state to be phenomenally conscious but inaccessible to the subject's later reasoning, if its content can't be put to any cognitive use. Mark which desiderata are non-negotiable for any serious view and which are commitments of particular camps. Then flag any pairs or larger subsets of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say exactly what the conflict is. Finally, score global workspace theory briefly against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria — the scorecard is only a sanity check, since I'll use the list to structure the seminar.

Open with a one-sentence statement of the central tension any such theory must resolve, then give the numbered desiderata. Hard limit: 900 words.
