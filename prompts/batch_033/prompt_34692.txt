I've been asked to give the keynote address at a conference on "Religious Accommodation: Philosophical Foundations and Legal Practice," jointly sponsored by a law school's program on law and religion and a humanities institute's Kierkegaard research group. The audience is roughly half employment lawyers and state-court judges who decide religious accommodation cases, and half philosophers of religion who work on Kierkegaard. The organizers have asked me to address whether Kierkegaard's concept of the teleological suspension of the ethical — the idea, developed in Fear and Trembling under the pseudonym Johannes de Silentio, that Abraham's obedience to the command to sacrifice Isaac involves a suspension of the universal claims of the ethical in virtue of an absolute relation to God — has any bearing on how a liberal legal order should think about religious accommodation.

Here is the difficulty. The lawyers in the room tend to think of accommodation cases as a balancing exercise: individual religious conscience on one side, a neutral and generally applicable law on the other, and the question is how much weight the conscience claim should receive. The Kierkegaard scholars in the room think this entire framing misunderstands what is at stake in the Abraham story — that the teleological suspension of the ethical is not about giving extra weight to individual conscience against collective norms, but about a structural transformation of the relation between the individual and the ethical itself. If I simply use Kierkegaard as a literary garnish on a standard legal argument about balancing tests, the philosophers will be annoyed. If I give a close reading of the pseudonymous dialectic and then conclude that the lawyers have been confused all along, the lawyers will have learned nothing they can use.

I need the speech to do something harder: the Kierkegaardian concept has to do real argumentative work. It should not merely illustrate a point I could make without it. It should change how a lawyer would think about the next accommodation case they handle. Please write the speech.
