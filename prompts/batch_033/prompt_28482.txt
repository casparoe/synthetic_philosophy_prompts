I've been trying to get clear on my views about consent for a while, and I'd like an external consistency check before I commit to them in writing. Here's where I've landed:

(1) Valid consent requires that the consenting party be adequately informed. Consent given under material deception is invalid, full stop — it doesn't matter whether the person would have consented anyway had they known the truth.

(2) The distinction between coercion (which invalidates consent) and mere hard choices (which don't) turns on whether the pressure comes from a threat or an offer. Threats invalidate consent; offers, even extremely attractive ones that the offeree can't reasonably refuse, do not.

(3) Someone who is intoxicated, in severe emotional distress, or otherwise temporarily incapacitated cannot give valid consent during that episode, regardless of what they say.

(4) However, a prior decision made while competent can extend forward and cover actions taken during a later period of incapacity. For instance, an advance directive can make it permissible to treat someone who is currently unable to consent.

(5) Implicit or tacit consent is a genuine form of consent in at least some contexts — for example, by voluntarily participating in a practice whose rules are publicly known, one can consent to those rules.

(6) The bar for what counts as "adequately informed" should be sensitive to the stakes. For low-stakes, everyday interactions, the informational requirement is minimal; for high-stakes decisions, it's demanding. But the underlying principle is the same in both cases.

(7) There is no such thing as retroactive consent. If an action was not consented to at the time it occurred, the fact that the person later endorses it does not make it have been consensual.

I want to know: which of these are in genuine tension with each other, and which tensions are merely apparent? Please rank the tensions by severity, and for each genuine tension, identify which commitment(s) would need to be revised to resolve it, and at what cost.
