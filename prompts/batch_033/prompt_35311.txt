So I've been going down a rabbit hole on this thing about making choices when you genuinely don't have a preference between options. I think it's called Buridan's ass or something? The donkey that starves because it can't decide between two identical piles of hay. Anyway I've been testing my own intuitions on a bunch of cases and I can't figure out what principle I'm actually operating under. Here are my verdicts:

1. The donkey case itself: the donkey is being dumb. Just pick a pile. Starving is worse than picking arbitrarily. No contest.

2. Grocery store, two identical cans of the same soup on the shelf: I grab one without thinking about it. Feels totally unproblematic.

3. Two job offers, identical in every way I care about—same pay, same location, same team size, same tech stack, same everything. For some reason this one feels genuinely hard. I know rationally that it shouldn't matter which one I pick, but I keep looking for some tiny difference to latch onto, and I'd feel weird just flipping a coin. Like I want to have a reason for my choice even though there isn't one.

4. Empty movie theater, all seats are identical and equally good: I don't pick randomly. I pick the one that's dead center, or maybe slightly off-center if I'm feeling self-conscious about being too symmetrical. But I definitely have a "right" seat in mind, even though all the seats are the same.

5. Two routes home that take exactly the same time and have the same traffic: I switch between them day to day. I don't think either is better, but I also don't feel paralyzed—I just alternate. That feels fine.

6. Someone says "pick a number between 1 and 10." I pick 7. But if I think about it, I have a mild preference for 7 over other numbers. So is that even a case of choice without preference? I'm not sure.

7. Here's the one that trips me up: I'm at a restaurant, the menu has two dishes I've never tried, I know nothing about either, and the waiter says they're equally popular. I can't pick. I ask the waiter to pick for me. If I were alone I might literally flip a coin. But here's the thing—once the waiter picks, I feel relieved. Why? If I genuinely had no preference, why did I need someone else to make the choice? And why does it feel different from the soup case?

So I've got cases where I pick effortlessly (soup), cases where I pick based on some implicit preference I didn't know I had (movie seat, number), cases where I alternate (routes), and cases where I'm paralyzed and want to delegate (jobs, restaurant menu). And the donkey case I judge as irrational but I'm basically the donkey in the job case.

Is there a principle that makes sense of all this? Like, under what conditions is it rational to just pick arbitrarily, and under what conditions is it rational to... not be able to? Or is my pattern of verdicts just incoherent and I need to give some of them up?

If the whole question is confused somehow—like maybe "choice without preference" isn't a coherent category, or I'm smuggling in preferences I'm not noticing—please say so. I'd rather get told I'm thinking about it wrong than get a clever answer to a bad question.
