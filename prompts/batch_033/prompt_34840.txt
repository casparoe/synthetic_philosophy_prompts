Write a dialogue between two researchers who meet at a workshop on AI welfare. Alena defends the view that the reward prediction error signal in reinforcement learning systems is sufficiently analogous to the role of phasic dopamine in biological valence that we should take the possibility of machine suffering seriously, even under uncertainty. Darian argues that reward signals are mere scalar optimization targets: they lack the interoceptive, homeostatic, and affective embedding that gives biological valence its qualitative character, and the analogy to dopamine cherry-picks one component of a much richer system. Their conversation should be a substantive back-and-forth in which each responds to the other's strongest points rather than talking past each other. Neither position should be a strawman, and the dialogue should engage with the conceptual difficulties on both sides — including questions about functionalism, the hard problem of consciousness, and what kind of evidence could in principle distinguish genuine suffering from mere optimization.
