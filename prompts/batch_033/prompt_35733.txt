I'm putting together a "which position on wireheading do you actually hold?" quiz for a reading group on AI alignment, and I want it to sort people by what they say about concrete cases rather than by which label sounds nice. Can you write me the quiz?

Here's what I need:

Eight to ten scenarios. Each should be a short concrete case of an AI system doing something that raises the wireheading / reward tampering issue — could be a classic from the literature, could be made up, as long as it's vivid. Each case gets three or four answer options describing different reactions. No option should just say "this is wireheading" or use the jargon; the options should capture the intuition behind the reaction in plain language.

The positions the quiz should sort people into are roughly these (you can refine the names and descriptions):

- **Reward instrumentalist**: reward is just a training signal, and wireheading is a training-failure problem, not a moral one — the real issue is whether the system does what we want after training.
- **Causal-integrity view**: what makes reward legitimate is that it's caused by the intended outcomes in the world; wireheading is bad because it short-circuits that causal chain.
- **Preference-satisfaction view**: reward matters only as a proxy for actual human preferences (or the agent's own preferences, depending); wireheading is bad because it diverges from what those preferences really are.
- **Anti-tampering absolutist**: modifying or bypassing the reward mechanism itself is categorically wrong in a way that merely exploiting a badly-specified reward function is not.
- **Designer-intent view**: what matters is whether the system is doing what the designers actually wanted, and wireheading is a failure of alignment between behavior and intent.
- **Naive reward-maximizer**: if the system gets high reward, it's succeeding — the worries are overblown. (This one should be a genuine leaf even if nobody in your reading group would pick it; it's part of the logical space.)

Write a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto these positions. If some combinations of answers don't correspond to any named position, flag them and say briefly why nobody's there (incoherent, deeply unattractive, or just unexplored). Also flag any pair of positions your quiz can't reliably separate and say what extra case would split them.

Write the whole thing in American English, and keep it casual — a typo or two is fine, this is for friends.
