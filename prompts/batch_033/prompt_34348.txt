I teach a mixed undergrad/grad seminar on risk and collective action — 16 people, one 80-minute session. Next week I want to do the unilateralist's curse, but I don't want to lecture it: I want them to walk into it themselves before I name it. Design an in-class game where each player has a private, noisy signal about whether some risky action is a good idea, and any single player can unilaterally pull the trigger on that action for the whole group. The punchline they should hit on their own is that the group ends up acting far more often then any individual would endorse, and that adding more independent deciders makes it worse, not better. Give me rules, materials, a points-based payoff scheme (no real money, and no calling it a curse until the debrief), a timeline for the 80 minutes, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto the formal structure — especially the point that the group's effective threshold is the most optimistic member's threshold rather than any kind of average or vote. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle each, and be honest about which feature of the curse the game can't capture and that I'll have to just say outright.
