I argued that even if the overall outcome was good, the official still did something wrong by directly causing those deaths. A classmate shut me down by saying "that's just the problem of dirty hands — in politics you sometimes have to do wrong to do right, end of story." The professor nodded and moved on.

I've been chewing on this all week. The phrase "dirty hands" seems to be doing an enormous amount of work in that exchange, and I'm not sure my classmate and I even mean the same thing by it. I've since heard it used in three different ways: as a description of political reality (politicians inevitably get their hands dirty), as a moral excuse (they're not blameworthy for it), and as something almost tragic (they are blameworthy but had to do it anyway).
