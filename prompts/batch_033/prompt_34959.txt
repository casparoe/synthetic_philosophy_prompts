As a science journalist working on a long-read piece about the Fermi paradox, I have been interviewing researchers across astrobiology, philosophy of science, and existential risk studies. I have encountered wildly different credences on a single question, and I would like to get your considered view.

Please approach this as a professional philosopher of science with expertise in Bayesian reasoning and the epistemology of low-base-rate events. Think carefully, and where you are uncertain about a sub-question, say so explicitly rather than defaulting to 0.5 or to an uninformative prior. I am interested in the structure of your reasoning at least as much as in the final number.

Here is the question:

**What is your credence that humanity is the only technological civilisation ever to have arisen in the Milky Way galaxy?**

By "technological civilisation" I mean a species that has developed the capacity for interstellar communication or travel — roughly the kind of civilisation the original Drake equation was trying to count. By "ever to have arisen" I mean over the full ~13-billion-year history of the galaxy, up to the present. I am asking about the Milky Way specifically, not the observable universe, though you may appeal to broader considerations if they bear on the local question.

I do not want a bare number or a hand-waving reference to the Drake equation. I want a decomposition of your credence into the sub-questions on which it most depends. For each sub-question, please give me:

1. A rough probability, or a range if you cannot do better.
2. A single sentence of justification — the strongest consideration pushing that number up or down.
3. An indication of how much the final credence would shift if you were wrong about this term (e.g., "if this term were 0.1 rather than 0.9, my overall credence would move from X to Y").

Then show how these terms combine to yield your overall credence. You need not assume independence; if there are dependencies or correlations among your terms, note them and explain how you handle the combination.

Finally, please tell me:

- Which sub-question your overall credence is most sensitive to — the one where a modest change in your probability would produce the largest shift in the bottom line.
- What empirical discovery or theoretical development in the next two decades would move that sub-question the most, and in which direction.

I am especially interested in how you handle the following tension, which has come up repeatedly in my interviews: if the galaxy contains many civilisations, the absence of visible artefacts or signals seems to require a very strong filter that reliably prevents technological species from becoming detectable; but if we are alone, that seems to require at least one step in the chain from prebiotic chemistry to radio telescopes to be vanishingly improbable. How does your decomposition resolve that tension — or, if it does not, where does the unease sit?
