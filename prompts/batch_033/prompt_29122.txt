I've come to accept something like Dennett's "competence without comprehension" thesis — the idea that Darwin and Turing each pulled off a strange inversion of reasoning by showing that you can get the outward appearance of design, purpose, or intelligence from processes that themselves have no understanding whatsoever. As a 19th-century critic of Darwin put it: "In the theory with which we have to deal, Absolute Ignorance is the artificer; so that we may enunciate as the fundamental principle of the whole system, that, IN ORDER TO MAKE A PERFECT AND BEAUTIFUL MACHINE, IT IS NOT REQUISITE TO KNOW HOW TO MAKE IT." Turing's parallel inversion, in Dennett's borrowing of the same language, is that to be a perfect and beautiful computing machine, it is not requisite to know what arithmetic is. The upshot is that comprehension — understanding, insight, knowing-what-you're-doing — is not a prerequisite for competence; it's a late-arriving product of mindless processes, not their source.

I want to know what would count against this view. Not the standard creationist objections or Searle's Chinese Room, which I've already found unpersuasive, but the arguments and evidence that a defender of the view should genuinely worry about. What observations, formal results, or conceptual difficulties would put real pressure on the claim that comprehension is always downstream of comprehension-free competence? Are there domains where the arrow plausibly runs the other way — where understanding does irreducible explanatory work? Does the position risk collapsing into triviality if "competence" is defined broadly enough? And has the thesis been qualified in ways that insulate it from counterevidence — and if so, is that a problem for a view of this kind, or just the nature of a high-level philosophical claim? I'd like the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward, and I'd appreciate an honest assessment of how exposed the position actually is. If you think the question as posed rests on a confusion, say so plainly rather than humoring the framing.
