A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about the public dispute over no-excuses charter schools, and I came away unsatisfied with my own side of the argument. She defended the KIPP and Success Academy model — strict uniforms, silent transitions in the hallways, demerit systems, required eye-tracking during lessons, public correction of small infractions — as a form of liberation for the students it serves, on the grounds that it teaches low-income children the habits and self-command that wealthier families pass down privately. I found myself insisting that the whole apparatus was Foucauldian, but I could not make the mapping precise, and the argument collapsed into slogans. I want to work out the theoretical question properly, whatever it implies about the policy question.

Take the no-excuses model as it actually operates and analyze it through Foucault's account of disciplinary power. Be rigorous about the mapping: what corresponds to the partitioning of space and the timetable, to hierarchical observation, to normalization and the micro-penalty, to the examination; where the panoptic structure sits and who observes whom; and what kind of subject the system is designed to produce. Then run the analysis in the other direction and say what the case does to the theory. Which features of the no-excuses classroom does Foucault's account illuminate, and which does it distort or simply leave unaddressed? Does the case confirm the idea that discipline is a technology for producing self-monitoring subjects, or does it complicate the claim that disciplinary power is content-neutral and indifferent to the interests of those subjected to it — can a regime be disciplinary in Foucault's sense while still being genuinely in a student's interest? I want you to commit to a definite reading and defend it, with reasons, rather than laying out interpretive options for me to choose among. I am less interested in whether these schools are good policy than in whether Foucault's machinery, applied carefully, actually illuminates what goes on inside them.
