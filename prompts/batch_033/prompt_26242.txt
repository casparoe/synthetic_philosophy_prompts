I'm running a reading group for philosophy grad students, and next week we're on moral testimony — the debate about whether you can legitimately form moral beliefs just by taking someone else's word for it. Instead of having everyone read papers and then discuss, I want to kick things off with a short dialogue I'll write (or have them read aloud) between an optimist and a pessimist about moral testimony, and then use that to launch discussion.

Write me that dialogue. Both characters should be sharp and take each other seriously — no strawmen. The pessimist should press the idea that moral understanding requires working things out for yourself, that there's something deferential or agency-undermining about outsourcing your moral views to someone else. The optimist should push back hard: we defer to experts in every other domain, why would morality be special, and anyway maybe it's arrogant to insist on figuring everything out yourself when others know better. I want real back-and-forth where they respond to each other's actual moves, not just trading speeches. Maybe four or five exchanges, ending somewhere productive but unresolved — the point is to surface the tensions, not settle them.
