I'm applying for a six-month, one-course-release research fellowship and need a one-page proposal written. The topic I want funded: when human activity destroys or degrades a habitat, should the resulting effects on wild animal welfare change our moral evaluation of that activity, and if so, in which direction? The existing debate seems deadlocked. Some argue intact, high-productivity habitats are better because they sustain many lives; others argue they are morally worse because the lives they sustain contain pervasive predation, parasitism, starvation, and disease. My half-formed suspicion is that this is not a dispute more ecological data will settle. It turns instead on how one compares two counterfactual populations: a dense, high-suffering one and a sparse, low-suffering one. That raises a philosophical problem about whether a state of affairs can be better or worse without being better or worse for any particular individual, and about whether death should be counted as the loss of a life that would have been worth living, as the end of a life that would on balance have been negative, or as something that resists aggregation across a population. I would like the proposal to convert this into a determinate question: what exactly does a claim about the net welfare of an ecosystem assert, and under what conditions would habitat destruction count as reducing wild animal suffering? Please write the proposal with a precise statement of the question, an account of why the existing positions leave it open, a method indicating what I would actually do month by month and why a philosophical rather than an ecological approach is needed, an explicit statement of what would count as the project failing, and a realistic scope for six months. Do not include citations or a bibliography; I will add those myself.
