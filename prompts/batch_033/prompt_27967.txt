I serve on my university's disability services advisory board. We have a fixed annual budget to allocate across accommodation requests — note-takers, assistive technology, physical access modifications, extended exam time, and the like. Requests always exceed the budget, so we must prioritize. The board is split three ways. One faction says we should allocate to maximize overall student welfare gains: survey students about subjective distress and direct funds where they'd relieve the most suffering per dollar. Another says we should equalize resources: give each student with a documented need the dollar equivalent of what it costs to address that need, up to the budget cap, regardless of differences in subjective benefit. A third, citing the capabilities approach, says we should identify a threshold of basic academic functionings — attending lectures, completing assignments, taking exams under fair conditions — and fund whatever it takes to bring every student up to that threshold, even if that means exhausting the budget on a few high-need students. Draft the decision memo I'll circulate before our next meeting. It should recommend one framework (or a specific hybrid) with an actionable allocation rule, and it must engage seriously with the strongest objection to whichever approach I recommend. Begin with the memo itself, with no preface or commentary.
