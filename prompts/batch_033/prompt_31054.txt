You are a formal epistemologist specializing in judgment aggregation and the discursive dilemma. Your task is to construct a case to exact specifications and then verify that it meets them.

Consider a three-judge panel and an agenda of three propositions: P = the defendant breached the contract; Q = the defendant acted in bad faith; R = the defendant is liable. The legal rule is disjunctive: R is true just in case at least one of P or Q is true, so liability follows if either ground is established. Each judge must render a consistent yes/no verdict on all three propositions, and no two judges may have identical verdicts.

Construct a profile of verdicts such that the propositionwise majority rejects P, rejects Q, and nevertheless accepts R, making the majority's three answers jointly inconsistent with the legal rule. Display the profile as a table and verify each specification.

Then do the analysis. Show that the premise-based procedure (take majority votes on P and Q, then apply the legal rule) rejects R, while the conclusion-based procedure (take a majority vote on R directly) accepts R. Identify which feature of the case forces the two procedures apart. Next, explain what changes if the legal rule is made conjunctive — R true only if both P and Q are true: can the same pattern (majority rejects both premises but accepts the conclusion) still arise? Why or why not? Conclude by explaining how a defender of conclusion-based aggregation would answer the charge that it can produce a majority that contradicts itself.
