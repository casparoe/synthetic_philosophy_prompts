I spent my career as an evolutionary biologist, and I have recently been reading G. E. Moore's *Principia Ethica*. I am struck by his claim that the greatest intrinsic goods are the pleasures of human intercourse (by which he means friendship and affection) and the enjoyment of beautiful objects. He argues that these are good in themselves, not merely good as means to something else, and he develops the idea of organic unities — wholes whose value is not the sum of the values of their parts — to explain how consciousness and its objects combine to produce value.

I would like you to restate Moore's position on beauty and friendship using the vocabulary of evolutionary biology: inclusive fitness, proximate and ultimate causation, sexual selection, signaling theory, kin selection, reciprocal altruism, byproducts, mismatch, and whatever else from the evolutionary toolkit seems appropriate. Translate each of his main claims as faithfully as you can into that framework. Then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression at all in evolutionary terms. I am particularly interested in whether Moore's concept of intrinsic goodness has any counterpart in evolutionary biology, and whether the failure of translation tells us something about the limits of the evolutionary framework or about the coherence of Moore's claims. Please write in continuous prose only, as you would in a letter — no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
