After a close relative suffered a stroke, I began thinking hard about what it means for experience to be "in" the brain, and I keep encountering the same picture: consciousness as an inner theater. There is a stage on which sensory content appears, a spotlight of attention that selects one item at a time, and a spectator—the self—who watches the performance and narrates what it sees. People use this picture to argue that there must be some place in the brain where all the pieces are brought together and presented to the self, because otherwise who is watching the show? I have also read that the multiple drafts model is meant to replace this picture, and that the theater leads us astray. I would like an audit of the analogy. What corresponds to the stage, the spotlight, and the audience? Where does the mapping break down? Which of those failures actually matter for the claim that there is a central place, or a single self, to which experience is presented—and does that conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table? If some other analogy does better, I would want to know that as well.
