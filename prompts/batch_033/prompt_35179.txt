I don't get what the sphex wasp is supposed to show about free will. The standard example: the wasp paralyzes a cricket, drags it to its burrow, leaves it at the entrance while it goes inside to check, then comes back out to drag it in. If you move the cricket a few inches while the wasp is inside, it comes out, drags the cricket back to the entrance, and checks again—and it'll do this over and over if you keep moving the prey. People use this to illustrate sphexishness, meaning rigid, mechanical behavior. But the wasp is clearly responding to the world: the cricket isn't where it left it, so it redoes the final step. That seems flexible, not rigid—just inefficient. What exactly is supposed to be missing in the wasp, and how does that connect to the kind of flexibilty humans have?
