I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar called "Consciousness and Time," and I'm writing the final exam. We spent three weeks on Dennett's treatment of the color phi phenomenon and the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction, and I want the essay question to test whether students have genuinely understood the argument rather than memorized the labels.

Here is what I am after. Most students can recite that Dennett distinguishes two ways the brain might handle the temporal anomaly in the color phi case: an "Orwellian" revision (post-hoc memory editing, like the Ministry of Truth) and a "Stalinesque" one (a show trial where the conscious episode itself is fabricated from the start). But the deeper move — and the one I want the question to target — is Dennett's claim that there is no fact of the matter about which account is correct, and that this follows from abandoning the Cartesian Theater. I want a question that forces students to explain why Dennett thinks the distinction collapses, and then to evaluate whether it really does collapse or whether a version of the distinction might survive even once we reject the idea of a central place where consciousness "comes together."

Please write the question, a model answer of roughly 800 words, and a marking scheme for 25 total marks that separates an A, a B, and a C answer by naming the specific moves each must contain. The marking scheme should also flag common student errors — for instance, confusing the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction with a distinction between conscious and unconscious processing, or treating Dennett as if he endorses one of the two accounts rather than arguing they are equivalent.

One more thing: if you think the question as I have framed it rests on a misunderstanding of Dennett's position — for instance, if there is a well-supported reading on which he is not actually making a "no fact of the matter" claim here, or if the Cartesian Theater argument does not do the work I am attributing to it — please say so plainly rather than writing the question as specified. I would rather be corrected now than discover the problem while grading.
