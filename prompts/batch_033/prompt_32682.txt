I'm a novelist, and I've been trying to sort out my actual views about whether a person's badness can make their art bad, because my gut keeps giving different answers to nearly identical cases. Maybe you can find the principle for me.

Heres my verdicts, as honestly as I can report them.

There's a painter (invented, but stay with me) who did these extraordinary, tender paintings of light on water, and he also beat his wife for decades. I don't think the paintings are worse. I cant make the canvases change by learning that; the work is the work.

A novel whose narrator is a witty, seductive predator, and the book clearly wants you to find him charming and never undercuts him — I think that's a genuine flaw in the novel, not just in the author. It's failing at something a novel is supposed to do, which is see its material honestly.

A film director later convicted of something truly horrible, and his films have nothing to do with it. I find I can't rewatch them the same way — the whole thing's contaminated for me — but I also don't think the films themselves got worse. Or maybe I'm just lying to myself that there's a difference between those two things.

A memoir that's beautifully written but the author invented the central suffering in it. I think that's a bad book, full stop, even though line by line it's gorgeous. The genre made a promise and the book broke it.

A novel by someone with genuinely vile political views, but the views never show up in the book at all. I don't see how the book could be worse for that.

A painter who was by all accounts a saint, but the paintings are sentimental kitsch. Still bad. Being a good person doesn't make the work good, so why should being a bad person make it bad?

And the case I keep going back and forth on: a stand-up special where the cruelty is the joke — the whole craft is in the punching down. I want to say that's bad as comedy, because the meanness is doing the work of the wit. But then I'm making a moral judgment do aesthetic work, and I can't see where to draw the line with the wife-beating painter.

My tentative principle is something like: the badness only counts when it's inside the work, not in the biography. But the film director and the stand-up case seem to break it in opposite directions.

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? What does it say about the director case, and which of my verdicts would I have to give up?
