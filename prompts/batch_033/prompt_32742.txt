I'm running a grad seminar on individuation and the identity of indiscernibles, and I want a handout that sorts students into a position on Black's two-sphere universe before they've done the reading. Could you write me a decision tree (or flowchart) that starts from their gut reaction to the case and ends at a named position on what individuates qualitatively identical objects? Open with this case: a universe containing nothing but two iron spheres, exactly alike in every intrinsic respect — same size, mass, composition, temperature, color, everything — sitting one mile apart. First split should be whether the student thinks there really are two spheres, or whether they think the 'two' is a misdescription and there's really only one. Then branch on claims like: whether being at different places in space counts as a difference in properties, whether distinctness needs some ground or can just be brute, whether each sphere has a primitive thisness, and whether its false or just not fully meaningful to describe the world as containing two spheres. Somewhere in the tree, use a swap variant too: while the student sleeps, the two spheres trade places; ask whether that's a real change or not. Leaves I'd like: bundle theory with the PII as a substantive necessary truth (one sphere), substantivalism about space (two spheres, individuated by absolute places), haecceitism (two spheres, primitive thisness), brute distinctness (two spheres, no ground), and the verificationist-ish line that the description is incoherent. Add any natural extra leaves you think belong, but cap it at seven or so. Phrase each branch question so a student can answer it without knowing which position it leads to — no asking 'do you believe in haecceities?', ask about the case. Then flag the pairs of leaves your tree can't really separate, and note any combinations of answers in the logical space that have no leaf and whether they're empty because incoherent or just unexplored. I care more that the tree comes from the questions and the space of answers than from a list of -isms, so don't build it by starting from the names.
