I am a novelist, not a professional philosopher. While researching a book whose protagonist is a physicist gradually forced to give up every picture of matter as made of little things, I have found myself persuaded, more than I expected, by ontic structural realism: the view that what there is, at the deepest level, is structure, and that the apparent relata of physical relations are not fundamental objects standing outside the web of relations but nodes or aspects of that web. I want to write the opposing case honestly, so I am asking you to tell me what would count against the position, not as a caricature but as a map of its real exposure.

Treat the view as a live target and make the needed distinctions: epistemic structural realism, on which we can know structure but not the nature of the things so structured, versus ontic structural realism, on which structure is all there is; and within ontic structural realism, the eliminative version versus the more moderate priority-based version, since a defeater for one may leave another standing. Then identify and rank what should move a committed structural realist, from fatal to merely awkward. Include at least these: the Newman-style triviality worry about whether a purely structural claim has enough content; the charge that relations without relata are incoherent, or that the view covertly turns relations into a new kind of relata; the observation that quantum mechanics, the usual source of support, underdetermines any metaphysical reading and can also be read as licensing only a modest epistemic structuralism; and the suspicion that ontic structural realism, pressed far enough, collapses into a form of empiricism or into a bundle theory under another name. For each, say whether it is a genuine defeater, a repairable difficulty, or a bullet the view knowingly bites, and say what a defender would concede rather than dismiss. Also tell me what, if anything, the view predicts or explains that its rivals do not, and whether that gives it any empirical purchase.

I would also like your honest judgment about whether the view has been insulated: whether its defenders have qualified it until it makes no empirical or explanatory difference, and if so whether that is a defect in a metaphysical position of this kind or an acceptable cost of doing metaphysics. Finally, tell me which consideration would move you most if you held the view, and where in a novel its weakest point would most naturally be dramatized.
