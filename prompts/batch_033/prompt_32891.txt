We've been hashing through these self-locating belief puzzles and I've been keeping track of his takes. Here's what he's said so far:

- Sailor's Child problem: Sailor flips a coin, heads he has one kid, tails he has two. You're his kid. He says your credence in heads should be 1/2.
- God's coin toss: God flips a coin, heads he makes one person, tails he makes two. You wake up in a room not knowing which person you are. He says credence in heads is 1/2.
- The Presumptuous Philosopher: Two theories, one with way more observers, equally good on the non-indexical evidence. He says you shouldn't update toward the theory with more observers.
- The Doomsday Argument: He says you should update toward doom (shorter future) based on your birth rank.

He hasn't told me what he thinks about the plain old Sleeping Beauty problem yet, and I want to guess before I ask him. The way I see it, he's running some kind of self-sampling assuption—he's treating himself as randomly selected from the set of actual observers, not from all possible observers. But Sleeping Beauty is the same observer waking up multiple times, not different observers. So does he stick with the same reference class (observers, not observer-moments) and say 1/2? Or does he shift to counting awakenings and say 1/3?

I want a committed prediction—don't just lay out the options, tell me what you think he'd say and why. And if you can think of a follow-up case that would really nail down what rule he's running, I'd love to hear it.
