For a term paper in my biology class I am looking at whether species picks out something real in nature or is just a classification we impose, and I have noticed that every species concept seems designed around a few favorite cases and quietly fails on others. Before I judge any particular definition, I want a full specification of what a definition of species is even supposed to accomplish. Please lay out the desiderata: the cases any adequate account must handle or explain why it cannot, such as asexual organisms, ring species, natural hybrids, populations known only from fossils, and bacteria with extensive horizontal gene transfer; the distinctions it must respect, including the difference between the species category and particular species taxa, and the difference between what makes something a species and how we identify species in practice; and the constraints from evolutionary biology it may not contradict. For each requirement, give the case or problem that motivates it, and indicate which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one school. Also flag any pairs of desiderata that look jointly unsatisfiable, and say which single requirement does the most to eliminate the leading species concepts. A brief scorecard for the biological, phylogenetic, and ecological species concepts is fine, but keep the emphasis on the criteria themselves.
