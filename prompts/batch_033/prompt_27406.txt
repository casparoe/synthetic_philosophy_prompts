Please argue that the obfuscated-arguments problem is fatal to the AI-safety-via-debate proposal, not just a practical hurdle. Here's the setup I have in mind. The debate idea (roughly Irving/Christiano/Amodei) says: we have two AIs debate a question in front of a human judge, and even if the judge can't evaluate the arguments from scratch, the honest AI will expose flaws in the dishonest one's reasoning, so truth wins. The worry is that a dishonest AI can construct arguments that are deliberately hard to untangle — not by making them complex in a way the topic genuinely requires, but by adding indirection, exploiting the judge's known biases, burying the weakest inferential step under a mountain of true-but-irrelevant detail, using true premises to gesture at false conclusions without quite asserting them, that kind of thing. The honest AI can try to say "this is obfuscated," but that's just another claim the judge has to evaluate, and the obfuscating AI can obfuscate at the meta-level too. So it looks like the debate setup smuggles in the assumption that the judge is robust to obfuscation — but the whole reason we're using debate in the first place is that we don't trust unaided humans to evaluate these arguments. I want you to argue that this is a structural problem: for any judge with bounded cognitive resources, there exists an obfuscation strategy that lets falsehoods win debates. And then, after making the strongest case you can, tell me whether you think theres actually a way out or whether this really does kill the proposal.
