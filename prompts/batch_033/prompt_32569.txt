I'm an economist, I live in utility functions over states of the world. Some states have people in them, some don't. Restate Benatar's asymmetry — the one where the absence of pain is good even if nobody's around to enjoy it, but the absence of pleasure isn't bad unless someone's actually deprived — in my vocabulary. States, outcomes, utility assignments, the domain of the utility function, counterfactuals about who exists. As faithful as you can.

Then the residue report. Which bits of the asymmetry translate cleanly into constraints on a utility function? Which bits force you to fudge — like assigning utility to "absent" beings, or treating nonexistence as a state-with-a-value? And which bits just have no expression at all? I want to know whether the asymmetry is a real structural feature my framework is blind to, or whether it evaporates once you get strict about whose utility your measuring in which state.
