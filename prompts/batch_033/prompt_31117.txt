Write a dialogue between two people who disagree about whether weakness of will is best understood as a form of hyperbolic discounting. One of them holds that what we call akrasia is really just time-inconsistency: the person at t1 cares a lot about the present, so a smaller sooner reward (the snooze button, the second slice) outweighs a larger later good when it's close in time, and then the later self looks back and calls it weakness. On this view there's no single moment where the agent judges one thing and does another; there are just successive selves with different discount rates. The other replies that this misses akrasia proper, which is synchronic: you can judge right now, all things considered, that you shouldn't take the second slice, and take it anyway, with no gap between judgement and action and no shift in your evaluation. That isn't a preference reversal over time; it's a failure to act on the judgement you hold at the very moment you act.

Have them work through the snooze-button case, the cake case, and the case of someone who pays for a commitment device (e.g. an app that blocks a website) in detail, and have them push each other on what each view says about self-control, regret, and whether the akratic person is criticisable. Neither should be a strawman: each should have sharp replies to the other's strongest points, and they should respond to what the other actually says rather than talk past each other. By the end they should have narrowed down where their arguement really lies, even if neither has convinced the other.
