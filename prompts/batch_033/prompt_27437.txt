I've come around to the view that religion is best explained as a natural phenomenon — not as a response to anything supernatural, but as a byproduct of ordinary cognitive machinery that evolved for other purposes. The basic story: we've got hypersensitive agency detection (better to mistake a rustle for a predator than miss the real one), theory of mind that overapplies (we intuit minds behind events), and social learning biases that make us credulous toward minimally counterintuitive concepts that spread well. Throw in some group-level selection dynamics and you've got a pretty complete naturalistic account. I find this compelling and it seems like the default scientific stance.

But I want to know what would count against it. Not just theological objections — I mean, what evidence or arguments should actually move someone who holds this view? What would a world look like where this naturalistic story is false, or at least importantly incomplete? I want the defeaters ranked from "this would basically overturn the whole framework" to "this is awkward but you can patch around it." And I'd like an honest assessment of whether the view, as typically defended, has been insulated against falsification — and if so, whether that's a problem for a view that presents itself as empirical.
