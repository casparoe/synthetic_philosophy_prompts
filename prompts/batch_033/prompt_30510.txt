I'm on the steering committee of a consciousness research center that's allocating this year's seed grants, and we've hit a wall. Several proposals use heterophenomenology — you know, the Dennett-style method where you treat subjects' first-person reports as a text to be interpreted and explained by the scientist, rather than as authoritative descriptions of what their experience is actually like. The committee's split right down the middle. One camp thinks this is the only method that doesn't sneak Cartesian assumptions into the lab; the other camp says it's just behaviorism with extra steps — that by treating reports as something to be explained rather than believed, you've rigged the game so that subjects can never be taken at their word about consciousness, and the method can't in principle discover that a first-person report was accurate all along. I need to draft a short decision memo: fund, don't fund, or fund with conditions. Take the strongest version of each side's worry seriously — not just a diplomatic split-the-difference — and give us something we can actually vote on, with criteria clear enough to apply to future cycles too. What do you recommend?
