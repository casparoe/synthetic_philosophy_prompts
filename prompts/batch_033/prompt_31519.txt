I've been volunteering at a natural history museum, helping sort a backlog of specimens and artifacts, and I keep having to decide whether the stuff in a tray is one object or many. It's made me realize I have strong but weirdly specific intuitions about when a bunch of things compose a further thing — the special composition question — and I want to know what principle, if any, is behind them.

Here are my verdicts.

A hammer, handle and head firmly attached: one object. The same handle and head lying seperately on a workbench: two objects, no third thing.

A pile of sand: one object. The same grains scattered by wind across a mile of beach: no object, just many grains.

A living cat: one object, and its molecules are parts of it. The same cat a day after it died: still one object — the corpse is a thing. The ashes after cremation, scattered: no object.

A rock: one object. A rock and the moon: two objects, no scattered object composed of both. But the solar system — sun, planets, moons, asteroids — I want to say is one object, which feels like it contradicts the rock-and-moon verdict and I cant say why.

A deck of 52 playing cards, stacked in its box: one object. The same 52 cards mixed into a shoebox with 500 other cards: no object composed of just those 52.

Two people holding hands: no object composed of the two of them.

I know the big positions: universalism says any things whatsoever compose something, nihilism says there are no composite objects at all. Both feel crazy to me, so I'm clearly a restricted-composition person. But restricted by what? My verdicts don't obviously line up with only living things (the corpse bothers that), or with causal integration (the pile of sand bothers that), or with parts arranged to serve a function (the rock bothers that). My working hunch is something about the parts being arranged so they interact in a stable way, but the pile of sand and the rock-and-moon both cause trouble for that too.

What principle, or small set of candidate principles, best captures these verdicts? Which of my verdicts does it force me to give up? And what does it say about cases I haven't made up my mind on: a forest, a swarm of bees, a mosaic on a wall, the letters that spell a word on a page?
