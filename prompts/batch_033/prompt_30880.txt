ok so my friend is obsessed with this idea that all creativity is basically variations on a theme, like you take something and turn knobs on it until it becomes something new. and she has super specific verdicts about which variations count as actually creative and which are just knob-twiddling. ive been trying to reverse-engineer the rule shes running because shes weirdly consistent.

here are her verdicts so far:

- a dj who speeds a song up by 20% and changes nothing else: not creative, she says thats just the tempo knob.
- a painter who does the same chair like 30 times with slightly different colors: not creative, just the color knob.
- transposing a melody into a new key and calling it an arrangement: not creative.
- a cover that changes the key and the tempo but nothing else: not creative, she says its still just the obvious knobs.
- but a cover that changes the key, the tempo, the instrumentation, and also does this thing where the gaps of silence between phrases keep stretching out in a weird pattern: creative. and she specifically points at the silence thing, like thats the knob nobody thinks to turn.

next week shes going to hear a version of a song that is identical to the original except for one thing: the performer only varies those gaps of silence between phrases, in a really deliberate pattern. same notes, same tempo, same key, same instruments, no added harmonies, nothing else changed at all.

what would she say about that one? im less interested in the actual yes/no than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her verdicts just as well, and which case would best tell them apart.
