The debate between higher-order and first-order theories of consciousness has been running for decades without a clear resolution. Higher-order theorists maintain that a mental state is conscious only if the subject is aware of that state in some further way — paradigmatically, via a higher-order thought or a higher-order perception directed at it. First-order theorists maintain that consciousness is an intrinsic property of certain representational states themselves, requiring no additional state that takes them as an object. Both sides draw on the same empirical findings — blindsight, change blindness, binocular rivalry — and both sides can construct cases where a state seems to meet one theory's conditions for consciousness while failing the other's. Yet neither camp has moved. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is, not a verdict on who is right. Is it a verbal dispute, where one side uses "consciousness" to pick out introspective access and the other uses it to pick out qualitative character? Is it a disagreement about what counts as an adequate explanation — about the explanatory desiderata themselves? Is there a hidden empirical question that, if resolved, would break the impasse? Or is it something else entirely? And given your diagnosis, what, if anything, would constitute progress?
