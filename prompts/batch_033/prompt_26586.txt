I keep noticing a pattern in moral discourse that I don't have a name for, and I'm hoping you can help me identify it—or, if nothing established fits, help me carve it out from nearby concepts and give it a precise definition.

The pattern is this: Person A blames Person B for some wrongdoing. Person B responds not by denying the wrong, but by pointing out that Person A has committed some *different* moral failing—not the same kind of thing, but something else that, in B's view, renders A unfit to blame anyone, or at least unfit to blame B. The claim is that A's moral record is sufficiently tarnished that they lack the standing to issue blame, even for wrongs they themselves have not committed.

Here are two instances that feel quite different in force:

(1) A friend criticizes me for being late to dinner, and I retort that they routinely gossip about mutual friends. The wrongs are unrelated—lateness and gossip—but I am treating their gossiping as disqualifying them from blaming me. This feels like a cheap deflection.

(2) A public figure who has been credibly accused of covering up workplace harassment gives a speech condemning another country's human rights record. Critics say he has no standing to lecture anyone about moral matters, given his own conduct. This feels as though it has more force, even though the wrongs are different in both kind and scale, and even though the condemnation of the human rights record might be entirely correct.

I know this is not the standard hypocrisy-undermines-standing case, because the blamer has not done the *same* thing. It is also not quite the tu quoque fallacy, which is about inconsistency between one's own behaviour and one's blame. And it is not mere whataboutism, which is usually a deflection tactic that does not specifically invoke the idea of standing. It seems closer to something like a "clean hands" requirement for blaming, but that phrase usually implies complicity in the very same wrong, or at least in the kind of wrong being blamed.

My question is: is there an established term for this specific move—challenging someone's standing to blame on the basis of a *different* moral failing? And if there is not, can you help me think through whether such challenges ever have genuine normative force, and what distinguishes the cases where they do from the cases where they do not? I am less interested in the label itself than in the taxonomy: what exactly is the phenomenon, how does it relate to the better-known concepts I have mentioned, and where are its boundaries and edge cases?
