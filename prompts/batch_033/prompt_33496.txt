Analyze the US war in Afghanistan (2001–2021) through the lens of the dollar auction / escalation of commitment. I know the basic idea—two bidders keep raising their stakes because pulling out means losing everything they've already sunk in, even though the rational move was never to enter the auction past a certain point. What I want is a careful mapping: who are the players in the auction (is it the US vs. the Taliban? the US vs. its own past self? something else?), what count as the bids and the payoffs, what the "withdrawal" option actually looked like at different moments, and whether the model captures the dynamics of the surge, the counterinsurgency strategy, and the eventual collapse in 2021. Then give me an honest account of what the dollar auction model misses or distorts—for instance, does it handle the fact that different administrations had different goals, or that the "auction" lasted twenty years with shifting domestic audiences and shifting rationales? And finally, what does the Afghan case show about escalation of commitment as a theory—does it confirm it, complicate it, or reveal that the model was never really built for something this sprawling?
