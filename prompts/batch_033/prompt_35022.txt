In 1974 Karl Popper concluded that Darwinism was not a testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research program: a framework that can guide the formulation of testable hypotheses without itself being empirically refutable. Four years later he announced that he had changed his mind and that natural selection is testable after all. Analyze this episode as a case study for Popper's own criterion of demarcation. First, spell out exactly what the criterion requires of an empirical scientific theory, and reconstruct the strongest version of the 1974 charge: what would natural selection have to be like for it to fail the test? Then reconstruct the 1978 recantation: what had to change, in the theory or in the notion of testability, for the same criterion now to pass it? Assess whether the recantation genuinely follows from the criterion or whether it requires weakening it, for instance by treating testability as a matter of degree, by admitting single-case or historical predictions, or by lowering the standard from refutability to disconfirmability. Finally, say what the episode shows about the demarcation criterion itself: does it show that the criterion misclassifies historical sciences, that its official statement was always more elastic than it looked, or that the episode is not a genuine anomaly? Where the criterion has nothing to say about Darwinism, say so rather than forcing it to speak. Write the answer in American English.
