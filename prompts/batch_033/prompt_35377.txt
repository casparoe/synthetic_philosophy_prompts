I retired a few years ago from a career in geochemistry and have been slowly working my way into philosophy of science, lately Ian Hacking's stuff on psychiatric classification. I'm drafting a short essay for a reading group and would be grateful for comments before I inflict it on them. The working title is 'Why My Feldspar Doesn't Talk Back.' The basic idea: in geochemistry, a classification like granodiorite picks out what Hacking calls an indifferent kind — the rock neither knows nor cares how its classified. Psychiatric categories like ADHD or dissociative identity disorder are different. The people classified respond to the classification, come to understand themselves through it, present to clinicians differently, and eventually the diagnostic criteria get revised partly in response to the people the earlier criteria helped shape. So the kind loops back on itself.

I'm trying to make three points, and I'd especially like comments on whether I'm getting Hacking right or just reading my own scientific habits into him.

1. The looping effect is not just a feedback loop at the level of individual behavior, like a self-fulfilling prophecy. It operates at the level of the kind itself: what it is to be a person of that kind changes over time.
2. Hacking's dynamic nominalism (am I using that term right?) isn't the same as old-fashioned social constructionism. He isn't saying the categories are unreal or arbitrary, just that they have a historical trajectory that rocks don't have.
3. Here's where I'm stuck. Hacking develops this for human kinds. Do natural kinds ever loop? Once we started classifying certain rocks as granite, did geology change the population of granites? I want to say no. But consider invasive species: we classify a species as invasive, and then management practices change its distribution, which in turn changes what invasive means in practice. Is that a loop in Hacking's sense, or is it missing the self-interpretation part?

And a broader worry: if looping makes human kinds unstable in this way, what's left of psychiatric diagnosis? I can't tell whether Hacking thinks a disorder like schizophrenia is still real, or whether I'm just smuggling in a geochemist's picture of what real has to mean.

Any comments are welcome, but I'd particularly appreciate pushback on points 1 and 2 if I've garbled the distinction between individual-level feedback and kind-level looping, and on the schizophrenia worry.
