I chair the board of a private foundation that funds education research, and we must decide whether to fund a proposal for a large genome-wide association study of educational attainment. One faction opposes funding on the grounds that educational attainment is already known to be substantially heritable, and that funding the study would lend credibility to the view that differences in educational outcomes are largely fixed by genes and that school-based interventions can have only limited effect. Another faction replies that heritability is a legitimate, well-established statistical concept, and that refusing to fund the study on these grounds amounts to suppressing inconvenient findings. Please draft the decision memo I will circulate before next week's board meeting. It should recommend a single course of action — fund, reject, or fund with conditions — and should make the reasoning about heritability precise enough that both factions can see exactly what heritability does and does not imply. It should also state honestly the strongest consideration favoring the side whose position the memo does not adopt.
