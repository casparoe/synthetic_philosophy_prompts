ok so I've been reading a bit of pragmatics lately — just the SEP, some intro stuff — after a recurring fight with my partner made me realize I don't actually understand how much of what I say is "in" the words and how much is between them. Here's the thing I keep noticing and can't find a good name for.

Take a sentence like "I ate some of the cookies." Everyone instantly hears that you didn't eat all of them. Textbook scalar implicature, right? But here's what bugs me: it doesn't feel like an inference. Nobody actually runs through the steps — "if he'd eaten all of them he'd have said so, he's being cooperative, so he must mean some-but-not-all." It just hits you, immediately, like a second meaning baked into the word "some" itself. And if you try to cancel it — "I ate some of the cookies, in fact I ate all of them" — it sounds weird, almost contradictory, in a way that canceling a fresh implicature doesn't. Compare: "She's warm, but I don't mean to imply she's not kind." That's a real implicature getting canceled and it feels totally fine.

So it's like there's this middle zone between "part of the literal meaning" and "calculated on the fly from the cooperative principle" — a kind of implicature that's gotten so routinized it's practically fossilized. Is there an established term for this exact thing, and how is it supposed to be different from plain ambiguity on one side and a fresh conversational implicature on the other? Don't just give me a label — I want to know what the actual difference is supposed to consist in and where the boundary cases live.
