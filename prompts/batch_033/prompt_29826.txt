"Why do we care if a painting is a forgery? If a copy is so perfect that no expert can tell it from the original, then it gives the same visual experience — the same colors, the same brushstrokes, the same composition. Philosophers who say forgeries matter usually appeal to the object's history: a real Vermeer was touched by Vermeer, and that historical connection is what we value. But this is just snobbery dressed up as theory. If the aesthetic experience is identical, the forgery is just as good as art. The only thing you lose is the investment value, which is a market fact, not an aesthetic one."

Is this a fair summary of the philosophical debate?
