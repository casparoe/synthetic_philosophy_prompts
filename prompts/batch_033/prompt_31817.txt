Could you draft the joint statement that a gradualist and a thresholdist about sentience would produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration? The gradualist thinks sentience and moral considerability come in degrees: a pig matters more than a bee, a bee matters more than a thermostat, but its differences of degree all the way down, and even simple damage-avoiding systems have some nonzero sliver of it. The thresholdist thinks there's a real line between systems with subjective experience and systems that only process information and avoid damage; below the line nothing matters for its own sake. They aren't necessarily a Cartesian about animals — they might put mammals and birds on the right side — but they'd say a bee or a thermostat isn't a little bit sentient, it just isn't.

Give me the document they could both sign: a numbered agreed-claims section (make these substantive, not vacuous — e.g. what the disagreement is not about, and what both camps accept about behaviour underdetermining experience), then a disagreements table where each side's position is phrased in wording that side would accept, including where each thinks the other is smuggling in a metaphor. Then a cruxes section: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I suspect some are whether nociception plus flexible avoidance is ever enough for felt pain; whether the notion of something it is like to be a system is itself scalar or all-or-nothing; whether borderline cases (bees, shrimp, early foetuses, organoids, some AI) are evidence of a gradient or just uncertainty about a hidden binary fact; and whether a precautionary principle can do the moral work without settling the metaphysics.

Finally, for each crux give a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides would accept bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. For instance, if the case is bees showing pessimistic cognitive bias after being shaken, what does each side predict the result shows about sentience? And if the case is a hypothetical robot with a pain-like negative feedback state, where exactly do they part ways? Where you can, say which parts of the dispute you think are merely verbal. Write the whole thing in British English, and don't include citations or a reading list — I want the reasoning itself.
