My opening challenge: You claim that war preserves the ethical health of a people much as the blowing of the winds keeps the sea from the foulness of a prolonged calm. But the wars of your own lifetime, especially the Napoleonic wars with their mass conscription and enormous casualties, seem to show that this "rational moment" is just a metaphysical way of blessing pointless slaughter. How can you maintain that the actual destruction — the deaths of tens of thousands of particular human beings — is rational, without thereby implying that every one of those deaths is justified?
