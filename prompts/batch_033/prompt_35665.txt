I've been going back and forth on this stuff about self-locating beliefs — how to reason when one of the things you're unsure about is which person you are, or how many people there are in total. I keep seeing the self-sampling assumption and the self-indication assumption (SSA and SIA) mentioned, but neither one has ever quite matched my gut across all the cases, so I want to just lay out my honest verdicts and ask you to find the principle behind them, if there is one.

Here are the cases. (1) God's coin toss: a god flips a fair coin; heads means he creates exactly one person in a room, tails means he creates ten people, each in there own identical room. You wake up in a room knowing the setup but not the coin. My gut says 50/50 on heads — the coin was fair and I'd wake up in a room either way, so being this particular person rather than another tells me nothing. (2) The presumptuous philosopher: two cosmological theories make all the same observable predictions, but T1 says the universe contains a hundred billion observers and T2 says it contains a hundred trillion. A philosopher says T2 is vastly more likely because our existence is more probable under a theory with more observers. My gut says that's nonsense; both theories predict someone like me exists, and how many other observers there are isn't evidence about which theory is true. (3) Doomsday: I learn I'm roughly the 60-billionth human ever born. Hypothesis A says humanity goes extinct soon, after maybe 100 billion total; hypothesis B says we spread through the galaxy and there will be trillions of future humans. The doomsday argument says A is much more likely, since if B were true it would be a crazy coincidence that I happen to be this early. And I feel the pull of that, even though it also feels like a trick — my gut says my birth rank is some evidence against B, but I can't say why, and part of me says my position in the sequence shouldn't tell me anything. (4) Sleeping Beauty: on Sunday a fair coin is flipped. Heads, she's woken Monday only. Tails, she's woken Monday and Tuesday with amnesia between, so the wakings feel identical. Each waking, what's her credence in heads? My gut says 1/2: the coin is fair and she knew in advance she'd wake up at least once, so waking gives no new information. Though I admit 1/3 has a pull.

The obvious problem is that (1) and (2) seem to say self-location is never evidence, while (3) seems to say it sometimes is. Is there a single principle — SSA, SIA, or something else — that gets all of these or close, and which of my verdicts would I have to overturn? And what does your preferred principle say about a coin toss where tails creates infinitely many people?
