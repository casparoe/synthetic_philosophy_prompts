I am preparing a discussion for an undergraduate seminar on Plato's moral psychology, and I want to pair the ring of Gyges passage from the Republic with H. G. Wells's The Invisible Man. Glaucon's challenge, as I understand it, is that once the fear of detection is removed, the unjust life is more rational than the just one: give a just person and an unjust person each the power of invisibility, and the just person will behave indistinguishably from the unjust person, because justice is practiced only under compulsion and for the sake of reputation. Wells's Griffin is often treated as a natural test case, since he becomes invisible and rapidly descends into theft, arson, and murder.

But I worry that this reading is too quick. Griffin is not simply a just man handed impunity: from early in the novel he is secretive, resentful, and already capable of stealing from his own father, and his invisibility is not only a license to act unseen but also a condition of radical isolation and bodily alienation. I would like a careful reading of the novel through Glaucon's challenge that does justice to both texts. Does Wells's story actually support the claim that an ordinary person given guaranteed impunity would throw off the restraints of justice, or does it show something narrower—that a particular kind of already antisocial character is unmade by invisibility, while the surrounding society remains conventionally moral? Does the novel locate the source of Griffin's corruption in impunity itself, in the disconnection of never being seen by others, or in the physical and psychological strain of invisibility? And does the novel push back against the Republic's framing by suggesting that being seen and answerable to others is not merely an external incentive for good behavior but partly constitutive of moral agency?

I would also be glad to know which episodes in the novel most reward close attention in class, and I would like the reading to end by saying what Wells's version of the thought experiment adds to the philosophical debate that Glaucon's abstract example leaves out.
