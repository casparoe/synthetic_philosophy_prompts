A philosophy article I read summarizes Quine's attack on the analytic–synthetic distinction as follows. Quine argues that analyticity can be defined only in terms of notions such as synonymy, meaning, or semantical rules, and that these notions are themselves no clearer than analyticity, so the distinction is circular and should be abandoned. From this he concludes that there are no analytic truths, and that every sentence, including the truths of logic and mathematics, is equally open to revision in light of experience. Mathematics therefore turns out to be just another empirical science, differing from physics only in how reluctant we currently are to give it up. I would like to know how much of this I can rely on. Please evaluate the summary claim by claim: which parts capture Quine's actual argument, which parts overstate or distort it, and whether the final claim about mathematics follows from the parts that are accurate.
