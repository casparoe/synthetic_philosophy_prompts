Since 2019, fertility providers in several countries have offered a service that goes beyond screening IVF embryos for monogenic disorders such as cystic fibrosis. Using genome-wide genotyping, they compute a polygenic risk score for each embryo — an aggregate estimate of genetic liability for conditions like schizophrenia, type 2 diabetes, and coronary artery disease, and in some offerings for traits such as predicted educational attainment. Prospective parents can use these rankings to choose which embryo to transfer. The practice is marketed as a way to give a future child the best odds of a healthy and flourishing life. It is commercially available in the United States with minimal regulation, restricted to serious disease in some other jurisdictions, and banned in yet others.

Treat this practice as a piece of applied philosophy. Your task is not to evaluate its scientific validity or its predictive accuracy, but to extract and examine the philosophical commitments it presupposes. In particular, address the following questions in the sections described below.

**Section 1: The implicit theory** (roughly 200 words)
Reconstruct the view of parental obligation, the value of traits, and the nature of disability that the practice seems to presuppose. Does it presuppose something like the principle of procreative beneficence — that prospective parents should select, from the possible children they could have, the one expected to have the best life? If so, what conception of a "better life" is built into the choice of which scores to report and select on? Does the practice presuppose that a life with schizophrenia or diabetes is straightforwardly worse in a way that licenses selection against it, and if so, what does that commit it to about the value of the lives of existing people with those conditions?

**Section 2: Strictly presupposed vs. merely compatible** (roughly 200 words)
Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with. For example, must the practice commit to a full welfarist or perfectionist theory of value, or is it compatible with several? Does it require that polygenic scores track something morally relevant about future well-being, or only that parents have a right to act on them? Identify the minimal commitments the practice cannot shed without becoming unintelligible as the practice it is.

**Section 3: The eugenics question** (roughly 150 words)
Critics charge that embryo selection on polygenic traits is eugenics. Proponents reply that it is liberal eugenics — driven by individual parental choice rather than state coercion, and aimed at welfare rather than racial or class hierarchy. What does the practice presuppose about whether this distinction matters? Does the liberal framing succeed in insulating the practice from the moral objections that attach to historical eugenics, or does the practice presuppose that those objections were about coercion and state power rather than about something deeper — the attitude expressed when we select against certain kinds of lives?

**Section 4: Underdetermination** (roughly 150 words)
Identify where the practice is philosophically underdetermined: points at which several rival theories would license it, or where the practice takes no stand. For instance, could the practice be endorsed by a pure autonomy view (parents may select for any trait they value), by a harm-prevention view (parents should prevent predictable suffering), and by a perfectionist view (some traits are objectively better), all at once? What does the practice not settle?

**Section 5: What would change under a rival view** (roughly 150 words)
Suppose we replaced the practice's implicit theory with the expressivist or disability-critique view: that selecting against embryos with a given trait expresses the judgment that lives with that trait are less worth living, and that this expressive content is itself a moral harm. How would the practice have to change? Would it have to stop altogether, restrict itself to lethal childhood conditions, or alter how it frames and markets its services? Be specific about what would differ.
