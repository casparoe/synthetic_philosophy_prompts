I recently started advising a union federation on automation and AI, and I keep coming back to a tension in Marx's writing. On one side, he analyzes value as socially necessary labor time; on the other, he sometimes suggests that once production becomes scientific and machine-based, labor time stops being an adequate measure of wealth. If Marx were alive today and given a detailed picture of contemporary AI—large language models, algorithmic management, warehouse robotics, and the automation of cognitive as well as physical tasks—what do you think he would predict? I am particularly interested in whether he would expect AI-driven automation to dissolve the value relation he described, or whether he would expect capital to keep reconstituting value by generating new forms of necessary labor. And what would he predict about the political consequences: the composition of the working class, the length and intensity of work, and the obstacles to redirecting productivity gains? I am less interested in whether he would approve of the technology than in which specific mechanisms from his account of machinery he would expect to operate, and where he would think that account needs revision once machines begin taking over coordination and judgment rather than just physical production.
