I'm in Year 12 and my philosophy teacher has set us an unusual assignment for half-term: instead of an essay, we're to write a short piece of fiction that dramatises a philosophical problem we've discussed in class. I've chosen to write about the gradual-replacement version of the mind-uploading thought experiment — the one where, rather than scanning your brain all at once, each neuron is replaced one by one with a functionally identical artificial substitute over the course of several months. The person stays conscious throughout, reports no change in experience, and at the end has a fully synthetic brain that behaves exactly as the original did. The question, of course, is whether the resulting person is still you, and if so, at what point during the process that stopped being true — or whether the whole question rests on a mistake.

I'd like to set the story in a near-future clinic where this procedure is offered as a treatment for a degenerative neurological condition. My protagonist is someone in their early twenties who has just been diagnosed and is weighing up whether to go through with it. The philosophical tension I want to explore isn't just the abstract identity question but something more specific: the protagonist's dawning realisation that the people around them — family, partner, doctors — seem to have already made up their minds about whether the post-procedure person will really be them, and that these judgements reveal more about the other people's theories of personhood than about any objective fact. I'd also like the story to touch on a subtler issue: if the protagonist goes ahead with the procedure, they will have continuous memories of deciding to do it, but a version of them that refused would also have continuous memories of having made that choice. What does that mean for whether the decision is genuinely theirs?

Could you write this story for me? Aim for about 1,500 to 2,000 words, and try to let the philosophical questions emerge through dialogue and situation rather than through narration that lectures the reader.
