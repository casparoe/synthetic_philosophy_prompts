My philosophy of language professor has spent the term on vagueness, and all her example verdicts concern a single sorites series for "heap," running from many grains down to one. Her record so far: she calls it true that 100,000 grains make a heap and false that one grain makes a heap; she accepts as true, even for the borderline count of 10,000 grains, "Either 10,000 grains make a heap or they do not"; she accepts as true "There is a number n such that n grains make a heap and n−1 grains do not," while denying that any such n can be identified; and she rejects as false "For every n, if n grains make a heap then n−1 grains make a heap." Next week she must rule on the claim that the sentence "10,000 grains make a heap" is either true or false. I am less interested in the bare prediction than in the theory she is running. Identify the two main candidate theories—epistemicism and supervaluationism—say what each predicts about this claim, give your best guess about her verdict with reasons, and name the single further case that would most decisively settle which theory she holds.
