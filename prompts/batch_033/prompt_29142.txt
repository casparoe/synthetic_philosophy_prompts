I recently got into an argument with a friend who summarized Kripke's Naming and Necessity after reading a secondary source and listening to a philosophy podcast. They gave me what they claimed was the standard picture, and I wrote down their summary as faithfully as I could:

"Kripke's central insight was that proper names are rigid designators — they refer to the same individual in every possible world where that individual exists. So 'Aristotle' picks out Aristotle in every possible world, while 'the teacher of Alexander' is non-rigid because it picks out whoever taught Alexander, and that differs from world to world. From this, Kripke showed that there are necessary truths we can only know a posteriori: 'Water is H2O' is necessarily true because water just is H2O in every possible world, but we had to do empirical science to discover it. The upshot, on this picture, is that all necessary truths turn out to be knowable only a posteriori — once you break the link between necessity and apriority, you see that necessity has nothing to do with what we can figure out from our armchairs. Metaphysics just becomes empirical science done at a higher level of generality, and Kripke is essentially the philosopher who finished off the rationalist idea that pure reason can reveal the structure of reality."

I've read parts of Naming and Necessity but it's been years, and while some of this sounds familiar, other parts strike me as off — particularly the claim that Kripke makes all necessary truths a posteriori and the bit about finishing off rationalism. But I'm not confident I could articulate exactly what's wrong. Could you go through this summary sentence by sentence and tell me which claims a careful reader of Kripke would accept, which they'd push back on, and which they'd say are outright misrepresentations? I'd also like to know whether the conclusion — that Kripke eliminates a priori knowledge of necessity — follows from the accurate parts of the summary, or whether it depends on the distorted parts. And if the summary is missing something important about the relationship between necessity and apriority, I'd like to know what that missing piece is and how it bears on the broader picture my friend is trying to paint.
