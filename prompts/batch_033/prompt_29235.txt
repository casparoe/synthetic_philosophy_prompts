I've been reading Dennett's ladder of creatures — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — and I keep thinking the whole thing is a recursion: each rung internalises the generate-and-test loop of the rung below, so what gets proposed and what gets killed changes as you climb. I'd like a poem that gets this right instead of just name-dropping the four labels. Could you write a pantoum, four quatrains, one rung per stanza? You know the form: the second and fourth lines of each quatrain come back as the first and third of the next, and the closing quatrain folds the opening quatrain's first and third lines back in. That recycling seems perfect for a tower where each level is literally built out of the one below, and I'd like the repeated lines to shift meaning each time they return — the way "testing" means dying at the Darwinian level, extinction of an overt behaviour at the Skinnerian level, disconfirmation inside the head at the Popperian level, and being revised or dropped by other people at the Gregorian level. I know the rungs aren't seperate in real creatures — most of us are a messy mix — but keep each stanza on its own rung anyway. Make it accurate enough that someone who knows Dennett's taxonomy could point to each stanza and say which rung it is and what does the dying there — organism or lineage, overt behaviour, inner hypothesis, imported tool. And someone who doesn't know the taxonomy should still get a decent poem, not a diagram. The closing quatrain should make the opening lines read differently than they did at the bottom: by the top, the same words should carry the Gregorian point that the tools we think with were tested by other minds, and that's the only reason we get to think as cheaply as we do. Oh, and please write it in British English.
