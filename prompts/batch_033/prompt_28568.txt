i keep thinking about that Hofstadter idea where the self isnt a thing but a pattern, and when you know someone deeply enough a coarse-grained copy of their pattern starts running in your own head — so when they die theyre not simply gone, theres a dimmer, grainier version of them still flickering around in the people who loved them. not a soul, no spooky stuff, just a lower-resolution instance of the same self-referential loop. its comforting and a little awful at the same time.

write a villanelle from the point of view of someone who has just lost a close friend and keeps catching the friends patterns surfacing in their own mind — they hear her objection before they can finish their own sentence, they laugh like her at the wrong moment, they can predict exactly how shed take some piece of news. the two refrains should carry the philosophy: one line doing the work of the thought that she was never the stuff, only the pattern, and the other doing the work of the thought that a dimmer copy of her is still running in you. dont just use my wording — make the refrains actually good, and make them yours.

the form should do the conceptual work: in a villanelle the refrains come back in different stanzas, each time slightly changed by where they land, the way the copied self shows up in a new brain at lower resolution; and the final quatrain should put the two refrains side by side in its last two lines so the consolation and the loss stop looking like opposites — the dimmer copy really is her, and it really isnt enough. a specialist should recognize the view without you naming it, and someone who has never read Hofstadter should still get a poem about grief that means something.
