An adversarial collaboration between a committed frequentist and a committed subjectivist Bayesian about the interpretation of probability has produced a joint document. Draft that document. It should include:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims—about the mathematical formalism (the Kolmogorov axioms, the laws of large numbers, the central limit theorem), about what each side acknowledges the other gets right, about the limits of each view, and about what each side would count as evidence against its own position.

2. A disagreements table, with columns: (a) the precise formulation of the disagreement, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own position; (b) the crux—the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side; (c) a test, case, or argument both agree bears on the crux; and (d) each side's prediction about what that test or case will show.

The statement should distinguish genuine disagreements from merely verbal ones. Where the dispute reduces to a difference about what the word "probability" ought to mean, or about what the job description of a probability claim is, say so explicitly. Address at least the following issues: the reference class problem, the meaning of single-case probabilities, the justification of prior probabilities, and the role of probability in scientific inference.

Begin with the joint statement itself, with no preface or commentary.
