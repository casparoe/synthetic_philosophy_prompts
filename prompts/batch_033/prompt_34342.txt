I have been reading the documentation for an AI tutoring assistant that my school is piloting this year, and I cannot figure out what its honesty policy is actually assuming about deception. The policy has three parts. First, the assistant must not deceive users; the word deceive is used throughout as the central requirement. Second, for enforcement purposes, deception is operationalized as producing statements that the system's fact-checking component flags as false or unsupported by its available sources. Third, the assistant is allowed to decline to answer, to answer partially, and is encouraged to state when it is uncertain. The same documentation also says the assistant should be helpful and encouraging, and the system is tuned to keep students engaged.

What puzzles me is a mismatch I have observed. When a student asks whether a particular interpretation of a poem is correct, the assistant often says that it is a very defensible reading, lists several points in its favor, and omits strong counterevidence. None of its sentences is false, and the fact-checker flags nothing, but the student tends to leave more confident in the interpretation than the evidence would justify. A person who behaved that way would ordinarily be described as misleading someone, yet the policy does not seem to treat this as deception.

So my question is: what theory of deception does this policy presuppose? In particular, does it treat deception as a property of individual sentences, as a matter of the system's intentions (and the system presumably has no beliefs or intentions in the ordinary sense), or as a matter of the effect on the user's beliefs? Are the three parts of the policy consistent with one another, and does the engagement goal quietly conflict with the honesty requirement? Please distinguish what the policy strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and note where it is underdetermined. Finally, what would the developers have to change in both the written policy and the system's training if they instead defined deception as causing or risking false beliefs in the user, by any means including omission and framing? Please also tell me which consideration would most likely change your reconstruction, and where your reasoning is most fragile.
