In a working group on AI governance, a colleague repeatedly closed discussion of whose values a system should serve by saying the answer is obvious: "we should align AI with human values." The slogan is doing a great deal of work, because the room was disagreeing about exactly the questions it seems to settle—whose values, how conflicts among people's values should be handled, and whether "values" means preferences, moral beliefs, interests, or something else. Please disambiguate the slogan by laying out the distinct precise claims it could be used to make: for example, a claim about serving each individual user's values, a claim about serving some aggregate of everyone's preferences, a claim about serving objective moral values, a claim about serving only values that survive a liberal-neutrality or overlapping-consensus constraint, and any further readings you can isolate. For each reading, say (a) whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible specification, or a claim that is false, incoherent, or too vague to act on; and (b) whether that reading actually answers the question of whose values should govern when people disagree. End by identifying which reading, if any, does the work my colleague used the slogan for, and which readings only appear to settle the question.
