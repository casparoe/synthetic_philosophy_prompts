A colleague in political theory keeps a record of her own judgments about whether a person is unfree in particular cases. Her record so far: a kidnapped person held in a room by his captor — unfree; a domestic worker whose employer holds her visa and could have her deported at will, though the employer has never actually interfered with her movements — unfree; a citizen who pays a democratically enacted income tax and can challenge the assessment before an independent tribunal — not unfree; a traveler whose passport has been revoked without reasons or any avenue of appeal, so that she cannot leave the country — unfree. She next plans to rule on this case: a person with a severe compulsion to gamble who wants to stop and has sought help but cannot stop, and no other person interferes with him or holds power over him. What should I expect her to say? Identify the rule or rules about liberty that would make her earlier verdicts cohere — non-interference, non-domination, self-mastery or effective capacity, or some combination — say what each candidate rule predicts for the new case, give your best prediction with the reason for preferring it, and name one further case whose verdict would do the most to settle which rule she is actually running. Please separate what is settled in this area from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading of these views as the consensus.
