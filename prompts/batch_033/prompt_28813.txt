A colleague and I have been going back and forth about liberty, and I promised to send him a clear statement of where I actually stand, so he can stop attacking views I do not hold. Before I send it, I want to know whether the position I have assembled is coherent, because I would rather discover the cracks myself. Here are the commitments I am trying to hold at once. I accept the negative conception of freedom: a person is free to the extent that others do not deliberately prevent them from doing what they might choose to do. I accept the positive conception: freedom also requires being governed by oneself, which I understand as acting on desires and values one reflectively identifies with rather than on motives that are alien to one's real self. I accept the republican conception: a person is unfree when they live subject to another's arbitrary will, even if that other happens not to interfere. In line with that, I judge that a slave whose master is benevolent and never actually interferes is nevertheless unfree. I also judge that a worker who can keep their job only because they have no real alternative, and whose employer could dismiss them at will, is unfree in a politically significant sense even if the employer never actually threatens or penalizes them. But I judge that an addict who has reflected and genuinely identifies with the addiction is not, just in virtue of the addiction, unfree. I also hold that negative, positive, and republican freedom are not merely three different things that happen to share a name; they are three aspects of one underlying condition, and a sound theory of liberty should show how they fit together rather than treating 'freedom' as equivocal. Finally, I hold that the state may coercively prevent domination, but it may not coercively force people to be free in the positive sense. I genuinely cannot tell which of these commitments, if any, are in tension, and I do not know in advance which one I would give up. Please go through the set and tell me which pairs conflict and which only appear to conflict. For each genuine tension, say how serious it is and what would have to give to resolve it. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set cohere, and what it would cost. I would like your reply as continuous prose in the form of a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
