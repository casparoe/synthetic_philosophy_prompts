For a graduate seminar in formal political theory, write a compact decision tree that takes a student from the starting commitment that Sen's liberal paradox is a real incompatibility — no social decision procedure can satisfy unrestricted domain, Pareto efficiency, and minimal liberalism together — to a named position in the surrounding debate. The tree should be built from the logical space of ways to respond to the theorem, not from a list of labels, and every branch node should be a question a student could answer on the basis of what they already believe, without knowing which position the answer points toward.

Use at least these branch nodes, in an order of your choosing: (1) whether Pareto efficiency may be overridden when it would coerce someone on a matter inside their own protected sphere; (2) whether a protected sphere should be modeled as pairwise decisiveness over outcomes at all, or rather as a game form; (3) whether individuals may waive or trade their decisiveness when both stand to gain; (4) whether preferences that are entirely about what someone else does in their own private sphere — meddlesome preferences — should be admitted into social evaluation; and (5) whether the social preference relation must be acyclic or transitive, or whether a cycle may be accepted and handled by a non-preference-based tie-breaker. One node must present Sen's Prude/Lewd case as a concrete scenario, phrased so a student can answer it without knowing the case's name or history.

Leaves must include at least: rights as side-constraints, rights folded into welfare, waivable or alienable rights, game-form rights, domain restriction on meddlesome preferences, and preference relaxation. For any combination of answers that no one in fact defends, mark the leaf unnamed and say in one sentence whether the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot genuinely separate, and state the further question that would split them.

Format the deliverable as an indented outline: each branch node is a numbered question with explicit yes/no pointers, and each leaf is a bolded position name followed by at most one sentence. The entire tree, including all leaf annotations and flags, must be no more than 800 words.
