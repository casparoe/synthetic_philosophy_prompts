So there's this weird condition called pain asymbolia where people report feeling pain — like they can tell you its intensity, location, quality (burning, stabbing, etc.) — but they just don't mind it. They don't withdraw, don't wince, don't want it to stop. It's not analgesia (they do feel something) and it's not just stoicism. Philosophically this seems like a big deal for theories of pain: it looks like the "badness" of pain can come apart from the sensation itself. But I've heard people push back and say that what's really going on is that these patients have a degraded or incomplete pain experience, not that the badness is a separate thing added on top. Can you walk me through the dialectic here? Specifically I want to understand: if you're a defender of the view that pain is essentially bad (like, badness is built into the experience of pain itself), what's your best response to the asymbolia data, and where does that response end up looking shaky?
