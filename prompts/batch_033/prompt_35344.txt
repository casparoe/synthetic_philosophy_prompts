I'm designing a handout for a seminar on self-locating belief and want a decision tree that takes a student from their intuitive reactions to concrete variants of the Sleeping Beauty problem to a named position in the debate. The tree should sort students by what they say about cases, not by which label they recognize.

Here is the base scenario to assume throughout: On Sunday, Beauty is told that a fair coin will be flipped. If it lands Heads, she will be awakened on Monday only. If Tails, she will be awakened on Monday, given a memory-wiping drug, and awakened again on Tuesday. The awakenings are indistinguishable from the inside. Beauty knows all of this on Sunday.

Please build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names: first identify the diagnostic questions on which positions genuinely come apart, then locate the actual positions in that space. For any combination of answers that no one (to your knowledge) defends, flag it and briefly diagnose why — is the combination incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored?

The leaves must include at least: the standard thirder, the standard halfer, the double-halfer, and any view on which self-locating beliefs are not governed by the usual Bayesian update rule. If there are important sub-varieties within these (e.g., views that agree on the awakening credence but diverge on what Beauty should think upon learning it is Monday, or on what betting odds she should accept), distinguish them where the tree permits.

Phrase every branch node as a question a student could answer without already knowing which position it leads to. Where possible, use concrete variants — e.g., a version where Tails yields a thousand awakenings, a version where Beauty is offered a bet each time she wakes, or a version where the Monday and Tuesday rooms differ in some observable feature — rather than asking about abstract commitments like "do you accept conditionalization?"

Finally, flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate: views that give the same answers to all your diagnostic questions yet are distinct positions in the literature.

The response should indicate how confident it is in what it claims and single out the point it is least sure of.
