I serve on the hiring committee of a mid-sized physics department. We have one tenure-track line in theoretical physics, and we have narrowed the field to two finalists. Candidate A works on decoherence and the Everett interpretation, with a strong publication record in Physical Review Letters and related journals. Candidate B works on quantum information theory with no stake in any particular interpretation, and has a roughly comparable record. The committee is sharply divided. Some members argue that work on interpretations is not physics — the Everett, Copenhagen, and Bohmian programs make no divergent empirical predictions, so choosing among them is metaphysics, and a physics department has no business hiring someone to do it. Others reply that the Everett program has been scientifically productive (decoherence came out of it, and it has clarified the conceptual structure of quantum mechanics), that a department of our size can afford one person working on foundations, and that interpretive work is part of what theoretical physics has always done. The chair has asked me, as the committee member who has followed these debates most closely, to draft a short memo for the full committee recommending whether to hire Candidate A, hire Candidate B, or reopen the search. The memo should recommend a specific course of action, give the reasoning, and engage seriously with the strongest argument for the losing side. The committee votes on Thursday.
