Here's the argument, numbered so you can point to specific steps:

(1) Van Fraassen's Reflection principle says: for any rational agent, P_now(H | P_later(H) = x) = x. That is, conditional on your future credence being x, your current credence should be x.

(2) This principle obviously needs a rider: your future self must be rational and must have at least as much evidence as you do now. Otherwise you'd have to defer to your drunk self, or to a self that's forgotten crucial evidence. Van Fraassen himself says something like this.

(3) Now here's the case that bothers me. I'm currently agnostic about some hard philosophical question — say, whether moral realism is true. Credence: 0.5. I plan to spend the summer reading all the main arguments on both sides, carefully, with no new empirical evidence entering the picture. Pure reasoning.

(4) I know myself well enough to have some expectations about where I'll end up. I think there's a 60% chance that after the summer I'll be at credence 0.2 (leaning against realism) and a 40% chance I'll be at credence 0.8 (leaning toward it). I don't know which, but I know the terrain well enough to know those are the two stable endpoints, and I know my own intellectual temperament well enough to assign those probabilities.

(5) Expected future credence: (0.6 × 0.2) + (0.4 × 0.8) = 0.12 + 0.32 = 0.44.

(6) By Reflection, my current credence should be 0.44. But it's 0.5. So I'm irrational by Reflection's lights.

(7) But wait — if I'm irrational, the fix is to adopt credence 0.44 now. But that means I should shift my credence before I've done any of the reading! The reading is supposed to be what moves me. If I defer to my future self now, I'm skipping the very process that makes my future self worth deferring to.

(8) And here's the kicker: if I do adopt 0.44 now, then I've changed my credence without doing the reasoning. But then why am I still planning to do the reading? I've already arrived at the credence the reading was supposed to produce. The reading becomes epistemically redundant.

(9) This seems to generalize: if Reflection is a norm, you can never rationally anticipate that pure reasoning will change your credence. You must always already have the credence you would have after reasoning. But that can't be right — the whole point of reasoning is to get somewhere you're not already at.

(10) So either Reflection is false, or we can never rationally predict the results of our own future reasoning, or there's something else going on.

I'm pretty sure (4) is where the trouble starts — maybe you can't coherently assign probabilities to the outputs of your own future reasoning? But that seems empirically false.
