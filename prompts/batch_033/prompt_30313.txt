Health systems and insurers commonly pay for growth hormone when a child has a diagnosed deficiency, but not for a healthy short child whose parents would like him to be taller. Sports regulators ban growth hormone in healthy athletes, yet allow the same substance under a therapeutic use exemption when an athlete has a diagnosed condition. Both institutions seem to be drawing a line between restoring someone to normal functioning and improving someone who is already normal, and they treat the same intervention differently depending on which side of that line it falls. That puzzles me, because the intervention itself has not changed; only the diagnosis attached to the person has. I would like to know what theory of health, disease, or normal human functioning this pattern of decisions presupposes. Does it strictly require such a theory, or could the same practices be justified without one? And if the therapy/enhancement distinction turned out not to be principled, what exactly would these practices have to change?
