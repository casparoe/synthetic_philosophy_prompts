I'm not a philosopher or anything, just someone who fell down a rabbit hole reading about whether insects can suffer. The position I've ended up with is roughly this: even if you think any individual insect is pretty unlikely to be conscious, there are so many of them that the expected amount of suffering is enormous, so it would be a mistake to just write insects off. I keep seeing people treat "we can't know if bees feel pain" as a reason not to worry about them, and that seems backwards to me — the uncertainty plus the numbers is exactly what should make us worry.

But I want to know where this view is actualy vulnerable. What would count against it? I don't just mean the obvious "insects aren't conscious" reply. I mean: what evidence about insect behaviour or nervous systems should actually move me, and by how much? Are there arguments that the whole way of reasoning — small probability times huge number — breaks down here, and when should I accept that? Does my view predict anything different from the "only worry about big-brained animals" view, or have I set it up so that no finding about insects could ever change my mind? I'd rather know if I've made the position unfalsifiable without noticing.

If you could rank the things that count against it from "this would basically kill the view" to "this is just a bit awkward", including the ones someone who basically agrees with me would have to admit count against it, and be honest about whether the view has become too insulated to be useful, that would help. I think the thing that would shake me most is solid evidence that insects have no structures that could support any kind of felt experience — but maybe I'm weighting that wrong.
