I was a field geologist for most of my working life and only got into philosophy after I retired, so Ive probably butchered this, but here goes. I keep coming back to this idea in decision theory called evidential cooperation in large worlds. As I understand it: if the universe is big enough that there are lots of agents out there who are similar to me and reasoning through roughly the same choice, then what I do is evidence about what those agents will do, even when we cant causally affect each other at all. So it can be rational to choose the action I would want all the similar agents to choose, including cooperating with agents whose values arent identical to mine, because my choosing it raises the probability that they choose the matching thing. Is that close to right?

Here is what I actually want. Please describe the most important implications of this idea for the ordinary "my little bit won't matter" cases — voting, cutting my carbon footprint, eating less meat, giving a few hundred dollars to a cause. Im not after the old "what if everyone did that" universalizability line; I want to know what this specific evidential, decision-theoretic idea changes about those choices, if anything. And I would especially like to know where the analogy breaks down — that is, where my action is not much evidence about what other similar agents do, and why.
