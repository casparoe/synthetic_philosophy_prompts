Ok so I've been reading the endurantism vs perdurantism debate in metaphysics for a while now and I'm genuinely puzzled about why it's still going. For anyone who needs the quick version: the question is how objects persist through time. Endurantists say an object is wholly present at every moment it exists — the object at t1 is literally numerically identical to the object at t2, and change is the same object having different properties at different times. Perdurantists say objects are four-dimensional space-time worms that have temporal parts — the object at t1 and the object at t2 are different temporal slices of the same worm, and change is just different slices having different properties.

The arguments on both sides are super familiar at this point. The perdurantist says endurantism can't handle temporary intrinsics without weird maneuvers — like saying properties are relations to times, or time-indexing them — which makes intrinsic properties end up relational in a way that feels like cheating. The endurantist says perdurantism gets the phenomenology wrong: I don't experience myself as a succession of time-slices, I experience myself as the same thing enduring. Plus there's trouble with de re modal attributions (if I have a temporal part that is sitting, is it essentially sitting? that seems wrong, but if it's only accidentally sitting, then isn't there a deeper me that has the property accidentally, and aren't we back to endurantism?). Then there's the argument from vagueness, the argument from special relativity, the argument from change, etc. Every move has a countermove and it really feels like everyone's just picking a side based on which bullet they'd rather bite.

My working theory is that this isn't a factual disagreement — there's no hidden experiment or observation that would settle it, since both theories are empirically equivalent. But I can't tell if it's a disagreement about which theoretical virtues matter more (parsimony of what kind? fidelity to ordinary language? ideological simplicity?), or a genuine metaphysical dispute where the two sides just have different bedrock intuitions about identity and persistence that can't be argued for, or maybe something sneakier like both sides are using "object" to mean subtly different things without noticing. And if it really is just about which theoretical package you prefer aesthetically, why has it generated hundreds of papers as if there's a fact of the matter?

I want a diagnosis, not a verdict for one side. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a new formal result, a conceptual distinction that dissolves the whole thing, or is this the kind of debate where people should just admit they have incommensurable starting points and move on?

Give me a one-sentence summary of your diagnosis as the last line.
