Write a dialogue between two people arguing about whether the rigidity of the Sphex wasp's behavior shows that human free will is an illusion. One of them (call her Maya) thinks the Sphex is a vivid illustration of what's wrong with compatibilism — sure, the wasp is stupid, but we're just a more complicated version of the same thing, and the fact that our "reasons-responsive" mechanisms are more sophisticated doesn't amount to freedom in any sense worth wanting. The other (call him Leo) thinks the Sphex example actually helps his case: the wasp's failure mode is precisely that it can't update its behavior in light of new information, and that's the hallmark of non-agency — genuine agency just is the capacity to notice and adjust. They should engage with each other's actual points, neither should be a strawman, and they should push each other into the details rather than trading slogans. Feel free to include a beer or two.
