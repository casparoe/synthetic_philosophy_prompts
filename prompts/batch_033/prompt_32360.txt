ok so I've been going down a rabbit hole reading about split-brain patients (you know, the ones where surgeons cut the corpus callosum to stop severe epilepsy) and it's messing with how I think about consciousness. I'm a software enginner, not a philosopher, so I might be using words wrong, but here's roughly where I've ended up:

(1) I'm a physicalist. Consciousness is just something brains do. No soul, no extra ingredient, just neural activity.

(2) In split-brain patients, there are genuinely two separate conscious experiences happening in one skull. It's not just that different modules are out of sync—there are literally two subjects of experience, each with their own perceptions and intentions, and they can't directly share what they know.

(3) In a normal brain with an intact corpus callosum, consciousness is unified. I'm one subject. There's a single "what it's like to be me."

(4) The reason normal brains have unified consciousness is the corpus callosum acting as a high-bandwidth bridge. The two hemispheres are constantly syncing state, and that syncing is what makes two processors into one experiencer. So unity is basically an information-sharing phenomenon.

(5) If you could gradually replace my neurons one at a time with silicon chips that replicate the input-output function, I'd stay conscious through the whole process and it would still be me at the end. (I know this is sci-fi, I'm asking about the principle.)

I keep going back and forth on whether these actually fit together. For example, if (2) is true and cutting the corpus callosum turns one subject into two, then what does that say about how "unified" the normal case really is? Like, if unity is just a matter of bandwidth (as (4) suggests), then isn't normal consciousness just a committee whose members agree so fast they don't notice the seams? That seems to undermine (3) being about genuine oneness rather than just tight coordination.

Also, (5) and (2) together seem weird: if you can split one subject into two by cutting a cable, could you merge two subjects into one by connecting them with enough bandwidth? And if you gradually replaced your neurons and then someone cut your corpus callosum, would there be two of "you" or just two new people?

I want someone to go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like they are. Rank the tensions by how serious they are—don't just list everything as equally bad. For each genuine tension, say what the cheapest fix would be and what you'd have to give up.
