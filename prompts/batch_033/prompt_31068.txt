ok so heres the deal. im a postdoc in a cog neuro lab, and for the last year ive been running experiments on the timing of conscious awareness relative to motor preparation. and the data keeps slapping me in the face with something that looks a lot like epiphenomenalism—the idea that conscious experience is real but causally inert. i didnt come into this wanting to believe that. i actually thought epiphenomenalism was a joke in undergrad. but now i cant shake it, and ive been reading the phil mind literature and i kind of think i have a way to defend it against the obvious objection everyone reaches for, which is the paradox of phenomenal judgment.

you know the one: if consciousness doesnt cause anything, then our judgments about our own conscious experience are caused purely by physical processes, not by the experience itself. so the fact that our judgments track our experiences has to be some kind of massive coincidence or pre-established harmony. it makes our knowledge of our own consciousness totally mysterious.

my take is that the tracking isnt a coincidence because the same physical processes that cause the judgment also cause the experience—theyre two effects of a common cause. so theres no need for the experience to cause the judgment directly. ive got a version of this argument worked out, with some empirical predictions about what kinds of mismatches between experience and judgment we should and shouldnt see in the lab.

heres my problem. im presenting this at our departments philosophy-neuroscience reading group in three weeks. the audience is:

- mostly systems neuroscientists who are empirically sharp but suspicious of philosophy. they want to know what they can actually measure and what the model predicts.
- one philosopher who is a hardcore type-identity theorist (consciousness literally IS a brain process, so epiphenomenalism is just confused about the metaphysics)
- another philosopher who is a functionalist and thinks the causal role of a state is what makes it the mental state it is, so the whole idea of a causally inert quale is incoherent

i need a prep sheet for the q&a. give me the objections ill actually get from this group, ordered by how much damage theyll do—not the objections from the philosophical literature generally, but the ones these specific people will raise, phrased the way theyd say them. for each: a two-minute reply, and a note on where my reply is softest. and include the question im praying nobody asks.
