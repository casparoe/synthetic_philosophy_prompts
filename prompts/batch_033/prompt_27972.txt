I'm writing a survey of theories of material constitution and I want to start by laying out what any adequate theory must do. Give me the desiderata: the puzzle cases a theory must handle (the statue and the clay, Tibbles losing its tail, the ship of Theseus, Dion and Theon, and any others you think are essential), the distinctions it must respect (e.g., between constitution and identity, between sortal and non-sortal predication), and the logical or metaphysical results it must not contradict (Leibniz's Law, the transitivity of identity, and so on). For each requirement, give the consideration that makes it a requirement. Mark which are non-negotiable and which are optional commitments one could reasonably set aside. Then tell me whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — that is the part I most want to see. Throughout, separate what is settled among those working on material constitution from what is genuinely contested, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
