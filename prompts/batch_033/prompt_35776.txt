I've been trying to sort out my views on the Orwellian vs. Stalinesque debate about the timing of conscious experience, and I'm all over the place. Here's where I land on the standard cases:

Color phi (red dot, then green dot at a different spot — people see one moving dot that changes color midway): I think the color change is genuinely experienced, not just misremembered. The brain waits for the second flash and constructs the experience retrospectively. Stalinesque.

Cutaneous rabbit (taps at wrist, elbow, shoulder in quick succession — people feel taps at evenly spaced points along the arm, including spots never touched): Same — the intermediate taps are genuinely felt. Stalinesque.

Libet's readiness potential (brain activity starts before the conscious decision to move): I think the conscious 'decision' is a retrospective construction. The real decision was unconscious. Orwellian.

Stopped clock / chronostasis (first glance at a clock seems to last longer than subsequent seconds): The duration is stretched in memory, not in the experience itself. Orwellian.

Flash-lag effect (a moving object is seen ahead of a briefly flashed stationary object that's physically aligned with it): I lean Stalinesque — a real-time perceptual error, not memory revision — but I'm shakier on this one.

So I seem to be Stalinesque about spatial/perceptual construction and Orwellian about temporal/agency judgments. Is that a principled distinction or am I just pattern-matching? And what would my pattern say about a case I haven't decided on: the phonemic restoration effect, where a cough replaces a phoneme in a sentence but you 'hear' the missing sound — is the restored phoneme genuinely experienced or just remembered as having been heard? If my pattern doesn't give a clean answer, which of my verdicts should I reconsider?
