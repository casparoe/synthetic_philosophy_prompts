Next month I'm defending a claim about higher-order evidence to a philosophy department with a strong formal-epistemology contingent, and I want to walk in knowing how the question period will go. The claim: a fully rational agent cannot believe p while also believing that her total evidence does not support p. My route is not a flat coherence-between-levels principle. I argue (1) that a belief about what one's evidence supports is itself part of one's evidence, in roughly the way a reliable instrument's reading is evidence about the quantity it measures; (2) that full rationality requires matching one's first-order attitudes to one's total evidence; and (3) that a body of evidence containing 'my evidence does not support p' does not support believing p. So the akratic combination fails on ordinary evidentialist grounds, without any special anti-akrasia axiom.

The audience: two Bayesians who think rationality is coherence and treat evidence as whatever gets conditioned on; three people who work on peer disagreement and higher-order evidence (one has argued that higher-order evidence is just evidence, one has defended splitting first-order and higher-order attitudes so that akrasia can be locally rational, one is genuinely undecided); and a senior person who suspects the whole dispute is about the word 'rational.' They will not be moved by a single intuition case; they will want to know whether premise (1) is doing all the work, whether the conclusion overgenerates to cases where the higher-order belief is itself irrational, and whether the view collapses into Uniqueness or forbids rational peer disagreement.

Write my Q&A prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get from this audience — the genuinely distinct ones, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are — each phrased the way a member of this audience would put it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks, and say what makes it the dangerous one. Be committed: tell me which objections have real force and where I should concede, where I should hold the line, and which point I should pre-empt in the talk rather than leave to the questions.
