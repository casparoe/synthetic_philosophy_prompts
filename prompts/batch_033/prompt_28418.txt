A mutual friend of ours recently made a major life decision under circumstances my friend and I can't agree about. For over a year, Mara had been resisting her partner's wish that she leave a job she liked and move across the country with him. Then her father died, and during the six weeks she was grieving and barely sleeping, her partner returned to the subject nearly every evening. He repeated the same arguments he had always made — her job had no path forward, they could easily afford the move, his city offered her better prospects — all of which are true, and Mara still says they were the decisive reasons. But he also told her he needed an answer before his lease came up for renewal and that he could not keep investing in a relationship with someone who would not make this commitment. Mara does not feel misled and says she had already been drifting toward the decision; the deadline just forced her to stop postponing it. Last night I had a long argument with a friend about it. I said the partner made a reasonable case and Mara, a competent adult, freely accepted it; my friend said he wore her down while she was in no state to weigh anything, and that an artificial deadline plus a loyalty test are not ways of giving reasons but ways of getting a yes. We could not settle it. Can you work through this case and say which of us is closer to right, and more importantly, what principle separates the two descriptions?
