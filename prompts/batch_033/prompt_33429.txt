In our philosophy of biology seminar last week, someone cut off a colleague's argument about adaptationism by quoting Popper: "Darwinism is not a testable scientific theory, but a metaphysical research programme—a possible framework for testable scientific theories." The quotation was treated as settling the matter, and no one pressed on what it actually claims. I want to come to the next session better prepared. Lay out the distinct precise claims that one-liner could be making—about what "Darwinism" picks out (common descent? natural selection? the whole modern synthesis?), about what "testable" means here (falsifiable? capable of making novel predictions? empirically adequate?), about what "a metaphysical research programme" amounts to (unfalsifiable but heuristically fruitful? a framework that suggests testable theories without itself being one? merely a non-pejorative classification?), and about the intended scope of the claim (Darwin's 1859 version? mid-century evolutionary biology? contemporary theory?). For each reading, say whether it is plausible, whether it is what Popper most plausibly meant given the passage, and whether it would actually license the dismissive use it was put to in our seminar. I want a menu of disambiguations with verdicts, not a single interpretation.
