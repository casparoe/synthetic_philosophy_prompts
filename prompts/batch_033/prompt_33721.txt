The debate about whether indicative conditionals—ordinary if-then sentences like "If it rains, the picnic will be cancelled"—have truth conditions has been running since at least Lewis's triviality results in 1976, and as far as I can tell, the literature has settled into a stable impasse. On one side, truth-conditionalists hold that indicative conditionals express propositions that are true or false at each world; they differ internally about what those truth conditions are (material conditional, Stalnaker's selection-function semantics, and more recent attempts to block the triviality proofs by modifying the semantics or the logic of the conditional). Lewis's results showed that under seemingly mild assumptions, the probability of a conditional's truth cannot systematically equal the conditional probability P(C|A)—yet the conditional probability is precisely what speakers seem to assign to conditionals. On the other side, the suppositional or no-truth-conditions view (Adams, Edgington, and others) takes this as evidence that indicative conditionals simply do not have truth conditions. Instead, the probability we assign to "If A then C" just is the conditional probability P(C|A), and the role of conditionals is not to describe the world but to express a posture of reasoning under a supposition. The two sides have been trading arguments for decades: about embeddings (can conditionals be negated, embedded in disjunctions, or appear in the consequents of other conditionals?), about whether the Equation P(If A then C) = P(C|A) is a genuine datum or a mistake, about whether triviality can be avoided by giving up closure under conditionalization or by adopting a non-classical logic, and about what a semantics of natural language is even supposed to deliver. Nobody has conceded.

I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this a substantive disagreement about what there is—whether there is a fact of the matter about the truth of a conditional—or is it a disagreement about what we want from a semantics, or about which data are non-negotiable? Are the two sides answering different questions under one heading (one about the metaphysics of content, one about the psychology of reasoning)? My working hypothesis is that the two camps are committed to different methodological starting points—one treats embedding behavior as the decisive test for truth-conditionality, the other treats the Equation as a rock-bottom datum—and that these starting points are themselves not argued for but chosen, which would make the impasse deeper than it looks. But I'm not confident this is the right diagnosis.
