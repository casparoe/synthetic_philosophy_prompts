Ok so I spent my career in biology — developmental stuff mostly, not neuroscience — and I've been reading philosophy of mind the last couple years since retiring. I keep coming back to the split-brain stuff and I can't get my views to settle. Here's roughly where I am right now and I'd like someone to tell me if this package even hangs together:

(1) I think the standard split-brain experiments — flashing "key" to the left field and "ring" to the right, the patient says "ring" but picks up the key with their left hand — show that there are genuinely two streams of conscious experience in there, each with access to different information. I dont see how you describe those results without saying that.

(2) But I also find the unity-of-consciousness thesis pretty compelling: the idea that conscious experience is necessarily unified, that you can't just have two separate phenomenal fields cohabiting a single mind. There's something deeply weird about saying there are literally two subjects of experience in one head.

(3) I'm a physicalist. Consciousness is something the brain does. No spooky stuff.

(4) I think it matters that split-brain patients outside the lab report feeling like a single unified self and function fine as one person. If there really were two conscious subjects in there, you'd expect more practical fragmentation — and you don't get it. So I take that as evidence against the strong two-consciousnesses reading.

(5) I'm also drawn to the idea that the unity of consciousness is tied to personal identity in a pretty tight way: if your consciousness genuinely splits into two non-communicating streams, then you've got two persons, at least for as long as the split lasts. I don't think you can have it both ways — one person, two consciousnesses.

So that's the set. I can feel that some of these are pulling against each other but I'm not sure which ones and how badly. Go through them and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look like they are. Rank the tensions by severity. And if I have to revise something to get a consistent package, what's the cheapest fix?
