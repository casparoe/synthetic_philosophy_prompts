A friend of mine keeps a running record of her own choices among small monetary gambles. All payoffs are nonnegative dollar amounts, and she treats each choice as isolated, with no background-wealth complications. Her recorded choices so far are:

1. She chose $30 for sure over a 50–50 chance of $100 or $0.
2. She chose a 50–50 chance of $100 or $0 over $20 for sure.
3. She chose $40 for sure over a 50–50 chance of $100 or $0.
4. She chose a 50–50 chance of $100 or $60 over $65 for sure.

I am about to offer her the same gamble from item 4—a 50–50 chance of $100 or $60—against $75 for sure. Predict what she will choose. I am less interested in the bare prediction than in the rule she seems to be running: expected utility with a concave utility function, risk-weighted expected utility with a linear utility function, or something else. Say how well each candidate rule handles the full record, which gives the better account of it, and what further pair of gambles would most cleanly separate the leading rules. Please write your answer in American English.
