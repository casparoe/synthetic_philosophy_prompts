I've been going down a bit of a rabbit hole lately reading about public sector targets — the kind of thing New Labour was so keen on in the 2000s — and I keep coming back to the same example. The NHS has this rule that 95% of patients attending A&E must be admitted, transferred, or discharged within four hours of arrival. On its face, totally reasonable: nobody should be stuck on a trolley in a corridor for half a day. But the more I read about how hospitals actually respond to it, the more I wonder whether the whole enterprise rests on assumptions that don't really hold up. You get patients being wheeled into "clinical decision units" that are technically not A&E so the clock stops, even though they're still waiting for a proper bed. You get ambulances stacked up outside with patients inside them because the four-hour window only starts when the patient is formally booked in at reception. A few years ago some trusts were caught just altering the numbers. And what strikes me is that nobody in charge seems to think any of this calls the target itself into question — it's always framed as an implementation issue, a bit of bad behaviour by a few managers, rather than something the target's very structure invites.

So my question isn't really about whether the four-hour target is good policy or bad policy. It's more philosophical, I suppose. What does the practice of setting and enforcing a target like this actually presuppose? I mean, what picture of the world do you have to hold — about what quality of care is, about the relationship between a number and the thing it's supposed to measure, about how the people being measured will behave — for this practice to make sense? And if you rejected that picture, if you thought instead that the most important dimensions of care can't be captured by a single metric, or that people will predictably game a measure once you attach stakes to it, what would accountability even look like? Do you just abandon targets entirely and trust professionals, or is there some third way that doesn't lean on the same presuppositions?
