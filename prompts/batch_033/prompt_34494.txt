Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the debate over whether there is a moral difference between lying and misleading.

The two camps are as follows. The Asymmetry camp holds that, other things equal, lying is morally worse than merely misleading: lying adds a distinctive wrong over and above whatever is wrong with the deception itself, perhaps because it abuses the practice of assertion or because it leaves the hearer with no true statement to fall back on. The Parity camp holds that any moral difference between lying and misleading is only apparent or derivative, traceable to contingent factors such as how easily the deception is detected, how much deliberation it requires, how readily the deceiver can deny having deceived, or how much the deceiver commits to the false content; on this view there is no wrong-making feature peculiar to lying as such.

Write the document such a collaboration would produce, in the following numbered sections.

1. Agreed claims. Provide a list of substantive claims both camps can sign, including at least: working definitions of lying and misleading that both accept, with an explicit treatment of the hard boundary cases such as answering a question with a true but calculatedly misleading assertion; a precise statement of the question that makes clear the comparison is ceteris paribus and concerns a distinctive wrong-making feature rather than overall wrongness in particular cases; and an agreed description of one or two paradigm cases in which the two behaviors come apart.

2. A disagreements table. Give each camp's position in wording that a careful member of that camp would accept as a fair statement of its own view, and then state in neutral wording the residual question the two formulations disagree about. Neither side may be stated as a strawman.

3. Cruxes. Identify the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. These might include, for example, whether the distinctive norm governing assertion is doing independent moral work, whether the hearer's epistemic position or opportunity to resist the deception differs in a morally relevant way, whether the felt difference tracks something in the deception itself rather than in the deceiver's exposure to blame, or whether any genuine difference is one of degree rather than kind. For each crux, specify a concrete case, argument, or test that both camps agree bears on it, and record each camp's prediction about what that case or test will show, together with what each camp specifies in advance would count as evidence against its own view.

4. Verbal-dispute audit. State which parts of the disagreement, if any, you conclude are merely verbal, and explain why the remainder is not.

The document should be one that a reasonable member of each camp could actually sign as a fair joint statement of the current state of their disagreement; it should not be a compromise between the two views. Write in American English.
