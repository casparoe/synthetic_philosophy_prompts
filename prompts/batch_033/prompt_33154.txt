A private foundation has committed roughly five postdoctoral researcher-years over three years to conceptual and theoretical work bearing on a single practical question: whether habitat destruction tends, on balance, to reduce or increase the suffering of wild animals. The program officer is genuinely undecided, and the intended downstream audience is conservation biologists and environmental policymakers who would alter their habitat decisions if the answer were known with confidence. The grants may go to philosophers of biology, theoretical ecologists, or welfare scientists who work with models rather than field data.

Your task is to produce the research agenda she should fund: rank the open questions worth prioritizing, and defend the ordering rather than merely listing the questions. Among the candidates are whether total population size is a reliable proxy for total welfare; whether an ecosystem can be assigned a net welfare at all, or whether welfare is only a property of individual organisms; how to aggregate welfare across species whose sentience, lifespan, and life histories differ by orders of magnitude; whether the burden of proof should fall on those who treat wild lives as net positive or on those who treat them as net negative; how density-dependent mortality and habitat quality interact to set average welfare; and whether "habitat destruction" is too coarse a category to license any general verdict, since draining a wetland, clearing a forest, and paving over a meadow yield different replacement habitats with different welfare profiles. You should feel free to add questions not listed here or to merge several of these.

For each question you rank highly, I want the reasoning: why it is upstream of other work (what depends on it), how tractable it is for conceptually oriented researchers rather than field empiricists, whether settling it would actually change a conservation or land-use decision, and what evidence over the next two or three years would move it up or down the list. Also identify at least one question that is often treated as central in this area but that you would deprioritize because its answer would not change much downstream, and say why.
