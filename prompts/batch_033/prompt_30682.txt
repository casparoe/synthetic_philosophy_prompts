Argue that teleosemantics faces an indeterminacy problem that survives even its best solutions to the disjunction problem. Take as your central case a single representational mechanism that was originally selected for tracking one property and was later exapted to track a second, incompatible property, with both etiologies contributing to its present maintenance. Show that the standard ways of fixing a unique content for the state — appealing to the conditions that explain current selection, to the consumer's use of the state, to the distinction between proper and accidental function, or to a weighted selection history — either yield conflicting contents or tacitly presuppose an intentional interpretation of the system. Conclude by saying which commitment of the teleosemantic program should be revised, if any, in light of this. Write your answer in American English.
