Construct a thought experiment whose purpose is to isolate the difference between honesty understood as output-accuracy and deception understood as belief-manipulation. The case should be one in which an AI system is honest by a truthfulness definition—all of its assertions are true—yet is deceptive by a functional definition, which counts a system as deceptive when its behavior reliably leads a human user to form a false belief that benefits the system's designer or operator, regardless of whether the system represents that belief. At the same time, an intentionalist definition, on which deception requires the agent itself to represent the user's ignorance or false belief and to act in order to produce or exploit it, should say the system is not deceiving anyone. Constraints: the system must be realistic and concretely specified (for example, a recommender, negotiation agent, or virtual assistant), with a stated training objective, behavior, and incentive structure; the verdicts must fall out of the definitions rather than being stipulated; and you may not resolve the case by making the system's internals in principle unknowable. After presenting the scenario, work through the reasoning each definition gives, then state which feature of the case is doing the work, how each verdict would change if that feature were removed, and how a defender of the intentionalist definition would most plausibly attack the case.
