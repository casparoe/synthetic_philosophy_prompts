Before I engage further with the literature on responsibility gaps in autonomous systems, I want the full specification of what a solution must accomplish — too many proposals solve one carefully chosen case while ignoring the one next door. Please write the desiderata for an adequate theory of responsibility attribution when autonomous systems cause harm. For each requirement, give the motivating case or conceptual pressure that makes it a genuine constraint, not just a label.

Among the issues I need addressed:

- The distinction between a responsibility gap and a merely undischarged responsibility. Is the problem that no one is responsible, or that we cannot identify who is? What turns on whether the gap is epistemic, metaphysical, or both?
- Whether forward-looking responsibility (obligations to prevent, insure, compensate, regulate) and backward-looking responsibility (blame, punishment, desert, reactive attitudes) generate different desiderata, and whether a theory can satisfy one set while failing the other.
- The status of the control condition. If a system learns in deployment and produces behavior its designers could not have anticipated in detail, has the control condition been violated, or does it need revision — and if revision, what principled stopping point prevents control from becoming vacuous?
- Whether a theory must always identify some human as the responsible party, or can instead argue that genuine gaps are acceptable — and if the latter, what constraints such acceptance must meet (e.g., victims must still be compensated, or the practice must survive a publicity condition).
- The relationship to distributed and collective responsibility. If responsibility is spread across designers, trainers, deployers, users, and regulators, at what point does diffusion become elimination? What distinguishes a diluted but genuine responsibility from no responsibility at all?
- Whether the kind of system matters: fully autonomous versus advisory versus supervisory, deterministic versus stochastic, transparent versus opaque. Which features of a system generate which kinds of gap, and must a theory handle all of them or only some?

Then flag any pairs of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. For instance, if we insist on both a robust control condition (no backward-looking responsibility without the right kind of control over the specific action) and the requirement that victims not be left without some responsible party, do these come apart given the nature of online learning and inscrutable representations? That kind of tension is what I most want mapped — the desiderata list itself should surface where the problem is hardest.

Finally, give a brief scorecard for the main families of response — strict liability for deployers, distributed moral responsibility across the design-and-deployment chain, treating sufficiently autonomous systems as responsible agents in their own right, and revisionist views that accept gaps as a feature rather than a bug — against the list, and identify the single desideratum that does the most eliminative work.
