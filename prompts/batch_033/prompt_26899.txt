Since I was drafted onto the policy committee of a small political party last winter, a friend has been pressing Simone Weil's case that political parties are by their nature hostile to independent thought and ought to be done away with. I have tried to reconstruct the argument in my own terms, and this is what I get:

(1) A political party is an association whose defining goal is its own growth: more members, more votes, more offices, more control of the state.

(2) An association whose defining goal is its own growth will, when truth and growth conflict, sacrifice truth to growth. Its members' collective interest in the association's success will outweigh their individual interest in accuracy, and the association will select for people willing to make that trade.

(3) Therefore every political party, whatever its platform and however decent its members, is structurally disposed to corrupt its members' relation to the truth.

(4) Therefore political parties should be abolished, or at least no one who cares about thinking clearly should belong to one.

I find (4) extreme, but I cannot locate the bad step. What unsettles me more is that the same reasoning seems to extend to almost any durable association: a university with an endowment and a reputation to protect, a trade union that needs members, a newspaper that needs subscribers, a church that seeks converts. Each has an interest in its own continuation and growth that can conflict with the truth, and each will sometimes fudge. But I am not prepared to conclude that universities, unions, newspapers, and churches should be abolished. So either the argument overgenerates and something in (1)–(4) is too strong, or there is a suppressed premise that makes parties different from those other bodies, or the overgenerated conclusion is simply one I am refusing to accept out of comfort.

Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I would most like to keep the claim that there is something structurally wrong with political parties, but not at the price of condemning every institution that has a self-preservation interest. I need to decide whether to remain on the committee, so I want to know whether this argument is sound, and if not, where it fails.
