I am a student in an upper-level seminar on animal minds and moral status. We have been working through the scale argument for taking insect welfare seriously: because insects exist in numbers on the order of ten to the eighteenth power, even a low probability that any given insect is sentient yields an enormous expected amount of welfare at stake, which makes dismissing the issue harder than it first appears. Please argue that this argument is more robust than its critics allow. In doing so, address at least these objections: that a low probability of sentience is not the right kind of basis for moral concern; that aggregating welfare across such vast numbers is illegitimate or leads to absurd conclusions; and that deep uncertainty about insect sentience should leave us agnostic rather than moved to action. Make the strongest case you can, and be explicit about the point at which your defense must rest on a substantive ethical assumption rather than on the empirical scale of the question.
