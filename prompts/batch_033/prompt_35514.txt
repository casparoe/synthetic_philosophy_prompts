I am writing a position statement on practical reasons, in the debate between internalist and externalist theories of reasons, and I would like a consistency audit of the set of commitments I have ended up with before I show it to anyone. I am not after a literature survey or a reading list; I want you to work from the positions as I state them.

I hold, first, that a consideration is a reason for an agent to act only if there is a sound path of deliberation from what she already cares about — her desires, projects, attachments, and loyalties — to her being moved to act on it. Second, I think that path can be long and indirect: learning new facts, noticing relevant similarities, resolving conflicts among one's aims, and vividly imagining what a course of action would actually be like can all lead an agent to acquire new motivations. An agent can therefore have a reason she would sincerely reject and might never in fact arrive at, provided the path from her present motives to the action exists. Third, I want to say about the familiar callous person, who mistreats others and appears to have no motive, however buried, to treat them better, that if there really is no such path from anything he cares about to kinder conduct, then saying that he has a reason to treat them better is not a plain factual report; it functions instead as blame, or as an attempt to bring the missing motivation about, or as an expression of what we wish were true of him. Fourth, I also want to say that such a person is nonetheless irrational, or at least defective in his deliberation, and not merely cruel; something is wrong with how he deliberates, not only with what he does. Fifth, I hold that reasons for belief are different: evidence gives me a reason to believe something whether or not believing it would serve any aim of mine, and a person can have an epistemic reason she has no motive to heed. Sixth, I hold that reasons are objective in at least this sense: two agents with the same motivational starting points and the same non-normative circumstances have the same reasons, and whether an agent has a reason is a matter of fact about which she can be mistaken.

I would like you to go through these commitments and tell me whether the set hangs together. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what the least costly revision would be; for the apparent tensions, say why they dissolve on inspection. Finally, tell me whether there is a single revision that would resolve the most, and what it would cost me. Please write your answer in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as you would in an email or a letter, and please write in British English.
