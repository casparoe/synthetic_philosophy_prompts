I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about separating the art from the artist, going by my gut on real cases. Here's where I land:

- I can still listen to Wagner's operas and be genuinely moved by them, even knowing he was a virulent antisemite. The music doesn't feel tainted to me.
- But I can't watch a Roman Polanski film without thinking about what he did, and I honestly don't want to. It ruins the experience.
- There's a contemporary painter whose work I used to love, and then it came out he'd been serially abusive to his students. I took his prints off my wall. I couldn't look at them the same way.
- Yet I have no problem with Caravaggio, who literally killed a man. I don't even think about it when I look at his paintings.
- I'd still teach Hemingway in a literature class, but I'd feel weird about assigning a whole course on him without mentioning his personal life and the way he wrote about women. The work stands on its own but the silence feels like complicity.
- A comedian whose jokes are just offensive — not connected to any actual harm they've done — I can enjoy if the jokes are funny enough. But if it turns out they actually believe the awful stuff and act on it, I can't. The same jokes feel different.
- I walked out of a movie once when I learned mid-film that the director had assaulted someone. I don't think I'd have walked out if I'd learned before buying the ticket; I'd just have not gone. That seems inconsistent but it's how I actually felt.
- But if a song I already love turns out to be by someone awful, I keep listening to it. I don't know why music feels different from film.
- I think Michael Jackson's music is great and I still listen to it, but I feel gross about it in a way I don't with Wagner, and I can't articulate the difference.

Is there a principle or set of principles that captures all of these, or close? And what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on — Kanye West's early albums, which I loved before everything went off the rails and which don't themselves contain the views he later embraced?
