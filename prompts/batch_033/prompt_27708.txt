I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about moral compromise, and I've been collecting my gut verdicts on cases where two people with different value systems each give up something they regard as wrong so that both get more of what they value. I want to know what principle, if any, captures these calls, what it says about a case I'm still stuck on, and which of my verdicts it would force me to drop.

Here are the cases.

A vegan and a halal-observant Muslim co-host a big family dinner. The vegan buys and serves a halal chicken dish for the Muslim side, and in exchange the Muslim side agrees that everything else on the menu is plant-based and takes all the leftovers. I think that's fine.

A pacifist city councilor votes for a modest increase in the police weapons budget in exchange for a much larger youth-mentoring program that she thinks will prevent more violence than the weapons will cause. I think that's not fine, and I'd be genuinely angry if she did it.

A socialist and a libertarian councilor trade votes: the socialist gets a public broadband network, the libertarian gets zoning changes that let developers build denser market-rate housing. Each thinks the other's policy is a real mistake but not a serious moral wrong. Fine.

A doctor who thinks assisted dying is wrong agrees to refer patients to another doctor who will carry it out, on the condition that the hospital opens a palliative-care ward. I think that's not fine, even though I can see the ward would help more people than the referrals hurt.

A reporter who treats source confidentiality as absolute agrees to name one low-level source in exchange for the government dropping charges against three other journalists' sources. I think that's not fine, but it feels less bad than the doctor case, and I can't say why.

The pair that's bugging me is the vegan one and the pacifist one. Both are "do a small wrong to get a larger amount of what you value," and I feel completely different about them. Is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? And what does it predict about the case I keep going back and forth on: an environmentalist who thinks any new highway construction is wrong votes for a small highway interchange in exchange for a large package of transit funding and wetland restoration?
