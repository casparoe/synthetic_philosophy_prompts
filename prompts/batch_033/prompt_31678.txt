Both Mikhail Bongard's pattern-recognition problems and Nelson Goodman's "new riddle of induction" involve a finite set of examples that radically underdetermines the concept or rule one is supposed to extract from them. Compare the two challenges. In particular: (1) Is the underdetermination exposed by Bongard problems the same kind of underdetermination that Goodman identifies with predicates like "grue," or are they distinct problems that happen to converge on the same broad worry? (2) If a satisfactory solution to one were found, would it carry over to the other, or do they demand fundamentally different kinds of theoretical resources? (3) Which of the two poses the deeper obstacle to the idea that concepts can be learned or induced from examples alone, and why? In your answer, give at least one concrete Bongard-style example (described in words) and explain how a Goodman-style grue-like predicate would complicate the induction, or why it would fail to do so.
