I'm in a political philosophy class on moral disagreement and compromise, and we spent a whole seminar on the idea that people with different value systems can get gains from trade by compromising morally: each side gives on something they think is only mildly wrong to get the other side to give on something they think is really wrong. I've been trying to figure out whether this actually works, and I'd like a probability estimate with the reasoning laid out, not just a vibe.

Here's the case I keep coming back to. Two legislators, Priya and Sam. Priya thinks factory farming is a serious moral catastrophe and that a gas-car ban would be a mild overreach (so mildly wrong to vote for). Sam thinks the gas-car ban is a serious moral priority and that a factory-farming ban would be a mild overreach (mildly wrong to vote for). They cut a deal: Priya votes for Sam's gas-car ban, Sam votes for Priya's factory-farm ban, both pass. By Priya's lights the outcome is a big net gain (huge good from the farming ban minus a small bad from the car ban); by Sam's lights it's also a big net gain.

What's your actual credence that this vote trade is morally permissable for each of them — not just prudent or mutually advantagous, but something neither of them does wrong in doing? I want a number, and I want the decomposition: how much rides on whether there's enough of a shared scale of moral importance across their two value systems for the gains-from-trade logic to even get a grip; how much on whether voting for a policy you believe is wrong is itself wrong even when it secures something you think is much more important (so how deontological vs consequentialist their own views turn out to be); how much on whether treating your moral convictions as bargaining chips damages integrity or the norms of democratic decision-making; and any other terms you think belong. Give each term a rough probability and a one-line justification, show how they combine into the final number, and tell me which term your credence is most sensitive to — and what evidence or argument would move it most.
