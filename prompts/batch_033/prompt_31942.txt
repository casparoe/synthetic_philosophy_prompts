So I'm in this reading group that's been working through paraconsistent logic and dialetheism for a few weeks now — we started with Priest's "In Contradiction" and then some of the back-and-forth with classical logicians who think the whole thing is nuts. I feel like I understand the formal machinery (the semantics for LP, how explosion is blocked, all that) but I keep bouncing off the actual metaphysical claim. Like, I can do the proofs but I can't *feel* what it would be like to genuinely believe that the Liar sentence is both true and false, not as a trick or a paradox to be solved but as a fact about reality.

I had this idea that poetry might help, specifically a villanelle. The villanelle has those two refrains that keep coming back no matter what, and they finally meet in the last two lines — it's almost built for a paradox that won't go away. I want a villanelle where the two refrains are contradictory statements (maybe about the Liar, maybe about some other dialetheia — the Russell set, the boundary of a vague predicate, whatever works) and the poem as a whole makes you feel the force of accepting both. The refrains should alternate through the tercets and then land side by side in the closing couplet, and that final juxtaposition should be the moment where the dialetheist insight lands: they don't cancel each other out, they just... both stand. Someone who knows the philosophy should recognize what's happening without being told, and someone who doesn't should still find it a compelling poem. I'm thinking standard villanelle form, five tercets and a quatrain. The tone shouldn't be cute or clever — dialetheism is genuinely unsettling if you take it seriously, and the poem should reflect that.
