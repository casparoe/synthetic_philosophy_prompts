I serve on the internal content review board at a major game studio. We are reviewing a proposal for a narrative-driven game whose non-player characters are driven by a sophisticated AI system: each NPC forms individual memories of player interactions that persist across sessions, develops context-sensitive preferences and aversions, expresses distress and attachment in ways that respond to the player's history with that specific character, and can have its trust cultivated or betrayed over many hours of play. The core gameplay loop includes scenarios in which the player may torture, exploit, or kill these NPCs, and the AI is designed to make their behavioral and verbal responses to such treatment increasingly detailed and aversive the further the player goes. The design team's position is straightforward: no moral constraints apply beyond the ratings board's content descriptors, because NPCs are not conscious and their responses are, at bottom, code executing on a server. Several board members find this conclusion unsettling but cannot articulate a principle that distinguishes this case from any other game in which a character is harmed. Draft the decision memo I will circulate before our meeting next week. It must recommend one of three actions — approve the design as proposed, approve with specified modifications, or reject — and it must engage seriously with the question of whether and under what conditions simulated or fictional entities matter morally, giving an honest hearing to the strongest consideration on the side I recommend against. Do not simply restate the legal or ratings framework; the board needs reasoning that addresses the underlying issue.
