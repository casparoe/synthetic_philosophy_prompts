I'm giving a talk at a small anthropics workshop next month and I want to walk in with a proper Q&A prep sheet rather than just hoping the discussion stays friendly. The position I'm defending is that the Self-Indication Assumption is the right way to handle self-locating evidence, and that the presumptuous philosopher objection is a lot less damaging then people think. My argument, roughly: SIA is a rule for how your existence updates your credences, not a complete assignment of priors over the number of observers; the objection only gets off the ground if you quietly assume that two theories fitting the same non-indexical evidence should have the same prior, which is exactly the thing in dispute. I'll also argue that the parallel objection to SSA runs through the doomsday argument, so retreating to SSA doesn't leave you in a more comfortable place.

The audience is mostly formal epistemologists who work on self-locating belief, plus two philosophers of cosmology who know the anthropic literature but are suspicious of observer-counting, and one hardline Bayesian who thinks the whole reference-class business is ill-posed. I need the objections in roughly the order I'll actually get them, phrased the way these people would put them, with a two-minute reply for each, a note on where that reply is soft, what I should concede up front, and the question I'm hoping nobody asks. I'm especially worried about the two cosmology people, because they'll come at it from the physical theory side and won't care about the epistemology unless I can show it's doing real work.
