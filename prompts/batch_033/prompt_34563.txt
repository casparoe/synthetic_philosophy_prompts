I'm in a political philosophy reading group that has been debating the paradox of tolerance for the better part of a term. Two members in particular have been at it for weeks: one defends what you might call the Limit View — that a tolerant society cannot extend tolerance to those who would destroy it, and must therefore be intolerant of intolerance itself if it is to survive. The other defends the Unconditional View — that tolerance is, by its nature, a commitment to non-interference with beliefs and practices one disapproves of, and that the alleged paradox dissolves once you distinguish tolerating speech from tolerating rights-violating action. They have agreed to try an adversarial collaboration and produce a joint statement they can both sign. I have been asked to draft it, but I am too close to the debate and keep framing things in ways one side rejects. I would like you to write it instead.

The document should open with a numbered list of claims both parties can accept — about what tolerance is, what the paradox asserts, what historical cases establish, what the practical stakes are, and what both sides agree is not in dispute. These should be substantive enough that the list itself represents progress. It should then contain a disagreements table with the following columns: (a) the precise formulation of each residual disagreement, worded so that each side would accept the characterisation of its own view; (b) the crux on which the disagreement turns — the sub-question that, if settled, would move at least one side; and (c) a concrete test, hypothetical, or argument both sides agree bears on that crux, together with each side's prediction of what the test would show. If you conclude that some part of their dispute is merely verbal, identify it and explain why.

I want a committed response — one that makes definite judgements about what can and cannot go into the joint statement, rather than laying out options. Please keep the document to no more than 950 words.
