I've been going back and forth with a friend about this idea of "free-floating rationales" — the thought that there can be reasons for things even when no mind actually entertains or grasps those reasons. Like, the idea that a reason could just be out there, unattached to any reasoner. My friend keeps saying this is obviously incoherent: a reason is always someone's reason, it's the kind of thing that has to be had by a subject. I'm not so sure, but I'm struggling to come up with a really clean example that would make the idea vivid and not just sound like spooky metaphysics.

So what I'd love is: can you give me a concrete example of a situation where it's at least plausible that there's a reason for something — a genuine normative or explanatory reason — but there is no actual mind that has or entertains that reason? And walk me through why the example has that feature. I'm especially interested in cases where the reason isn't just unknown to everyone (that seems easy — lots of reasons are unknown), but where it's not even the kind of thing that could be cashed out in terms of what some idealized agent would think, or what some community would converge on. Like, a reason with no reasoner at all, not even a hypothetical one. If you think no such example works, I'd want to hear why the attempt to construct one always collapses.
