So I've been collecting my friend Dana's verdicts on these anthropic puzzle cases, and I want you to predict what she'd say about a new one. She's been going through a list of thought experiments about what you should believe when you don't know who you are or how many people exist, and I've got her answers on four of them.

The first one is the hundred rooms case. A hundred people wake up in identical rooms; one room has a red wall and the other ninety-nine have blue walls, and no one knows which room they're in. Dana says each person's credence that they're in the red room should be one percent. The second is a coin flip where heads means everyone's light is red and tails means everyone's light is blue, with the same number of people either way. Dana says you just update on seeing red the ordinary Bayesian way. The third is two theories of the universe that make exactly the same prediction about how many observers exist, and you know you'd exist under both; she says the fact that you exist doesn't favor either theory, becuase you could only ever find yourself existing. The fourth is a duplicator that makes a thousand copies of you, after which you wake up in a room not knowing whether your the original or a copy; she says you should be about 1000 to 1 that you're a copy.

Now the new case. A fair coin is flipped. If it lands heads, exactly one observer comes into existence, alone in a windowless room. If it lands tails, exactly one million observers come into existence, each alone in a windowless room. You wake up in a room with no memory of the toss and no way to tell which it was. What should your credence be that the coin landed heads?

Do you think Dana will say 50/50, or about one in a million, or something else? I'm less interested in the number than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rule or rules that fit her first four verdicts just as well, which rule is your best guess and why, and what single additional case I should put to her to settle it. Please write the whole thing as continuous prose, like an email, with no headings, bullets, bold, or tables.
