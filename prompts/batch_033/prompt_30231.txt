I am a third-year undergraduate in a democratic theory seminar and will be running a 45-minute session for our 12-person class on the boundary problem: who should be included in the demos before democratic procedures can operate, and why that question cannot be settled democratically without circularity. Design a structured simulation in which participants, with no introductory lecture, must constitute a demos for a binding decision that affects both current insiders and outsiders (for example, a university policy affecting students, staff, and nearby residents). Arrange the roles and rules so that participants discover for themselves that every inclusion rule either presupposes a prior demos or appeals to a principle such as that all affected interests should be included or that all those subject to the resulting coercion should be included, which then needs its own specification; include at least one moment where these two principles visibly select different people. By the end, participants should have a felt grasp of the circularity and of how the main inclusion principles can pull apart. The design should include a timeline, materials, a debrief mapping specific moments to the theoretical positions, a list of what will predictably go wrong on a first run and how to handle each failure, and an honest note on which feature of the real boundary problem the simulation cannot reproduce. Return the design as a JSON object with fields: title, setting (group size, duration, materials), scenario, roles (array of objects with role, information, objective), phases (array of objects with phase, minutes, instructions), debrief (array of objects with moment, concept, facilitator_question), likely_failures (array of objects with failure, symptom, fix), cannot_reproduce (string).
