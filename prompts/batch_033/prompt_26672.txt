I'm in a philosophy club at school, and we've been reading Douglas Hofstadter's *Gödel, Escher, Bach* — specifically the dialogue about Aunt Hillary, who turns out to be an ant colony. The idea is that individual ants are simple creatures following simple rules, but the colony as a whole seems to exhibit something like intelligence, maybe even a mind. The question is whether that colony-level mind is a real thing, or just a convenient way of talking about the ants.

That got me thinking about a practice I encounter all the time: group projects. At my school, when we do a group project, the whole group gets a single grade. The teacher evaluates the final product — the poster, the presentation, the report — and assigns one mark to all four of us. But here's what puzzles me: the same school also has a policy stating that grades are meant to reflect individual achievement. The same teacher who gives group grades will, in other contexts, insist that grading must be fair to each student as an individual. And when a group project goes badly, students immediately start giving accounts of who did what — "I wrote the whole introduction, she didn't do anything" — as if the group grade is somehow missing the real story. Yet the school doesn't abandon group grading; it seems to think both practices make sense side by side.

I'm trying to figure out what philosophical commitments are baked into the practice of assigning a single grade to a group. Does it presuppose that a group can be a genuine agent in something like the way the ant colony might be — that a group can have a quality like "excellence" or "shoddiness" that isn't just the sum of what each member contributed? Or can you defend group grading without going anywhere near that idea? And is the practice actually consistent with the same school's commitment to individual assessment, or is there a genuine tension that the school is simply ignoring? If we replaced the presupposed view with a different one — say, a thoroughgoing individualism about achievement — what would have to change in how the school evaluates group work?
