I'm on parental leave with my first kid, and I've gotten weirdly fixated on a category of actions I keep noticing: things people praise you for even though nobody would say you were required to do them. Change a blowout at 3am and you're a great dad; refuse and, at most, you're a little disappointing. That gap between 'good' and 'required' has sent me down the supererogation rabbit hole, and I'm about to start reading the actual theories — the classic saint-and-hero line, the people who think the whole category is incoherent, the consequentialists trying to make room for it. Before I read any more of the the theories, I want the spec: what should I be asking of a theory of supererogation?

Write me the desiderata. For each one, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say whether it's non-negotiable or just a preference of one camp. I want at least these covered: the distinction between good-but-not-required and merely permissible, with a case that keeps them apart; the paradox any theory has to dissolve (if an act is morally best, why doesn't 'ought' apply to it?); the asymmetry between good-beyond-duty acts and bad-but-not-forbidden ones, if that asymmetry is real (and if it isn't, say why the denial is itself a requirement on a theory); and the psychological constraints on doing a supererogatory act while thinking of it as optional, since that thought might ruin it. Flag any pair of these you think can't be jointly satisfied — that's the part I most want. Format: exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion. Four items should be the desiderata; the fifth should say how confident you are in the list overall and which single requirement you're least sure is really a requirement.
