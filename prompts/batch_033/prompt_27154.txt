I'm taking a graduate seminar on epistemic logic this term, and we've been working through the problem of logical omniscience — the awkward fact that standard Kripke models treat knowledge as closed under logical consequence, which would mean that anyone who knows the axioms of Peano arithmetic knows all its theorems, including the ones no mathematician has yet proved. During last Tuesday's discussion, one of the students — a formal epistemologist who has been defending the models all semester — said, with some exasperation, 'Knowledge is closed under logical consequence. That's just what knowledge is. The models are idealisations, not descriptions of actual psychology.' When someone pressed him on whether a child who knows that 2 + 2 = 4 thereby knows that there are infinitely many primes, he clarified that he only meant closure under 'trivial' or 'one-step' consequence. But then the discussion spiralled, and by the end I had no idea what claim was actually being made, let alone whether it helps the models.

I would like to go to office hours with a proper map of the options. Can you take the slogan 'knowledge is closed under logical consequence' and lay out the distinct precise theses it could be expressing?
