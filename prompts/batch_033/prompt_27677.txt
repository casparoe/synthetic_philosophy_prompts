My friend and I have been going back and forth for months about what makes someone the same person over time, and she has this whole pile of sci-fi thought experiments she likes to run. She's not a philosopher or anything, she just works the night shift and has a lot of time to think. I've been writing down her verdicts, and they feel pretty consistent to me, but I cant figure out what rule is actually doing the work underneath them. Here's what she's said so far: if a machine scans you, destroys your body and brain, and rebuilds an exact copy on Mars with all your memories and personality, she says you survive, you wake up on Mars. If your whole brain is transplanted into a different body, she says you survive. But if a brain injury completely wipes your memories and changes your personality, same body and same brain, she says the old person didn't survive in any way that matters.

The next case I want to spring on her is the split one: your brain is divided into its two hemispheres, and each half is transplanted into its own fresh body. Both people wake up with your memories and personality, both insist they're you, and the original body and whole brain are gone. I'm less interested in the exact answer than in the rule she seems to be running, what other rules fit her old verdicts just as well, and what each of those rules would say about the split case. Give me your best guess about what she'll say, why you'd bet on that rule rather than the alternatives, and what one follow-up scenerio would most cleanly settle which rule she's actually using.
