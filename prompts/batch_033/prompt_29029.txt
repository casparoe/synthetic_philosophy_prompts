Consider the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem: with at least three alternatives, every resolute social choice function that is strategy-proof, onto, and defined on the unrestricted domain of ordinal preferences is dictatorial. The following are standard ways in which the literature responds to, or tries to escape, this impossibility. Please rank them from least costly to most costly as concessions, where the baseline is the original ideal of a resolute, deterministic, non-dictatorial, strategy-proof aggregation of arbitrary ordinal preferences over three or more alternatives. Use a tiered ranking (for example: negligible cost / moderate cost / severe cost / prohibitive cost), and give a one-sentence justification for each placement.

(a) Domain restriction: require preferences to be single-peaked or otherwise structured, so that a non-dictatorial, strategy-proof rule such as the median rule becomes available.
(b) Weakened resoluteness: allow the rule to return a set of tied winners rather than a single winner.
(c) Randomization: replace deterministic rules with lotteries over outcomes, such as the random dictatorship rule.
(d) Weakened strategy-proofness: accept mechanisms that are only approximately strategy-proof, strategy-proof in the large, or immune only to obvious manipulations.
(e) Cardinalization: abandon purely ordinal preferences, elicit cardinal utility or willingness to pay, and use mechanisms such as Vickrey–Clarke–Groves.
(f) Outright acceptance of dictatorship: retain strategy-proofness and resoluteness on the unrestricted domain at the cost of concentrating all power in one voter.

In your answer, state any ties explicitly, and if two responses are ranked together, explain why neither concession is clearly worse than the other.
