I was talking with a friend about whether it's ever okay to believe something without having enough evidence for it, and he gave me this summary of the classic James-vs-Clifford debate:

"Clifford argued that it's morally wrong to believe anything on insufficient evidence. His famous example was a shipowner who convinces himself his leaky ship is seaworthy because he wants to make the voyage — Clifford said he's guilty even if the ship somehow makes it, because the wrong was in the believing itself. James pushed back and said that's too strict. His point was that for certain big questions — especially religious belief — the evidence is never going to settle things, and if you wait for proof you'll just end up suspending judgment forever. In those cases, James said, your 'passional nature' gets to decide, and it's legitimate to believe what helps you live a better life. Basically, Clifford said 'follow the evidence wherever it leads,' and James said 'evidence has limits, and when it runs out, go with what works.'"

I've read a bit of James and this feels like its missing some important qualifications, but I'm not sure I could say exactly where it goes wrong. Can you go through his summary and tell me what's accurate, what's distorted, and whether the overall picture he's giving me of the debate is fair?
