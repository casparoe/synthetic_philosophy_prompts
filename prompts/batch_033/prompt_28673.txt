So I recently got elected to the board of my housing co-op — mostly because nobody else wanted to do it — and we keep getting complaints about this old rule that the back door of the building has to stay locked between 11pm and 6am. The back door has the same keypad as the front door and the back parking lot is right there, so it isnt really a security thing; its just a rule from the 70s that nobody can explain. I wanted to propose deleting it, and my friend (who did one political theory course and loves this stuff) told me I was walking straight into Chesterton's fence. His summary of the idea went like this: 'Chesterton's fence says you should never tear down an institution or a rule unless you can fully explain the original purpose it was put there to serve. But with most old rules you can never fully recover the original purpose, so the safe conclusion is to leave them alone. Burke is basically making the same point: society is too complicated to redesign, inherited practices should get the benefit of the doubt, and the burden of proof is always on the person who wants to change something. So if you can't give a complete account of what the back-door rule is for, you have to keep it.'

I'm not a 'rip it all down' person and I do think old rules sometimes encode knowledge we've forgotten, but something about the 'never' and the 'complete account' feels off to me, and I'm not sure Burke and Chesterton are actually saying the same thing. Can you tell me which parts of his summary are fair to the actual positions, where he's overstating or conflating, and whether his conclusion about my back-door rule actually follows from the accurate parts? And if I'm honest, I'd like a way to push back next time that doesnt just misrepresent him the way I suspect he's misrepresenting Chesterton.
