Molyneux's problem asks whether a person born blind who could distinguish a cube from a sphere by touch would recognize them on first sight if they gained vision. But we now know about cross-modal plasticity: in congenitally blind people, the visual cortex gets recruited for tactile and auditory processing. So when someone gains sight through surgery later in life, their visual cortex is already organized around touch. Doesn't this mean the question is empirically settled — not in the way Locke or Molyneux expected, but settled nonetheless? The answer is no, and the reason is cortical reorganization, not a lack of innate cross-modal transfer. So why do philosophers still treat it as an open problem?
