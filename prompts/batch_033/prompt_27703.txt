A blog post I read summarized Kim's causal exclusion argument like this: "Kim showed that nonreductive physicalism can't account for mental causation. If your decision to raise your hand is a mental event distinct from any physical event in your brain, and the movement of your hand already has a complete physical cause, then by causal exclusion the mental event is either redundant or a second cause over and above the physical one. Since nonreductive physicalists won't identify the mental event with a physical one, and won't accept systematic overdetermination, their view forces them to treat the mental event as epiphenomenal. So the only defensible positions left are reductive physicalism or dualism." I've got the gist but it seem a bit too tidy to me.
