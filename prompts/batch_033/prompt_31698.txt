I've got a puzzle I keep tripping over. The whole online privacy regime—cookie banners, privacy policies, the little "I agree" checkbox—seems to treat consent as the thing that makes data collection okay. Click accept and whatever the company does with your behavioral data, location history, and the rest is treated as legitimate. But then the same society treats a hacked database or someone reading your email as a violation even if you never bothered to secure anything. So consent does all the moral work in the first case and almost none in the second. What view of the value of privacy does the consent-and-privacy-policy regime presuppose? Is it privacy-as-control, privacy-as-secrecy, privacy-as-protection-from-power, or something else? More carefully: does the practice strictly require one of those theories, or is it compatiable with several? And if you swapped in a different theory of privacy's value, what would actually have to change about how consent banners and privacy policies are designed?
