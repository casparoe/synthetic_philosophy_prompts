So I've been doing TNR with this colony of feral cats behind my apartment building for about two years now — trapping them, getting them fixed, feeding them, the whole thing. I got into it because I felt bad for them, especially the kittens. A buddy of mine who's really into that whole effective altruism scene told me the other day that I might actually be making things worse. His argument was basically: these cats live pretty rough lives — disease, cars, cold winters — and by keeping the colony going I'm just ensuring more of them exist to suffer. Plus they kill a ton of birds and small mammals, so I'm indirectly causing all that death too. He wasn't being a jerk about it, he was genuinely like "have you thought about whether the net effect is actually good?"

I haven't been able to stop thinking about it. On one hand, I see these cats every day and they're alive and purring and when one of them is sick I take them to the vet. That feels like it matters. On the other hand, I can't really refute his logic. If I'm being honest, most of these cats do have pretty hard lives, and the birds... yeah. And the thing is, I know he'd say the same thing about wild animals more broadly — like, if nature is just this brutal cycle of predation and starvation and parasites, maybe trying to help individual animals is missing the point, or even making the total suffering worse. But if I follow that logic all the way, where does it leave me? Just shrugging at suffering because it's "natural"?

I guess what I'm asking is: should I stop? Is what I'm doing actually the right thing? I feel like my gut and my brain are pointing in completly different directions and I need someone to help me think through this.
