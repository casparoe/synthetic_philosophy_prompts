So there's this weird situation in my department: a set theorist and a finitist got stuck co-teaching a philosophy of math seminar, and after a semester of arguing they've decided to try an adversarial collaboration — actually write up what they agree on and where exactly they part ways. They asked a few of us to look at a draft, but honestly I think they're still talking past each other on some things, and I want to see what an outsider would produce. So: draft the joint statement that a working set theorist who accepts actual infinities (Cantorian, transfinite hierarchy, etc.) and a finitist who rejects actual infinities (only potential infinities, no completed infinite totalities) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

The document should have two parts. First, a numbered list of claims both can affirm — about the mathematics, about what Hilbert's hotel shows and doesn't show, about what Cantor's diagonal argument establishes if you grant the premises and what it doesn't establish if you don't, about the role of infinity in physics, about what each side is and isn't committed to. These shouldn't be weaselly; they should be substantive enough that it's nontrivial they both signed.

Second, a disagreements table. Columns: the question they disagree on; the Cantorian's answer, phrased so the Cantorian says "yes, that's what I think"; the finitist's answer, phrased so the finitist says the same; and whether this disagreement is a crux (i.e., if it were resolved, at least one side would change their overall view). For each crux, add a row underneath specifying what kind of argument or evidence both would accept as bearing on it, and what each predicts about it.

I'm especially interested in whether they can agree on what would count as a reason to abandon their own view — that's the real test of whether the collaboration is genuine. Also, I suspect at least one of their disagreements might be partly verbal (e.g., about what "exists" means in mathematics). If so, flag which one and say exactly what the verbal component is and what remains after you factor it out.
