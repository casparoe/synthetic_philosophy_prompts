I am the student member of my high school's Student Government Constitution and Rules Committee, and I need a short written opinion to circulate before our meeting on Thursday. Please take on the role of a professional philosopher who specializes in social epistemology and collective decision-making and who is also experienced at drafting institutional policy memos. Think through the strongest version of each side before you settle on a recommendation, and then draft the memo I will circulate.

The case. Our student government is revising the section of the constitution that governs how contested honor-code violations are adjudicated. At present, a five-member Honor Council panel—students who are elected and then trained in hearing procedures—hears the evidence and votes; a finding of responsibility requires at least four of the five votes. A petition has now qualified a proposed amendment for the ballot. Under the amendment, any student found responsible by the Honor Council could demand a school-wide referendum, conducted through the school app, in which every student could vote; the majority vote of the entire student body would be final and binding.

The committee must decide whether to endorse the amendment, reject it, or send back a revised version, and we are split. One faction argues that the whole student body is more likely to reach the correct verdict than five elected students: the process is more democratic, less vulnerable to capture by a clique, and, as one member keeps putting it, a large group of voters who are each more likely right than wrong will outperform a small panel—he cites the Condorcet jury theorem. The other faction argues that a school-wide vote would be worse than the panel, because students would vote on rumors, friendships, and social pressure, many would not examine the evidence, and their votes would be strongly correlated with one another; on this view the theorem's reassuring conclusion is mathematically sound but its conditions fail badly here.

Please draft the memo. It should recommend one concrete course of action for the committee to take, with the reasoning behind it; it should spell out the conditions that would have to hold for a school-wide majority vote to be a reliable way of finding the facts, and say whether they hold in our setting; and it should engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side rather than dismissing it. End with an actionable bottom line the committee can actually vote on. Do not include citations or a reading list; the committee needs your reasoning applied to this proposal, not references.
