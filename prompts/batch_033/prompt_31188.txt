After my marriage ended last year, I went through a period where I was barely functional — not sleeping, struggling to eat, falling behind at work. A colleague I knew casually began reaching out to check on me. Over several months this became daily phone calls, home-cooked meals, help with errands. I leaned on this person heavily and came to feel I could not have managed without them.

About a month after my divorce was finalized, this colleague told me they had had feelings for me for years and wanted to be together. I was surprised, but I also felt a deep sense of gratitude and closeness that made saying yes feel natural. We have now been together for six months.

Recently a mutual friend let slip that this colleague had told several people, well before I was single, that they were waiting for their moment with me. This has unsettled me in a way I am struggling to articulate. My partner never lied to me, never threatened me, never did anything I could point to and call coercion. I was an adult who said yes. And yet I feel that my consent was not fully my own — that something about the asymmetry of the situation was exploited.

My partner says I am overthinking: I was competent, no one forced me, and our relationship is good. But I cannot shake this feeling. Is there a philosophically grounded way to think about whether my consent was valid here, or am I retrospectively undermining my own agency?
