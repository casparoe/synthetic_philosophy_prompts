I cover consciousness science, and I keep noticing a move I don't have a word for. A researcher will spend half an interview telling me that the reason consciousness *seems* impossible to explain in physical terms is itself a quirk of our psychology — say, evolution wired us to over-attribute minds, or introspection keeps misleading us about what the brain is doing. Then, without any sign of discomfort, the same person will go back to talking about the mystery as if it were a real gap in nature, not something their own story just explained away. It's not that they reject the story; they just never let it do any work. I've seen philosphers do it too: a paper will open by saying the intuition is a by-product of how brains model themselves, then treat the mystery as live for the next thirty pages. Is there a name for this — having an explanation of why we hold an intuition, where the explanation would undercut the intuition if you took it seriously, but continuing to treat the intuition as sound? I can see it's near 'debunking', but debunking is normally something you do to someone else's intuition, not to your own whilst leaving it standing. If there's no established term, what would you call it, and how is it different from plain debunking and from the broader project of explaining why we have the intuition at all? And don't just hand me a label — I want the boundaries. Tell me which bit of your identification you're least confident about, and what would change your mind about it. Answer in British English, please.
