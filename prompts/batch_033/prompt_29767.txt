I'm trying to work out what I actually think about the nonidentity problem, going by my gut on cases rather than theory. Here are my verdicts; they feel consistent to me but I suspect they aren't.

A country has a choice between a fast industrial boom that uses up a resource and a slower path that conserves it. The boom leaves people two centuries later with a decent but noticeably worse life than they'd have had under conservation, and because the policy changes who meets whom and when, none of those future people would have existed under the conservation policy. Verdict: the boom was wrong.

A fourteen-year-old has a baby she isn't ready to raise well. The child's life is hard but clearly worth living. If she had waited a few years, a different child would have existed. Verdict: it was a bad decision, but I don't think she wronged the child.

A couple conceives a child with the settled plan of raising it as an unpaid servant from a young age. The child's life is on balance worth living, though constrained and painful in ways that could have been avoided. Verdict: wrong, and it wrongs the child — not just the parents or the community.

A lab botches a genetic test and a woman isn't told she carries a condition; she has a child with a serious but not devastating disability, and the child's life is worth living. If the lab hadn't botched it, this child would never have existed, because the couple would likely of had a different child, or none. Verdict: the lab wronged the parents, but I don't think the child was wronged by being born.

Our current emissions will make life in 2200 harder than it would have been under a cleaner path, and here too the specific people alive in 2200 wouldn't have existed under that cleaner path. Verdict: wrong, and I do want to say those future people have a complaint against us, even if no particular one of them is worse off than they would have been.

The case I can't settle: a couple can conceive now or wait six months, and they know that if they wait, the different child would have a much easier, happier life. They conceive now, and the child who is born has a good life. Is that wrong, and if so wrong to whom?

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? And what does it say about the case I can't settle? Which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? I want a committed answer: state one principle (or one small candidate set) precisely, defend it, and don't just lay out the options. Start by stating the principle in a sentence or two, and keep the whole thing to at most 700 words.
