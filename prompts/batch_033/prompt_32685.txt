I'm a high school student, and over the past few months I've noticed a pattern in two different parts of my life that I can't quite place. Last month, my school's student council ran a fundraising auction, and the last item was a $40 gift card. Bidding opened at $5 and climbed normally until two students got drawn into a back-and-forth. Once the bids passed $40, the rest of the room started laughing, but the two kept raising each other. The runner-up finally dropped out at $63 and, under the rules, still had to pay her last bid and received nothing. The winner paid $67 for a $40 card and looked relieved rather than happy. Afterward, I realized that neither had been simply stubborn: the person who was behind at any given moment faced losing whatever she had already bid and getting nothing, and raising by one more increment was the only move that gave her a chance to avoid that loss. But the same reasoning applied to the other person, so every raise recreated the trap for the new second-place bidder.

A few weeks later I watched something similar happen with no opponent at all. My environmental club had decided to build a large display for the district sustainability fair. We spent around $120 on materials and several weekends on construction. By the second week the design was clearly unstable, we were behind schedule, and someone suggested we abandon it and submit a smaller poster instead. But another member argued that we had already put in too much to stop, and that stopping now would mean all of that time and money was wasted. The club agreed to spend a little more to try to salvage it. The same conversation repeated at the next two meetings, and each additional purchase and evening of work made stopping feel more wasteful, so we kept going long past the point where the display could possibly be worth what we were putting into it. We did finish something, but it didn't place, and we ended up well over budget.

I keep coming back to the similarity between these two situations. In both, an earlier investment became a reason to invest more, and each new investment strengthened that reason even as the overall situation got worse. But in the auction there were two people whose choices fed each other, while in the club there was one group arguing with itself and nothing to win. Is there an established name for the general pattern, or are these two different things that only look alike? I am less interested in the label itself than in the boundary: what exactly makes the auction case run the way it does, what exactly keeps the project case going, and whether a single account can cover both without blurring an important difference. If there is no single established concept, I would appreciate a precise characterization of each and, if useful, a name that marks the distinction.
