Write an essay on whether money pump arguments succeed in establishing genuine requirements of diachronic rationality. Consider the following challenge: money pumps appear to show that agents with cyclic preferences or non-exponential discounting can be exploited, but exploitation is a practical liability, not necessarily a rational defect — an agent might simply be unlucky to live in a world with shrewd traders, and the fact that someone could take her money does not by itself show that her preferences are incoherent. Address the foresight reply (that an agent who sees the full sequence of trades coming can block the pump at the first trade) and whether it undermines the argument's normative force or merely relocates the irrationality to an earlier step. Weigh the analogy to Dutch book arguments for probabilism: if Dutch books are now widely accepted as diagnostically useful for exposing incoherence despite similar worries about foresight and exploitation, should money pump arguments be treated the same way? Reach and justify a conclusion about what, if anything, vulnerability to a money pump actually demonstrates.
