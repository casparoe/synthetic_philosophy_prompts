I'm a student in a philosophy of mind course, and for my final project I'm drafting a set of interview questions for Daniel Dennett about his multiple drafts model of consciousness and his critique of the Cartesian theater. I want the questions to go beyond having him restate the view. In particular, I want at least one question on each of the following pressure points, with a follow-up that anticipates his most likely reply:

- The intuition that there must be a place and time where conscious experience comes together — why isn't the burden of proof on Dennett to show there isn't one, rather than on the Cartesian theater defender to show there is?
- The relationship between the multiple drafts model and empirical findings on temporal binding (e.g., the color phi phenomenon, Libet-style experiments): does the model make any predictions that differ from what a Cartesian theater theorist could accommodate post hoc?
- The distinction between Orwellian and Stalinesque revisions: Dennett argues that in some cases there is no fact of the matter about which is occurring. Press him on whether this is a claim about epistemology (we cannot tell) or metaphysics (there is no fact), and what would settle that distinction.
- How the model handles phenomenal consciousness: does the multiple drafts model eliminate it, reduce it, or explain it? If the latter, what does the explanation look like in a concrete case?
- The "fame in the brain" metaphor: what work does it do that "multiple drafts" does not already do, and does it risk smuggling back in a homunculus who decides which contents become famous?

For each question, include the follow-up I should ask if he gives the reply you would expect from his published work. The questions should be ordered to build on each other, so that later questions draw on distinctions established earlier.
