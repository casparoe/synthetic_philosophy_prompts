I'm running a philosophy of probability reading group and we've been going back and forth on the Sleeping Beauty problem for weeks. The halfers and thirders in the group are talking past each other and I'm getting tired of it. I want them to produce an adversarial-collaboration document — the kind of thing where both sides sit down and actually hash out what they agree on, where exactly they part ways, and what would change their minds.

Can you draft that document? It should have:
- A numbered list of claims both sides can sign (about the setup, about what Beauty knows when, about what the question is even asking)
- A table of disagreements, with each position stated in wording its own defenders would accept
- The cruxes — the sub-questions where if you settled them, at least one side would have to budge
- For each crux, some test case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about what it shows

Don't just summarize the debate — actually try to produce the document they could both put their names on. If you think some of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why.
