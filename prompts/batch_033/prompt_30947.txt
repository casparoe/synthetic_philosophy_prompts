So I got into this argument with a friend who's pretty religious and it kind of spiraled into me writing out this whole chain of reasoning that I now can't figure out how to escape. I'm a software engineer so I'm used to debugging logic but this one has me stumped. Here's basically what I ended up with:

(1) Either God commands X because X is morally right, or X is morally right because God commands X. (This is the Euthyphro thing, right? I looked it up after.)

(2) If God commands X because X is morally right, then moral standards are independent of God — God is just tracking morality, not creating it. So God isn't the source of morality, He's more like a perfect moral thermometer.

(3) If X is morally right because God commands X, then God's commands are what make things right or wrong.

(4) If God's commands are what make things right or wrong, then if God had commanded something like torturing innocent people for fun, that would be morally right.

(5) If torturing innocents would be right just because God said so, then morality is arbitrary — it has no deeper grounding than whatever God happens to will. It's like a variable whose value depends entirely on a divine assignment statement with no constraints.

(6) So either God isn't the source of morality, or morality is arbitrary.

(7) If morality is arbitrary in that way, it can't ground real obligations. Like, if "wrong" just means "God happens to dislike it," that doesn't seem like a real reason not to do it — it's just a fact about God's psychology.

(8) So either God isn't the source of morality, or there are no genuine moral obligations.

(9) But standard theism says both that God is the source of morality AND that we have genuine moral obligations.

(10) So theism is incoherent.

I showed this to my friend and he said I was missing something about God's nature — that God couldn't command cruelty because it's against His nature, which is goodness itself. But then I thought, wait, doesn't that just push us back to branch one? If God's nature is the standard of goodness, then goodness is independent of God's commands — it's built into what God is, and God's commands just reflect it. So we're back to God not being the source of morality, He's just the messenger. The commands aren't doing any real moral work.
