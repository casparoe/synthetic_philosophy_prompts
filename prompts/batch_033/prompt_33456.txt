Ok so I've been chewing on the moral trade idea for a while, and I want a professional philosopher who works on moral philosophy / decision theory to audit my current view before I turn it into a blog post. Think carefully, maybe start by brainstorming which pairs of commitments look like they might pull against each other, then tell me which tensions are real and rank them by severity. I'm less interested in a lecture on the literature and more in knowing where my own set of views is load-bearing in a way I haven't noticed.

Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Moral trade is real and underused. If someone else and I have different moral views, and we can each do something that advances the other's values more (by their lights) than it costs us (by ours), we should usually just do it. Refusing because it feels like selling out or impurity is usually a mistake.

2. I'm a value pluralist. There are multiple irreducible goods, no common currency that all values convert into, and some cross-category comparisons are genuinely indeterminate — there's just no fact of the matter which option is better, not even a hard-to-find fact.

3. I accept a dominance principle: if option A is better than option B by every affected party's own lights, then A is better, full stop. I dont see how you reject that without making group decisions spooky.

4. I also think there are agent-centered constraints. There are things I shouldn't do myself even if doing them produces more total value — e.g., directly harming an innocent person to prevent a slightly larger harm elsewhere. My integrity is partly about not lending my agency to things I judge wrong.

5. Concrete case: I'd happily trade with a deontologist friend — I'd give to their rights-focused charity (which I think is fine but clearly lower-impact than my top cause) in exchange for them giving to mine. That seems obviously fine.

6. But I wouldnt help promote a cause I think is actively harmful, even if the other side offered to fund my top cause at a rate that leaves me ahead by my own lights. Like, I wouldn't do comms work for an anti-vax group as part of a trade. I'd feel like I was making myself an instrument of something I think is wrong.

So does this hang together? Which pairs are actually in tension, which just look like they are, and what's the cheapest revision? Be specific — rank the tensions rather than saying it all coheres.
