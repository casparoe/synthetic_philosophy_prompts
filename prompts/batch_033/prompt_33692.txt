I need a thought experiment built to order. I want a case where a mental condition is luminous—the subject is always in a position to know they're in it when they are—but the subject fails the KK principle for a proposition they know, and the failure is structural, not just a matter of not having bothered to reflect. Constraints: no sorites or gradual-change setups (so this isn't just a Williamson anti-luminosity argument in disguise), no vagueness in the proposition, the subject should be a cognitively ordinary human, and the case shouldn't turn on failures of logical omniscience. The interesting structural feature would be something about the subject's cognitive architecture or the nature of the proposition that blocks higher-order knowledge while leaving first-order knowledge intact. After laying out the scenario, walk through: (a) why the condition is luminous, (b) what proposition the subject knows and why they don't know that they know it, (c) which feature of the case does the work. Then say what a defender of KK would attack, and whether removing that feature makes the case collapse into a standard anti-luminosity argument.
