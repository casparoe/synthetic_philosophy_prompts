The whole novel kind of turns on whether they can actually produce something together, and I want the document they end up writing to feel real. Like, not a caricature of philosophy but the kind of thing actual philosophers would recognize.

I need you to be the expert here — your a professional philosopher specializing in philosophy of language and vagueness. Take your time, think carefully, maybe brainstorm the key pressure points between these views before you write.

What I want: draft the joint statement / adversarial collaboration document these two characters would produce. It should include:

- A numbered list of claims both can sign. These shouldn't be vacuous — they should capture the real common ground, including things like what the sorites paradox actually shows, what a solution needs to do, what both views are trying to preserve.
- A disagreements table. For each point of disagreement, give the precise formulation of each side's view, worded so that each side would say "yes, that's exactly what I think" (not a strawman).
- The cruxes — the sub-questions where, if you could settle them, at least one side would have to budge.

One thing I'm especially interested in: are their parts of their dispute that turn out to be mostly verbal? Like, once they really nail down what each means by "truth" or "boundary," does some of the disagreement evaporate? If so, flag that.

The epistemicist (think Williamson-style) holds that vague predicates have sharp boundaries we can't know. The supervaluationist (think Fine-style) holds that vague predicates are semantically incomplete and admit multiple precisifications, with truth as supertruth. Don't dumb this down — my readers include philosophy grad students who'll spot if I fake it.
