I sit on the standing governance committee of a mid-sized city. We decide, case by case, whether a particular question should be handed to a randomly selected citizens' assembly, kept with the elected council, or referred to an expert or adjudicative body. A new proposal is now before us, and I would like a careful analysis of what our own precedents commit us to before we vote.

Our precedents, in chronological order:

1. We endorsed a citizens' assembly to draw up a long-term plan for cutting the city's carbon emissions, with the elected council retaining final approval of the implementation budget.
2. We rejected a citizens' assembly for the annual budget, keeping it with the elected council and its finance officers on the ground that it requires technical knowledge and ongoing accountability.
3. We endorsed a citizens' assembly to redraw the boundaries of the council's electoral wards, removing the task from the elected council's hands entirely.
4. We rejected a citizens' assembly for a request by a minority-language school to be exempted from a curriculum requirement, referring the matter instead to a rights-focused adjudicative panel.
5. We endorsed a citizens' assembly to review the city's use of public-area surveillance cameras and to recommend a new policy on retention and oversight.

The new proposal is that a citizens' assembly should decide whether to introduce a city-centre congestion charge, with the proceeds going to public transport. The charge would apply broadly to vehicles entering the centre. Our transport staff have said they can brief any assembly on the technical details in a few sessions. The elected council is split, and several members have said privately that they would vote against the charge because of the next election.

What should we decide if we want to be consistent with our own precedents? Which of our earlier decisions is the closest analog, and which would be the most misleading to treat as controlling? If our precedents genuinely underdetermine this case, set out the two or three principles that each fit them, say what each principle dictates here, and identify the further case that would best tell the principles apart. Please write the answer in British English.
