I keep running into a line of argument that goes like this: whatever you do to a video game character is no different from what happens to a character in a novel when the author writes their death — it's all fiction, no one is actually harmed, so there's no moral issue. The analogy between video games and novels gets used to settle the question in one move: since we don't worry about the ethics of reading about a character's suffering, we shouldn't worry about the ethics of causing it in a game. I've been trying to think through whether this actually works. Can you walk me through what maps onto what — what corresponds to the reader, the author, the text, the character's fixed fate — and then identify where the mapping breaks? I'm especially interested in which disanalogies matter for the moral question and which are just surface differences. And I want to know whether the conclusion — that virtual actions are morally weightless — can survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was doing all the work by quietly importing features of novels that video games don't share.
