An argument I have been turning over seems to show that a sufficiently well-informed being could violate the second law of thermodynamics, and I cannot find the step that fails.

(1) A Maxwell demon that opens a trapdoor only for fast molecules and blocks slow ones would make one chamber hot and the other cold without doing any work.
(2) The only thing the demon needs in order to do this is information about each molecule's position and velocity.
(3) Acquiring and storing that information costs nothing thermodynamically: a measurement merely correlates the demon's memory with the gas, and a memory that is never erased dissipates no heat.
(4) So a demon with enough information could lower the entropy of an isolated system without expending work, contradicting the second law.
(5) The second law is too well confirmed to abandon, so one of (1)–(3) must go; yet each looks innocent.

Which premise should be rejected, and why does it look so harmless beforehand? What exactly is wrong with the rejected premise, and what does the repair commit one to about the relation between information and thermodynamics?
