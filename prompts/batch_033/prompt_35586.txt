Please give me your considered credence in the following proposition, understood as the central negative thesis of hinge epistemology:

P: At least some of the commitments that are held fixed in ordinary inquiry — for example, that there is an external world, that the Earth existed long before any of us were born, that our memory is not systematically deceptive — are not properly evaluated as justified or unjustified, known or unknown, rational or irrational. Their role is not that of supremely well-supported empirical beliefs, but that of enabling conditions for the very practice of forming and assessing beliefs. By "correct" I mean that this diagnosis survives the best available Moorean and evidentialist replies and is needed to explain what goes wrong in radical skeptical challenges.

I want a number, but I want it derived rather than asserted. Please decompose P into the sub-questions on which your credence depends, assign a probability to each, and show how they combine into your overall credence. Include at least the following terms, and add others if your reasoning requires them:

1. The kind question: does the notion of a hinge pick out a genuine epistemic kind, or is it a metaphor that dissolves once we distinguish very high confidence from exemption from assessment?
2. The parity question: are the differences between hinge commitments and ordinary high-credence empirical beliefs differences in degree or in kind?
3. The Moorean challenge: how likely is it that Moore's common-sense response to skepticism shows that at least some of these commitments can be known with as much certainty as anything?
4. The cost question: does exempting hinges from epistemic assessment carry unacceptable costs, such as a slide into relativism or an inability to say why a conspiracy theorist's fixed commitment is not on all fours with a respectable hinge?
5. The exegetical question: how much of the view's appeal depends on a particular reading of Wittgenstein's On Certainty, and how confident are you in that reading?

For each term give a probability and one or two sentences of justification. Then state how the terms combine: if they are not independent, say how the dependencies run. Give the resulting credence in P. Finally, identify which term your final number is most sensitive to; if that one sub-question were resolved against you, how far would P move, and what evidence or argument would move it most?

To make the target concrete: my own credence in P is around 0.6, while a colleague who thinks Moore effectively settled the matter is around 0.2. After giving your own number, say where the disagreement between a 0.6 and a 0.2 most plausibly lives, given your decomposition.
