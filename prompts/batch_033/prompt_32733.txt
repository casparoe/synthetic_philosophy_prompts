I'm TAing a grad seminar on practical reason and we're about to hit the unit on hard choices and incommensurable values. I want to make a "which position do you actually hold?" quiz for the first day — not a graded thing, just a handout to get people to see where they stand before we read the papers. The idea is that they answer concrete cases and then the scoring dumps them into a position, ideally one they didn't realize they held. I want it to feel faintly embarrassing in the way that only a personality quiz at a philosophy seminar can be.

The positions I need on the board: strong commensurabilism (everything cashs out in one value currency like pleasure or preference satisfaction), weak commensurabilism (values are plural but any two options can still be ranked by some covering standard), the parity view (some options are on a par — not better, not worse, not equal, but comparable in a different way, and you resolve the choice by committing), the incomparability view (some values are genuinely incomparable, end of story), the epistemic view (there's always a fact of the matter about which is better, hard choices are just ignorance), and the voluntarist line (in hard cases there's no fact of the matter at all, and you constitute yourself by choosing). I know the parity and voluntarist views overlap in some versions — if your quiz can't cleanly separate them, flag that and explain why.

I want eight to ten questions, each a little vignette. Mix it up: career paths, a medical triage, trading off artistic integrity against financial stability, a friendship versus a principle, a weird tech scenario where you can upload one of two incompatible value-profiles. Each question should have three or four answer options that capture different structural commitments about the choice itself — e.g., "one is clearly better once you think it through," "they're roughly equal so it doesn't really matter," "there's no common scale so the question has no answer," "you have to just pick and that picking is what makes it the right choice." No question should ask anything like "do you believe values are commensurable." 

For the scoring key: I especially want the quiz to tease apart the epistemic view from the parity view. They sound similar in the abstract — both say there's something real going on — but they come apart in cases where you have perfect information and still feel the normative pull of both options. Build at least two questions around that distinction. Also, I want the answer key to note unoccupied combinations. For instance, if someone consistently answers like a strong commensurabilist on the ranking questions but like a voluntarist on the resolution questions, that seems like an unstable combination — say why. If two positions live in the same leaf because your questions can't reach the difference between them, say that too rather than pretending they're distinct.
