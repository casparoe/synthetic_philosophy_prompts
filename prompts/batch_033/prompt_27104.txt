Write a short dialogue between two equally competent researchers who disagree about what makes every 'a' an 'a'. One defends a decompositional view in the spirit of the Letter Spirit program: a letterform belongs to a category because it instantiates an abstract schema of roles—bowl, stem, crossbar, and the like—standing in specified relations, while style is a separate parameter that changes only how those roles are rendered. The other defends a holistic, exemplar-based view: membership in a letter category is graded similarity to remembered exemplars, and the supposed split between roles and style is imposed by the theorist rather than found in the forms themselves. Have them work through one hard case in detail: the difference between a one-story 'a' (a single bowl with an attached vertical stroke) and a two-story 'a' (an upper closed loop over a lower bowl). Each should press the other's actual commitments, respond to what the other has just said, and adjust their position where honesty requires it. The dialogue should leave their disagreement sharper and more specific than it was at the start, not resolve it by fiat. End with a single sentence beginning "Summary:" that states the residual disagreement in one sentence.
