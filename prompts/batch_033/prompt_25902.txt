During a political philosophy seminar, a participant repeatedly invoked the slogan "taxation is theft" as though it were a decisive objection to any proposal involving redistributive taxation. When someone asked him to unpack what he meant, he simply restated it: taxation just is theft, full stop. The discussion moved on, but I want to return next week with a more careful treatment.

I suspect "taxation is theft" conceals several distinct claims that differ dramatically in their plausibility and in what they would actually entail. Can you lay out the precise theses this slogan could be asserting? For each reading, please assess whether it is (a) trivially true, (b) substantive and defensible but controversial, or (c) false, and explain why. Then tell me: which of these readings, if any, would actually do the argumentative work the slogan was being asked to do — namely, ruling out redistributive taxation as illegitimate without further discussion? And which reading do you think people who deploy this slogan in ordinary political argument most likely have in mind, whether or not they have articulated it to themselves?
