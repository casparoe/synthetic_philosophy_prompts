I keep going around in circles on this and I'd like someone to cash it out properly. International law has a two-track setup for war. Track one: waging aggressive war is a crime, the aggressor state is doing something legally very wrong, and the people who plan and order it can be prosecuted for it. Track two: the individual soldier on the aggressor side, if they follow the laws of war — wear a uniform, only target combatants, don't torture prisoners, that sort of thing — can lawfully kill enemy soldiers and is not treated as a murderer or as an accomplice to the aggression. And a soldier on the just side has exactly the same permissions and prohibitions. The official story is that the justice of the war and the rules governing the fighting go in seperate boxes, and that the boxes are symmetric across both sides.

What I want to know is what theory of soldiers' moral responsibility this practice actually presupposes. Specifically: does it presuppose the "moral equality of combatants" — the idea that a soldier fighting an unjust war and a soldier fighting a just war are morally on a par in what they may do — or does it only presuppose something weaker, like a pragmatic convention that can't function unless it's symmetric? I'm also stuck on the asymmetry: we're willing to criminalize the leaders who order the war but not the ordinary people who carry it out. That seems to presuppose something about what soldiers are in a position to know or judge, but I can't tell whether the law needs that claim or just finds it convenient.

Reconstruct the implicit theory for me. Give me the strongest version of what the practice has to be committed to, not just what it's compatible with, and be honest about where the practice is underdetermined — where several different philosophical views could underwrite the same rules. Then tell me what would actually have to change in how soldiers are treated if you swapped in the view that unjust combatants do wrong and are blameworthy for fighting. Dont give me citations or a reading list; I want the philosophical reconstruction, and I don't care whether you can name the legal instruments.
