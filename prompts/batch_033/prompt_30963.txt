I just got back from a three-hour argument with a friend about laws of nature. He kept saying laws are "just summaries" and I kept saying they have to be "something more" — but by the end I wasn't even sure we were disagreeing about the same thing, or whether there were five positions in play rather than two. I want to get clear on the logical geography before we go another round.

Please act as a professional philosopher of science with deep expertise in the metaphysics of laws. I'd like you to build a decision tree that takes someone from a few concrete diagnostic questions all the way to a named position in the Humeanism / anti-Humeanism debate. Think carefully about the space of possible views before you write — start by brainstorming the key fault lines that genuinely separate positions, then build the tree from those rather than from a memorized list of -isms.

Each branching node should be a question someone can answer without already knowing the philosophical labels. Use thought experiments where they help: a universe containing exactly one particle for its entire history, a property that suddenly starts behaving differently tomorrow, alien properties that never happen to get instantiated, the difference between "all emeralds are green" and "all emeralds are grue," and so on. The tree should have roughly six to ten leaves.

Constraints:

- Build from the logical space, not from names. If some combination of answers has no defenders, note it and say briefly why — is the combination incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored?
- If two named positions land on the same leaf because your questions cannot pull them apart, flag that explicitly rather than pretending the tree distinguishes them.
- For each leaf, give the position's name, a one-sentence summary, and its most awkward commitment — the bullet a defender would least like to bite.

After you present the tree, step back and tell me: which branching question in your tree is most contestable — the one where, if it were answered differently, the whole structure would reorganize? Where is your reasoning about how the positions relate most likely to fail? In other words, what would change your mind about the taxonomy itself?
