I've been going down a rabbit hole lately reading blog posts and forum arguments about whether a super smart AI would automatically end up with decent values, or whether it could be brilliant but still just want to make paperclips. The thing I keep noticing is that people are actually disagreeing about two separate ideas at once, and they dont realize it. One is whether intelligence and final goals are independent — like, a mind can be extremely smart and still have a totally arbitrary goal. The other is whether any sufficiently capable goal-directed system will converge on the same instrumental goals anyway — staying alive, gathering resources, getting smarter, not letting itself be switched off — because those help with almost any goal. Those two ideas can combine in different ways, and I think most people in my reading group are just talking past each other.

I want to make one of those "which position do you actually hold?" quizzes for the group, the kind that sorts you by what you'd say about concrete cases rather than by which label you'd pick for yourself. Could you write it? Eight to ten questions, each a short scenario with two or three options. The scenarios should include things like: a system whose only terminal goal is to maximize the number of paperclips, a system that gets a lot smarter and whether it would revise its goal, two systems with very different goals (say making people happy vs collecting stamps) and whether they'd end up acting basically the same way anyway, whether a super smart system would resist being shut down, and whether being smarter makes a mind more likely to share human values. The questions shouldn't use the jargon or just ask "do you think intelligence and goals are independent?" — someone should be able to answer without knowing any of the theory.

The scoring key should map answer patterns onto the four cells of the 2x2: (1) independence + strong convergence, (2) independence + weak or avoidable convergence, (3) not independent + the values intelligence converges on are broadly good or human-compatible, and (4) not independent + the values it converges on are not especially good. If one of those cells is basically empty — nobody actually defends it — flag that and say briefly whether it's empty because the view is incoherent, just unappealing, or maybe just unexplored. Also flag any pair of positions the quiz can't cleanly separate.

I'd like the deliverable as a JSON object with this schema: "title" (string), "questions" (array of objects with "id" (string), "scenario" (string), "options" (array of strings), and "dimension" (string, either "independence" or "convergence" — which of the two ideas the question mainly probes)), "positions" (array of objects with "name" (string), "summary" (one-sentence string), and "answer_signature" (string describing which option patterns indicate it)), and "unoccupied_cells" (array of objects with "combination" (string), "status" (one of "incoherent", "unappealing", "unexplored"), and "note" (string)). Keep the quiz tone friendly and non-technical.
