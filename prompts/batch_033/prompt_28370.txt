I'm a journalist who covers the science-and-ideas beat, and I've been following the kidney-market debate for a few months now. I've interviewed people on both sides — the "commodification corrupts" crowd who think selling organs would turn the body into a Walmart, and the economists who think the ban on sales is literally killing people on dialysis waitlists. They talk past each other constantly, and I'm tired of writing he-said-she-said pieces that don't move anything forward.

So here's what I want. Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed commodification critic (someone in the Sandel/Satz tradition, who thinks certain goods are degraded by market exchange) and a committed market advocate (someone who supports regulated markets in kidneys, and probably also thinks anti-price-gouging laws are counterproductive). The document should be something both could actually sign.

Structure it like this:

- A numbered list of claims both sides can agree on. About the facts of the kidney shortage, about what's at stake normatively, about where the burden of proof lies — whatever you can get them both to sign without either one grimacing.
- A disagreements table: the precise points where they part ways, with each side's position stated in wording that side would actually endorse. Not caricatures.
- The cruxes: the sub-questions where, if you could settle them, at least one side would change its mind. I suspect one crux is whether markets crowd out altruistic donation and another is whether "repugnance" is a reliable moral heuristic or just status-quo bias dressed up, but you tell me.
- For each crux, if possible, a concrete test or piece of evidence both would accept as bearing on it, and what each side predicts about it.

Don't let them settle into a "reasonable people can disagree" shrug. Push them to be precise about what the disagreement actually is. If part of it turns out to be different weightings of the same evidence, say so. If part of it is a verbal dispute about what "commodification" even means — and I've got a hunch some of it is — say that too, and clean it up.
