I am a novelist at work on a book whose protagonist is a former poultry-plant worker who has become an animal-welfare advocate, and the book turns in part on whether the position she has assembled about the ethics of food production and wild animal suffering is internally coherent. In the course of research I have settled on the following set of commitments, and I intend to have the character defend something close to this package. Before I build the book's argument around it, I would like a careful audit of whether the commitments hang together.

The commitments are these:

1. The badness of an animal's suffering or premature death does not depend on its species, nor on whether the animal is wild or domesticated.

2. Given the scale of suffering and death involved in industrial animal agriculture, a person who can avoid animal products at reasonable cost is under a genuine moral requirement to do so; veganism, or something near it, is the default position.

3. Plant-based agriculture is not clean: tillage, harvesting, and pest control kill and injure very large numbers of field animals and insects. The choice between an omnivorous and a vegan diet is therefore not a choice between a harmful and a harmless option, but between different distributions of harm.

4. For someone who is going to eat meat regardless, beef is morally preferable to chicken. A single cow yields a great many more meals per individual life taken, whereas chicken production runs through vastly more individual animals, raised in far worse conditions, per unit of meat.

5. We should not, in general, intervene to reduce suffering among wild animals. Predation, starvation, and disease in nature are not ours to manage, and attempts to manage them would be presumptuous and ecologically reckless.

6. If an agent can prevent a serious harm at modest cost without thereby causing a greater harm, the agent has at least a pro tanto reason to prevent it, whatever the species of the victim.

I am not asking for a survey of animal ethics or for a bibliography. I want a consistency audit of this set. Please identify which commitments are in genuine tension with one another, distinguish those from the pairs that only appear to be in tension and dissolve on closer inspection, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, specify which commitment would most cheaply be revised and what that revision would cost; then tell me which single revision would leave the most coherent overall position. Some of these will pull against one another, and some will turn out to sit together, and I would rather know which is which than have all of them treated as equally problematic.
