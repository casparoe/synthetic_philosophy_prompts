I just spent two hours arguing with a friend about Maxwell's demon, and I've come away with a set of views I'm not confident hang together. I'd like an audit before I commit to them in writing. Here is where I've landed:

(a) The demon presents a genuine challenge to the second law — it is not a trivial mistake about where the work comes from, and you need to engage with the information-processing angle to see why it fails.

(b) Landauer's principle — that erasing a bit of information costs at least kT ln 2 in entropy — is the key insight that resolves the paradox.

(c) But a demon with unlimited memory who never erases seems to evade Landauer's principle entirely. If the demon simply accumulates records without ever clearing them, it can keep sorting molecules and reducing the gas's entropy indefinitely, so Landauer's principle cannot be the whole resolution.

(d) The measurement step itself is thermodynamically reversible in principle — Bennett showed this — so you cannot appeal to irreducible measurement costs to plug the gap left by (c).

(e) The correct resolution is that the demon-plus-gas-plus-memory is a closed Hamiltonian system obeying Liouville's theorem; the apparent entropy decrease in the gas is exactly compensated by the buildup of correlations between the demon's memory and the gas, and a proper coarse-graining that includes these correlations shows that total entropy never decreases.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity and be specific about what would have to give.
