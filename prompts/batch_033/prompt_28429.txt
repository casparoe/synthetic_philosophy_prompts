I am a retired physicist who has recently begun working through the philosophy of mind, and I would like help understanding the debate between illusionism and realism about phenomenal consciousness. Please take on the role of a professional philosopher who specializes in consciousness and the metaphysics of mind. Before you write, think carefully about the strongest available formulation of each position, so that neither side comes out as a caricature; do not include this preparatory reasoning in your final output.

Then write a dialogue between two equally able philosophers. One is an illusionist, who maintains that phenomenal consciousness — the subjective, first-personal character of experience — is an introspective illusion, and that reality contains no irreducible phenomenal properties. The other is a phenomenal realist, who maintains that the existence of subjective experience is the thing we know with greatest certainty, and that any theory denying it refutes itself. Have them engage directly with each other's arguments: the realist should press the objection that an illusion is itself a conscious experience, and the illusionist should answer that objection rather than deflecting it. The dialogue should bring out the precise point at which their disagreement becomes fundamental — where it stops being an empirical matter and turns on what introspection can and cannot tell us. Aim for roughly 600–900 words.
