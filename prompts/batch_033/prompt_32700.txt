Hi, so I work at a mid-sized family foundation. We fund stuff across a bunch of different areas — arts education, environmental restoration, legal aid for tenants, a summer camp for kids with disabilities, you get the idea. My job is to evaluate grant proposals and recommend which ones to fund, and our board basically rubber-stamps whatever I bring them.

The problem is we get way more good proposals than we can fund, and I keep hitting a wall where I'm trying to compare things that don't seem comparable. Like, how do I weigh a program that teaches music to incarcerated teenagers against a wetlands restoration project against a free clinic? They're all good in totaly different ways. My board chair just says "go with your gut," but my gut feels like it's making arbitrary calls dressed up as judgment.

I know philosophers have thought about this — incommensurable values, hard choices, stuff from Isaiah Berlin, Ruth Chang, people like that. I don't need a seminar on it. What I need is an actual procedure I can follow when I have to put five wildly different proposals in front of me and pick two. Something step-by-step that doesn't pretend the incommensurability away (like "just monetize everything" or "use a forced ranking") but that helps me make a defensible choice without fooling myself about what I'm doing.

The procedure has to be something I can actually run in a couple hours per batch, it should flag the points where I'm making a value judgment rather than applying a rule, and it should be honest about what kinds of proposals it'll fail on. Once you've laid out the procedure, walk me through applying it to three proposals: (1) a legal aid clinic that keeps families from being wrongfully evicted, (2) an after-school music program in a low-income neighborhood, and (3) a project to restore a polluted urban creek. Then show me a case where the procedure gives me an answer I'm not going to like — where it points in a direction that feels wrong but the logic holds up and I'd have to override it explicitly. Finally, tell me what the procedure can't handle and where I should just stop and call a board meeting instead.
