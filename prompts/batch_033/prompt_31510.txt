I sit on a disciplinary panel for the General Medical Council. We keep seeing cases where a doctor's decision had some awful side-effect — they prescribed a drug that had a rare but devastating complication, or they discharged a patient who then deteriorated — and the question is whether the harm was intentional. If it was, that's the difference between a suspension and being struck off. The trouble is, I've been reading about the Knobe effect and its made me doubt whether we're actually tracking intentionality or just punishing bad outcomes. I want a decision procedure my panel can use: a set of questions to ask in order, with clear stopping points, that help us tease apart whether we're attributing intention because of the action itself or because of how we feel about the outcome. It needs to be something non-philosophers can actually run in a hearing, and it should flag where the procedure itself might be vulnerable to the Knobe effect. Then apply it to two cases: (1) a doctor who prescribed a standard antibiotic knowing it carried a 0.1% risk of anaphylaxis, and the patient died; (2) a doctor who falsified a discharge summary to cover up a mistake, and the patient was fine.
