I teach a seminar on free will and moral responsibility every other year, and I've started to notice something that's driving me a little nuts. We spend a couple weeks on Strawson's "Freedom and Resentment," and the students always have the same arc: first they're excited because it seems like Strawson has cut through the whole compatibilism/incompatibilism mess by shifting the question to the reactive attitudes. Then someone points out that the hard question just comes back — if determinism is true, are our reactive attitudes justified or not? And then we're back in the same old debates, just with fancier vocabulary about "participant stance" and "objective attitude."

And it's not just students. The literature has been chewing on this for sixty years now — Watson, Wallace, Galen Strawson, Pereboom, Vargas, the whole crowd — and it feels like nobody has moved. Strawson's optimists think he showed the skeptical worry is idle because we can't actually give up the reactive attitudes; they're constitutive of human life. The pessimists say that's just a psychological fact, not a normative one, and the question is whether the attitudes are justified, not whether we can shake them. And then there's a third camp that says the whole thing is a pseudo-problem because "justified" is the wrong category to apply to reactive attitudes at all — they're not the kind of thing that stands in need of justification, any more than perceptual beliefs do.

I'm trying to figure out what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it a verbal dispute about what "justification" means for an attitude? Is it a factual disagreement about what would happen to our psychologies if we really internalized determinism? Is it a bedrock normative disagreement where both sides just have different starting points about what matters, and neither can argue the other out of them? Or is it what I suspect — that Strawson's reframing didn't actually dissolve the old problem, it just gave us new language for the same intractable standoff, and we're stuck becuase the question really is that hard?

Give me a diagnosis, with reasons, and tell me what (if anything) would count as progress given your diagnosis.
