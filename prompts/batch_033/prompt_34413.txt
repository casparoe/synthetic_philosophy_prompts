I've been trying to sort out what I actually think about separating the art from the artist, because I keep noticing my own reactions are all over the place and I want to know if there's a principle underneath them or if I'm just rationalizing whatever I already liked. Here are my honest verdicts on a bunch of cases:

Caravaggio literally killed a man in a street fight. I can stare at The Calling of Saint Matthew all afternoon and feel nothing but awe. The murder doesn't enter into it.

Roman Polanski drugged and raped a thirteen-year-old, fled the country, and never served time. I used to think Chinatown was one of the greatest films ever made, and now I can't watch it. It feels wrong to sit there appreciating his craft.

Wagner was a vicious anti-semite whose writings helped shape Nazi cultural ideology. I still listen to the Ring Cycle and find it overwhelming. But there's a little asterisk in my head now, a kind of low-grade unease that wasn't there before I knew.

Bill Cosby built a whole career on being America's wholesome dad, and then it turned out he was drugging and assaulting women the whole time. I can't listen to his comedy at all. The art feels like it was part of the crime — the persona was the cover.

Michael Jackson: I've read the allegations, I find a lot of it credible, and I still put on Off the Wall and enjoy it. I'm not proud of this, but it's where I actually land.

Gauguin abandoned his wife and five kids, moved to Tahiti, and took multiple teenage girls as "wives," giving them syphilis. I can still look at his paintings and find them beautiful. But I feel slightly worse about it than Caravaggio, and I can't articulate why.

Lovecraft was so racist it reads like self-parody — even by the standards of 1920s New England he was extreme. I still read his stories. When the racism is right there on the page it ruins the sentence, but the rest of the story still works for me.

Kanye West's last few years: I can't listen anymore, not even the old albums. Whatever I loved about them is just gone. The person he's become has retroactively poisoned the music.

So I'm trying to extract a principle. It seems like time-since-death matters (Caravaggio vs. Polanski), whether the art itself encodes the failing matters (Cosby vs. Jackson), and maybe whether the artist is still out there benefiting from my attention matters (Kanye vs. Wagner). But I'm not sure these add up to one coherent view, and some pairs — especially Caravaggio vs. Polanski, where both did horrific things but one bothers me zero and the other makes the art unwatchable — make me suspect I'm just inconsistent. What principle best captures these verdicts? Which ones does it force me to abandon? And what does it say about a case I haven't mentioned: Hemingway, who was a misogynist and an anti-semite but wrote some of the cleanest prose in English?
