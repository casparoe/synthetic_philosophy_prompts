After thirty years calibrating instruments, I've spent my retirement reading the Vienna Circle, and Neurath keeps pulling me back — the idea that knowledge is a boat you rebuild at sea, plank by plank, because you can never put it in dry dock. Lately it's his picture language that's got me: ISOTYPE, those rows of little repeated figures he used to teach workers statistics without words. What moves me is the tension in it. The dream was a universal language, but a picture doesn't point at the world by itself — you have to be taught the legend, and the legend is just more pictures. No instrument reads itself; you calibrate it against a standard somebody made.

Write me a poem, maybe 20 to 30 lines, free verse but with one strict device. Each stanza should open with a short, flat, declarative line repeated word for word, like an ISOTYPE row — one little figure per unit, never a bigger one. The repetition is the point, not enlargement. As the poem goes on the repeated line can shift a little, or the count can change, and the turn should come when the speaker realizes the repeated sign doesn't interpret itself: somebody had to draw the key, and the key is just another sign. End unsettled, the way Neurath leaves you — no stepping off the boat to check.

Keep it accurate: for Neurath the pictures are conventional, not natural or transparent, and the international picture language is an instrument, not a foundation. But no jargon, and dont quote him — if you need the boat bit, paraphrase it.
