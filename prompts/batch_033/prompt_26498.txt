Please argue that Williams's integrity objection to utilitarianism is badly misread as a demandingness complaint. On your view, the objection isn't that utilitarianism asks agents to give up too much, but that its model of impartial justification misrepresents what it is for an agent's deepest commitments to give her reasons at all: even when the utilitarian verdict lines up with what her ground projects already favor, the route by which she's supposed to arrive at that verdict alienates her from those commitments. On this reading, the one thought too many case matters not because the rescuer reaches the wrong conclusion, but because his needing to run the justification through impartial considerations at all is itself the distortion. Defend this reading against the reply that Williams is only doing moral psychology and has no argument that utilitarianism gets the wrong answers.
