I am a distributed systems engineer with no formal training in economics. I have recently encountered references to the socialist calculation debate — the early-to-mid-20th-century argument among Ludwig von Mises, Friedrich Hayek, and Oskar Lange about whether a centrally planned economy can allocate resources rationally without market prices. As I understand it in broad strokes: Mises claimed that without private property and the price signals it generates, rational economic calculation is impossible, because there is no common unit in which to compare the relative scarcity of heterogeneous capital goods. Hayek deepened this into an argument about dispersed, local, and often tacit knowledge — knowledge of time, place, and circumstance that no central authority can gather, let alone keep current. Lange replied that a central planning board could simulate market prices through a trial-and-error procedure: announce provisional prices for capital goods, instruct enterprise managers to follow marginal-cost pricing rules, observe whether shortages or surpluses appear, and adjust prices up or down until supply and demand clear — replicating the tâtonnement process Walras described for competitive markets.

From my vantage point, this sounds like a debate about centralized versus decentralized coordination, and distributed systems has thought about that problem carefully. My working vocabulary includes: leader election, consensus protocols (Paxos, Raft), gossip protocols, the CAP theorem, the cost of state synchronization, vector clocks, local versus global state, the trade-off between consistency and availability in the presence of partitions, and the difficulty of maintaining an accurate global view from partial, asynchronous, and lagged local observations.

- Where does each side locate the bottleneck — computational capacity, communication bandwidth, latency, staleness, or something that has no obvious distributed-systems analogue?
- What does each side assume about the information available locally at each node, and about whether and how that information can be communicated to other nodes or to a central coordinator?
