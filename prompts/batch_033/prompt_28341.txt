I sit on a three-member panel that decides whether certain research projects may go forward. Our governing rule is explicit: a project is approved exactly when both of two conditions are met — the protocol is methodologically sound, and the consent procedures are adequate. Each of us reads the full application, records a yes or no on each condition, and records a yes or no on the overall decision. Individually, none of us is inconsistent: whenever a member judges both conditions to be met, that member votes to approve, and whenever a member judges at least one condition unmet, that member votes to reject.

Last quarter this produced the following pattern, twice. Member A judged the methodology sound but the consent inadequate, and voted to reject. Member B judged the consent adequate but the methodology unsound, and voted to reject. Member C judged both conditions met and voted to approve. The chair then tallied the votes by question. The methodology passed 2–1, on the votes of A and C. The consent procedures passed 2–1, on the votes of B and C. Yet the overall decision was 2–1 to reject, on the votes of A and B. The panel was, collectively, in the position of having found that each condition was met while also finding that the project should not go forward.

I have since noticed a looser version of the same thing on a hiring committee, where four criteria are each listed as requirements, but the final vote is understood to be a holistic judgment rather than a mechanical conjunction of the four. There too the committee can find that each criterion is satisfied by a majority while the appointment is rejected by a majority, even though no individual member has to be inconsistent.

I am told this is just the Condorcet paradox, or an instance of Arrow's theorem, but that seems wrong to me: those concern ranking candidates or preferences, whereas here we vote on propositions and each of us is individually consistent. I want to know whether this phenomenon has a name of its own, and if so, what exactly it covers. More important than the label, I want the carving: is the group being irrational in the first case, and is the second case the same phenomenon or only a resemblance? What are the nearest concepts, how does each differ, and where are the edges — when do the two ways of tallying come apart, and when must they coincide?
