I've been trying to get clear on what I actually think about the fine-tuning argument, and I keep finding that my gut reaction ("obviously something is going on") and my reflective view ("but wait, selection effects") pull in different directions. So I want to actually put a number on it and see where the action is.

Here's the proposition: "The fact that the fundamental constants and initial conditions of our universe appear to permit life is evidence that substantially favors some non-anthropic explanation — a multiverse, design, deeper necessity, or something else — over the hypothesis that we just got lucky and anthropic selection accounts for the whole thing." Not asking whether the explanation is definitely design or definitely multiverse, just whether the fine-tuning data gives us reason to go beyond "we won the cosmic lottery and of course we only observe a winning ticket."

Give me your credence on that, but decompose it. I want to see terms like: the probability that there's genuine fine-tuning and not just parameter freedom we don't yet understand; the probability that anthropic reasoning alone can handle the apparent tuning given that it's real; the probability that a multiverse mechanism is independently plausible enough to do work here; and whatever else you think belongs. For each term, a number and a one-liner. Then the combination. Then tell me which term you'd most want to resolve — the one where new evidence or a new argument would shift your overall credence the most.

And if the whole excercise of putting a single credence on this is misguided — if I'm lumping together things that need to be kept apart, or if "evidence favors" is too vague to take a number — tell me that instead of playing along. I want the pushback.
