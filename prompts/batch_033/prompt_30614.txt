my debate club has been stuck on this for like a month now. one person's a prioritarian — roughly, she thinks what matters is making people better off, but a given improvement matters more the worse off the person already is. the other is an egalitarian of the kind who thinks inequality itself is bad, not just its effects. the standard knock on the egalitarian is the leveling-down objection: imagine a change that makes the best-off worse off and nobody better off. inequality goes down, sure, but it seems like nothing good happened, so how could equality itself be valuable? the egalitarian says that's a misreading, and the prioritarian says its exactly why we should drop equality and just be prioritarians. i want the document an adversarial collaboration between the two of them would produce. what can they both sign as a numbered list of agreed claims — about the leveling-down case itself, about what each theory says, about what a change has to do to count as an improvement? then a disagreements table, with each side's view written the way that side would actually phrase it. then the cruxes — i suspect one is whether equality has value in itself and another is whether a change that helps nobody can ever make the world better in any respect — and for each crux, a case or argument both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. if some of their fight is just about words like 'equality' or 'better,' say which part and why.
