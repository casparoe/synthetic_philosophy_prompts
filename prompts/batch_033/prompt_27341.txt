I am a PhD student in political philosophy, currently seconded to a state-level reparations task force. The commission is beginning to receive claims from communities alleging historical injustices, and the commissioners have asked me to develop a structured decision procedure their staff can use to triage and evaluate claims before they reach the commissioners for final determination.

The procedure should be grounded in the philosophical literature on compensatory and restorative justice — in particular, the principles governing when present-day entities bear obligations to redress past wrongs, how to handle counterfactuals about what would have happened absent the wrong, and how to treat cases where both wrongdoers and victims are long dead. But the staff who will apply it have backgrounds in law, history, or public policy, not philosophy, and they have roughly two days per initial claim.

I need a step-by-step framework: a sequence of questions to ask about each claim, with guidance on what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, where the procedure stops with a rejection, and where it flags a case for commissioner judgment because the question turns on a value choice the procedure cannot resolve. Please mark those judgment calls explicitly and explain why the procedure cannot settle them.

Then run the procedure on three cases:

(1) A claim by descendants of residents displaced by a mid-20th-century urban renewal project that destroyed a thriving minority business district, where the municipal government that authorized the project still exists.

(2) A claim by a present-day indigenous group seeking compensation for land seized in the early 19th century, where the seizing colonial government no longer exists but its successor state does, and the group was forcibly relocated multiple times since.

(3) A claim where the procedure should, by its own lights, recommend rejection even though the historical injury is undisputed and severe. Explain why that outcome follows from the principles you have built in, and say whether this reveals a limitation of the procedure, a limitation of the underlying principles, or neither.
