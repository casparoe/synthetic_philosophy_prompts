so ive been meditating on and off for a couple years and also reading some buddhist stuff, and theres this thing that keeps happening that i dont have a word for. when im just living my life — walking to the store, arguing with my brother, whatever — theres this very strong sense that theres a "me" in here, like a little homunculus behind my eyes running the show. it feels totally obvious and solid. but when i actually sit down and try to *find* the thing — like, really look for the observer, the one who's having the experience — i cant. i find thoughts, sensations, emotions, memories, but the supposed owner of all that is just... not there. the looking itself seems to make it disappear.

then i get up, make tea, and five minutes later the self is back, as solid as ever. and what i notice is: it only seems to be there when im not looking for it. the moment i turn attention on it, it vanishes, and the moment i stop, it reconstitutes itself like nothing happened.

so its not just that i sometimes feel like a self and sometimes dont. its that the presence or absence of the self depends on whether im *attending* to it. like trying to see your own eyes without a mirror — the thing youre looking with is the thing youre looking for, and the act of looking scrambles the target.

is there a name for this specific dynamic? not just anatta or no-self in general, but this particular thing where the self seems to be constitutively elusive — there when youre not looking, gone when you are. and if theres a name for it, how does the tradition (or the philosophical literature) deal with the fact that the self just comes back the second you stop paying attention? is that seen as a problem for the no-self view, or is it exactly what the view predicts — like, the self is precisely this kind of unstable, attention-dependent phenomenon?
