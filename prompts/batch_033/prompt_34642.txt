I've been working through Pascal's Pensées in a reading group, and I keep returning to his treatment of the relationship between justice and force — roughly, the claim that what we call justice is largely custom held in place by the capacity to coerce, and that norms of justice, stripped of enforcement, lack real grip. A colleague in the group takes the opposite position: that justice has standing independent of force, that power can and should be measured against standards it did not create, and that Pascal's view collapses normativity into description. We have been arguing for months without the argument advancing. I would like you to draft an adversarial-collaboration summary that we could both sign.

Structure it as follows. First, a numbered list of claims we both accept about the relationship between justice, force, and custom — these should be substantive rather than truisms, and each must be worded so that neither of us would want to qualify or retract it. Second, a table of disagreements. For each row: state the disagreement precisely, with the Pascalian view and the opposing view phrased so that their respective holders would endorse the wording; identify the crux underlying that disagreement (the sub-question that, if answered, would shift at least one position); and, where you can, propose a concrete case or scenario that both sides agree would bear on the crux, along with each side's predicted judgment about it.

Throughout, avoid letting either position caricature the other. If one side would say "that's not what I mean," the wording has failed. The deliverable is a map of the dispute, not a resolution of it.
