I've ended up convinced, against my initial instincts, that when a sculptor forms a lump of clay into a statue, the statue and the lump are two different things in the same place at the same time. My reason is that they have different histories and different limits on what they can survive: the lump existed before the shaping and would survive being squashed back into a ball, while the statue began only when the clay took the right shape and would be destroyed by that same squashing. If the two can come apart in what they can and cannot survive, I find it hard to see how they could be the same thing.

What I want is a serious map of what would count against this position. I am less interested in the flat objection that two physical things cannot share a location, unless it can be made to bite. I want the deeper difficulties: descriptions the position is forced to give that seem unacceptable, other cases it has to treat the same way but where the two-things verdict feels wrong (a ring and its gold, a person and their body, an object repaired piece by piece), the charge that the distinction is only in our way of talking rather than in the world, and any route by which the position quietly becomes the one-thing view after all. I would also like to know what the position is committed to that its rival is not — in how we count things, how we describe changes, and what we say survives a change — and which of those commitments could turn out to be false. For each objection, I would like to know whether it is fatal, merely awkward, or only apparent, and which of them a defender of the two-things view would themselves concede as real costs rather than dismiss as assuming what they deny. Finally, I would like an honest answer to whether the position can be protected against all of this only by adding qualifications, and whether a view that has to qualify itself that much is in trouble for that reason alone. The objection that would move me most is one that shows the two-things view committed to a claim about the world that is not just strange but false.
