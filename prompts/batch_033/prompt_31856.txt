Before I read any more theories of populism, I want the full spec, not another scorecard. Write me the desiderata for an account of 'the will of the people' good enough to tell when an appeal to it is legitimate and when it's just a faction or a leader talking. The cases it has to get right: a 52–48 referendum treated as settled for a generation, a mass movement with no formal vote behind it, a president who lost the popular vote but still claims a mandate, a poll majority that wouldn't lift a finger for the thing it says it wants. The distinctions it can't collapse: majority preference vs. a collective subject, an empirical claim about what the people want vs. a legitimating rhetoric, constitutive vs. merely causal support. The results it has to respect: Condorcet cycles, Arrow-style impossibility results, the discursive dilemma, and the boundary problem — who counts as 'the people' in the first place, and the account cant just stipulate it. Flag which of these are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's preferences, and tell me which subset, if any, is jointly unsatisfiable. That last part is what I actually want to see, so spend your time there.
