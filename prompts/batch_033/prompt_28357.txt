here's the question that was posted:

"Can someone explain Nozick's utility monster? What's the argument and what is it meant to prove?"

and here's the answer someone gave:

"Sure. The utility monster is a thought experiment from Robert Nozick's book Anarchy, State, and Utopia (1974). You imagine a being that is ridiculously efficient at converting resources into happiness — way more efficient than any human could ever be. So for every unit of food or money or whatever you give it, it produces vastly more utility than if you gave that same resource to a normal person. If you're a utilitarian, you're committed to maximising total utility, so logically you'd have to keep feeding the monster at everyone else's expense until the marginal utility equalises. The point is that this seems morally insane — it shows utilitarianism doesn't respect the separateness of persons and can demand extreme sacrifices from some for the sake of others. It's basically the same kind of problem as Parfit's repugnant conclusion: both show that simply adding up welfare across individuals leads to conclusions no reasonable person would accept."
