I keep getting into this argument on our curriculum committee. One of my collegues is very pro-AI, and whenever someone raises the worry that students will stop actually understanding the material, he says using a language model is just like using GPS. You still decide where you're going, you still do the driving, you just offload the route-finding; nobody thinks GPS destroyed our ability to navigate, and the people who paniced about calculators turned out to be wrong. So, in his view, the erosion-of-understanding worry is just another moral panic about a new tool.

I'm not convinced, partly because I've heard the GPS case used in the opposite direction, as evidence that offloading a skill does quietly erode it. I want you to audit the analogy. Start with the correspondence table: what's the GPS unit, the driver, the route, the destination, the mental map, and the map-reading skill that might atrophy. Then tell me where the mapping earns its keep and where it breaks. Pay special attention to the places where the value of the activity is in the doing rather than the arriving, and to which features of the GPS case the analogy quietly imports that don't carry over to writing or reasoning with a language model. Then say whether the relaxed conclusion — stop worrying, it's just a tool — survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If it doesn't survive, tell me what a better analogy would be, if there is one.
