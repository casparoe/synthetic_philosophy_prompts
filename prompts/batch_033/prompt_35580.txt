i've been thinking about dennett's line about darwin and turing both pulling off a "strange inversion of reasoning" — darwin showing you can get design without a designer, turing showing you can get intelligence without an understander. i want a villanelle about these two inversions. the two refrains should be: one that captures darwin's inversion (competence without comprehension in the living world), and one that captures turing's (competence without comprehension in the mechanical world). the way the refrains keep coming back and finally land together in the last two lines should do the work of showing they're really the same inversion — that the same vertigo is at the bottom of both. the poem should be accurate enough that someone who knows the idea would nod along, and someone who doesn't should still get a real poem out of it. no just name-dropping darwin and turing and calling it a day.
