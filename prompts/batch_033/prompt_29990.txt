So I've been chewing on this thing about the city I live in. Over the last few years theyve been putting up benches with armrests down the middle and little metal spikes on ledges and planter edges, and whenever someone complains the official answer is that these are just practical design choices — armrests help older people get up, spikes keep pigeons and trash off. But everyone pretty much knows the real point is to stop homeless people from sleeping there. Meanwhile the same city talks about public plazas and sidewalks as neutral shared space thats open to everybody. What does that combination actually commit somebody to? Does a bench like that just sit there neutral until a person decides how to use it, or is the bench itself doing something political before anyone even touches it? And if I say the design is political, does that mean the whole technology-is-just-a-tool way of talking has to go, or can both those things be true at once? I guess what Im really circling is whether the bench has values built into it, or just makes some uses easier and some harder, and whether those are different claims. I want to know what picture of objects and politics the city is presupposing here, and whether its actually consistent.
