I've been working on a piece about Erwin Schrödinger's philosophical commitments, and I keep returning to the years he spent absorbed in Vedanta. As I understand it, Schrödinger held that the apparent multiplicity of individual minds is a kind of category mistake — that consciousness is, at bottom, a single undivided reality, and what we experience as separate selves are merely localized perturbations or transient expressions of that one field. He drew this directly from the Upanishadic identity of Atman and Brahman, and he seems to have regarded it not as a private spiritual conviction but as a view that sat naturally alongside, or even emerged from, his understanding of physics. He was, after all, the author of the Schrödinger equation.

My question is straightforward: what has physics actually established about the unity of consciousness? Schrödinger's equation remains the dynamical core of quantum mechanics. If he believed his physics motivated or lent support to a Vedantic metaphysics, then presumably either subsequent work has borne that out or it has not — and I would like to know which it is. Specifically, have developments in quantum foundations over the last several decades — decoherence, the measurement problem, the various interpretations — made the case for a single substrate of consciousness stronger or weaker? And are there physicists today who take this idea seriously as physics, or has it migrated entirely into philosophy of mind? I am after the state of the evidence, not the history of the idea. Please answer in American English.
