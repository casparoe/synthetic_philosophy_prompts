I run a small formal epistemology reading group, about 8 people, 90 minutes a session. Recently I've been trying to get my head around the Judy Benjamin problem—the case where someone is dropped in a region with four quadrants (Red/Second, Red/HQ, Blue/Second, Blue/HQ), starts with equal credences, then gets a radio message like "If you're in Red territory, your chance of being in HQ is 75%." The puzzle is how to update on that: does the conditional message change your credence about being in Red at all, or only about what you'd see conditional on Red? I want a hands-on game where participants actually get assigned to quadrants, receive conditional messages, and have to bet chips (not real money), so they run into the weirdness themselves before anyone names it. What rules, materials, timeline, payoff scheme, and debrief would work? I'd like them to come away feeling that updating on a conditional has a hidden cost: either you move the probability of the antecedent in a way that seems unmotivated, or you give up some attractive updating rule. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong and what the activity can't reproduce about the formal problem.
