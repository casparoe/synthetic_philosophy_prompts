So my sister is marrying a composer, and they asked me to give a toast. Here's the thing: they met in a graduate seminar on Schopenhauer and music, and apparently their first real conversation was an argument about whether Schopenhauer was right that music isn't like the other arts — painting and poetry represent the Platonic Ideas, which are already one step removed from the Will, but music supposedly bypasses the Ideas entirely and is a direct copy of the Will itself. That's why Schopenhauer puts music at the top of his hierarchy of the arts and says it doesn't just represent feelings, it is the feeling, the striving, the movement of the Will without the conceptual intermediary.

Im not a philosopher, I'm a civil engineer, but I've been trying to understand this well enough to work it into the toast without embarrassing myself or them. The problem is that from what I've read, Schopenhauer's Will is this blind, insatiable, never-satisfied striving that's basically the source of all suffering, and he thought romantic love was just the Will-to-life tricking us into reproducing — the species using individuals for its own ends. Not exactly wedding material. But they clearly find something beautiful and true in his view of music, and I want to honor that.

Can you help me write a three-minute toast that engages honestly with Schopenhauer's idea — that music gives us something more direct and unmediated than the other arts can — and makes it land as genuinely celebratory for two people who take that idea seriously? The room is mixed: their advisor and a few grad students will catch every reference, but most of the guests are family who just want to hear something warm. I don't want to paper over the pessimism; I want to find a way through it that's true to the view and still says something about why these two are good together. If there's a way to use Schopenhauer's own ranking of the arts — music above all — to say something about what they found in each other, that would be great.
