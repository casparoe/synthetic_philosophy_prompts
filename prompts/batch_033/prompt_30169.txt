I am a philosophy professor whose own work sits within the Davidsonian program in the theory of meaning, and for the past several years I have also been attached to a computational linguistics group; I have picked up the field's working vocabulary well enough to use it without embarrassment. I keep finding myself, in conversations with my lab colleagues, restating radical interpretation in their terms, and I suspect the restatement is sometimes doing real damage to Davidson without my noticing. I would like you to do the translation carefully and then tell me where it breaks.

Restate Davidson's project of radical interpretation in the working vocabulary of contemporary NLP and machine learning — pretraining and fine-tuning, weak supervision, semantic parsing from denotations, grounding, non-identifiability, regularization, distribution shift — as faithfully as you can. Treat the radical interpreter as a model that must learn a mapping from utterances and contexts to truth conditions without any prior lexicon or translation manual; treat assent and dissent as a weak training signal; treat the principle of charity as a prior or regularizer; treat the holistic assignment of truth conditions as joint optimization over a whole language; and treat triangulation between speaker, interpreter, and shared environment as a form of grounding or multimodal supervision. Be systematic and honest: for each element, say whether it translates cleanly, what you had to distort to make it fit, and what has no counterpart at all in the NLP vocabulary.

Then give me the residue report. I especially want your committed view on what the failures of translation show. Is the NLP vocabulary missing something central to Davidson — for example, a normative constraint on interpretation rather than a merely statistical one — or is the residue a sign that radical interpretation was never in the business of specifying a learnable function in the first place? Do not settle for laying out possibilities; make definite choices about the mapping and about which side of each failure the problem lies on.
