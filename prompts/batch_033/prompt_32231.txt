Something's been bugging me about how AI companies talk about their models. They run all these benchmarks — TruthfulQA, reasoning tests, etc. — and report scores as if the model is being truthful or reasoning. But when the model says something false or dangerous, the PR line is "it's just predicting tokens, it doesn't have beliefs or intentions." What theory of mind does the practice of benchmarking actually presuppose? Because if you genuinely thought the system had no beliefs, benchmarking for "truthfulness" would be like testing a toaster for honesty. But if you think it does have beliefs, you're commited to some version of the intentional stance. And I'm not sure the people doing this have thought through what they're actually presupposing. Also, is deploying these models as medical advisors or legal assistants consistent with the benchmarking practice, or does it presuppose something stronger? Give me a committed answer — don't just lay out the options, tell me what you think the practice actually commits them to, and whether it's coherent.
