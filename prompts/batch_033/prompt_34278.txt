I have settled on a view about what Descartes's cogito actually proves, and I want it stress-tested. Here is the view: the cogito — the reasoning that moves from the activity of doubting to the certainty of one's own existence — establishes something real but far narrower than is often claimed. Specifically, it proves that there is thinking occurring and that this thinking cannot be attributed to nothing; it warrants the claim that I exist only in the sense that there is a subject of this thinking, where "subject" means nothing more than that-which-thinks. It does not prove that this subject is a substance, that it is simple or immaterial, that it persists through time, or that it is identical with the self that walks around and has a biography. The enduring Cartesian ego is a metaphysical overreach built on top of the cogito, not something the cogito itself delivers. On this reading, the meditator is entitled to assert his own existence only as a thinker while thinking, and the rest of the Second Meditation's argument — that the mind is better known than the body — is a separate inferential step that may or may not succeed.

I want to know what would count against this deflationary reading. What textual evidence from the Meditations — specific moves Descartes makes after the cogito, or claims he relies on later in the work — would push toward a thicker reading of what the cogito proves? What philosophical considerations would show that the thin reading is unstable: that the cogito cannot secure even the minimal conclusion without already smuggling in the thicker metaphysics it purports to avoid? And what developments in recent philosophy of mind or epistemology — for instance, work on self-reference, immunity to error through misidentification, or the transparency of self-knowledge — bear on this question, and in which direction?

Please sort the considerations from those that would be decisive against the thin reading to those that would merely require refining it. I would also like an honest assessment of whether the thin reading itself has been insulated against counterevidence by being stated too vaguely, and whether that is a problem for a reading of this kind. Finally, in your response, indicate how confident you are in the claims you make and single out the point you are least sure of.
