My friend runs a small cross-cultural experimental philosophy lab — mostly American, Western European, East Asian, and South Asian samples, all vignette studies. Over the years she's gotten pretty settled about which philosophical intuitions travel and which ones dont. From what I remember her reporting:

- On a name/reference case (a man is known by a famous name, but it turns out he didn't actually do the famous thing; someone else did), she found big differences: Western participants tend to say the name refers to the man who actually did the work, East Asian participants more often say it refers to whoever everyone uses the name for. She counts this one as varies.
- On the footbridge version of the trolley problem, she found no differences: almost everyone says don't push. Robust.
- On a consensual incest case (adult brother and sister, contraception, no harm, nobody finds out), she found no differences: everyone says it's wrong, and most can't give reasons. Robust.
- On a free will / moral responsibility case set in a deterministic universe (abstract, no vivid details), she found real differences: Western participants more willing to say the agent is still morally responsible, East Asian participants less so. Varies.
- On a teletransporter personal identity case (the original body is destroyed and an exact duplicate is assembled), she found differences in whether people say the person survives. Varies.

Next week she's running a new study: a concrete, emotionally vivid moral responsibility case — a man with a brain tumor that causes him to commit a violent act, and the vignette is written to be upsetting — then it asks whether he's morally responsible. im trying to predict what she'll find.

I'm less interested in the bare prediction than in the rule she seems to be running about when intuitions vary across cultures. What are the candidate rules that fit her track record equally well? Which rule predicts what for the brain-tumor case? Give me your best guess and why, and tell me which follow-up study would cleanly separate the remaining candidates. Also say what would change your mind about the prediction, or where the inference from her track record is shakiest.
