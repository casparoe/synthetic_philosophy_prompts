I keep hearing the Humean and anti-Humean camps on laws of nature talk past each other — one side says laws are just the best summary of the mosaic of local facts, the other says no, laws are what make things happen, they govern. I want the document these two people should have produced after actually doing an adversarial collaboration, instead of another fifteen years of talking past each other.

Write the joint statement a sophisticated Humean (roughly the Mill–Ramsey–Lewis best-system view) and a sophisticated anti-Humean (laws as real nomic facts, relations between universals, or governing — the Armstrong/Dretske/Tooley sort, or a powers view) could both sign. It should include:

- a numbered list of claims both sides can sign. Not "we both believe in physics" filler — the real shared ground: what a law has to do that an accidentally true generalization doesn't, what the standard arguments leave open, what the debate is not about.
- a disagreements table. For each remaining difference: the claim stated once in wording the Humean would accept as a fair statement of their own view, once in wording the anti-Humean would accept as theirs, plus a neutral statement of the question they split on.
- the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I'd expect something about whether best-system laws can explain their instances or only codify them, whether the mosaic could have differed while the laws stayed fixed, and whether "nomic necessity" is a real addition to the facts or a projection of our systematizing. If you think one of those is a red herring, say so and replace it with a better one.
- for each crux, the case, argument, or thought-experimental setup both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

The hard part is wording things so neither side winces at it's own column, and not smuggling in a verdict. If you conclude part of the dispute is verbal — say, over what "explain" or "govern" means — put that in its own subsection and say exactly which part is verbal.

End with a short note on where you're least confident in your formulations, and where a real Humean or anti-Humean might refuse to sign. And if the framing itself is off — for instance if you think "anti-Humean" lumps together views that shouldn't share a column — tell me that plainly instead of forcing the document.
