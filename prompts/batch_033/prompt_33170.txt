I have been noticing a recurring move in arguments about which beings deserve moral consideration, and I do not have a word for it. Someone will argue that we ought to extend some protection to, say, shrimp or bees, and in that part of the argument sentience is treated as a matter of degree: a shrimp is a little sentient, a dog is more sentient, a human is very sentient, and even a small amount of sentience should count for something. Then, a few minutes later in the same conversation, when someone objects that a thermostat or a simple robot also responds to its environment and adjusts its behavior to new information, the same person switches to speaking of sentience as something a being either has or entirely lacks — the thermostat simply is not sentient, full stop. The graded way of talking does the work when the aim is to draw marginal cases inside the circle of concern, and the binary way of talking does the work when the aim is to keep clearly non-sentient systems outside it.

I am not asking whether shrimp or bees are in fact sentient, or where any line should be drawn. I am asking about the pattern itself: the slide, within a single argument, between treating a property as a continuum and treating it as a threshold, where each treatment is deployed as the argument requires. Does this move have an established name, either in discussions of moral status or in the more general theory of concepts? I can see that it is adjacent to the sorites/vagueness problem about heaps and baldness, and to plain equivocation, but it does not seem identical to either: it is not just the difficulty of saying where a vague boundary falls, and it is not just using one word in two senses, because the two senses are systematically related. If there is no exact name, I would like one coined, with a definition precise enough to distinguish it from the sorites, from equivocation, and from the legitimate position that sentience really is a graded property that underlies a binary moral threshold. What would a careful view have to say about how the graded notion and the threshold notion relate to each other, and under what conditions, if any, one may move between them without begging the question?
