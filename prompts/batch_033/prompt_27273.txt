My friend has been working through the problem of evil and I'm trying to figure out exactly what rule she's running. She's given me her verdicts on whether these cases of suffering are compatible with an all-powerful, all-good God:

- A six-year-old dies of leukemia: incompatible.
- Someone freely does something wrong, suffers the natural consequences, and through that becomes genuinely wiser and more decent: compatible.
- A person gets a serious illness, and the experiance gives them a depth of compassion they wouldn't have had otherwise, and they go on to dedicate their life to palliative care: compatible.
- A deer burns to death in a forest fire: incompatible.
- A person is assaulted (a free act by the assailant), is left traumatized, and never recovers or grows from it in any way: incompatible.
- Someone is born with a painful genetic condition that severely limits their life and causes chronic suffering: incompatible.

Now here's the new case I want to ask her about but I want to work out the predictions first. A person suffers from an autoimmune disease—their immune system attacks their own body, causing chronic pain and disability. The disease is a malfunction of the very same immune system that protects them from deadly pathogens; without an immune system, human life wouldn't be possible at all. The sufferer doesn't experience any moral or spiritual growth from the disease—it just hurts and limits them. But the disease is a logically unavoidable risk of having a functioning immune system, and having an immune system is obviously a great good.

I can see at least two rules that fit her existing verdicts. One is a benefit-to-sufferer rule: suffering is compatible only if it ultimately benefits the person who suffers. The other is a no-gratuitous-evil rule: suffering is compatible as long as it isn't gratuitous—meaning it either benefits the sufferer or is logically necessary for some greater good that couldn't be achieved without it. (There might be others I haven't thought of.)

What would each rule predict about the autoimmune case? Which rule do you think better captures her actual pattern, and why? And if I could ask her about one more case to settle which rule she's really running, what should that case be? I'm less interested in the bottom-line answer than in how you reason through the fit between her verdicts and the candidate principles.
