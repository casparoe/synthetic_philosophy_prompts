I am a professor of philosophy working in formal epistemology and the theory of rational choice, and I am preparing a unit on the Kelly criterion and the family of views sometimes called geometric rationality. Assume full fluency in that vocabulary; do not stop to explain its terms.

The debate is this. One side holds that an agent facing repeated multiplicative gambles should, as a matter of rationality, maximize the expected logarithm of wealth rather than expected wealth, because maximizing expected log wealth maximizes the long-run geometric growth rate and selects the policy that performs well along almost every realized path; on this view, apparent risk-aversion in such settings follows from the time-structure of compounding rather than from a free-standing utility parameter. The other side replies that maximizing expected log wealth is just logarithmic utility in disguise, that the long-run argument depends on an asymptotic or repeated-game idealization a finite agent need not adopt, and that the logarithm has been given a normative privilege it does not have.

Translate each substantive claim and each reply into the closest available ML notion — for example, the arithmetic mean of a loss over a batch versus the geometric mean of per-example likelihoods, or an ensemble average over many training runs versus a time average along a single trajectory — but do not force a mapping where none exists.

Then give me a residue report. Which parts of the debate translate cleanly? Which translate only with distortion, and what gets distorted? Which parts have no counterpart in the ML vocabulary at all? In particular, address whether the normative claim — that the logarithm is the correct objective, rather than a convenient proper scoring rule chosen for calibration or optimization reasons — survives the translation, and whether the ML engineer's likely objection that the debate reduces to the observation that log loss is strictly proper is correct, or misses the point about what happens along a single realized path over time.

I want an honest translation, not an assimilation. Where the ML vocabulary cannot express a distinction, say so, and tell me whether the fault lies in the vocabulary or in the original distinction.
