I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and a puzzle about an infinite process has been nagging at me. Consider a lamp whose switch is toggled infinitely many times before one minute has elapsed: it is switched on at t = 0, off at t = 1/2, on at t = 1/4, off at t = 1/8, and so on, with each interval half the length of the one before. Every toggle occurs before t = 1, so at exactly t = 1 the sequence is complete. Yet the sequence of states has no final entry: it simply alternates on, off, on, off and never settles. I gather philosophers disagree about what to say about the lamp at t = 1. One position is that the lamp must be in some state at t = 1, but it cannot be on, because every time it was switched on it was later switched off before t = 1, and it cannot be off, because every time it was switched off it was later switched on before t = 1; the scenario is therefore incoherent, and this is supposed to show that such supertasks are impossible. The other position is that the description of the switch operations simply does not determine the state at t = 1: it is consistent to add that the lamp is on, that it is off, or even that it no longer exists, so there is no contradiction, only an underdetermined specification.

Please write a dialogue between two people defending these two positions. Make both characters intelligent and careful, with neither a strawman. The dialogue should involve real back-and-forth, with each person responding to the other's actual arguments rather than restating their own view. Have them press on the crux of the disagreement: whether the state at t = 1 has to be fixed by the preceding states, whether "on" and "off" are the only possibilities once the switch history is silent, and whether a completed infinity licenses an inference the description does not actually contain. Aim for something rigorous enough that a mathematically literate reader without philosophy training can follow, but do not turn it into a survey or a reading list. Write the entire piece in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables; present the dialogue as flowing prose, like an email or a letter, not as a labeled script.
