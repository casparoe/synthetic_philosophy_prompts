I work as a programme officer at a medium-sized philanthropic foundation — we fund things in global health, education, and occasionally more speculative stuff. Every grant cycle we get at least a handful of proposals that follow the same pattern: the applicant argues that their intervention has a very small chance of producing an astronomically large benefit (preventing human extinction, unlocking trillions in economic value, ending all disease, that sort of thing). The expected-value calculation, if you take their numbers at face value, always dominates everything else in our portfolio — a one-in-a-million chance of a trillion-dollar payoff is still a million in expectation, which is more than our entire annual budget. But most of these proposals also feel, well, like Pascal's mugging: someone's found a way to make the numbers come out right by picking a big enough payoff and a probability that's small enough to be unfalsifiable but not so small that the maths looks silly.

My executive director has asked me to write a decision procedure that our grants team can use to sort these proposals: which ones deserve genuine evaluation (maybe even funding) and which ones we should dismiss as muggings without spending a week on due dilligence. The procedure needs to be something a non-philosopher can run in an afternoon, it should produce a clear recommendation at the end, and it should flag the points where the procedure itself is making a judgment call rather than applying a rule. It also needs to be honest about the fact that we can't just fall back on "maximise expected value" because that's exactly what the mugging exploits.

I'd like you to write this as a step-by-step procedure with decision points. Then apply it to three hypothetical proposals: (a) a project that claims a 0.01% chance of preventing an AI-related existential catastrophe, valued at 10^14 dollars; (b) a project that claims a 0.1% chance of doubling the income of everyone in a specific low-income country through a novel economic intervention, valued at roughly 10^10 dollars; and (c) a project that claims a 10^-9 chance of bringing about lasting world peace through a global meditation initiative, valued at — they say — 10^20 dollars. I want one of these to come out in a way that would make my team uncomfortable — flag that when it happens.

Please write in British English, take a committed position on how the procedure should work rather than surveying options, and end with a one-sentence summary of the procedure's core principle as the last line.
