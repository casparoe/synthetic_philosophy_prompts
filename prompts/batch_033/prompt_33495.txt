A friend and I argued late into last night about whether AlphaGo's 2016 victory over Lee Sedol tells us anything about minds, and we kept circling back to Dennett's four kinds of creatures: Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian. I want a careful case analysis, not a recap of either the taxonomy or the match, both of which I already know. Take the AlphaGo that beat Lee Sedol 4–1 in March 2016 and analyze it through Dennett's tower of generate-and-test. Give me a precise mapping: where in the system does the supervised learning from human expert games fall, where does the reinforcement learning from self-play fall, where does Monte Carlo Tree Search fall, and is there anything in the pipeline that genuinely deserves to be called Darwinian or Gregorian? Run the analysis in both directions — what the four kinds illuminate about the architecture, and what the architecture does to the taxonomy. Then take a definite position. I do not want a balanced menu of readings: tell me whether AlphaGo is best understood as a confirmation of the tower, a refutation of its neat progression, or evidence that the four kinds are really components an artifact can mix in ways the ladder never anticipated — and defend that verdict. Be explicit about the places where the mapping has to be forced or stretched, because I suspect that is where the interesting conclusions live. If it matters to your verdict, consider the 2017 successor that dispensed with human game records; if it does not matter, say why.
