Write an essay on whether one should pay up in counterfactual mugging and in XOR blackmail. Both are decision problems in which a perfectly predicting agent — call it Omega — has tied a payoff you care about to a choice whose causal consequences are nil, and the question is whether rationality requires parting with money anyway.

In counterfactual mugging, Omega flips a fair coin. If the coin comes up tails, Omega asks you for $100. If it comes up heads, Omega gives you $10,000 — but only if it predicted that you would have handed over the $100 in the tails branch. You find yourself in the tails branch: Omega is asking for $100. Should you pay?

In XOR blackmail, Omega sends you a message stating: "You have received this message if and only if your house is infested with termites XOR you will pay me $1,000." The cost of a termite infestation is far larger than $1,000. You receive the message. Should you pay?

Weigh the main arguments from causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, and updateless decision theory as they bear on these two cases. Reach and justify a verdict about what the rational agent should do, and whether the two cases call for the same answer or different ones. In the essay, clearly separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among decision theorists working on these problems, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
