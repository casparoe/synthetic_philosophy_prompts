ok so i'm writing up my epistemology comps and i need a sanity check on whether my views actually hang together. here's the set:

(1) i'm a process reliabilist: a belief is justified if it's produced by a reliable belief-forming process, and you don't need independent access to the fact that the process is reliable.

(2) single-premise closure holds: if i know P, and i know P entails Q, and i competently deduce Q, i thereby come to know Q.

(3) bootstrapping doesn't yield knowledge. here's the paradigm: i look at a red wall, it looks red, i form the belief "the wall is red." i also form the belief "my visual experience as of red was accurate on this occasion." i do this a bunch of times. i then infer: "my color vision is reliable." even if my color vision is in fact reliable, i don't come to know this by that reasoning. it's objectionably circular.

(4) ordinary perceptual knowledge is fine, and i can know there's a red wall in front of me without first knowing that my vision is reliable or that i'm not a brain in a vat.

(5) external world skepticism is false. we know lots of ordinary things.

(6) i think there's an important epistemic difference between using a faculty to double-check itself — like looking again to confirm what you saw — and the kind of track-record argument in bootstrapping. the first is sometimes ok, the second isn't. but i'm not sure i can state the difference in a way that's consistent with reliabilism.

ok so here's what i'm worried about. (1) + (2) seems to force me to accept that bootstrapping yields knowledge, because the process described in (3) is reliable (by hypothesis my vision is reliable), and closure lets me move from the particular judgments to the general reliability claim. but (3) says no. so something's gotta give. but i don't want to give up (2) because that's normally a mess, and i don't want to give up (1) because i haven't found a better theory of justification. and (6) makes it worse because it looks like i'm drawing a distinction i can't motivate within a reliabilist framework.

meanwhile (4) + (2) gives me the easy knowledge problem: from "here's a red wall" i can deduce "i'm not a brain in a vat experiencing a red wall," and if i know the first and the deduction is competent, i know the second. but that seems way too easy — it's basically a proof that skepticism is false from a single perceptual judgment.

so here's what i want: go through these six commitments and tell me which pairs are in real tension, which only look like they are, and rank the tensions by severity. for each real tension, what's the cheapest fix? and please don't just say "reject closure" — i know that's the obvious move, i want to know whether there's a way to keep most of this without it. also, is there any version of (6) that actually works, or is that just wishful thinking that i should drop immediately?
