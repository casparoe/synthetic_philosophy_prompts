Two people in my reading group got into an argument about Parfit's hitchhiker that I couldn't referee. The setup they both accepted: you're stranded in the desert, a driver stops and will take you home only if you sincerely promise to pay him a thousand dollars once you're there. He's good enough at reading people (or your source code, if you prefer the modern version) to know whether you'll actually pay. Once you're home, paying buys you nothing, and the driver can't enforce the promise.

N said the rational answer is obviously to not pay: once you're home the thousand dollars is pure loss, the rescue is already a sunk cost, and a clear-eyed agent doesn't burn money for no future benefit. P said that's exactly the agent the driver leaves in the desert. If you're the kind of agent who pays, you get rescued; if you're not, you don't. So paying is the winning policy, and calling non-payment 'rational' confuses the act with the policy.

N came back: the disposition was useful ex ante, but that doesn't make the later act rational. By the time you're home, the driver has already rescued you and your disposition is whatever it is; no future benefit can come from paying. That's like saying you should keep paying for a gym membership you no longer use because paying for it is what got you in shape.

P replied: that time-slice view is the whole mistake. You're one continuing algorithm, not a sequence of independent agents, and the algorithm 'pay when I've promised, even when it's locally costly' is the one that survives repeated Parfit scenarios and gets picked up. If your self-model includes being a payer, paying now is just acting as the agent who wins, not making an error.

N said: in the one-off case, with no reputation and no future drivers, all of that drops out, so P is smuggling in iteration to save the policy. And if the case is 'driver reads your code and rescues iff you would pay,' the right move is to precommit or self-modify so that you pay — but that's a different claim then saying the unmodified you, after rescue, should pay.

P answered that the unmodified you is just the loser; the question was never what a time-slice should do, and a 'rationality' that tells you to die in the desert is using the wrong word.
