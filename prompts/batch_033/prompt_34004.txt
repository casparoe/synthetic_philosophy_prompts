My brother was in a bad car accident a little over a year ago and he's now in what the doctors call a minimally conscious state, so I've been reading about what it even means to say someone is "conscious" and whether it can be measured. One of his neurologists mentioned Integrated Information Theory in passing, and a friend of a friend who works in AI sent me a popular science article that summarizes it. The article's summary goes something like this. IIT says consciousness is identical to integrated information: a system is conscious to the extent that it generates information that is more than the sum of what its parts generate, quantified by a number called Φ (phi). The theory starts from five properties every experience has — it exists for the one having it, it has structure, it is specific rather than vague, it is unified, and it has definite boundaries — and then requires any physical substrate of consciousness to have matching causal properties. Because Φ is a quantity, the article continues, consciousness is a matter of degree, so any system with positive Φ, even a simple photodiode, has a little bit of experience; that, it says, is why a lot of people call IIT a form of panpsychism. It also says the theory has been confirmed in the clinic: when doctors stimulate the brain with transcranial magnetic stimulation and measure the echo, the "perturbational complexity index" tracks whether a patient is conscious, which is exactly what IIT would predict. Finally it concludes that a sufficiently detailed computer simulation of a human brain would have to be conscious, since it would have the same integrated information as the real thing.

I keep comming back to this article when I think about my brother, and I want to know how much of it I can actually trust. Could you answer as someone who works on theories of consciousness — philosophy of mind or the neuroscience side — and go through the summary carefully? For each part, tell me what's accurate, what's a common simplification, what's subtly wrong, and what a defender of IIT would object to. Please also seperate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present a contested take as if it were the consensus.
