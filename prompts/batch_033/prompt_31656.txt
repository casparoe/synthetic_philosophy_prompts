I've been working through the philosophical literature on imprecise probabilities, and I'm stuck on what seems like a straightforward objection. The core motivation is that when evidence is sparse or ambiguous, adopting a single precise credence — say, 0.5 — is arbitrary: your evidence doesn't warrant that degree of specificity. The proposed remedy is to adopt a set of probability functions, typically summarized as an interval like [0.3, 0.7]. But now the boundaries of the interval seem to face the same charge of arbitrariness. Why is it any less arbitrary to claim that the probability lies within [0.3, 0.7] than to claim it is exactly 0.5? In both cases you're drawing lines that your evidence, being sparse, seems insufficient to justify. What am I missing?
