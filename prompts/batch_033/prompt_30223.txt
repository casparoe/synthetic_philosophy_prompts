So I got into a long argument with my friend Sam last night about imprecise probabilities, and by the end I genuinly couldn’t tell if I was defending a view or just a pile of intuitions that don’t fit together. Here’s what I’m committed to, roughly:

(1) Rational belief states are sets of probability functions, not single precise credences. Most real-world evidence underdetermines any exact number, and picking one is just false precision.

(2) Updating a representor is done pointwise: when I learn E, I replace each probability function P in my representor with P(·|E), and the new representor is the set of all those conditional functions. This feels forced rather than optional.

(3) Cost-free information can’t rationally leave you worse off or less informed. If a theory tells you that any possible outcome of an experiment would make your state less informative, that’s a reductio of the theory, not a discovery about learning.

(4) The dilation examples really bother me. I don’t think they’re just a harmless formal artifact of imprecise probabilities; they look like cases where any outcome of an experiment widens your interval for the thing you care about, and that seems to violate (3). Sam says this means pointwise conditioning has to go, but I’m not sure whether the problem is (2), (3), or something else.

(5) In decisions, I’m ambiguity-averse in roughly the Ellsberg sense. I don’t think you’re allowed to just choose one arbitrary member of your representor and maximize expected utility with it; the imprecision should show up in what you do.

(6) But I don’t think the representor is a second-order probability distribution. I’m not treating my uncertainty as if there’s a probability over the probability functions in the set.

(7) I also have no problem assigning a precise 1/2 to a known fair coin toss, even though I’m an imprecise probabilist.

I’d like someone to audit this rather than lecture me. Which pairs of these are in genuine tension, which only look like they are, and how severe is each? For the real tensions, tell me what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs. Also, because I’ve seen people argue about dilation as if the correct response is settled: separate what’s actually settled from what’s genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don’t present a contested move like dropping pointwise conditioning as the obvious consensus.
