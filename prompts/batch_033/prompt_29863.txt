I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, and the book I'm working on has two characters who keep getting into the same fight: one is a committed lexical threshold negative utilitarian, the other is a classical hedonistic utilitarian who thinks pleasure and pain sit on a single scale and can be traded off at any ratio. The plot forces them to co-author a joint statement after years of argueing past each other, and I want the actual document they'd produce, or close enough that I can steal it for the novel without it sounding like a parody.

Draft the statement for me. I want it to include: a numbered section of claims both can genuinely sign, about what negative utilitarianism says, what the lexical threshold claim amounts to, what commensurability would require, and what they agree on about extreme suffering; then a disagreements table, where each row is one proposition worded so each side would accept the wording of its own position, with a column for each side's stance; then a short list of cruxes, the sub-questions where settling them would actually move one of them; and for each crux, a concrete case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Don't let them hide behind slogans. I want the exact wording on: whether there is any amount of mild pleasure that would justify a single episode of intense agony; whether the threshold applies within a life, across lives, or both; whether the real disagreement is about value or about decision-making under uncertainty; whether very small probabilities of extreme suffering should dominate certain moderate benefits; and whether they actually disagree about comensurability or just about how steeply pain should be weighted. The classic moves should be on the table: the classical utilitarian accuses the other of letting a one-in-a-trillion chance of torture swamp every ordinary decision, and the lexical threshold person accuses the classical utilitarian of being willing to torture someone for enough ice cream. And if part of their fight turns out to be merely verbal, say which part and why.
