I've been reporting a piece on how much of our mental life is in the head versus fixed by the world outside us, and I keep returning to Putnam's Twin Earth argument for semantic externalism. The position, as I understand it, is that the meanings of at least some of our terms — natural-kind terms like water being the paradigm — are not determined solely by internal psychological states but are partly constituted by the actual stuff in our environment that we are causally connected to. On Twin Earth, where the clear liquid in the lakes is XYZ rather than H₂O, Oscar's twin does not mean water by his word, even though the twins are internally identical. Meaning is not in the head.

I want to know what would count against this view. Not just what critics have said, but what its defenders would have to concede as genuine counterevidence. Which empirical discoveries, which thought-experiment intuitions, which results in neighboring fields would put real pressure on semantic externalism? Are there developments that have arguably already occurred that should worry an externalist? And I would like an honest assessment of whether the position has been qualified in ways that insulate it from falsification — and if so, whether that insulation is a problem for a thesis of this kind. I am especially interested in defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and in whether the position risks anything at all.
