I'm putting together a handout for an undergrad course that mixes philosophy and economics, and I want to start the unit on welfare and utility with one of those "which position do you actually hold?" sorting quizzes — the kind where students answer questions about cases and only then find out which view they've been implicitly relying on. The class has been going back and forth between two impulses: that utility is just a label for whatever someone chooses, and that utility is something like well-being or happiness that a person's choices can get wrong. I'd like eight to ten questions, each built around a concrete case rather than a slogan: a woman who says she wants to quit smoking but buys a pack every day; someone picking between two jobs who can rank them but can't say by how much she prefers one; a policymaker trying to compare winners' gains to losers' losses; a person whose choice flips when the same options are framed differently; someone who acts on principle against what would make him happy. Each question should have two or three answer options a student could pick without knowing the technical vocabulary.

The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these positions: a Samuelson-style revealed preference view (choice constitutes or defines preference, and utility is just a numerical representation of it); a mentalist view on which preference is a real psychological attitude and choice is only fallible evidence for it; preference-satisfaction welfarism, where welfare consists in getting what you prefer, whatever that is; a hedonist or experienced-utility view on which utility is felt pleasure or satisfaction and choices can be systematically mistaken about it; and a Sen-style view on which welfare is plural and you can't read it off behavior without adding normative assumptions. The sorting has to come from the cases, not from questions like "which of these labels sounds right?" Also, flag any two positions your quiz can't cleanly seperate with these questions, and if some pattern of answers comes out empty or incoherent, say so and say why you think nobody lands there. The students have had a semester of intermediate micro and a couple weeks of moral philosophy, so you can gesture at things like WARP or menu dependence but shouldn't lean on them.
