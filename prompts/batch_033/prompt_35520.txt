I work as a software engineer at a company that builds generative image models. In internal discussions about the ethics of what we're shipping, I keep hearing a particular analogy: that training a model on millions of existing artworks is no different in principle from a human painter spending years studying art history, visiting museums, and absorbing influences. The conclusion people draw is that if we don't consider human artists to be plagiarists or copyright violators for having influences, we shouldn't consider these models to be either — the process is structurally the same, just faster and at scale.

I want to think through whether this analogy actually holds up, and I don't trust myself to spot all the places it might quietly break. Give me a correspondence table: what in the machine-learning pipeline maps onto the artist's education, their memory, their influences, their taste, their act of making a new work? Then walk through where the mapping breaks, and — this is the part I most need — separate the disanalogies that matter for the copyright and creativity question from the ones that are merely cosmetic differences in substrate or speed. Finally, tell me whether the conclusion (that there's no objection here that doesn't also apply to human artistic practice) survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was quietly carrying the argument the whole time.
