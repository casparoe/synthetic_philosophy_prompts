We've been on Sartre for two weeks and I'm trying to nail down what actually makes something bad faith. I went through a bunch of cases and wrote down my gut calls, and now I want to know what principle (if any) captures them. I also have a rough idea for a principle but it might be garbage.

Cases:

1. The waiter who's totally absorbed in his role — every gesture is a performance, he's like a robot playing waiter. Bad faith. He's denying his transcendence, pretending he's a thing.

2. My uncle who cheats on his taxes and says "everyone does it, the system's rigged, I don't have a choice." Bad faith. He made choices and is pretending otherwise.

3. Someone with severe OCD who says "I can't stop checking the stove, I've tried everything." Not bad faith. That's a real compulsion, not a story he's telling himself.

4. The classic date case — woman lets the man hold her hand but treats it as a neutral object, refuses to "notice" the sexual meaning. Bad faith.

5. My friend who's been saying she wants to leave our hometown for six years. Every time I ask there's a new reason — her mom's health, the job market, "maybe after the winter." I genuinely can't decide if this is bad faith or not. The constraints are real-ish? But also she's clearly avoiding something.

6. Someone in a rigid caste system who says "this is my place, it's what I am." I lean bad faith but I worry I'm just being culturally arrogant — maybe that really is their facticity in a way I don't get.

7. Soldier following an immoral order: "just following orders." Bad faith, obviously.

My tentative principle: bad faith is when you treat yourself as having less freedom than you actually do, in order to avoid the anguish of choice. But that makes case 3 tricky — the OCD person IS treating themselves as having less freedom, but it's not "in order to" avoid anguish, the anguish is the whole problem. So maybe the principle needs something about the function of the self-deception? Like, it only counts as bad faith if the story you're telling yourself serves to let you off the hook for something you could actually choose?

Test that against the cases and tell me where it breaks. And what would this principle say about someone who says "I can't leave my religion, I was raised in it, it's who I am" — is that bad faith or just facticity?
