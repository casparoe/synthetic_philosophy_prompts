On the blogs and forums where I have spent years reading about AI and rationality, there is a recurring argument about whether a large language model can be said to understand the text it processes, or whether its knowledge of some fact is really "in" its weights. I have recently been rereading Douglas Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach: An Eternal Golden Braid, and the chapter on the location of meaning keeps striking me as directly relevant to that argument in a way I have not seen anyone spell out. Hofstadter separates three levels of information in a message: the frame message, which is roughly the fact that something is a message at all and the cue to look for a decoding mechanism; the outer message, which is the information carried by the structure and symbol-patterns that tells you how to decode the inner message; and the inner message, which is the content itself. The usual debates about model understanding seem to me to proceed as though the inner message alone settles the matter: people probe activations, inspect weights, or test whether a fact can be elicited by a sufficiently clever prompt. If Hofstadter is right that meaning is spread across the frame, the decoding machinery, and the content, then that way of talking may be asking the wrong question, or at least an incomplete one.

Please describe the most important implications of this frame/outer/inner distinction for claims of the form "the model understands X" or "the model's knowledge of X is located in its weights." In particular: what would have to be true about the frame and outer messages in a given case before it is reasonable to attribute understanding to the model itself rather than to the larger system made up of model, interface, trainer, and user? Does the distinction suggest concrete tests or observations that could separate cases in which the inner message genuinely carries the understanding from cases in which the understanding is being supplied from outside the model? And does it give us a precise way to state why "the meaning is in the token stream" is not merely false but confused? I am not looking for the generic point that meaning depends on context; I want the specific consequences that follow from keeping the three levels distinct.
