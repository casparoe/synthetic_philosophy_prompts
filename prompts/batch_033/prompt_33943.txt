The substantivalism-versus-relationism debate about space and time has been running since Newton and Leibniz, and in its modern form it feels to me like it has completely stalled. The physics isn't in dispute: general relativity is diffeomorphism invariant, the hole argument is widely taken to show that treating spacetime points as individuals gets you radical indeterminism, and nobody thinks Newton's bucket settles anything on its own any more. Yet philosophers still sort themselves into substantivalists and relationists, and the moves have barely changed in decades. My working hypothesis is that the real disagreement is about what it takes for two putatively different worlds to be genuinely different — whether a Leibniz-shifted world or a diffeomorphism-related model counts as a distinct possibility — and that this is a modal-metaphysics dispute wearing philosophy-of-physics clothes. Is that right? If so, what kind of disagreement is it, and what would actually count as progress? If not, where's the actual friction? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of the positions, and I'd rather you told me if the question is just ill-posed.
