Write the joint statement that a classical set theorist and a constructive mathematician could both sign at the conclusion of a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the acceptability of Zorn's lemma. The classical side regards Zorn's lemma as a standard and legitimate tool, and regards the existence theorems it yields as genuine results about the mathematical universe. The constructive side regards a proof via Zorn's lemma as failing to exhibit the object whose existence it asserts — it supplies no construction, no witness, and no method — and therefore as not establishing existence in the only sense that carries mathematical content.

Begin with a numbered list of claims both sides can sign. The list should include: a precise statement of Zorn's lemma; its equivalence over ZF with the Axiom of Choice, the Hausdorff maximal principle, and the well-ordering theorem; the sense in which proofs using Zorn's lemma are nonconstructive; the standard applications (for example, the existence of a basis for every vector space, the existence of maximal ideals in every nonzero commutative ring with identity, and the extension of every consistent set of first-order sentences to a complete theory); the restricted cases in which a construction or a choice-free proof is available (for example, finite-dimensional vector spaces, or spaces with an explicit well-ordering or an explicitly presented basis); and the standard metamathematical results that ZFC is consistent relative to ZF and that ZF plus the negation of the Axiom of Choice is consistent relative to ZF, signable as facts about formal systems rather than as endorsements.

Then give a disagreements table. Each row should state a single question, the classical position in wording a classical mathematician would accept, and the constructive position in wording a constructivist would accept. Separate the disagreements that are genuine from the ones that are verbal. In particular, address whether the dispute over the word "existence" is substantive or terminological, and say explicitly which part, if any, is verbal and why.

Then identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. At minimum, address these three: (1) whether the iterative conception of set supports the Axiom of Choice, or is neutral on it; (2) whether the standard applications of Zorn's lemma admit constructive or choice-free proofs in the general case, and what follows if they do not; and (3) whether a nonconstructive existence proof shows that an object exists in the intended domain, or only that a certain formal theory proves a certain sentence. For each crux, specify a concrete case, theorem, or argument that both sides would accept as bearing on it — for example, the Hamel basis of the reals over the rationals, or the independence and relative consistency results mentioned above — and record in advance what each side predicts about how that case, theorem, or argument turns out.

The document must be one both parties could put their names to: agreed claims should be neither vacuous nor slanted, and the residual disagreement should be made exact rather than softened.
