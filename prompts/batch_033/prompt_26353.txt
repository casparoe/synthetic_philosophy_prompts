I'm a grant officer at a small foundation that funds economic alternatives—worker co-ops, community land trusts, mutuals, that kind of thing. Our board has gotten really into Mill’s later writings on socialism, the stuff where he’s curious but full of caveats, and they want every proposal we fund to be consistent with that mindset. Trouble is, nobody here has time to re‑read the *Chapters on Socialism* before every docket, and the board won’t just let me wing it. I need a decision procedure I can run a proposal through in about half an hour—something I can actually use while staring at a stack of grant applications. It should be a checklist or flow‑chart in words, grounded in Mill’s late views, but turned into steps a non‑philosopher can follow. Mill cared about things like the danger of a central despotism squashing individual spontaneity, the need for experiments in living to compete so we learn what works, the right of exit, the dread of a “stationary” arrangement that kills improvement, and the promise of worker self‑management. I need those turned into clear yes/no/flag questions, with stop rules where a proposal should be dumped, and points where the procedure honestly says “this is a values call, not a fact call.”

Once you’ve laid out the procedure, please run it on three proposals we’ve actually gotten, and show me exactly what happens at each step:  
(1) A city‑wide mandate that all new commercial developments must be owned as worker cooperatives, with a municipal board setting wage ratios.  
(2) A grant for a loose federation of six independently owned worker‑run cafés that share a common name, a minimum wage floor, and a training program, but keep their own books and can leave the federation at will.  
(3) A rural land‑trust community where residents own their houses but can never sell on the open market—sales must go back to the trust at a price set by formula, and the trust decides who gets the house next.

Note that I’m pretty fond of that third one, so don’t go easy on it just because I like it. I need to know where the procedure smacks it down, if it does. And please, point out the steps that are really just my own prejudices dressed up as a rule.
