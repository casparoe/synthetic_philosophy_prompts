The topic I keep returning to is luck egalitarianism and the harshness objection: the charge that the view licenses leaving people to suffer the consequences of their own imprudent choices. I have been following this debate on the forum for a while, and it increasingly looks as though the two sides are answering different questions when they call a person responsible. One question is whether a bad outcome is attributable to the person's agency in the sense relevant to moral appraisal; the other is whether the person should be made to bear the resulting costs, in the sense relevant to what they are owed by others. My suspicion is that a luck egalitarian can concede the first while resisting the second, and that a good deal of the standoff comes from not keeping the two questions apart.
