I've been reading Montaigne's essay on cannibals (the one about the Tupinamba) and I keep going in circles. Here's the argument as I understand it.

(1) Montaigne says the Tupinamba aren't barbarous for eating their dead enemies, because barbarous is just what we call customs that arent ours; there's no outside standard to judge another culture by.
(2) But then he turns around and calls European customs barbarous — burning people alive, torturing prisoners, massacring people over religion.
(3) If (1) is right, then from inside European custom those practices are just custom too, so he has no more basis to condemn them than he has to condemn the cannibals.
(4) If instead there is some standard beyond custom, then he needs to say what it is and why it clears the Tupinamba but not the Europeans.
(5) So the essay either contradicts itself or leans on an unstated standard it never defends.
(6) Either way, the relativism that lets him defend the cannibals proves too much: it takes away the very standard he needs to attack European cruelty.

I'd like to keep both his defense of the cannibals and his attack on European cruelty, so I'm hoping the mistake is somewhere in how I've set this up. Which step should I reject, and what do I have to give up to keep both? Keep the answer under 500 words.
