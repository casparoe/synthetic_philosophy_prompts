I'm an engineer, not a philosopher, but I've ended up in a long-running argument with my coworker Maya about whether a model "actually has" a concept or whether we're just reading one into the activations. Maya runs our interpretability team and she's got pretty firm verdicts on a bunch of cases. She says the classic single neuron that fires for cats is not a representation, just a detector, because nobody has shown the model uses it as a cat-variable. She says a linear probe that reads off sentiment from hidden states with high accuracy doesn't show the model represents sentiment, because the probe could be doing the work. She says the first principal component of our embeddings, which tracks sentence length, is just a summary statistic. She says a polysemantic neuron that fires on a bunch of unrelated things isn't a representation either. But she does say the circuit people found in small transformers trained on modular addition is a real representation — the one where a specific attention head and MLP path is responsible for the algorithm, and if you ablate it the model stops getting the right answer, and if you patch its activations the output changes in exactly the predicted way. She also says a single neuron in a vision model that only fires for dogs counts as a real representation after they showed that ablating it makes dog classifications drop (and cat classifications are unaffected), while turning its activation up does the reverse.

Now we've got a new result. Somebody trained a sparse autoencoder on our LLM and found a feature that activates almost exclusively on text about financial fraud. It's not a single neuron or head — it's a direction spread across hundreds of neurons. When they add a multiple of that direction to the residual stream during a forward pass, the model's completions shift to be about fraud and it gets way more likely to flag a transaction as fraudlent. They haven't done the ablation version on normal fraud text, and they haven't tied it to any specific neuron or head.

I want to know what Maya would say about this one. I don't just want a yes/no — I want to know what principle she's actually applying, which alternative principles also fit her track record, and what single extra test or case would force her to choose between them. If you think her past verdicts are actually inconsistent, say that and say which one she'd have to give up.
