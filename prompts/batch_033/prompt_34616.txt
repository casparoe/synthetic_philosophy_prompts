My older sister just had her first child, and at a family gathering my cousin — who has taken a few college philosophy courses — made an argument that has been troubling me ever since. He said that having children is morally risky in a way that not having them is not. His reasoning was: if you bring a child into existence and that child suffers terribly, you have harmed someone who would not have existed otherwise, and you bear responsibility for that harm. But if you decide not to have a child, you have not harmed anyone by depriving them of a happy life, because there is no one there to be deprived. So the possibility of suffering always gives you a reason not to have children, but the possibility of happiness does not give you a reason to have them.

This seems like it must go wrong somewhere — surely my sister has not done something morally questionable just by having a baby — but I cannot locate the mistake. Is there a name for this kind of argument? What are the strongest objections to it?
