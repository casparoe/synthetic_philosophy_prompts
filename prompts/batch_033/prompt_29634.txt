I'm putting together a "which theory of reduction and emergence do you actually hold?" quiz for a philosophy of science reading group I run — mostly science grad students who aren't philosophers. I want eight to ten questions, each a concrete scientific case, with two or three answer choices per question. The scoring key should map patterns of answers onto something like: eliminativism, Nagelian reductionism, non-reductive physicalism, weak emergence, strong emergence, and pragmatic pluralism if you think it's a distinct view.

The important thing: questions should sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label sounds good to them. So no question should use the words "reduction," "emergence," "supervenience," or "physicalism." I want cases like: Conway's Game of Life gliders, temperature as mean kinetic energy, ant colonies, whether the properties of water are fixed by the properties of H and O atoms, whether a perfect molecule-by-molecule simulation of a neuron makes talk of "neural firing" redundant, phase transitions and critical phenomena, and whether an economic recession is anything over and above individual decisions. Use whatever cases actually separate the positions — you don't have to stick to my list.

Each question should do genuine diagnostic work: if a question gets the same answer from everyone except strong emergentists, it's coasting. I want questions where the positions genuinely come apart. And in the scoring key, flag any pairs of positions the quiz can't reliably separate, and say whether that's because the distinction is a matter of emphasis (so maybe the labels collapse) or because you'd need a different kind of question entirely.

Also, please note any combination of answers that no reasonable person would give — the unoccupied regions of the logical space — and briefly say whether that's because the combo is incoherent, just implausible, or simply underexplored.
