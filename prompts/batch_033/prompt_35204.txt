I'm organizing a workshop on the foundations of set theory, and I want the centerpiece to be an adversarial-collaboration document between two camps on the continuum hypothesis.

Camp A holds that CH has a determinate truth value and that set theory should aim to settle it by adopting new axioms — for example, by appealing to large cardinals, forcing axioms, or considerations of maximality. Camp B holds that CH is genuinely indeterminate: the universe of sets admits equally legitimate models in which CH holds and models in which it fails, so the question has no unique answer, and set theory is better understood as describing a multiverse of such models.

Draft the joint statement that representatives of both camps could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The document should contain:

(1) A numbered list of claims both camps agree on. These should cover: what the formal independence results (Gödel, Cohen) establish and what they do not establish; what each camp acknowledges as a legitimate motivation for the other; and what each camp would count as progress, even if it did not settle the debate.

(2) A disagreements table with these columns:
- "Issue": the precise point of disagreement.
- "Camp A's position": stated in wording a Camp A advocate would endorse.
- "Camp B's position": stated in wording a Camp B advocate would endorse.
- "Substantive or verbal?": your assessment of whether this is a genuine disagreement about set theory or a difference in how the two camps use words like "true" or "exists."

(3) For each substantive disagreement, identify a crux — a sub-question such that resolving it would move at least one side — and, where possible, describe a test, argument, or mathematical development that both sides agree would bear on that crux. Record each side's prediction about the outcome of that test in advance.

If you conclude that part of their dispute is merely verbal, say so explicitly in the document and explain which part and why.

After the joint statement, provide a structured summary as a JSON object with these fields:
- "agreed_claims": array of strings, one per agreed claim.
- "disagreements": array of objects, each with keys "issue", "camp_A_position", "camp_B_position", "substantive_or_verbal", "crux", "test_if_any", "camp_A_prediction", "camp_B_prediction".
- "verbal_dispute_portions": array of strings describing any portions you judge to be merely verbal.
