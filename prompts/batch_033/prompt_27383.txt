A long-form magazine article I read recently summarized Bostrom's simulation argument like this:

"Nick Bostrom proved that we are almost certainly living in a computer simulation. The logic is straightforward: any civilization that reaches a sufficiently advanced stage will start running vast numbers of simulated realities, and since simulated minds will vastly outnumber original ones, the probability that we are among the originals is effectively zero. This is really just Descartes' evil demon updated for the digital age — if we are in a simulation, all our sensory evidence is unreliable and we cannot know anything about the world outside our own minds. Bostrom himself takes the conclusion so seriously that he has suggested we should try to live interesting lives, so that our simulators don't get bored and shut us down."

I'd like you to assess how fair this summary is. Please go through it claim by claim and tell me what Bostrom's actual argument says, where the summary gets things right, where it subtly distorts them, and whether the practical recommendation at the end has any basis in his writings. I'm particularly interested in whether the comparison to Cartesian skepticism is illuminating or misleading.
