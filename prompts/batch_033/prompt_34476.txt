I just finished jury duty and it got me thinking about information cascades — I'd read about them in a psych class years ago but never actually watched one unfold in real time. Our foreperson, Mallory, was kind of the star of the show. Here's what she did across four different votes during deliberations:

1. First charge, first vote was 9-3 for guilty. Mallory was in the minority (not guilty). After the majority walked through their reasoning, she switched to guilty on the second vote.

2. Second charge, first vote was 6-6. Mallory was for guilty. After discussion, she stayed guilty.

3. Third charge, first vote was 10-2 for not guilty. Mallory was in the majority. She stayed not guilty.

4. Fourth charge, first vote was 9-3 for guilty. Mallory was in the minority again. But this time the majority basically just kept repeating the prosecutor's closing — no new reasoning, no engagement with the defense's points. She stayed not guilty.

Now we're on the last charge and the first vote was 7-5 for guilty. Mallory's in the minority (not guilty). The majority offered some arguments that Mallory herself said were "actually pretty compelling" but not "slam-dunk." The second vote is tomorrow morning and I'm trying to call it.

What do you think Mallory will do? More importantly, what rule is she running? I can think of at least two rules that fit her first four votes perfectly but give opposite predictons on this one. Walk me through what each rule says and which one you'd bet on. And if you had to design one more vote to nail down which rule she's really using, what would it look like?
