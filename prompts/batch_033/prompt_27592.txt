I've long been persuaded by the view that conceivability is a reliable, if fallible, guide to metaphysical possibility. Roughly: if I can coherently conceive of a scenario — say, water failing to be H₂O, or a mind existing without a body — that gives me prima facie reason to think the scenario is metaphysically possible. I know about the distinction between epistemic and metaphysical possibility, and about the necessary a posteriori, but I take those to show that conceivability needs to be handled carefully, not that it's worthless. What I want is an honest map of what would count against this view: which arguments, which cases, which theoretical costs should move me? I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I'd like to know which ones a committed defender of the conceivability-possibility link would themselves concede as counting against them, as opposed to dismissing. Please say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you're least sure of.
