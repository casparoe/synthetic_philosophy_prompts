I've been rewatching *Severance* and can't stop thinking about it alongside Dennett's "Where Am I?" In Dennett's thought experiment, his brain is removed and placed in a vat, controlling his body remotely via radio link; the puzzle is where Dennett the person is located — at the brain, at the body, or at the point of view. *Severance* presents a related puzzle: the severance procedure splits memory so that the "innie" exists only on the severed floor and the "outie" only outside it, with no shared experience. The show treats innie and outie as distinct persons, yet they share a body, and the outie's initial decision to sever binds the innie to a life they never chose. I want a reading of *Severance* through the lens of Dennett's puzzle and the personal identity debate it opens up. What theory of personal identity does the show implicitly commit to — psychological continuity, biological continuity, something else? Where does it locate the person when innie and outie conflict? Are there moments where the show's own metaphysics becomes inconsistent, and does Dennett's framework reveal something about those moments that a casual viewer would miss? I'm writing an essay on this and want the reading to take both the philosophy and the show seriously, without reducing either to an illustration of the other.
