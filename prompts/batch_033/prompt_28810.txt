Write a villanelle about the epistemology of peer disagreement — the debate between conciliationism and steadfastness. The two refrains should capture the core commitments of each position. One refrain should give voice to the conciliationist thought that learning an epistemic peer disagrees with you is itself higher-order evidence that you may have reasoned poorly, and that rationality requires moving your credence toward theirs. The other should give voice to the steadfast thought that you possess first-person access to your own evidence and reasoning, and that this access grounds your belief in a way that another person's contrary verdict does not undercut. The villanelle form should do argumentative work: the refrains alternate, each reasserting itself after the other has spoken, and they finally meet in the closing couplet — not resolved, but juxtaposed, much as the debate itself resists resolution because each side can diagnose the other's position as itself an instance of the phenomenon under dispute. The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with the Elga, Christensen, and Kelly literature would recognize the views, and well-crafted enough that a reader unfamiliar with the debate would still find it a good poem.
