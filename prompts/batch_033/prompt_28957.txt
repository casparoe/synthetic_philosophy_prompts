So I've somehow ended up giving a talk at our company's ML reading group next month, and I'm going to argue that inverse reinforcement learning has a pretty fundemental blind spot when it comes to actually recovering what humans *want* (as opposed to just what they do). My basic line is: IRL treats all behavior as optimal with respect to some reward function plus maybe a noise term, but real human behavior is shaped by a ton of stuff that isn't preference at all — false beliefs about the world, social pressure, akrasia, habits, constraints people don't even know they're operating under. The math can't tell the difference between "she walked to the cafe because she prefers the scenic route" and "she walked to the cafe because she's wrong about which way is shorter." And if you're using IRL to infer human values for alignment purposes, that distinction matters a lot.
