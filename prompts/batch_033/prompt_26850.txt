I am a novelist, and I have been trying to work out what I actually believe about the limits of free expression by recording my responses to cases. My verdicts run as follows. A novel that gives a real, identifiable living person invented private scenes of cruelty and publishes them as fiction: I find this acceptable when the book signals clearly that it is invention, and unacceptable when it is crafted so that readers will take the scenes as documented fact. A satire that savages a religious figure so fiercely that believers experience it as a deep wound: acceptable. A memoir that fabricates episodes and markets them as true: unacceptable, though the wrong feels like a betrayal of the reader rather than a harm to some third party. A thriller that includes an accurate, workable method for producing a lethal poison: unacceptable. A thriller that inhabits a mass shooter sympathetically and might, in a disturbed reader, tip the balance toward violence: I am inclined to say acceptable, so long as it issues no call to action. An afterword to a novel that denies the Holocaust while presenting itself as historical scholarship: unacceptable. A novel that re-imagines a dead author with invented love affairs: acceptable, even over the estate's distress. Non-consensual deepfake sexual imagery of a real person: unacceptable. The publication of leaked documents exposing illegal state surveillance: acceptable.

I keep reaching for Mill's harm principle as the spine of all this, but it plainly does not fit the deception cases as it stands, and I am not sure how it distinguishes the poison manual from the sympathetic shooter. Is there one principle, or a small set of candidates, that captures these verdicts? What must be added to, or changed in, the harm principle to make it fit? Which of my verdicts does the best version of the principle force me to abandon? And what does it say about the case I cannot settle: a novel that makes a paranoid political ideology emotionally compelling to a reader who later acts violently on it, without ever advocating or instructing anything?
