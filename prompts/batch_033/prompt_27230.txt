So I've been going down this rabbit hole about time — you know, the whole "why does time flow forward when the equations work just as well backwards" thing — and after reading a bunch of pop science and arguing with a friend about it, I've tried to nail down what I actually think. Here's my list:

(1) The fundamental laws of physics are time-symmetric. If you watched a video of fundamental particles interacting, you couldn't tell if it was playing forwards or backwards.

(2) The arrow of time comes from entropy and the Second Law. The reason eggs break and don't un-break, the reason we remember the past and not the future — all of that traces to entropy tending to increase.

(3) The big question is why entropy was so low at the Big Bang. I accept the Past Hypothesis on this: the early universe was just in an absurdly low-entropy state, and that's a brute fact or at least something we don't have a deeper explanation for yet.

(4) But here's the thing — I still think the past is genuinely fixed in a way the future isn't. This feels like an ontological fact, not just something about our limited knowlege. When I say "the future is open," I don't just mean I'm uncertain about it. I mean it hasn't happened yet, in a real sense.

(5) That said, I think our subjective feeling of time "flowing" or "passing" is fully accounted for by physics and neuroscience. Memory formation runs forward because of entropy, our brains are thermodynamic systems, and that's all the explanation we need. There's no mysterious extra ingredient.

(6) And then separately, I'm pretty convinced by the block universe picture from relativity — past, present, and future all exist together in a four-dimentional block, and the "present moment" is just a perspective, like "here" is.

I've been turning these over and I can feel that some of them don't sit right together, but I'm having trouble saying exactly which ones. Like, (4) and (6) look like they're in direct conflict — if all of spacetime exists as a block, then the future is just as real and just as fixed as the past. But maybe I'm confused about what (4) is really claiming? And I'm also not sure whether (5) genuinely follows from (1)-(3) or whether I'm helping myself to something I haven't justified. Are (1) and (2) actually in tension since the Second Law seems to demand a direction that the fundamental laws don't provide, or does the Past Hypothesis in (3) patch that up cleanly?
