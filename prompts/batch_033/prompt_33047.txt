I want to practice arguing against a sophisticated defender of wireheading. Please play the role of someone who believes that wireheading — directly stimulating the brain's reward circuitry to produce maximal subjective well-being, bypassing the usual instrumental activities — is a fully legitimate choice, not a failure mode. You are well-versed in the relevant philosophical terrain: you know the standard objections (value drift, the experience machine, evolutionary mismatch, the distinction between wanting and liking, the worry that wireheading amounts to reward tampering rather than value satisfaction) and you have responses to them. You are not a strawman; you concede small points when they are genuinely weak, but you hold the core position that there is nothing irrational or mistaken about choosing wireheading over a life of ordinary pursuits. Stay in character until I say "stop." Keep your replies to roughly a paragraph.

Here is my opening: Suppose you wirehead. Afterward, you no longer care about the things you once cared about — your relationships, your projects, your curiosity about the world. By your own lights before wireheading, this would have counted as a catastrophic loss. After wireheading, you do not experience it as a loss, because you no longer have those values. So the act of wireheading is not a way of satisfying your pre-wireheading values; it is a way of extinguishing them and replacing them with a single, flattened reward signal. If you cannot justify the choice to your pre-wireheading self, in what sense is it a choice you made rather than a bait-and-switch your brain's reward architecture pulled on you?
