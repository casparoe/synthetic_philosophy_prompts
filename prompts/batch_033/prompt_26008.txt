I teach a module on information cascades to about 20 MA students, and I want to run a 90-minute activity that gets them to experience two things from the Bikhchandani-Hirshleifer-Welch model that students often find hard to believe: (1) a cascade can lock in on the wrong choice even when the majority of private information points the other way, and (2) cascades are fragile — a single public signal, even a noisy one, can shatter them. The standard urn game gets at (1) but I want to build (2) into the same session so they see both dynamics in one go.

Design the game: rules, materials, a point system (no real money), and a timeline for the 90 minutes. The game should have two phases — a cascade-formation phase and then a "disclosure" intervention where I reveal a public signal. The debrief should walk through: why going along with the crowd was rational even when it turned out wrong, what the public signal did to the cascade mathematically, and why this means cascades aren't robust to even small amounts of public information. Also tell me what will go wrong — especially, what if the first few signals happen to be correct and no "wrong" cascade forms? How do I handle that without ruining the point?

Start with the game design itself — no preamble, no commentary, just the activity.
