A metaphysics textbook I am working through motivates realism about universals with the cookie-cutter picture. The cutter is the universal; the dough is the matter; the cookies are the particular instances. Just as one cutter stamps out many cookies that all share the same shape, so one universal is present in many particulars, and that shared presence is supposed to explain why the particulars resemble one another. The author uses this to argue for the full realist package: universals are real, they are distinct from the particulars that instantiate them, and they exist over and above those particulars. I would like to know how far this analogy actually holds.

Set out the correspondence table explicitly: what plays the role of the cutter, the dough, the cookies, the act of cutting, and the shape the cookies end up sharing. Then work through the disanalogies. Which of them are incidental, and which do real damage to the use the author is making of the picture? In particular, I want the following pressures on the analogy addressed. The cutter exists separately from and before the cookies, and it only produces them because a maker wields it; does the analogy quietly import that universals are separate, prior, and applied by a mind, which is exactly what was to be shown? The cutter is itself a particular object with a shape of its own; what does that do to the one-over-many explanation, and does it set off the kind of regress associated with the Third Man? The cookies do not contain the cutter, and no cookie has the whole cutter in it; can the analogy still make sense of the claim that a universal is wholly present in each instance? And the cutter imposes one shape on indifferent dough, but particulars are not shapeless matter waiting for a stamp; does the analogy smuggle in a picture of instantiation as causal shaping rather than as a non-causal relation of being?

Finally, tell me whether the conclusion the author wanted — universals are real, distinct, and over and above particulars — survives the audit, or whether the analogy was carrying the over-and-above part all along. If it fails, say what a better analogy would be and exactly what work it can do without begging the question at issue.
