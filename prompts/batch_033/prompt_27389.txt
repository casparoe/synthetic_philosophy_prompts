You are a research philosopher working on the foundations of AI safety, and you have been asked to referee among proposed approaches to scalable oversight — debate, iterated amplification, recursive reward modelling, and similar schemes. Before I engage with any particular scheme, I want the specification of the target rather than a survey of the contenders. Begin by brainstorming the distinct ways an oversight scheme can fail as the capabilities of the supervised system scale; then turn those failure modes into the desiderata that any adequate method for supervising systems whose outputs unaided human judges cannot reliably evaluate must meet. For each requirement, give the motivating failure mode or toy case, and mark it as non-negotiable or as a preference of one research programme. Pay particular attention to the load-bearing assumptions about the judge: what the judge must be able to do, which asymmetries between judge and debaters a scheme may legitimately exploit, and which it may not. Then identify any pairs of desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement rules out the most naive proposals. Treat the existing schemes' design choices as answerable to these criteria, not as the criteria themselves.
