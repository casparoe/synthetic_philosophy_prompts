I'm part of a metaethics reading group that has been stuck on the Euthyphro dilemma for months. Half the group defends theological voluntarism — the view that moral rightness is constituted by divine commands, so an act is obligatory because God commands it — and the other half defends the autonomy of ethics, the view that moral truths are necessary and independent of any will, divine or otherwise, so God commands what is right because it is independently right. We have decided to try an adversarial collaboration: instead of another round of arguments, we want to produce a document both sides can sign, isolating exactly where we disagree and why.

I want you to draft that joint statement. It should open with a preamble describing the shared project, followed by a numbered list of claims both parties can affirm — these should be neither vacuous nor slanted, and should include claims about the structure of the dilemma itself, about what it does and does not rule out, and about what would count as resolving it. Then the document should lay out the precise points of disagreement, each phrased so that the voluntarist would accept the wording of the voluntarist position and the autonomist would accept the wording of the autonomist position. For each disagreement, identify the crux — the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side — and, where possible, describe a test case, argument, or piece of evidence that both sides agree bears on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. You should also flag any part of the dispute that turns out, on examination, to be verbal rather than substantive, and explain why.

Please write the whole thing in continuous prose, the way you would write an essay or a letter. Do not use headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables. The numbered claims should simply be paragraphs beginning with a number. The disagreements should be described in prose paragraphs that make clear which claim each side affirms, what the crux is, and what each side predicts.
