Write an essay on the paradox of tragedy: the puzzle of why we willingly seek out and apparently enjoy artworks that evoke sadness, grief, or horror, when we ordinarily go to considerable lengths to avoid such feelings in daily life. Survey the principal explanations on offer—catharsis, the conversion of painful emotions into aesthetic pleasure, the cognitive or ethical value of tragic insight, the second-order satisfaction we take in our own compassionate response, and the idea that our enjoyment is not of the suffering itself but of the artistry with which it is rendered. Engage with the objections each account faces: for instance, why catharsis seems to lack empirical support, why conversion theories struggle to explain cases where the distress is too intense to be 'transformed,' and why cognitive-value accounts risk making tragedy indistinguishable from a lecture. Do not end on a merely descriptive or non-committal note; argue for the view, or combination of views, that you take to be most defensible. Please write in British English.
