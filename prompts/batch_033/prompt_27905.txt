I am a high school student working on a research paper about algorithmic fairness, and before I read more proposals I want to know what standard to hold them to. Please write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of fairness for algorithmic decision systems has to deliver. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and say whether it is non-negotiable or just a preference of one camp. Cover at least these: the cases a theory must classify correctly (for example, explicitly using a protected category, using an apparently neutral feature that acts as a proxy for it, treating otherwise identical individuals differently, and harms that appear only at the group level); the distinctions it must not collapse (group versus individual fairness, process versus outcome, and bias in the training data versus bias in the decision rule); and the formal results it must not contradict, especially the impossibility results showing that combinations of otherwise attractive criteria such as calibration, equalized odds, and demographic parity cannot all be satisfied at once except under narrow conditions. Then identify which subsets of the requirements are jointly unsatisfiable, and which single requirement does the most to exclude the standard proposals. Finally, score the main families of proposals — statistical parity, equalized odds, calibration-based views, individual fairness, and causal approaches — against the list in a sentence or two each, but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivation rather than on the scorecard.
