Construct a thought experiment that works as the converse of the standard counterexamples to the simple conditional analysis of dispositions. The simple conditional analysis says that an object has a disposition to manifest M in response to stimulus S just in case, were it to undergo S, it would manifest M. The familiar counterexamples run in only one direction: finks and masks are cases in which the object has the disposition but the counterfactual is false, so the analysis denies a disposition that is present. I want the other direction, built out of ordinary materials rather than the science-fiction devices people usually reach for.

Build a case in which the counterfactual is true — the object would manifest M if it underwent S — but the object lacks the disposition. The case must be a mimic, not a reverse fink: the manifestation would occur because an external factor would bring it about, not because the stimulus would cause the object to acquire the disposition. Use a recognizable everyday disposition (fragility, solubility, toxicity, flammability, or something similarly pedestrian); no magic, no science fiction, no perfect predictors, and no stipulating the verdicts. Keep the scenario to a single paragraph. The object must lack the disposition before, during, and after the stimulus, and its intrinsic disposition-relevant nature must remain unchanged; the truth of the counterfactual must be owed entirely to the external factor.

After the scenario, do the following.

(1) State precisely the object, the disposition D, the stimulus S, and the manifestation M.

(2) Show that the counterfactual — were the object to undergo S, it would manifest M — is true, and give the causal route by which M would occur.

(3) Show that the object nonetheless lacks D, and state the criterion of disposition-possession you are using (for example, that the object lacks the relevant intrinsic causal basis, or that it would not manifest M if the external factor were removed).

(4) Verify the constraints one by one: why the case is a mimic rather than a reverse fink, and why it is neither a fink nor a mask.

(5) Identify the feature of the case that is doing the work, and say what happens to the counterfactual and to the verdict of the simple conditional analysis if that feature is removed.

(6) Say how a defender of the simple conditional analysis would try to rule out your case — for instance, by requiring that the manifestation issue from the object's own intrinsic nature — and assess whether that amendment works, including what residual problems it still faces from the standard fink and mask cases.
