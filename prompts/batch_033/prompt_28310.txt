I'm writing a dissertation chapter on the epistemology of deference to future selves, and I'd like a consistency audit on the set of views I've ended up holding. I've been living with these commitments long enough that I can no longer tell whether they fit together or whether I've been helping myself to a package that comes apart under pressure. Here they are:

(1) I accept Van Fraassen's Reflection principle. For any proposition A and times t₁ < t₂, my credence at t₁ in A, conditional on my credence at t₂ in A being r, should equal r. Informally: I should treat my future credences as expert forecasts and defer to them.

(2) I think that if I have reason to believe my future self will be cognitively compromised — drunk, severely sleep-deprived, in the grip of a powerful emotion — I should not defer to that future self's credences, and I should take practical steps (precommitment, binding) to prevent that future self from acting on them. Ulysses and the Sirens gets this right.

(3) I hold that epistemic akrasia is always irrational. There is no legitimate state in which I believe P while simultaneously believing that my total evidence does not support P. An agent who thinks "P, but my evidence says otherwise" has made a mistake somewhere.

(4) I accept conditionalization as the correct norm for updating: when I acquire new evidence E between t₁ and t₂, my credence at t₂ in any proposition A should equal my credence at t₁ in A conditional on E. Rational credences evolve by conditioning on the evidence received, and nothing else.

(5) I think that if I learn I will forget some evidence I currently possess — say I am reliably told that a drug will selectively erase my memory of a particular experimental result — I should not now adjust my credences toward what my future, less-informed credences will be. The fact that I will lose evidence does not make my current, better-informed credences any less justified, and I should not anticipate the forgetting by lowering my confidence now.

I need to know whether these five commitments form a consistent package. Please go through them systematically: identify every pair that is in genuine tension, rank those tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to be revised to resolve it. Also flag any pairs that look as though they are in tension but aren't, and explain why the apparent tension dissolves on closer inspection. Finally, tell me which single revision would do the most to restore overall consistency, and what that revision would cost — which cases or intuitions I would have to give up.
