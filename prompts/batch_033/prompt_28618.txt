ive been reading the panpsychism literature and the combination problem debate feels totally gridlocked to me. the panpsychist says look, if fundamental particles have some tiny bit of experience, then when theyre arranged in the right way (like in a brain) those micro-experiences combine into the macro-experience we know and love. the critic says how?? youre just asserting that combination happens, you havent explained it at all. then the panpsychist offers an analogy — maybe something about how physical properties combine in novel ways, or how holism works in quantum mechanics — and the critic says analogies arent explanations. then the panpsychist says the critic is holding them to an unfair standard because nobody can explain how non-conscious matter produces consciousness either. and were back where we started. this has been going on since at least the 90s and i cant tell if anyones actually moved.

so my question is: what kind of disagreement is this, structurally? is it that we dont agree on what an acceptable explanation would look like — like, the panpsychist thinks an analogy plus a promissory note counts as progress and the critic wants a mechanism? is there a hidden empirical bet in here somewhere, maybe about what future neuroscience will or wont be able to explain? or is it a clash of bedrock intuitions about whats intelligible — some people just find the idea of subjects combining into a bigger subject intuitively fine and others find it literally incoherent? and given your diagnosis, does this debate ever resolve, or is it one of those things where we just run out of arguments and go home?
