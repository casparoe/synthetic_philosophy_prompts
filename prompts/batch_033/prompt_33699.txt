I spent thirty years in ecology before I retired, so I'm used to systems thinking, but moral philosophy is new to me and I've been reading Strawson's essay on the reactive attitudes — the one where resentment, gratitude, indignation, and blame are part of how we treat each other as persons, and where he says that when someone is exempted we shift into what he calls the objective attitude (my wording, not his). Lately I can't stop thinking about the Hinckley trial as a test of all this: the man who shot Reagan, found not guilty by reason of insanity in 1982, the public's fury, and Congress tightening the insanity defense a couple years later.

Work it through for me as a case study in Strawson's framework, and please don't just present the options — I want you to take a definite position and argue for it.

Specifically: in the episode, who is doing the excusing or exempting and who is the target of the reactive attitudes — the jury, the public, the psychiatrists, the victims, the politicians who rewrote the law afterward? What does Strawson's distinction between excuses (he didn't mean it, he didn't know) and exemptions (he isn't the kind of being to whom resentment applies) do with a case like this? Was the verdict a genuine suspension of the reactive attitudes, an attempt to suspend them, or something messier — and does it matter that the suspension was reached through psychiatric testimony rather than through ordinary interpersonal recognition? Then the aftermath: does the public anger and the statutory reform confirm Strawson's line that our reactive attitudes are not something we can revise away by a theoretical decision, or does it reveal that his picture of exemptions is to clean for the way forensic psychiatry and politics actually draw the line?

Here's where I want the commitment: is the Hinckley episode evidence for Strawson's view that the reactive attitudes are inescapable and prior to theory, or evidence that his account of exemption is too tidy to handle a real insanity defense? Pick one side and argue for it, and tell me what the episode would have had to look like for the other side to be right.
