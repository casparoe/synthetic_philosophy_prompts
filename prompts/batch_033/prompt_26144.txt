I'm working through the literature on the is-ought gap and the naturalistic fallacy, and I keep finding that different camps talk past each other because they disagree on what would even count as bridging or respecting the gap. Before I go further, I want a specification of the job description: what must any adequate account of the relationship between descriptive and normative claims actually deliver?

Give me the desiderata — the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must respect, the results from neighboring fields it cannot contradict — with the argument or example that makes each one a requirement. Then flag which are non-negotiable constraints on any account and which are parochial commitments of particular traditions (Humean, Moorean, expressivist, naturalist, and so on).

I am especially interested in whether some subset of these desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied. If, for example, the features that make moral language cognitive enough to handle embedding in conditionals and negation also make it impossible to explain why fundamental moral disagreement does not resolve the way fundamental factual disagreement does, that is the kind of tension I want surfaced — the desiderata list itself would then be a result, not just a prelude.

A brief scorecard of how the main families fare against the list is welcome, but the real work should be in motivating and defending each criterion. If you think some commonly demanded feature — that an account must vindicate the autonomy of ethics, say, or that it must be compatible with metaphysical naturalism — is not a genuine desideratum but a question-begging move by one side, say so and justify that judgment.
