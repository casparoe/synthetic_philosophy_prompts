I work on content moderation at a mid-size social platform and we're trying to pin down our slur policy. Right now we do something like: if the slur is directed at a specific person, it's taken down. If it's in a quoted context (like someone reporting what was said to them), we leave it up. If it's clearly reclaimed (we have some signals for this), we leave it up. And if it's in what we call "academic" or "meta" discussion (someone talking about the word itself), we also leave it up.

The problem is this is really hard to operationalize — we're basically guessing at speaker intent and context from text alone, and we're wrong a lot.

I'm trying to figure out what philosophical theory of slurs this policy actually commits us too. Like, does it assume that the harm of a slur lives in the speaker's intent, or in the word itself, or in the effect on the audience, or in something social? And if we're allowing reclaimed uses, what does that presuppose about who can legitimately reclaim? Does the policy hang together or are we smuggling in incompatible assumptions?

I'm not trying to get the right answer here, I just want to know what view we're implicitly signing up for so I can at least make the implementation consistent with it.
