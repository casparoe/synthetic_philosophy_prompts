I've been trying to get my head around Hacking's looping effects for ages, and I want a case built to order rather than another rehash of the multiple-personality example. Here's the spec.

Build me a realistic scenario where a psychiatric classification loops in the direction people usually don't reach for: the category gets narrower and more uniform over time, and it ends up more predictively valid than it was, not less. The loop has to run through the classified people's own self-understanding and behaviour—not through clinicians quietly rewriting the criteria, and not through resource incentives like disability payments, school accommodations, or access to services. Nobody should be faking, malingering, or strategically performing the label for an external payoff. One paragraph, ordinary setting (a school, a clinic, an online community), nothing exotic. You can invent a diagnosis or lightly disguise a real one; I'm not asking for references. The point of the exercise is to get a case where the looping story has to do real explanatory work in exactly the situation where a biomedical story is most tempting.

Then run two accounts on it. First, the straightforward biomedical/essentialist account: the category tracks a stable underlying disorder, and any apparent tightening or increase in validity is just better ascertainment or diagnostic refinement, not feedback from the label. Second, Hacking's interactive-kind account: the classified people respond to being classified, that response changes what the kind is like, and the change feeds back into the classification. I want the scenario to be one where the looping account gets the verdict right and the biomedical account gets it wrong, but where the biomedical account's reading is still tempting enough that a sensible person could hold it. Dont let the case cheat by making the biomedical account obviously wrong from the start.

After the scenario, walk me through the loop step by step: the initial label, the uptake, the behaviour change, and the feedback into the next round of classification. Then tell me which single feature of the case is doing the real work, what happens if you remove it, and how a defender of the biomedical account would steelman their side and attack the case—and whether their attack actually lands.
