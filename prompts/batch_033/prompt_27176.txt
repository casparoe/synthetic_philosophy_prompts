I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece that tries to connect a pattern I keep tripping over in different domains. I'd love it if you could put on your philosopher hat — philosophy of action, practical rationality, something in that neighborhood — and help me figure out if this thing has a name.

Here's the pattern. An agent (person, institution, government) knows their future self is going to face some temptation or pressure that'll make them act against their own current goals. So they set something up *now* — a rule, a barrier, a structure — that makes it genuinely costly or difficult for the future self to give in. Not just a resolution or a promise, but an actual external constraint that binds later behavior. I've seen this in a bunch of places:

- Constitutional design: you write a constitution whose amendment process is deliberately slow and cumbersome (supermajorities, multiple rounds of voting) because you know that future legislatures, in a moment of panic, will want to shred civil liberties.
- Addiction medicine: disulfiram makes you violently sick if you drink. You take it in the morning knowing that your 11pm self at the party is not going to share your priorities.
- Software: I talked to a dev who uses a site-blocker that, once you set a timer, has literally no override — delete the app, restart the phone, doesn't matter, you're locked out of Twitter till it expires.
- And then there's the old story of Odysseus having his crew tie him to the mast so he can hear the sirens without steering the ship into the rocks, while the crew plug their ears and are under orders to ignore whatever he says.

What I'm trying to figure out is whether all of these are the same kind of thing, or whether I'm lumping together different phenomena that just look similar. My instinct is that the Odysseus case is importantly different from the constitutional framers case: in one, your future self will *genuinely prefer* to be let loose (their preferences have temporarily changed), while in the other, the future legislators know exactly what they want — they just disagree with the earlier generation about values. But I don't have the conceptual vocabulary to draw that distiction cleanly, or to know whether that's even the distinction that matters.

So: is there an established concept (or family of concepts) that covers this territory? If yes, I want to know how they relate to each other, what the boundaries are, and whether there's a good umbrella term. If no single concept does the job, I'd be happy with a coinage and a crisp definition. What I'm really after is the carving — what's essential, what's incidental, and where the edges get fuzzy. A bare label won't help me.

Take your time, think it through step by step. Maybe start by brainstorming the candidate concepts you'd reach for, then work out which fits best and why the others don't quite land.
