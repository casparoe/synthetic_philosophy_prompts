I host a small philosophy podcast, and next week's guest is a philosopher known for defending mereological nihilism, the answer to the special composition question that says composition never occurs: strictly speaking, there are no tables, chairs, or planets, only simples arranged in certain ways. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having the guest restate the view. Include at least one question on each of the following, each with a follow-up keyed to the reply the guest is most likely to give: (1) the strategy of paraphrasing ordinary talk about composites into talk about simples, and whether that strategy vindicates ordinary assertions or quietly replaces them with different claims; (2) how the view handles thinkers and persons, and whether denying composite persons forces the nihilist to deny that anything thinks; (3) whether the dispute with universalist and restricted-composition answers is substantive or merely verbal, and what could settle that; (4) the consequences for ethical and prudential thought, and whether the view collapses into a purely verbal revision with no practical import. End with a question about what would have to be true, or what evidence could come in, for the guest to abandon the view.
