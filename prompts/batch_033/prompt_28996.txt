A philosopher of biology has been developing an account of when two instances of a biological process count as the same mechanism. Her published verdicts on a series of cases are as follows:

1. The same protein kinase cascade controls cell-cycle entry in both Xenopus and zebrafish embryos, with identical components and causal organisation. Verdict: same mechanism.

2. In a related teleost species, a gene duplication means two paralogous kinases jointly perform the role that a single kinase performs in zebrafish. The paralogues are structurally very similar to the original kinase, and the overall organisation of the cascade is unchanged. Verdict: same mechanism.

3. In Drosophila, an evolutionarily unrelated kinase—structurally dissimilar but functionally equivalent—fills the same role in an otherwise identical cascade. Verdict: different mechanism.

4. Under heat shock in Xenopus, the same kinase cascade components are recruited into a different pattern of interactions that produces cell-cycle arrest rather than entry, via a rewired causal pathway. Verdict: different mechanism.

5. Two isolates of the same bacterial species implement a chemotaxis signalling pathway using the same set of proteins with the same causal topology, but the isolates differ in which of two redundant phosphatases they express more heavily. The overall organisation and outcome are unchanged. Verdict: same mechanism.

She is now writing about a newly characterised case: in a basal chordate, the same kinase cascade controls cell-cycle entry with identical organisation to the Xenopus and zebrafish cases, but one of the kinases—clearly homologous to the zebrafish kinase—has diverged so substantially in its binding domains and tertiary structure that it is now classified in a different protein subfamily. She has not yet published her verdict.

What should we expect her to say, and why? Identify at least two rules that are consistent with her first five verdicts, say whether they agree or diverge on the new case, and, if they diverge, state which rule her likely verdict would commit her to. If you think her existing verdicts genuinely underdetermine her response to the new case, say what further case would settle the matter.
