I've been trying to get clear on what exactly the Löbian obstacle implies for AI alignment, and I've ended up with a set of views that I want to check for consistency before I write them up. Here they are:

(1) The Löbian obstacle is a real constraint: an agent that reasons in a system at least as strong as PA cannot prove the soundness of its own future reasoning, and this means it cannot fully trust any successor it builds that uses the same reasoning system.

(2) Switching to a probabilistic framework — logical induction, reflective oracles, that cluster of ideas — dissolves the obstacle rather than merely relocating it. An agent that assigns probabilities to logical statements instead of proving them doesn't hit the same diagonalization.

(3) The obstacle is not just about self-modification. It also means that a static agent cannot, even in principle, construct a complete justification for trusting its own current conclusions — the regress bottoms out in something unproven.

(4) Despite (3), an agent should act as if it trusts its own reasoning. The alternative — treating every inference as suspect until independently verified — is computationally and practically worse by the agent's own lights.

(5) Humans are not a counterexample to the claim that the obstacle matters. We don't actually trust our own reasoning in the Löbian sense; we rely on social calibration, empirical feedback, and heuristics that an isolated AI might lack.

I want to know: which of these are in genuine tension, which only look like they are, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity and for each say what would have to give and what the revision costs.
