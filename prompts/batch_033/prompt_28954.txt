Please give me your credence that there exist free-floating rationales — normative reasons that genuinely count in favor of some belief, action, or attitude without being reasons for any actual or possible agent. Decompose the question into at least the following sub-questions: (a) whether reasons are essentially relational, such that a reason must always be a reason for some particular reasoner; (b) whether there are mind-independent normative truths that could serve as the grounding basis for unpossessed reasons; (c) whether the best account of epistemic normativity — reasons for belief — permits reasons that no one has ever entertained, or whether the very idea of an unentertained epistemic reason collapses into mere propositional availability. Assign a rough probability to each sub-question, explain how they combine to yield your overall credence, and identify which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to. Then tell me what kind of argument or thought experiment would shift that sub-question's probability the most.
