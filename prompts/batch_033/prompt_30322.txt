I've been following the literature on truth for a while now, and what strikes me most is how little the dialectic between deflationists and substantivalists actually moves. On one side, deflationists hold that truth is not a substantial property: to call a proposition true is just to assert it, or to endorse it, or to use it inside a generalization, and the entire content of the concept is captured by instances of the equivalence schema — the proposition that p is true if and only if p. On the other side, substantivalists, whether correspondence theorists, identity theorists, or others, insist that truth is a genuine property or relation doing real explanatory work: it is what inquiry aims at, what assertion is normatively governed by, and what ties language to the world. The objections on both sides have become extremely familiar. The deflationist is accused of being unable to state her own view without invoking the very notion she is trying to deflate, of having nothing illuminating to say about why truth functions as a norm of assertion and belief, and of handling the liar paradox only by ad hoc maneuvers. The substantivalist is accused of positing a property that never does any independent explanatory work and of mistaking a logical device for a deep metaphysical relation.

My working hypothesis is that the two camps are not answering the same question: one is giving an account of the function of the word true in our language, while the other is giving an account of a property or relation in the world, and the appearance of a single dispute is kept alive by the fact that both use one word. But I am not confident that is right — the impasse could equally be a substantive disagreement about whether truth has an explanatory role, or a disagreement about what a theory of truth is supposed to be a theory of in the first place. I want a committed diagnosis rather than a survey of options: is this impasse verbal, substantive, or the product of two different questions sharing a name? Ground the diagnosis in what each side actually says, and then tell me what, given that diagnosis, would count as genuine progress — a distinction, a formal result, a new kind of case, a change of question — or, if you think no progress should be expected, say why. Be explicit about whether your diagnosis leaves you taking a side, and do not retreat into saying the debate is merely complicated.
