I'm teaching a grad seminar this spring on manipulation and legitimate persuasion, and the thing that keeps happening is that two people in the room talk straight past each other. One of them is convinced manipulation is essentially a matter of hiddenness: the manipulator conceals the fact that they're trying to influence you, or at least conceals how they're doing it, so the target can't see the influence attempt for what it is. The other thinks manipulation is essentially about bypassing or short-circuiting the target's rational capacities: it doesn't matter whether its hidden, what matters is that the influence works through pressure, emotion, fatigue, framing, or whatever rather than through reasons the target could endorse. They've been at it for weeks, and I'd like them to actually produce something constructive instead of re-running the same examples.

Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the hidden-influence camp and the rational-bypass camp. I want a numbered list of claims both sides can genuinely sign — about paradigm cases (Iago, subliminal advertising, a guilt-tripping parent, a transparent offer of a large reward), about the difference between manipulation and coercion, and about what a case has to look like before it counts as a problem case for the theory. Then a disagreements table: for each point of disagreement, give the precise formulation, phrased so each side would accept the wording of its own view. Then the cruxes — the sub-questions that, if settled, would move at least one side — and for each crux, at least one concrete case or test both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. If some of their dispute turns out to be merely verbal, say which part and why. No reading lists or citations; I want the substance. And if the whole framing is confused or the task can't be done well, say so plainly instead of handing me a document nobody could actually sign.
