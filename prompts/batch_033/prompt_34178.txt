I'm working through Maxwell's *Theory of Heat* (1871) and I'm stuck on a detail in the demon passage that I think matters more than people let on. Here's the passage:

"But if we conceive of a being whose faculties are so sharpened that he can follow every molecule in its course, such a being, whose attributes are as essentially finite as our own, would be able to do what is impossible to us. For we have seen that molecules in a vessel full of air at uniform temperature are moving with velocities by no means uniform, though the mean velocity of any great number of them, arbitrarily selected, is almost exactly uniform. Now let us suppose that such a vessel is divided into two portions, A and B, by a division in which there is a small hole, and that a being, who can see the individual molecules, opens and closes this hole, so as to allow only the swifter molecules to pass from A to B, and only the slower molecules to pass from B to A. He will thus, without expenditure of work, raise the temperature of B and lower that of A, in contradiction to the second law of thermodynamics."

What's bothering me is the phrase "whose attributes are as essentially finite as our own." Maxwell seems to be going out of his way to insist this thing isn't supernatural — it's finite, like us. But the whole later resolution of the paradox (Szilard, Landauer, Bennett) turned on showing that the demon does pay a cost: the information-processing steps have an irreducible thermodynamic price. So here's my question: is Maxwell's insistence on the demon's finiteness a clue that he already half-saw the information-theoretic resolution, or is it doing completely different work in the argument? I want a committed reading — take a stand. Walk me through what "essentially finite" is actually doing in this passage, whether it points toward the later exorcism or is just a rhetorical move to stop people from dismissing the thought experiment as magic, and what the text itself supports. If you think it's ambiguous, tell me which reading is stronger and why.
