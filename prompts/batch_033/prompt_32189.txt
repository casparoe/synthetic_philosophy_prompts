My father died six months ago, and I've been reading philosophy of death to make sense of it — not as a distraction, but because the abstract questions suddenly felt concrete. I'm now at the point where I've settled into a set of views, and before I start writing them up (I'm a PhD student in a neighboring field and I've been encouraged to turn this into a paper), I want a cold-eyed consistency check. Here's where I've landed:

(a) The deprivation account is correct: death is bad for the one who dies because it deprives them of the goods they would have had — the experiences, projects, and relationships that a longer life would have contained.

(b) The Lucretian symmetry argument fails, and it fails for a reason more specific than the usual appeal to the direction of time. The asymmetry is grounded in the fact that I could have died later than I actually will, but I could not have been born substantially earlier than I was — my origins are necessary to my identity. So prenatal nonexistence was not a deprivation of goods I could have had, whereas posthumous nonexistence is.

(c) For a state of affairs to constitute a harm to someone, it must be bad for that person at some time, and that time must be one at which the person exists. Harms are relations, and both relata need to exist for the relation to obtain.

(d) Posthumous events can harm a person. If I spend my life building an institution and it is dismantled the week after my death in a way that frustrates the purpose I gave my life to, that is a harm to me — not just to the people who survive me.

(e) The rationality of fearing death does not depend on being able to locate the harm at a particular moment. It is sufficient that death deprives me of a life I would have valued; the fear is proportionate to the magnitude of the deprivation, not to my ability to solve the timing puzzle.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what the least costly revision would be. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
