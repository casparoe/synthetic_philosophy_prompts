My physics study group keeps invoking Wigner's line that mathematics is unreasonably effective in the natural sciences, usually as if it settles the argument. The guy who leans on it most is using it against my claim that math is a human invention: he says group theory was developed for purely internal mathematical reasons and then showed up in particle physics, and non-Euclidean geometry showed up in general relativity, so mathematics can't just be something we made up. I don't see how one slogan is doing that much work. What are the different precise claims that line could actually be making — from the flat-out banal to the strong metaphysical ones — and which of those readings, if any, would actually support his anti-invention conclusion? Also mark which readings are basically uncontroversial and which are genuinely contested among people who work on this; I don't want a contested view dressed up as the consensus.
