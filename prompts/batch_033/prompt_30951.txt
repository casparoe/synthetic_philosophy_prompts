Write a dialogue between two people arguing about the Judy Benjamin problem. Here's the setup in case it's useful: Judy is a soldier dropped at night into a region divided into Red and Blue territory, and each territory is split into a Headquarters area and a Second Company area. She starts out with equal probability on all four sub-regions. Then a radio message tells her that, conditional on being in Red territory, the odds are 3 to 1 that she's in Second Company rather than Headquarters; the message says nothing about Blue.

One character defends the standard cross-entropy-minimization answer: she should update by minimizing relative entropy subject to the new conditional, which makes her probability of being in Red drop from 1/2 to about 0.47, even though the message was only about a conditional. This character thinks that shift is fine — the message does bear on Red after all, and a globally least-information update is allowed to move the antecedent's probability.

The other character defends the view that a purely conditional message shouldn't move the probability of its antecedent at all: P(Red) should stay 1/2, and she should only reallocate inside Red so that P(Second | Red) = 3/4. This character thinks the cross-entropy result is an artifact of the distance measure, not something the message licenses.

Make both sides equally sharp and have them actually respond to each others points rather than talking past each other. Neither side should be a strawman.
