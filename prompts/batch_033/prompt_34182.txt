I'm a retired field biologist — spent 30 years studying primate social behavior — and I've gotten into philosophy in retirement. Now I sit on my university's IACUC, the animal research ethics committee. I know, the biologist on the ethics committee, how original. But I've been reading Nozick lately and its really scrambled how I think about the standard cost-benefit approach we use on these protocols.

We've got a protocol right now that's a perfect test case. A neuroscience lab wants to run an experiment on macaques involving a pretty painful procedure — not lethal, but chronic pain for several weeks, then euthanasia — to study a neural mechanism that might eventually help treat a rare form of chronic pain in humans. The science is solid, vet care is fine, numbers are minimized. By our usual utilitarian calculus (reduce suffering, maximize benefit, the three Rs, etc.) it probably scrapes through, though barely.

But reading Nozick has me stuck on something deeper. Even if animals aren't rights-bearers in the full Kantian sense, are there still side-constraints on what we can do to them? Is there a line where we can't treat a macaque as a pure means, even if doing so would produce net benefits for humans? Nozick talks about the tension between "utilitarianism for animals, Kantianism for people," and I think this protocol is sitting right on that fault line.

The comittee is split three ways. Two want to approve (benefits are real, welfare standards are met). Two want to reject (pain level too high for the probability of translatable results). And two of us, including me, are stuck on this deeper Nozickian question and can't figure out how to operationalize it into a vote. The chair wants me to draft a decision memo — a written opinion recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions — that engages seriously with the Nozickian worry rather than just the standard regulatory framework. It needs a concrete bottom line the committee can vote on, and it should treat the strongest consideration on the losing side honestly rather than just dismissing it.
