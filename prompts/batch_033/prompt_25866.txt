So my city did this thing last year where they picked 40 random people — like jury duty but for policy — and had them spend several weekends learning about climate change from scientists and economists, deliberating in small groups with facilitators, and then writing up policy recommendations for city council. They called it a "citizens' assembly." And I was honestly impressed by the recommendations — they were nuanced in ways I wouldn't expect from a regular ballot initiative.

But here's what's been bugging me. The same city also runs regular elections where millions of us vote on candidates and ballot measures with none of that infrastructure. No expert briefings, no facilitated deliberation, no stipend to compensate us for the time it takes to actually understand a complex issue. We just show up and mark bubbles based on whatever we happen to know.

So the city is basically running two completely different systems for figuring out what the public wants or thinks. One system treats political judgment like something you have to construct through a careful process — you bring people together, give them information, let them talk it through, help them revise their views. The other system treats political judgment like its already there inside each person, fully formed, and you just need to count it up.

What I can't figure out is whether these two systems even make sense together. If you really believed that ordinary people, given time and access to expertise, can produce thoughtful policy recommendations — which the assembly model assumes — then why wouldn't you want the whole system to work that way? And if you don't believe that, if you think the assembly model is just a gimmick, then what's the point of doing it at all? It's like the city has one theory of knowledge for the special occasion and a totally different theory for everyday governance, and nobody seems bothered by the contradiction.

I guess what I'm asking is: what theory of democracy and what theory of knowledge does this two-track setup actually presuppose? Are the presuppositions of the assembly track and the election track compatible with each other, or is there a genuine tension? And if you took the assembly model's assumptions seriously — that political knowledge emerges from structured collective inquiry — what would the rest of the system have to look like?
