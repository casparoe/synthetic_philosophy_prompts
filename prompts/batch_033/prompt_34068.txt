I've been revisiting Mill's On Liberty in a reading group, and we keep circling around a tension that I haven't seen addressed head-on in the secondary literature I've encountered. Mill's harm principle seems to draw a bright line: the only justification for restricting someone's liberty is the prevention of harm to others. But the paradox of tolerance, as formulated by Karl Popper roughly a century later, suggests that a society of unlimited tolerance will eventually be destroyed by the intolerant — that preserving a tolerant society may require refusing to tolerate those who would use the platform of tolerance to dismantle it.

I'm trying to work out whether Mill's framework has the resources to handle this, or whether the paradox of tolerance exposes a genuine limitation in the harm principle. On one reading, Mill's position seems to force the conclusion that intolerant speech and organizing must be permitted right up until the point where they cause concrete harm — by which time it might be too late. But Mill also carves out exceptions: he doesn't think the harm principle prohibits all forms of social pressure, he acknowledges that some forms of expression (like incitement to violence before an angry mob) cross the threshold, and he is willing to restrict the liberty of those he considers incapable of rational self-governance. The question is whether these qualifications are enough to contain the threat Popper identifies, or whether Mill would have to revise his framework if he had to confront the paradox directly.

If you had to reconstruct Mill's likely response to the paradox of tolerance — working from his own principles and the full range of his arguments in On Liberty and elsewhere — what would that response look like? Would he bite the bullet and accept that a tolerant society must tolerate its own gravediggers, or would he find a way to exclude them without abandoning the harm principle? And if the latter, how exactly would the argument go, and would it require stretching the notion of "harm" in ways that Mill himself would have resisted?
