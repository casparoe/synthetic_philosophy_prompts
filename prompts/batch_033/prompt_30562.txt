Here is an argument against the self-indication assumption that I cannot find the flaw in.

(1) One piece of evidence I have is that I exist as an observer at all.
(2) The self-indication assumption (SIA) says that, other things equal, a hypothesis that predicts N observers is favored by a factor of N, conditional on my existence, over a hypothesis that predicts only one observer.
(3) Consider two cosmological theories with equal prior probability, which make identical predictions about all the ordinary, non-indexical data but differ only in how many observers they predict: 10^10 versus 10^20.
(4) By (2), my existence favors the second theory over the first by a factor of 10^10.
(5) So, before I have looked through a telescope, I should already be overwhelmingly confident that the universe contains at least 10^20 observers.
(6) But that is absurd: I cannot settle a substantive cosmological question from the mere fact of my own existence.
(7) Therefore SIA is false.

I would most like to keep SIA, because it seems to me the cleanest way to block the doomsday argument. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
