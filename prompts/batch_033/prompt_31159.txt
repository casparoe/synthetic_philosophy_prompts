Suppose you are advising the theory lead of a small alignment research group that can devote roughly two person-years to conceptual work on iterated amplification and distillation (IDA): the family of proposals in which a model is trained by decomposing difficult tasks into subtasks that an amplified human overseer can evaluate, and then distilling the resulting decisions into a model that performs the whole task directly. The group's central worry is that such schemes assume, rather than establish, that alignment and competence survive recursive decomposition and distillation — that individually adequate subtask judgments compose into an adequate judgment of the whole, and that distillation does not simply make the overseer's errors more consistent and more scalable.

Produce a prioritized agenda of the open conceptual questions in this area, ordered by how important they are to settle before anyone should trust that a decomposition-plus-distillation pipeline preserves alignment and competence. For each question, explain why it is upstream of the others (which current lines of work depend on an unargued answer to it), how tractable it is for conceptual researchers rather than empirical ML work, and what result, counterexample, or observation over the next two years would move it up or down your ranking. I want a defended ordering, not a survey: make explicit the criteria you are using (for example, upstreamness, tractability, neglectedness, and how much downstream work the answer would change) and the trade-offs among them.

In particular, address where the alignment or competence is most plausibly lost: in the choice of subtask decomposition, in the aggregation of subtask evaluations, in the overseer's ability to notice that a decomposition has changed the problem, or in the distillation step itself. If you regard any of these as a red herring, say so and rank it accordingly.
