I have been thinking about something that comes up every time my friends and I use large language models, and I am hoping someone here can tell me whether it has a name in philosophy, or whether it is just a loose way of speaking that nobody has bothered to categorise properly.

The situation is this: when I interact with ChatGPT or a similar system, I find myself saying things like "it thinks I meant X" or "it's trying to steer the conversation toward Y" or "it believes it already answered that." I also catch myself getting annoyed at it, as if it had intentions. And yet I know, at least in the abstract, that the system is not thinking or believing or trying anything — it is generating tokens from a statistical model. What puzzles me is that this way of talking does not feel like a metaphor in the way that saying "my phone is angry at me" feels like a metaphor. When I say my phone is angry, I know I am being playful; when I say the model thinks I meant something else, it feels like I am genuinely describing the most efficient way to predict what it will do next. And this seems to extend beyond AI. I have noticed that people describe thermostats as "wanting" to keep the room at a certain temperature, or describe a chess-playing program as "hoping" to trap the opponent's queen, or describe evolution as "designing" an eye — and in each case the vocabulary seems to be doing real explanatory work, not just adding colour.

I have two questions, but they are really versions of the same question. First, is there an established term for this practice of attributing beliefs, desires, and intentions to systems that almost certainly do not have them, where the attribution is nevertheless useful for prediction? Second, if there is such a term, what is its precise boundary? I want to know how this practice differs from anthropomorphism (which feels like a mistake, projecting human traits onto non-human things), from taking a metaphor literally, and from genuinely inferring that the system has a mind. If there is no established term — or if the existing terms only partly fit — I would want to know what the nearest candidates are, why each one misses something, and what a better name and definition would look like. A bare label would not satisfy me; I am after the carving of the concept, not the word.
