So I've been put in charge of redesigning our medical residency training and my boss keeps saying we need graduates who have real clinical know-how, not just textbook knowledge. This got me reading about the knowing-how vs knowing-that distinction, and I started keeping track of how my friend (she did her PhD on exactly this, philosophy postdoc now) rules on cases when we talk over drinks. Here's what I have, her actual words as best I remember:

- Bike rider who can't explain the physics of balance: "That's knowing-how, obviously."
- Someone who memorized a cycling physics textbook but can't ride: "No, that's pure knowing-that, zero knowing-how."
- Chess grandmaster who can explain every move: "Has both, they're different things but not incompatible."
- Amnesic pianist who plays pieces from before the amnesia but doesn't remember learning them: "Still knowing-how, the ability survived the memory loss."
- Person who aces a multiple-choice test on swimming technique but has never been in water: "Knowing-that only, don't kid yourself."
- Surgeon who does a flawless appendectomy but can't name half the structures: "Knowing-how, and a good thing too or we'd all be dead."
- Sommelier who IDs wines reliably but says she just knows, can't say how: "Knowing-how, no question."

Here's the case I actually need to decide about, because it's basically what our new training produces: a resident who can diagnose a particular condition correctly and reliably, but only by consciously walking step by step through a memorized decision tree. If you interrupt her mid-reasoning she has to start over, she has zero intuitive feel for it, and if you ask her how she knows she just recites the tree. She's slow but her accuracy is genuinely good.

What's my friend going to say about her? And more importantly — what rule is she implicitly running such that all seven verdicts fit? I can see at least two different principles that cover her pattern, and I think they diverge on my resident. Name them, say which predicts what, and give me your best guess with why. If there's a further case I could casually bring up next time we get drinks that would settle which rule she's actually running, tell me what it is.

Keep it under 400 words, I actually need to act on this.
