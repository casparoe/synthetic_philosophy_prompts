I'm a game theorist — I work on mechanism design, credible signaling, and equilibrium selection. I keep running into philosophers who argue about why promises bind, and I have a suspicion that everything they're saying can be restated in game-theoretic terms without much loss. But I want to check that suspicion honestly.

Restate the main positions in the promissory obligation debate — the expectation-based view (promising creates an obligation because you've raised someone's expectations and they'd be harmed if you breach), the practice or convention view (promising is a useful social practice and the obligation comes from the rules of the practice, like the rules of a game), and the view that promissory obligation is basic or sui generis and can't be reduced to anything else — in the vocabulary I actually use: commitment devices, cheap talk, correlated equilibrium, reputation, incomplete contracts, and so on. Be as faithful as you can.

Then the residue report: what translated cleanly, what you had to fudge, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. The thing I'm most curious about is whether the distinction between a *moral* reason to keep a promise and a *prudential* reason (reputation, coordination, avoiding sanctions) can even be stated in my terms. If it can't, is that a sign that my vocabulary is missing something real, or a sign that the philosophers' distinction is less clear than it looks?
