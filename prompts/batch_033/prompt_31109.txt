Governments and agencies doing cost-benefit analysis on climate, health, and infrastructure policy discount future benefits at something like 3% a year, so a life saved in 2100 count for much less than a life saved today. Part of that discount rate is standardly decomposed into a pure rate of time preference plus a term reflecting the fact that future people will be richer, and sometimes a risk or opportunity-cost term. What theory of value does this practice presuppose? In particular, does it commit the agency to the view that later well-being matters less simply because it occurs later, or is it fully compatible with the view that all lives count equally? Reconstruct the implicit axiology, say which parts of the practice strictly require pure time preference and which merely fit well with it, and tell me what would actually have to change in the practice if the pure rate of time preference were set to zero while the other terms were kept.
