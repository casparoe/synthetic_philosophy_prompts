I help look after a small nature reserve owned by a land trust, and the board is stuck on what to do with a four-acre patch of old pasture that has grown into a nearly impenetrable thicket of multiflora rose and autumn olive. Some of us want to clear it back to open meadow; others want to leave the thicket alone because it is dense with nesting birds, rabbits, and small rodents. I have no academic background in ecology or philosophy, but I have been reading on my own about whether wild animals' lives contain more suffering than enjoyment, and I have realized I no longer know whether clearing the patch would make things better or worse for the animals themselves.

I would like your actual credence — a number between 0 and 1 — that clearing the patch (cutting the shrubs, treating the stumps, and mowing once a year to keep it open) would reduce the total suffering of the wild animals living on those four acres over the next ten years, compared with leaving the thicket as it is. Please do not stop at saying it depends on one's view of wild animal welfare; I want a number and the reasoning that produces it.

Decompose the credence into the sub-questions you judge most important. Assign each a rough probability, with a sentence or two of justification, and show how the parts combine into your final number. I would expect the decomposition to say something about at least these questions: whether the thicket's animals live lives that are on balance more painful than pleasant; whether clearing mainly prevents future animals from existing or mainly displaces animals that go on living elsewhere; how much acute suffering the clearing itself causes through nest destruction, starvation, exposure, and predation; and whether the replacement meadow supports enough animals whose lives are on balance better than the thicket lives it eliminates. If you think total suffering needs a more precise definition before the question has a truth value, state the definition you are adopting and answer under it. Then identify which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, and say what observation, natural experiment, or argument would move that number most.
