In a recent seminar, we discussed Nozick's utility monster, and I came away convinced that it's a decisive refutation of utilitarianism. The reasoning seems airtight: utilitarianism says we should maximize total utility. A utility monster gets vastly more utility from each unit of resources than ordinary humans do. Therefore, utilitarianism demands we divert all resources to the monster, leaving everyone else with nothing. Since no plausible moral theory can demand that, utilitarianism is false. Yet I keep encountering philosophers who treat utilitarianism as a live option. What am I misunderstanding about the force of this argument?
