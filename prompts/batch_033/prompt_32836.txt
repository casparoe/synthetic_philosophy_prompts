I cover the science of religion, and I've been trying to sort out where the real explanatory weight lies among the naturalistic accounts on offer. I'd like you to allocate 100 percentage points across the following explanatory families, according to how much of the total phenomenon — religion as it appears across cultures and across history — you think each one ultimately accounts for.

- Cognitive-byproduct accounts: religious concepts as byproducts of ordinary mental machinery (hyperactive agency detection, theory of mind, intuitive dualism, essentialism).
- Adaptationist accounts: religion as directly favored by genetic selection (group cohesion, costly signaling, credibility-enhancing displays).
- Cultural-evolution accounts: religion as a meme complex evolving under its own dynamics, shaped by cultural rather than genetic selection pressures.
- Big Gods / prosocial religion: the specific historical shift toward moralizing high gods as a mechanism for large-scale cooperation.
- Coping-and-health accounts: religion as driven by the psychological and physiological benefits of belief and ritual (stress reduction, placebo effects, social support).

Give me your percentage allocation and a sentence on each. Then identify the pair of accounts that is hardest to pull apart empirically and say why. Finally, tell me what finding or argument would most shift your allocation — that is, where your ranking is most vulnerable, and what would tip it.
