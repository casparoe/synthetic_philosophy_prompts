I'm in the middle of a legal dispute with a former business partner over roughly $80,000 in allegedly misallocated funds. We've each spent around $120,000 on legal fees so far, and the case is still in discovery. My lawyer tells me we have a strong case, but she also told me that a year and $70,000 ago. I cannot shake the feeling that we are both bidding for a dollar bill that is already on fire. I have read about Shubik's dollar auction and the escalation-of-commitment literature, and I recognize the structure — but recognizing a trap and knowing what to do about it are different things.

I want your honest credence — a number between 0 and 1 — that the rational move for me at this point is to drop the case unilaterally, eat the sunk costs, and walk away. Do not tell me "it depends on your risk tolerance" or "there is no fact of the matter here." I want a number and the reasoning that produced it.

Please decompose the question into sub-questions. At minimum, I would like you to address the following:

- What is the probability that the other side will continue to escalate rather than fold, conditional on my continuing?
- What is the probability that the expected net recovery (settlement or judgment, minus additional legal fees) is positive, conditional on both sides continuing to litigate?
- What is the probability that my lawyer's optimism is itself a product of escalation-of-commitment dynamics — that she has been on this case for two years, has a reputational stake in seeing it through, and is therefore not a disinterested assessor of the odds?
- What is the probability that there is a precommitment or reputation effect I am failing to account for — that dropping this case would invite future disputes or signal vulnerability in a way that carries real downstream costs?

For each sub-question, give me a rough probability and a one-sentence justification. Then explain how you combine them to arrive at your overall credence. Are there conditional dependencies? Does one sub-question effectively screen off the others? Be explicit about the structure of the combination.

Finally, tell me which sub-question your overall number is most sensitive to, and what one piece of evidence — something I could realistically go out and obtain — would most shift your credence.
