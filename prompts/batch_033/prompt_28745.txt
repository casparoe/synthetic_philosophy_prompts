I'm in a philosophy of mind class and we just read the extended mind stuff — Clark and Chalmers, Otto and his notebook, the parity principle, all that. I get the rough idea: if an external thing does the same job as an internal cognitive process, we should count it as cognitive. But when I apply it to myself, the view starts to sound crazy and I'm not sure if that's my confusion or the view's. My phone is where my calendar, contacts, and a huge chunk of my memories live. By the parity logic it seems like my phone is literally part of my mind. But a part of a mind has to be a subject of experience, or at least partly constitute one — and there's obviously nothing it's like to be my phone. So either the extended mind thesis is comitted to saying my phone is conscious in some way, or "part of the mind" is just a metaphor. Which is it? I'd rather you commit to one answer and defend it than lay out options, because right now I can't tell if the thesis is radical or empty.
