So I've got this friend who keeps saying "everything happens for a reason" whenever something goes wrong. Like, lost his job? "Everything happens for a reason." His girlfriend broke up with him? "Everything happens for a reason." He even said it when his cat died. And I've been trying to figure out what exactly he means by this, because it seems to me like it could be saying a bunch of very different things, and some of them are obviously true in a boring way and some of them are just... not.

Like, on one end of the spectrum, maybe he's just saying "every event has a cause," which is basically determinism, and yeah sure, physics. But he seems to be getting something way more loaded out of it — he's using it to mean that there's some kind of purpose or plan, that the bad thing happened *for the sake of* some future good thing. And that feels like a huge leap. I've also noticed that when people say this, they don't seem to apply it symmetrically — nobody says "everything happens for a reason" when they win the lottery, they say it when they're trying to cope with something awful. And it's weird because if you take it literally, it should apply to good things too, right? But it doesn't seem to function that way in practice.

I guess what I want is someone to lay out all the different precise claims that "everything happens for a reason" could actually be making, from the most minimal (like, every event is determined by prior causes, which is just causality) to the most ambitious (the universe has a plan, God, karma, whatever), and tell me which of them are actually defensible, which are just comforting nonsense, and which one my friend is probably relying on when he uses it to cheer himself up. And also, is there a version of this that's actually true and nontrivial, or is the whole thing either false or vacuous? Like, I'm worried the phrase is doing work precisly because it's slippery — it sounds deep but the moment you pin it down it either collapses into something obvious or something you'd need a lot of faith to believe.
