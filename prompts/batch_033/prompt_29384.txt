I'm a PhD student in judgment and decision-making, and I keep running into a presentation of the inside view/outside view distinction that strikes me as odd. The inside view is often described as a kind of base-rate neglect: the forecaster focuses on the details of the particular project and ignores how similar projects have actually turned out. If that is the right way to understand the distinction, then the outside view looks like a straightforward rational corrective, and any appeal to the special features of one's own case would be bias to be overridden rather than information to be weighed. Is there any principled role left for the inside view in forecasting or decision-making, or is the distinction simply a rebranding of base-rate neglect? I'd like a committed answer: take a definite position and argue for it, rather than laying out the options. Please write in American English.
