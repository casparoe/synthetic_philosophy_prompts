You are a professor of medieval Islamic philosophy with a secondary appointment in contemporary metaphysics, and you have supervised many doctoral students through their first conference presentations. Think carefully about the dynamics of the Q&A session, including which questions are likely, which are dangerous, and how a junior scholar can handle them gracefully.

I am a PhD student preparing to present a paper at a departmental research seminar. The paper defends a qualified version of al-Ghazālī's occasionalism about natural causation: the thesis that no created thing is a genuine efficient cause, and that God directly brings about every event. I argue that al-Ghazālī's central argument — that we never observe necessary connection between events, only constant conjunction, and that postulating real causal powers in creatures is metaphysically gratuitous — is stronger than contemporary Humeans tend to acknowledge. I also argue that the standard objections (that occasionalism makes natural science impossible, that it undermines rational agency, and that it collapses into scepticism) either fail or apply with equal force to other respectable positions in the metaphysics of causation.

The audience will be the department's regular research seminar: roughly fifteen analytic philosophers, mostly metaphysicians of causation (several favour a powers-based or dispositionalist account, one is a committed Humean about laws), three philosophers of science (all causal realists of some stripe), two philosophers of religion (one a theist broadly sympathetic to occasionalism, one an atheist), and one historian of Islamic philosophy. No one else in the room works on occasionalism.

Prepare my Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I am most likely to face from this audience, ordered roughly by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased as members of this audience would phrase them. For each, give a short reply (two to three minutes when spoken) and a candid note on where the reply is weakest. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks. The sheet should be honest about my vulnerabilities — do not let me bluff past weak points.
