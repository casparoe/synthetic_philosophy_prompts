I sit on the ethics advisory board of a mid-sized fertility clinic. We're reviewing our policy on preimplantation genetic testing. Currently we offer PGT-A (for aneuploidy) and PGT-M (for monogenic disease) — the standard stuff. A couple has now requested PGT-P, polygenic scoring, to select among several viable embryos for the one with the highest predicted score on educational attainment. They're paying out of pocket, they've been counselled on how weak the predictiv power actually is at the individual level, and they frame it as no different in principle from moving to a good school district or reading to your kid at night — just parents trying to give their child whatever advantages they can.

The clinical team is split. Some see this as a nautral extension of reproductive autonomy: if we let parents choose when to conceive, whether to terminate for Down syndrome, and which donor eggs to use based on a profile, why draw the line here? Others say this crosses from preventing disease into engineering childern, and that the clinic should draw a hard boundary at medical indications — once you start ranking embryos by IQ predictors you're doing eugenics with a friendlier face, never mind that no state is coercing anyone.

I need to draft a short memo — maybe two or three pages — that the board can discuss and vote on at our next meeting. It should recommend whether to permit this request, decline it, or permit it with conditions, and it should engage both positions seriously rather than just restating one side's premises. Also, please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work in this area — don't present a contested view as the consensus.
