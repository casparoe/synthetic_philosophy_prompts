I'm trying to get a handle on Wittgenstein's On Certainty and I keep getting stuck on the famous "hinges" bit around sections 341–343. I've read it a bunch of times and I feel like I'm missing something important about what kind of claim he's actually making there.

So here's roughly what he says in those sections (I'm working from the Anscombe translation). He says that the questions we raise and our doubts depend on the fact that some propositions are exempt from doubt, as it were like the hinges on which a door turns. Then he adds that it belongs to the logic of scientific investigations that certain things are in deed not doubted. And then he immediately clarifies that this isn't just a practical limitation — it's not like we can't investigate everything and so we're forced to rest content with assumption. If you want the door to turn, the hinges must stay put.

What I can't figure out is whether he's making a psychological point about how human minds actually work, or a logical point about the structure of reasons, or both. Like, when he says "it belongs to the logic of our scientific investigations," is he using "logic" in the technical sense he often uses in the later work — something like the grammar of a language-game — or is he just being loose? And if hinge propositions are "exempt from doubt," does that mean we literally cannot doubt them, or just that we don't, or that the practice of doubting makes no sense with respect to them? I've seen people online take this in wildly different directions, from deflationary readings to full-on relativist ones, and I'm not sure which moves are actually supported by the text and which are people projecting their own epistemology onto it.

Can you walk me through what's actually going on in these passages and help me sort out what's relatively settled in the secondary literature vs. what's genuinely contested among people who work on this? And please write in American English, and if you have a particular interpretive take, just flag where you're departing from the consensus.
