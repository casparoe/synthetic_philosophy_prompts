I've been defending a view of consciousness that goes roughly like this: conscious experience is a "user illusion" — it's like the desktop interface on a computer, presenting a simplified, serial, narratively coherent model of what's actually massively parallel, unconscious cognitive processing. The conscious self isn't the initiator of action; it's a post-hoc story-teller. The brain does stuff, and the conscious mind confabulates an explanation for why "I" did it. The key evidence for me is the Libet-style timing experiments, split-brain confabulation studies, the general finding that conscious access lags behind the neural events that correlate with decisions, and the broader evolutionary argument that consciousness is a cheap interface, not the engine.

I want to know where this view is actually exposed. Not just the standard objections, but a serious map of its vulnerabilities. Specifically:

1. What would be a genuine anomaly — a finding that doesn't just add nuance but that the view cannot accomodate without becoming a different view? What would the world have to look like for the user-illusion theorist to say "ok, I was wrong"?

2. What predictions does this view make that its rivals (views where consciousness is causally efficacious in a more direct way, or where conscious deliberation is genuinely in the driver's seat for some class of decisions) do not make? Have any of these been tested?

3. Are there cases where the "user illusion" framing has been used to dismiss evidence that should have been taken seriously? I'm thinking of things like the higher-order thought theories or the global workspace stuff — does the user illusion view have a genuine competitor, or does it just absorb every rival by reinterpreting their findings?

4. Has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability? If I say "consciousness is an illusion" but then concede that the illusion itself is conscious and that illusions have real effects, am I saying anything at all? I want an honest assessment of whether the view risks anything.

I want the defeater map: what kills the view, what wounds it, what it shrugs off. And I want to know whether defenders of the view have actually specified what would count against it, or whether they've just left it vague enough to survive anything.
