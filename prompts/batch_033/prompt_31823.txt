i keep noticing this pattern and i dont know if it has a name. it's like a coordination problem but with a nasty twist. here's the pattern: there's some bad equilibrium that everyone wants to escape, and the escape requires collective action—like a treaty, or a new regulation, or a social norm. but the very process of being stuck in the bad equilibrium erodes the *capacity* for collective action. like, the trust, the institutions, the political will, the shared information—all the things you'd need to coordinate your way out—are precisely what the bad equilibrium destroys over time. so you get this vicious cycle: the worse the problem gets, the harder it becomes to solve, because the problem itself consumes the resources you'd use to solve it. 

a concrete example: imagine an industry where firms are in a price war that's driving everyone toward bankruptcy. they'd all benefit from a truce. but the price war has made everyone so suspicious of each other and so cash-strapped that they can't even sit down to negotiate, let alone trust any agreement. or think about climate change: the longer we fail to coordinate, the more extreme the weather gets, and the more governments have to spend on disaster relief and border security, which leaves less political capital and budget for the coordinated transition that would prevent the disasters in the first place.

is there an established term for this specific configuration—where the capacity to solve a coordination problem is itself one of the things the coordination problem degrades? i know this is somewhere near collective action problems, tragedy of the commons, moloch-type traps. but in those standard stories, the *capacity* to coordinate is usually treated as exogenous—you either have it or you don't. what i'm describing is the case where the capacity is endogenous to the trap and gets eaten by it. does this have a name, and if not, what would you call it and how would you distinguish it from the standard cases?
