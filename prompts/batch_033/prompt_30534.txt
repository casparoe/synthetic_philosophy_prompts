hey, I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about the philosophy of hard choices—the kind where you're torn between two lives and it isn't obvious that one is better. I've absorbed enough of the academic stuff to have a set of views, but I want a consistency check before I build a whole article around them. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Value pluralism is true: there's no single master value (utility, happiness, preference-satisfaction, whatever) that all values reduce to.

(2) In a lot of genuinely hard choices—stay in my hometown vs. move for a job, have kids vs. not, law vs. music—the alternatives are incommensurable. By that I mean neither is better than the other, and they're not exactly equal either.

(3) When alternatives are incommensurable in that way, I think it can still be fully rational to choose either one—not just "not irrational" or "permissible," but rational in a thicker sense.

(4) I also think the choosing can do real work: after you commit to one of the alternatives, you can make it the case that you had most reason to do what you did. Reasons aren't all lying around waiting to be discovered before the choice.

(5) I think the incommensurability here is objective, not just a limitation of our ability to measure. There's genuinely no fact of the matter about which option is better, even in principle.

(6) But I also think an account of rational choice has to be able to say, at least after the fact, what you had most reason to do. If it can't, then "both options are rational" collapses into "neither is forbidden," and that seems too weak.

(7) Concrete case: a friend of mine was torn between a high-paying job in finance and going back to school to become a social worker. I dont think she made a mistake either way, but I also don't think flipping a coin would have been just as good as actually deciding.

Go through these and tell me which commitments are actually in tension, which only look like they're in tension, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each real tension, say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs. And tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
