I serve on my university philosophy department's executive committee. For the past thirty years we have awarded the annual Frege Prize for the best undergraduate essay in logic or philosophy of language, funded by an alumnus who specified the name in the gift agreement. Last semester a group of graduate students submitted a formal petition asking us to rename the prize. Their petition centers on Frege's political diary from 1924 — a document that was suppressed by the original editors of his Nachlass and only published in 1996 — which reveals that in the final year of his life Frege held virulently antisemitic views, admired Hitler and Ludendorff after the failed 1923 putsch, and devoted serious analytical attention to plans for expelling Jews from Germany and dismantling parliamentary democracy.

The petition argues that continuing to honor Frege by name signals indifference to these commitments, and that the department should find an alternative namesake — perhaps one of the many women or underrepresented figures whose contributions to logic have been historically overlooked. Several senior faculty have pushed back: they argue that Frege's contributions to logic and the philosophy of language stand entirely apart from his late political views, that judging historical figures by contemporary moral standards is anachronistic, and that renaming the prize would set a precedent that could unravel other named honors in the department. Two committee members have also pointed out that the donor agreement may legally constrain us from changing the name without the donor's consent, and the donor is deceased.

The dean has asked our committee to produce a short decision memo — no more than three pages — with a clear recommendation (retain the name, rename the prize, or some third option) and the reasoning behind it. I have been tasked with drafting it. The memo needs to engage seriously with the strongest arguments on both sides, not just the ones I agree with, and it must be something the full committee can actually vote on. Please draft the memo.
