The slogan was clearly doing real argumentative work against the proposal, but I could not pin down what exact proposition was being asserted, and the conversation moved on before I could press him on it. I want to come to the next meeting with a disambiguation in hand. Please lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making—about the statistical validity of a metric, about the incentives facing the people being measured, about what a decision-maker can infer from an optimized metric, or about a methodological rule we should follow—and for each reading, say whether it is a near-tautology, a plausible but defeasible empirical claim, a strong and probably false universal, or a normative principle. Then identify which reading or readings would actually support rejecting citation-based funding, and spell out what that reading would need to be true about citation practices in order to do that work. I am less interested in a single "correct" interpretation than in a menu I can use to make the argument precise.
