I had one of those conversations last night that ended with my friend and I both saying we're just talking past each other, and neither of us quite believing it. The topic was deflationism about truth. I lean deflationist: I can't see what the word 'true' adds beyond a device for endorsing sentences and generalising, and I kept pressing the disquotational line that calling the claim that snow is white true just is saying that snow is white. My friend is some kind of inflationist: he kept saying truth has to be a real property because it's the aim of inquiry, it's what beliefs are answerable to, and it does real work in a theory of meaning. By the end we'd both rehersed the standard moves and nobody had shifted, and it felt less like one of us was wrong and more like we were doing different things with the same word. But the literature has looked like that for decades, as far as I can tell.

So what I want is a diagnosis of why this debate doesn't move. Is the disagreement between deflationism and inflationism substantive, verbal, or are the two sides answering different questions — one about the function of the word 'true', the other about the nature of truth? Does the equivalence schema already smuggle in something the inflationist wants, or does the appeal to inquiry and norms beg the question? Given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a distinction, a formal result, a reframed question — or should we expect none and stop treating it as one debate? I'm not after a survey of the positions; I want the structure of the standoff. Keep it to 600 words at most, and write in British English.
