I'm a PhD student in moral philosophy, and I've been collecting my own gut verdicts on tradeoff cases because they seem not to add up, and I want to know if there's a principle that gets them.

Here's roughly where I stand. (1) A twenty-minute mild headache is worth enduring for a really good meal or a hundred dollars; in the mild range, pleasures and pains just trade off. (2) I would not accept a single minute of the worst kind of agony—the sort of pain people describe as making them loose control of their own minds—in exchange for any amount of pleasant experience, however large, spread over however many people. A billion blissful afternoons don't seem to put a dent in one minute of that. (3) But an hour of very bad, though clearly not maximal, pain does seem compensable: I'd say yes to a lot of money or to guaranteeing a lifetime of well-being to several people in exchange for it. The price is steep, but finite. And here's the part that bothers me: the felt difference between "very bad" and "the worst" is small, but my verdicts treat it as a chasm. (4) When I compare pains with each other, everything behaves normally: two episodes are worse than one, a longer episode is worse than a shorter one, and I'd always prevent the worse of two pains if I could only prevent one.

The case I get stuck on is this. Suppose I can either prevent one minute of the worst agony for one person, or prevent one minute of a very bad (but not worst) pain for a second person and also give a billion people a blissful afternoon. My gut says: prevent the worst agony; the pleasure doesn't tip it. But when I compare the two pains directly, the difference between them is exactly the small kind of difference that, lower down the scale, a modest pleasure would compensate. So why won't a billion blissful afternoons compensate it here? And a related one: I'd prevent a million people from having an hour-long migraine before I'd shave a single second off one person's worst agony, but I can't tell whether that's consistent with the rest.

Is there a principle that captures all or most of these verdicts, and which ones does it force me to drop? I'd also like to hear what it says about a case I keep avoiding: whether a very long stretch of moderate suffering—say, years of low-grade pain or depression—could ever be worse than one brief episode of the worst agony, and why.
