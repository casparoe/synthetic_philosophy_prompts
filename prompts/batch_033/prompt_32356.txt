I want to get clear on what a theory of infinite ethics is even supposed to do before I read another proposal. Write the desiderata: what any adequate framework for evaluating choices that affect infinitely many individuals must deliver — the cases it has to handle, the intuitive principles it should preserve or explain the failure of, the formal constraints it must satisfy (transitivity, completeness, etc.), with the case or argument behind each. Then flag which subsets of desiderata are known to be jointly unsatisfiable, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the kind of thing one camp insists on and another happily gives up. If the whole idea of laying out desiderata for a theory is itself part of the problem — if the lesson of the impossibility results is that we should stop treating infinite ethics as a domain where a single theory can satisfy a stable list of requirements — say so plainly instead of playing along.
