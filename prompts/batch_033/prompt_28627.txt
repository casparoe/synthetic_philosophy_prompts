I sit on my lab's internal deployment-safety board, and we have a decision due next week. I spent last night arguing with a friend who kept insisting that any safety measure that slows a model or costs benchmark points is an alignment tax the market simply will not pay, and I want to make this decision from our own precedents rather than from that argument. Our prior rulings: we approved a refusal-training layer that cost three percentage points on our benchmark suite for a public chatbot; we approved, with conditions, a staged rollout and extra monitoring for a frontier model even though it added two weeks of delay; we approved a five-point safety package for a public coding assistant because it substantially closed a specific exploit; we rejected a ten-point safety layer for an internal research tool with restricted access, judging the marginal gain too small; and we rejected a further eight-point package for that coding assistant on the grounds that its base safeguards were already adequate. The case now before us is a frontier general-purpose model slated for public API release. The safety team proposes a package — refusal and unlearning work aimed at one serious misuse mode, plus real-time monitoring — that would cost roughly six percentage points on general benchmarks and three weeks of schedule, and that would substantially reduce but not eliminate that misuse mode. If we want to be consistent with our own record, what should we decide? Which of our earlier rulings should weigh most heavily as the closest analogy? And if our precedents underdetermine the decision, what rule would we be committing ourselves to under each option, and what further case would best reveal which rule we have actually been following? Please write the answer in American English.
