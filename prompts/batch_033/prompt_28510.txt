She is well known for arguing that purported counterexamples (cases involving hypoxia, exhaustion, or cognitive impairment where an agent's first-order reasoning seems sound despite their higher-order evidence to the contrary) can be explained away once we carefully distinguish propositional from doxastic justification. Her book also develops a novel account of why higher-order evidence about one's own cognitive limitations does not transmit downwards to defeat first-order justification in the way critics allege.
