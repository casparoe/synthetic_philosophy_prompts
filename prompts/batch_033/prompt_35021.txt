ok so here's the thing — my friend and i are both grad students in early chinese philosophy and we've been going back and forth on the mencius-xunzi debate for way too long. we're finally trying to do an adversarial collaboration to figure out what we actually disagree about, and we'd love help drafting the document.

the two positions are:

- **Mencian camp** (that's me): human nature (xing 性) contains innate pro-social tendencies — the "four sprouts" of compassion, shame, deference, and a sense of right and wrong. moral development is about nurturing and extending these sprouts; they're not just blank potential, they're directional. a person who fails to develop them is like a sprout that withered, not like a seed that never got planted.

- **Xunzian camp** (my friend): our inborn dispositions are self-interested. we naturally desire food, warmth, comfort, status, and we'll fight over scarce resources unless something external restructures us. morality is a human invention — ritual (li 禮) and the sages' accumulated wisdom reshape us the way a craftsman straightens wood. the heart-mind (xin 心) can deliberate and override impulse, but that capacity itself is neutral, not good.

we want the document to have these sections:

1. **agreed claims** (numbered). the stuff we both accept, whether about the source texts, child development, the role of culture, or whatever. this should be substantive — not just "we both think Mencius and Xunzi are worth reading" but the surprising places where our views actually converge.

2. **disagreements table**. each row states one point of disagreement, with the mencian and xunzian positions each phrased so its own side would say "yeah, that's what i think." no strawmen.

3. **cruxes** — the sub-questions whose answers would genuinely move at least one of us. for each crux, give a concrete test or argument we both agree bears on it, and record in advance what each camp predicts the outcome will be. (could be an empirical prediction from developmental psych, a cross-cultural observation, a thought experiment, whatever.)

4. if any part of our dispute turns out to be mostly verbal — like we're using "xing" differently or we mean different things by "good" — name that and say why.

we're not looking for someone to adjudicate who's right. we want the residual disagreement made exact, with the cruxes laid out so we know what we'd actually need to go investigate. and please write it in the style of a document we'd both actually sign — constructive, fair, not hedging everything into meaninglessness.
