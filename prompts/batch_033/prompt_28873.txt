Write a villanelle about Schlick's view that the meaning of life is found not in some ultimate purpose but in activities done for their own sake—what he calls play, as opposed to work, which is merely instrumental. The two refrains should capture the central tension in his argument. One refrain should voice the persistent temptation to ask what life is for: the question that launches an infinite regress in which each end becomes a means to a further end and the question never lands. The other refrain should voice Schlick's answer that meaning is in the present activity itself, in the doing rather than the done. The alternation of the refrains across the tercets should enact the way the question keeps reasserting itself and the answer keeps returning, each passing the other. The final couplet, where the two refrains meet, should do the work of resolution: the question is not answered by finding a final purpose but by seeing that it was looking past the moment where meaning already is. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who knows Schlick's essay would recognize the argument, not just the name.
