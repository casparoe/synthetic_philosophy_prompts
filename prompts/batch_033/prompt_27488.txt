I've been trying to work out what I actually think about free will, partly because my brother got diagnosed with schizophrenia last year and partly because I quit smoking around the same time and I still can't tell weather it was me who quit or just circumstances. I've been going on gut verdicts about cases, and I'd like to know what principle, if any, is behind them.

Here are the verdicts. A person in a fully deterministic universe who deliberates about a job offer, listens to reasons on both sides, changes his mind when a friend makes a good argument, and then acts: I see no problem calling him free and responsible. A man who becomes violent because of a brain tumor, and goes back to normal when it's removed: not free, not to blame. An alcoholic who hates his drinking, has done AA and rehab, and relapses anyway: responsible, but less than the ordinary case, and I can't say why. A woman with severe OCD who washes her hands until they bleed and says she knows it's irrational but can't stop: I don't think she's free in the washing, even though she's doing it on purpose. A bank teller who hands over the money at gunpoint: free in some thin sense, since she chose to comply, but not morally to blame. Someone raised from birth in a cult who as an adult helps cover up abuse: I go back and forth, because the cult formed him, but he also seems to know right from wrong in other parts of his life.

I notice I'm treating the tumor and the addiction differently even though both look like brain stuff, and I'm treating cult upbringing as freedom-undermining even though a normal upbringing also shapes you. So my question is whether there's one thing I'm tracking in all these, or a couple of different things I've been lumping together under "free will." If you had to state the principle behind my verdicts, what would it be, what does it say about my smoking case (for years I told people I didn't want to quit, while privately hiding how much I smoked and making attempts to cut down), and which of my verdicts does it force me to give up? I'd rather have a simple principle that overturns one of my gut calls than a complicated one that saves them all, but if the honest answer is that there are two or three different notions here, say so and tell me what each one is good for — and which kind of freedom is actually worth having, not just which one fits a definition.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
