I just got back from a long argument with a friend about hedonism, and I have a half-formed idea that I think could become a solid term paper. My friend was defending the view that pleasure is the only thing intrinsically good, and I was pressing the heterogeneity problem: pleasures don't seem to share any single phenomenal quality. A warm bath, the satisfaction of solving a proof, the rush of hearing a favorite song, the relief of a headache lifting — these feel different in ways that resist reduction to a common feeling tone. My friend's reply was that pleasures are unified functionally: they are just experiences we have a pro-attitude toward, ones we want to sustain. I pushed back that this collapses hedonism into a desire-satisfaction view, and we went in circles for another hour.

But here is what I realized afterward: both of us were assuming that for hedonism to work, pleasure has to be a single natural kind with a unified essence shared by all its instances. What if that assumption is wrong? What if pleasure is unified the way biological species are unified — not by a single essential property but by a homeostatic property cluster, a set of properties that tend to co-occur reliably enough to support inductive generalizations but that need not all be present in every case? The question I want to write a paper on is: can a homeostatic property cluster account of pleasure answer the heterogeneity objection while still doing the normative work hedonism requires — namely, explaining why all and only pleasures are intrinsically good?

Turn this into a term paper proposal. I need a precise statement of the question, an explanation of why the existing positions in the hedonism literature leave it open, a description of the approach I would take (including why the homeostatic cluster model rather than the obvious alternatives like a functional-attitude account or a simple disjunctivism about pleasure), an account of what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project failing, and a realistic sense of scope — this is for a roughly 6000-word paper in one semester. Do not worry about citations; I will add those myself.
