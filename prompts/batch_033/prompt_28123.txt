I am a novelist writing a novel in which one of the main characters is a philosopher who works on personal identity. Across several earlier chapters I have established, through her remarks and lectures, her settled verdicts on a series of imaginary cases, and I want the scene I am now writing to remain consistent with them. Her track record is as follows:

- A patient's entire brain is transplanted into a healthy donor body. She says the patient survives and wakes up in the donor body.
- A patient's body is destroyed but the brain is kept alive in a vat and supplied with simulated experience. She says the patient survives.
- A patient's cerebral hemispheres are destroyed while the brainstem keeps the body breathing and circulating blood. She says the person is gone and only a living body remains.
- A patient spends a month in a reversible coma with no mental activity and then wakes with memory and personality intact. She says the patient survived throughout.
- A person's body below the neck is gradually replaced by prosthetics over a decade while the brain is left untouched. She says the person survives throughout.

Now I must write the scene in which a colleague asks her about a teletransporter. The machine scans a person's brain and body, destroys the original, and assembles at the destination an exact physical replica with the person's memories, habits, and temperament. I need to decide what she would say: does the person survive as the replica, does the person die, or does something more qualified happen?

I am less interested in a bare answer than in the reasoning behind it. What are the plausible principles that fit her existing verdicts? What does each principle predict about the teletransporter? Which answer would make her most consistent with what I have already written, and why? I also have a further case in mind that may settle which principle she is really running: a drug that permanently erases all of a person's memories, values, and skills while leaving the same brain to build a new mind from nothing. Would her verdict on that case settle the matter, or is there a better case for distinguishing the remaining principles?
