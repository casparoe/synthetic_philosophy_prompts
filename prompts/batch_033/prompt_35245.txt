During a seminar on animal ethics, I was defending the view that sentience is a matter of degree — that a pig is more sentient than a mouse, a mouse more than a honeybee, and that our moral obligations should track these gradations rather than treating all sentient beings as all-or-nothing moral patients. A classmate cut me off with "Either you feel it or you don't," and the professor nodded as if that settled the matter. I'm not convinced it does. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan could be expressing, from the trivial to the substantive, and say for each whether it is plausible and whether it actually blocks the gradation view I was defending? I want to come to the next seminar with a clear menu of readings so I can press the classmate on which one he means.
