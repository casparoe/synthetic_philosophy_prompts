For a graduate seminar in formal epistemology and decision theory, I need a decision tree that sorts a reader's commitments about how rational agents should represent and act under severe uncertainty — evidence that is sparse, conflicting, or purely structural — into a named position. Begin the tree from a single entry question: when evidence underdetermines a precise probability, is a rational agent always required to adopt some single precise credence function, or may her rational state be set-valued? Build the tree from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of labels: first write the diagnostic questions as claims about concrete cases, then locate the actual positions among the combinations of answers, and briefly diagnose any combination no position occupies as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

The leaves must include: uniqueness-style precise Bayesianism; permissive precise Bayesianism; imprecise probabilism with incomplete preferences and dominance-based choice (maximality and E-admissibility, distinguished if the tree can do so); coherent lower previsions; maxmin expected utility; Choquet expected utility; Hurwicz-criterion or variational ambiguity preferences; the smooth ambiguity model; and Dempster-Shafer belief functions.

Use at least the following concrete cases in the diagnostic questions: an urn with 30 red balls and 60 balls that are black or yellow in unknown proportion, together with the two pairs of bets that make up the Ellsberg choices (red versus black; red-or-yellow versus black-or-yellow); a coin whose bias is completely unknown; and two equally trusted forecasters who report sharply conflicting probabilities for the same event. Phrase each node so a reader can answer it without already knowing which position it leads to, and without needing to recognize the theoretical labels.

Aim for roughly nine leaves. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot really separate, and say what further question would separate them if one exists. Do not include any bibliography or citations.
