So I've been reading Dennett on sphexishness and I think I've found a regress that makes me doubt the whole framework. Here's the argument:

(1) A behavior is sphexish if the creature persists in it even when the behavior no longer makes sense given the creature's own goals — the wasp keeps re-checking the burrow even though the cricket is right there and the burrow is fine. Sphexish behavior isn't free; it's just a rigid routine.

(2) What makes human behavior non-sphexish? Presumably that we can notice when a routine isn't working and switch to something else. We have second-order monitoring.

(3) But my second-order monitoring could itself be sphexish. Suppose whenever I notice I'm in a rut, I reflexively do the opposite of whatever I was doing — that's just a fancier rut. It's still insensitive to the specific reasons in the situation. To be genuinely non-sphexish, my second-order monitoring has to itself be flexible and reason-sensitive, which means I need third-order monitoring. And so on.

(4) Any finite creature only has finitely many levels of monitoring. At the top level, whatever it is, the process just executes — it isn't monitored by anything further. So the top level is sphexish by the original criterion.

(5) If the top level is sphexish, then the whole system is sphexish, because a chain of oversight that bottoms out in a rigid routine is still, globally, a rigid routine — just a more elaborate one.

(6) So no finite creature can be non-sphexish. Humans are finite creatures. So humans are sphexish. So humans aren't free.

Now I'm pretty sure humans are free (or at least, if we're not free it's not because of this argument). So something went wrong. But I can't tell whether the problem is in (1) — maybe sphexishness was never the right criterion for freedom — or in (5) — maybe the top level doesn't "infect" the whole system — or somewhere else. And if I reject (1), I need a new story about why the wasp isn't free, which seems like it'll mess up the whole Dennettian project. Which premise do I reject and what am i paying?
