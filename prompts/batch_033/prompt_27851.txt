Some background so this doesn't come out of nowhere: I've been the one keeping the bankroll for our weekly poker game (really just a spreadsheet and a Venmo account), and I went down a rabbit hole reading about the Kelly criterion to figure out how much of the pool we should be staking on side bets where we think we have an edge. Two of the regulars, Marcus and Dana, have been arguing about it for a month, and they asked me to referee. I honestly cant, so I'm asking you. Here's their exchange as I remember it:

(1) Marcus: If you have a real edge, the rational bet size is the Kelly fraction, because that's what maximizes the geometric growth rate of your bankroll. For an even-money bet with a 60% chance of winning, Kelly says 20% of your roll. Any fixed fraction above or below that gets left behind in the long run, almost surely.

(2) Dana: That's only true if what you're maximizing is expected log wealth. Log is one utility function among many. For a single bet, if you actually have an edge, expected value is linear in the stake, so maximizing expected value means bet as much as they'll take. Anything less is just risk aversion wearing a formula.

(3) Marcus: It's not about taste, it's about time. If you're going to bet again and again, bet far enough above Kelly and your bankroll is driven toward zero almost surely even though every individual bet is +EV. That's exactly how gamblers with a real edge still go broke. Bet-by-bet expected value ignores the sequence.

(4) Dana: But you dont get an infinite sequence of identical, independent bets. You get a finite, lumpy one, and full Kelly can drop your roll by half in a short bad run, which changes what you can stake later. Also, you never know your edge exactly — you estimate it — and the uncertainty means full Kelly is overbetting relative to what your edge probably is. Half Kelly is more robust for a real person with real uncertainty.

(5) Marcus: Half Kelly is just a rule of thumb. If you're going to deviate from Kelly, tell me what utility function you're actually maximizing, otherwise you're just scared. And if you do pick a utility function, your optimal fraction follows from it — but then don't call it Kelly. Kelly is the log case, full stop.
