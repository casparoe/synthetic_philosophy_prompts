I'm in a philosophy of statistical mechanics course and we've spent the last couple weeks on Maxwell's demon and the usual exorcism stuff — Szilard's engine, Bennett's point that the measurement can be done reversibly, Landauer's principle. The version we get is: the demon can in principle do the measurement without paying any entropy cost, but it has to record what it sees, and eventually it's memory fills up and it has to reset bits, and resetting a bit in a bath at temperature T dumps at least kT ln 2 of heat into the environment, so the entropy the demon removed from the gas gets paid back when the memory is erased. So the position I've ended up holding is that the demon is exorcised specifically by information erasure, not by measurement, and that the moral of the whole episode is that information is physical.

What I want is a map of what would count against that position — not the generic "well maybe the demon is impossible for some other reason," but the actual ways it could fail. Would a demon with unbounded memory that never erases but still cools one side of the box count, or does that just quietly change what you count as the system? Would a clean experimental violation of Landauer's principle be decisive, and how would you tell a real violation from a sloppy setup? I know there's a skeptical literature (Earman and Norton and the people replying to them) about whether Landauer's principle is a fundamental principle or just a claim about one particular erasure protocol, but I can't tell how live that is. I'd like the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward, and an honest verdict on whether the view has been protected by qualifications like "in any cyclic process" or "eventually the memory has to reset" — and whether being insulated that way is a problem or just how physics gets done. What would personally move me most is a concrete scenario or experiment the erasure story gets wrong, not just a reinterpretation of the same math.
