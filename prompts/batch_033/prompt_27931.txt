I'm a philosopher of mind, and I've been teaching a graduate seminar on consciousness this term. In our sessions on the hard problem, I keep noticing a secondary puzzle that my students circle around without quite articulating, and I've realized I don't have a clean label for it either.

Here is the phenomenon. The hard problem, as standardly framed, is the question of why and how physical processing gives rise to subjective experience — why there is something it is like to be a physical system at all. That is one thing. But there is a distinct puzzle that keeps surfacing in our discussions, and it goes roughly like this: we are physical systems, products of evolution by natural selection, whose cognitive machinery was shaped entirely by pressures to survive and reproduce. Why would such a system — a lump of organized matter — ever come to represent *itself* as facing a hard problem? Why does it strike us, so forcefully and near-universally, that there is an explanatory gap between the physical facts and the facts of experience? One can imagine a zombie version of a philosopher of mind who behaves exactly like a real philosopher of mind, writes the same papers, reports the same intuitions, and yet has no phenomenal consciousness. The fact that we are not zombies — or at least that we take ourselves not to be — does not yet explain why we find the hard problem so compelling. The question is not "why does consciousness exist?" but "why do creatures like us *think* there is a special puzzle about consciousness in the first place, and why does that puzzle present itself with the particular shape it has?"

This is not the same as asking for an error theory about belief in consciousness, though it could lead there, and it is not the same as the hard problem itself. It is a question about the cognitive architecture, evolutionary history, and perhaps the cultural transmission of a certain kind of philosophical intuition — the intuition that there is something radically puzzling about the relationship between the physical and the phenomenal. I can think of fragments of literatures that touch on it: work on the evolution of consciousness, on introspective illusions, on the psychological sources of dualism, on the cognitive science of the "explanatory gap." But I do not know whether this exact question has been carved out and given a name of its own. If it has, I would like to know what it is called, how it is defined, and how it relates to neighboring questions — the hard problem, illusionism, the explanatory gap, the knowledge argument. If it has not been named, I would be interested in what you would call it and how you would draw its boundaries. I am less interested in the label itself than in getting clear on what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.

Please keep your answer to under 600 words.
