Construct a thought experiment in which a Lange-style planning board has fully met Mises's calculation objection — there are money prices for capital goods, adjusted by trial and error, and enterprise managers follow Lange's rules, minimizing cost at the board's prices and producing where marginal cost equals price — yet the system still fails, and fails for a Hayekian reason rather than a Misesian one. Constraints: set the case in a single concrete industry, keep the scenario to one paragraph, exclude corruption and repression, and make the failure arise from dispersed or tacit local knowledge that the price mechanism fails to elicit, not from the absence of prices. Identify the single feature that makes the failure Hayekian rather than Misesian, say what happens if that feature is removed, and finish with the strongest Lange-style response and whether it concedes Hayek's point.
