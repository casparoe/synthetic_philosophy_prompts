ok so i'm a phd student in philosophy working on moral psychology and i'm TA-ing this upper-level undergrad seminar on virtue ethics. we're about to hit the situationist critique (doris, harman, etc.) and the prof is letting me run one session -- 15 students, 75 minutes.

i really want them to *feel* the situationist point in their bones, not just read about it. you know how when you first encounter the darley and batson good samaritan study it's kind of vertigo-inducing? like, wait, whether someone stops to help a stranger is mostly about whether they're in a hurry, and whether they just heard a lecture on the good samaritan makes basically no difference? i want that feeling, but generated by something they do in class, not by a paper they read the night before.

constraints: no deception that would make anyone feel shitty afterwards (so no staging a fake emergency where they think they failed a moral test), no real money needed, and ideally the setup is simple enough that i'm not spending 20 minutes explaining the rules.

what i'm picturing is something where they make predictions about behavior -- either their own or other people's -- and then something happens that reveals a gap between their character-based expectations and actual outcomes. or maybe they themselves are put in slightly different situations without realizing that's the variable, and then we look at the aggregate difference. the key is that the situationist insight should emerge from what they do, not from me explaining it afterwards.

the debrief should connect the dots to the philosophical claims: the global vs. local trait distinction, the fundamental attribution error, and what it means for the virtue-ethical idea that character is where moral evaluation should primarily land. also i want them to see why the situationist critique isnt just "people are inconsistent" -- its a specific challenge to the predictive and explanatory power of character traits.

also: tell me what'll predictably go wrong when i run this, and be honest about which part of the situationist critique the activity can't get at and i'll have to just explain. (i'm guessing the activity can't really capture the "negative" part of the situationist claim -- that even if there are local traits, they dont do the normative work virtue ethics needs them to do -- but maybe i'm wrong.)
