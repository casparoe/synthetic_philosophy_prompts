I chair my university's hiring committee. We currently evaluate candidates sequentially: every member reads the full file and writes an independent rating, but then we discuss and re-rate in order of seniority, with earlier ratings visible to everyone. In three recent searches, the first two or three ratings have set the range for nearly all later ratings, and candidates discussed late in the order rarely move, even when their files are comparable. Some members defend the process: if our most senior colleagues are well-calibrated, later raters are rational to update heavily on their judgments, and we should not throw that information away. Others reply that this is precisely a case where individually rational updating produces a worse collective judgment, because later members' private information never enters the record and the agenda order ends up doing the hiring. Draft a short decision memo for the committee to vote on next week. It should recommend a concrete procedure for collecting and aggregating evaluations in our next search, explain the reasoning for it, and give an honest treatment of the strongest argument for keeping the current sequential process.
