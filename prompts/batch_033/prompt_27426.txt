I've been teaching Dennett's "Kinds of Minds" in my philosophy of cognitive science seminar and the students keep pushing me on something I'm not sure I have a clean answer to. The Popperian creature (which can let its hypotheses die in its stead, running internal simulations rather than physically trying things out) and the Gregorian creature (which imports mind-tools from its culture — language, arithmetic, diagrams, the whole scaffolding) are supposed to be distinct rungs on the ladder. But I've started wondering whether the Gregorian isn't just a Popperian whose environment happens to contain the offloaded cognitive artifacts of conspecifics. If a Popperian creature can internalize a model of its physical environment and test actions against it, what's so special about internalizing a model of a *cultural* environment — words, notations, heuristics — and running simulations in that? Is there a difference in kind here, or just a difference in the *contents* of the inner simulation?

My colleague in the anthropology department says I'm missing the point entirely, that Gregorian creatures don't just simulate in a richer medium, they restructure what simulation even is because the tools themselves are cognitive — a written numeral isn't just a thing you model, it's part of the modeling machinery. But that sounds to me like it could still be described in Popperian terms, just with external props. So here's my question: compare Dennett's Popperian and Gregorian creatures and give me your best case for whether the Gregorian level marks a genuine qualitative shift in cognitive architecture, or whether it's better understood as a Popperian creature doing its thing in a culturally enriched niche. I'm less interested in what Dennett himself would say than in what the strongest version of each position looks like, and whether there's a way to settle the question that doesn't just come down to how we decide to draw the line.
