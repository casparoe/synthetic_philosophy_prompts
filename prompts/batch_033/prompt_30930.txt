I've been trying to explain something to a freind and keep getting tangled up, so I want a careful writeup. Here's the setup. A factory makes solid cubes, and all you're told about a given cube is that its side length is somewhere between 0 and 1 cm, with every value in that range equally likely. Ask for the probability that the side length is less than 1/2, and you'd say 1/2. But describe the exact same cube by the area of one of its faces, which ranges between 0 and 1 cm². If every face area in that range is equally likely, then the probability that the face area is less than 1/4 is 1/4 — and a face area below 1/4 is exactly the same event as a side length below 1/2. Describe it by volume, which ranges between 0 and 1 cm³, and you get 1/8. So one and the same event gets probability 1/2, 1/4, or 1/8 depending on which description you treat as uniform. This is the same kind of trouble as Bertrand's famous chord paradox, and it looks like a serious problem for the principle of indifference — the idea that when you have no reason to favor one possibility over another, you should assign them equal probabilities.
