I'm reading Pascal's Pensées for a philosophy elective, and I'm stuck on a passage from the section on justice and force. Here is the passage in translation:

"It is proper that what is just should be obeyed; it is necessary that what is strongest should be obeyed. Justice without force is helpless; whereas the use of force without justice is tyrannical. Justice without force is futile, for there shall always be the wicked; but force without justice is always to be condemned. It follows that we must always combine justice and force and, to this end, what is just must always be made strong, or what is strong just."

I understand the basic problem he is laying out — justice needs force to be effective, and force needs justice to be legitimate. But the last sentence is troubling me. He says we must either make what is just strong, or make what is strong just. These seem like radically different projects: the first sounds like giving power to just institutions, while the second sounds like dressing up whoever happens to hold power in the language of justice after the fact. Is Pascal treating these as equivalent? Is he being cynical here, or is there a more careful argument at work that I am missing? I would appreciate help unpacking what he actually means by this "or," and whether he thinks both paths are equally valid.
