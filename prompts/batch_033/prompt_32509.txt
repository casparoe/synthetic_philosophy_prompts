I'm trying to get clear on what we're even asking for when we talk about "common knowledge of rationality" (CKR) in epistemic game theory. Before I read another paper that assumes it or tries to formalize it, I want the full specification: what must an adequate theory of CKR actually deliver? Write the desiderata — the phenomena it must explain, the puzzles it must dissolve, the distinctions it must respect, the formal results it must be consistent with — and give the case or consideration behind each requirement. Flag which are non-negotiable (any theory that fails them is a non-starter) and which are contested or reflect one camp's preferences. In particular, I want to know: are there subsets of these desiderata that are jointly unsatisfiable given existing results (e.g., the tension between CKR and backward induction in the centipede game, the impossibility of common knowledge arising from finite communication, or the way common knowledge of rationality plus common priors forces agreement)? If so, which tradeoffs are forced on us, and which single requirement does the most to exclude candidates? If the whole framing of CKR turns out to be confused or the question as posed can't be done well, say so plainly instead of pretending it's a clean request.
