I'm teaching an upper-level undergrad aesthetics course and I want to give my students a diagnostic quiz before we start the unit on whether you can separate the art from the artist (and the related question of whether immoral art can be good art). The idea is they answer a bunch of concrete cases, and then based on the pattern of their answers they get sorted into a position—but they don't know which position they're gravitating toward until the end. I want the thing to sort them by what they actually say about cases, not by which label sounds nice.

I need about 8 questions, each a specific case with 2–4 answer options. The positions I want to sort them into are roughly:

- Radical autonomism: moral and aesthetic value are completely independent domains. A work's moral character, and the artist's moral character, are simply irrelevant to whether it's good art.
- Moderate autonomism: moral flaws can be aesthetic flaws, but only when they're internal to the work and actually disrupt the aesthetic experience. The artist's biography is irrelevant unless it manifests in the work itself.
- Ethicism: a moral defect in a work is always an aesthetic defect pro tanto (it always counts against the work aesthetically), though it can be outweighed by other aesthetic merits.
- Immoralism: a moral defect can sometimes be an aesthetic merit—the transgressive, unsettling, or dangerous quality of a work can be part of what makes it artistically valuable.
- Biographical contextualism: the artist's life and character are always relevant to evaluating the work, because artworks are partly constituted by their origins.

The questions should be written so students can't just sniff out which answer goes with which position. The cases should feel real and force them to actually think. And I need a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions—some answers might contribute to more than one position, and that's fine, the pattern across all eight is what matters.

Oh, and please write the whole thing in American English. I'm grading too many papers to deal with extra u's all over the place.
