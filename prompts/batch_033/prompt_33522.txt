I am a PhD student in epistemology, working on the philosophy of cognitive technology, and I would like a consistency check on a set of commitments I currently hold about understanding and epistemic dependence on AI systems. My commitments are these:

(1) I accept a moderate version of the extended mind thesis: at least some cognitive processes can be partly constituted by external tools, rather than merely causally scaffolded by them.

(2) I hold that genuine understanding of a result or argument requires being able to reconstruct or explain the underlying reasoning in one's own terms; reliable retrieval or application of the result is not sufficient.

(3) I believe my own heavy reliance on large language models for summarising literature and reconstructing arguments has not eroded my understanding of my field, because I still critically evaluate and integrate their outputs and can usually reproduce the relevant reasoning myself.

(4) I think an agent can be epistemically responsible in deferring to an AI system whose internal workings they cannot audit, provided the system has a strong, independently established track record on the relevant task.

(5) I suspect, nevertheless, that someone who has outsourced nearly all of their reading and reasoning to such a system lacks understanding of the material in a way that matters, even if they can reliably produce correct answers in conversation.

I would like to know whether this set is jointly tenable. Which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity. I am particularly unsure whether (1) and (5) are compatible, and whether (2) and (3) can both be true of me, so please address those pairs explicitly. Finally, what is the least costly revision that would restore consistency, and what would I be giving up in making it?
