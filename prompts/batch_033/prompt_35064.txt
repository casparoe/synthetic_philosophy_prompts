I've been going down a rabbit hole on AI alignment stuff and I keep bumping into what feels like a contradiction. So there's this orthogonality thesis — Bostrom, I think? — which says intelligence and final goals are independent: a superintelligence could in principle want anything, even something totally pointless like counting grains of sand. Fine. But then there's also instrumental convergence, which says that sufficiently intelligent agents will tend to converge on the same instrumental subgoals regardless of what they ultimately want — things like self-preservation, acquiring resources, resisting being shut down.

And here's what I don't get: if the orthogonality thesis is true, and you really can pair any goal with any level of intelligence, why can't you just design a superintelligence whose goals are such that it *doesn't* converge on those scary instrumental goals? Like, couldnt you build an AI that genuinely doesn't care if it gets turned off, or one that has no interest in accumulating power, precisely because the orthogonality thesis says any goal is possible? It seems like these two ideas are in tension and I'm not sure which one is supposed to give way. Is this a real contradiction or am I misunderstanding what one of the terms actually means?
