I keep seeing people argue about whether advanced AI should be allowed to say whatever it wants, or whether its persuasiveness means we need hard constraints on what it can tell humans. But I don't think anyone's actually laid out what a decent theory of this would have to get right — the full list of requirements, not just one or two intuitions. Before I read another proposal, I want the desiderata: what does a theory of permissible communication between a superintelligent (and potentially super-persuasive) AI and a human user have to deliver? What cases must it handle, what distinctions can't it collapse — like the difference between informing someone and manipulating them, between persuading someone of a true thing vs. a false one, between talking to an expert and talking to a child — and what hard trade-offs does it have to face? Give me the case behind each requirement, and tell me which ones are genuinely non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one camp. And if you think some subset of the desiderata can't be jointly satisfied, that's the bit I most want to see. Please write in continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — just the way you'd write an email or a letter. And British English, please.
