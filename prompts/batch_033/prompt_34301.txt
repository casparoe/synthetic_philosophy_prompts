I spent my career in the natural sciences and have only recently begun reading philosophy seriously. A generally reliable popular essay I read recently summarized Kant's essay on the murderer at the door as follows: "Kant held that lying is absolutely forbidden, so when a would-be murderer asks whether your friend is hiding in your house, you are morally required to tell him the truth, even though this means betraying your friend. He even says that if you lie and the murderer kills your friend, you are responsible for the death, whereas if you tell the truth you are blameless. Since virtually no one accepts that one must hand a friend over to a murderer, the essay is usually treated as a reductio of Kant's whole moral theory." This strikes me as likely to be unfair, but I do not yet know the text well enough to say where. Would you go through this summary sentence by sentence and tell me which parts a careful reader of Kant would accept, which are overstatements or conflations, and whether the concluding reductio claim survives once the accurate parts are separated from the distortions?
