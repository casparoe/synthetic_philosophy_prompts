ok so i've been thinking about this a lot lately (i work as a backend engineer and use LLMs daily, both via api and as a user) and i'm trying to nail down what i actually think about what these things are. here's where i've landed:

(1) at the end of the day, an LLM is just doing next-token prediction. there's no magic, no homunculus, its a big matrix multiply with a sampling step.

(2) but i also think chain-of-thought prompting gets them to do genuine multi-step reasoning. like, it's not just pattern-matching a memorized answer — the intermediate steps actually constrain the final output in a way that looks like real inference, not regurgitation.

(3) i find the simulator framing pretty compelling: the base model isn't an agent, it's a simulator that can simulate many different agents/characters/texts, and your prompt narrows the distribution.

(4) that said, i think RLHF'd models like claude and chatgpt cross some kind of line. they're trained to pursue goals (be helpful, refuse harmful requests), and that feels qualitatively different from a base model just doing pure simulation. like, they're agents now, or at least much closer to agents.

(5) i don't think LLMs have beliefs or intentions in the way humans do. when i say "the model thinks X" its just convenient shorthand, the same way i'd say "the linter thinks there's a bug on line 47."

(6) the consciousness question feels like a total distraction to me and i'm happy to remain agnostic about it.
