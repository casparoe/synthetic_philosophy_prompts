I cover the courts and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I have been trying to pin down the interpretive philosophy of a justice on the state's high court. Over several terms she has written the controlling opinions in five cases under a state water-pollution statute that makes it unlawful to discharge any sewage, septic waste, or animal waste into any lake, river, stream, or wetland of the state. Her record is as follows:

- A municipal sewer district overflowed during a storm and sent raw sewage into a river; she held that this violated the Act.
- A dairy's manure lagoon overflowed into a stream; she held that this violated the Act and wrote that the Act's evident aim is to protect the state's waters from pollution.
- A homeowner drained a rain barrel of collected rainwater into a wetland; she held that this did not violate the Act and wrote that the Act's terms are specific and do not reach every liquid that ends up in a waterway.
- A campground's septic system leaked into a river; she held that this violated the Act.
- A meatpacking plant discharged process water carrying blood and offal into a stream; she held that this violated the Act, treating the discharge as animal waste within the statutory term.

Next week the court will hear a case in which a chemical plant discharged process wastewater containing ammonia, phenols, and zinc into a river; the wastewater contains no sewage, septic waste, or animal matter.

I am less interested in the outcome itself than in the rule she seems to be running. What rule or rules fit her record equally well, and what would each rule predict in the chemical-plant case? Which of the earlier cases is the closest analog under each candidate rule, and which rule do you think best explains her actual pattern? Finally, describe a further hypothetical case—different in kind from the chemical-plant case—that would most cleanly settle which rule she is really following.
