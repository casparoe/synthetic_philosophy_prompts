Two philosophers of mathematics — one a Quine-Putnam-style indispensability platonist, the other a fictionalist in the tradition of Field — have agreed to an adversarial collaboration after years of talking past each other at the same three conferences. Draft the joint document they'd produce. It needs: a numbered list of claims both can sign (about mathematical practice, about what a semantics for mathematical discourse must account for, about what the two views agree is not at issue); a disagreements table where each position is stated in wording its own proponent would endorse; the cruxes — the sub-disputes that, if resolved, would actually change someone's mind; and for each crux, a test or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. One of them suspects the whole debate about the "existence" of numbers is partly verbal; if you think so too, flag which parts and why.
