I am a PhD student in philosophy of mind working on pain and affect, and I keep seeing theories of the badness of pain built to handle ordinary acute pain while pain asymbolia is treated as an afterthought. Before I read further, I want the full specification of what an adequate theory of the badness of pain has to deliver, with pain asymbolia as the load-bearing case.

By pain asymbolia I mean the rare neuropsychological condition in which a person can identify a noxious stimulus as painful, locate it, and rate its intensity, but reports that it is not unpleasant: they do not mind it, may not withdraw, and do not seek relief. This looks like a dissociation between the sensory-discriminative dimension of pain and its affective-motivational dimension, and it is the case I want the desiderata to be built around.

Please write the requirements list. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it — for example, whatever forces the distinction between pain intensity and pain unpleasantness, whatever forces a stance on whether asymbolic pain is really pain, and whatever empirical dissociations a theory cannot contradict. Include the standard contrast cases where they sharpen a requirement: ordinary acute pain, reports in which an analgesic leaves the sensory quality largely intact while the unpleasantness recedes, pain without tissue damage, and so on. Keep the weight on the criteria rather than on scoring the theories.

Then do three further things. First, mark each requirement as non-negotiable for any adequate theory or as a preference of a particular camp (evaluativist, representationalist, imperativist, attitudinal, or otherwise). Second, say whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable or in serious tension — in particular, if asymbolic pain is genuinely pain, can a theory both make badness intrinsic to pain and honor the dissociation, and if not, which requirement has to give? Third, identify the single requirement that does the most to eliminate the standard candidates, and explain why it is that one.

Format the response in these sections, in this order:

1. The target and the central case — 2–3 sentences stating what a theory of pain's badness is meant to explain and what asymbolia establishes.
2. Non-negotiable requirements — the bulk of the response; one requirement per paragraph or a table, each with its motivating case or consideration.
3. Negotiable or camp-specific requirements — the demands that reflect theoretical taste rather than adequacy, with a sentence on whose taste each reflects.
4. Tensions and jointly unsatisfiable subsets — 1–2 paragraphs on where the list may not be co-satisfiable and which requirement you would give up first.
5. The requirement that does the most work — 1 paragraph on the single criterion that excludes the most standard theories.
6. Where I am least confident — 1 paragraph identifying which requirement you are least sure is a genuine desideratum, and what argument or empirical finding would lead you to drop or demote it.
