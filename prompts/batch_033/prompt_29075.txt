I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on Mill's political philosophy, and I'm setting the final exam. This term I've noticed that my students can recite the harm principle fluently and even know that Mill exempts peoples he judged not yet capable of self-government from its scope, but they have not actually worked through whether his writings on India and colonial rule are a coherent extension of his liberalism or a standing embarrassment to it. I want the final to force that confrontation.

Please write one essay question on Mill's views on India and colonial rule that cannot be answered by summarizing the harm principle or the despotism-for-barbarians justification. The question should be answerable in about an hour and should make students engage with at least two of the following pressure points: whether the carve-out restricting the liberty principle to peoples capable of improvement by free discussion is principled or ad hoc; the tension between Mill's anti-paternalism toward individuals in On Liberty and his defense of paternalistic despotism toward whole peoples; the means-end problem of whether despotic tutelage can actually cultivate the capacities for self-government it claims to be building; and the tension between his fallibilism about received opinion and his confidence that colonial administrators understood the interests of subject peoples better than those peoples understood themselves. Please also provide a model answer of about 600 words and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific interpretive and argumentative moves each must contain, along with the specific mistakes that should cost marks. The model answer should model how to adjudicate between the "consistent extension" reading and the "betrayal of his own principles" reading rather than merely reporting both, and the marking scheme should make clear that a student who only reports Mill's stated justification has not answered the question.
