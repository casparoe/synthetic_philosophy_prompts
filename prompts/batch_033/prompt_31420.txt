I'm designing a Nomic-style game in which players start with a small set of rules and can, by voting, change any rule — including the rules that say how rules may be changed. A friend looked at my draft and argued that the whole enterprise is incoherent. His argument runs like this. (1) At any time, the validity of a rule-change is determined by the amendment rule in force at that time. (2) Suppose the amendment rule currently in force is Rule A: a proposal becomes a rule just in case it receives a two-thirds majority. (3) The players then vote, by exactly two-thirds, to replace Rule A with Rule B: a proposal becomes a rule just in case it receives a simple majority. (4) If that vote is valid, then after the vote Rule B is in force and Rule A is gone. (5) But then the vote's validity depends on a rule that, once the vote succeeds, no longer exists; the act is supposed to draw its validity from a rule that the act itself extinguishes. (6) So the vote cannot be valid: a valid change would destroy the very thing that made it valid. (7) Therefore Rule A cannot be changed at all, whatever its content happens to be; even a rule reading "amendments require only a simple majority" would be unchangeable by the same reasoning. (8) But an amendment rule whose content makes no difference to how it may be changed is not really governing its own amendment. So a game in which rules govern their own amendment is impossible.

I would most like to keep premise (1) and the thought that an amendment clause can genuinely govern its own replacement, since that is the feature I built the game to explore; my friend says the only way out is to ground the game's rules in something outside the game, such as a designer's authority the players cannot touch. I can't see which step to reject, and I want to know whether the flaw is in the reasoning itself or in one of the plausible premises I would have to give up.

Costs and alternatives: spell out what rejecting that step or premise costs, what account of self-amending rules results, and what it predicts about a further case I haven't considered — for example, an amendment rule that says it can never be amended (200–300 words). Verdict: give the bottom line on whether Nomic-style games are coherent, and which of my claims survive (75–100 words).
