I've noticed a pattern in conversations, especially in self-help spaces and online advice forums, and I'm wondering if it has a name. Someone expresses anxiety about a situation — say, a looming layoff, a health scare, a difficult relationship — and they get told: 'You can only control your own response, not what happens to you.' Which is fair enough as far as it goes. But I keep seeing the same thing happen next: the advice-giver treats the boundary between what's controllable and what isn't as obvious and fixed, when actually drawing that boundary is half the work. Like, is 'how my boss perceives my performance' in my control or not? Partly, but not entirely. The advice seems to function as a conversation-ender that lets the advice-giver feel wise without engaging the specifics. Is there a name for this — not the general idea that some things are in our control and some aren't (which I know goes back to the Stoics), but the specific rhetorical move of using that distinction to shut down further discussion about where the line falls? And if there isn't a name, what would you call it, and how would you distinguish it from the genuine philosophical principle?
