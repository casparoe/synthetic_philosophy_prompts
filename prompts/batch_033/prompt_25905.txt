so i'm running a session at my local rationalist meetup — maybe 20 people, 90 minutes, most have read the sequences or at least know the bayesian greatest hits. i want to do an activity on information cascades that makes people *feel* the pull of the cascade from the inside, not just nod when i explain the BHW model afterwards.

here's what i'm picturing: a sequential guessing game where everyone gets a private signal (like a card drawn from a deck) about whether the hidden state is A or B, and they announce their guess publicly one at a time. the private signals are informative but noisy — say, 70% accurate. the key thing is that after two or three people guess the same way, it becomes rational for later players to ignore their own signal and follow the crowd, and the whole group can end up confidently wrong together. i want people to experience that exact moment of "well my card says B but three people ahead of me all said A, so maybe my card is the unlucky one…" and then they go with A, and the next person sees *four* people saying A and it gets even harder to dissent.

what i need from you: concrete rules, materials (i can print cards or hand out slips, nothing fancy), a points system that doesn't require real money, and a debrief that walks through what happened in terms of the formal model. the debrief should map specific moments in the game onto concepts: public vs private information, the probability that a cascade is wrong even when everyone is updating rationally, why cascades are fragile (a single player with a strong signal or high confidence can shatter one), and the unsettling thing where nobody is being individually irrational but the group outcome is terrible. also, be upfront about which feature of real-world cascades the game *can't* reproduce and that i'll need to supply myself during the debrief.

also: what will predictably go wrong the first time i run this? i'm guessing some people will try to be clever contrarians and break the cascade on purpose, or someone won't understand their private signal, or the first few guesses come out split and no cascade forms at all. tell me how to handle each of those, and any other failure modes you forsee.

one hard constraint: no upfront lecture. the instructions should be minimal — just enough to play. they should discover the phenomenon and *then* we name it. if i have to explain what an information cascade is before we start, i've already lost.
