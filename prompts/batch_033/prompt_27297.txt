So I was reading this post on a friend's blog about Dennett's "Quining Qualia" paper, and the author gives what they call a "quick and dirty" summary of the Chase and Sanborn thought experiment. Here's how they put it:

"Dennett's 'Quining Qualia' is his famous argument that qualia — those raw feels of experience, the redness of red, the painfulness of pain — simply don't exist. His strategy, borrowed from Quine, is to show that the whole concept is hopelessly confused. The centerpiece is the Chase and Sanborn intuition pump. Two coffee tasters both used to like Maxwell House but now dislike it. Chase says his taste has gotten more sophisticated — he now detects flaws he used to miss. Sanborn says the coffee itself tastes different to him than it did; same standards, different experience. Dennett's point is that there's literally no fact of the matter about which one is right, because the supposed distinction between 'how things seem to you' and 'how you judge them' is an illusion. If you can't tell whether your qualia changed or your standards changed, then 'qualia' isn't doing any real explanatory work. Qualia are a philosopher's fiction — a pre-scientific intuition we should just drop."

The author then says Dennett is basically repackaging logical behaviorism and that the obvious reply — "but there IS something it's like to taste coffee!" — is a decisive refutation that Dennett never really addresses.

I've read some Dennett (Consciousness Explained, mostly) but I've never read the original "Quining Qualia" paper, and something about this summary feels off to me — like its making Dennett's position easier to knock down than it actually is. At the same time I know Dennett really does say some pretty extreme-sounding stuff about qualia, so maybe the summary is basically right and my gut reaction is just the usual resistance people have to eliminativism.

Can you walk me through what's accurate in this summary, what's subtly misrepresented, and whether the "but there's something it's like!" objection is actually the knockdown argument the blogger thinks it is?
