The setup, if you're not familiar: the digger wasp (Sphex) does this elaborate routine where it brings a paralyzed cricket to the entrance of its burrow, leaves it at the threshold, goes inside to inspect, comes back out, and drags the cricket in. If you move the cricket a few inches away while the wasp is inside, the wasp comes out, finds the cricket, and drags it back to the threshold—but then goes inside to inspect again. You can do this forty times and the wasp never learns to skip the inspection. It's this perfect image of mechanical rigidity masquerading as intelligent behavior. The term "sphexish" gets used to describe any behavior that looks flexible or thoughtful but is actually just a fixed program that falls apart when you perturb it.

My friend's position is that this is basically us. All of human behavior, no matter how creative or spontaneous it looks, is sphexish at bottom—just a vastly more complicated set of programs, but still deterministic, still rigid if you could see the right level of description. My position is that there's a difference that matters: the wasp cant notice that its routine has become pointless, but we can notice our own patterns and step back and revise them. It's not that we're non-deterministic, it's that we have the capacity for a certain kind of self-monitoring and flexibility that the wasp demonstrably doesn't.
