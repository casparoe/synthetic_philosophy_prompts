so i've got this cousin who went full moloch-pilled after reading some blogs and now he uses trap logic to explain literally everything. last week he told me my decision to recycle is pointless and here's the argument he gave:

(1) in any situation where cooperating benefits the group but defecting benefits the individual, absent enforcement, defection dominates.
(2) enforcement requires enforcers, who are themselves individual actors facing their own cooperate/defect calculus.
(3) so enforcers face a second-order trap: if they enforce honestly the group benefits, but if they take bribes or slack off they personally benefit.
(4) by (1), this second-order trap also collapses to defection unless there are meta-enforcers.
(5) this regress never bottoms out — at every level the enforcers face the same trap.
(6) so no stable cooperative norm is possible without an infinite regress of enforcement.
(7) but stable cooperative norms obviously exist (we mostly don't murder each other, contracts get honored, etc).
(8) therefore (1) is false — the original logic of the trap must be wrong somewhere.

he said this with a straight face and now i can't stop turning it over. i'm pretty sure the step from (4) to (5) to (6) does something sneaky, but i can't articulate what. also maybe (1) is true for some settings but not all, and the argument quietly assumes every setting is the same kind of game. which step should i reject here, and what do i actually have to give up if i reject it — does the whole moloch framework collapse or just get restricted?
