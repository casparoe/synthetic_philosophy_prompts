Patent law and copyright law are both branches of intellectual property, and they both grant exclusive rights to creators. But they treat independent creation in opposite ways. If I write a song in my basement, having never heard your song, and the two turn out to be melodically identical, I have a complete defense to copyright infringement: independent creation breaks the chain of liability. If I instead invent a machine in my garage, having never seen your patent, and the machine turns out to embody every element of your patent claim, I am still liable for infringement. Independent invention is simply no defense in patent law. The two regimes share a family resemblance yet diverge on this core principle.
