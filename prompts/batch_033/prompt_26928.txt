You are a professional epistemologist who has taught Nozick's tracking theory and its main rivals. I am making a handout for an epistemology seminar: a "Which position in the closure debate do you actually hold?" sorting quiz.

Write eight to ten questions, each a concrete case—lottery ticket, zebra at the zoo, fake-barn county, brain in a vat, a method that would have yielded the same belief even if false, a raised-stakes case—with two or three answer options committing the respondent to a verdict about whether the protagonist knows. Sort people by their verdicts on cases, not by which label they prefer, so no question may ask whether the respondent accepts closure or endorses sensitivity.

Build the instrument from the logical space, not from a list of names. The diagnostic axes are: whether knowledge requires sensitivity; whether knowledge is closed under known entailment; whether uneliminated but non-salient alternatives defeat knowledge; and whether the standards for "knows" shift with stakes. First determine which answer combinations your questions allow, then locate the named positions in that space: Nozick-style tracking, Dretske-style relevant alternatives, safety-based epistemology, and closure-first Moorean invariantism. For unoccupied combinations, say briefly whether the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of positions the quiz cannot cleanly separate.

Before drafting, brainstorm briefly which cases force the sharpest separations, but do not include that brainstorming in the handout. Keep the response under 1,200 words, with a scoring key usable by a student who has never encountered any of these labels.
