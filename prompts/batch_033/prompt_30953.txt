I recently retired from a long career in the natural sciences, where I was used to theories that specify what changes and what persists, and I have begun reading seriously in the philosophy of mind and artificial intelligence. The frame problem keeps surfacing, and I have gathered that it is not one problem: there is a narrow version about how a formal system is to represent the effects of an action without having to list every fact the action does not change, and a broader version about how any finite creature or machine manages to ignore almost everything it knows when deciding what to do. Before I spend more time on proposed solutions, I would like the desiderata laid out. What must a solution to the frame problem, in either or both of these senses, actually do to count as a solution? I want the requirements with the case or consideration behind each, and I want you to mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are only preferences of a particular research program. I would also like to know whether any pair of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, or has so far resisted being met together, since that seems to me the most informative thing a specification can tell me. Please separate what is settled in the literature from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Write in continuous prose, as in a letter or an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
