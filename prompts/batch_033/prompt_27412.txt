I'm a graduate student in philosophy of psychiatry, and I've been arguing with my advisor about Ian Hacking's concept of "looping effects" and what it actually implies for the future of diagnostic classification. As you likely know, Hacking's idea is that psychiatric categories are "interactive kinds": unlike indifferent kinds such as electrons, the people classified under a psychiatric label are aware of the classification, and that awareness changes their behavior and self-understanding, which in turn alters the meaning and extension of the category itself. The category loops. My advisor grants that this is philosophically important but insists it has no practical bite — the DSM revision process, she says, is driven by clinical utility and biological research, not by conceptual worries about self-fulfilling categories. I'm not so sure. It seems to me that some categories (I think of ADHD, dissociative identity disorder, perhaps borderline personality disorder) have been so thoroughly shaped by looping that the official classification system will eventually have to reckon with the phenomenon explicitly, if only to maintain its own credibility.
