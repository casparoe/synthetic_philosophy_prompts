I'm a novelist and I've got a situation that's been eating at me. My sister went through something really awful about five years ago — a drawn-out divorce with a genuinely cruel husband, some details I won't get into, but she's only really started to rebuild her life in the last year or so. She told me a lot of things during that time she's never told anyone else.

Here's the thing. I'm writing a novel right now and the central relationship in it is basically her marriage. Not the surface details — I've changed names, setting, professions, all that — but the emotional core is pulled directly from what she told me in confidence. And I think it's really good. Like, the best thing I've written. It has this rawness that I don't think I could of invented from scratch.

If I publish it, there's a real chance it helps people — readers who've been through something similar and feel alone, people who don't understand what that kind of marriage does to a person. I genuinely believe that. But I also know my sister would recognize herself if she read it, and it would feel like a betrayal. She trusted me, and I'm using her pain for my work.

I tried to think about this systematically. On one side: her hurt, our relationship, the trust she gave me. On the other side: the book's value to readers, my career, the fact that I've changed the material enough that no one else would know it's her. When I lay it out like that, the calculation seems to tilt toward publishing — the good to many readers outweighs the harm to one person. But every time I try to settle on that conclusion, I feel like I'm doing something wrong just by weighing it that way. Like the fact that I'm standing back and treating my sister's suffering as a cost and a benefit in a ledger is itself a kind of betrayal, regardless of what the ledger says.

Is there something off about how I'm thinking about this, or am I just uncomfortable with what the math points to?
