In my school philosophy club we have been discussing Molyneux's problem: whether a person born blind who could reliably tell a cube from a sphere by touch would, immediately after gaining sight, be able to say which was which before touching either. The question has been argued for over three centuries, and reports from newly sighted patients have not settled it; each side reads the same imperfect performance as supporting its own view. I am not asking for a summary of the positions or a verdict. I want a diagnosis of why the debate will not end. Is the disagreement fundamentally an empirical one about what newly restored sight can do, a conceptual one about whether touch and vision share a single underlying idea of shape, or a dispute about what should count as recognition in the first place? Give the strongest case for each reading, say which is correct and why, and then explain what would count as progress: a further experiment, a clearer distinction, or the conclusion that the question as posed is ill-formed. Please answer in British English.
