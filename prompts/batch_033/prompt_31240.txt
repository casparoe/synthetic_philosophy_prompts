I'm giving a seminar next month to a conservation biology department, and I want a Q&A prep sheet, not a polished defense. The thesis I'll defend is limited: that the welfare case against habitat destruction is much weaker than conservationists assume, because habitat loss reduces the number of wild animals and the lives it prevents are, on average, brief and painful — most die before maturity from starvation, predation, disease, or exposure — so the net effect on suffering may be neutral or positive. I am not arguing that we should pave habitat; I am arguing that the assumption that protecting habitat automatically protects animals' interests doesn't follow from the animals' own welfare. The audience is mostly conservation biologists and restoration ecologists who value intact ecosystems and slip easily between biodiversity and animal welfare; a few work on reintroductions and disease ecology, and at least one will say I'm handing developers a moral license. Give me the objections I'll actually get, in roughly the order they'll come and ordered by how likely and damaging each is, phrased the way this audience would phrase them, with a short reply to each and a note on where the reply is softest. Include the question I should be most afraid of. Write it as continuous prose, the way a colleague would email me back — no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables. At the end, tell me how confident you are in your read of this audience and which part of the prep sheet you're least sure about.
