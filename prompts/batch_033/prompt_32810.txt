I am drafting a position statement on rational belief for a workshop, and I should be grateful for a rigorous audit of whether the commitments below form a stable package before I circulate it.

(1) Threshold: an agent is rationally entitled to believe a proposition when, and only when, the probability of that proposition on the agent's evidence is above a threshold that lies strictly between one half and certainty.

(2) Conjunction: if an agent is rationally entitled to believe p and rationally entitled to believe q, then the agent is rationally entitled to believe p and q.

(3) Lottery: in a fair lottery with one million tickets and exactly one winning ticket, an agent is rationally entitled to believe of any particular ticket that it will lose, and rationally entitled to believe that some ticket will win; the agent is not rationally entitled to believe that every ticket will lose.

(4) Preface: an author who has written a long book and checked each claim with appropriate care is rationally entitled to believe each individual claim in the book, and is also rationally entitled to believe that at least one claim in the book is false; she is not rationally entitled to believe that every claim in the book is true.

(5) Consistency: an agent cannot be rationally entitled to believe every member of a set of propositions that are jointly inconsistent.

(6) Parity: the lottery case and the preface case are structurally on a par, and a correct theory of rational belief should diagnose them in the same way.

I cannot yet see where the strain lies, and I would rather not prejudge which commitment to revise. Please audit the package: which commitments are genuinely in tension, and which merely appear to be? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each specify what would minimally have to give and what the revision would cost. Please write the audit in British English.
