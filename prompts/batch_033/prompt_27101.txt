I am a retired natural scientist — my working life was spent in an experimental field — and I have come to philosophy of mind late. The idea that most interests me is Dennett's heterophenomenology: the proposal that a science of consciousness should treat subjects' reports of their experience as a text to be interpreted, rather than as authoritative descriptions of an inner phenomenal world. If I were to devote the next several years to working through one strand of this debate, which open question about heterophenomenology should I prioritize, and why? I want a defended ranking rather than a survey. Please identify the main open questions — for instance, whether the method is genuinely neutral among theories, whether its bracketing of the subject's authority is stable, what it can and cannot recover about phenomenal character, and how it should handle reports of ineffability or of absent experience — and order them by their importance for empirical consciousness research, their tractability for someone with a scientific background but no graduate training in philosophy, and the number of downstream questions an answer would settle. Say explicitly what trade-offs you are making among those criteria, and what new evidence or argument over the next few years would most change the ordering.
