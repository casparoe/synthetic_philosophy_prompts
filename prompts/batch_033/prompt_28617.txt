I keep noticing a pattern in my own thinking and I'm not sure if there's a name for it in the literature, or if it's just a me-problem.

Here's the situation. I'll work through some complicated question — say, whether to switch jobs, or what I think about some political controversy. I'll sit with it for a while, weigh the pros and cons, follow the arguments where they lead, and I'll end up pretty convinced of some conclusion. The reasoning feels solid at the time.

But then I'll notice: I did all that reasoning while I was, for instance, running on four hours of sleep. Or while I was unusually anxious about something unrelated. Or right after reading something I know tends to bias people on this exact issue. And I have independent evidence that people in those states tend to reason worse. So now I have this meta-level worry: my reasoning might not be trustworty.

The problem is, what do I do with that worry? If I try to "discount" my conclusion because I was tired, I have to reason about how much to discount it, and I'm still tired while doing that reasoning. There's no clean, unimpaired version of me I can hand the question off to. And if I wait until I'm well-rested and then re-evaluate, and I reach the same conclusion — great, but can I trust the re-evaluation, or am I just rationalizing something I'm already attached to?

Relatedly: suppose I conclude that yes, I should lower my confidence. By how much? It feels arbitrary. I can't run a controlled experiment on myself.

I've come across "epistemic akrasia" and "higher-order evidence," but neither seems to fit perfectly. Epistemic akrasia, as I understand it, is believing P while also believing your evidence doesn't support P — but I don't disbelieve my evidence supports my conclusion; I just worry my assessment of the evidence was compromised. And higher-order evidence is close, but it seems like a broader category, and I'm not sure the literature on it addresses this specific loop where the higher-order evidence calls your reasoning into question but you can only process it using that same reasoning.

So: is there an established concept that captures this exact knot — the one where you can't step outside your own cognitive state to evaluate whether that state is distorting your reasoning? And if there isn't, how should I think about what's going on here? I care more about getting the boundaries right than getting a label.
