A Schopenhauerian about music and a Hanslickian formalist have spent a year in good-faith adversarial collaboration. The Schopenhauerian holds that music is unique among the arts: where painting, poetry, and sculpture represent the Platonic Ideas—the objectifications of the Will at various grades—music bypasses the Ideas entirely and is a direct copy of the Will itself, which is why it moves us so immediately and deeply, without the mediation of concepts or images. The formalist holds, with Hanslick, that music's content and beauty consist entirely in its tonal forms—its melodies, harmonies, and rhythms—and that music has no extra-musical content; its so-called emotional effects are either caused by the formal properties themselves or are associations imported by the listener.

Draft the joint statement they would produce. I want a numbered list of claims both can sign, stated precisely enough that neither side feels the wording smuggles in the other's assumptions. Then a disagreements table: for each point of disagreement, give the Schopenhauerian's formulation of their own view, the formalist's formulation of their own view, and a neutral statement of what is at stake. Then identify the cruxes—the sub-questions on which at least one side would change their view if the evidence went a certain way. For each crux, describe a concrete case, experiment, or argument that both would accept bears on it, and record each side's prediction about what it would show.

Be committed: where you think part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why; where you think one side genuinely has the better of an argument, say so and give your reasons. The value of the document is in how precisely it locates the remaining disagreement, not in pretending the disagreement is smaller than it is.
