Write an essay on the following question. In Anarchy, State, and Utopia, Robert Nozick considers a hybrid moral position he labels "utilitarianism for animals, Kantianism for people": the interests of nonhuman animals are handled within a broadly utilitarian, maximizing framework, while human beings are protected by stringent deontological side constraints. Assess whether this hybrid is defensible. Focus especially on the boundary the view must draw between utilitarian patients and side-constraint-protected persons: does that boundary survive pressure from marginal humans and from animals whose capacities overlap with those of some humans, and can any non-arbitrary feature justify placing an individual on one side rather than the other? Weigh the strongest arguments for and against the hybrid, and close by defending a determinate conclusion.
