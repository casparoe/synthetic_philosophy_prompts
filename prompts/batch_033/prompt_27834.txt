I’ve been going round in circles on Locke’s prince and cobbler passage from the Essay Concerning Human Understanding (Book II, ch. 27, sec. 15), because people in discussions of mind uploading keep treating it as an early version of the view that what matters for survival is psychological continuity, not the body. Here’s the passage:

“For should the soul of a prince, carrying with it the consciousness of the prince’s past life, enter and inform the body of a cobbler, as soon as deserted by his own soul, every one sees he would be the same person with the prince, accountable only for the prince’s actions; but who would say it was the same man? The body, too, goes to the making the man, and would, I guess, to every body determine the man in this case; wherein the soul with all its princely thoughts about it, would not make another man: but he would be the same cobbler to every one besides himself.”

I’m not sure I actually follow what Locke is doing here. He says everyone sees the resulting person would be the same person as the prince and accountable only for the prince’s actions, but then he says no one would say it was the same man. So what work is the distinction between “same person” and “same man” doing? Is this just a verbal distinction, or is he actually arguing that the person goes where the consciousness goes while the man stays with the body? And if the underlying view is that personal identity consists in sameness of consciousness, does that actually support the idea that a gradual replacement of someone’s brain, neuron by neuron, with functionally equivalent artificial parts, would preserve the same person as long as consciousness and memory are preserved? Or would Locke’s example not settle that because it’s a one-off transfer of a soul carrying consciousness, whereas gradual replacement has no moment where a completed consciousness jumps to a new substrate? I’d like help pulling apart what the passage commits him to and what it leaves open, because I want to use it in a paper on uploads without overreading it.
