I am a graduate student in evolutionary biology, and I keep running into the same move in public debates about evolution. Someone on the creationist side will cite Karl Popper to the effect that Darwinism is not a genuine scientific theory but a "metaphysical research programme." The line they usually invoke, or paraphrase, is this one from Popper's 1976 autobiography:

"I have come to the conclusion that Darwinism is not a testable scientific theory, but a metaphysical research programme—a possible framework for testable scientific theories."

On the other side, biologists and philosophers of science almost always reply with a single fact: Popper recanted. They quote his Darwin Lecture, where he said:

"I have changed my mind about the testability and logical status of the theory of natural selection; and I am glad to have an opportunity to make a recantation."

And that is usually treated as the end of the matter. But I have read enough of the surrounding texts to suspect that neither side is being precise about what Popper was actually claiming, what he later took back, and what he did not. The word "Darwinism" alone is doing a lot of work—it could mean the historical fact of common descent, the mechanism of natural selection, the claim that natural selection is the primary driver of adaptation, or something else entirely. And "metaphysical research programme" is a term of art in Popper's system that does not simply mean "unscientific nonsense."

I want to go beyond the bumper-sticker exchange. Take Popper's original claim that Darwinism is a metaphysical research programme, and lay out the distinct precise theses that this slogan could be asserting. For each candidate thesis, tell me: (a) whether it is plausible on its own terms; (b) whether it is something Popper actually held, given his broader philosophy of science; and (c) whether it is among the things he recanted in 1978. Then, with that menu on the table, tell me which reading best captures what Popper probably meant when he first made the claim, and which reading the creationist who cites it today is most likely trading on. Finally, say whether the standard rebuttal—"he recanted"—is fully responsive to the use the slogan is being put to, or whether something important survives the recantation that the rebuttal misses.

Please write the answer in American English.
