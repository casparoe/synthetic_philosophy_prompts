I'm preparing a paper on Parfit's teletransporter and the question of what matters in survival, and I'd like a consistency check on the set of views I've arrived at before I commit to them in print. Here is where I currently stand:

(1) I accept Parfit's central claim that what matters in survival is psychological continuity and connectedness — what he calls Relation R — rather than numerical identity. Identity is a one-one relation that does not capture what we actually care about when we anticipate our own future.

(2) In the standard teletransporter case — my body is scanned, destroyed in the process, and a molecule-for-molecule replica with all my psychological states is assembled on Mars — I survive. The person on Mars is me in the way that matters.

(3) In the fission case — my brain is divided and each hemisphere (both functionally equivalent) is transplanted into a new body, producing two people each psychologically continuous with me — I do not survive. Neither resulting person is me, and I do not survive as both.

(4) Survival in the "what matters" sense must be a one-one relation. It cannot be the case that I survive as two numerically distinct people.

(5) In the branch-line case — I am scanned as in the teletransporter, a replica is created on Mars, but the scanner does not destroy my original body; however, it emerges that my original body has suffered fatal damage during the scan and I will die within a few days — I survive as the replica on Mars, and the original is also me during his remaining days. There are temporarily two of me.

(6) The question "did I survive?" should always admit of a determinate answer. Survival is not a matter of degree.

I can feel that some of these are pulling against each other, but I'm not sure which ones or how seriously. I'd like you to go through these six commitments and identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension but aren't on closer inspection, and which single revision would do the most to restore consistency. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally problematic.
