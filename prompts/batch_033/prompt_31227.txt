I teach a grad epistemology seminar and I want to give them an adversarial-collaboration document on the Meno problem — why is knowledge better than mere true belief? The two camps: (A) a virtue epistemologist who says knowledge = true belief creditable to intellectual competence, and its distinctive value comes from being an achievement (achievements are finally valuable, not just instrumentally); (B) a reliabilist who says knowledge = true belief from a reliable process, and the extra value is purely instrumental — a reliable process is a good thing to have because it yields more true beliefs, but there's nothing intrinsically better about a reliably-formed true belief than a lucky guess that happens to be right.

I want you to act as an expert in contemporary epistemology and write the actual document they'd produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Please think through the positions carefully — maybe brainstorm the points of agreement and disagreement before writing. The document should have:

(1) A numbered list of claims both can sign. Don't make these vacuous; find the real substantive overlap.

(2) A disagreements table with precise formulations of where they part ways, worded so each side would accept how its own view is stated.

(3) The cruxes — sub-questions such that answering them would shift at least one camp.

(4) For each crux, a concrete case or test both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded.

If you think any part of their dispute is merely verbal, say which and why. And dont pull punches — if one side has to swallow something uncomfortable to sign a claim, note the tension.
