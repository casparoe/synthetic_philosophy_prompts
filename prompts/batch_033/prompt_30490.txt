Compare a Ulysses contract, in which an agent binds his or her own future self, with constitutional precommitment, in which a polity binds its future decision-makers. Identify the respects in which constitutional precommitment is not simply a Ulysses contract scaled up, and assess whether those disanalogies matter normatively. In particular, address the fact that Ulysses binds only himself, whereas constitutional provisions bind successive generations who never consented to the constraint. Does this difference undermine the standard precommitment rationale for constitutionalism, or can that rationale be reformulated so as to survive it? Justify your answer.
