Over the last month my flatmate and I keep having the same row, and everytime he ends it with 'liberty is just the absence of interference.' The argument is about whether someone on a zero-hours contract is free: I say that if you have to take whatever shift is offered because you can't afford to say no, then you aren't free in any interesting sense, and he says nobody is coercing you, you signed the contract, so you're free. The line sounds like a definition, but it can't just be a definition, because if 'interference' means anything that affects your options then no one is ever free, and if it only means being physically restrained then almost everyone is always free.

I want to pin down what he might be claiming before we have this row again. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that slogan could be expressing — what counts as interference, whose interference counts, whether threats or penalties count, whether a lack of realistic options counts — and for each reading say whether it's a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or just false? And then tell me which of those readings would actually support his conclusion that the zero-hours worker is free, and which would undermine it. A menu with verdicts rather than one long essay would be ideal.
