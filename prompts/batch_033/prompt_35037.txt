I'm a PhD student in philosophy working at the intersection of emotion theory and philosophy of mind. I'm writing a dissertation chapter on the dispute between judgment theories of emotion and perceptual theories, and I want to clarify where the two views genuinely come apart rather than offering different descriptions of the same phenomenon. Please write a dialogue between a judgment theorist and a perceptual theorist, both equally sophisticated, who are trying to identify the exact point of disagreement between them. They should focus on a single case: feeling fear while watching a horror film that one knows to be fictional. The judgment theorist must explain why the fear persists despite the absence of the relevant evaluative judgment (one does not judge the monster to be dangerous), and the perceptual theorist must explain what makes the fear about the monster rather than about one's own bodily state. Each should grant the other's strongest point before pushing back, and there should be at least three genuine exchanges where each responds to what the other just said. End with each character stating what kind of empirical finding would count as evidence against their own view.
