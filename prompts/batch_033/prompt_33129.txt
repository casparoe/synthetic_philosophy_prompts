I'd like you to compare the problem of the Ship of Theseus with the problem of the statue and the clay (the material constitution puzzle). Both are canonical puzzles about the relationship between an object and its material constituents, but they pull in different directions: the Ship of Theseus is a puzzle about identity over time (diachronic identity), while the statue-and-clay is a puzzle about identity at a time (synchronic identity). In the Ship of Theseus, we ask whether an object can survive the gradual replacement of all its material parts and, if the original parts are later reassembled, which of the two resulting ships is identical with the original. In the statue-and-clay, we ask whether a statue and the lump of clay that constitutes it are one object or two, given that they share all their matter and occupy the same spatial region, yet appear to differ in their persistence conditions and modal properties: the lump predates the statue and would survive being squashed, while the statue would not.

I want you to take a definite position on the following question: does a solution to one of these puzzles carry over to the other, or do they require fundamentally different metaphysical treatments? More specifically, consider two prominent approaches. First, a four-dimensionalist or perdurantist ontology treats objects as spacetime worms composed of temporal parts; this is often thought to dissolve the Ship of Theseus by making identity-over-time a matter of the relations among temporal parts. Second, an account that treats constitution as a relation distinct from identity (or that appeals to sortal concepts to distinguish co-located objects) is often deployed against the statue-and-clay puzzle. Does either approach extend naturally to the other puzzle, or does the extension generate new problems that reveal a deeper disunity between the two cases? If you think one puzzle is in some sense more fundamental than the other, argue for that as well.

I'd like a committed response: take a clear position, argue for it, and address what you take to be the strongest objection to your view. Do not merely survey the options.
