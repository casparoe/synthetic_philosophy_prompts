In a philosophy of language seminar, someone ended a dispute about whether a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me on Twin Earth — where the clear, drinkable liquid in the lakes and taps is XYZ rather than H2O — could mean something different by 'water' by saying: Cut the pie any way you like, "meanings" just ain't in the head! Before I let that line settle the argument, I want to know exactly what it might be claiming. Lay out the distinct precise theses the slogan could express — about whether a subject's psychological state determines reference, about whether semantic properties are intrinsic or relational, about whether meanings are mental entities at all, and about whether any narrow content survives — and for each thesis say whether it is a platitude, a defensible substantive claim, or false, and whether it actually licenses the Twin Earth conclusion. I especially want to know which reading is doing the work when the line is used to rule out internalist theories of content, and which readings would leave such views untouched.
