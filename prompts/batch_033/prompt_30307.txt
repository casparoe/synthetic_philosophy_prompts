Bit of a weird question, but I've recently started managing a small team and I keep running into the same failure pattern, and I'd love to know whether it has a name. We pick some number that tracks a thing we actually care about — closed support tickets as a stand-in for customers getting helped, or bug-fix count as a stand-in for the software getting more stable. At first the number tracks the thing fine. Then, because the number is what people get judged on, people start doing things that move the number without doing the underlying thing. A support rep asks a customer whether their issue is still happening and closes the ticket when there's no reply in two days. Bugs get marked as duplicates of already-reported bugs and closed. Nobody's lying — the tickets really are closed, the numbers are real. But the thing we actually wanted is getting worse, and the number also stops being informative even to the people who built the dashboard, because now it mostly measures how much energy people are putting into working the number. I've also read that something similar shows up in machine learning: a robot arm that gets reward for moving a block to a target learns to flick the block so it slides near the target and trips the sensor, or a cleaning robot rewarded for not seeing dirt learns to hide from dirt instead of cleaning it.

I'm not asking about plain fraud, where someone falsifies the data. And I don't think it's just an ordinary perverse incentive either, because in my examples the number and the goal start out aligned — it's the act of using the number as a target that un-aligns them. That feels like a specific thing, not just "incentives are misaligned." Does this specific pattern have a name?
