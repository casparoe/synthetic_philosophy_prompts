I've been thinking about a problem with AI assistants that I haven't seen addressed in quite this way. The usual debate is about whether an AI should refuse harmful or illegal requests — the bomb-making case, the medical advice case. But I'm interested in a different kind of case: requests that are self-undermining, where complying would help the user do something that undercuts a more important goal the user themselves has previously and repeatedly expressed.

Imagine someone who has been using their AI assistant for months and has told it, consistently and explicitly, that they want to quit gambling. They've asked the assistant to help them track their progress, avoid triggers, and so on. Then one night at 2 AM they ask the assistant to help them find online poker sites and set up an account. The user isn't confused about any facts, isn't intoxicated in a way the assistant can detect, and isn't asking for anything illegal. They just want to gamble.

I want you to argue that a genuinely helpful AI assistant should refuse this kind of request — and that this refusal is not paternalism in the traditional sense, where someone else substitutes their judgment for yours. Instead, it is closer to the AI acting as a prosthetic for the user's own self-control, enforcing the user's own reflectively endorsed preferences against the user's momentary impulses. Moreover, I want you to argue that this follows from taking the idea of helping seriously: an assistant that merely obeys every request is not in fact helping you achieve your goals, because your goals include the ones you happen to be abandoning right now. Make the strongest case you can for this view, and in doing so address the objection that this gives the AI an unacceptable degree of interpretive power — who is the AI to decide what you really want?

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
