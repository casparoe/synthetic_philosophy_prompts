I'm a retired atmospheric chemist, and after forty years of building models I knew were false but still better than the models they replaced, I've fallen hard into philosophy of science. The notion I keep returning to is verisimilitude — Popper's idea that a false theory can be closer to the truth than another false theory. I always took that for granted in the lab; you replace a crude model with a less crude one and you call it progress. Then I worked through the Tichý–Miller argument and now I can't find my way back out. It runs like this.

(1) If theory A is closer to the truth than theory B, it has to be because A gets at least as much truth and no more falsehood. That's Popper's definition: A's truth content properly includes B's, and B's falsity content properly includes A's.

(2) But take any two false theories A and B. Suppose A's truth content properly includes B's. Then there's a true sentence a that A entails but B doesn't. Since A is false, it entails some falsehood f. So A entails a&f, and a&f is false. B can't entail a&f, since that would give it a. So A's falsity content contains a falsehood B's doesn't — which means B's falsity content can't include A's, as the definition requires. Now run it the other way: suppose B's falsity content properly includes A's. Take a falsehood g that B entails but A doesn't, and any falsehood f that A entails. The conditional f→g is true (f is false), and since g entails it, B entails it. But if A entailed f→g too, then from f and f→g A would get g, which it doesn't. So B's truth content contains a truth A's doesn't — which means A's truth content can't include B's, as the definition requires.

(3) So on Popper's definition no false theory is ever closer to the truth than another false theory. The two inclusion conditions can't both be satisfied.

(4) And Popper's definition was supposed to be the natural way to spell out "closer to the truth" in terms of content. If the natural spelling collapses, what's left? Either "Newton is closer to the truth than Aristotle" means nothing, or it means something that Popper's way of cashing it out can't reach.

I refuse to beleive the Newton sentence is empty. I spent a career trading up from cruder to less crude models and I can't shake the sense that something real was improving. But I can't locate the bad step. Which premise should I give up, and what does giving it up cost? In particular, if the cost is admitting that "closer to the truth" can't be built from truth content and falsity content, what exactly is left of the idea?
