So a friend and I just spent the whole evening going back and forth about whether beliefs and desires actually do anything, and he kept reaching for that careenium picture — Hofstadter's billiard table with the little magnetic balls (the simms) and the clumps they form (the simmballs). His whole position came down to "the symbols push the substrate around." As in: the higher-level patterns, the simmballs, are the ones doing the real causal pushing, not just the individual simms bouncing around underneath. He used that to brush off my worry that if the particle level is causally closed, then our thoughts are just riding along for free, epiphenomenally.

But the more we argued, the more obvious it got that neither of us could actually say what that one sentence amounts to. Does it mean the simmballs exert some kind of extra force over and above the simms, like genuine top-down causation that the physics of the table would have to include? Or is it just the boring claim that you can describe the same particle motions at a higher level, and the higher-level description is the one we happen to find useful? Or is it something about multiple realizability — like the same simmball can be made of different simms, so the causal work is really being done at the clump level? Those all feel different, and they can't all be what he meant.

Can you lay out the distinct precise claims "the symbols push the substrate around" could be making — from the trivial to the radical — and say for each whether it's plausible, and most importantly whether it actually answers my epiphenomenalism worry or just changes the subject? I want a menu I can bring back to him, because right now I'm not even sure were disagreeing about the same thing.
