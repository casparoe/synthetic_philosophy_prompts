ok so I've been reading Thinking Fast and Slow for this book report thing (well, parts of it, it's realy long) and the whole inside view vs outside view thing really got to me. Like the story about the curriculum textbook where they asked everyone how long it would take and the inside view said 2 years but the actual base rate for similar projects was 7-10 years and they still didn't finish until year 8. That's such a cool example.

But I kept thinking about it and I ended up with this argument that seems to show the outside view doesn't actually work, which is kind of a problem since it's supposed to be the whole fix for the planning fallacy. My friend and I arged about this for like an hour and we couldn't figure out who was right. Here's the argument:

(1) The outside view tells you to set aside the specifics of your own project and instead look at the base rate: how did similar projects turn out, how long did they take, what fraction failed, etc.

(2) But to do that, you have to first decide which projects count as "similar" to yours. You need a reference class.

(3) The only way to pick a reference class is to look at the features of your own project — its scope, the people involved, the resources, the domain — and judge which other projects resemble it along the relevant dimensions. That's the inside view.

(4) So the outside view can't get off the ground without the inside view already doing the work of picking a reference class.

(5) And if the inside view is what picks the reference class, then the same optimism bias that infects the original plan is going to infect the choice of reference class. You'll pick a class that makes your project look more achievable than it really is.

(6) So the outside view doesn't actually bypass the bias — it just moves it to the step where you choose what to compare yourself to.

(7) If that's true, then the whole inside/outside distinction is way less useful than Kahneman makes it sound, because the "outside view" is really just the inside view in a trenchcoat.

I feel like step (3) is where this might be wrong but I can't articulate exactly why. Like maybe there's a way to pick reference classes that doesn't depend on the inside view? Or maybe the outside view isn't supposed to eliminate the inside view completely, it's just supposed to add a check? But then what does the check actually check if you can rig the reference class?

So yeah, which premise do I reject, and what does rejecting it do to the rest of the framework?
