I help run a casual philosophy discussion circle that meets in a coworking space every few weeks. We're not academics — a couple of us work in software, one is a retired high-school teacher, another is a paramedic — but we've been reading our way through some of Hofstadter's work, and next session I'm supposed to lead a discussion on the Careenium thought experiment from I Am a Strange Loop. The core question, as I understand it, is whether the higher-level patterns — the simmballs, the symbols — can be said to push the physical substrate around, or whether all the pushing is strictly bottom-up, with the simmballs being nothing more than shadows cast by the symms. In other words: do symbols have any causal traction on the stuff that realizes them, or is talk of symbols pushing neurons around just a convenient shorthand we've mistaken for explanation?

I've gone over the chapter enough times that I think I have the thought experiment straight in my own head, but I'm less confident about how to structure the session so that the group actually wrestles with the hard version of the question rather than spending the whole time clarifying what a simmball is or, worse, settling into a comfortable position too early and then just defending it. The session is seventy-five minutes. I don't want you to explain the Careenium to me — I want a plan for how I should explain it to them. What idea has to be in place before I even describe the billiard table and the magnetic symms? What's the one example or analogy I should use to carry the whole thing, and what does it need to have and what does it need to lack? What misconceptions should I expect the group to form — I'm guessing one is that the simmballs are merely useful fictions with no reality at all, and another is that granting causal power to higher-level patterns commits you to something spooky or dualistic — and at exactly what point in the sequence should I head each one off? What's a question I can ask near the end that would tell me whether they've understood the issue rather than just repeated the thought experiment back to me?
