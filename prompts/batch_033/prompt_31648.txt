Okay so I've just had this massive arguement with my mate down the pub about Scanlon's contractualism and the aggregation problem. He kept saying that if you can save one person or five people you obviously save the five, end of. And I was trying to explain that for Scanlon the real question is wether you can justify what you do to each person individually, and that just adding up the numbers doesn't automatically give you the right kind of reason. We went round in circles for ages and neither of us budged, but it left me thinking this would make a really good piece of fiction. Could you write a short story about a character who has to make a decision where aggregating across many people pulls one way, and the need to justify the decision to each individual pulls the other? I'd like it to be genuinely a story, with a specific person and a specific situation, not a disguised essay. And don't resolve the tension neatly at the end — let it stay uncomfortable.
