Write a dialogue between two equally sophisticated decision theorists, one committed to causal decision theory (CDT) and one committed to functional decision theory (FDT), who are arguing about whether to pay in the following scenario.

Omega — a perfect predictor of your behavior — has just explained the situation: it tossed a fair coin. The coin came up tails, so Omega is now asking you for $100. Omega adds that if the coin had come up heads, it would have paid you $10,000, but only if it had predicted that you would have paid the $100 in the tails case. The prediction was made before the coin toss, based on Omega's perfect model of your decision procedure. The coin has already landed; nothing you do now can causally influence whether it landed heads or tails, nor can it causally influence the $10,000 that was or was not placed in the heads branch.

The causal decision theorist holds that a rational agent should choose the act with the best expected causal consequences — evaluating acts by intervening on them in a causal model and tracing downstream effects. The functional decision theorist holds that a rational agent should choose among possible decision functions (or policies) by computing the expected payoff of instantiating that function, taking into account all cases in which a predictor's output is logically correlated with the function rather than causally downstream of the act.

The dialogue should contain genuine back-and-forth: each character should respond to the other's actual points, and neither should be a strawman. At some stage, have the causal decision theorist introduce the XOR blackmail case as a challenge to the functional decision theorist. In that case, a perfect predictor sends you a message stating that it has sent the message just in case exactly one of the following is true: your house is infested with termites (which would cost you $1,000,000 in damage) or you pay it $1,000. The functional decision theorist should respond to this challenge and explain why their view treats the two cases differently.

After the dialogue, provide a JSON object with the following fields:

- "cdt_position": a 2–3 sentence summary of the causal decision theorist's position and core rationale.
- "fdt_position": a 2–3 sentence summary of the functional decision theorist's position and core rationale.
- "central_disagreement": a 1–2 sentence statement of the exact point on which the two part ways.
- "strongest_point_for_paying": the single strongest argument made in the dialogue for paying the $100.
- "strongest_point_against_paying": the single strongest argument made in the dialogue against paying the $100.
- "xor_blackmail_role": a 1–2 sentence account of how the XOR blackmail case was deployed in the exchange and whether it succeeded as an objection.
- "unresolved_issue": the main point left unsettled at the end of the dialogue, or "none" if the dispute was fully resolved.
- "steelman_assessment": a 1–2 sentence judgment of which side had the stronger case in the dialogue as written, and why.
