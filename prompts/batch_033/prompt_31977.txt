Experimental philosophy has been investigating the Knobe effect for over twenty years now. In the classic scenario, a chairman approves a profit-maximizing program knowing it will harm the environment, says he does not care about the environment, and most people judge that he harmed the environment intentionally. But when the word "harm" is replaced with "help" — the chairman is equally indifferent — most people judge that he did not help the environment intentionally. This asymmetry is robust: it has been replicated across cultures, age groups, and numerous scenario variations, and it launched a research program that now spans hundreds of experiments.

What remains unsettled is why the asymmetry occurs. Broadly, one camp treats it as evidence that moral judgment infiltrates our very concept of intentional action — roughly Knobe's original interpretation. Another camp argues that the asymmetry is driven by non-moral features that merely happen to covary with bad outcomes: norm violations, inferences about an agent's "deep self" or values, or the counterfactual reasoning people engage in when something goes wrong. There are further subdivisions, but the fundamental divide — is moral judgment doing the work, or is something else doing the work that only tracks moral valence in these scenarios? — has barely moved in two decades.

This looks like a case where everyone has the same data and no one changes their mind. I want a diagnosis of why this debate has stalled. Is it a verbal dispute about the word "intentionally"? Is it a disagreement about what a concept is and what counts as evidence about one? Are the two camps answering different questions — one about the psychology of judgment, the other about the semantics of the term — under a single heading? Or is there a hidden empirical question that, if someone could design the right experiment, would settle it? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress?

Please separate what is settled in this literature from what is genuinely contested among philosophers working on it, and avoid presenting a contested interpretation as if it were the consensus.
