ok so ive been going down this rabbit hole about when a bunch of things actually count as one thing. like take a hammer — the handle and the head are two things, but together theyre also one thing (the hammer). but if i put my coffee mug next to my phone on the desk, thats not a new thing, thats just two things near each other. so whats the rule? when do some parts actually compose a whole and when are they just... parts?

from what ive pieced together reading around online theres a few main answers people give:

- nihilism: parts never compose a whole. there are no tables or chairs or hammers, just particles arranged table-wise or whatever. composite objects dont really exist.
- universalism: parts always compose a whole. any random collection of stuff — your left earlobe, the moon, and my shoelace — is an object. its just that most of these objects are scattered and boring so we never talk about them.
- the life view (i think its called organicism?): parts compose a whole only when theyre caught up in a life — so a cat is a real thing but a chair isnt. something about living organisms being the only genuine composites.
- common sense / contact view: parts compose a whole when theyre stuck together or fastened in the right way. so a hammer counts, a pile of sand doesnt, a house counts even though its just a bunch of bricks arranged house-wise.
- brutal composition: there IS a fact of the matter about when composition happens but we cant give an informative answer — its just a brute fact that varies case by case. kind of a giving up answer but maybe honest?

could you rank these from most to least plausible and give a sentence or two for each about why it lands where it does? im less interested in which one is "correct" in some technical sense and more in which one actually makes the most sense if youre just trying to think clearly about what exists. like if you had to bet your own intuitions, how would you order these?
