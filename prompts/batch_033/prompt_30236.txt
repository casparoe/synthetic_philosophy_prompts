ok here's where i've landed on originals and forgeries and i want to know if the whole set hangs together before i put it in a paper. (1) a perfect forgery of a painting is perceptually indistinguishable from the original, so the two are on a par in there aesthetic properties. (2) the value of a painting as a work of art is entirely a matter of its aesthetic properties. (3) but i still think there's good reason to prefer seeing the original rather than the perfect forgery, and that this isn't just market fetishism or snobbery. (4) i don't have the same instinct about a novel or a symphony: a faithful copy of the text or the score give you the whole work, and calling a copy a forgery seems beside the point. (5) i'm also tempted to say that the circumstances in which a painting was made are part of what it is as a work, even when i couldn't tell from looking at it alone. go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest fix is. if something has to go, which single revision does the least damage to the rest?
