So in the last couple weeks I've had this sentence thrown at me in two different arguments, and I cant tell if its the same claim or two totally different ones. First, a friend defending the idea that philosophical zombies are possible (and that physicalism is therefore false) said something like: if you can conceive of a molecule-for-molecule copy of a person with no inner life, then such a creature is possible. When I pushed back he just repeated 'conceivability is a guide to possibility' like that settles it. Then, in a totally different conversation, someone said you can't conceive of a world where the laws of logic fail, and used that to argue that no such world is possible. Same slogan, opposite jobs: one moves from can-conceive to possible, the other from can't-conceive to impossible.

I want to know what that slogan could actually be claiming. What are the different precise principles 'conceivability is a guide to possibility' might stand for -- e.g. what kind of conceiving (imagining? picturing? not seeing a contradiction? having a perfectly clear idea?), what kind of possibility (logical? metaphysical? physical? epistemic?), and is it a rule of thumb, a definition, or a claim about how the mind connects to modal reality? Lay the readings out as a menu from the trivial to the radical, say which are platitudes, which are substantive and maybe defensible, and which are just false. And then tell me which reading each of my two friends actually needed for their argument, and wether that reading is one of the plausible ones or not.
