I'm on my city's long-range planning and ethics board, and we've got a new proposal to vote on. Our past decisions: we rejected a chemical plant because it would raise asthma rates in a nearby town even though it would bring jobs; we rejected a plan to defer maintenance on a dam because a failure decades later could kill the families who live downstream; we approved a buyout program for flood-prone homes because it made future residents safer and current owners were compensated; and we rejected a cheaper refrigerant that would slowly leak and sicken workers and neighbors. The new proposal would lock in a cheaper gas-based energy mix now. It won't hurt anyone alive today, but it will make life noticeably worse for the people living 200 years from now—still worth living, but worse than it would of been under the cleaner alternative. The catch: our analysts say the choice between the two plans will itself change who gets born over those two centuries, so the people who'd live under the cheaper plan wouldn't have existed under the cleaner one. What should we decide if we want to be consistent with ourselves? Which precedent is the closest analog, and if our precedents underdetermine it, what principle are we adopting with each choice?
