I serve on my country's pandemic preparedness advisory committee. We must issue a recommendation by Friday on which of two mutually exclusive antiviral stockpile strategies to fund. Strategy A: a broad-spectrum prophylactic that, deployed early in a future pandemic, would reduce each person's infection fatality risk by an estimated 0.015% — preventing roughly 5,000 deaths across the population, though no individual averted death can be identified. Strategy B: a therapeutic reserved for patients who develop a rare but otherwise-always-fatal secondary complication; it would fully cure the estimated 350 patients per pandemic who would develop it — identifiable, nameable patients whose lives depend on the drug being available. Our statutory mandate is to maximize population health, but several committee members argue that aggregating tiny risk reductions across millions of people to justify preferring Strategy A treats persons as mere loci of value rather than as separate individuals whose claims must be considered one by one. Draft the committee's recommendation memo. It must state a clear actionable recommendation and must engage seriously with the strongest argument for the option you recommend against.
