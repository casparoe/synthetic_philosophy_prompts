I'm on a hiring committee at work and we're deadlocked between two candidates. I genuinely think Candidate A is the better hire — she'd bring a perspective our team really lacks. My colleague genuinely thinks Candidate B is better — her technical chops are stronger. Yesterday he pulled me aside and proposed a deal: he'll back A this round if I back B next round (we're hiring again next quarter). His pitch was basically: we each get the outcome we think is right once, instead of both being overruled both times. On paper it seems like a clean compromise.

But I can't shake the feeling that something's wrong here. Voting for B next round would mean voting for someone I believe is the worse candidate, even if it's just once. It feels like I'd be treating my judgment about what's best for the team as a bargaining chip rather than, well, a judgment. At the same time, I can't point to who's harmed by this — the candidates don't know about the deal, and the team gets one of each kind of hire instead of two of the same.

Is there a principled reason this kind of trade is objectionable, or am I just being squeemish about normal compromise? And if it is objectionable, does that mean I should reject the deal even if it means my candidate probably loses both rounds?
