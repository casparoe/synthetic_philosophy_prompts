I am a PhD student in epistemology, and I am trying to decide where to focus my dissertation within the cluster of issues surrounding the dogmatism paradox. By that paradox I mean roughly this. Suppose a subject knows some proposition p. If knowledge is closed under known entailment, the subject seems able to know, of any piece of evidence that tells against p, that it is misleading; after all, p itself entails that anything pointing away from p is misleading. But then the subject seems rationally permitted to set counterevidence aside without examining it, and knowledge looks like a license to ignore future evidence. That conclusion is hard to accept, yet each step of the reasoning is difficult to reject.

I have about four years and a background in formal epistemology, and I have no prior commitment to any particular diagnosis of the paradox. I am not looking for a survey of the existing responses or a reading list. I want your considered judgment about where the live work is: which two or three open questions or lines of work in this area should I prioritize, and in what order?

I am especially interested in questions about whether epistemic closure is the right place to lay the blame; how defeat and higher-order evidence should be modeled; whether the deepest issue is a norm governing attention and inquiry rather than knowledge or justification; and what the paradox implies for how one should respond to peer disagreement. But I want your judgment about whether these are the right threads at all.

For each item you rank, say why it is upstream of other work, how tractable it is for someone with my profile, what would count as real progress over a few years, and what evidence would move it up or down the ranking. Also identify which well-known responses or side debates you would deprioritize, and why. I am asking for a committed agenda: make definite choices, defend the ordering, and defend the criteria you are weighting, rather than laying out the options and leaving me to choose.
