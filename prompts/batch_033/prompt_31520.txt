One character's basically an eliminative materialist — she thinks folk psychology is a failed research program and that eventually we'll stop talking about "beliefs" and "desires" the way we stopped talking about phlogiston. The other character thinks this is totally unhinged, that you cant even state the eliminativist position without relying on the very concepts you're trying to eliminate.

I need to write this scene, and I want it to feel real — not like one side is a punching bag. Could you write me a dialogue between these two? I want real back-and-forth where they actually respond to each other's points. The eliminativist should press hard on the idea that folk psychology makes failed predictions and has been stagnant for millennia, while real sciences advance. The defender of folk psychology should push back with the strongest arguments — maybe the self-refutation worry, or the fact that belief-desire explanation is wildly successful at predicting behavior, or that eliminativism confuses a pragmatic tool with a scientific theory.

Both characters should be sharp and neither should walk away convinced, but the reader should feel the tension. Not looking for novelistic polish, just tight philosophical dialogue. Maybe around 800-1000 words?
