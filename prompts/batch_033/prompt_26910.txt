I'm on my town's arts commission and we're deadlocked on a proposal to put public money into a small municipal theater — a converted storefront with a resident company doing maybe six shows a year. One commissioner keeps invoking Rousseau's Letter to d'Alembert, arguing that theater doesn't improve a community's morals, it just gives people a place to sit in the dark and watch counterfeit emotions, and that public money should go to participatory things instead: festivals, dances, public games, that kind of thing. He's not saying ban the theater, just don't subsidize it. The other side says that's nostalgia for 18th-century Geneva, and that a modern town is too big and too pluralistic for everyone to dance in the square, so a theater is exactly the kind of shared public life we can actually have. Draft the short decision memo I'd circulate before next week's vote: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a concrete timeline. Engage the Rousseauian objection seriously — don't just call it old-fashioned — and give a fair statement of the strongest consideration on whichever side loses. After the memo, add a brief structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: "recommendation" (one of "approve", "reject", "approve with conditions"), "conditions" (array of strings; empty if none), "strongest_rousseauian_consideration" (the strongest version of the objection, as your memo engages it), "strongest_losing_side_consideration" (the best point made by the side you recommend against), "key_disanalogy" (the main way this proposal differs from Rousseau's Geneva), and "timeline" (what should happen by when).
