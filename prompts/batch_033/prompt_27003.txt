I keep seeing hinge epistemology papers — the stuff that grew out of Wittgenstein's On Certainty — that are really about whether hinges are beliefs or rules or animal certainties, and I feel like the field skipped a step. Before I read another one, I want the full spec: what does a theory of hinge commitments actually have to get right? Give me the desiderata with the case or consideration behind each — Moore proving he has hands by holding them up, the way the proposition that there is an external world is groundless but not up for grabs, the way something that stands fast in one inquiry can become an open empirical question in another, how hinges get replaced over time, whats going on when two people have different hinges — and say which of these are non-negotiable and which are just the preferences of a particular camp. Then flag any two desiderata that can't be jointly satisfied, and tell me which single requirement rules out the most existing views. I'll do the scorecard myself. Keep it tight and in American English.
