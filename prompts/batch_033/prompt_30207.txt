I run a product team at a mid-size software company. Every quarter we have to decide whether a new AI feature should ship as a tool — something a human drives, where the model does a bounded task like drafting or summarizing — or as an agent, meaning the system takes actions on its own: chains steps together, sends messages, changes records, spends money. The default around here has become "make it an agent, it demos better," and I want a reusable decision proceedure my team can actually run in an afternoon before any code gets written, with engineers and PMs in the room but nobody who knows the philosophy. Please write the procedure as an ordered checklist: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure says stop and keep it a tool, and where it has to hand the decision to a person because the question is one of values or risk appetite rather than anything formal. Ground it in the actual tool/agent distinction and the reasons people worry about agentic systems — blast radius, reversibility, whether a human checkpoint is real or just theater, misspecification, accountability — but don't give me a literature review. Then run the procedure on three cases: (1) a support bot that drafts replies and can also send them and issue refunds up to $50, (2) a contract-review feature that summarizes a contract and flags unusual clauses but cannot edit or send anything, and (3) a coding assistant that can open pull requests and, with one click from a developer, merge them. Tell me where the procedure gives an answer I'm not going to like, and what the procedure cant settle. Please write the answer in British English.
