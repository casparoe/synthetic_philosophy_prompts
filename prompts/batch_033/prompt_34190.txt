We worked through the chapters on Gödel numbering and the incompleteness theorem, and I think I followed the technical content fine. But when Hofstadter starts drawing analogies between self-referential mathematical statements and self-aware minds, I start to lose the thread, and I'm hoping you can help me figure out where I'm going wrong.

Here's the chain of reasoning I keep ending up with, and I can't tell which link is broken. Gödel showed that for any consistent formal system powerful enough to express arithmetic, there exist true statements that the system cannot prove — but we, standing outside the system, can see that they're true. Doesn't that demonstrate that human reasoning has access to something that formal systems, by their nature, lack? And if that's right, then wouldn't any AI — which is, at bottom, a formal system implemented in code — necessarily fall short of genuine understanding or consciousness? It seems like Gödel's theorem would be a direct proof that Strong AI is impossible: no matter how sophisticated your program, there will be truths it can't reach that a human mind can. I'm surprised this doesn't come up more in AI safety discussions, since it seems like it would settle the question of whether machines can *really* think.

But I also get the sense that Hofstadter himself doesn't intend this conclusion. He seems optimistic about machine consciousness, and the whole framing of the book — the strange loops, the tangled hierarchies — reads more like an attempt to explain how consciousness could arise from formal systems, not an argument that it can't. So I'm clearly misreading the argument. I'm not sure whether the mistake is in my understanding of Gödel (maybe the "we can see it's true" move is less straightforward than it looks), or in my assumption that a computer program is the same kind of thing as a formal system in the relevant sense, or in something else entirely about what the Gödelian analogy is meant to illuminate.

You're an expert in philosophy of mind and the history of the Gödelian anti-mechanist arguments. Please think through this carefully and help me locate the confusion — I'd rather you point out the false presupposition I'm making than try to answer the question as I've framed it.
