Two friends of mine got into it about the color phi experiment and neither would budge, so I'm bringing it here. Quick setup: a red dot flashes on the left, then a green dot flashes on the right a split second later, and subjects report seeing one dot glide across and change from red to green about halfway. The puzzle is that the color change seems to be experienced before the green dot even shows up.

(1) Priya said the only coherent reading is Stalinesque: the brain holds awareness up for a beat, stitches the two flashes into a single moving dot, and only then lets the finished product into consciousness. So there was never a real-time experience of two separate dots; the subject's first and only conscious experience was the edited version.

(2) Tom said that's backwards. The subject has a real-time experience of the first dot, and then the second; what gets altered is the memory afterward, Orwellian-style. By the time they report it, the brain has overwritten the record with the moving-dot story. Experience runs on schedule; it's the autobiography that gets revised.

(3) Priya answered that this is worse, not better. The subject isn't just retrieving a corrupted file; they're reporting the experience itself. On Tom's view there would have to be a fact about what they actually experienced — two separate flashes — that differs from what they say, and no one, including the subject, could ever access it. The Stalinesque story doesn't multiply inaccessible facts like that: the only experience there ever was is the one they report.

(4) Tom said that's exactly the trap. Both stories assume there's a single moment when an experience is finished and a single place where it gets presented, so the only question is whether the edit happens just before or just after that moment. But that's leftover Cartesian theater. The brain just runs lots of processes at different times; there's no fact of the matter about whether the editing was pre- or post-consciousness, because consciousness isn't a moment in the projector.

(5) Priya pushed back that the difference does matter. On the Orwellian view the subject really did consciously see a green dot on the right at a particular time, and then that experience was erased; on the Stalinesque view they never did. If the stimulus were painful, or if someone had to say exactly when awareness occurred, these would be different claims about the world, not just different wording.

(6) Tom said sure, but every measurement you could make — asking them, timing a button press, reading out a scan — goes through the same late machinery, so the two stories are rigged to predict exactly the same observations. If no possible evidence could tell them appart, the fight isn't about the facts; it's about which way of drawing the phrase conscious of we prefer.
