Two friends of mine got into it at dinner about a mutual acquaintance who'd done something pretty shitty — basically ghosted a long-term partner right when they were going through a health crisis. One friend, Elena, was furious and said the guy was a coward and a bad person. The other, Tom, said look, the guy was raised by parents who modeled exactly that kind of conflict avoidance, he never learned to handle hard conversations, and given his whole life history up to that moment, he was going to do exactly what he did. Elena said that's an explanation, not an excuse — plenty of people with shitty parents still manage not to abandon their sick partners. Tom said that's the point: those people had some other factor in their history that made them different, and none of us chose our histories or our characters. Elena said if you follow that logic, nobody is ever responsible for anything, which is absurd. Tom said it's not absurd, it's just uncomfortable, and the idea that anyone is ultimately responsible for who they are is incoherent. Elena said the whole concept of moral responsibility doesn't require you to have chosen your character from scratch — it just requires that you could have done otherwise in the moment. Tom said "could have done otherwise" is doing all the work and it's exactly what's in question: given who he was at that moment, could he actually have done otherwise? Elena said yes, he could have, and the fact that he didn't is what makes him blameworthy. Tom said that's libertarian free will and it's magic. It went on like this. I was sitting there thinking they both had a point. Can you walk through their exchange and tell me who's right about what?
