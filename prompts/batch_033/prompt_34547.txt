the approach that makes the most sense to me right now is to define honesty and deception for AI systems purely in terms of the causal history of the outputs. roughly: an output is honest when the training process that produced it was set up to track truth, and deceptive when there's some feature of the training process -- like reinforcement from human raters who are themselves being fooled, or a kind of reward hacking that severs the connection between what the system "says" internally and what it outputs -- that systematically routes around truth-tracking. on this view, deception isn't about what the system "wants" or "believes," it's about whether the causal chain from training data to output is truth-conducive or has been corrupted at some point.
