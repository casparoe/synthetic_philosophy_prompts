ok so I'm in this seminar on moral responsibility and we've been doing all these case-based discussions. my professor keeps giving verdicts on whether someone is an appropriate target of resentment or blame but she hasn't actualy told us the theory she's working from — she wants us to figure it out. here's what she's said so far, i've been writing them down:

1. your friend forgets your birthday because she's been completely overwhelmed taking care of a parent with dementia. verdict: not an appropriate target of resentment.

2. your friend forgets your birthday because she just couldn't be bothered — she was binge-watching a show and didn't care enough to remember. verdict: appropriate target of resentment.

3. a person with advanced alzheimer's says something deeply cruel to you. verdict: not an appropriate target of resentment.

4. a four-year-old deliberately breaks your laptop after you told her she can't have a second cookie. verdict: not an appropriate target of full-blown resentment (she said the kid might be a target of something milder, like frustration, but not the kind of resentment you'd direct at an adult).

5. a coworker spreads a rumor about you to make themselves look better, knowing it'll damage your reputation, purely out of career ambition. verdict: appropriate target of resentment.

next week's case is the one i'm trying to predict. it's about a person diagnosed with antisocial personality disorder — the old "sociopath" label. they're not psychotic, they understand right from wrong, they have no external constraints on their behavior, but they genuinely lack the capacity for empathy or moral concern. they deliberately cheat you in a business deal for personal gain, knowing it'll harm you, and they feel nothing about it. are they an appropriate target of resentment?

i'm less interested in the bare yes/no than in the reasoning. i can see at least two different rules or theories that fit all five of her verdicts equally well. what are they? what would each one predict about the sociopath case? which rule do you think she's actually running, and why? and if you had to design one more case to settle which rule it is — a case where the two theories definitely diverge — what would that case be?
