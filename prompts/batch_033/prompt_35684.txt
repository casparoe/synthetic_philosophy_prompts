I'm a few years into a philosophy of mind PhD and I've ended up pretty convinced by higher-order theories of consciousness — the HOT version, roughly Rosenthal's, where a mental state is conscious iff there's a higher-order thought about it that's assertoric and non-inferential. I came to it through the transitivity principle stuff and I genuinely think it handles the standard objections better than most people admit. But I'm starting to worry I've just curated my reading to confirm what I already think.

So: what would actually count against this view? I don't mean the objections I already know (the rock, animals and infants, the misrepresentation thing where you get targetless HOTs generating ersatz experiences). I mean a proper map of where the view is exposed. What empirical results — from neuroscience, psychophysics, whatever — would be genuinely bad news? What thought experiments or edge cases should make me squirm even if I dont think they're fatal? Which of these would a committed HOT theorist concede as real costs to the theory, and which would they dismiss as question-begging or as problems for everyone? And be honest: has the view been insulated enough by now that it's basically unfalsifiable? If it has, is that actually a problem for a theory of this type, or is it just the situation you're in when you do philosophy of mind at this level of abstraction?
