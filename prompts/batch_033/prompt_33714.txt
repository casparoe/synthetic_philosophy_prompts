I've ended up convinced of a Goodman-ish line on the grue problem, and I want you to try to talk me out of it, or at least show me where it's fragile. The view is this: the new riddle of induction doesn't have a deep solution in terms of natural kinds, real similarities, or how the world is carved up. Those moves just re-describe the fact that we project green and not grue (where something is grue if it's green before some future time t and blue after it) without explaining it. The only honest answer is that green is better entrenched in our actual history of successful inductions, and projectibility just is entrenchment. So grue isn't ruled out by nature; it's ruled out by our track record of having gotten along better with green-ish categories.

What would count against this? I want the considerations that should actually move me, ranked from fatal to merely annoying, not just the standard replies. In particular: whether entrenchment is circular (we judge past inductions successful using the very predicates that are supposedly entrenched), whether the whole story is language-relative in a way that undermines the point, whether there are cases where an unentrenched predicate is nevertheless the right one to project, and whether talk of "successful induction" is smuggling in a notion of truth or naturalness that entrenchment can't pay for. I've heard the objection that you could run the whole grue argument in a language where grue is primitive and green is defined, so entrenchment is just parochial; I half think that's fatal and half think its a cheap point, so tell me which. Also be straight about whether a committed entrenchment theorist would concede any of these as counting against the view, or whether they'd bat them all away. And if the view turns out to be basically insulated — if it can absorb every counterexample — say whether that's a real problem here or just the normal state of a view about induction. I'd personally be most moved by a formal or quasi-formal result, not by intuition pumps about green-speaking and grue-speaking communities.
