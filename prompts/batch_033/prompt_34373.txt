After a long argument with a friend about whether any moral theory can cope with infinitely many people, I realized we were each rejecting the other's view by a different ad hoc test. Before I read further in this area, I want the full specification of what an adequate theory of infinite ethics would have to deliver. Assume the purpose is to rank worlds, and ideally to guide choice, when the set of morally relevant beings may be infinite. Write the desiderata: the cases any such theory must get right (countably many people at positive welfare, welfare sums that diverge, adding or removing finitely many people, rearranging populations, Pareto improvements affecting infinitely many individuals, and the like); the distinctions it cannot collapse (for example, between a total's being infinite and its being undefined, or between a ranking's existing and its being action-guiding); and any formal constraints or impossibility results it cannot contradict. For each desideratum, give the motivating case or consideration behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of one camp. Then say whether some subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable — that is the part I most want to know. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivation rather than on scoring particular theories.
