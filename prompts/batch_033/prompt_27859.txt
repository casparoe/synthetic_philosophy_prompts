I'm organizing a workshop on Frege's puzzle about identity statements, and I want to include a session structured as an adversarial collaboration between two participants who have been debating this for years. One defends a broadly Fregean solution: the view that the cognitive difference between "Hesperus is Hesperus" and "Hesperus is Phosphorus" is explained by a semantic distinction between sense and reference — names express modes of presentation, and identity statements are informative when the names flanking the identity sign present the same object under different modes. The other defends a broadly Millian solution: the semantic content of a proper name is exhausted by its referent, and the informativeness of true identity statements is to be explained at the level of pragmatics, psychology, or the propositional attitudes themselves, not by positing an extra layer of semantic value.

I'd like you to draft the joint statement these two could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The document should include: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides accept — about the data that need explaining, about the logical behavior of identity, about what counts as a constraint on a solution, and about what both agree are non-solutions; (2) a disagreements table with the precise formulations of their dispute, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side to revise their view; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction or diagnosis recorded in advance. Please be careful to avoid formulations that one side would reject as a caricature. In particular, the Millian should not be made to say that "Hesperus is Phosphorus" is uninformative, and the Fregean should not be made to say that senses are mysterious inner objects. Where you suspect part of the disagreement may be verbal rather than substantive — for instance, about what counts as "semantic" — flag that and explain why.
