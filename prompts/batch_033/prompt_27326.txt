I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I have agreed to give a half-hour talk on panpsychism to a well-read but non-specialist audience. I do not want the talk to stay at the level of asking what if everything is conscious; I want its spine to be the combination problem: the difficulty of seeing how micro-experiences distributed across the fundamental constituents of reality could yield the single unified consciousness I am having now, even granting that such micro-experiences exist. Plan the explanation rather than giving it to me. Which concepts have to be secured first, and in what order, before the problem can be stated without sounding like science fiction? What one concrete example or analogy should carry the exposition, and what false suggestions would that example need to be protected against? Which misconceptions should I expect from this audience — I would guess a slide toward treating the view as warmed-over animism and an assumption that complexity obviously produces consciousness, so there is no residual problem — and at what point in the sequence should each be headed off? What should I openly decline to settle at this level? And what final question could I put to the audience to tell whether they have understood the problem as a problem rather than merely found the view exotic?
