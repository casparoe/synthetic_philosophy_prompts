So my friend and I spent like three hours last night arguing about philosophical zombies, and I woke up with this objection that I can't shake. He was defending the zombie argument against physicalism — you know, the one that goes:

(1) Zombies are conceivable (a world physically just like ours but with no conscious experience).
(2) If something is conceivable on ideal rational reflection, it's metaphysically possible.
(3) So zombies are possible.
(4) If zombies are possible, the physical facts don't entail the phenomenal facts, so physicalism is false.

I was pushing back, and at some point I threw out what felt like a knockout: if that argument works for consciousness, it works for everything. Consider "bion-zombies" — beings physically identical to us but lacking the property of being alive. Same molecules, same metabolic reactions, same everything physical, just... not alive. I can conceive of that. So by (1)-(4), bion-zombies are possible, life doesn't supervene on the physical, and vitalism is true. But vitalism is obviously false — life is nothing over and above certain kinds of physical organization. So the zombie argument proves too much and must be unsound.

He didn't buy it. He said the life case is disanalogous because we have a complete functional analysis of what it is to be alive, so the conceivability of bion-zombies doesn't survive ideal reflection — once you understand what "being alive" means, you see that a physical duplicate can't fail to be alive. But with consciousness, there's an explanatory gap: no amount of functional or physical information seems to tell you what it's like to be something, so the conceivability of zombies does survive ideal reflection.

But here's what's eating at me. Isn't that response just assuming that consciousness isn't functionalizable in the way life is? A physicalist about consciousness thinks it is — or at least that it supervenes on the physical with a posteriori necessity, like water and H2O. From the physicalist's perspective, the two cases are exactly parallel: in both, we have a prima facie conceivability that's supposed to be defeated by the actual identity. So my bion-zombie objection only works if you already grant that life is physical, which everyone does, but the zombie argument only works if you already grant that consciousness isn't physical, which is exactly what's at issue. It feels like we're going in a circle.

So here's what I need: walk me through exactly which step of my bion-zombie argument fails, and tell me whether rejecting that step forces me to give up anything in my critique of the original zombie argument. Is my objection question-begging, or is his response question-begging, or are we both missing something?
