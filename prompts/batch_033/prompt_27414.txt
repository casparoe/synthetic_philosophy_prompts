Epistemic status: genuinely stuck, and a little worried the whole thing is a bait-and-switch.

A friend from the rationality meetup I go to has been pressing me on Moore's proof of an external world, and he laid out an argument that I can't shake. It goes roughly like this. (1) Moore's proof is a deductively valid argument: he holds up one hand, then the other, adds the premise that hands are external objects, and concludes that at least two external objects exist. (2) If the premises of a valid argument are known to the person giving it, then that person has proved the conclusion. (3) Moore knows he has hands — and since having hands entails not being a handless brain in a vat, the same move also proves that skeptical scenario false, no extra work needed. (4) What's doing the work is that Moore doesn't answer the skeptic's arguments on their own terms; he just says he's more certain of the premise about his hands than he is of any premise in the skeptical argument, and treats that as enough. (5) So the method is: whenever some commonsense claim you're certain of conflicts with the conclusion of an argument, you can reject the argument without finding the flaw. (6) But that method is available to everyone. A creationist can say he's more certain that life was specially created than he is of any premise in the argument for evolution by natural selection, and reject the whole thing without engaging it. A flat-earther can say he's more certain the ground is flat than of any premise in the argument for a round earth, and reject the whole thing too. (7) A method that lets you reject any argument whose conclusion you dislike, without engaging it, is just dogmatism. So either Moore's proof rests on dogmatism, or there's some principled difference between Moore and the creationist that I can't state without it sounding like special pleading.

I'd like to keep (2) and (3), because I think Moore is onto something about not every piece of knowledge needing a non-circular proof. But I also can't see how to deny (7). Wierd thing is, I can feel something slip between (4) and (6), but every time I try to name it, it comes out as "well, Moore's case is just different," which is exactly the kind of ad hoc move I'd roll my eyes at if someone else made it. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? And if you could keep the answer in American English, that'd be great.
