You are a professional philosopher who works on collective decision-making and on how individual rights fit together with shared preferences. Please think through this slowly, and before you give advice, lay out the structure of my situation as you understand it.

I live with my housemate, Dana. A friend lent us a novel that I find genuinely disturbing; Dana thinks it is brilliant and important. There is only one copy, so there are exactly three options: Dana reads it, I read it, or neither of us reads it and we return it unread.

Our preferences, from best to worst:

- Dana: I read it, then Dana reads it, then neither of us reads it.
- Me: neither of us reads it, then I read it, then Dana reads it.

To explain the strange parts: Dana would most like me to read it because she thinks it would loosen me up, and if I will not read it, she would rather read it herself than see it go unread. I would most like neither of us to read it, but if one of us is going to read it, I would rather read it myself than have Dana read it, because I do not want her exposed to it.

We agreed on two principles for deciding. First, each of us has final say over what we ourselves read; Dana cannot force me to read something, and I cannot stop her from reading something. Second, if we both prefer one option to another, that option should win.

When we applied the two principles, we went in a circle:

- By the second principle, "I read it" beats "Dana reads it," since we both prefer that.
- By the first principle, "Dana reads it" beats "neither reads it," because Dana has final say over her own reading and she prefers to read it.
- By the first principle, "neither reads it" beats "I read it," because I have final say over my own reading and I prefer not to read it.

So each option is beaten by another, and there is no winner. Which principle should we give up or amend, and why? Is there a way to keep the spirit of both? I cannot tell whether we made a mistake, or whether the circle is telling us something about what happens when you combine private choices with shared preferences. Please do not tell us to just talk it out or find a compromise; we have talked it out, and the circle is the result. I want to understand what is going on.
