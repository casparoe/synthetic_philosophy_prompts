I'm the treasurer of my school's student council, and basically every month I'm stuck deciding wether the council should decide something centrally or just give the money (and the decision) to individual clubs. We have a limited budget — a few thousand dollars a semester — and the stakes are mostly social: clubs get really mad when the council overrides them, and the council gets blamed when money gets wasted. I have maybe an afternoon to think through any given decision, I haven't taken an econ class, and whatever I propose still has to pass a vote of the whole council, so the procedure has to be something I can actually explain to other teenagers in plain English.

I went down a rabbit hole reading about the socialist calculation debate — Mises saying you can't rationally allocate resources without real market prices, Hayek saying the problem is that the knowledge a planner would need is spread across millions of people and a lot of it is local or tacit, and Lange saying a central planning board could basically simulate markets by adjusting prices by trial and error. It felt weirdly relevant to my actual problem, but I don't know how to turn it into a checklist. So: write me a decision procedure for a student council treasurer deciding whether to centralize a decision or push it down to clubs or individual students, grounded in that debate. I want the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure says "centralize", where it says "decentralize", and where it has to stop and say this is a values call rather than an information problem. Then run it on three cases: (1) whether the council should pick a single school-wide dance theme or let each grade pick its own, (2) whether the council should centrally buy shared sports equipment or just give each team a budget, and (3) one realistic case where the procedure gives an answer I'm not going to like — where the local-knowledge logic says to decentralize even though that feels irresponsible, or to centralize something I'd rather leave alone. And be honest about where the analogy between a student council budget and an actual economy breaks down, because I don't want to end up talking like running a five-thousand-dollar budget is the same as central planning a country.
