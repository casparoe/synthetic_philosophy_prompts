I'm a journalist working on a piece about the weird afterlife of philosophical thought experiments, and Nietzsche's eternal recurrence is my case study. I've read enough to know there's a real split: some scholars think Nietzsche meant it as a literal cosmological thesis — time is circular, everything repeats exactly — and others say it's purely an existential test (can you affirm your life hard enough to will its eternal repetition?).

Give me your credence that Nietzsche himself actually believed the cosmological version. Not "what's the best reading" — I mean, what probability do you assign to the proposition that Nietzsche, in his own head, took the eternal recurrence to be literally true about the universe?

Then decompose that number. What sub-questions does your credence break into? For each, give me a rough probability and the main consideration driving it. Then tell me which sub-question your overall number is most sensitive to, and what piece of evidence — if you could magically get it — would move you most.

And one thing: if you think the whole framing is confused — that Nietzsche didn't have a stable "belief" here, or that the cosmological/existential distinction is ours not his, or that asking what dead philosophers privately believed is a category mistake — say so. I'd rather get that than a polite number built on a shaky premise.
