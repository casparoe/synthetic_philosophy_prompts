Two friends and I have been running a reading group on the metaphysics of dispositions — the conditional analysis, finks, masks, antidotes, all the usual trouble cases — and we're trying to turn it into a real research collaboration. We've got maybe three years of sustained work in us, and we can realistically take on two or three questions, but not the whole field. Between us we cover metaphysics, philosophy of language, and philosophy of science, so we'd like to pick questions that actually benefit from that mix rather than ones a single person could handle just as well.

Some candidates we keep circling: whether the conditional analysis can be patched up to handle finkish and masked dispositions, or whether the fix is worse than the disease and the whole analysis should go; whether dispositions must be intrinsic, and what to do with apparently extrinsic ones like being a key for a particular lock; whether the finks/masks/antidotes taxonomy is principled or just a collection of counterexamples with a common vibe; whether pan-dispositionalism can avoid the regress worry without quietly reintroducing categorical properties; and whether the semantics of dispositional ascriptions — gradability, comparatives, multi-track dispositions — should be driving the metaphysics at all.

What I'd like from you is a defended agenda: which two or three of these (or which questions we're missing) should we spend the three years on, and in what order? For each, I want the reasoning, not a survey — why its upstream of other problems, how tractable it is given our mix of backgrounds, what the division of labor would look like, and what we could learn in the first year that would make you demote it. Be honest about trade-offs: a question can be deep and still be a bad bet for us.
