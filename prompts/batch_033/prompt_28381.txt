ok so we just finished reading A Monster Calls in my english class and i straight up cried at the end even though i knew the whole time it was fiction — like obviously there is not an actual talking yew tree monster. and i wasnt the only one, half the class was wrecked. but heres what i cant stop thinking about: why? like, i knew conor wasnt real, i knew his mom wasnt real, i knew none of it happened. and normally i dont cry about things i know arent real — i dont cry when i imagine my house burning down unless i actually think its on fire. so whats going on?

i looked this up and apparently philosophers call this the "paradox of fiction" and its been a thing for a while. the basic puzzle is: we have real emotional responses to fictional stuff, we know its fictional, but we usually only have emotional responses to things we believe are actually happening. those three things cant all be true at the same time but they all seem right.

i want you to read A Monster Calls through that lens. like, use the book to think about the paradox and use the paradox to think about the book. does the book itself have anything to say about why we feel things for made-up people? because the monster literally tells conor stories within the story, and conor has huge emotional reactions to those — it honestly feels like the book is doing philosophy without saying so. does the paradox of fiction explain what happened to me and my classmates, or does it miss something that the book gets right? are there specific scenes where the standard philosophical moves (like the "suspension of disbelief" thing or the "we're really sad about real people in similar situations" thing) just dont capture whats going on?
