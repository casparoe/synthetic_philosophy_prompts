I recently came across money pump arguments—the idea that if you have intransitive (cyclic) preferences, a clever trader can offer you a series of trades that each look individually beneficial but collectively leave you back where you started, minus some money. The conclusion is supposed to be that intransitive preferences are irrational.

The money pump only works if I blindly keep accepting trades. But a rational person can learn and adapt. So why does the money pump argument still have force once we acknowledge that real agents can just refuse to participate in sequences of trades that they can see will leave them worse off?

It feels like the argument is conflating two different things: the claim that cyclic preferences are structurally irrational, and the claim that they leave you vulnerable to exploitation. If I'm never actually exploited, what's left of the irrationality charge?
