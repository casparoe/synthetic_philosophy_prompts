I'm in a reading group that's been going back and forth for weeks about Aumann's agreement theorem, and the two factions have started talking passed each other. The common-prior camp reads the theorem as a deep constraint on rational disagreement: if two people are honest Bayesians with a common prior, their posterior probabilities for a given event can't be common knowledge and differ, so any persistent disagreement has to trace back to private information, bounded rationality, or a failure of common knowledge. The prior-skeptic camp thinks the common-prior assumption quietly does all the work: once you assume everyone starts from the same prior and updates by the book, the result is close to a bookkeeping consequence of the setup, so it says little about actual disagreement, where different priors are exactly what's at issue.

I want you to draft the document these two camps would produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, a text both could actually sign. Structure it as a numbered section of claims both accept — covering the formal statement of the theorem, exactly what each condition contributes, and what the theorem does and does not rule out — followed by a disagreements table. For each row, give the residual disagreement, phrase each side's position in wording that side would accept, and mark which disagreements are genuine rather than verbal; if part of the fight is about words, say which part and why. Then identify the cruxes: the subquestions where settling them would actually move at least one side. For each crux, give a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree would bear on it, and state each side's prediction in advance. I'd rather have sharp, non-vacuous formulations than diplomatic mush.
