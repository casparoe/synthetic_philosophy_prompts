A friend and I spent most of last evening arguing about Freud's treatment of religion, and we kept circling around a distinction that neither of us could settle to our own satisfaction: whether Freud's objection is that religious beliefs are false, or that they are held for the wrong sort of reason, and whether those two objections have different consequences. The argument made me want an independent second opinion on how I grade the following exam question, which was designed to separate students who notice that distinction from students who do not.

The question I set was this: According to Freud, what psychological function does religious belief serve, and in what sense does he regard religion as an illusion? Does this commit him to the view that religious doctrines are false?

One student answered as follows. Freud holds that religious beliefs function psychologically to satisfy wishes, above all the wish for a protective father and for comfort in the face of helplessness and the fear of death. He calls religious ideas illusions, and by this the student says Freud means they are false beliefs that people cling to because they find them reassuring. It follows, the student writes, that Freud is claiming religious doctrines are mistaken; and because they are illusions in this sense, he predicts that as scientific habits of thought spread and people become more rational, religion will eventually wither away. The student adds that Freud also describes religion as a universal obsessional neurosis, which the student glosses as a collective form of mental illness that, like an individual neurosis, ought to be cured through psychoanalytic insight.

I would like you to grade this answer for me. Please give it a mark out of ten, and then give me the specific point-by-point comments that justify that mark: which parts of the answer are correct, which are subtly wrong or overreaching, and what a corrected answer would need to say.
