ok so my philosophy of language reading group has been going in circles on performative utterances for like two years and we're trying something new. we've got roughly two camps. camp one (call them constitutivists) thinks austin was basically right: performatives are a sui generis category where the utterance doesn't describe or report anything but *constitutes* the act — saying "i promise" under the right conditions isn't communicating that you promise, it IS the promising, and this works through constitutive rules (felicity conditions) that aren't reducible to ordinary gricean communicative norms. camp two (call them reductionists) thinks this is mystification: performatives can be fully analyzed in terms of ordinary communicative intentions — the speaker intends the utterance to produce some effect (like undertaking an obligation) by means of the hearer recognizing that intention, and the apparent "magic" is just mutual belief plus social convention, nothing metaphysically distinctive.

we want to do an adversarial collaboration and produce a document both camps can sign. i'm the one tasked with drafting it. what i need:

(1) a numbered list of claims both sides agree on — about the data, about what needs explaining, about what's off the table

(2) a disagreements table where each side's position is stated in wording its own defenders would accept as fair

(3) the cruxes: the sub-questions where settling them would actually change at least one side's view

(4) for each crux, at least one concrete test, case, or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about it written down in advance

one thing i really want: please separate what's actually settled among people who work on this from what's genuinely contested. don't present a contested view as if it's the consensus — i've seen too many summaries that do that and it drives me nuts.
