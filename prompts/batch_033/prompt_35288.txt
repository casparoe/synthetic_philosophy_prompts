You know the classic spectrum arguments — if A is better than B by a tiny margin, and B is better than C by a tiny margin, and so on, you can chain these together and end up with something clearly worse than A being declared 'better than' A. The repugnant conclusion is the famous case, but there are others (Parfit's pain spectrum, the non-identity problem chain, etc.). My friend thinks this just shows that 'better than' must be transitive and therefore our intuitions about the endpoints are wrong — we should bite the bullet on the repugnant conclusion. I think it's more plausible that 'better than' is nontransitive, at least in some domains, and that our endpoint intuitions are doing real normative work.
