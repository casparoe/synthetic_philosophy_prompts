I work in procurement for a university's dining services, and I have been asked to develop a standing procedure for choosing among protein sources — chiefly beef, chicken, and plant-based products — when our annual contracts come up for renewal. These decisions arise a few times a year, cover several million meals, and are publicly contested; students and faculty challenge our choices, and we need to be able to show the reasoning rather than assert a conclusion. I have about half a day per proposal, no formal background in philosophy or animal welfare science, and the people who will actually use the procedure are other procurement staff with the same profile. Final sign-off rests with a committee of dietitians, sustainability officers, and student representatives, so the procedure may hand unresolved questions to them, but it must tell them exactly which question they are deciding and what turns on it.

The procedure should operationalize a single principle: among the available options, select in the way that minimizes expected suffering, counting farmed animals, wild animals affected by feed and crop production, and animals affected through climate change, while acknowledging that expected suffering is not one number but a family of estimates under different weightings. Please write it as a reusable checklist or sequence of ordered questions that a non-specialist can complete in half a day using supplier data. For each step, say what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to the committee because the remaining question is one of values rather than fact. Build in the distinctions that actually matter in the beef-versus-chicken and crop-cultivation debates: animals per unit of protein, intensity and duration of suffering, feed conversion and crop deaths, land use and the displacement of wild animals, and the contested question whether bringing additional animals into existence, or preventing them from existing, should itself count as a harm or a benefit.

Then run the procedure on three proposals: replacing beef with chicken in the main dining hall; replacing chicken with a soy-based patty made from conventionally farmed soy; and sourcing beef exclusively from local grass-fed pasture. Make sure at least one of these returns an answer a sustainability-minded procurement officer would find unwelcome — for example, refusing a plant-based option on evidential grounds or approving an animal product I expected to reject — and explain which feature of the case forces that result. Finally, say what the procedure cannot do: which inputs it treats as fixed, where it will misfire if applied outside its intended scope, and which residual uncertainties remain even when it is run correctly.
