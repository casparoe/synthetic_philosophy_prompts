I was reading a philosophy blog and the author summarized the dogmatism paradox like this:

"You know something, say that the store closes at 9. You also know that if the store closes at 9, then any evidence seeming to show it closes at 8 must be misleading. So by a simple deductive step, you now know that any such evidence is misleading — before you've even seen it. That means you can just ignore any sign on the door that says 8, because you already know it's wrong. The paradox is that this reasoning seems valid but it obviously licenses being closed-minded, so something has to go: either knowledge isn't closed under deduction, or 'knowing' something doesn't let you dismiss counterevidence sight unseen."
