I'm a journalist who covers the intersection of physics and philosophy, and I'm preparing a feature on what relativity implies for our picture of time. In the course of reporting, I've settled into a set of commitments that I need to stress-test before I commit them to print. I'd like you to audit the set for consistency — identify which commitments pull against each other, which tensions are only apparent, and what the most economical revisions would be.

Here is where I've landed:

(1) The Rietdijk–Putnam argument is sound. Special relativity entails that there is no objective, frame-independent present moment slicing through spacetime. I take this as established.

(2) I am an eternalist: all events — past, present, and future — are equally real. The block universe is the correct metaphysical picture.

(3) Our experience of time as flowing or passing is a cognitive construction, analogous to the way the visual system builds a stable three-dimensional percept from two-dimensional retinal stimulation. It is useful, but it is not a transparent window onto mind-independent temporal reality.

(4) The thermodynamic arrow of time — the second law together with the low-entropy initial condition of the universe — fully accounts for the asymmetries we observe between past and future: why we remember the past and not the future, why records accumulate, why the past seems fixed and the future seems open.

(5) I hold that free will, in the sense that matters for ordinary life, is compatible with the block universe. What counts is that our actions issue from deliberation and character, not that the future be metaphysically unsettled.

(6) However, I also hold that moral responsibility of the kind that underwrites retributive punishment — genuine desert and blame, not merely deterrence or rehabilitation — requires that the agent could genuinely have done otherwise at the moment of action.

(7) I regard the growing block theory (past and present real, future not yet real) as a non-starter, because it reintroduces a metaphysically privileged present, which is exactly what the Rietdijk–Putnam argument rules out.

Please go through these and identify which pairs are in tension and how severe each tension is. Distinguish genuine conflicts from tensions that dissolve on closer inspection. For each genuine tension, specify what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to restore overall coherence.
