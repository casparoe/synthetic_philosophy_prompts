I'm preparing for a comprehensive exam in metaphysics, and the part I keep putting off is the grounding and fundamentality literature, because it feels like different authors are solving different problems under the same vocabulary. One is worried about what makes it true that the rose is red because it is crimson; another about whether there must be a bottom level of reality; another about whether the cosmos as a whole is prior to its parts or the reverse; and they all call it "grounding." Before I read any further, I want the full specification of what a theory of metaphysical grounding and fundamentality has to deliver. Write the desiderata: for each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it. The list should cover at least the following. First, the examples a theory must classify correctly — the scarlet rose and its being red, the disjunction and its true disjunct, the existence of Socrates and the existence of his singleton, the whole and its parts, a gunky world with no simples, and logical truths if these are grounded in nothing at all. Second, the distinctions it must not collapse — grounding versus causation, grounding versus necessitation or supervenience, full versus partial versus mediate ground, being ungrounded versus being fundamental, and the explanatory "because" versus any worldly relation it may or may not report. Third, the formal features it must ascribe or refuse — irreflexivity, asymmetry, transitivity, well-foundedness — with the case for and against each. Then flag the pairs of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied: for instance, well-foundedness together with the claim that every non-fundamental fact has a ground once endless descent is admitted; or hyperintensionality together with the thesis that grounds necessitate what they ground; or treating grounding as a real relation together with the claim that every grounding fact must itself be grounded. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable constraints on any adequate theory and which are merely the preferences of a particular camp — foundationalists, priority monists, grounding necessitarians, and the like. Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude the main extant positions, and why; keep that part brief, because the criteria themselves are what I want to work through.
