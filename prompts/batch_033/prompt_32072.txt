I'm the dean of a school of public policy, and I'm giving the commencement address this year. I want to build the speech around the concept of Moloch — the idea of multipolar traps in which competing rational agents are driven, by the logic of the situation, to sacrifice what they value most in a race to the bottom. Climate change, arms races, the AI alignment problem, regulatory races between jurisdictions, even the dynamics that make social media platforms toxic — all of these can be understood as Moloch at work.

The audience is about two hundred graduating MPP students who have spent two years studying policy analysis, plus their families. Some faculty will recognize the concept immediately; most family members will never have heard of it. The speech needs to be honest about the structural forces graduates will face, but it is also commencement — it cannot simply be a lecture about how everything is terrible. It needs to give them something to carry forward.

Please write the speech with the following sections, in this order:

- **Opening** (roughly 150 words): A hook that lands the occasion warmly without starting dark. Establish your voice and the stakes without naming Moloch yet.

- **"What we're up against"** (roughly 300 words): Introduce Moloch and the structure of multipolar traps. Use at least one concrete example that is not the usual climate or arms-race examples, and explain it in a way that families can follow without feeling condescended to. The example should make the mechanism vivid — show how individually reasonable choices compound into a collectively ruinous outcome.

- **"Three moves"** (roughly 400 words): Three specific strategic responses to Moloch-like dynamics that policy professionals can use. Each move should be named and illustrated with a short, real-world example. The three moves should be distinct in kind — e.g., changing the incentive structure, enabling credible commitment, and changing the game itself by altering who the players are or what counts as winning. Do not present these as easy or guaranteed; acknowledge the difficulty.

- **Closing** (roughly 150 words): Return to the graduates and the occasion. Land on something genuinely hopeful without papering over the difficulty of the work ahead. Avoid platitudes about "making a difference"; earn the hope.
