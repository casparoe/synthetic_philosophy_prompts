I'm putting together a session for a bioethics reading group and I want to make a handout that helps people figure out where they actually stand on the therapy/enhancement distinction — not which label they find symapthetic, but what their commitments actually commit them to. The group's read a mix: some Bostrom and Savulescu on why the distinction is a mess, some Norman Daniels on why it's still useful for justice in healthcare, some Kass-style bioconservatism, and a bit of the welfarist stuff that says we should just talk about wellbeing and drop the distinction entirely. Predictably, everyone's talking past each other.

What I want is a taxonomy structured as a decision tree. Each branch node should be a yes/no question about a conceptual or normative claim — not 'do you think enhancement is okay?' but something like 'does the concept of normal species functioning pick out a natural fact, or is it normatively constructed?' that actually splits the logical space. The leaves should be the resulting positions, each with a name if one exists in the literature (or 'unnamed' if the combination is coherent but nobody's really defended it). I want maybe 8–12 leaves. For any leaf that's empty, tell me whether it's empty because the view is incoherent, because it's deeply unattractive, or because it's just underexplored and someone should write the paper. Also flag any pairs of leaves your tree can't separate — don't pretend the tree is finer-grained than it is. And please, when you're characterising the debate, separate what's actually settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this stuff. No presenting a contested view as the consensus. Write it in British English.
