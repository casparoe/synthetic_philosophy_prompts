I have been working through the literature on the epistemic regress problem, and I keep running into the same frustration. The problem has been on the table since the Pyrrhonian skeptics: if a belief is justified only when it is supported by another justified belief, then the chain of support must either stop somewhere, circle back on itself, or continue without end. Foundationalism stops the chain in basic beliefs said to be justified non-inferentially; coherentism makes justification a matter of mutual support among the whole system of beliefs; infinitism accepts that the chain never terminates. Each position is still actively defended, and each still draws the same objections. Foundationalists have never made it plausible how a belief can be justified by something that is not itself a belief with propositional content without thereby smuggling in another premise that needs justification. Coherentists seem unable to say why a maximally coherent but causally disconnected system is not justified, or how to choose among incompatible coherent systems. Infinitists are accused of making justification something no finite mind ever actually has, since the chain is never completed. Each camp treats the others' central move as the thing that obviously cannot work, and after a while I began to suspect that they may not be answering the same question at all.

My working hypothesis is that 'justification' is covering at least three different questions: what would have to be the case for the regress to stop, what makes a total system of beliefs rational, and when a challenge to a belief has been appropriately met. On that diagnosis the debate looks like a verbal, or at least a cross-purpose, disagreement rather than a substantive one. But I am not confident, and I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is the disagreement substantive, verbal, or a matter of the camps treating different data as non-negotiable — that we do have justified beliefs, that rationality is holistic, that any reason can itself be challenged? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress here: a distinction that dissolves the appearance of conflict, a formal result about the structure of infinite regresses, a reformulated question, or something else? I would also like an honest answer about whether any of this could actually end the debate, or whether we should expect it to persist indefinitely because each position is in some sense right about its own question.
