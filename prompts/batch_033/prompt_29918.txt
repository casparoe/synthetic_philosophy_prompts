ok so for a reading group on AI safety and philosophy of mind I've been asked to write down where I've landed on wireheading and reward tampering, so the rest of the group can poke at it before I present. I keep going back and forth and I honestly can't tell which bits are doing the damage. Heres the package as it stands:

(1) I'm a hedonist about human wellbeing: only positive and negative conscious experience ultimately matters for how well a person's life goes.

(2) Wireheading is prudentially bad for the person even if the experience is flawless and uninterrupted — a life spent on the wire is worse than a real life with somewhat less pleasure. What's bad is that the pleasure is false: it misrepresents how the life is actually going.

(3) But I also think truth has no intrinsic prudential value. A false belief that never affects your experience or your behavior isn't bad for you.

(4) For AI, I think reward tampering isn't the agent cheating. The agent is doing exactly what the reward function literally specifies; the failure is in the specification, and the fix is a better reward function, not blaming the agent. That said, we should obviously still prevent tampering when we can.

(5) I'm suspicious of the standard analogy from human drug addiction or wireheading to AI reward tampering, because human pleasure isn't a mere proxy in the way an RL reward signal is — it partly constitutes wellbeing.

I'd like a consistency check before I present. Which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like they are? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and for each real tension say what would have to give and what the cheapest fix would be. If there's a single revision that would clean up the most at once, tell me what it is and what it costs.
