I'm preparing a talk for an EA Global conference next month where I'll defend the claim that the simulation argument, if taken seriously, substantially weakens the case for fanatical longtermism. The rough argument: if we are probably in a simulation, then the far future that longtermists want to influence sits downstream of a base reality to which we have no causal access, and our actions may only affect one simulated branch among many — which dilutes expected value in a way that standard longtermist calculations do not account for. I just spent three hours arguing about this with a friend who is a committed longtermist and knows the simulation literature well, and the conversation exposed several places where my argument is vulnerable. I need to be ready for Q&A.

The audience will be mostly researchers at longtermist EA organizations. They are not Bostrom skeptics; most of them take the simulation argument seriously as a live possibility. But they are deeply invested in the idea that the far future dominates the expected value of our actions, and they will resist any argument that threatens that conclusion. They tend to think in terms of expected value calculations, Bayesian reasoning, and decision theory.

Please give me exactly five objections I am most likely to get from this audience, ordered from most damaging to least damaging. Each item should be a compact prep-sheet entry: the objection phrased as an EA longtermist would actually put it in Q&A, my best two-sentence reply, and a note on where that reply is softest. Include the question I should most hope nobody asks. No introduction or conclusion — just the five numbered items.
