Before I read another paper that tries to sink hedonism with the heterogeneity problem, I want the full spec instead of the usual gesture. You know the gesture: people point at a handful of pleasures — the itch-scratch, the relief when the scan comes back clean, the moment a proof clicks, the first drag of a cigarette after a long flight — and say "nothing in common, so hedonism is adding apples and oranges." Fine. But what would a theory of pleasure actually have to deliver to count as having answered that? Write the desiderata. Give me each requirement with the case or consideration behind it, and don't let the requirements be weaselly: cover relief-versus-pleasure, pleasures you'd rather not be having and pains you do want, the dissociation between wanting and liking that the reward literature keeps turning up, whether Mill's higher/lower distinction is internal to pleasure or bolted on from outside, and why the category has to be unified at all rather than just being a disjunction. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable for anyone and which are just one camp's wishlist (a single felt quality, reduction to desire, etc.). Then tell me which pair of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied, and which single requirement does the most to knock out the standard views. A short scorecard for felt-quality, attitudinal, and functional theories at the end is fine, but the criteria are what I'm actually after.
