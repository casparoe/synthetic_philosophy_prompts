I'm putting together a decision tree for a reading group on anthropic reasoning, specifically about the doomsday argument. The idea is: you start with the basic setup of the argument and answer a series of questions, each about a specific premise or move in the reasoning, and the tree spits out which position you actually hold (wether you knew its name or not).

The tree should have maybe 6-8 leaves. The positions I want covered are: the straight-up doomsday pessimist (the argument is sound and we should worry), the SIA rejoinder (your existence is evidence for more people), the reference-class deflationist (the argument dissolves once you notice the reference class is arbitrary), the anthropic skeptic (the whole framework of self-locating probability is confused), the prior-dominance response (the conclusion is only as strong as your prior, and reasonable priors swamp it), the simulation/atypicality response (we have independent reason to think our apparent birth rank is misleading), and the "something must be wrong but I don't know what" position.

Each branching question should be a concrete scenario or a claim someone could evaluate without knowing the jargon. Flag any pairs of positions the tree can't cleanly seperate. Also, if there are logically possible combinations of answers that no one actually defends, note those and say why they're empty.
