been turning this over in my head for months and i thought maybe a poem would help, or at least be a different way of looking at it. it's about those choices where you're caught between two things that both matter deeply but in ways that don't seem to share any common measure — like choosing between a vocation that serves others and being present for your family, or between creative work and political commitment. it's not that you can't decide, it's that the whole idea of weighing them feels like a category mistake. i think i read somewhere that philosophers call this incommensurability.

could you write a villanelle about this? the two refrains should be the two values pulling the speaker apart — make them concrete and specific, not just labels. here's the thing: the form should do the philosophical work. a villanelle has those two lines that keep coming back, alternating, and then they meet in the final couplet. that structure should track what's philosophically interesting here — the way incommensurable values keep reasserting themselves, neither one absorbing the other, no higher principle resolving them. the last two lines shouldnt offer a synthesis or pick a winner. they should just hold the two things side by side, and that juxtaposition is the point.

i want it to be right enough that someone who knows the literature would recognise its about incommensurability and not just indecision. and someone who doesn't know the jargon should still feel the ache of it and maybe understand something about why some choices hurt the way they do.

sorry if thats a tall order. i just think the villanelle is weirdly perfect for this — the way it refuses to move on, keeps circling back to the same two thoughts.
