I'm a high school student taking an advanced elective in philosophy of mind this semester, and I'm currently working on a term paper about the cognitive penetration of perception. I've read enough to grasp the basic definition: roughly, that cognitive penetration occurs when higher-level cognitive states — beliefs, desires, expectations, or conceptual frameworks — directly alter perceptual experience itself, rather than merely influencing how we interpret, judge, or attend to what we perceive. But the more I read, the more I find myself tangled in what seem like borderline cases, and I'm having trouble articulating what exactly makes something count as cognitive penetration rather than one of several closely related phenomena.

For instance, when I learn to identify a birdcall and suddenly I can pick it out from background noise where before I heard only undifferentiated sound, that seems like my cognition is reshaping my perceptual experience. Yet I gather this is classified as perceptual learning, not cognitive penetration, and I'm not sure I understand why. Similarly, if I'm anxious about spiders and consequently my attention is drawn to every small dark shape in the corner of my vision, is my fear penetrating my perception, or is this just attentional modulation — and what hangs on the difference? And if I'm told a wine is expensive and it genuinely tastes better to me as a result, is that cognitive penetration of my gustatory experience, or is it merely a shift in my judgment about the experience while the experience itself remains unchanged? I've also encountered the idea that cognitive penetration is supposed to be distinct from the kind of top-down influence where cognition simply tells perception where to look or what to sample next. But that distinction feels slippery to me: if my expectations are guiding my perceptual sampling, and that sampling in turn shapes what I end up experiencing, hasn't cognition indirectly penetrated perception?

I'd be grateful for an explanation that helps me draw these distinctions clearly. In particular, I'd like to understand what criteria philosophers use to separate genuine cognitive penetration from perceptual learning, attentional effects, and post-perceptual judgment shifts, and why these distinctions matter for broader debates — for example, about whether perception is modular in Fodor's sense, or about whether scientific observation can ever be theory-neutral. I'm less interested right now in whether cognitive penetration actually occurs empirically and more interested in getting conceptually clear on what the claim amounts to in the first place, and why the boundaries are drawn where they are.
