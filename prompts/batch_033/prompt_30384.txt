I have been unable to find the fault in an argument that appears to show that a rational agent may never revise her preferences, not even in response to new evidence. The conclusion seems to me absurd, but each step looks plausible on its own, and I cannot see which of them to surrender.

Here is the argument.

(1) A rational agent cannot be made into a money pump: there is no sequence of trades, each of which she prefers at the time she makes it, that returns her to her original holding minus a fee.

(2) Suppose an agent revises her preferences in the light of evidence: there is a pair of options A and B such that before she learns E she prefers A to B, and after she learns E she prefers B to A. A and B themselves have not changed, and she is not indifferent at either time.

(3) A trader who knows that E will be revealed to her can, before she learns E, offer to take her B and give her A in exchange for a small fee. Since she currently prefers A to B, and the fee is small enough not to reverse that preference, she accepts.

(4) After E is revealed, the trader offers to take her A and give her B back, again for a small fee. Since she now prefers B to A, and the fee is small enough not to reverse the new preference, she accepts.

(5) She now holds B again, poorer by the two fees, and each trade was one she preferred at the moment she made it.

(6) Therefore any agent who revises her ranking of some pair of options in response to evidence is exploitable in this way; and by (1), any such agent is irrational.

(7) But it cannot be a requirement of rationality that one's rankings among options be permanently fixed and immune to evidence. A rational agent should be able to revise her values, or at least it cannot be that every such revision is a rational defect.

(8) So either (1) is too strong, or something goes wrong between (2) and (6). Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If you weaken (1), state the strongest no-exploitation principle you think survives, and say whether it still condemns intransitive preferences held at a single time.

I should like to keep both the thought that preference revision can be rational and the thought that there is some deep connection between rationality and not being exploitable; but I cannot see how to keep both without one of them being altered beyond recognition.

Please write your answer in British English.
