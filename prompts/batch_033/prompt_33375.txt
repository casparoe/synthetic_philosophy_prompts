For the past several years, two philosophers of mathematics in my department have been running an adversarial collaboration. One is a committed Neo-Cantorian: she holds that the actual infinite is coherent, that transfinite set theory describes real mathematical objects, and that rejecting actual infinities would cripple mathematics as we know it. The other is a strict finitist: he holds that the only legitimate mathematical objects are those we can construct in finitely many steps, that talk of "completed" infinities is a conceptual mistake, and that classical mathematics can be reconstructed — and where it cannot, it should be revised — without quantification over actually infinite totalities. They have now agreed to produce a joint document, and they have asked me to draft it, since I have been the neutral moderator of their sessions. What they want is not a compromise but a precise mapping of what they share and where they still diverge.

The document should include the following sections. (1) A numbered list of claims both can sign — substantive claims, not merely verbal truces — covering: what the formal apparatus of transfinite set theory gets right as mathematics, even on the finitist's view; what Hilbert's Hotel and similar thought experiments do and do not establish; what the finitist is and is not denying about the reliability and utility of mainstream mathematical practice; and what each side concedes the other has successfully shown. (2) A disagreements table, each row giving a precise residual disagreement with the Neo-Cantorian's position stated in wording she would endorse and the finitist's position in wording he would endorse — not slogans like "infinities do or don't exist" but the actual point of divergence given everything else they have agreed on. (3) A list of cruxes: the sub-questions about which each is most confident that, were the evidence to favor the other side, they would have to shift substantially. (4) For at least three cruxes, a test, argument, or piece of evidence that both accept bears on it, with each side's prediction or interpretation recorded in advance. If, in the course of drafting, you conclude that some portion of their dispute has been merely verbal — that they have been using a term such as "exists," "actual," or "object" in different ways while agreeing on the underlying mathematical and modal facts — identify that portion, say what the real remaining disagreement is once the verbal issue is cleared away, and reframe the dispute accordingly.
