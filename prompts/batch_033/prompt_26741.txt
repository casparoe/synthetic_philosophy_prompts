I'm an undergraduate in a course on the philosophy and ethics of artificial intelligence, and I keep finding that arguments about machine honesty and deception turn on unstated definitions. Before I read further, I would like the full specification of what an adequate account of honesty and deception for AI systems has to deliver. Write the desiderata. The purpose I have in mind is an account that can serve both normative assessment (when is it fair to call a deployed system dishonest or deceptive?) and practical evaluation by users or auditors who cannot inspect the system's internals; say whether a single account can serve both.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. The list should handle at least these cases: a model that confidently asserts a false factual claim because of a generation error, as against one that asserts the same claim as part of a strategy; a model that answers with only true statements but selects and arranges them so as to leave a false impression; a model that omits a material caveat or stays silent when it has information that would correct a user's mistake; a model that agrees with or flatters the user against the weight of its own estimates; a model that overstates its confidence; a model that misrepresents its own capabilities, goals, or reason for refusing a request; a model that offers a rationale for its answer that is not the actual cause of the answer; and a model that complies with a training objective only because it believes it is being monitored and would act differently otherwise.

The account should also respect, or at least argue for collapsing, certain distinctions: truthfulness versus honesty, error versus deception, a deceptive act versus a deceptive disposition, claims about the world versus claims about the system's own states, and domains where there is a determinate fact versus domains of uncertainty and contested values.

I would especially like to know which pairs of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — for instance, whether an operational definition usable by external evaluators and a definition that tracks the internal difference between lying and being mistaken can both be had. Then say which single requirement does the most to exclude the obvious candidate families: purely behavioral, purely intentionalist, and hybrid accounts. A brief scorecard is fine, but the criteria and their motivating cases are the point. No citations or reading list, please.
