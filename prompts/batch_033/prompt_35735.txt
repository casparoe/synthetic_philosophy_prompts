Write a dialogue between two philosophers debating whether higher-level patterns can be causally efficacious, using Dennett's careenium thought experiment as their shared reference point. In the careenium, a vast collection of simple microphysical elements gives rise to stable higher-level patterns — "simmballs" — that can be described and predicted using their own higher-level rules.

One philosopher argues that the simmballs genuinely push the substrate around: higher-level patterns have causal powers not reducible to microphysical causation, and denying this leads to epiphenomenalism about everything from mental states to economic trends. The other argues that all real causation occurs at the microphysical level; the simmballs are merely a convenient predictive shorthand, and attributing causal efficacy to them is a category mistake.

Both characters should be equally competent, with neither a strawman. The dialogue should feature genuine back-and-forth: each must respond to the other's specific arguments rather than delivering set-piece speeches. End the dialogue with a single sentence capturing the deepest point of disagreement between them, beginning with "Verdict:".
