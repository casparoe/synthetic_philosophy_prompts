So my friend and I have been having this ongoing argument about what makes a life go well — it started as a late-night dorm conversation and somehow it's been going for months now. I've been keeping track of her verdicts on different cases, and she's pretty consistant but I can't figure out what theory she's actually running. Here's what I've got so far:

- Nozick's experience machine: she says that's a bad life, even if the experiences are amazing and you never find out you're plugged in.
- The happy deceived spouse (thinks his wife loves him, his kids respect him, but it's all a lie): bad life.
- Normal flourishing life — meaningful work, close friends, moderate pleasures, some achievements: good life.
- A life of constant physical pain and emotional anguish, no redeeming features at all: bad life.
- A melancholic artist who creates genuine masterpieces and has deep friendships, but experiences very little pleasure — she's just wired to be kind of sad all the time: good life.
- A life of constant mild pleasure from TV, snacks, and casual hookups, no achievements or close relationships, the person is satisfied and doesn't want anything more: bad life.

Now here's the case I want to ask about. Imagine someone who spends their entire life counting blades of grass. Not for any larger purpose — they just find it genuinely fulfilling. They have no false beliefs about what they're doing, they've reflected on all the alternatives, and they wouldn't choose any other life. They achieve nothing by any conventional standard, they form no close relationships, and the pleasure they get from grass-counting is pretty mild. They're not hurting anyone, they're just... counting grass. What would my friend say about this life? And more importantly, what theory or theories is she running — informed desire-satisfaction, objective list, perfectionism, something else — and which further case would settle which one it is? I'm less intrested in the actual answer than in the mapping of the logical space here.
