In a comparative politics course, the instructor handed out this one-paragraph gloss of the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem:

'Gibbard and Satterthwaite proved that there is no perfect voting system. With three or more candidates, any voting rule that is strategy-proof — meaning no voter can ever get a better outcome by submitting a ballot that misrepresents their true preferences — must be a dictatorship, where one voter's top choice always wins. It follows that every non-dictatorial voting method is manipulable: there will be some election in which someone does better by voting insincerely. The practical lesson is that strategic voting is unavoidable in any real election, and so ranked-choice methods are no more trustworthy than plurality voting.'

I can't tell how much of this to trust. Could you go through it sentence by sentence and say which parts a social choice theorist would accept, which parts are overstatements or silently drop conditions, and whether the final claim about ranked-choice versus plurality actually follows from the accurate parts?
