**Question:** In *Reasons and Persons*, Derek Parfit uses the teletransporter thought experiment to challenge ordinary assumptions about personal identity and survival. Explain what the teletransporter case is, what Parfit takes it to show, and how it connects to his broader claim that "identity is not what matters." Be precise about the relationship Parfit sees between psychological continuity, psychological connectedness, and numerical identity.

**Student Answer:**

Parfit's teletransporter is a machine that scans your body and brain, destroys the original, and creates an exact replica on Mars. The replica has all your memories, beliefs, desires, and personality traits. Parfit argues that this case shows that what matters in survival is not numerical identity—being one and the same person over time—but psychological continuity. Since the Mars replica is psychologically continuous with the person who entered the machine, Parfit concludes that you do survive teletransportation. He takes this to mean that personal identity just consists in psychological continuity, and that our ordinary belief that bodily continuity is required for survival is mistaken. The claim that "identity is not what matters" therefore means that we should replace the concept of personal identity with the concept of psychological continuity when we think about survival. Parfit extends this reasoning to fission cases: if one person's psychology branches into two successors, both would be psychologically continuous with the original, so both would be identical to the original person. This shows that personal identity can be a one-many relation, which further undermines the ordinary view that identity must be one-one.
