My philosophy of biology professor has given the following verdicts over the term:

1. The heart's pumping of blood is a function of the heart.
2. The heart's making a thumping sound is not a function of the heart.
3. A spider web's catching insects is a function of the web.
4. A peacock's train attracting mates is a function of the train.
5. A digestive enzyme's breaking down a food molecule on which the organism has depended for many generations is a function of the enzyme.
6. A metabolic byproduct that makes no contribution to the organism's survival or reproduction is not a function.

Next week she will be asked about a new case. A bacterial population in an environment containing a novel toxin has just acquired, by a single mutation, an enzyme that degrades the toxin. The mutation has only just arisen, so selection has not yet acted on it; nevertheless, cells carrying the mutation are already surviving and reproducing at a higher rate than cells without it. Is the enzyme's degradation of the toxin a function of the enzyme?

I am less interested in the bare yes/no than in the principle behind it. Predict her verdict. In doing so, identify the account of function she seems to be running, and state which other accounts fit her earlier verdicts just as well. Then say what each candidate account predicts about the new case, give your best guess as to which prediction she would endorse and why, and specify one further case that would best settle which account she is actually running. If her earlier verdicts genuinely underdetermine the new case, say so, and explain what principle she would be adopting with each possible answer.
