So I've been arguing with a friend about prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection. We both agree that the leveling-down objection is a real problem for straightforward egalitarianism — like, if you make the rich poorer without making anyone better off, that's not an improvement, and views that say it is are missing something. But we disagree about whether prioritarianism actually escapes it. My friend thinks prioritarianism is basically just egalitarianism with better PR: the reason you give priority to the worse off is ultimately because you care about inequality, and if you really interrogate why you'd apply a concave weighting function to well-being, you end up back at the same intuition that the leveling-down objection undermines. I'm not so sure — I think there's a genuine difference between caring about the absolute level of the worse off (even if inequality is the heuristic that gets you there) and caring about the distribution itself.

I want your actual credence that the leveling-down objection is fatal to prioritarianism. Not just "it's a problem for egalitarianism" — we already agree on that. I mean: does it, on reflection, also kill prioritarianism? And I want the number decomposed. Specifically, break it into: (a) the probability that the leveling-down objection is fatal to telic egalitarianism; (b) the probability that prioritarianism's justification ultimately collapses into egalitarian reasoning (i.e., the priority weighting can't be motivated without implicitly valuing equality itself); (c) the probability that even if prioritarianism is genuinely distinct, the leveling-down objection reveals a structural problem with prioritarianism anyway — maybe something about how the weighting function is calibrated, or about the separateness of persons; (d) any other term you think belongs. Give me a number for each, explain how they combine (are they conjunctive? is there some other structure?), and tell me which term you're least confident about and what kind of argument or evidence would shift it most.
