ok here's a weird one. i'm a phd student in phil of mind / cog sci and i've been living with Dennett's four kinds of creatures for a diss chapter. i want a sonnet — a real one, iambic pentameter, volta and all — that moves through Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures in order, but without ever naming them. here's the structural idea: the octave covers the first two kinds (both learn by actual trial and error in the world, one across generations and one within a lifetime — so the octave should feel iterative, cumulative, maybe with a rhyme scheme that reinforces that). the volta IS the Popperian shift — the moment a creature can test hypotheses internally, letting them die in its stead instead of dying itself. the sestet handles Gregorian creatures: minds shaped by language, culture, borrowed cognitive tools. the closing couplet should land on something sharp — maybe about where we stand now, or about whether the hierarchy has another rung.

the poem has to be accurate enough that someone who knows the taxonomy would recgonize each kind from the description alone, and good enough as a poem that someone who doesn't know Dennett would still find it worth reading. think of yourself as a poet who also happens to be a professional philosopher of mind. take your time, brainstorm a bit if you need to, and write in American English.
