I'm a retired evolutionary biologist, been running a philosophy reading group for about a year now — mostly other retired science types, a dozen of us, we meet for two hours every other week. We've been reading Dennett's "From Bacteria to Bach and Back" and we're about to discuss the two "strange inversions of reasoning" — Darwin's (design without a designer) and Turing's (competence without comprehention, or computation without a mind that understands).

I want to do something different this time. Instead of just talking about it, I want the group to *experience* the inversion. I want an activity where they follow stupidly simple rules individually, and collectively produce something that looks clever or designed — and at the end they realize nobody in the room understood what they were building while they were building it. The rules each person follows should be the kind of thing a protein could do: if this, then that.

Here are the constraints:
- 12 people, in a room with moveable chairs, a whiteboard, and space to walk around
- The activity should take about 40-50 minutes, with another 20 for debrief
- Each person's instructions should fit on an index card
- No special materials beyond paper, cards, markers, maybe some string or tape — nothing electronic
- I don't want to telegraph the outcome. The "aha" should come from the result, not from me explaining what's supposed to happen

What I need from you:
- The actual game or activity, with rules, setup, and step-by-step facilitation notes
- What the "aha" moment is and roughly when it should land
- A debrief script that connects the activity to both Darwin's inversion and Turing's inversion, and notes which one the activity captures better (I suspect it'll lean one way)
- An honest account of what will probably go wrong the first time, and a backup plan
- Whether there's some feature of the inversions that the activity just can't reproduce, and how I should fill that gap in discussion

I should mention: this group is smart but they're not philosophers — they're retired scientists who read carefully but don't have the vocabulary. So the debrief should translate the philosophical points without assuming they know the jargon. Also, one of the geologists is the kind of guy who will try to reverse-engineer the whole thing from the rules before we start, so tell me how to handle that.
