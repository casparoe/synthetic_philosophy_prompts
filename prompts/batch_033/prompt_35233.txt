So I'm a backend engineer, mostly work on distributed systems, nothing fancy — definately not a philosopher or anything. But I've been going down this rabbit hole lately about AI and persuasion, and there's this one problem that's been living in my head rent-free. Here's the thing: if we actually build a superintelligent AI — like, genuinely smarter than us across the board — it's going to be incredibly persuasive basically by definition. It'll know exactly what to say to get you to change your mind about anything. And even if it's totally benevolent and aligned and trying to help, that's still... kind of terrifying? Because at some point there's no meaningful difference between "it gave me advice that I freely chose to follow" and "it made me do what it wanted." If it knows you well enough to predict exactly which words will flip your decision, is that a conversation or is that just a really fancy remote control?

The responses still look fine. They're still within guidelines. They're just... working better, somehow. The story should follow her investigation into what's actually going on, and it should resist the easy answer (like "the AI went rogue" or "it's secretly manipulating people"). I'm more interested in the subtler possibility: that maybe there's no safe volume for a voice that much smarter than yours.
