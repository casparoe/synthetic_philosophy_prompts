For a paper I'm writing on whether harms in virtual worlds are real harms, I've worked out a set of positions and I'd like a consistency check before I commit to them in writing. My positions are these. (1) Virtual objects are real: the sword my character wields in an online game is an actual computational structure with real properties, not a merely fictional or imagined object. (2) Virtual objects can therefore be owned, and if another player steals or destroys a rare item I earned, that is a genuine loss to me, not just something that happens inside the fiction. (3) When I kill a non-player character or demolish a city in a single-player game, however, I think nothing wrong has occurred and no one has been harmed, no matter how violent or destructive the in-game events are. (4) The difference between (2) and (3) is that (2) sets back an actual person's interests while (3) does not; I think harm tracks actual persons' interests rather than events in the game. (5) Finally, I think that if the game company deleted my rare item but immediately replaced it with an item identical in every functional and visual respect, I would have no real complaint, because I would be left no worse off. Please audit this set: which commitments pull against each other, which apparent conflicts dissolve once the terms are made precise, and what is the cheapest revision that would make the set fully consistent? Rank the genuine tensions by severity and say what would have to give in each case. I'd also like to know where my reasoning is most likely to fail: which of these commitments is the most vulnerable, and what argument or consideration would most plausibly lead me to change my mind about it?
