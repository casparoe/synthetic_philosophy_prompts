i'm a PhD student in philosophy of language and i'm trying to nail down my commitments on Moore's paradox before i build a dissertation chapter around them. i've been going back and forth on this for weeks and honestly i'm too close to it to see where the problems are. here's where i've landed:

(1) Moore-paradoxical sentences ("p, but I don't believe that p") are logically consistent — there's no formal contradiction, and there are possible worlds where they're true — but they cannot be sincerely asserted without irrationality.

(2) The explanation for the irrationality in (1) is pragmatic, not semantic. The problem isn't that the sentence expresses a contradiction, but that asserting p conventionally implicates (or expresses, or whatever the right word is) that the speaker believes p. So the overall assertion pragmatically conveys "I believe p" and "I don't believe p," which is a kind of pragmatic contradiction.

(3) I'm independently committed to the knowledge norm of assertion: one should assert p only if one knows p. (The Williamson line, roughly.)

(4) First-person present-tense belief ascriptions enjoy a special kind of authority: normally, if I sincerely judge "I believe that p," I'm right. There's a presumption that I know my own mind in this way.

(5) Belief is a dispositional, functional state — it's not a feeling or a conscious occurrence, and it's the kind of thing that can persist through dreamless sleep.

(6) Sincere assertion doesn't entail belief. You can sincerely assert p without believing p, as in cases of self-deception where the person is genuinely convinced they're telling the truth but their behavior and other attitudes reveal they don't actually hold the belief.

my main worry is that (2) and (6) are pulling in opposite directions — if asserting p doesn't require believing p, then what kind of "expression" relation is (2) relying on, and why does violating it produce irrationality rather than just, like, conversational awkwardness? but i'm also not sure whether (3) and (4) cause trouble for (1) in ways i haven't noticed. and i keep wondering if (4) and (5) are actually in tension, or if that's just the standard evans-style transparency response waiting to be deployed.

go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and rank the problems by severity. if there's a single revision that would clean up the most, tell me what it is and what it costs.
