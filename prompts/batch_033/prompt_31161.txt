So I work as a backend engineer and a bunch of us at work have been arguing about whether LLMs actually "understand" anything or whether they're just stochastic parrots. One of my coworkers who did a philosophy minor in college keeps bringing up Dennett's intentional stance like it's the conversation-ender. He dropped this summary in Slack:

"Dennett's whole point with the intentional stance is that asking 'does it really have beliefs?' is meaningless. If you can reliably predict a system's behavior by treating it as a rational agent with beliefs and desires, then it has beliefs and desires — full stop. There's no deeper fact about what's going on inside. That's why he says a thermostat literally believes the room is too cold and wants to warm it up. And it means we should say today's LLMs genuinely believe the claims they output, because the intentional stance works so well on them. The only alternative is the design stance, but Dennett showed that isn't a rival to the intentional stance — it's just a different explanatory level. So anyone insisting LLMs don't really understand anything is making a category mistake, and Dennett settled this decades ago."

Something about this feels too clean to me. My instinct is that theres a difference between how I believe something and how a thermostat "believes" something, and I'd guess Dennett would agree — otherwise why'd he spend so much time writing about consciousness and the brain? But I haven't read him and my coworker sounds confident. Can you go through this summary and tell me what parts a careful Dennett reader would nod at, what parts they'd say are distorted or overstated, and whether the jump to "LLMs genuinely believe what they say" actually follows from the view as Dennett laid it out?
