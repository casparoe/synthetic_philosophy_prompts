So I got into this massive arguement with my friend Dave last night — we were at a bar for like three hours — about AI alignment. He's not in tech but he read Bostrom and some blog posts, and his whole position is basically: alignment is impossible because it's just the genie problem. You know, you wish for something and the genie grants it in the most literal, catastrophic way possible. He kept saying "the AI is the genie, the objective function is the wish, and we're the idiot making the wish." And his conclusion was that we should just stop building advanced AI entirely because you can't solve the genie problem — any specification will have loopholes a superintelligence can find.

I tried to push back by saying a real AI isn't actively malevolent like a folkloric genie, it's just optimizing a misspecified goal, and that's a different kind of problem. But he said the distinction doesn't matter because the outcome is the same — you get paperclips or whatever. And honestly by the end I wasn't sure I had a good answer.

So I want someone to really audit this analogy. Like, actually lay out what corresponds to what — the wisher, the wish, the genie, the ironic fulfillment, the fact that in the stories the genie is bound to grant exactly one wish literally, the way the genie already knows the language and just chooses to interpret it adversarially. Where does the mapping hold and where does it break? Which features of the genie story does the analogy quietly import that aren't true of AI? And the thing I actually care about: does his conclusion — that alignment is hopeless and we should stop — survive once you put all the disanalogies on the table? Or was the analogy carrying the whole arguement?
