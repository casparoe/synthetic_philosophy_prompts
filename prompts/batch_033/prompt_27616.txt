After a long career as a biophysicist working on feedback control and homeostatic systems, I have spent my retirement reading philosophy of mind, and I am struck by how much turns on whether sentience is a threshold or a gradient. The reading group I host has been circling for weeks around a single question: where, if anywhere, does moral status run out between a honeybee and a thermostat? One of us insists there must be a sharp line somewhere; another says status simply fades out as the relevant capacities thin; a third thinks the whole lights-on-or-lights-off picture is a confusion borrowed from our machines.

I would like you to write a decision tree, suitable as a one-page handout for this group, that takes a reader from a few plain commitments about these cases to a named position on the gradation of sentience and moral status. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of labels: each internal node should be a question or a concrete case that someone can answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and each leaf should name the position, or mark it unnamed, with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Please organize the branching around the fault lines that have actually divided us: (1) whether the relevant property for moral status is subjective experience at all, rather than goal-directedness, complexity, or life; (2) whether sentience, once granted, is a threshold or a continuum; (3) whether degrees of sentience, if any, translate into degrees of moral status smoothly or in discrete tiers; (4) whether felt valence is required for any moral status, or whether neutral experience suffices; and (5) whether a non-sentient system such as a thermostat can have even minimal status derivatively, through interests, functional roles, or the preferences of others. Work these cases into the tree as diagnostic probes: a honeybee, a nematode worm, a cortical organoid, a simple reinforcement-learning agent, and a thermostat. The leaves should include at least strict sentientism, gradualist sentientism, biocentric gradualism, functionalist gradualism, and something like an information-theoretic panpsychism, but do not force these if the tree's logic does not yield them; flag the unoccupied combinations instead, and say whether each is unoccupied because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Six to nine leaves would be about right. Please also flag any pair of positions the tree cannot separate and state what further question would separate them. I would appreciate the answer in American English.
