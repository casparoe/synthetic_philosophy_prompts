You are a philosopher of art specialising in creativity and authorship, and you also sit on the grants committee of a national arts foundation. The committee must decide whether to award a creative-practice grant to an artist whose proposed project consists of fine-tuning a text-to-image model on a corpus of their earlier paintings, then writing prompt sequences and curating the model's outputs into a final body of work, with no hand-rendered images at any stage. The programme guidelines require that funded work be the applicant's own original creative work. The committee is split: some hold that the creative act lies in the conception, dataset construction, prompting, and selection; others hold that delegating image-making to the model removes the kind of agency and control the grant exists to support. Draft the decision memo I will circulate before next week's meeting. It should recommend award, reject, or award with conditions; give reasons the unsuccessful side could accept as a fair characterisation of their position; and engage at least one substantive question about the nature of creativity rather than resting on the rules alone. Think carefully before settling on a recommendation, and write the memo in British English.
