Over the holidays my cousin Max got really into an argument he found online, and now it's messing with his head. The way he puts it: think of all the humans who will ever be born. If civilization keeps going for even a few hundred thousand years — let alone spreads to other planets — that number would be in the trillions or more. But you and I were born within the first hundred billion or so. If you imagine picking your birth position randomly from the full list of all humans, being this early would be a crazy coincidence under the optimistic scenario. So, Max says, we should expect the list isn't much longer than what's already happened — meaning there won't be vastly more people after us, and humanity probably ends within a few centuries. He's taken this weirdly seriously: he stopped contributing to his retirement account and keeps saying there's no point worrying about long-term climate stuff. I've tried to tell him the reasoning is off, but I can't locate the mistake. He just says I'm rejecting it because the conclusion is uncomfortable and that its basic probability.

My other friend Priya says Max is only using half the logic. She thinks you also have to account for the fact that you exist at all, which already favors bigger worlds: if two theories are otherwise identical, and one says there are a trillion observers while the other says there are a billion, the fact that you exist should make you much more confident in the trillion-observer theory. That also sounds fishy to me — like getting to believe in a bigger world just because it writes more people into the story — but she claims it's the only consistent way to avoid Max's pessimism. I'd like to know who's reasoning well here, if either of them is, and what I should say to Max specifically.
