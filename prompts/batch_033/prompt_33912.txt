A philosophy podcast I listened to summarized the debate about stakes and knowledge roughly as follows:

Contextualists argue that whether you know something depends on how much is at stake for you. DeRose's bank case shows this: if you're just deciding whether to deposit a check on Saturday, you know the bank will be open based on your memory of visiting last week; but if your mortgage payment is about to bounce, you no longer know it. This is called pragmatic encroachment, and it shows that knowledge isn't purely epistemic — practical factors seep in.

I've since heard that contextualism and pragmatic encroachment are distinct views and that conflating them is a common mistake.
