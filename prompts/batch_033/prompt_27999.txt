Construct a variant of Kavka's toxin puzzle that isolates the role of self-binding as a proposed solution. In the original puzzle, you are offered a large sum if at midnight you intend to drink a toxin tomorrow that will make you ill for a day; the reward depends solely on your intention at midnight, not on whether you subsequently drink. One prominent reply is that you can form the intention by taking a commitment device now—for instance, ingesting a substance that will compel you to drink tomorrow, or signing an enforceable contract that makes non-compliance worse than the illness. Your task: design a realistic variant (no science fiction, no exotic psychology, no longer than two paragraphs) in which this self-binding move is available but fails to resolve the puzzle, because the puzzle reappears at the point of deciding whether to self-bind. After presenting the scenario, walk through: (a) why self-binding seems to dissolve the original puzzle, (b) how your variant blocks that move, (c) what structural feature of the original puzzle the self-binding solution was implicitly relying on and how your variant exposes it, and (d) whether a defender of self-binding has any remaining reply and, if so, what that reply costs them.
