I'm a newly elected city councilmember and a practicing Catholic. Next month I'm giving the annual "Faith and Public Life" lecture at our state university's politics department. The audience will be mixed: political philosophy faculty who know their Rawls and Audi inside out, graduate students, local clergy from several traditions, some of my fellow parishioners, and undergraduates who are mostly there for course credit.

The lecture needs to do one hard thing: explain why I never cite Scripture, Church teaching, or natural-law reasoning when I argue for a policy position in council meetings — even though my faith is the deepest source of my moral convictions. I need to make this case without making either the secular philosophers or the religious believers in the room feel condescended to or sold out. The philosophers will spot any sloppy version of the public-reason argument; the parishioners will know if I'm embarrassed of my faith. I want the speech to be intellectually honest and personally genuine, not a diplomatic evasion.

Please write the speech and then a commentary on it. Structure your response as follows:

- **The Speech** (approximately 1,200–1,500 words, about 10–12 minutes spoken). Use "//" to mark natural pauses or transitions.
- **Commentary** (approximately 400–500 words). Explain the key philosophical moves you made, the audience-management choices, and what you deliberately left unsaid or only gestured at — especially anything a Rawlsian or a theologian might notice and want to press me on afterward.
