so i went down a rabbit hole reading about von neumann and how he compared computers to brains, and theres this one idea in it that i cant stop turning over. as i understand it, he thought that from the outside the brain can look digital, because neurons either fire or they dont, like switches, but the actual operation underneath is a mix of digital pulses and analog, chemical, noisy stuff. and the big claim is that the brain's native language is not mathematics. math is something secondary, a later overlay built on top of whatever the nervous system is really doing, not the thing the nervous system itself speaks. that distinction has been living in my head rent free.

i want a poem about this, around fourteen lines, sonnet-ish but it doesnt have to be a textbook sonnet. heres the structural idea: the first eight lines should be locked-in and regular, clean iambic pentameter and tidy rhymes, describing the brain as a perfect digital machine, switches clicking on and off, logic gates, all of it. then the turn should be the moment the speaker realizes the brain isnt actually running on that clean binary language, that the pulses might be digital but the summing is analog and full of noise, and the mathematics is just a late invention layered over it. i want the form to do that work: after the volta the meter and rhyme should start to fray a little, not all the way into free verse, just enough that you can feel the tidy picture coming apart. the closing couplet should say what the brain is if it isnt a digital computer, without ever naming von neumann or using the word computer again. i dont need it to be scholarly, but i would be annoyed if it said the brain is purely analog or that there are no digital parts, because the on/off spikes are part of the story too.
