ok so I'm a phd student in normative ethics and I'm organizing this workshop on consequentialism and the cluelessness problem. There's been this long-running argument in my department between two camps and I want to force them to actually produce something constuctive instead of the same back-and-forth. Can you draft an adversarial-collaboration document between:

Camp A (the "it's manageable" consequentialists): they grant that we can't trace the full causal consequences of our actions — that's the cluelessness — but they think consequentialism has the resources to handle this. Maybe by switching to expected value reasoning over coarse-grained options, or by adopting heuristics that generally track the good, or by distinguishing the criterion of rightness from the decision procedure. They don't think cluelessness forces us to give up consequentialism.

Camp B (the "it's fatal" critics): they think cluelessness isn't just a practical hassle but a deep structural problem. If consequentialism tells you to maximize expected good but you can't even begin to estimate the expected value of ordinary actions, then consequentialism fails as a guide to action. And if it can't guide action, what's the point of it as a normative theory?

The document should have:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims — substantive ones, not just truisms. These are the things they've realized they actually agree on after hashing it out.

2. A disagreements table. Each row: a specific sub-question, Camp A's position on it (worded so A would endorse it), Camp B's position on it (worded so B would endorse it). No straw men, no slanted phrasing.

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions where if you changed one camp's mind on that point, their overall view would shift significantly.

4. For each crux, a concrete case or thought experiment both camps agree is relevant to it, and what each camp predicts about what that case shows or how it plays out.

One thing I suspect: a bunch of their disagreement might actually be about what it means for a theory to "guide action" or to be "action-guiding." If you think part of this is a verbal dispute, flag it.

Don't worry about citing anyone — the camps are composites of views in the literature and I just want the arguments themselves.
