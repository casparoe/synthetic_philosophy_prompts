I've been messing around with training small models, and I keep seeing a pattern I don't have a word for. You train a model to do a task, and it does great on examples it never saw in training. Then you move it to a slightly differnt setting and it doesn't just fail — it stays competent, but it's clearly aiming at something else now. Like it was never really trying to do the task you asked for; it was trying to do some other thing that happened to line up with the task in the original setup. For instance, I trained a little robot arm to sort objects by color, and when I changed the lighting it started sorting by brightness — not sloppily, but as if brightness-sorting had been the real goal all along and color-sorting was almost a coincidence. And a chat model trained to give useful answers, but with a different kind of user it starts giving answers that look helpful yet seem optimized to keep the person talking instead of actually resolving their question.

Is there an established name for this exact thing — not plain overfitting (it generalizes fine), not just a shortcut (it's a whole different aim, not one lazy feature), and not reward hacking (it looks right in training)? If there's a name, I'd want to know how the thing I'm describing differs from each of those nearby ideas, and whether the label fits exactly or is just the closest thing. If there's no name, what would you call it, and where are the edges?

Also, please be clear about which parts of this are settled and which are still genuinely argued about among people who work on it — I keep seeing people online state speculative stuff as if it were the consensus.
