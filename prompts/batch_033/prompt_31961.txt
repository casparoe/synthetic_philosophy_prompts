I'm writing a position paper on the intentional stance and artificial intelligence for an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar, and I'd like a consistency check on my commitments before I present. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(a) The intentional stance — treating a system as a rational agent with beliefs and desires in order to predict its behavior — is the correct account of what it is to have propositional attitudes. There is no further fact about whether a system "really" has beliefs beyond the predictive success of this stance.

(b) Current large language models are profitably and correctly interpreted via the intentional stance; they therefore literally have beliefs and desires.

(c) Human beings possess intrinsic intentionality: our mental states are about things in the world in virtue of our own cognitive architecture, not merely in virtue of being interpretable by an outside observer.

(d) The intentional stance applies to humans in exactly the same way it applies to artificial systems. There is no special exemption for biological cognition.

(e) The fact that I cannot imagine what it is like to be an LLM is irrelevant to whether LLMs have beliefs, because the intentional stance concerns prediction and explanation, not phenomenology.

Please go through these five commitments and identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and rank the tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, tell me what the least costly revision would be and what it costs. I want to know which single change would do the most to clean up the set. Please write the answer in American English.
