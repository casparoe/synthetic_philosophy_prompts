I keep coming back to the Sleeping Beauty problem and I'm getting frustrated, not with the problem itself but with the fact that the debate between halfers and thirders seems completely stuck. For anyone who hasn't seen it: Beauty goes to sleep Sunday night, a fair coin is flipped. If heads, she's woken Monday and that's it. If tails, she's woken Monday, given a drug that erases her memory of the waking, and woken again Tuesday. Each time she's woken she's asked: what's your credence the coin landed heads? Halfers say 1/2, thirders say 1/3. Both sides have arguments that feel airtight. The halfer says the coin was fair, she learns nothing new on waking (she knew she'd be woken at least once either way), so her credence should stay at 1/2. The thirder says: if we run this experiment 100 times, there'll be about 150 wakenings, and only 50 of them are heads-wakenings, so upon waking she should be at 1/3. Both can't be right.

What's driving me nuts is that this isn't a vague philosophical question where people are talking past eachother because the terms are fuzzy. It's a probability puzzle with a well-defined setup. You could simulate it in like ten lines of Python. And yet smart people have been arguing about it for decades and nobody budges. My hunch is that the disagreement isn't really about probability—it's about what the question even means. Specifically, I suspect halfers and thirders are using different rules for when you should update your credences, and those rules trace back to different intuitions about what a credence is even supposed to track in cases where your "location" in the world (which day it is, which copy you are) is part of what you're uncertain about. But I don't have the vocabulary to nail down whether that's right, or whether it's something else—like a disagreement about whether "being woken" counts as evidence, or a hidden disagreement about the reference class you should use.

So my question is: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it a substantive disagreement about probability that could in principle be settled, or is it a verbal dispute where the two sides mean different things by "credence," or are they answering different questions under the same name? And if you had to diagnose the impasse, what would actually count as progress—a new formal result, a distinction that dissolves the question, or is this just one of those things where we should accept that both answers are "right" relative to different frameworks?
