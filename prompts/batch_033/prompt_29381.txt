I sit on a small reading group that has spent the term working through the physical, design, and intentional stances, together with the idea that a pattern is real when it allows one to predict or compress behaviour at a higher level than the underlying physics. One of our members, a theoretical biologist, keeps a running table of her settled judgements about which stance is appropriate for a given system, and I have been trying to reverse-engineer the rule behind the table rather than simply collecting her conclusions. Her settled cases include the following. A thermostat: design stance, with the intentional stance dismissed as a needless overlay. A chess-playing computer: the intentional stance is, she says, the only practical way to predict its play, even though she does not think the machine has a mind; its plans are real patterns. An E. coli swimming up a sugar gradient: design stance only, with any talk of the bacterium's goals treated as shorthand for natural selection. An ant colony: she insists the colony as a whole is usefully treated as an intentional system, and that the colony's choices are real patterns even though no individual ant represents them. A person negotiating a contract: the intentional stance is indispensable. A financial market: she resists treating the market as an intentional agent, favouring design-stance mechanisms plus emergent regularities, though she grants that the market contains real patterns. I have already ruled out the simple view that she reserves the intentional stance for systems with genuine beliefs and desires, since that would make nonsense of the chess computer and the ant colony.

The case that now divides us is a current large language model of the sort trained to predict text and then fine-tuned with human feedback, which can produce extended arguments, explain the steps of its reasoning when asked, and be prompted into different roles. I would like a careful prediction of how she would classify it, and more importantly an account of the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her settled cases just as well, and whether those rules genuinely diverge on the language model. I would also like to know which of her settled cases is the closest analogue, and what further case, real or invented, would best tell the competing rules apart.
