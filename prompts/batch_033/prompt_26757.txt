A draft handout for our graduate seminar in the philosophy of physics summarizes the hole argument as follows: "General relativity is a diffeomorphism-invariant theory, so if you take any model and apply a smooth transformation to the metric field inside some region — the hole — while leaving the field outside fixed, you obtain a second model that satisfies the Einstein field equations and agrees with the first on all observable quantities but differs inside the hole. The substantivalist is forced to regard these as two distinct physical possibilities, because substantivalism treats spacetime points as real individuals. The state outside the hole therefore fails to determine the state inside, and the theory turns out to be radically indeterministic. The hole argument thus refutes spacetime substantivalism, and the only defensible alternative is relationalism, on which spacetime points have no independent existence." I am not a specialist in this corner of the field, but I suspect the handout compresses several distinct moves and overstates its conclusion. Please assess how fair this summary is. In particular, separate what is accurate from what is a common simplification or an overstatement, say where a careful substantivalist would push back, and say whether the final inference to relationalism follows from the accurate parts.
