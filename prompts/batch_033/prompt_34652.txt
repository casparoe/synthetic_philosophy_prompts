Ok so the St. Petersburg paradox: you flip a fair coin until it comes up heads, and if that takes n flips you get 2^n dollars. The expected value is infinite, but obviously no one would pay more than maybe twenty bucks to play. Doesn't this just straight-up refute expected value maximization as a theory of rational choice? Like why do people still talk about this as a "paradox" that needs resolving instead of just taking it as a counterexample that kills the whole framework?
