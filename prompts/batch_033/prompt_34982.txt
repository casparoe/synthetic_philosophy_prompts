Last night I spent a long time arguing with a friend about whether Gödel's incompleteness theorems show that no formal system could capture human mathematical thought. We kept talking past each other because we had different, unstated standards for what a responsible account of those theorems' significance outside mathematics would have to deliver. I want that standard made explicit before I go any further. Write the desiderata for an adequate account of what Gödel's incompleteness theorems do and do not show outside mathematics — adequate, that is, for sorting the legitimate philosophical uses of the theorems from the overreach. For each requirement, give the consideration or case that makes it a requirement. The list should address at least the following: the hypotheses of the two theorems (consistency, effective axiomatizability, and the amount of arithmetic that must be expressible) and why each hypothesis matters once someone exports the theorems beyond mathematics; the distinction between incompleteness, undecidability, and the unsolvability of the halting problem; the distinction between provability in a system and truth in an intended model; the fact that the theorems are constructive and already apply to surprisingly weak arithmetical theories; the standard alleged applications — to minds and machines, to the limits of artificial intelligence, to physics or law, to the claim that any conceptual scheme has blind spots — and what an adequate account must do with each; and the common move in which someone grants that the Gödel sentence is unprovable in the system but claims we can nevertheless see that it is true, which an adequate account must either explain or refuse and say why. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable constraints set by the mathematics and which are preferences of a particular philosophical camp. Flag any pairs of desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied — in particular, whether full fidelity to the hypotheses can be squared with preserving the sweeping anti-mechanist or limits-of-reason conclusions in their original form. Say which single requirement does the most to exclude the common overreadings. Close with a brief scorecard: how the deflationary reading, the anti-mechanist reading, and the reading on which the theorems set limits on all formal knowledge fare against your list.
