I have, over the past year, come to accept eliminative materialism about the propositional attitudes — the view that our ordinary concepts of belief, desire, intention, and the like belong to a folk-psychological theory that is, at bottom, false, and that a mature cognitive neuroscience will eliminate rather than reduce them, much as phlogiston was eliminated rather than reduced by modern chemistry. The arguments from the explanatory failures of folk psychology, from its historical stagnation, and from the radical mismatch between its categories and what we are learning about neural computation have, to my mind, tipped the balance. I find myself unable to go back to thinking of beliefs and desires as real entities that will find a place in a finished science of the mind.

What I want to know, and what I have had difficulty finding a clear answer to, is this: what would count against this view? I do not mean the standard objections I have already encountered — that eliminativism is self-refuting because the act of asserting it presupposes beliefs, or that introspection makes beliefs seem undeniable. I mean: what would have to be true, or what would we have to discover, for eliminative materialism to be false and for the propositional attitudes to earn their keep in a mature science? What kinds of empirical findings, what kinds of theoretical developments, what kinds of conceptual arguments would genuinely threaten the position?

I would like the answer sorted by how decisive each consideration would be — from fatal to merely awkward — and I would like an honest assessment of whether eliminative materialism, as it is standardly defended, has been qualified in ways that insulate it from falsification. If it has been, I want to know whether that insulation is a problem for a view of this kind, or whether it is the sort of thing one should expect from a thesis about the foundations of a whole domain. I would also like to know which of these defeaters a committed eliminativist would themselves concede as counting against the view, as opposed to ones that only critics press.
