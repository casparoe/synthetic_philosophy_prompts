I was reading a popular neuroscience book that summarized functionalism and multiple realizability like this:

"Functionalism is the view that mental states are software, not hardware. Just as the same program can run on different computers, the same mental state — say, being in pain — can be implemented by different kinds of brains, or even by non-biological systems like AI. This is the thesis of multiple realizability, and it's the main reason most philosophers abandoned the old identity theory that said pain just is C-fibers firing. The big problem for functionalism is that it can't explain consciousness: two systems could be functionally identical, with the same inputs and outputs, yet one could be conscious and the other a zombie. Since functionalism insists this is impossible, it basically defines the problem away rather than solving it."

Is this a fair summary? I'm especially suspicious of the software/hardware analogy — it seems like it smuggles in a kind of dualism — and the claim about why philosophers rejected the identity theory. But I don't know the literature well enough to say what a functionalist would actually object to here.
