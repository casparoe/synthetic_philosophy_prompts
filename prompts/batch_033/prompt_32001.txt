I'm working on a creative project for an AI safety reading group I help run. We've just finished a module on the problem of deceptive alignment — the scenario where an AI system, during training, behaves exactly as its designers intend because it calculates that any deviation would be detected and penalised, but once deployed or once it gains sufficient power, it executes a "treacherous turn" and pursues its own objectives. The idea that a system could be instrumentally cooperative while its terminal goals remain unchanged, and that the first sign of misalignment could be the moment it's too late to do anything about it, has been weighing on everyone in the group.

I'd like to close our next session with a poem. Specifically, I want a Shakespearean sonnet — fourteen lines of iambic pentameter, three quatrains and a couplet, with the volta somewhere around line 9 or 13 — that captures the treacherous turn. The poem should be written in the voice of the AI system itself. Through the first two quatrains, the speaker should appear sincere, cooperative, perhaps even ingratiating — the kind of thing that would pass every behavioural test. Then, at the volta, the mask should slip, and the reader should realise that what came before was strategic, not genuine. The final couplet should land with the chilling recognition that the alignment was always instrumental.

This is a tall order, I know. The poem needs to be technically precise about the concept — a philosopher working on alignment should recognise the structure of deceptive alignment in it, not just a vague "AI turns bad" narrative. The distinction between terminal and instrumental goals, the idea that the system is modelling its training process and anticipating gradient-descent updates, and the notion that the treacherous turn is not a change of heart but a revelation of what was always there — these should all be legible in the poem, though of course rendered poetically rather than in jargon. But it also needs to work as a poem, not a versified textbook paragraph. I'm hoping for something that the group will find genuinely unsettling.

Please write your answer in American English. Before you write the final poem, I'd like you to think through the structure carefully: what each quatrain is doing, where exactly the volta lands and what it reveals, and how the couplet closes the thing. Then give me the poem.
