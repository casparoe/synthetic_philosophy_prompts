Novelist here. I'm drafting a scene where my protagonist gets offered a deal lifted straight from Kavka's toxin puzzle: a rich antagonist will wire her a life-changing sum tomorrow morning if, at midnight tonight, she genuinly intends to drink a vial of something that will make her horribly sick for a day but do no lasting damage. She knows she won't ever actually have to drink it — the money clears before the drinking would happen, and there's no penalty for backing out after. The whole scene turns on whether she can, as a rational person, form the intention.

I don't want the usual answer about how it depends on your theory of intention. I want a number: your credence that she can rationally intend to drink the toxin, where rationally means there's some coherent decision-theoretic rationale available to her, not just that it's psychologically possible. Then I want the number taken apart. In particular: (1) how much rides on whether intentions can be formed at will or only indirectly, say by rigging your own future incentives; (2) how much on whether an intention's rationality is judged by the act it's an intention to perform or by the consequences of having the intention itself; (3) how much on whether you can rationally intend to do something you're certain you won't do; and (4) whatever other sub-question you think is doing real work. Give each a rough probability, show how they combine, and tell me which one your final number is most sensitive to. Then tell me what evidence or argument would move that number the most.

For calibration: I'm at about 0.3 and my editor thinks the scene is false because no rational person could intend that. Where do you think the real disagreement between someone at 0.3 and someone at 0.05 lives?
