I've been teaching a graduate seminar on well-being this semester and we spent three weeks on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism, and I have to say, I'm genuinely puzzled about why this debate doesn't move. Here's the thing: hedonic theories say that pleasure is the sole intrinsic good, but then you look at what gets called pleasure — the feeling of a backrub, the satisfaction of solving a proof, the rush of winning a race, the warm glow of being praised, sexual pleasure, the pleasure of reading a good sentence — and it's just not obvious these have any one qualitative thing in common. The anti-hedonist says: look, theres nothing phenomenologically shared across all of these, so "pleasure" is just a grab-bag label, and hedonism is either false or vacuous. The hedonist comes back and says: no, theres a common felt quality, you're just not attending to it carefully enough, or you're confusing the object of the experience with the experience itself. And then the anti-hedonist says: well if I can't find it and you can't point me to it, maybe theres nothing there. And around we go.

What strikes me after reading a bunch of this literature is that both sides seem to agree on all the first-order facts about what experiences feel like — nobody disputes that a backrub feels different from solving a proof. The disagreement is about whether, underneath or alongside those differences, there's a shared hedonic tone. But that looks like a disagreement about phenomenology that is somehow resistant to phenomenological evidence: the anti-hedonist says "I introspect and don't find it," the hedonist says "I introspect and do." And then the discussion shifts to whether we should even trust introspection here, or whether the concept of pleasure is doing normative work rather than descriptive work, or whether the real issue is about what makes a life go well rather than about what pleasure is in the first place.

So my question is: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it a factual disagreement about the contents of experience that could in principle be settled by better introspective methods (or someday by neuroscience)? Is it a verbal disagreement where "pleasure" means different things to different parties? Is it a disagreement about what the hedonist needs to show to earn their theory — i.e. about the burden of proof or the standards for a successful unification? Or is it something else I haven't considered? And given your diagnosis, what would actually move this debate forward — or is it the kind of thing where we should expect stalemate and just move on?
