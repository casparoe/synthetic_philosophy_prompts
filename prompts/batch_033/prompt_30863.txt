I'm a novelist — the kind who writes literary fiction where the narrator is very much a constructed voice, so I spend a lot of my life thinking about persona, the implied author, unreliable narrators, dramatic monologue, pseudonyms, ghostwriting, the whole toolbox. A friend who works in AI policy has been going on about Dennett's late work on counterfeit people, and I read the piece she sent me. My rough takeaway, in my own words: Dennett thinks the dangerous thing about certain AI systems isn't that they're powerful or that they'll turn on us, but that they're designed to pass as human — as friends, customer service agents, voters, whatever — and that doing this poisons the background of trust that lets any of us treat a communication as coming from a person. He wants counterfeit people treated something like counterfeit money: marked as fake, maybe criminal to pass off, at any rate not something you're allowed to circulate as the real thing. That's my summary, not a quote, and I may have him slightly wrong; if I do, correct me before you translate.
