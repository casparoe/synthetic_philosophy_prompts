I help run a small, independent research fund that can support roughly four conceptual researchers for two years, with a remit covering the foundations of AI alignment rather than empirical machine learning. We are about to allocate our next round among proposals that all fall under the loose heading of inner alignment: the problem of ensuring that a trained system is actually pursuing the objective its designers intended, rather than some correlated objective that produces the same behavior during training and then diverges later. I am not looking for a survey of positions; I want a defended judgment about where scarce conceptual work should go first.

The proposals cluster around a handful of live questions: whether goal misgeneralization is best treated as a special case of ordinary distribution shift or as a distinct phenomenon requiring its own theory; whether the mesa-optimizer picture — gradient descent selecting policies that are themselves optimizers — is a useful idealization or a systematically misleading frame; how to tell deceptive from non-deceptive misgeneralization without already knowing the system's true objective; whether inner and outer alignment can be separated cleanly, or whether the distinction blurs once the training process is specified in detail; and how much of the remaining problem is conceptual — about what it is for a system to have a goal at all — as opposed to technical, about inductive biases and training dynamics.

Please give me a ranking of the two or three questions most worth funding, assuming our comparative advantage is conceptual and philosophical. For each, explain which other questions depend on settling it first, how tractable it is with current conceptual tools, which downstream debates it would settle or reframe, and what developments over the next two years would make you move it up or down. Also flag any of the framings above that you think is itself confused in a way that should be cleared up before empirical work can be interpreted; I do not want to fund a research program that is sharpening a bad question.
