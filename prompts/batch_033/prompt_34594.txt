here's what i think i think:

(a) pure time preference — discounting future wellbeing just because it's temporally distant — is morally arbitrary and unjustified

(b) the non-identity problem is a real problem, not a pseudoproblem. Parfit was right that if our choices affect who gets born, we can't straightforwardly say we've harmed those future people by making them worse off than they would have been, because they wouldn't have existed otherwise

(c) i'm drawn to person-affecting views: an act can only be wrong if it's wrong for some particular person. i don't think we should be trying to maximize some impersonal quantity like total utility

(e) i don't think having a child makes me a hypocrite about climate change. if anything, loving someone who'll inherit the mess makes the moral urgency feel more real, not less
