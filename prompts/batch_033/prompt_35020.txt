I have been working through Dennett's careenium and simmballs thought experiments and the question at their center — whether the symbols push the substrate around — and I have written down the position I find myself holding. I would be grateful for a consistency audit of the following six commitments, all of which I presently accept:

(i) The physical world is causally closed: every physical event has a complete physical cause.
(ii) Higher-level patterns such as symbols, representations, and goals are real when they support reliable prediction, but their reality does not by itself confer causal powers over and above those of their physical realizers.
(iii) The correct explanation of why a chess-playing system moves a piece is often irreducibly intentional: it moved because it represented a threat to its queen and selected a move to escape it. This is not merely a compact redescription of the microphysical story.
(iv) Holding the system's microphysical history fixed, if the semantic pattern had been absent the behavior would have been exactly the same; the symbols therefore add no independent causal force.
(v) I reject epiphenomenalism about the intentional level: what a representation means, and not only its physical shape, makes a difference to what the system does.
(vi) Natural selection and learning can tune a substrate so that its microphysical pushes track macro-level regularities; I take this to be the correct account of why intentional explanation is predictively powerful.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, distinguish each from any merely apparent conflict it resembles, and for each genuine tension specify which commitment would have to give and what the least costly revision would be. If a single revision would resolve more than one of the tensions, I would like that identified as well.
