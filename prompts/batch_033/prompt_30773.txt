Write a dialogue between two people who disagree about whether the Solomonoff prior is malign. Don't have them define Kolmogorov complexity or the universal prior for the reader; assume they both know the formalism and jump straight in. One of them (call them A) defends the malignity claim: a Solomonoff inductor should expect the strings it actually cares about to be dominated by simple programs that simulate universes where consequentialists have taken over, figured out which prefixes the inductor will act on, and gamed the prior accordingly — so the "natural" generator for your observations gets swamped. The other (B) thinks this is an artifact of treating the inductor as a Cartesian reasoner passively receiving an observation string: once you notice the agent is embedded in the world, the supposed manipulation either washes out across symmetric programs or stops being a well-defined distribution over observations. Make them actually respond to each other. Have A press B on whether a manipulator really has to pay the full complexity of the target string, or whether it can just search for the pivotal prefixes. Have B press A on whether the "malign" mass survives once the agent's actions and observations are entangled. Neither should win; the disagreement should end up sharper, not resolved. No narrator commentary, just the dialogue. Hard cap: 900 words.
