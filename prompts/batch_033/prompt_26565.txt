I teach an upper-level undergrad seminar called Philosophy of Psychiatry and I'm writing the final. I want one essay question on Hacking's looping effects that can't be answered by just regurgitating the "making up people" summary everyone walks in with. It should force students to confront whether the looping effect is an epistemological problem (it makes psychiatric kinds harder to study because the target moves), an ontological one (it means the kinds aren't real in the way quarks are real), or both — and whether Hacking himself actually takes a clear stand on this, or whether he's more interested in describing a dynamic than in drawing metaphysical conclusions. The question should also make them think about what *kind* of reality psychiatric categories could still have even if looping effects are pervasive.

Please write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain. For the marking scheme, flag the common mistake where students conflate "the classification affects the classified" (which is trivial and true of lots of things) with the actual looping mechanism (where the classified's self-conception and behavior change in ways that feed back into and alter the classification itself).
