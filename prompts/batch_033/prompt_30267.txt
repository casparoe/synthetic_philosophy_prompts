Ok so a friend and I were arguing about whether it can ever be good *for the kid* to bring a kid into existence, and he ended up constructing this argument that I can't get out of. It seems to show way more then either of us believes, but I can't find the step to reject. Here it is:

(1) The procreation asymmetry says: for a person who is never brought into existence, the absence of pain is good, but the absence of pleasure is not bad — there's no one there to be deprived of it.
(2) There's no relevant difference between a person who never comes into existence and a person who stops existing. In both cases, after the fact, there's no one left to be deprived.
(3) So if the absence of pain is good in the never-born case, it's also good in the death case.
(4) And if the absence of pleasure isn't bad in the never-born case, it isn't bad in the death case either.
(5) So for any existing person, painlessly ending their life now has a good aspect — it prevents every future pain — and lacks the usual bad aspect, since the lost future pleasures don't count as bad.
(6) If an action has a good aspect and lacks the bad aspect, we ought to do it, all else equal.
(7) All else is equal here, assuming we could do it painlessly and without panic.
(8) So the asymmetry commits us to painlessly ending all sentient life, which is absurd.
(9) Therefore the asymmetry is false.

I'd much rather keep the asymmetry than accept (8), but I can't see how to block the slide from (1) to (8) without it looking like special pleading about birth. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
