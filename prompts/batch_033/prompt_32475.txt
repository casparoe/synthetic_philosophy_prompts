I've been defending a view about pain for a while now — basically, that the badness of pain is built right into the sensation itself. Like, when you stub your toe, the ouch-quality and the this-sucks-quality aren't two separate things that happen to co-occur; the hurting just is the badness. I know there's a whole literature on this and I'm not a professional philosopher (I'm a grad student in neuroscience, so I've bumped into the relevant stuff but mostly from the science side), but this view just seems obviously right to me from introspection. The idea that you could have the exact same sensation and it not be bad — that seems like a confusion.

But I keep running into cases that people bring up, like pain asymbolia patients who say things like yes it hurts but I don't mind it, or the classic morphine reports where people say the pain is still there but it's not bothering them anymore. I've always sort of dismissed these as people being confused about their own experience, or maybe the sensation actually changes and they're just bad at reporting it. But I'm trying to be more intellectually honest about this, and honestly the fact that these cases keep coming up in the literature is starting to make me wonder if I'm just protecting my view rather than testing it.

So here's what I want: I want you to act as a philosopher of mind who's sympathetic to my view but won't let it get away with anything. Take the position that the badness of pain is intrinsic to the sensation seriously, and then tell me, as thoroughly as you can, what would count against it. Not just the obvious stuff I already mentioned, but the full landscape. What empirical findings would be genuinely awkward? What conceptual arguments would a defender of my view have to bite bullets on? Are there ways my view could be qualified to handle the counterevidence without becoming empty? And honestly — am I just insulating my view from falsification? Start by brainstorming the strongest challenges you can think of, then organize them by how damaging they actually are, from merely awkward to probably fatal. I'd especially appreciate it if you could distinguish between challenges that a defender of my view would themselves concede as counting against them, versus challenges that only make sense from outside the view.
