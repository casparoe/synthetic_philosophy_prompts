I'm on the board of a small maritime museum down in Devon. Our main exhibit is the Perseverance, a whaling ship from the 1820s that's been sitting in our dry dock since 1923. The museum's founding charter says our purpose is 'to preserve the Perseverance for the public benefit.' Here's the thing: over the last hundred years we've replaced virtualy every piece of timber in her, bit by bit, across five major restorations and countless smaller repairs. The last survey says less than 3% of the current wood dates from her working life. The most recent restoration was in 2017, when we replaced the last of the original deck planking. Now the hull needs another £200,000 of work, and the Heritage Lottery Fund has told us they'll consider an application, but they want us to clarify whether we're asking them to fund the preservation of a historic ship or the maintenance of a replica. Our board is split right down the middle. Half says obviously it's the same ship — we've been looking after her continuously, the repairs were done gradually and with care, you wouldn't say a person stops being themselves because their cells get replaced. The other half says come off it, it's a replica that's been assembled over decades, and the charter probaly doesn't even apply anymore. I need to draft a recommendation for our board meeting next month. It should say what we should do about the funding application and how we should describe the ship to the public going forward, and it should take both sides seriously — I don't want a document that just picks a side and dismisses the other lot.
