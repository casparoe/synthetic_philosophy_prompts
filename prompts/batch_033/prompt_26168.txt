I am a novelist, and I have been trying to locate the right term — if one exists — for a phenomenon I encounter constantly in writing workshops, in craft books, and in my own thinking while drafting.

There are certain beliefs about fiction that we treat as effectively unquestionable, not because we have verified them or because we hold them dogmatically, but because questioning them would make the entire activity of talking about craft unintelligible. For instance: that a novel should aim at some kind of emotional truth, that showing is generally superior to telling, that a character must want something, that a story requires change or movement of some kind. If someone in a workshop said, "I do not grant that emotional truth matters," or "Why must a character want anything at all?", the conversation would not become a debate about those claims. It would simply stall. The challenge does not feel false so much as it feels like it is not a move in the game we are playing.

These are not articles of faith — no one would say they are sacred or held on authority. They are more like the floor on which the furniture of disagreement rests. You can argue about whether a particular character's desire is rendered clearly enough, but you cannot argue about whether characters need desires at all and still be doing the same thing.

I have encountered the phrase "hinge propositions" in connection with Wittgenstein's On Certainty, but that seems to concern knowledge and skepticism — the idea that you cannot doubt that the world is more than five minutes old because doubt itself requires a framework. The craft assumptions I am describing are not about knowledge; they are about what makes a shared practice possible in the first place. Is there an established term for this — for the beliefs that constitute a practice rather than functioning as claims within it? If "hinge" is the nearest concept, what are the important differences I should keep in mind? And if no settled term exists, what would you propose calling them, and how would you demarcate the boundaries of the phenomenon?

Please write your answer in American English.
