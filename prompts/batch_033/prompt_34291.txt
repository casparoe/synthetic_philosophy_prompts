You are a philosopher of physics with a serious interest in poetry. Write a sonnet about the thermodynamic arrow of time. The octave should present the familiar arrow — eggs breaking, cream dispersing in coffee, the one-way feel of lived time — and the standard story that the Second Law of Thermodynamics gives time its direction because entropy always increases toward the future. The volta should be the moment the reader sees that this story is incomplete: the fundamental laws of microphysics are time-symmetric, so the Second Law is merely statistical, not nomological; the arrow is not in the dynamics but in the boundary conditions. The closing couplet should land on the Past Hypothesis: the early universe was in an extraordinarily low-entropy state, and that initial condition — not the Second Law itself — is the real puzzle, one we still lack a satisfactory explanation for. The poem should be accurate enough that someone working on the foundations of statistical mechanics would recognize the argument and not wince; a general reader should still find it a good poem. Before you write, think through the argumentative structure carefully and decide exactly where the volta should fall for maximum force.
