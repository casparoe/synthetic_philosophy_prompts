Last night I had a long argument with a friend about whether my growing reliance on large language models is quietly costing me my understanding of the material I work on. It left me with a list of six things I seem to believe, and I want to know whether they form a consistent package before I try to defend any of them in print. Here they are:

(1) Understanding requires more than reliable output. It requires grasping how the parts of a subject fit together, and that grasp has to be mine rather than stored in an external system I merely consult.

(2) Knowledge can outrun understanding. I can know that a proof is correct because a competent colleague checked it, or that a calculation is correct because a calculator produced it, without myself understanding why it is correct.

(3) I am drawn to the extended mind thesis: under the right kind of coupling, an artifact such as a notebook, a heavily used phone, or an LLM interface can be a proper part of my cognitive process, not merely a tool I operate.

(4) My own increasing reliance on LLMs to summarize papers, reconstruct dialectics, and draft arguments is eroding my understanding of that material in a way that matters, even when the outputs are accurate and I could still check any one of them against its source.

(5) This is not a new kind of epistemic dependence. It differs only in degree from my dependence on textbooks, colleagues, search engines, and calculators.

(6) The right test for whether an instance of cognitive offloading is acceptable is whether I could, if prompted, reconstruct or independently verify what was offloaded, and whether the practice preserves that capacity over time.

Please audit this set. Which commitments are actually in tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension, and which pair should I be most worried about? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equal. For each tension, state what exactly pulls against what, whether the conflict is genuine or merely apparent, what the cheapest revision would be, and what that revision costs. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.

After the audit, append a JSON object with this schema:

- commitments: an array of my six commitments as strings, numbered 1 through 6.
- tensions: an array of objects, each with the fields:
  - pair: [integer, integer] giving the two commitment numbers;
  - severity: one of high, medium, low, or apparent_only;
  - genuine: a boolean;
  - mechanism: a one-sentence statement of what actually pulls against what;
  - cheapest_fix: which commitment to revise and how;
  - cost: what that revision gives up.
- ranked_pairs: the pairs in descending order of severity, most severe first.
- single_revision: an object with fields revise (which commitment), how (the revision), and cleans_up (an array of the pairs it resolves).
