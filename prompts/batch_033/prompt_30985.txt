I'm a software engineer, lead on a six-person team building an LLM agent that writes code, runs shell commands, and posts to internal Slack. We're about to ship it to a few hundred internal users, and the VP has to sign off on the risk plan. Every couple weeks someone drops a new safety idea into the design doc: more RLHF, red-team it, log all outputs, sandbox the file system, train it to refuse dangerous requests, have a second model review the transcript, build interpretability tooling. I have no formal philosophy background, but I've picked up enough to think there's a real difference between "control" measures and "alignment" measures — roughly, stuff that keeps you safe even if the model is actively misaligned versus stuff that only helps if the model is basically trying to cooperate. I need a reusable procedure I can run in a one-hour design review to sort a proposed measure into the right bucket and decide whether it's earned its eng time.

Write it as a checklist or decision tree I can follow without knowing the theory: the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure stops and says "this is not a control measure, this is a bet on alignment" and what that implies for how we treat it, and where it hands off to a human because the judgment call can't be eliminated. Then run it on three of the measures above — sandboxing the file system, a second model reviewing transcripts, and more RLHF — and make a definite call on each, even if the answer is one my team won't like. I'd rather you commit to verdicts with reasons than lay out a menu of considerations. And be honest about what the procedure can't do or will systematically get wrong.
