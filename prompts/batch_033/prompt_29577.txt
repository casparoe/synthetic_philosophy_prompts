I spent my career in molecular biology, and I have only recently begun reading philosophy of mind seriously. One debate I find both fascinating and frustrating is the dispute within panpsychism over the combination problem. On one side, constitutive panpsychists hold that the micro-experiences of fundamental entities genuinely compose the macro-experience of a human or animal subject — that combination has a solution, even if we have not yet articulated it fully. On the other side, cosmopsychists regard the combination problem as fatal to constitutive panpsychism and reverse the explanatory order: there is one fundamental cosmic subject, and individual subjects arise from it by some process of dissociation, limitation, or filtering.

I would like you to draft the joint statement these two camps could produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The document should include the following.

First, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign. These should not be vacuous; they should capture substantive common ground about what consciousness is like, what needs explaining, and what constraints a theory must satisfy.

Second, a disagreements table. For each point of genuine disagreement, state the constitutive panpsychist position and the cosmopsychist position in wording each side would accept for its own view. Be precise about where the disagreement lies — for example, whether it concerns the formal properties of composition, the metaphysical direction of dependence, or the empirical signatures of each view.

Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, describe what both sides would agree counts as a relevant consideration or test, and record what each side predicts.

I am less interested in seeing the debate reframed as merely verbal than in seeing what remains genuinely unsettled once terminological confusions are cleared away. If some of the disagreement turns out to be about words, say so, but focus on the remainder.
