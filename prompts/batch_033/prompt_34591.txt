I am trying to work out my views on the moral significance of political boundaries, and I would like a consistency audit of the following set of commitments I have arrived at. Please go through them and identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to be in tension, and rank the tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, indicate what would have to give, and say which single revision would resolve the most problems at the lowest cost.

Here are the commitments:

(1) Birthright citizenship is morally arbitrary in a way that matters. The distribution of life chances by birthplace is a global lottery, and no one deserves the advantages or disadvantages that flow from it. This arbitrariness substantially weakens the justificatory force of any claim that depends on it.

(2) We have special associative obligations to our fellow citizens — obligations that arise from the shared political relationship itself and are not reducible to universal duties efficiently discharged through a division of moral labor.

(3) These associative obligations are strongest when the state genuinely functions as a cooperative scheme for mutual benefit, and they are weaker when the state is little more than a system of coercion — which means their strength varies with the justice of the state's internal arrangements.

(4) A just global order would require dramatically more open borders than any wealthy state currently maintains, and wealthy states have demanding, enforceable duties of distributive justice to the global poor.

(5) Democratic self-determination requires that a bounded political community have the right to control its own membership, including through some immigration restrictions, though this right is not absolute.

(6) The duties in (4) are duties of justice, not charity, and they are at least as weighty as the associative obligations in (2) — so in cases of conflict, we cannot simply assume the latter take priority.
