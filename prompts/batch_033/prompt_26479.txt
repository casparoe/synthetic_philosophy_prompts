ok so i'm on my university's student life technology committee and we've got this startup pitching "ThrivePods" — basically super immersive VR pods they want to install in the student union. the pitch is students book 45-min sessions where they get to experience deeply fulfilling stuff: climbing everest, giving a TED talk, falling in love, whatever. the neural feedback makes it feel completely real in the moment. theyre marketing it as a mental health / well-being intervention.

my friend and i got into this massive argument about it last night. he thinks its obviously great — if it makes students feel genuinely happy and fulfilled, whats the problem? i kept pushing back that theres something hollow about it, that well-being isnt just about having certain experiences, its about actually doing things and being a certain kind of person. we went back and forth for hours and im still not sure where i land.

anyway the committee needs me to draft a recommendation memo before our vote next week. we have to decide whether to approve a one-year pilot of six pods in the student center, funded partly by student activity fees. the memo needs a clear yes/no/conditions recommendation and it has to engage seriously with both sides — including the strongest argument for the position im recommending against. the chair specifically said she doesnt want this to turn into a philosophy seminar but she does want the reasoning to be substantive.
