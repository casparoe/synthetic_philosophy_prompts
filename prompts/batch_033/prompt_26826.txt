I've been trying to get clear on what I actually count as an emotion, by going off my gut on a bunch of cases, and I'd like a philosopher of emotion (or cognitive scientist, whichever fits) to tell me what principle, if any, captures my verdicts. Here's where I land. Getting cut off in traffic and feeling that hot wave of anger: definitely an emotion. Being startled by a door slamming behind me: not an emotion, just a reflex, even though it feels like a tiny burst of fear. Being irritable all afternoon for no reason I can point to: I want to call that a mood, not an emotion, but I can't say why. Feeling proud when my sister graduates: emotion. Feeling hungry or tired: not an emotion. The wave of sadness when a character dies in a book: emotion, even though I know they're not real. A creeping "something's off" feeling in a dark parking lot: I'd call that fear, so emotion, even though I can't say exactly what I'm afraid of. Jealousy when I see an ex with someone new: emotion, and so is the guilt right after for being jealous. But being in love with my partner for years: that feels like something else — "emotion" isn't exactly wrong but it sounds too small. And fear in a nightmare: yes while it's happening, but its weird to call it an emotion once I wake up and it evaporates with the dream.

Is there one principle that sorts all of these the way I do, or close? I'd rather have a principle that forces me to change a few verdicts than a complicated list of exceptions. And what does the principle say about the cases I keep going back and forth on: grief that comes in waves over a year, the little shiver from a song, being hangry, and that wince you get watching someone about to do something embarrassing on a live show?

Before you settle on an answer, maybe start by going through the cases and flagging which of my verdicts you'd be most willing to overturn and why — I care more about the carving than the label. Then give me the principle, or the two or three candidate principles, say which of my verdicts each one can't keep, which you'd actually defend, and what it predicts for a couple of cases I haven't listed. Also tell me what would change your mind: which case, if my gut went the other way on it, would force you to a different principle, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
