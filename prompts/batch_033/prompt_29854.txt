I've been following a philosopher's work on lexical threshold negative utilitarianism — the view that there's a threshold of suffering intensity above which no amount of pleasure or lesser suffering prevented can justify inflicting it, while below the threshold, pleasure and suffering are commensurable and ordinary tradeoffs apply. I've collected her verdicts on a series of cases, and I want to know what she'd say about a new one, which candidate rule or rules best fit her track record, and what further case would cleanly distinguish them.

Her verdicts so far:

1. Inflicting a year of excruciating torture on one person to give billions an eternity of the highest bliss: impermissible.
2. Causing one person a mild headache for an hour to prevent a thousand people from years of moderate depression: permissible.
3. Causing one person the pain of a broken bone, untreated, to give a million people each a day of moderate pleasure: impermissible.
4. Causing one person intense nausea for an hour to prevent a million people from an hour of mild boredom: impermissible.
5. Causing one person a moderately uncomfortable itchy rash for a week to prevent ten thousand people from a year of severe chronic pain: permissible.

The new case: causing one person thirty seconds of the raw physical sensation of drowning — the overwhelming visceral distress, but without panic or the belief that one is dying — to prevent a billion people from each experiencing a full year of moderate but genuine suffering, say the level of a persistent tension headache.

What does she say about the new case? What are the distinct rules that fit her first five verdicts, which ones does the new case split, and what is the minimal further case that would force her to reveal exactly where and on what basis she draws the threshold?
