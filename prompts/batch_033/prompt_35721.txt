So I've been trying to get clear on what I actually think about adaptationism, and my gut keeps pulling me in different directions depending on the case. I want to know if there's a principle behind my verdicts or if I'm just inconsistent.

Here are my calls on a bunch of traits — whether they're adaptations, spandrels, or exaptations:

- The redness of blood: spandrel, no question. It's just a side effect of the iron-porphyrin complex in hemoglobin. Oxygen transport was what selection cared about; the color came along for the ride.
- The human chin: spandrel. It's a byproduct of the alveolar region shrinking relative to the mandible during hominin evolution. Nobody was selecting for a pointy chin.
- Bird feathers: originally an adaptation for thermoregulation, later exapted for flight. That feels exactly right to me — insulation first, aerodynamics later.
- The panda's thumb: adaptation. Yeah it's a clunky radial sesamoid bone, not a real digit, but it was shaped by selection for stripping bamboo. The fact that it's built from available parts doesn't make it a spandrel.
- Human language: adaptation. The design is too complex and the fitness payoff too obvious. I don't buy the story that it's a spandrel of big brains.
- Music: I go back and forth but I lean spandrel. It feels like it piggybacks on auditory scene analysis, social bonding mechanisms, motor coordination — all of which evolved for other reasons. But then I read about mothers singing to infants across every culture and I think, wait, maybe there was direct selection for that.
- Religious belief: spandrel. Hyperactive agency detection plus theory of mind plus coalitional psychology — none of them evolved for religion, but together they spit it out.
- The hollow bones of birds: adaptation for weight reduction in flight. No hesitation whatsoever.
- Menopause in humans: adaptation (the grandmother hypothesis). Selection for ceasing reproduction to invest in existing offspring and grand-offspring. I find the evidence pretty convincing.

Here's the thing that bothers me. I'm confident language is an adaptation and music is a spandrel, but when I try to articulate *why* I treat them differently, the reasons I give for language — complex design, universality, clear fitness benefit, early emergence — all seem to apply to music too. And the reasons I give for music being a spandrel — no dedicated module, co-opts older systems, hard to identify a specific selection pressure — have all been claimed for language by the spandrel camp. So either I'm wrong about language, wrong about music, or my implicit principle needs to be more nuanced than I realize.

Can you extract the principle or principles that best fit my verdicts, tell me where they force me to change my mind, and apply them to a case I haven't considered — say, the near-universal human tendency to make visual art (cave paintings, body decoration, etc.)? And I'd like you to tell me what argument or evidence would change your mind about whatever principle you end up endorsing — where's the reasoning most likely to fail, and what would it take for you to abandon it?
