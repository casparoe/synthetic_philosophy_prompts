ok so my ethics bowl team was prepping a case about a guy who was texting while driving and hit a kid who ran into the road, and someone pointed out that the only reason he's in serious trouble instead of getting a small fine is that the kid happened to be there. my freind built this whole argument out of it and now i can't stop thinking about it because the conclusion is clearly insane but every premise feels true. can you help me figure out where it breaks?

here's the argument:

(1) you can only be blamed for something if you had control over it — that's why we don't blame someone whose brakes fail.
(2) two drivers both look down at their phones for the same three seconds. in one case a kid runs into the road and gets hit; in the other the road is empty.
(3) neither driver controlled whether a kid would be there, so if blame tracks control, the difference in how much we blame them can't be justified.
(4) but we do blame them differently: one gets called a killer and faces prison, the other just gets a ticket and a lecture.
(5) you might say ok, the outcome was luck, but both are still to blame for the choice to look at the phone. except the choice itself came from how impulsive and distractible they are, which came from their genes and upbringing and a million other things they never chose.
(6) so the same logic that made the outcome not their fault also makes the choice not their fault.
(7) and if neither the outcome nor the choice is their fault, then nothing is ever anyone's fault — not just this driver, everyone, for everything.
(8) but that means we'd have to stop blaming people for murder and stop praising people for being brave or generous, because that's all luck too. that can't be right.
(9) so either the control principle in (1) is false, or nobody is ever responsible for anything.

i really want to keep (1) because it seems like the whole reason excuses work at all, but i also can't accept (7). is there a step where this argument sneaks from "you didn't control this particular thing" to "you didn't control anything"? or is it valid and i just have to pick which premise to give up? whichever it is, what does giving it up cost?
