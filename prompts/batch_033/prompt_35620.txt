You are a professor of moral philosophy whose research focuses on the ethics of blame, forgiveness, and moral responsibility. Please think through the following situation carefully, exploring multiple angles before settling on a conclusion. At the end, I would like you to identify the part of your reasoning you are least confident about and describe what kind of argument or consideration would most likely lead you to a different verdict.

Here is the situation.

I am a mid-level manager at a consulting firm. About four years ago, when I was a junior associate, I regularly padded my billable hours—maybe by 10 to 15 percent each week. I rationalized it at the time by telling myself everybody did it and the clients could afford it. I was never caught, and around two years ago I stopped, not because I got caught but because I gradually came to feel it was dishonest in a way I could not live with. I have never told anyone at the firm about this.

Last week I discovered that a junior associate on my team, someone I now supervise, has been doing almost exactly the same thing—padding his billable hours by a similar margin. I have access to the time-tracking data and I am confident about what I am seeing. As his manager, I am in a position to confront him directly or to report it to HR.

When I discussed this with a close friend, he said I have no right to call this person out. His argument: I did the same thing, I never faced any consequences, and I never even admitted it to the firm. If I confront this associate now, I am a hypocrite, and my blame would carry no weight. My friend thinks the right move is to stay out of it—or, if I care about the practice itself, to start by confessing my own past conduct and accepting whatever consequences come with that.

I am torn. On one hand, what the associate is doing is wrong, and as his manager I have a responsibility to address it. On the other hand, my friend's point about hypocrisy feels like it has genuine force, and I cannot tell whether I am just looking for a way to feel morally superior while ignoring my own record.

What should I do? Specifically: do I have the standing to blame this associate, given my own history? If your answer is that I lack standing, does that mean I should simply stay silent, or is there some other appropriate response? And if I do have standing, what difference does my own past make to how I should go about it?
