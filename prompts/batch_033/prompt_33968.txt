You are a professional philosopher specializing in the knowing-how / knowing-that debate, with a deep command of both the Rylean anti-intellectualist tradition and the post–Stanley and Williamson intellectualist responses. You are preparing material for a novelist who will use it to conduct an interview. Think carefully through the dialectic before drafting anything: begin by briefly mapping the standard moves and countermoves on each issue, then use that map to construct questions that genuinely press where the intellectualist position is weakest.

I am a novelist working on a book whose protagonist is a master luthier — someone who has spent decades learning to carve, bend, and varnish wood into instruments, and whose knowledge resides in her hands as much as in any proposition she could state. One of the novel's central tensions is the gap between what she can articulate about her craft and what she demonstrably knows how to do. To deepen my grasp of this tension, I have arranged to interview a philosopher who defends the intellectualist position on knowing-how: roughly, the view that knowing how to φ is a species of knowing-that — specifically, knowing that some way w is a way for one to φ, entertained under a practical mode of presentation.

I want the interview to be a genuine stress test of the view, not an exposition of it. Please draft eight to ten interview questions for me. I want at least one question on each of the following pressure points, and for each, a follow-up question keyed to the reply the guest is most likely to give:

- The regress worry: if knowing how to φ requires entertaining a proposition, does entertaining a proposition itself require knowing how to do so, and does that not lead to the very regress Ryle warned about? The likely reply will invoke the practical mode of presentation as something that does not itself require further know-how. I want a follow-up that presses on whether this move merely relocates the problem rather than dissolving it.

- The inarticulate expert: the luthier who says "I just know when the wood is ready" and cannot specify the proposition she allegedly knows. The likely reply will distinguish knowing-that from being able to articulate what one knows. I want a follow-up that asks what, if anything, constrains the content of the tacit proposition — why it is not simply a placeholder for whatever accounts for the skill.

- Animal cognition: creatures that plainly know how to navigate, build, hunt, or flee but lack the conceptual resources for propositional attitudes. The likely reply will either deny that animals possess genuine know-how or argue that they do entertain propositions in some minimal sense. I want a follow-up that explores what is lost under either horn — what the view is forced to say about the continuity between human and animal skill.

- The phenomenology of fluent action: what it feels like to be in flow, where explicit thought drops away and the agent seems to act without entertaining propositions at all. The likely reply will treat phenomenology as an unreliable guide to the metaphysics of mental states. I want a follow-up that asks whether the intellectualist can offer any positive account of why the phenomenology of skilled action is so systematically misleading, or whether the view simply brackets the first-person experience as irrelevant.

Beyond these four areas, include additional questions that draw connections between them — for instance, whether the same move that handles the regress also handles the phenomenology objection, or whether the animal-cognition and inarticulate-expert cases are really distinct problems or variants of the same underlying difficulty.

For each question-and-follow-up pair, I want the follow-up to genuinely probe the weakest point in the expected reply, not merely to shift the topic or restate the original objection in different words. If the standard reply has a known vulnerability in the literature, press on that vulnerability.

Finally, please include a brief framing note for me as the interviewer: indicate which parts of the dialectic are broadly settled among specialists and which remain genuinely contested, so that I do not inadvertently present a minority view as the consensus or a live debate as closed.
