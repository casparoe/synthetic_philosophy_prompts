I've been going down a rabbit hole on lexical threshold negative utilitarianism — the idea that there's some level of suffering so severe that no amount of pleasure (or mild suffering reduction) can compensate for it, and below that threshold normal tradeoffs apply. I'm trying to figure out what a PhD thesis in this area should actually focus on, assuming about three years of work.

What do you think are the four or five most important open problems here? Rank them and defend the ordering. I care about: (a) which problems, if resolved one way, would most change whether the overall view is defensible or collapses into something else; (b) which are tractable for someone doing conceptual/philosophical work rather than empirical psychology; (c) which are upstream of other questions. I'm less interested in problems that are just tidying up definitions.

A couple of the tensions I keep coming back to: the threshold-location problem (how do you non-arbitrarily say where the threshold is, and does the view even need a precise location to be action-guiding?), the aggregation-across-persons problem (if one person is at the threshold and a million others have mild pleasures, does the lexical priority still hold?), and whether the view ends up implying something fanatical — like that a 0.0001% chance of preventing threshold-level suffering swamps all other considerations. But maybe those aren't the right ones to prioritize, and I'm missing something deeper.

So: what's the agenda? And what would make you change the ranking — what evidence or argument over the next couple years would shift things around?
