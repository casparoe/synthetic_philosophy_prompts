You are an expert in the philosophy of science, specialising in the scientific realism debate, and you also have a practised hand at formal verse. I want you to write a villanelle about the pessimistic meta-induction and the structural realist's reply.

The pessimistic meta-induction runs: the historical record is a graveyard of theories that were empirically successful by the standards of their day — the phlogiston theory, the caloric theory of heat, Fresnel's ether theory of light — yet were eventually discarded as false. By induction, our own best theories are likely headed for the same fate. The structural realist replies: yes, but look at what survived. The mathematical equations of Fresnel's optics carried over into Maxwell's theory; the empirical regularities the caloric theory captured were preserved in thermodynamics. What perishes is the ontology — the stuff the theory said the world contained. What endures is the structure.

The villanelle's two refrains should do the argumentative work. One refrain should voice the anti-realist's induction — the recurrence of falsehood across the history of science. The other should voice the structural realist's answer — that structure survives. The form should track the dialectic: the refrains alternate through the tercets, each undercutting the other, until they meet in the final quatrain. That meeting should not resolve the argument into a tidy victory for either side. It should show that the structural realist has conceded something genuine — the induction is not a sophism; the graveyard is real — but has shifted the question from whether our theories are true to what in them has earned the right to be called knowledge.

The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of science working on the realism debate would recognise the argument and the specific move from ontology to structure, and would find the poem right rather than merely name-dropping. Someone who knows nothing of the debate should still find it a good poem. Use British English spelling and conventions throughout.

Before writing the final villanelle, think through the argumentative structure: what each tercet needs to establish or turn, and how the two refrains interact across the stanzas. Sketch this out briefly, then write the poem.

After the poem, include a final line beginning "Summary:" that states in one sentence what the poem argues about the pessimistic meta-induction.
