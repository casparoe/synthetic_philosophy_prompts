"The money pump argument is meant to show that having intransitive preferences is irrational. Suppose an agent prefers A to B, B to C, and C to A. These preferences form a cycle, which violates transitivity. A money pump works like this: a trader offers to exchange C for B plus a small fee. Since the agent prefers B to C, she accepts. Then the trader offers A for B plus a small fee. Since she prefers A to B, she accepts. Finally the trader offers C for A plus a small fee. Since she prefers C to A, she accepts. The agent now holds C again, exactly where she started, but she has paid three fees and is strictly worse off. This is exactly parallel to Dutch book arguments in probability theory: just as a Dutch book demonstrates that violating the probability axioms makes you vulnerable to a guaranteed loss, the money pump demonstrates that violating transitivity makes you vulnerable to a guaranteed loss. The argument succeeds in showing that rationality requires transitive preferences, because any agent with intransitive preferences can be turned into a money pump — they will reliably lose resources for no gain."
