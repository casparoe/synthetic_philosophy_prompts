I'm a PhD student in philosophy of biology, but my own work is on species concepts, so I'm basically an outsider to the animal communication literature, and there's a pattern I keep noticing there and in the anthropology reading I do on the side that I can't find a precise name for. The clearest example is a gazelle that, when it spots a predator, does this stiff-legged bouncing display instead of running; it looks like it's wasting energy and drawing attention to itself, and the usual story is that it's advertising how fit it is, and the display is credible because a weak gazelle couldn't afford to pull it off. Same shape with peacocks: the tail is heavy, costly to grow, and makes escaping predators harder, yet peahens apparently read it as a sign of quality. And in humans I see the same logic in goods whose entire point is that they're visibly wasteful, and in painful initiation rituals that seem to produce group trust precisely because a free-rider wouldn't pay the price.

What I can't sort out is wether this is one idea or several that the literature runs together, and its not obvious to me whether the difference matters. Sometimes the claim seems to be just that a signal can be reliable because it is expensive. Sometimes the claim seems stronger: that the expense is what the signal consists in, or that the signal is reliable only because the cost falls more heavily on low-quality individuals. Sometimes it sounds like a definitional point about what it takes for a signal to be believable, and sometimes it sounds like an empirical claim about which signals can persist under selection. Is there an established name or set of names for this, and how do the pieces come apart? I'm less interested in the label than in the carving: how does the minimal cost-based account differ from the stronger version, and from the still stronger claim that the signal works by imposing a burden that only a high-quality individual can absorb? And where do the edge cases fall — signals that can't be faked but aren't costly, signals that are costly but not more costly for low-quality signalers, signals where someone other than the signaler pays the cost? If there's no single name, I'd rather get a short taxonomy of the positions and their boundary cases than a bare label.
