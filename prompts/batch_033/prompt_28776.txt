I've been in the same argument about a hundred times now, in different friend groups, on reddit, at family dinners, and it never goes anywhere. The pattern is always: someone says "what you did was wrong," and the other person says "you've done the same thing, you're a hypocrite, you don't get to judge me." Then half the room says the hypocrite has a point — you lose your standing to call someone out if you're guilty of the same thing. The other half says thats a deflection — a true statement is true no matter who says it, and the criticism should be judged on its own. And then we go in circles.

What I want to know is: what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it a factual disagreement about whether hypocrisy makes someone less credible (like, empirically, are hypocrites worse at spotting wrongdoing)? Is it a verbal dispute where the two sides are using "you don't get to judge" to mean different things? Or is it a bedrock intuition thing where some people just feel viscerally that standing matters and others feel viscerally that truth matters, and neither side can argue the other into their camp? I've got a hunch its the third one, but I'd like someone to actually diagnose it — and tell me whether there's any way to move the thing forward, or if this is just one of those arguments that's built to loop forever.

Oh and when you answer: lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, no preamble, don't restate my question.
