I was reading this philosophy blog the other day and the author gave a summary of Susan Wolf's paper "Moral Saints" that went something like this:

"Wolf's argument is basically that being a moral saint — the kind of person who's as morally perfect as possible — is actually a pretty bad way to live. Moral saints are boring, they have no sense of humor, and they can't enjoy normal human pleasures like art, music, or good food because they're too busy trying to save the world. Her point is that morality demands too much from us and we'd all be better off if we just ignored it sometimes. So if you've ever felt guilty about not donating every spare dollar to charity or not volunteering every weekend, Wolf's paper is liberating — she's basically saying morality isn't nearly as important as we've been taught, and we shouldn't feel bad about prioritizing our own interests."

I've heard this paper is a big deal in ethics, but something about this summary feels off to me. Like, it sounds more like a self-help takeaway than a serious philosophical argument. Is this a fair representation of what Wolf actually argues? Walk me through what's accurate, what's distorted or exaggerated, and whether the conclusion the blogger draws — that Wolf thinks morality isn't as important as we think — is something Wolf herself would sign off on.
