I'm in a reading group that's been working through some Cold War nuclear strategy stuff — we did Schelling's *Arms and Influence* last month and now we're on some of the more recent debates about whether nuclear deterrence is actually stable or we've just been lucky. One guy in the group, whenever anyone starts questioning deterrence, just shrugs and says "nuclear weapons keep the peace." He says it like it's obvious, like it's a law of nature, and the conversation kind of dies there because nobody knows which claim they're supposed to be disagreeing with.

I've been trying to unpack what that sentence could even mean, and I keep finding more versions. Like, it could be the purely empirical claim that no two nuclear-armed states have ever fought a full-scale war — which is true but doesn't tell you whether nukes caused that or its just correlation. It could be the stronger causal claim that the presence of nuclear weapons makes great-power war impossible. It could be the game-theoretic claim that second-strike capability produces a subgame-perfect equilibrium where attacking is strictly dominated. It could be the historical claim that the Long Peace after WWII is best explained by nukes rather than by trade, democracy, or American hegemony. It could be the normative claim that whatever risks nukes create are worth bearing because the alternative is worse. It could even be something almost definitional — like, "peace" just means the absence of great-power war, so if we haven't had one, we're at peace, and nukes are part of the status quo that includes that peace.

I want to bring a breakdown to the next meeting. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims that "nuclear weapons keep the peace" could be making, flag which ones are trivial or near-tautological, which ones are substantive and actually defensible, which ones are probably false, and which one is doing the work when someone uses it to shut down a conversation about disarmament? And if there's a reading where the slogan is true but totally irrelevant to the disarmament question, I'd especially like that one called out.
