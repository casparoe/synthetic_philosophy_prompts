I'm a backend engineer and I keep running into this situation that's been bugging me. Here's a concrete case: I'll spend like an hour doing a really thorough code review on a PR, going line by line, and I conclude the code is solid, no issues. Then I find out three other senior engineers on my team all reviewed the same PR and they each independently flagged the same race condition — which I totally missed. Now here's the weird part: even after they tell me there's a race condition, I go back and re-read the code and I STILL can't see it. 

So I have two kinds of evidence pointing in different directions. On the one hand, my own direct read of the code (call it first-order evidence) says "this code is fine." On the other hand, I have evidence about the reliability of my read — namely, three people I respect all saw something I didn't (call that higher-order evidence). 

I've been told there's a whole debate in epistemology about "higher-order evidence" and something called "epistemic akrasia" — which I gather is about whether you can rationally believe something while also believing your evidence doesn't actually support it. Apparently some philosophers say that's never okay (anti-akrasia), and others say sometimes it is.

What I want to know is: what are the actual practical implications of these positions for someone like me? If I take the anti-akrasia view seriously, what am I supposed to do in the code review situation — just trust the group and say "there probably is a bug" even though I personally can't find it? Is that even a genuine belief at that point, or am I just deferring? And if someone holds the other view, does that mean I should stubbornly stick with my own read no matter how many people disagree? That seems insane. 

Basically I want to know what the debate cashes out to in terms of concrete advice for handling disagreements where you have reason to doubt your own reasoning. Don't just summarize the positions — tell me what they commit me to, practically speaking. And if there are nuances like "well it depends on whether the higher-order evidence is strong enough" or "it matters whether you can re-check your own work," I want those too. I'm trying to figure out if epistemology actually has something useful to say to an engineer or if it's all just armchair stuff.
