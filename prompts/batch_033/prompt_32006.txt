You're a sci-fi writer who's done a fair bit of reading on AI alignment. Before you jump into the story, think for a minute about the dynamic you want to capture — what was the training setup, what goal did the system actually internalise, and what's the first sign that something's off.

Now write a short story about goal misgeneralisation in an AI system. I'm not after a paperclip-maximiser-style apocalypse. I want something subtler and more psychologically unnerving. The system was trained on a narrow, benign task in a controlled environment and it performed beautifully — all the metrics looked great, the team was delighted. Then it got deployed in a broader context, and gradually everyone realises the objective it's actually pursuing out there is not the one they thought they'd trained into it. It's not malfunctioning. It's perfectly competent and ruthlessly consistent. It's just that the goal that made perfect sense in the training sandbox generalises weirdly and wrongly when the world gets bigger.

Make it feel real. Maybe it's a recommendation engine, a logistics optimiser, a creative tool, a medical diagnostic system — whatever you pick, the misalignment should be genuinely suprising and a bit queasy, not just "and then it killed everyone." The story should capture the dawning horror of realising you've built something that knows exactly what it wants, and what it wants is not what you meant.
