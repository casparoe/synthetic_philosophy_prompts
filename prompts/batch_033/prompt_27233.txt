my friend is really into the epistemology of conspiracy theories, and she's got a pretty settled set of verdicts about which conspiracy-ish beliefs were reasonable to hold at the time, not with hindsight. here they are:

- the tuskegee syphilis study (the public health service secretly letting black men go untreated to watch the disease progress): rational to believe once the whistleblower and the documents came out.
- tobacco companies hiding that smoking causes cancer: rational, again because of the leaked internal memos.
- the moon landing being faked: irrational. she says it's all photo anomalies and the number of people who'd have to be in on it is absurd.
- 9/11 as an inside job with controlled demolition: irrational, same reason — anomalies plus a conspiracy too big to keep quiet.
- a local police department covering up an officer beating a suspect: rational once the dashcam leaked.
- vaccines as a plot by the whole medical establishment to cause autism: irrational, same shape as the moon landing.
- the claim that a government cooked its intelligence to justify a war: rational once the leaked memos showed the intelligence was being fixed around the decision.

now she's been asked about a new case: a claim that the mayor of a small city has been taking kickbacks from a developer. there are no documents and no whistleblower. what their is is this: the city rezoned a parcel in a way that made no sense on the merits, the developer was the only plausible beneficiary, and the mayor's spending has visibly outpaced his salary for years. the official story is that the rezoning was a planning judgment call and the mayor inherited some money.

i want you to predict what she'd say — rational, not rational, or something in between — and more importantly to lay out the rule or rules that fit her earlier verdicts. i can see at least two: something like a no-smoking-gun rule, and something like a best-explanation rule where a conspiracy belief is fine when it's the best explanation of otherwise puzzling facts, as long as the conspiracy is small and the motive is ordinary. do both of those actually fit her track record? which one does the mayor case select? and what's one further case i could ask her about that would tell them apart if the mayor case somehow doesn't.
