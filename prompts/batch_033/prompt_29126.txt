I serve on my hospital's clinical ethics committee. We are drafting a triage protocol for the next respiratory pandemic: when we have fewer ECMO circuits than patients who need them, how do we decide who gets one?

Two camps have emerged. One camp argues that the protocol should maximize lives saved: if we have three circuits and two groups of patients—say, a group of two and a group of four, equally sick—we allocate to the four, because saving four is better than saving two. The other camp rejects this reasoning. They say each patient has an individual claim to care, and the fact that one group is larger does not give any individual in it a stronger claim. On their view, we should run a weighted lottery that gives every patient the same chance at a circuit, regardless of which group they belong to—so in the example, each of the six patients gets an equal shot, even though that means the smaller group might receive resources while the larger group does not.

Please draft a short decision memo for the committee that recommends one approach over the other, with reasons. The memo should state a clear recommendation, engage the strongest argument for the losing side honestly, and give the committee something it can act on. Also, please indicate which consideration or argument would be most likely to change your recommendation, or where your reasoning is most vulnerable.
