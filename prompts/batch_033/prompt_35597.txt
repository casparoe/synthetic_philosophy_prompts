Consider the following five interpretations of probability: classical (Laplacean), frequentist (limiting relative frequency), subjective (Bayesian), propensity, and logical (e.g., Carnap's or Keynes's). Please sort them into a tier list (S, A, B, C, D) according to how satisfactorily each handles the problem of single-case probability—that is, probability assignments to events that are unique or effectively non-repeatable, such as the probability that a particular patient will respond to a given treatment, or the probability that a specific rare astronomical event will occur within a certain window. For each interpretation, provide a one- or two-sentence justification for the tier you assign it. In your reasoning, consider how each interpretation addresses the reference-class problem, the distinction between epistemic and aleatory uncertainty, and whether it can yield a non-arbitrary numerical value for a single-case probability without tacitly collapsing into another interpretation.
