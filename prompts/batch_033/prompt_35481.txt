So I've been going back and forth with some friends in a reading group — half of them are really into evolutionary psychology (the Cosmides, Tooby, Pinker side of things) and the other half keep saying it's all just-so stories, you can't test any of it, it's storytelling dressed up as science. We've been at this for weeks and I'm tired of the ping-pong.

What I actually want, before I plant a flag, is the desiderata. What does an evolutionary explanation of a psychological trait have to deliver to count as a genuine scientific hypothesis and not a just-so story? I mean the full spec: what kinds of evidence would confirm or disconfirm it, what alternative explanations it has to rule out (byproduct, drift, cultural transmission, exaptation), what constraints from genetics or paleoanthropology it can't contradict, what the explanation has to specify about the ancestral environment and the selection pressure — all of it. For each requirement, give me the reason it's a requirement, not just the label. And then tell me which of these are non-negotiable (fail this and your writing fiction) and which are aspirational (even the best hypotheses only partly meet them).

If you want to illustrate by scoring a couple of well-known hypotheses against the list — say, disgust-as-pathogen-avoidance or depression-as-rumination-adaptation — that's fine, but the list is what I'm actually after. And if there are pairs of desiderata that pull in opposite directions (where satisfying one makes it harder to satisfy another), flag those — that's the kind of tension I suspect is driving a lot of the fights in the literature, and nobody in my group has laid it out that way yet.
