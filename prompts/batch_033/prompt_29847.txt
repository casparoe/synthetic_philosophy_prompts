I've been reading Schopenhauer and I keep getting stuck on his treatment of music relative to the other arts. He seems to say that the other arts present the Platonic Ideas, but music somehow skips the Ideas and presents the will itself directly, which is why it hits us harder then the others. But the will is supposed to be outside space, time, and causality, and music is just sounds unfolding in time. I'm not after the biographical or historical background; I want to understand the conceptual move. How is a succession of tones supposed to be a copy or objectification of something atemporal, and why does Schopenhauer think music would still be a significant art even if the world of individual things didn't exist? Please explain the underlying picture, and if the view has real internal tensions, spell those out rather than smoothing them over.
