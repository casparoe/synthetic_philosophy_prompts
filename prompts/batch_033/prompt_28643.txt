I own a small hardware store and somehow ended up on my city's licensing board, and right now we're stuck on a bar's application to run a weekly dwarf-tossing night. The setup: customers pay to throw a willing adult performer onto a thick padded mat, the performers are employees who've signed off and keep most of the door money, and the bar owner keeps telling us its just stunt work, no different from a wrestler taking bumps or a rodeo clown. A bunch of residents came to the last meeting and said the city shouldn't put its name on something that degrading even if everyone involved is a consenting adult — one man said it makes the whole town look like the kind of place that sells people's dignity for a cover charge. Then a performer sent in a letter saying this is the most reliable income he's had in years, and that being told he can't do it because other people find it undignified is more insulting to him than the job itself.

There's alot of heat on both sides, and the board is split down the middle, so I got stuck writing the short opinion to circulate before Thursday's vote. I'm not looking for a legal brief, just a clear recommendation — approve, deny, or approve with conditions like a six-month review or a cap on nights per month — with reasons the losing side would actually have to take seriously. The thing I keep circling is: if nobody's getting hurt and the performers want the work, what business is it of ours to say no? But then I think, some things are just wrong to make money off of, and saying it's their choice feels like it misses something. I want the memo to deal honestly with whichever side loses instead of just knocking it down.
