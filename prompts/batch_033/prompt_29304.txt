A philosopher I am interviewing next month defends a global evolutionary debunking argument in metaethics: on her view, the moral judgments we actually make are better explained by natural selection's having favored fitness-enhancing dispositions than by our tracking mind-independent moral facts, and this explanatory fact undermines the justification of those judgments. I need eight to ten interview questions that will not let her simply restate that argument. Include at least one question on each of four pressure points, and for each include a follow-up tailored to the reply she is most likely to give: (i) why the causal origins of a judgment should have any justificatory upshot at all, rather than merely explaining how the judgment arose; (ii) the worry that the argument overgeneralizes, since natural selection has also shaped the faculties behind mathematics and natural science; (iii) the third-factor reply, according to which moral truths and our moral judgments are correlated because some further feature of the world caused both; and (iv) whether the argument's own normative or evaluative premises would survive its corrosive conclusion. End the set with a question about what kind of evidence, empirical or philosophical, would most seriously weaken her confidence in the debunking conclusion.
