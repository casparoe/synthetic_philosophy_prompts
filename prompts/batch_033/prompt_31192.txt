I've been reading about Frankfurt cases and I'm struck by how decisively they seem to end the free will debate. The Principle of Alternative Possibilities says that moral responsibility requires the ability to do otherwise. Frankfurt shows this is false with cases where a counterfactual intervener would have forced the agent's hand, yet the agent acts on their own and is clearly responsible. Since the main argument for incompatibilism is that determinism removes alternative possibilities and therefore removes moral responsibility, and Frankfurt has shown that moral responsibility does not require alternative possibilities, the incompatibilist argument simply collapses. Why, then, do so many philosophers still identify as incompatibilists? Isn't this just a failure to accept a settled result?
