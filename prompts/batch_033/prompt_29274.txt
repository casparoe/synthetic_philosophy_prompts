I teach an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar and I'm writing the take-home final. I want one essay question on self-deception that can't be answered by just reciting the standard paradox (roughly: lying to yourself seems to require that you know the truth you're supposedly hiding from yourself). I want it to push students to say whether deflationary accounts — the ones that make self-deception a matter of motivationally biased belief, with no intention to deceive yourself and no contradictory beliefs — actually solve the paradox or just change the subject. The question should force them to take a stand on what would be lost if self-deception were nothing over and above ordinary wishful thinking. Write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme that seperates an A from a B by naming the specific moves each answer has to make. No citations needed; the exam tests argument, not reading lists.
