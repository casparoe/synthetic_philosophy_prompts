My political theory prof has been marking cases "free" or "unfree" all term, and I wrote down five of her calls: a slave whose owner never interferes and is by all accounts kind — unfree; someone robbed at gunpoint — unfree; a worker with no real alternative but to take a harsh, low-paying job — unfree; a shopkeeper bound by a business-hours law she voted for in a referendum — free; and a person living under a perfectly predictable dictator who never actually interferes — unfree. I know the standard trio of negative, positive, and republican liberty, and I cant figure out whether she's running one of them or some mix. Next week she's deciding three new cases: someone with severe agoraphobia who can't leave the house; a citizen fined under a law she voted against but that was passed by a democratic legislature after normal debate; and a monk who freely took a lifelong vow of obedience to an abbot with total discretion over his daily life. Predict her three verdicts, say what rule or mix best fits all eight, and tell me which of the new cases would do the most to pin her down. Also say what would change your mind: if I came back and told you she'd gone the other way on one of the original five, which reversal would most change your read of her, and where is your reasoning most likely to fail?
