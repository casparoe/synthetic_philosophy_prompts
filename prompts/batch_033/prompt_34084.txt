So I spent most of last night arguing with a mate about whether beliefs and desires are literally real, the way electrons are. He kept saying they must be because he's having one right now, and I kept saying introspection is exactly the sort of evidence that shouldn't settle it. We got absolutely nowhere, but I noticed something useful: we didn't actually disagree about the word 'real', we disagreed about a bunch of specific cases — chess engines, confabulating patients, thermostats, what it would mean if neuroscience stopped mentioning beliefs altogether. So I want to turn this into one of those 'which position do you actually hold?' quizzes for the small philosophy of mind blog I run.

Can you write me the quiz? Here's what I'm after. Nine questions, each built round a concrete case or scenario, with three answer options each. The options should be written so a reader can answer from their gut without knowing any of the jargon — so no 'are you an eliminativist?' and no 'do you think beliefs are real?'. The cases should include at least: a chess engine or thermostat, a split-brain or confabulation case, a future neuroscience that explains decisions without mentioning beliefs and desires, and a case where someone sincerely avows a belief but their behaviour says otherwise. Then write a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto these four positions: eliminative materialism about propositional attitudes, intentional realism (beliefs and desires as internal representational states with causal roles), dispositionalism (beliefs as behavioural dispositions), and instrumentalism or interpretationism (belief-desire talk as a useful predictive stance that needn't pick out inner states).

Two things matter to me. First, the scoring key has to do real work: say which questions seperate which positions, which positions the quiz can't tell apart, and which answer patterns look like hybrids or like no stable position at all. Second, because the argument with my friend kept coming back to confabulation, make at least two of the nine questions turn on cases where the person's sincere report of their own belief comes apart from what actually explains their behaviour — and make sure those questions are doing genuine diagnostic work, not just acting as tiebreakers.

Please dont include any author names, book titles or citations — the quiz is meant to sort people by what they say about cases, not by who they've read. And write the whole thing in British English. Dont worry if it reads a bit rough, I'll tidy it up before I post it.
