I'm a novelist working on a book about consciousness, and I've been reading the philosophical literature on panpsychism to ground the ideas. I keep running into the "combination problem" as the central objection: if every fundamental physical entity has some form of proto-consciousness, how do these micro-subjects combine into the single, unified consciousness I experience right now? But I'm struggling to see why this is treated as a distinct problem for panpsychism rather than just a restatement of the hard problem. The physicalist, after all, must also explain how non-conscious physical parts combine to produce consciousness. If panpsychism is supposed to dissolve the hard problem by making consciousness fundamental, and the combination problem is just the hard problem in different language, then panpsychism hasn't done any work. But the literature plainly treats them as separate issues, so I must be missing a distinction. What am I misunderstanding?
