I've been going down a rabbit hole on the philosophy of dreams — specifically the debate about whether dreams are experiences we have *while* asleep, or whether they're something more like confabulated memories that get generated at the moment of waking up. You've got Norman Malcolm on one side (the Wittgensteinian line: the concept of "experience" requires the possibility of outward criteria, so asleep-you can't be having experiences in the ordinary sense) and Daniel Dennett on the other (the cassette theory: what if a dream is just a memory-insertion, and there was no temporally-extended experience at all?). And then there's the empirical camp — people who point to REM sleep, lucid dreaming, and now all this fMRI dream-content decoding stuff — who think the whole philosophical debate is silly because obviously dreams are experiences, look at the brain scans.

Here's what's bugging me: this debate has been running since at least the 1950s, and as far as I can tell, nobody has budged. The Malcolm people think the Dennett people are making a category mistake; the Dennett people think the Malcolm people are doing armchair conceptual analysis that ignores the actual phenomenology; the neuroscientists think both camps are ignoring the data; and the philosophers think the neuroscientists are begging the question about what "experience" even means. I've been reading through this stuff for a couple months now and it honestly feels like everyone is just talking past each other in a really fundamental way.

I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it a verbal dispute about the word "experience"? Is it a substantive empirical question that we just haven't figured out how to test yet? Is it a clash of bedrock intuitions about whether first-person reports from sleep states count as evidence? Or is it something else — maybe the two sides are answering different questions under the same heading? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that would actually move this debate forward — a new experimental paradigm, a conceptual distinction that cuts through the mess, or should we just accept that the question is ill-posed and move on?
