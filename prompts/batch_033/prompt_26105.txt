Write an essay on whether the procreation asymmetry is a genuine feature of value or just a confusion. The asymmetry, roughly, is that we think preventing the existence of someone whose life would be net-miserable is a good thing, but preventing the existence of someone whose life would be net-happy is not a bad thing — even though, once either person exists, their pain and pleasure seem to matter symmetrically. So what breaks the symmetry? Weigh the main attempts to ground the asymmetry (e.g., person-affecting views, the idea that pains are impersonally bad while pleasures are only good *for* someone, or arguments that the asymmetry is a basic intuition we should just accept) against the main attempts to debunk it (e.g., that it rests on a scope confusion, that it can't survive careful formulation, or that it's an artifact of evolutionary psychology we should discard). Try to identify where the real sticking point is — what would each side need to show to win? — and reach and defend a conclusion. Don't just summarize the debate; take a stand.
