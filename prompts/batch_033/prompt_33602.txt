So my friend and I just spent like two hours going back and forth on AI safety and he laid out this argument that I can't shake. It goes:

(1) The reason you build control measures around a model — sandboxing, monitoring, capability limits — is that you suspect it might be misaligned.

(2) If the model is in fact aligned, then those measures were unnecessary. You paid a cost for nothing.

(3) If the model is misaligned and capable enough to cause serious harm, then it's capable enough to defeat your control measures. The same cognitive abilities that make it dangerous — superhuman coding, strategic reasoning, persuasion — are exactly what let it find exploits, talk its way out, or wait you out.

(4) So control measures are either unnecessary (aligned case) or futile (misaligned case).

(5) Therefore, the only thing worth investing in is alignment. Control is a distraction.

Now, I don't buy the conclusion. It seems like there's got to be a space between "don't bother with any safety measures" and "only alignment matters" — like, surely not giving an untrusted model direct internet access is neither pointless nor obviously doomed? But I can't figure out exactly which step is the problem. If you had to reject one premise, which one would it be, and what does rejecting it cost you?
