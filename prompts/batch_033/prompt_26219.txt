I've been reading stuff on the philosophy of virtual worlds lately, and there’s this debate that seems to have been going round in circles since maybe the late 90s. On one side you've got people who say virtual objects and virtual harms are totally real—that if I steal your rare sword in an MMORPG, that’s a genuine theft, a violation of your property rights as real as any digital asset. On the other side, some say no, its all fictional; the sword doesn’t exist except as a representation, so at most it's a breach of a game rule or a social convention, not a real moral wrong in the same way. It’s the same with harassment in a virtual reality social space: is that a real harm, or just a simulation of harm?

I’m not interested in who's right. I want to understand why this thing hasn’t moved in twenty-five years. The two sides seem to agree on all the observable facts—what code does, what players experience—but they keep talking past each other. My hunch is that theyre answering different questions under the same label: one group is asking 'are these objects real in the ontology of the world?' and the other is asking 'do they matter morally in the same way?' but maybe I’m oversimplifying. 

Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it a verbal dispute about the word 'real'? A deep clash of metaphysical intuitions that can't be argued for? A hidden normative question about when something gets moral considerability? And given your diagnosis, what would actually move the debate forward—a new distinction, an experiment (what kind), or do we just need to admit that the question is ill-posed and drop it?
