I'm writing a post about a fight that keeps breaking out in EA/rationalist spaces: whether it's fine to just adopt moral positions from the surrounding community (longtermism, a particular population ethics take, whatever) instead of doing the arguments yourself. One side says this is ordinary deference to expertise and you'd be silly not to; the other side says moral deference is different and there's something off about it even when you end up with the right view. I want a proper taxonomy of the positions on moral testimony / moral deference, built from the branching claims rather than from a list of names, so people get sorted by what they actually think rather than which label they like.

So: write me a decision tree / taxonomy tree for the question of whether there's something wrong with moral deference.

The root question should be roughly 'is there anything wrong with forming or revising a moral belief by taking someone else's word for it?', but adjust if you think a better root exists. Build the tree from the claims that actually do the splitting, for example: whether moral judgements are ordinary beliefs at all; whether the thing you want out of moral inquiry is true belief, knowledge, or understanding; whether the defect (if any) is epistemic or practical; whether the problem is specific to moral testimony or just one instance of a general worry about deference; whether offering moral testimony is as suspect as accepting it; whether moral expertise is even a thing. Use whichever of these earn their keep, add more if the space needs them, and don't add a branch just because it has a name.

Each branch node should be a yes/no question or a concrete mini-case a reader can answer without already knowing which position it leads too. So no 'do you side with the pessimists?' questions. Use at least two concrete mini-cases in the nodes: one about deferring to an org's moral ranking of cause areas, and one about taking a friend's word that a joke was offensive; add others if they help.

Make sure the leaves include at least these views: (1) the full optimism view — moral testimony is fine, it can give you justified belief or knowledge like any other testimony; (2) the understanding view — testimony can give true belief but not moral understanding, and understanding is what you should want, so deference is second-rate; (3) the moral-worth view — the problem isn't epistemic, it's that acting on borrowed moral beliefs doesn't stand in the right relation to the reasons; (4) an expressivist or quasi-realist view — moral judgements aren't ordinary beliefs, so 'testimony' is doing something else; (5) the no-moral-experts view — the question is moot because there's nothing to defer to. Add any other leaves the space demands. Where a combination of branch answers has no defender, list it and say whether it's empty because it's incoherent, because it's just unattractive, or because nobody's thought to occupy it. If two leaves end up indistinguishable by the tree, flag that pair and say why.

Audience is rationalist/EA blog readers: not professional philosophers, but fine with jargon and fine with being told their view has an awkward commitment. Aim for something like 8–12 leaves and no more than 6 or 7 branch nodes.

Return a single JSON object with these fields:
