I'm a philosopher working mostly in ethics and political philosophy, but I keep tripping over the same strategic pattern and I don't know whether it has a name in the game theory or rational choice literature. Here's the pattern. Two (or more) parties are in a situation where each would benefit from being the first to lock themselves into a course of action — and once locked in, they can't easily back out. The first one to commit gains an advantage, and the second gets stuck with a worse outcome. So both race to commit, and the result is that they end up committed to positions they might not have chosen if they could have coordinated, and the whole thing has a kind of self-propelling, out-of-control quality.

A few examples. Two countries both claim a disputed territory. Each would gain from being the first to put troops on the ground in a way that makes withdrawal politically impossible; the one that commits first gets the territory, the other is humiliated. So both rush to deploy, and the situation escalates. Or: two firms are deciding whether to enter a small market that can only support one of them. Each would benefit from being the first to build a factory, because once the factory is built the other firm knows it'll lose money by entering. So both race to break ground. Or: two politicians in the same party are deciding whether to announce a controversial policy position. The first to announce gets to define the terms of the debate and paints the other as a follower; the second looks reactive. So both rush to be first.

I know this is somewhere in the neighborhood of pre-commitment, Schelling-style strategic moves, and first-mover advantage. But none of those quite capture the racing aspect — the fact that the timing matters, that both parties are trying to be first, and that the process itself can produce outcomes nobody wanted. I'm also aware of the game of chicken and the idea that throwing your steering wheel out the window can win the game, but that seems to be about one party committing, not about both racing to commit.

So: does this phenomenon have an established name? If it does, what exactly is its scope — does it require that the commitment be irreversible, or just costly? Does it require that the parties be symmetric? And if it doesn't have a name, what would you call it and how would you distinguish it from the nearest concepts? I'm less interested in the label itself than in getting the boundaries right: what counts as an instance of this and what doesn't, and what the edge cases look like.

Also, please say how confident you are in your answer and single out whichever point you're least sure about.
