I am a philosophy professor whose research concerns the metaphysics of ordinary objects. For the past several months I have been meeting with a colleague in database systems; he is fluent in the vocabulary of tables, rows, primary and foreign keys, joins, materialized views, schemas, and query planning, and I can follow that vocabulary at the level of an informed outsider, though I am not a practitioner. Whenever I describe Peter van Inwagen's special composition question—under what conditions several things compose a further thing—and the main families of answers to it, my colleague insists that the entire debate is just about when a set of rows may be treated as a single table. On his telling, mereological nihilism, universalism, and the restrictive answers are simply different policies about which queries to materialize and which to leave as virtual views. I suspect this translation is partly illuminating and partly question-begging, and I want to know exactly where the boundary falls.

Please restate the special composition question and three of the main answers to it—mereological nihilism, universalism, that is, unrestricted composition, and one restrictive answer, such as the view that composition occurs exactly when the parts are caught up in the activity of a life—in the vocabulary of a database engineer. Use the terms precisely: base tables, rows, joins, materialized views, schemas, partitions, keys, and any others that genuinely earn their keep. Then give me a residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only under distortion, and which have no counterpart in the database vocabulary at all. I would also like your judgment on what the residue indicates—whether the database vocabulary is missing something the metaphysicians are genuinely arguing about, or whether some of the metaphysical debate looks, from that vantage point, like a dispute about which conventions to adopt. Above all, do not let the translation quietly assume that the norms of good database design settle a metaphysical question.
