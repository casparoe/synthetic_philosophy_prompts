I teach an upper-level ethics seminar, and in a recent session on the moral difference between lying and misleading, a colleague of mine defended the view that the distinction carries genuine moral weight by drawing an analogy with the doing/allowing distinction. On his telling, lying is to misleading as killing is to letting die: the liar actively inserts a falsehood into the stream of testimony and thereby causes the hearer's false belief, whereas the misleader utters something true and allows the hearer to draw the false conclusion herself, just as the agent who lets die allows a causal process already under way to run to its end. The analogy is doing real argumentative work for him: it is meant to license the conclusion that lying is, other things equal, morally worse than misleading, and more broadly that the moral significance of the lie/mislead distinction is of a piece with the moral significance of the doing/allowing distinction.

I am skeptical that the analogy carries as much as he wants, and I would like to address it carefully in the seminar rather than dismiss it. Could you audit the analogy for me? Set out the correspondence explicitly (a table would be fine): the speaker, the utterance, the hearer's inference, the resulting false belief, and whatever else you take to be in play. Then identify where the mapping strains or breaks, and which of those disanalogies matter for the conclusion he wants and which are incidental. In particular, I would like to know which features of the source the analogy quietly imports into the target — for example, the assumption that doing is worse than allowing, or that causes and allowings can be cleanly separated — and whether those imports are defensible once we are talking about testimony and inference rather than bodies and causal processes. Finally, tell me whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are explicit, or whether it rests on the analogy; if a better analogy would serve his purpose, I would welcome it. Since I want to be able to steelman the position before criticizing it, please also indicate what would change your mind: which consideration would lead you to a different verdict about how far the analogy holds, and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
