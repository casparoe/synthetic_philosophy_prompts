I've been reading through the animal ethics literature for a seminar, and I keep running into the claim that speciesism is structurally analogous to racism — that drawing a moral boundary around species membership is arbitrary in the same way that drawing one around race is, and that rejecting one while accepting the other is inconsistent. This analogy does a lot of argumentative work: it is used to conclude that our treatment of animals is not merely wrong but a form of prejudice, and that overcoming it requires something like the moral reorientation that accompanied the civil rights movement. But I have begun to suspect that the analogy may be carrying more weight than it can bear, and I would like to audit it systematically rather than just registering unease.

I want a full correspondence table: what in the racism case maps onto what in the speciesism case? I have in mind at least the group boundary itself, the ground offered for differential treatment, the historical and cultural mechanism that sustains it, the epistemic vice or cognitive failure involved, the nature of the harm done to the excluded group, and the proposed remedy. For each correspondence, tell me where the mapping holds up and where it breaks. I am especially interested in disanalogies that threaten the argumentative use of the analogy — points where the analogy quietly imports a feature of racism that has no genuine counterpart in the species case, or where the species case has a feature that racism lacks and that changes the moral calculus. For instance, race is widely understood to be a biological fiction constructed by a dominant group to rationalize exploitation, whereas species are real biological categories that predate any human moral system. Does that difference matter for the analogy's intended use, or is it a surface disanalogy that leaves the normative core untouched? If the breaks turn out to be deep, is there a better analogy that captures what is genuinely wrong with speciesism, or does the anti-speciesist conclusion have to stand on its own arguments without leaning on the racism analogy at all?
