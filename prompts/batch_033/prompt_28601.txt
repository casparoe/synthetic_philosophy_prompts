I spent my career as an experimental physicist, and now in retirement I have been working through the philosophy of physics — particularly the literature on Maxwell's demon and the thermodynamics of computation. One move I encounter repeatedly is the claim that Landauer and Bennett resolved the paradox by showing that information is physical. The popular version of this runs on an analogy: information is like a fuel. You can use it to do work (the demon opens and closes the trapdoor based on what it knows), but you have to pay for it (acquiring and especially erasing information carries an entropy cost), and the bill always at least matches the gain — so the second law stands. I have seen this analogy deployed to conclude that the demon presents no residual puzzle, just a confusion about whether information comes for free.

I would like you to audit this analogy. Specifically: (1) a table of correspondences — what in the thermodynamic domain maps to what in the information domain; (2) the points where the mapping breaks, distinguishing those that are merely cosmetic from those that matter for the argument; (3) an assessment of whether the conclusion — that the demon poses no threat to the second law — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was quietly importing assumptions (about the nature of measurement, the reversibility of computation, or the status of the Landauer bound) that need their own defense; and (4) if the analogy is misleading in important respects, what a better one would be.
