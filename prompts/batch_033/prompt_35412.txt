so my best friend is a philosopher who works on interventionist theories of causation — you know, the whole "X causes Y if you can wiggle X and Y wiggles in response" picture. shes marrying another philosopher who is a diehard counterfactual guy — "X caused Y iff had X not happened, Y wouldnt have happened." theyve been arguing about this for years and honestly its the foundation of their whole relationship. im a novelist, not a philosopher, and im giving the toast at their wedding. the room is gonna be half philosophy grad students and postdocs who will catch every reference and half family members who think "causation" is something you settle with a lawyer. i need a five-minute toast that honors both views, makes the philosophers laugh, and doesnt leave the aunts and uncles totally bewildered. it should say something real about what holds a marriage together — like, is love more like a counterfactual ("without you i wouldnt be who i am") or an intervention ("what can i do today to make this work")? but it cant be a lecture. it has to land as warmth and affection, not as a seminar.
