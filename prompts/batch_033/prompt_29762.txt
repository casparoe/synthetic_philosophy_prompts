I'm trying to get my head around Dennett's quining qualia argument, specifically the Chase and Sanborn coffee tasters. Chase says the coffee tastes exactly the same as it always did but he no longer likes it; Sanborn says he likes it just as much but it now tastes different to him. Dennett uses this to argue that qualia are an incoherent posit. But here's what I dont get: to even set up the two cases as a contrast, doesnt Dennett have to take Chase and Sanborn's introspective reports as authoritative about how the coffee seems to them? If their reports are good enough to build an argument on, hasnt he already admitted that there are stable facts about how things seem to a person over time — which is most of what 'qualia' was supposed to name? So isn't the argument self-undermining?
