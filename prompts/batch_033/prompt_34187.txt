I'm a postdoc in a vision lab, and I've somehow ended up as the person who has to settle a running fight about how we handle subjects' verbal reports. We run masking and binocular rivalry experiments, and on every trial the participant says whether they saw the stimulus, how clear it was, and sometimes what it looked like. Right now the lab is split: one RA codes "I didn't see anything" as if it's a fact about the display, another treats every report as an infallible readout of the subject's private experience, and a third wants to throw the reports away entirely and only use forced-choice performance. My PI told me to write a single protocol everyone has to follow, because every paper we publish turns on how these reports get coded, and I want it to be based on heterophenomenology, which I half-remember from a seminar as something like: take first-person reports seriously as reports of how things seem to the subject, but don't take them as authoritative about what is actually going on in their head, and stay neutral about whether the things the subject reports are real. That sounds right in the abstract, but I have no idea how to turn it into a procedure a sleep-deprived RA with no philosophy background can actually run before lunch.

Please write me that procedure: a step-by-step protocol for collecting, transcribing, coding, and using verbal reports in a consciousness experiment. I want the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each question, where the procedure stops and says "this report is unusable," and where it has to hand the decision to a human because the step is a judgment call rather then a rule. Then run the procedure on three cases: (1) a subject in a rivalry study says "I saw the face the whole time" even though the stimulus alternated between a face and a house every second and their button presses tracked the alternation; (2) a blindsight-type patient says "I don't see anything" but then guesses the location of the stimulus at 80 percent; (3) a subject in a masking study reports "it wasn't a visual experience, more like a vague hunch that something was there" on trials where the mask came too fast. Include at least one case where the procedure tells me to do something I'm going to hate, and say what the procedure can't settle. Also be straight with me about which parts of this are heterophenomenology actually doing work and which parts are just a fancy name for "write down what they said." And tell me how confident you are in the procedure overall and which step you're least sure about.
