On the transworld identity view, one and the same individual exists in many possible worlds and simply has different properties in different worlds. On the counterpart theory, no individual exists in more than one world; each world has its own individuals, and a modal claim about an actual thing is made true by a sufficiently similar counterpart of that thing in another world, where the counterpart relation is fixed by context and resemblance rather than by identity.

Restate each position in my vocabulary. I want to know, for each side, how it would represent a possible world, an individual, a de re modal claim, the distinction between essential and accidental properties, and the context-sensitivity of the counterpart relation. Pay particular attention to how each side handles the apparent problem of one and the same individual bearing incompatible intrinsic properties across worlds, because that looks to me like a schema-design problem.

Keep the translation honest: if my vocabulary has a built-in bias toward one of the two positions, say so in the residue report rather than letting it quietly settle the debate.
