You are to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers of psychiatry who have been arguing past each other for years. One, call her the realist, holds that psychiatric classifications (like major depressive disorder, schizophrenia, autism spectrum disorder) pick out genuine causal structures in the world — that the categories earn their keep by carving nature at something close to its joints, even if the joints are messy. The other, call him the looping theorist, holds that psychiatric classifications are interactive kinds: they alter the people classified, reshaping self-conceptions, social roles, clinical practices, and even the expression of symptoms, so that the classification brings about the very patterns it purports merely to describe, and the target is never stable in the way natural-kind realism requires.

They have agreed to produce a document that both can sign, containing the following:

(1) A numbered list of claims they both accept, without reservation. These should not be vacuous truisms. They should include claims about what the history of psychiatric classification shows, what looping effects actually are and are not, what the realist is and is not committed to, what counts as a successful psychiatric category by each side's lights, and what kinds of evidence would be probative. Formulate each claim so that neither party would chafe at signing it.

(2) A disagreements table. Each row should state a single, precise proposition over which they differ, phrased so that the realist would accept the realist-column wording of her view and the looping theorist would accept the looping-column wording of his. The table should separate genuine disagreements from ones that, upon examination, turned out to be verbal. For any verbal disagreements, state what the underlying terminological difference is and why it does not mark a substantive dispute.

(3) A list of cruxes — sub-questions such that, if both parties came to agree on the answer, at least one of them would change their view on a substantive disagreement in the table. For each crux, specify which disagreement it would resolve and in which direction.

(4) For each crux, describe a concrete empirical case, historical episode, or argument that both sides agree bears on it. The description should be detailed enough that a reader unfamiliar with the debate can see why it matters. Record, for each side, what they predict the case or argument will show, and what they would have to admit if the prediction went the other way.

Throughout, the document should distinguish between the claim that looping effects exist (which the realist may happily grant) and the claim that looping effects make psychiatric categories fail as real kinds. It should also distinguish between the claim that some psychiatric categories are poorly grounded (which the looping theorist and the realist may both grant) and the claim that all psychiatric categories are interactive kinds in the relevant sense. The aim is to produce a document that could appear in a journal as a model of adversarial collaboration, not a polemic or a compromise that papers over the cracks.
