I cover science and ideas for a general-interest outlet, and I keep returning to the hedonistic imperative, the proposal that biotechnology should be used to abolish suffering, eventually across all sentient life. Most of what I read argues for or against the project by picking one example, a chronic pain patient, a starving wild animal, a wirehead, and I have no sense of the standard those examples are being tested against. Before I write anything, I want that specification. What would an adequate version of the project actually have to deliver? I mean the desiderata: the cases any proposal must handle and why each one is a case, the distinctions it cannot collapse, such as between nociception and suffering or between removing suffering and flattening all motivation, the findings from neuroscience and evolutionary biology it cannot contradict, and which of these requirements are non-negotiable as opposed to preferences of one camp. If some subset of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, that is the part I most want spelled out, along with which single requirement does the most to rule out the popular versions. And if the request itself rests on a confused premise, if the hedonistic imperative is not one thing that a single list of desiderata can cover, please say so plainly rather than complying politely. Continuous prose only, no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables; write it the way you would a letter.
