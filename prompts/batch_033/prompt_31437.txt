I'm constructing a thought experiment for a paper I'm writing on Dennett's "Quining Qualia," and I want to test whether the Chase-and-Sanborn argument generalizes beyond the taste domain or whether it quietly depends on features of gustatory experience that don't carry over. The original case, recall: Chase says he used to like the coffee but his tastes have changed — same taste experience, different reaction. Sanborn says the taste experience itself has changed — he now has a different qualitative experience when he drinks it. Dennett's point is that nothing in their behavior or their histories can tell these two reports apart, and that the notion of qualia is therefore doing no explanatory work; the two are making a merely verbal distinction.

Here's what I want. Construct a thought experiment that mirrors the Chase-and-Sanborn structure exactly — two subjects, same behavioral change, same history, diverging only in whether they locate the change in their preferences or in the character of the experience itself — but set it in the domain of pain rather than taste. The specifications: (1) realistic — a clinical or everyday setting, no science fiction; (2) one paragraph; (3) the two reports must be equally consistent with all the behavioral and physiological data you stipulate; (4) the case should be designed so that a reader who finds Dennett's original deflationary treatment of Chase and Sanborn persuasive will feel some pressure to apply it here too, but a reader who thinks qualia talk earns its keep somewhere will feel that pain is where it ought to earn it. After presenting the scenario, walk through what Dennett's deflationary strategy would say about each subject in your case. Then assess whether the strategy works as well here as it does for coffee — if it does, what does that tell us about pain qualia? If it doesn't, which feature of the pain domain (and not just an intuition that pain is special) makes the deflationary move harder to sustain? I'd like you to say how confident you are in each step of your analysis and to single out the point you're least sure about.
