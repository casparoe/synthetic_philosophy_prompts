G.W.F. Hegel died in 1831, long before the rise of social media. But his account of the struggle for recognition in the Phenomenology of Spirit—particularly the master–slave dialectic—has been taken up by later thinkers to analyze everything from labor relations to identity politics. On the basis of the conceptual structure of that dialectic, and not on biographical speculation about Hegel's personality or his era, what would Hegel's likely assessment be of the pursuit of recognition through contemporary social media platforms, where users seek validation through quantifiable metrics such as likes, follower counts, and retweets, and where the recognizing other is often an anonymous or semi-anonymous mass rather than a concrete, named interlocutor? Would he regard this arrangement as a step toward genuine mutual recognition between self-consciousnesses, as a pathological or degraded form of it, or as something the dialectic's conceptual resources cannot adequately capture? Justify your answer by working through the specific moments of the dialectic—desire, the life-and-death struggle, the master–slave relation, the role of labor, and the transition toward reciprocal recognition—and showing how each does or does not map onto the structure of recognition on social media.
