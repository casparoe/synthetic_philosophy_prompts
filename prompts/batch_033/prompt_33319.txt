Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two Bayesian epistemologists who disagree about how to update on conditional information. The first holds that the rationally required posterior is the unique probability function that minimizes relative entropy (KL divergence) from the prior subject to the learned constraint — call this the MRE position. The second holds that learning a bare conditional probability constraint underdetermines the posterior; the correct update depends on the provenance of the information (what protocol the source followed, what the source would have reported in other states), and absent such provenance information, a family of distinct posteriors is rationally permissible — call this the underdetermination position.

Both accept the Judy Benjamin problem as a central test case: a prior uniform over four quadrants formed by crossing {Red, Blue} territories with {Headquarters, Second Company} areas; a fully reliable source reports that if the agent is in Red territory, the probability she is in Headquarters is q (with q ≠ 0.5). Neither Bayes conditioning nor Jeffrey conditioning applies directly, so the constraint must be handled by some other rule.

The document should include: (1) a numbered list of agreed-upon claims about the formal setup, what the constraint P(H|R) = q does and does not entail, and what standard conditioning rules can and cannot deliver; (2) a disagreements table with columns for the issue, the MRE position, the underdetermination position, and whether the disagreement is substantive or verbal; (3) a list of cruxes — sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side — and for each crux, a concrete test case or information-provenance scenario both would agree bears on it, with each side's advance prediction. Pay special attention to whether the dispute about P(Blue) changing in the MRE solution is a genuine normative disagreement or a disagreement about what counts as "having no evidence about Blue."
