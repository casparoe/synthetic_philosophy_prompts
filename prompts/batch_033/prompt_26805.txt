I've become convinced that the dialetheist response to the Liar paradox is correct: the Liar sentence ("This sentence is false") is both true and false, and this is a genuine contradiction — not a paradox to be dissolved but a fact about the limits of consistent language. On this view, we should adopt a paraconsistent logic (such as LP) that allows contradictions without triviality, and we should accept that some contradictions are true, including the Liar and perhaps others generated by the sorites paradox or by the limits of thought.

I want to know what would count against this view. What are the strongest objections, sorted by their force — from those that would be fatal if sustained to those that are merely awkward? I'd like the assessment to distinguish between objections that dialetheists themselves take seriously and have worked to answer, and those they would dismiss as misunderstanding the view. In particular, I want to know whether the revenge Liar problem — constructing a sentence that says of itself that it is false only, or that it is not true — is a decisive difficulty, or whether the dialetheist has resources to handle it. I also want to know whether the Curry paradox, which doesn't involve negation, poses a problem that the dialetheist cannot handle with the same tools. Finally, I want an honest assessment of whether dialetheism has been qualified in ways that insulate it from falsification, and if so, whether that is a problem for a view of this kind.
