I'm giving a talk next month at a workshop on the foundations of decision theory, defending the claim that acausal trade — cooperating with an agent one cannot communicate with, causally interact with, or bind through any enforcement mechanism, purely on the strength of each side's model of the other's decision procedure — is sometimes a rational strategy, and not merely a curiosity of Newcomb-style cases. My argument, compressed, is this: in the relevant cases each agent assigns high credence to a model of the other that includes the other's model of them; each agent's decision procedure is therefore predictive of the other's; and because each side's cooperative choice is evidence for, or a logical correlate of, the other side's cooperative choice, each can have a decision-theoretic reason to cooperate even though defection causally dominates. I am not defending the claim that acausal trade is always rational, only that there is a coherent and non-negligible class of cases in which it is.

The audience is mostly game theorists and economists who work on mechanism design and take it as obvious that, absent a mechanism, a contract, or a repeated-game incentive, defection is the only rational play in a one-shot interaction; there are also two philosophers who one-box in Newcomb's problem but regard acausal trade as a reductio of taking that verdict seriously, and a computer scientist who builds multi-agent simulations and will press me on what algorithm the agents are supposed to be running. Prepare my Q&A sheet. Give me the objections I will actually get from this audience, in roughly the order they will come, each phrased the way these people would phrase it, with a short reply and a note on where that reply is soft. I can already anticipate the generic objections; what I need is the specific form each takes from a mechanism designer, from a one-boxing philosopher who wants to stop short of acausal trade, and from someone who has tried to implement this kind of reasoning in code. Tell me which objections have real force and what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and include the question I am hoping nobody asks.
