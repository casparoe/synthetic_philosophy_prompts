Frank Ramsey died in 1930, long before the Allais paradox was discovered in 1953. Yet Ramsey's work on subjective probability and the measurement of utility through betting preferences was one of the founding contributions to the expected utility framework whose independence axiom the Allais paradox calls into question. Ramsey held that the content of a belief is exhausted by the actions it disposes us to take, and that probability and utility are to be derived from an agent's betting preferences under idealized conditions. He was also a pragmatist who treated normative questions about belief and action as answerable by reference to what would serve an agent's aims in the long run.

Given everything we know about Ramsey's philosophical views and his approach to probability and decision, what position do you think he would have taken on the normative status of the Allais paradox? Specifically, would he have treated the preference patterns that generate the paradox as revealing a genuine feature of rational choice that forces a revision of expected utility theory, or would he have treated them as errors to be corrected by some kind of reflective equilibrium process? He would not have had access to the later empirical literature on the paradox, so your answer should be grounded in his own philosophical commitments. Justify your answer by engaging with those commitments, and consider the resources within his framework that might push in each direction.
