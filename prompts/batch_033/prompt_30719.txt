ok so here's what im wondering. Keynes in his 1930 essay Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren had this whole argument that within a hundred years we'd basically solve the economic problem — capital accumulation plus technical progress would make scarcity a thing of the past — and he thought we'd be working like 15 hour weeks. but his real concern wasnt whether we'd get rich enough, it was whether we'd know what to do with ourselves once work stopped being the centre of life. he called this the problem of "how to occupy the leisure" and worried that the old work ethic was so baked into human psychology that we'd have a kind of collective nervous breakdown when freed from it.

fast forward to 2025 and the 15 hour week never really happened for most people, but something else is going on: large language models and AI are starting to automate the kind of cognitive and creative work that Keynes probably would have assumed was safe from mechanisation. at the same time you've got the FIRE movement, UBI experiments, the great resignation, people talking about "post-work" futures, and also just a lot of ambient despair — doomscrolling, meaning crises, the feeling that even people with objectively comfortable lives arent exactly living "wisely and agreeably and well."

so my question is: if you sat Keynes down and walked him through 2025, what would his diagnosis be? would he say his core argument was basically right but the timeline was off, and all the weird stuff we're seeing is the beginning of the adjustment crisis he predicted? or would he revise the deeper claim about the good life — maybe conclude that the problem was never just about getting over the work ethic but about something else he missed? i'm especially interested in what he'd make of the fact that the people most liberated from work (aristocrats, trust fund kids, early retirees) often don't seem to be the happiest or most flourishing — does that count against his thesis or does it just mean they havent actually escaped the work-ethic hangover?

and one more thing: he wrote this essay during the great depression, which is kind of wild — here's the world in economic freefall and he's out here saying the long run problem is too much leisure. do you think the 2025 equivalent of that move would be someone in the middle of an AI-driven unemployment wave writing about how actually were about to have more meaningful lives — and would that be crazy or prophetic?
