So I'm on the board of this small family foundation — my great-aunt left some money and we give away maybe $200K a year. Nothing huge but it matters to us. We've been funding bed net distributions in sub-Saharan Africa for years, and I've seen the data, it's solid: roughly speaking we prevent a malaria death for every few thousand dollars we spend. I feel good about that, it's concrete, I can point to it.

But last month one of my cousins on the board brought in this friend of his who works at an AI safety org, and this guy made a pitch that's been keeping me up at night. His argument was basically: look, there's maybe a 0.1% chance that advanced AI goes catastrophically wrong in the next few decades and literally ends human civilization. Their research program, if it succeeds, might reduce that risk by, say, 10% of what it currently is. Do the math: that's a 0.01% chance of saving literally everyone alive today plus all future generations — call it 10^11 or 10^12 lives in expectation, even if you discount the future pretty heavily. Compared to saving maybe 50 lives a year from malaria, the expected value just isn't close. Not even in the same universe.

And yet. Something in me recoils from this. If I follow this logic, shouldn't I also sell the foundation's entire endowment and put it all into whatever has the tiniest chance of preventing the most catastrophic outcome? There's always some new existential risk, some longshot with astronomical stakes. It feels like the reasoning has no stopping point — it just eats everything. But I also can't find the flaw in the expected value argument itself. Like, I can't point to the step where it goes wrong, I just don't want to live in a world where all our giving gets funnelled into 0.001% chances of saving the universe. My cousin thinks we should shift half our giving to AI safety starting this year and honestly I'm tempted but also deeply unsettled.

I guess my question is: am I being irrational to hesitate here? Is there a principled reason to not let tiny probabilities of astronomical outcomes dominate all our decisions, or am I just flinching away from an uncomfortable conclusion?
