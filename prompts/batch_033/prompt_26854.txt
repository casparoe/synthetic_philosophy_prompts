I've been thinking about Frank Jackson's thought experiment where Mary the neuroscientist knows every physical fact about color vision but has spent her whole life in a black-and-white room, and when she finally steps outside and sees a red rose, she learns something new. I find the knowledge argument convincing: if she learns a new fact upon having the experience, then not all facts are physical facts, and physicalism is false. I want to know what would count against this argument. I am not asking for the standard replies I have already come across — that she merely gains an ability rather than factual knowledge, or that she acquires a new concept without acquiring a new fact. What I want is a map of what this position is actually exposed to. Which possible empirical findings, conceptual developments, or results in neighboring areas would genuinely threaten it? What would a defender of the knowledge argument have to concede as counting against their view, and what would they dismiss as question-begging or as merely relabeling the phenomenon? I would like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want an honest assessment of whether the argument has been qualified in ways that insulate it from falsification — and if it has, whether that is a problem for an argument of this kind.
