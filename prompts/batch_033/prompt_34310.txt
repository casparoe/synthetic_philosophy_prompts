And it's a bad theory. It has all these explanatory failures (dreams, mental illness, the binding problem, whatever), it hasn't made progress in millennia, and it's not even smoothly integratable with neuroscience. So, he says, beliefs and desires are going to go the way of phlogiston. Just like we stopped believing in phlogiston when we got a better theory of combustion, we'll stop believing in beliefs when neuroscience matures. The word "belief" will sound as quaint as "caloric fluid." He deploys this analogy to shut down basically any objection — "you're just clinging to the old theory," that kind of thing.

And I get that analogies aren't supposed to be perfect, but this one is doing a ton of argumentative work for him, and I'm not sure it can carry it. So I want you to audit the analogy. Which ones are just surface-level and which ones actually matter for the conclusion he's trying to draw?
