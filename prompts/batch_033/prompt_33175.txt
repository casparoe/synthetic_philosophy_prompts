I'm helping to put together a research agenda for a small group (four to six people) that wants to spend the next two to three years on foundational questions about logical uncertainty. The group has strong backgrounds in mathematics, theoretical computer science, and formal epistemology, but they are not in a position to run empirical ML experiments. Their long-term interest is in understanding how bounded agents should reason about the logical consequences of their own beliefs and actions—the kind of thing that bears on the problem of designing AI systems that reason under deductive uncertainty about their own future reasoning.

The core of the area, as I understand it, is the logical induction framework and its surrounding literature: the logical induction criterion, the idea of a logical inductor as a sequence of probability distributions that dominate polynomial-time computable trading strategies, and the various results about what properties such inducers satisfy—convergence to logical truths, coherence in the limit, asymptotic learning of logical equivalences, and so on. But there are also a number of open questions that branch off from this. Some are internal to the theory: whether convergence results can be strengthened to finite-time bounds, or how to handle reflection principles and self-trust without falling into paradox. Others are about connections to nearby areas: how to define logical counterfactuals in a principled way, how logical induction relates to various decision theories (updatelessness, functional decision theory), and whether the framework can be extended from passive belief formation to agents that actively choose actions whose consequences depend on their own future reasoning. Still others are architectural: whether the inductor framework can be made computationally tractable enough to guide the design of practical systems, or whether it should be treated as a normative ideal that practical systems approximate.

What I need from you is a prioritization: which three or four open questions in this cluster are most worth the group's time, and in what order should they be tackled? I want the reasoning behind the ordering, not just the list. For each question, I'd like to know: (a) how upstream it is—how many other questions hinge on its answer; (b) how tractable it is for a group with a purely theoretical toolkit; (c) what a satisfactory resolution would look like concretely—what theorem, construction, or conceptual clarification would count as progress; and (d) what would cause you to deprioritize it—what evidence in the next year or two would make you conclude it's a dead end or less important than it seemed. If there are important questions in the area that you think the group should explicitly not work on, and why, I'd like to hear about those too.
