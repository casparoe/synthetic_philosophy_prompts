I'm on my town's citizen advisory committee — we're supposed to make recommendations on the big zoning overhaul, and we've got twelve members. The thing is, people are starting to split into two camps that basically act like mini parties: there's a pro-development bloc and a preservationist bloc, and they meet separately before our full meetings, come in with pre-cooked positions, and then spend the sessions just trying to score points. I've been reading Simone Weil on my own — she thought political parties were basically a disease, that they kill independent thought and turn everything into us-vs-them. I want to propose we adopt a rule that committee members cant caucus in advance or coordinate as a bloc. But I know the counterargument: people say its natural and even efficient for like-minded folks to work together, and banning it is just suppressing free association. I need to draft a short memo to the committee, recommending we adopt the no-caucus rule. It should lay out the reasons, take the other side seriously, and give a concrete proposal for how we'd actually run meetings instead (like round-robin speaking or something). Keep it to something we could actually vote on at our next meeting.
