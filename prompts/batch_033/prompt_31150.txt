Question: "Present Fitch's proof that if all truths are knowable then all truths are known, and state what the result is standardly taken to show."

Student's answer: "Assume for reductio that some truth is unknown: p ∧ ¬Kp. The knowability principle says that every truth is possibly known, so this conjunction is possibly known: ◇K(p ∧ ¬Kp). Since knowledge distributes over conjunction, K(p ∧ ¬Kp) implies Kp ∧ K¬Kp. Since knowledge is factive, K¬Kp implies ¬Kp. So K(p ∧ ¬Kp) implies Kp ∧ ¬Kp, which is impossible; hence the conjunction p ∧ ¬Kp is unknowable, contradicting the knowability principle. Therefore, if every truth is knowable, then there are no unknown truths: every truth is known. The standard lesson is that this refutes semantic anti-realism, since the anti-realist's identification of truth with knowability collapses into the absurd conclusion that we are omniscient."
