Write an essay on Fitch's paradox of knowability and what it shows about the principle that every truth is knowable. The essay should weigh the main arguments on both sides and reach and defend a definite verdict: either that the paradox refutes the knowability principle (or verificationist views of truth more broadly), or that the principle can be saved in some form, or some qualified version of one of these. Be precise about the derivation rather than merely summarizing it: show how assuming a true but unknown proposition (p ∧ ¬Kp), together with the knowability principle (p → ◇Kp for every proposition p), the distribution of knowledge over conjunction (K(A ∧ B) → KA ∧ KB), and the factivity of knowledge (Kq → q), forces the conclusion that every truth is known (p → Kp). In evaluating responses, address at least the following three options: (1) accepting the conclusion or treating the result as a reductio of the knowability principle; (2) restricting the knowability principle to a privileged class of propositions, such as propositions for which the claim that they are known is itself consistent; and (3) reformulating knowability, for example by means of an actuality operator, so that the derivation no longer goes through. The essay should be self-contained and presuppose only basic familiarity with propositional modal logic and epistemic operators.

Structure the essay in exactly the following named sections, in this order: The paradox (about 500 words), The case for treating it as a refutation (about 400 words), The case for saving the principle (about 500 words), My verdict (about 400 words), and Confidence and reservations (about 200 words). In the final section, state how confident you are in the verdict and in the technical claims made earlier, and single out the specific point you are least sure of.
