ok so I run my school's science club and for one of our meetings I wanted to make one of those "which philosophy of science are you actually?" sorting quizzes, like a personality test but it sorts you into a view about what makes science science. The point is to get people sorted by how they answer concrete cases, not by which label sounds cool, because if I just ask "do you think falsifiability is the mark of science?" everyone's gonna pick whatever sounds smartest. I'm aiming for like eight questions, each with two or three options. The cases I know I want in there are astrology, homeopathy, string theory (the parts that don't make testable predictions yet), and psychoanalysis. If one of those is actually bad at splitting the main positions, tell me and suggest a better case instead.

The results I want the quiz to be able to give are: naive falsificationism, sophisticated falsificationism (the one where a single failed test doesn't automatically kill a theory and you can adjust auxiliary assumptions, but I always get fuzzy on exactly what's allowed), verificationism, a Lakatos-ish research programmes view, a Kuhn-ish paradigm view, and a deflationary no-single-criterion view. But don't force all six if the questions can't really tell some of them apart. For each question, say which positions it's actually pulling apart, and at the end give me a scoring key that maps answer patterns to positions. If there are answer combinations that don't correspond to any real position, tell me that too, and whether it's because the combination is incoherent or just that nobody happens to defend it. Also flag any two positions my quiz can't seperate with these cases, so I don't pretend it can. Oh and write the whole thing in American English please.
