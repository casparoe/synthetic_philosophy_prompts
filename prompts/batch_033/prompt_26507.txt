I am writing a novel set in a near-future polity whose founding charter declares that all citizens are entitled to equality. The narrative turns on the internal tensions of that principle — the charter says "equality," but the society's institutions must operationalize it, and what they choose to equalize determines everything from the tax code to the architecture of public space to the grievances that drive my characters into conflict. I need to understand the logical space of possible answers before I commit my fictional society to one, because the choice will shape which resentments are rational, which inequalities persist lawfully, and what a dissident in this world would have to deny.

Please construct a decision tree that takes someone from the vague commitment "citizens should be treated as equals" to a specific egalitarian position. Each branch node should be a concrete institutional question I can answer as the author without knowing the philosophical labels in advance — questions like "does the state monitor how satisfied people report feeling, or does it distribute something measurable and leave satisfaction to them?" or "does the society compensate someone for a disadvantage they brought on themselves through a free choice?" The leaves should name the position (welfarist, resourcist, capabilities egalitarian, luck egalitarian, relational egalitarian, and any others you think belong in the space) and include one sentence on what that position's most awkward commitment would look like in a society honestly trying to live by it.

Where the tree cannot cleanly separate two positions — if they answer every diagnostic question the same way — flag that rather than forcing a distinction. Also note any combinations of answers that are logically possible but that no serious position occupies, with a brief diagnosis of why the combination is empty (incoherent, deeply unattractive, or merely unexplored). The deliverable should help me design a society whose internal contradictions are authentic to the philosophy, not a caricature of it.
