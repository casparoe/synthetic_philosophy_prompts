I'm a grantmaker at a foundation that uses the importance–tractability–neglectedness framework to prioritize causes, but in practice different reviewers apply it so differently that the framework mostly provides cover for prior intuitions rather than disciplining them. I want a decision procedure my team can actually follow — not a scoring rubric, but a structured sequence of questions with stopping rules — that forces us to be explicit about what we're assuming and where we disagree. We review roughly fifteen proposals per quarter, each reviewer has about an hour, and most of us have policy or economics backgrounds without formal training in ethics or decision theory. The procedure should produce one of three outputs: fund, do not fund, or escalate to the board with a specified disagreement (when two reviewers diverge on a values question the procedure identifies but cannot resolve).

Please run the procedure on two proposals: (1) a campaign to phase out lead ammunition, where the harm is well-documented, the intervention is a known policy lever, but several other environmental groups already work on this; and (2) funding foundational research into whether digital minds could suffer, where the stakes are potentially astronomical but we have almost no empirical basis for estimating either the probability or the scale. I want at least one of these to produce a recommendation my team will resist, so I can see whether the procedure actually constrains us rather than ratifying what we already think. Finally, tell me what kinds of proposals the procedure will systematically mishandle, and which judgment calls it merely relocates rather than resolves. Give me a committed answer — take definite positions on what the procedure should look like and defend them, rather than laying out options and leaving me to choose.
