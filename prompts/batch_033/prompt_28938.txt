I'm drafting a short paper on Kripke's rule-following paradox, and I'd be grateful for comments on the following argument before I commit to it. The paper targets the dispositionalist response to the skeptic. I reconstruct the skeptic's challenge as follows: for any candidate fact about a speaker that is supposed to constitute her meaning addition by '+', the skeptic shows that the fact either underdetermines what she should answer in novel cases or collapses into mere behavior. The dispositionalist replies that meaning addition is constituted by the speaker's disposition to answer with the sum, where 'disposition' is understood not as what she in fact would say when prompted but as what an appropriately idealized version of her would say under appropriate conditions. My objection is that this idealization reintroduces the normativity problem: specifying the idealization requires saying which conditions are the ones that reveal the rule rather than distort it, and that selection is itself a normative matter. So the dispositionalist has not reduced semantic normativity to descriptive fact; she has smuggled a normative standard into the very idea of an ideal disposition.

I suspect this is simply a restatement of the familiar normativity objection, but I am not entirely sure. Is the argument too quick? In particular, could a dispositionalist grant that 'ideal conditions' are selected by norms while insisting that those norms are only epistemic or pragmatic rather than semantic — and would that concession be enough to save the view? I would also welcome comments on whether my reconstruction of the skeptic's challenge is fair, and on what you take to be the strongest version of the dispositionalist reply that my argument still fails to address.
