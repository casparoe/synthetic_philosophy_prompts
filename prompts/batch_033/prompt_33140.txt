Draft an adversarial-collaboration document between a proponent of the view that conscious experience involves a unified "workspace" or "stage" where contents are made available to a central system (a family of views that Daniel Dennett characterizes as presupposing a "Cartesian theater") and a proponent of Dennett's multiple drafts model, according to which there is no central presentation of conscious contents and instead consciousness is distributed across parallel, continuously revised streams of content with no single "final draft."

The document should include:

(1) A numbered list of claims both parties can sign — covering the shared empirical data, the phenomena a theory of consciousness must account for, and any methodological commitments they hold in common.

(2) A disagreements table. Each row should specify a single point of disagreement, with the workspace theorist's position stated in one column (in wording that party would endorse) and the multiple-drafts theorist's position stated in the other (in wording that party would endorse). Avoid formulations that either side would regard as a caricature of its view.

(3) The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party toward the other's position. For each crux, explain why it is a crux — what hangs on it, and what each side would need to concede if the crux were resolved against them.

(4) For each crux, specify at least one empirical test, phenomenological case, or argument that both parties agree would bear on it. Record each party's prediction about the outcome in advance, and describe what pattern of results would count as evidence for or against each view.

Throughout, pay careful attention to whether any part of the dispute is terminological rather than substantive. If, for example, the two parties use "conscious content" to mean different things such that some apparent disagreements dissolve once the terms are disambiguated, identify those points explicitly and explain why the remaining disagreement — if any — is not merely verbal.
