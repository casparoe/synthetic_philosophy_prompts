I'm organizing a discussion session for an interpretability reading group I run with some colleagues, and I'd like to commission a dialogue that captures a genuine unresolved tension in the field — the kind of thing that comes up in Q&A sessions but rarely gets spelled out in papers. The topic is mechanism identity: when we find a circuit in one neural network and then go looking for "the same" circuit in another network (different random seed, different training data, or even a different architecture), what criteria should we use to decide whether we've actually found it?

The two positions I want represented are these. (1) A functionalist view: two circuits are the same mechanism if they perform the same computational function — same input-output mapping across relevant distributions, same behavior under intervention, same role in the larger system. On this view, implementation details like which specific heads are involved or how the computation is distributed across layers are incidental; insisting on structural isomorphism mistakes the implementation for the mechanism and risks missing genuine generalizations across models. (2) A structuralist view: mechanism identity is about the causal topology at the level of features — the nodes and edges in the information-flow graph. If two circuits route information through different intermediate representations or have different connectivity patterns, they are different mechanisms, even if they produce identical outputs on all current test distributions. On this view, collapsing mechanism identity into functional equivalence defeats the purpose of opening the black box in the first place.

Please write a dialogue between two researchers — call them Mira (the functionalist) and Kai (the structuralist) — who are comparing circuits they've found in two different training runs of the same architecture. Both should be competent and make their best case; neither should be a strawman. The dialogue should involve real back-and-forth where each character responds to the other's actual arguments rather than talking past each other. I'd like it to surface the deeper methodological question — what counts as the "right" level of description for individuating mechanisms, and whether this disagreement is substantive or partly verbal — without resolving it neatly. A concrete running example (say, an indirect object identification circuit, or whatever you think works best) would help keep things grounded. Aim for roughly 8 to 12 exchanges.
