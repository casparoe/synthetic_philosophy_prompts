I'm teaching a course on creativity and computation next term and I want to open the first seminar with a poem that does Hofstadter's knob-twiddling idea instead of explaining it. You know the picture: genuinely new things almost never come from nowhere; you take some familiar structure and turn its parameters, and the creative act is knowing which knobs to twiddle, how far, and which of the resulting variations are worth keeping — the crux is recognizing which variations matter, not generating them. Id like a villanelle. The form should do the work: the two refrains shouldn't repeat verbatim the way they do in a textbook villanelle; each time a refrain returns it should be a small, controlled variation of itself, one word or image shifted while the line stays recognizable, the way a theme gets varied. Choose refrains that can actually absorb those changes. The closing quatrain should make the reader feel the recognition of the variation as the point — the poem should be doing creativity, not describing it. Someone who knows GEB should see exactly what your doing, but it should also just hold up as a poem.
