Here is an argument about updating on conditional information that seems to me to prove too much, and I would like to know where it goes wrong.

Consider the Judy Benjamin problem. A soldier is dropped into a region divided into four quadrants: Red-Second, Red-Headquarters, Blue-Second, and Blue-Headquarters. Her prior is uniform over the four quadrants. She then receives a message from headquarters: if she is in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 that she is in Headquarters Company area rather than Second Company area. The standard procedure for updating on such a conditional-probability constraint is to select the posterior distribution that satisfies the constraint and minimizes cross-entropy relative to the prior. That procedure yields a posterior in which her probability of being in Red territory falls from 0.5 to approximately 0.47.

Now consider the following chain of reasoning:

(1) The message constrains only the conditional probability of being in Headquarters given Red.
(2) A rational update must not change credences on matters about which the evidence is silent.
(3) The evidence is silent about whether she is in Red rather than Blue; it says only how her location would be distributed if she were in Red.
(4) Therefore, a rational posterior should leave her probability for Red at 0.5, while making her conditional probability of being in Headquarters given Red equal to 3/4.
(5) The minimum cross-entropy posterior violates this: it changes her probability for Red.
(6) But the minimum cross-entropy rule is the least arbitrary known way to update on such constraints; abandoning it seems to leave no principled rule for learning a conditional probability.
(7) So either rational updating on conditional information is impossible, or a rational update can change credences on matters about which the evidence is silent.

Both horns seem unacceptable. Where does the argument go wrong? Is it the slide from 'the message is a conditional' to 'the message is silent about the marginal'? Or is the standard rule genuinely irrational here? I am inclined to hold on to (2), but I want to know what I am paying if I do.
