I've been thinking about Hofstadter's MU-puzzle and the idea of "jumping out of the system" (JOOTSing) — you know, how within the MIU formal system you can bash your head against the rules forever and never derive MU, but the moment you step outside and notice that no rule ever decreases the number of I's in a string unless it's by a multiple of three, the whole thing becomes obvious. What do you think are the most important implications of this for how we should think about the limitations of large language models? Do they have a kind of JOOTS problem — are there things they fundamentally can't "see" because they're stuck operating within a certain kind of system, and if so, what would count as stepping outside it? Or is the analogy misleading when applied to something like an LLM?
