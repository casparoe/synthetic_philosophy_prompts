I'm TAing an undergrad political philosophy course and I've got a stack of short-answer questions on Nozick to grade. One of the exam questions was:

"Reconstruct Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain argument from Anarchy, State, and Utopia. Be explicit about which principles of distributive justice the argument is aimed at, and state as precisely as you can what the argument does and does not establish."

Here's one student's answer, reproduced exactly:

"Nozick wants to show that end-state principles of distributive justice, which he also calls patterned principles, cannot be maintained without constant interference with liberty. He asks us to suppose that some favored pattern, like equality or distribution according to need, is realized in a distribution D1. Wilt Chamberlain, a famous basketball player, arranges to receive 25 cents from each ticket sold, and a million people voluntarily pay it, so he ends up with $250,000. The resulting distribution D2 is more unequal, so it no longer fits the original pattern. But since D1 was just and each transfer was voluntary, Nozick says D2 must be just as well. The only way to preserve the pattern would be to keep interfering, by banning such transfers or taxing them away. Nozick therefore concludes that any end-state principle is incompatible with liberty, and that only his own historical, unpatterned entitlement theory is acceptable."

I need to give this a grade and point-by-point comments the student could actually learn from. I'm not looking for a one-line verdict. Please mark exactly which steps in the reconstruction are right, which are subtly off, and why; if there are any conflations or overreach, call them out specifically and say what the correct distinction or the more limited conclusion would be. If you'd give partial credit, say roughly how you'd allocate it.

Since I have to calibrate the rest of my grading to this, please also say how confident you are in your assesment overall, and single out the point you're least sure about.
