I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, so I'll state the view in my own terms and ask you to keep the answer self-contained. I've come to accept an externalist account of meaning: what at least some of my words refer to is not fixed entirely by what is inside my head. My internal state — my concepts, my dispositions, my entire neural configuration — could be identical to someone else's while our words pick out different things, because part of what fixes reference lies outside the speaker: the actual substance in the environment that a word has been causally tied to, and the way the surrounding linguistic community divides the labor of identifying it. The Twin Earth case, the fact that I can competently use a term like 'gold' while a jeweler is the one who actually fixes its extension, and the strong intuition that by 'water' I mean the local stuff rather than anything functionally indistinguishable all push me toward this.

I want to know what would count against it before I keep building on it. Don't give me a reading list or a bibliography. I want the considerations that should actually move me, sorted by how decisive they would be: (1) the observations, possible cases, and arguments that a committed externalist would themselves concede as counting against the position, not just what internalist critics say — and for each, what the world would have to be like for it to go through; (2) the consequences the view is stuck with — for instance, about whether I can know the contents of my own thoughts — that, if unacceptable or empirically false, would pull the view down with them; and (3) which distinguishing predictions the view makes that its internalist rivals don't, and whether any of those have already come out the wrong way. Mark each as fatal, seriously damaging, or merely awkward, and say which part of the view is doing the work — the claim about natural kind terms specifically, the claim about all words, the claim about reference as opposed to thought content — and where the position is most exposed. Finally, tell me honestly whether the view, as it is usually defended, risks anything at all or has been hedged into unfalsifiability, and whether that hedging is a problem for a claim of this kind.
