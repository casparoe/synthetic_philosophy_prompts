I spent thirty years in a physics lab before retiring, and lately I've been reading moral philosophy — Williams, Nussbaum, the whole lot. One thing that's really gotten under my skin is the question of whether genuine moral dilemmas actually exist. You know, situations where two moral demands genuinely conflict and there's no correct resolution, no matter how carefully you think about it. Some philosophers say no — there's always a right answer, you just have to weigh things properly. Others (like Bernard Williams) say that's nonsense, that some conflicts leave a moral remainder no matter what you choose, and pretending otherwise is a kind of self-deception.

I've been trying to get my head around this, and I think a good story might help more than another argument. Could you write me a short story that explores this tension? I want something that doesn't lecture — just a narrative that puts a character in a situation where the reader genuinely feels the pull of both sides of the dilemma, and maybe comes away less sure about whether there's a "right" answer.

Don't make it about something cartoonishly dramatic like the trolley problem. Make it subtle. Something where the conflict is between two deeply held comittments that both seem non-negotiable, and the character has to live with whatever they choose.

After the story, please include a section called "Commentary" — maybe 300 words or so — where you explain the philosophical choices you made in constructing the dilemma and how you tried to make it resist easy resolution.
