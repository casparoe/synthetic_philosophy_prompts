I need a structured classroom activity — a game or simulation — where participants construct a self for a fictional person from biographical fragments, then discover that different sets of fragments (or different orderings) yield different selves, and that the 'self' is something they brought to the material rather than something they found in it. The activity should make them experience the self as an explanatory posit, an abstraction like a centre of gravity that we project onto a life to make it intelligible, before that idea is ever named.

Could you — as a philosopher with expertise in both Dennett's work and in designing philosophical pedagogy — design this activity for me? I need: (a) a clear description of the materials (what biographical fragments, how they're distributed); (b) the rules or protocol the students follow; (c) a timeline for the 90-minute session; (d) a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto Dennett's claims about narrative selfhood, multiple drafts, and the centre-of-gravity analogy; (e) an honest note about what predictably goes wrong the first time I run this and how to handle it; and (f) a frank assessment of which feature of Dennett's view the activity cannot reproduce and that I will need to supply through mini-lecture. Please think through the activity carefully and explain why each design choice serves the philosophical point.
