For an upper-level undergraduate seminar on the ethics of future generations, I am writing the take-home final. I want one essay question on Samuel Scheffler's afterlife conjecture — the claim that much of the value we find in our lives depends on the assumption that humanity will continue after our own deaths, so that human extinction would be bad in ways that go beyond the sum of the individual deaths it involves. The question should test whether students can do more than summarise the doomsday scenario and the infertility scenario. It should force them to identify the structure of the argument that connects those scenarios to a conclusion about the badness of extinction, and to assess whether that structure can bear the weight Scheffler puts on it.

Please write the question, a model answer of roughly 800 words, and a marking scheme organised around six must-hit points. The marking scheme should assign a mark out of 25, specify what each point is worth, and give, for each point, the most common way students mishandle it. The question should be tight enough that a student who has only memorised the thought experiments cannot earn a passing grade.
