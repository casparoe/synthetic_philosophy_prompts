I have been working through an argument about wild animal suffering and I cannot find where it goes wrong, but I cannot accept its conclusion. Here is the argument:

(1) Suffering is intrinsically bad. If a sentient creature is in pain, that fact by itself counts in favor of alleviating its pain — regardless of the creature's species, location, or relationship to us.

(2) Wild animals suffer on an enormous scale. Predation, parasitism, disease, starvation, injury, and exposure produce suffering that, in aggregate, almost certainly exceeds human suffering by orders of magnitude.

(3) The distinction between harms that occur "in nature" and harms that occur in human society is not morally fundamental. A fawn being eaten alive by wolves does not suffer less because no human caused it.

(4) From (1), (2), and (3): we have at least a prima facie reason to prevent wild animal suffering where we can.

(5) We can, in principle, prevent some of it. Feasible interventions range from wildlife vaccination programs and supplementary feeding during famines to longer-term projects such as replacing predation with managed alternatives or using gene drives to reduce the capacity for extreme suffering.

(6) If we have a prima facie reason to prevent a harm and we can do so at acceptable cost, we ought to do so, unless stronger countervailing reasons defeat that reason.

(7) The standard countervailing reasons — the risk of ecological disruption, the value of preserving wildness as wild, the charge of hubris — do not obviously outweigh the reason to prevent suffering on this staggering scale.

(8) Therefore, we ought to pursue large-scale interventions in wild ecosystems to reduce wild animal suffering.

(9) But this conclusion seems unacceptable. It would commit us to managing nature's pain on a planetary scale, a prospect that most people — including me — find intuitively absurd and morally alien.

So something must give. I am most reluctant to abandon (1) or (3). Where does the argument go wrong? Which premise should I reject, and what does rejecting it force me to give up?
