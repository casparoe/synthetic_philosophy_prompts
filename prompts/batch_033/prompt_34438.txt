Over the past year I have found myself increasingly committed to Van Fraassen's Reflection principle: the idea that my present credences ought to be constrained by the credences I expect to have in the future. More precisely, my probability in a proposition now, conditional on my future self assigning it probability r, should itself be r — I should treat my anticipated future opinions as the opinions of an expert I defer to, unless I can point to new evidence I will gain in the interim. I have defended the principle in a seminar, leaning on the Dutch strategy argument that someone who violates Reflection is vulnerable to a diachronic book. But I have come to suspect that I have only heard the friendly version of the story, and that the principle survives largely because its defenders quietly restrict its scope whenever a counterexample appears. This stopped being a merely technical question for me when I began planning around a decision my future self would have to carry out under conditions I know will degrade my judgment, and I realized I was genuinely unsure whether rationality required me to defer to that future self.

I want an honest map of what would count against Reflection. Which cases — forgetting, cognitive decline, drugs or other interventions, being systematically misled later, self-locating or indexical cases — should a defender of the principle concede are genuine counterexamples, and which are merely outside its intended scope? Which lines of argument actually bite, and which trade on confusing a conditional credence with something else? I would like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, including an assessment of whether the principle has been qualified into near-vacuity — a norm that amounts, in effect, to deferring to your future self except in precisely the cases where such deference seems plainly wrong — and whether that is a serious problem or a respectable feature of a norm of rationality. I would personally find cases where my future self is less informed or less competent than I am now the most persuasive; I want to know whether that intuition survives scrutiny, and what a committed defender would say in response.
