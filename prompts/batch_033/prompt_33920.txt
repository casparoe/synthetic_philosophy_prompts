Clinical research treats the placebo response as a nuisance. In many randomized controlled trials, a drug must beat a sugar pill by a margin, and the placebo arm is treated as a baseline to be subtracted so that the drug's "true" effect can be isolated — even though placebo responses in pain and depression are themselves well documented. The practice seems to presuppose that the effect of a treatment and the effect of expecting a treatment are separable contributions to a symptom: the symptom is a bodily signal the drug dampens, while belief merely adds noise on top. Reconstruct the implicit theory of mind, perception, and symptom experience that this trial design presupposes. Does the methodology actually commit anyone to a Cartesian or modular picture, or is that only a convenient approximation that several theories could license? Then evaluate it from the standpoint of predictive processing and the free energy principle, on which symptoms like pain and low mood are themselves inferences and expectation is part of the generative mechanism rather than a confound.
