Please argue that hyperreal-valued utilitarianism fails as a solution to the infinities in ethics—not because of technical quibbles about the construction, but because it merely relocates the problem of arbitrariness rather than resolving it. Specifically: the choice of a free ultrafilter on the natural numbers is not a harmless technical parameter but a morally loaded selection that determines which infinite populations count as better than which others, and no non-arbitrary principle picks out one ultrafilter over another. Moreover, the standard reply—that any free ultrafilter yields the same verdicts on all "ordinary" finite comparisons and that we can therefore remain agnostic about which one is correct—is inadequate, because the whole point of the hyperreal approach was to break ties in infinite cases where finite-limiting methods gave no answer. If the approach only works when supplemented by a choice we have no reason to make one way rather than another, it has not succeeded in giving us a reason to adopt its verdicts. Defend this line of argument against the best version of the hyperrealist reply you can construct, and close by considering whether the failure of the hyperreal approach tells us something deeper about the prospects for any totalizing solution to infinite ethics. Take a definite position throughout and give your reasons.
