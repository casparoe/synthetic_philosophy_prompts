Two people in my reading group have been arguing for a year and I think they should've done an adversarial collaboration instead. One is a robust moral realist: she says moral claims like 'torturing cats for fun is wrong' are the kind of thing that can be true or false, at least some are true, and their truth doesn't depend on what anyone approves of. The other is an expressivist: he says moral claims don't state facts at all — they express attitudes, plans, praise, blame, and so on. They mostly talk past eachother, and I can't tell how much of the fight is real and how much is about words like 'true,' 'fact,' and 'belief.'

Write the joint statement they should have produced. Include a numbered section of claims both can sign, worded so neither would wince; then a disagreements table where each side is stated precisely and in wording its own side would accept; then the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of them. For each crux give a concrete case or argument both would agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction ahead of time. I have a hunch one crux is about what's going on when moral claims show up inside conditionals and negations ('if stealing is wrong then asking your brother to steal is wrong'), and another about whether the link between judging something wrong and feeling some motivation not to do it is part of the meaning or just a common psychological fact. Be explicit about which parts of the dispute are merely verbal. The last line should be a one-sentence summary of the deepest real disagreement, with nothing after it.
