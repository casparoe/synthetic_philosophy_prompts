I want to step back from the back-and-forth about whether cognitive penetration of perception occurs and ask instead: what would an adequate theory of the phenomenon have to get right, regardless of whether it ultimately vindicates or debunks the claim that cognition penetrates perception? Please give me the full list of desiderata — the cases a theory must handle, the distinctions it must respect, the empirical constraints it must satisfy, and the explanatory demands it must meet — with the reasoning behind each.

At minimum, I expect the list to address the following. First, the distinction between a cognitive state's genuinely penetrating perceptual experience and its merely shifting attention or altering post-perceptual judgment, and why this distinction is not just terminological but matters for what the theory is about. Second, the structural constraint illustrated by the El Greco fallacy as applied to cognitive penetration — roughly, that if a cognitive state is claimed to alter perception in some way, the theory must be consistent about whether that alteration applies to the perception of the very outputs the cognitive state is supposed to explain. Third, whether a theory must accommodate penetration by conative and evaluative states (desires, fears, values) in addition to doxastic states (beliefs, expectations), and if so, whether the mechanism must be the same. Fourth, whether the theory must specify a locus of penetration — early vision, late vision, amodal completion, multisensory integration — and what turns on getting the locus right. Fifth, what the theory must say, if anything, about the epistemic consequences of penetration: does it threaten perceptual justification, and must a theory of penetration take a stand on that?

For each desideratum, tell me what consideration or case forces it onto the list, and whether it is a non-negotiable requirement or a preference of one side of the debate. If you think some pair of requirements cannot be jointly met — for example, because satisfying one would make the phenomenon empirically untestable — that is the part I most want to see. Finally, if there is a single requirement that does the most to narrow the space of viable theories, identify it and explain why.
