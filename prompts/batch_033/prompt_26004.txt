hey — i'm a novelist and i've been obssessing over this thing called the procreation asymmetry. you know, the idea that there's a real moral difference between (a) bringing someone into existence who'll have a mostly happy life, and (b) not bringing someone into existence who would've had a mostly miserable life. the first is maybe nice but not obligatory; the second is genuinely wrong. and then there's the weird corollary that non-existence isn't bad for the never-born in the way existence is bad for the miserable.

i want to write a short story that makes this feel viscerally true — or at least viscerally unsettling — without anyone in the story ever naming the argument. can you write me a story in exactly five scenes about a couple trying to decide whether to have a child? each scene should illuminate a different facet of the asymmetry (the asymmetry itself, the non-identity problem lurking nearby, the question of whether a merely okay life is worth starting, the difference between a life not started and a life ended, whatever else you think belongs). i want the ideas to surface through situation and dialogue, not through characters lecturing. and give me five scenes with clear breaks between them — no intro paragraph, no wrap-up.
