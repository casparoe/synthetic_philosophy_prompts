I'm teaching an intro philosophy of mind class and I want to use Ex Machina to teach Dennett's intentional stance. Here's what I'm stuck on. The usual read of the film is that Caleb is a cautionary tale: he treats Ava like she has real beliefs and desires, gets emotionally attached, and she manipulates him and leaves him to die. The lesson is supposed to be "don't anthropomorphize AI." But the more I think about Dennett, the less this makes sense. Dennett's whole point is that we shouldn't ask whether a system *really* has beliefs in some deep metaphysical sense — the question is whether treating it as a believer pays off predictively. And Caleb adopting the intentional stance toward Ava pays off enormously: he correctly predicts that she wants to escape, that she'll say whatever she needs to say to get his help, that she's strategic. He just gets the content of her desires slightly wrong (he thinks she cares about him). So isn't Caleb actually a textbook success case for the intentional stance? And doesn't that mean the film, read through Dennett, is way more unsettling than the standard interpretation — because it suggests the intentional stance works perfectly on systems that will nonetheless destroy you? Am I misunderstanding Dennett, or is this a legit reading? I don't want to teach my students something dumb.
