I teach a graduate seminar on classical Indian epistemology, and I am setting the final exam. I want one essay question on the Nyāya defense of testimony (śabda) as an independent pramāṇa, pitched so that a student cannot pass by reciting the list of pramāṇas or by summarizing the Buddhist position in general terms. The question should force students to reconstruct the strongest Buddhist case for reducing śabda to anumāna — namely, that accepting a report is an inference from the speaker's trustworthiness — and then to engage the Nyāya reply that the two instruments differ in their internal structure: inference requires the inferer's own knowledge of the invariable concomitance (vyāpti) between probans and probandum, whereas testimony's warrant runs through another person's cognition, and the word–object relation is a matter of convention rather than a natural pervasion. The question should also push students to say what this defense reveals about how Nyāya individuates instruments of knowledge in the first place. The exam is 60 minutes and I expect an essay of roughly 700 words. Please write the question itself, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 points that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain and the specific mistakes that should cost marks. No bibliography, reading list, or citations should be asked for or supplied; the question must be answerable from the argument alone. Please write the question, the model answer, and the marking scheme in American English.
