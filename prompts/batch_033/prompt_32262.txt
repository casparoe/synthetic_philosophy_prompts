I've been trying to sort out my views on population ethics by going through cases one at a time, and I'm hoping you can help me extract the principal that's driving my intuitions—or tell me which verdicts I need to abandon.

Here's where I land:

1. World A: a billion people at welfare level 100 (flourishing). World B: two billion people at welfare level 90 (still really good lives). Total welfare is higher in B, and I think B is better. No hesitation.

2. Same World A, but now compare it to World Z: a hundred billion people at welfare level 1—lives barely worth living, a tiny bit more pleasure than pain. Total welfare is roughly the same as A. I think A is clearly better. Z feels repugnant even if the arithmetic lines up.

3. World C: five billion people at welfare 50. World D: those same five billion at 50, plus an extra billion at welfare 10 (lives worth living, but only just). I don't think D is worse than C. But I'm not convinced D is better either. I want to say D is at least permissible.

4. Deciding whether to have a child you know would have a miserable life (welfare -50): clearly wrong, worse than not having them. Deciding whether to have a child you know would have a very happy life (welfare +80): not wrong to refrain, and not required to have them. This asymmetry feels rock solid to me.

5. World E: a billion at welfare 100. World F: a billion at 100, plus one extra person at welfare 0.1—a life barely above zero. I don't think F is worse than E. But I also feel weird saying F is better. It's unsettling to say we improved the world by adding a life that's 0.1 above the threshold.

6. World G: a billion at 100. World H: a billion at 100, plus a billion at welfare -5 (lives not worth living). H is worse than G. No hesitation.

7. Here's the problem. If I accept that adding a billion at welfare 10 doesn't make the world worse (case 3), and adding one person at 0.1 doesn't make the world worse (case 5), then by chaining these moves I seem to get pushed all the way to Z—which I rejected in case 2. So cases 2, 3, and 5 can't all stand together as I've described them.

Is there a principle—or a small set of candidate principles—that captures most of these verdicts? Which ones does it force me to revise, and why those? And what does the principle say about a case I haven't listed: World A (a billion at 100) versus World K (a billion at 100 plus a billion at welfare 1—all lives barely worth living, but not the full hundred-billion extravaganza of Z)?
