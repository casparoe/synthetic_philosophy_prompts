I am a PhD student in formal political theory, working on epistemic justifications of majority rule, and I have been trying to get clear about how much of the classical Condorcet jury theorem survives when its idealizations are relaxed one at a time. I would like you to rank the following five departures from the classical theorem by how much of the theorem's core result each one preserves. By the core result I mean the pair of claims that (a) the majority is more likely to be correct than a randomly selected individual voter, and (b) the probability that the majority is correct tends to one as the number of voters grows without bound. Consider each departure separately, with the other features of the classical model held fixed.

The departures are:

1. Heterogeneous competences: voter i votes correctly with probability p_i, the p_i differ across voters, and the mean competence of the electorate remains above one-half as the electorate grows; votes remain independent.
2. Correlation through the state only: votes are independent conditional on the state of the world, and each voter's signal is correct with probability above one-half in each state, so that any unconditional correlation among votes is generated entirely by the state as a common cause.
3. Exchangeable common-cause correlation: votes are positively correlated through a shared cause that is not reducible to the state, with the pairwise correlation bounded away from zero as the electorate grows.
4. A shared public signal: before voting, every voter observes the same public signal of fixed precision in addition to her own independent private signal, and then votes sincerely for whichever alternative her posterior favors.
5. Pre-vote deliberation: voters truthfully reveal some or all of their private signals to one another before voting, and then vote on the basis of their updated views.

Please rank these five from the one that preserves the most of the core result to the one that preserves the least, with ties allowed, and give a one-sentence justification for each placement. If a departure preserves one of (a) and (b) but not the other, or if its effect depends on a parameter such as the precision of the public signal or the strength of the common-cause correlation, say so and explain how that affects its ranking.
