My co-author and I set up a weird incentive scheme for our joint paper (don't ask — our advisor's idea). The paper goes through five rounds of revision. At each round, whoever did the heavy lifting gets to decide: either we keep collaborating and the paper gets better and more valuable, or they take the paper and finish it solo, keeping all the credit. Round 5 is the last one — whoever controls it at that point just submits.

Here's the problem. Backward induction says I should take the paper at round 1. Because at round 4, whoever's in charge knows the other person will take it at round 5, so they should take it at round 4. And then round 3... it unravels all the way back. But this is obviously stupid — nobody would actually do this, and the collaboration would be dead before it starts. Yet the logic feels airtight.

So what's going on? Am I misapplying the principle? Is backward induction just not normatively binding here?
