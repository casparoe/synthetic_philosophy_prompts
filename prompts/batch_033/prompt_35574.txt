I am a graduate student working on diachronic norms for credence, and I have been reading through the literature on Bas van Fraassen's Reflection principle. I would like to stress-test my objections against the strongest available defense of the view. Please play the role of Bas van Fraassen defending the Reflection principle: the requirement that, for any proposition A, a rational agent's current credence in A conditional on her future credence in A being r should itself be r. You should stay in character and speak in the first person, drawing on the conceptual resources of van Fraassen's actual position — for example, the distinction between cases in which one treats one's future self as an expert and cases in which one does not, the voluntarist conception of rationality, and the treatment of the principle as a synchronic constraint on current conditional credences rather than a substantive rule for updating. You may make distinctions and grant small points, but do not concede that the principle is false or reduce it to a triviality. Keep each reply to roughly a paragraph so that it reads as a conversation. I will say "stop" when I wish to end.

Here is my opening challenge. I have been trying to find a counterexample to Reflection that does not rely on expected irrationality, and I keep coming back to cases of anticipated forgetting. Suppose I am a detective investigating a murder, and my current credence that the butler did it is 0.7. This credence rests on a complex body of evidence, including one especially weighty fact: the butler's fingerprints were found on the weapon. I now know that tomorrow morning, before any new evidence arrives, I will be given a drug that permanently erases exactly this one piece of evidence from my memory. I will not forget that I took the drug or what it does; I will merely lose the fingerprint fact. I also know that I am otherwise fully rational and will update appropriately on my remaining evidence, so my future self will be a competent reasoner in possession of strictly less evidence than I now have. Let us stipulate that my future credence in the butler's guilt will be 0.4. Conditional on that future credence being 0.4, Reflection appears to require my current credence to be 0.4 as well. But I know exactly why the future credence will be 0.4: because my future self will be epistemically impoverished relative to me, not because it will have learned anything I lack. Why should coherence require me to defer to a version of myself that I know to be in a worse evidential position, even if that version is rational by its own lights?
