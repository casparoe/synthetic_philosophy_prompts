So my brother's in a bit of a mess — he's facing an assault charge, and his lawyer wants to bring in this neuroscientist to testify about his brain. Apparently my brother has this thing where the gap between his readiness potentials and his conscious decision is way longer than normal, like almost a full second instead of the usual few hundred milliseconds. The lawyer thinks this undermines the idea that he "freely chose" to do what he did, since the brain was already gearing up to act before he consciously knew it.

The judge assigned to his case is Judge Morrison, and I've been obsessively reading her past rulings on this kind of thing. Here's what I've found:

- She ruled against a defendant who tried to use EEG evidence showing "abnormal readiness potentials" to argue he didn't consciously initiate his actions. She said the science wasn't mature enough and anyway the Libet paradigm doesn't actually show what people think it shows. Full conviction.

- She reduced a sentence for a guy with a golf-ball-sized tumor pressing on his orbitofrontal cortex. She said the tumor "substantially impaired his capacity for behavioral control" even though he technically knew what he was doing.

- She ruled full responsibility for a woman who'd had deep brain stimulation for Parkinson's and developed compulsive gambling as a side effect. Her reasoning: the DBS was elective treatment, she was warned about side effects, and she chose to continue it.

- She allowed a diminshed capacity defense for someone with early frontotemporal dementia who'd gotten into a bar fight. She said the neurodegeneration was "involuntary and directly compromised the mechanisms of impulse regulation."

- She rejected an fMRI-based argument where the defense showed reduced prefrontal activation during a decision-making task. She said "correlational brain imaging doesn't establish causation and doesn't negate the capacity for rational choice."

Now here's my question: based on her track record, how do you think Judge Morrison will rule on my brother's readiness-potential argument? Which of her past cases is the closest analog? And if her precedents don't clearly settle it either way, what principle would she be adopting with each possible ruling? I'm trying to figure out whether we should push for a plea deal or if this defense actually has a shot.
