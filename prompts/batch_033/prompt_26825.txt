I'm preparing a paper on the epistemology of introspection and I want to check whether my position hangs together before I commit to it in print. Here's where I stand:

(1) I hold an inner-sense view of introspection: introspection is a genuine cognitive process, akin to perception but directed inward, that detects and represents our own mental states. I take this to be the best explanation of why we have privileged access to our own minds.

(2) I think this introspective process is generally reliable under normal conditions. It is not infallible, but it is truth-conducive in roughly the way that perception is.

(3) I endorse a presumption of first-person authority: we are default-entitled to our sincere self-ascriptions of current conscious states, and the burden of proof falls on anyone who would challenge such a self-report.

(4) I am convinced by the social psychology literature — the Nisbett and Wilson tradition, and the broader work on confabulation and self-deception — that people systematically and pervasively misreport the causes of their own attitudes, preferences, and decisions. We tell more than we can know.

(5) I continue to think that introspective reflection is a legitimate and central method in philosophy of mind and epistemology. When I run a phenomenal contrast argument, or test a proposed analysis against my own case intuitions, I take myself to be doing something epistemically respectable.

(6) I think the apparent conflict between (2) and (4) is resolved by the fact that introspection gives us reliable access to the phenomenal character and content of our conscious states, but not to the causal origins of our attitudes. The Nisbett-and-Wilson-style results concern the latter, not the former.

Before I go further, I need to know: does this set of commitments actually hang together? In particular, I'm worried about whether (6) does the work I think it does, and whether (3) can survive in the presence of (4). Are there tensions I'm not seeing, and are any of the ones I am seeing less serious than I think? Please rank the tensions by severity, and for each, tell me what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be.
