I've been running a little philosophy reading group at my local library since I retired (I was a physical chemist, so I'm still getting my sea legs with all this). Next month we're tackling the Ship of Theseus, and I want to put together a decision tree that sorts people into the main positions on the puzzle without any of the jargon. The idea is each person answers a series of concrete cases, and by the end they land on whichever view their answers actually commit them to, regardless of which label they showed up liking.

I want the tree to cover at least these positions: the continuously repaired ship is the real ship (spatiotemporal continuity), the reassembled ship is the real ship (the original parts matter), both are the ship (so identity isn't transitive or there were two ships all along), and the question has no determinate answer (conventionalism or anti-realism). If other positions naturally fall out of the logical space, include them, and flag any combinations of answers that nobody seems to defend, with a sentence on why — is the view incoherent, just unattractive, or unexplored?

The questions should be about cases, not labels. No question should be "do you think identity is a matter of spatiotemporal continuity?" Instead, give me cases like: a bicycle where parts get replaced one at a time over years, a club that disbands and reforms, a river that changes course, a human being undergoing gradual cell turnover, a building taken apart brick by brick and rebuilt on a different site. The cases should pull apart the intuitions the big positions rely on. And please make the first question a really easy one — like, replace one plank of a ship, is it the same ship? — so someone who's never thought about any of this before can get their footing.

For the deliverable: no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables. Just continuous prose, the way you'd write an email. Walk me through the tree node by node, say what each question is and what the branches mean, and end with the leaves and what each position is stuck with — every view has to swallow something awkward, and I want to know what that is for each one.
