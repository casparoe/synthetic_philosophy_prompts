In the absent-minded driver problem, a driver must take the second of two indistinguishable exits. Exiting at the first exit yields payoff 0, exiting at the second yields payoff 4, and continuing past the second exit yields payoff 1. The driver cannot remember which exit he is at, and the ex ante optimal strategy is to exit with probability one-third at each information set. I want to understand why this is not dynamically inconsistent. When the driver is actually at an intersection, the only epistemic state he can be in is that of seeing an intersection and knowing nothing else. Should he not then assign probability one-half to being at the first exit and one-half to being at the second? If so, exiting has expected payoff 2, while continuing has expected payoff 1 under the usual reading, so the action-stage calculation appears to recommend exiting outright rather than exiting with probability one-third. If no new information arrives between the planning stage and the action stage, how can the same agent rationally adopt a plan he would not confirm at the moment of acting? In particular, what exactly is wrong with the equal posterior probability of one-half?
