I've been thinking about the idea of moral uncertainty — roughly, the view that just as I ought to take empirical uncertainty into account when deciding what to do, I should also take uncertainty about which moral theory is correct into account. Even if I am fairly confident that, say, a consequentialist framework is right, I cannot be completely sure that Kantianism or contractualism or virtue ethics is not the true moral theory. The question that interests me is not whether this idea is philosophically defensible — I have read enough to find the basic case plausible — but rather what it actually demands of me in practice.

Specifically, I would like to know what the most important practical implications of taking moral uncertainty seriously would be for an ordinary person making everyday decisions. I am thinking about things like dietary choices, career plans, charitable giving, how I treat friends and family, and political involvement. I am especially interested in what happens when one tries to move from the abstract idea to concrete guidance. For instance: how does one compare degrees of wrongness across theories that do not share a common currency of value, so that one can meaningfully weigh the possibility that an action is deeply wrong under one theory against the possibility that it is merely suboptimal under another? Does the attempt to take every live moral theory into account lead to a kind of practical paralysis — since for almost any action, there is probably some coherent moral view that condemns it — and if so, is there a principled way to restrict which theories one must take seriously? And what should one do about the fact that some moral theories treat certain considerations as absolute constraints rather than as considerations to be weighed, which seems to make expected choiceworthiness calculations break down?

I am not looking for a survey of the literature or a summary of what particular philosophers have said, but for your own best attempt to think through what moral uncertainty actually implies for someone trying to live a decent life while acknowledging that they might be wrong about what decency requires.
