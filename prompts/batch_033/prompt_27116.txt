I hold the view that early visual perception is cognitively impenetrable in the sense that the contents of conscious visual experience are not directly causally influenced by what the perceiver believes, desires, or knows. I take this to be an empirical claim about the architecture of the visual system, not a conceptual truth — it could turn out to be false, and I want to know what it would take for me to conclude that it is.

Tell me what would count against the impenetrability view. I want the evidence and arguments that should move someone who holds this position, sorted by how decisive they would be, and I want you to distinguish several layers:

First, what empirical findings would directly falsify the claim? Which experimental paradigms could in principle show cognitive penetration, and what would a clean demonstration look like? I want to know which existing studies are taken by penetrability-theorists to have already done this, and whether an impenetrability-theorist can honestly dismiss them — and if so, on what grounds.

Second, what conceptual or architectural considerations would undermine the position even if no single experiment were decisive? For instance, if the distinction between perception and cognition turns out to be impossible to draw in a principled way, or if the modularity framework that the impenetrability claim depends on collapses for independent reasons, does the claim lose its content? Tell me whether the impenetrability view is committed to a specific architecture of mind, and if that architecture were abandoned, whether the view would be refuted or merely rendered irrelevant.

Third, what would a successful positive case for cognitive penetrability need to look like, beyond poking holes in the negative case? If impenetrability is a default that gets the benefit of the doubt, what shifts the burden? I want to know whether the impenetrability view itself makes predictions that its rival does not — predictions whose failure would count against it — and whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability by adding ever more epicycles about what counts as "early" or "direct" or "visual."

Finally, rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Which of them would a committed impenetrability-theorist themselves concede as counting against the view, and which would they dismiss as question-begging or as attacks on a straw man? I want an honest assessment of how exposed the position actually is, and if it turns out to be well insulated, whether that insulation is a problem for a view of this kind.
