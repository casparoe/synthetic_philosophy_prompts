Suppose I'm helping a small funder decide where to put about five years of money into research on fish and crustacean welfare in aquaculture. The board keeps going in circles about what the actual bottleneck is. One camp says the open question that matters most is whether fish are conscious in the way that makes pain morally urgent at all — if their nociceptive responses are more like reflexes, a lot of the welfare agenda is built on sand. Another camp says fish are the easy part, and the real uncertainty is crustaceans: farmed shrimp and prawns outnumber fish by a lot, and the evidence for sentience there is much murkier, so the stakes of getting the boundry wrong are huge. A third camp says the conceptual work is close to irrelevant for near-term policy, because the biggest gains are operational — making electrical stunning work at slaughter speeds, figuring out whether lower stocking densities actually improve welfare on real farms, getting water quality monitoring good enough to catch slow suffocation events. And a fourth camp says even if you settle all of that, you still have no agreed way to weigh a vast number of short, low-intensity lives against a smaller number of longer, more intense ones, so the weighting question is the real crux.

If you were advising this funder, which two or three of these questions (or ones I haven't listed) should they prioritize, and in what order? I'm less interested in the list than in the reasoning: for each, why it's upstream of the other work, how tractable it is for the kind of people the funder can actually support, whether it's neglected, and what concrete evidence over the next few years would push it up or down the ranking. I'm not after a survey of the field — I want to know what deserves the money and why.
