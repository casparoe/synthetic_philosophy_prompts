In a seminar on existentialism, a classmate repeatedly invoked the Sartrean dictum that we are "condemned to be free" as though it settled every dispute. To a student who said she felt trapped by her upbringing, to another who argued that poverty forecloses meaningful options, and to a third who asked whether compulsions or unconscious drives constrain choice, the reply was always some version of "Sartre shows you're condemned to be free, so what you're describing is just bad faith." I can feel that this slogan is being asked to bear more weight than it can, but I lack the distinctions to push back precisely. What are the distinct claims "condemned to be free" could be asserting — ranging from the relatively modest to the most radical — and which of those readings would actually license dismissing the three objections my classmate encountered? I want a menu of readings with, for each: what the reading says, whether it is independently plausible, and whether it justifies the move my classmate was making.
