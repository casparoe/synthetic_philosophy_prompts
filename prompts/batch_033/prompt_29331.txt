I'm a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, and I'm putting together a handout for a small reading group on diachronic rationality and money pumps. The angle I care about is practical rather than exegetical: whether a predictably time-inconsistent agent—a hyperbolic discounter, or someone in a Ulysses-and-the-Sirens situation—can count as rational through precommitment or by sticking to a plan, and whether a future self is best treated as a separate agent. Please write a decision tree, or a taxonomy of positions if that fits better, on the question: what, if anything, is wrong with an agent whose choices can be turned into a money pump?

Build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of names. Each branch node should be a diagnostic question that a reader can answer without already knowing which position it leads to—for instance: whether a rational agent may have cyclic or predictably time-inconsistent preferences; whether the correct response to a foreseen preference reversal is to constrain one's future options now, to bind oneself to a plan and then stick to it, or to bargain with one's future self; and whether rationality attaches to whole agents across time or only to time slices. Use two running cases: the classic smaller-sooner/larger-later reversal (on Sunday the agent prefers the larger later reward, on Tuesday the smaller sooner one) and a precommitment case where the agent can now remove a tempting option that a later self will otherwise take.

The leaves should include at least these: the coherence view (cyclic or time-inconsistent preferences are simply irrational), sophisticated choice, resolute choice, naive choice, the multiple-selves view, and time-slice rationality; add further leaves if the space demands them. For every unoccupied combination of answers, note briefly whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any two leaves the tree cannot separate. Aim for a five-minute self-sorting instrument: roughly three to five branch questions and six to nine leaves, each leaf labeled and followed by one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
