So I've been going down this rabbit hole on pain asymbolia lately — my mom has chronic pain from fibromyalgia and I started reading about pain to understand what she's going through, and somehow I ended up in this weird corner of the literature about people who can feel pain but don't mind it. Like, at all. They'll describe the sensation accurately (burning, stabbing, whatever) and even rate its intensity normally on a scale, but they don't find it unpleasant, don't withdraw from it, don't want it to stop. And I've become pretty convinced that this shows something important: that the badness of pain isn't built into the pain experience itself. It's a separate thing — an evaluative or motivational layer that normally comes along with pain but can in principle come apart from it. So pain isn't essentially bad; it's contingently bad because of how our brains happen to wire the sensory and the affective together. The sensation itself is just information; the "ouch" is added downstream.

I know this position has its critics though, and honestly I want to stress-test it before I get too attached. I'm not a philosopher — I'm a cognitive science undergrad — so I might be missing obvious problems, or the kinds of problems that are obvious to people who do philosophy of mind. What I want is: what would count against this view? I mean, what observations, what experimental results, what conceptual arguments, what alternative interpretations of the asymbolia data would genuinly undermine the claim that pain's badness is separable and not intrinsic to the pain experience? I want the defeaters ranked from "this would basically kill the view" to "this would be awkward but survivable." And I'd especially like to know which of these a defender of the view would themselves concede as counting against them (as opposed to things only an external critic would bring up). I'm not looking for a survey of the literature — I want a map of where the view is exposed.

Give me exactly seven items, each a sentence or two describing the defeater and how serious it is, ranked from most damaging to least, with no introduction or conclusion.
