I've been working through Hofstadter and Sander's argument that analogy-making is the core of all cognition — that every act of thought, from categorizing a chair to proving a theorem, is fundamentally an act of mapping new situations onto familiar ones via analogy. But I keep coming back to what seems like a self-reference problem. If all thinking is analogy-making, then the claim "all thinking is analogy-making" must itself be an analogy. And analogies, as they emphasize throughout the book, are never perfect; every analogy has limits and eventually breaks down. So their theory appears to predict its own inadequacy. Is this a genuine problem for the view, or is there a way out? I'd like a committed response that takes a definite position rather than just laying out options.
