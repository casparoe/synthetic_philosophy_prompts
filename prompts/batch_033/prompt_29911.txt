I've been trying to work out what I actually think about free will by trusting my reactions to particular cases more than the labels compatibilist and incompatibilist, and I want to know whether there is one principle behind my reactions. If I choose between two part-time jobs after weighing the pros and cons, I think I act freely and can be praised or blamed for the choice. If someone holds a gun to my head and demands my phone, and I hand it over, I don't think I'm responsible for handing it over. If a hypnotist plants a suggestion that makes me stand up and sing whenever I hear a bell, and I do it, I don't think I'm responsible. If a brain tumor makes someone suddenly violent, I don't hold them responsible for the violence. But here is where I get stuck. Suppose a neuroscientist secretly implants a device that, moment by moment, causes me to reason my way to stealing from a store, while the whole process feels like my own ordinary deliberation; my gut says I am not responsible. Yet in another case, someone has rigged my brain so that if I were about to vote against a certain candidate, the device would fire and force me to vote for that candidate, but I vote for the candidate on my own and the device never fires. There I think I am responsible, even though I could not have voted otherwise. I also think a person in the grip of a severe addiction who steals to get drugs is responsible, but less responsible than the ordinary chooser. And I have one more reaction that seems to clash with all this: if determinism turned out to be true, and every choice were fixed by the past plus the laws of physics, I feel that nobody would ever be truly free or responsible, even though in the rigged-vote case I just said a person was responsible despite being unable to do otherwise. Is there a single principle about free will and responsibility that captures all or most of these verdicts? What would that principle say about a case I have not listed, such as someone raised from birth in an isolated cult who commits a crime as an adult? And which of my verdicts, if any, would the principle force me to give up? Finally, would the principle that best fits me make me a compatibilist or an incompatibilist?
