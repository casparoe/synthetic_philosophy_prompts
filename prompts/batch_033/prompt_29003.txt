Assume Karl Marx could be given an accurate, non-ideological briefing on the following five features of contemporary machine learning and its economic deployment, but on nothing else that came after his death. Using only commitments he actually defends in his own writings on machinery, value, and labor — chiefly the analysis of machinery in Capital, Volume I, and the treatment of automation and accumulated social knowledge in the Grundrisse — predict the position he would take on each development. For each item, state the prediction and the specific Marxian commitment or argument that licenses it; where the Grundrisse and Capital point in different directions, say which he would have to prioritize and why, and where neither yields a determinate answer, flag that instead of inventing one. Do not quote and do not cite page numbers; the justification must be internal to the position. Format: exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.

The five developments:

1. Large language models substituting for skilled mental labor in translation, coding, drafting, and similar work.
2. Algorithmic management of nominally independent gig workers through platform apps that set prices, assign tasks, and monitor output.
3. The capture of publicly produced text, images, and code as privately owned training data, enforced through licenses and copyright.
4. Proposals for an unconditional basic income justified by the claim that automation has made work scarce.
5. The enormous fixed-capital investment in data centers and specialized chips required to train and serve models, and its likely effect on the rate of profit.
