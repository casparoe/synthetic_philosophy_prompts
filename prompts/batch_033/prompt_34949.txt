I'm a novelist, mid-career, and I'm deep in revisions on a political novel set in a near-future where a government agency launders its worst decisions through deliberately soft, bureaucratic language. The problem is I keep worry that my own prose is doing the same thing on the page. I've been rereading Orwells "Politics and the English Language," and the thing that sticks with me is his claim that sloppy, inflated, euphemistic language isn't just an aesthetic failing — its how people and institutions make terrible things sound neutral or even respectable without noticing their doing it. I don't want my narrator to be complicit in the exact thing the book is supposed to be exposing.

So here's what I'd like: a revision procedure I can actually run on my manuscript, page by page, maybe fifteen minutes per scene. I'm not a philosopher and I don't have time to stop and theorize mid-draft. I want a checklist or a sequence of tests, grounded in Orwell's views about language and political thought, that helps me catch the places where a sentence is drifting into euphemism, dead metaphor, passive constructions that hide who did what, or abstraction that lets the reader (and me) off the hook. It should say what counts as a fail at each step, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it has to hand the decision back to me because it's a matter of voice or art rather than evasion. Then run it on three short passages of the kind I actually write: (1) a sentence about a police killing that I'm tempted to render as "an incident occurred"; (2) a bit of political speech in dialogue that uses words like "pacification" and "collateral damage"; and (3) a paragraph of plain, concrete, honest prose that the procedure might flag anyway — I want to see it give me an answer I won't like. And be honest about what the procedure can't catch: Orwell's rules were written for essays and journalism, and a novel has characters who should talk badly, narrators who should sometimes be unreliable, and prose that's allowed to be strange. Tell me where the procedure will misfire and what I'll still have to decide as a writer.
