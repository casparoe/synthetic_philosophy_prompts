Before I read another paper on health and disease, I want the spec sheet first. What does an adequate theory of disease actually have to get right, and which of the usual demands are just naturalist or normativist camp preferences rather than real constraints? Give me the cases it has to handle — asymptomatic hypertension, an infection the person never notices, infertility in someone who dont want kids, addiction, chronic pain with no finding, conditions that got declassified like homosexuality — and the distinctions it can't collapse: having a condition vs it being bad for you, disease vs illness, risk factor vs disease. Include the statistical stuff it has to respect too, like the reference-class problem for any view that says disease is just subnormal functioning. For each requirement, say what case or consideration makes it a requirement and not just a taste. Then do the part I actually care about: are any two of these desiderata jointly unsatisfiable, and which single criterion does the most to kill the standard views (biostatistical naturalism included)? The scorecard can be a table, but keep the weight on the criteria themselves.
