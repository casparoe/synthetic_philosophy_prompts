I've been thinking about something for a while and I'd like to test it properly. Here's the idea: a person couldn't have come from a different sperm and a different egg than the ones they actually came from. If my parents had conceived a month later, or even a minute later, that child wouldn't have been me — it would have been a sibling, someone else entirely. My particular origin — that exact sperm from my father and that exact egg from my mother — is essential to being me. If you try to imagine a world where I exist but came from different gametes, you're not really imagining me; you're imagining someone else.

I arrived at this on my own, and I later learned that some philosophers call it "origin essentialism." I find it intuitively compelling. It seems to capture something real about what makes each of us the particular individual we are. But I want to know how exposed this view really is. I don't want to just collect arguments that confirm it; I want to know what would count against it.

So my question is: what would have to be true — or what would we have to discover — for origin essentialism to be wrong? I'm not asking for a list of objections from the literature. I'm asking for a map of the view's vulnerabilities. What kinds of cases, thought experiments, or consequences would put real pressure on it? Which ones would be fatal and which merely awkward? Are there things we already know (from biology, from logic, from ordinary ways we talk about identity) that should already make me nervous about the view? And is the view structured in such a way that it can't really be falsified — has it been qualified into a corner where nothing could count against it? If so, is that a problem, or is that just how metaphysical views of this sort work?

I'd also be curious to hear: what would a committed origin essentialist themselves concede counts against their position, even if they ultimately think the view survives it? I want to separate the defeats that even defenders take seriously from the ones they dismiss as misunderstandings.

Please structure your answer so that I come away with a clear sense of what the view risks and what it doesn't, ranked from the most serious challenges to the least. And at the very end, please give me a one-sentence summary of your overall assessment, enclosed as \answer{...}.
