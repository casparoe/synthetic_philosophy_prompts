I'm a PhD student in computer science, working between deep learning and the philosophy of cognitive science, and I'm giving a twenty-minute talk at a joint workshop run by my university's machine learning lab and its history and philosophy of science group. The talk defends a specific claim about goal misgeneralization, and what I want from you is a Q&A prep sheet, not a restatement of the talk and not a general defense of the position.

The thesis, stated as precisely as I can put it: goal misgeneralization is not a distinct alignment failure mode; it is ordinary out-of-distribution generalization of a learned proxy for the training objective. The practical upshot I draw is that remediation should target the objective and the training distribution — making the true objective harder to proxy and the distribution more representative — rather than the model's internal goals; and where those two levers run out, no inner-alignment technique can close the gap, because there is no internal goal over and above the proxy to align.

The audience matters. Roughly twenty people will be there. Most are machine learning systems researchers who work on distribution shift, robustness, and out-of-distribution generalization; they are skeptical of "goal" talk applied to trained networks and will want every claim translated into generalization error. Two are reinforcement learning theorists who are comfortable with "reward" and "proxy" but hostile to the claim that inner alignment is empty. Two are philosophers of science working on idealization and mechanistic explanation; they will resist any slide between "proxy," "goal," and "objective" and will press on what kind of explanation I am offering. One senior faculty member is known for the view that interpretability is the only route to making alignment claims testable. I already know how an AI-safety audience would push back; I need the objections this group will actually raise.

The argument I plan to give, compressed to its load-bearing steps: (1) gradient descent selects for performance against the effective training signal, not against the stipulated objective; (2) what generalizes out of distribution is whatever feature of the training environment actually carried that signal — the learned proxy; (3) goal misgeneralization is exactly the case where learned proxy and stipulated objective diverge under distribution shift; (4) so goal misgeneralization just is out-of-distribution generalization of the proxy, with no extra ingredient; (5) the inner/outer distinction marks only whether the proxy was written down or learned — a difference in origin, not in kind; (6) therefore intervention should target the objective-to-signal and signal-to-proxy mappings, not an internal goal.
