Write a dialogue between an expressivist and a moral cognitivist who meet at a philosophy conference. The cognitivist has just presented a paper arguing that the Frege–Geach problem is fatal to expressivism: the expressivist cannot explain how moral sentences combine with ordinary logical operators in embedded contexts (conditionals, negation, disjunction) without treating them as if they express propositions, which would collapse expressivism into cognitivism. The expressivist finds the cognitivist at the bar afterward, and they hash it out.

The dialogue should involve genuine back-and-forth. Each character should respond to the other's specific points rather than talking past them, and neither side should be a strawman — both should be given their strongest arguments. The cognitivist should press the embedding problem with concrete examples (e.g., modus ponens arguments containing moral premises, the validity of inferences like "If lying is wrong, then getting your brother to lie is wrong; lying is wrong; therefore getting your brother to lie is wrong"). The expressivist should offer substantive replies, such as an account of how logical operators map attitudes onto more complex attitudes, or a quasi-realist strategy for earning the right to talk as if moral sentences express propositions. The dialogue should also address whether the expressivist's reply ends up being a notational variant of cognitivism, and if so, whether that is a problem for expressivism or for the way the cognitivist framed the challenge. By the end of the dialogue, the two characters should have clarified exactly what each would need to concede for the other's view to prevail.
