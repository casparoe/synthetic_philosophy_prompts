I keep meeting the frame problem in two guises, and I want to stop evaluating proposed solutions until I have a clear specification of what a solution has to do. One guise is representational: how a formalism should say what changes and what persists when an action occurs, without writing down every non-change by hand. The other is epistemological: how a finite agent should determine which of indefinitely many true facts are relevant to what it is about to do or predict. Please write the desiderata for a theory or formalism that claims to solve the frame problem, with the argument or case behind each requirement, and mark each as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp. Address at least the following, and say explicitly if you think one of them is not a genuine requirement.

- Default persistence: a system must be able to conclude that unmentioned facts persist across an event or action without being told so explicitly, and without enumerating frame axioms for every action-fluent pair.
- The Yale shooting problem and its relatives: the system must select the intended model in which the gun stays loaded through a delay and the shot kills, while excluding the anomalous model in which the gun unloads itself during the delay and the victim survives; say which requirement does the excluding.
- Defeasibility: the system must be able to withdraw earlier conclusions when new information arrives, and must say what it is about the later information that defeats a default rather than merely contradicting it.
- Ramifications and qualifications: indirect effects and implicit preconditions must be handled without either missing the indirect effect or sliding back into exhaustive enumeration.
- Relevance without enumeration: the system must determine which true facts bear on an action or query without an oracle and without searching through everything that happens to be true.
- Tractability: the proposal must be usable in principle by a bounded agent or a real program, not merely correct in the limit.
- The distinction between the two guises: the solution must not conflate the logical task of representing what persists with the epistemological task of knowing what matters, even if it addresses both.

Then identify which pairs or triples of these requirements are jointly unsatisfiable, or nearly so. For example, does default persistence combined with complete temporal projection force multiple extensions, and does relevance combined with tractability admit any honest solution? This is the part I most want: the incompatibilities, not a forced synthesis. Finally, provide a short scorecard of how situation calculus with successor-state axioms, circumscription, default logic, and the event calculus fare against the list, and say which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidates.
