Here's the question:

Arrow's theorem is often summarized as saying that no fair voting rule exists. Gibbard and Satterthwaite proved a related result about strategy-proofness. State what the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem says, explain how it relates to Arrow's theorem, and whether it implies that every voting method for three or more candidates is manipulable. If any methods escape the theorem's conclusion, identify them and explain why.

The student wrote:

Arrow showed that any social welfare function with three or more alternatives, unrestricted domain, Pareto efficiency, and independence of irrelevant alternatives has to be dictatorial. Gibbard–Satterthwaite is the strategic analogue: any strategy-proof social choice function with three or more alternatives is dictatorial, assuming it's onto. So among ranked methods, every non-dictatorial rule is manipulable. The two theorems are closely related — G-S is basically the single-winner, incentive-based version of Arrow's result. In practice, then, any voting method with three or more candidates is either dictatorial or manipulable. The only real exceptions are two-candidate elections, where majority rule is strategy-proof, and approval voting, which doesn't ask for a ranking and is strategy-proof, so it slips through the theorem's assumptions. So the upshot is pretty general: for any realistic multi-candidate election, you can't have both non-dictatorship and strategy-proofness.
