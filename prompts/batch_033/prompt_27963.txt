You are the facilitator of an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers who have been arguing for years about Bas van Fraassen's Reflection principle. One defends the view that Reflection is a genuine requirement of rationality (when properly qualified to exclude cases of anticipated evidence loss or cognitive impairment). The other holds that Reflection is at best a useful heuristic — that rational agents can permissibly violate it in a range of cases that no plausible qualification can exclude without rendering the principle empty.

Your task is to draft the joint statement they would produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The document should include the following sections:

1. **Agreed claims.** A numbered list of claims both parties can sign. These should be substantive — not just truisms about what the words mean — and should cover the formal statement of the principle, its initial motivation, the canonical counterexamples (e.g., cases involving foreseen memory loss, drugs that impair reasoning, or Ulysses-and-the-Sirens scenarios), and any qualifications both sides agree are necessary before the principle is even a candidate for a rational requirement.

2. **Disagreements table.** For each point of remaining disagreement, give:
   - The issue stated in neutral terms.
   - The defender's position, phrased so the defender would accept the wording.
   - The critic's position, phrased so the critic would accept the wording.

3. **Cruxes.** The sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, specify a concrete case or argument that both sides agree bears on it, and record what each side predicts about that case — stated in advance, so that the predictions are falsifiable.

4. **What would change each side's mind.** For each party, a frank statement of which argument, case, or consideration would lead them to abandon or substantially revise their position, and where they think their own reasoning is most vulnerable to error.

Throughout, separate disagreements that are genuine from ones that turned out to be merely verbal. If the collaboration revealed that some portion of the dispute rests on different understandings of "rational requirement," say so explicitly and specify exactly how each side uses the term. If there are formulations that both sides now regard as misleading or question-begging, note those as well and explain why they were set aside.
