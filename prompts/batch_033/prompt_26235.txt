I've been chewing on the Born rule problem in many-worlds for a while now and I've basically settled on the view that it's a genuine unsolved problem, not just a confusion. The way I see it: the Schrödinger equation gives you this branching structure with amplitudes attached to each branch, but amplitudes aren't probabilities — they're complex numbers that show up in the wavefunction. To say you should expect to find yourself in a branch with probability proportional to |amplitude|² is to add something that isn't in the bare dynamics. The Deutsch-Wallace decision-theory stuff and the Sebens-Carroll epistemic separability principle both feel to me like they're building in what they're trying to derive.

What I want to know is: what would actually count against this position? Not the standard replies (I've read the papers), but a map of what would make me wrong. What kind of result — a theorem, a new physical principle, an experimental outcome — would show that the Born rule really does fall out of the formalism without extra assumptions? And conversely, what would it take to show that my worry is just confused, that I'm asking for something that doesn't make sense? I also want to know whether my position is even risky — am I making a claim that could turn out false, or have I just defined "smuggling in" in a way that guarantees I'll never be satisfied? If it's unfalsifiable, is that a problem here, or is that just how philosophy of physics works?
