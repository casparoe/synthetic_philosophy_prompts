Two members of a logic seminar have spent the term arguing about the Liar paradox and have now agreed to a good-faith adversarial collaboration. One holds a paracomplete view: the Liar sentence, "This sentence is false," is neither true nor false, and classical logic errs by assuming that truth and falsity are exhaustive. The other holds a dialetheist view: the same sentence is both true and false, and classical logic errs by assuming that truth and falsity are exclusive. Write the joint statement the two of them should produce.

The statement should open with a numbered list of claims both parties can sign in the wording given. These claims should cover at least: what the Liar shows about the unrestricted T-schema together with classical logic; the ordinary truth and falsity ascriptions that both theories preserve; the strengthened Liar ("This sentence is not true") and Curry's paradox as constraints that any adequate theory must address; and the predictions on which the two theories coincide, including their shared refusal to classify the Liar as simply true or simply false.

Then provide a disagreements table with three columns: the issue; the paracomplete formulation, phrased so the paracomplete party would accept it as its own position; and the dialetheist formulation, phrased so the dialetheist would accept it. Rows should include at least: the status of the Liar itself; whether truth and falsity are exhaustive; whether truth and falsity are exclusive; which classical principle each side rejects; and what the strengthened Liar shows.

Next, identify the cruxes: the sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one side. For each crux, give the sentence, case, or argument that both parties accept as bearing on it, and record in advance each party's prediction about what it will show. Include at least one crux about independent examples, outside the paradoxes, of truth-value gaps or of true contradictions; one about whether the paracomplete view can handle the strengthened Liar without collapsing into dialetheism or resorting to inexpressibility; and one about whether a paraconsistent logic, with explosion removed, can preserve enough ordinary reasoning for mathematics and everyday inference.

End with a brief section identifying any part of the dispute that, after the collaboration, both parties regard as merely verbal — for instance, a divergence in what "false" or "not true" is being used to mean — and state the distinct readings that made it appear substantive.
