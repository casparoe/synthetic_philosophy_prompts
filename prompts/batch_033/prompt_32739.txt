I have been trying to work through the scholarship on Kepler's relationship to astrology, and the debate seems to have stalled in a way I find unsatisfying. One camp reads him as a sincere reformer who rejected the zodiac signs, houses, and other inherited devices as arbitrary but defended planetary aspects because he took them to pick out real geometrical and harmonic relations that could be studied for their influence on earthly events. Another camp reads him as a modern astronomer who practiced astrology mainly for livelihood and whose defenses of it are best read as either ironic or carefully circumscribed attempts to demote the discipline while continuing to use it. Both camps cite the same passages and accept the same basic biographical facts, yet neither moves. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not really about what Kepler believed but about what should count as evidence of a historical figure's belief — whether published treatise, private correspondence, lived practice, or occasional dismissive remark should carry the weight. Diagnose this impasse: is it a substantive historical disagreement, a disagreement about standards of evidence, or two different questions being asked under the name Kepler and astrology? Give the strongest version of each side's implicit criterion, and say what would actually count as progress.
