I am trying to work out my own views on political freedom and would like an audit of whether the following seven commitments are jointly tenable. I am not sure where the problems are, and I would rather not signal which I am most attached to.

(1) The core of political freedom is negative liberty: freedom as the absence of deliberate interference by other persons.

(2) Positive liberty — freedom as self-mastery or self-realisation — picks out a genuine and important political value that is not reducible to negative liberty.

(3) The republican conception of freedom as non-domination (freedom from arbitrary power, not merely from actual interference) identifies a real dimension of unfreedom that the negative-liberty tradition systematically overlooks.

(4) The state should be neutral between rival conceptions of the good life; it should not justify its actions by appeal to the superiority of any particular vision of human flourishing.

(5) A slave with a benevolent master who never actually interferes is nonetheless unfree, and this is a fact about the slave's political freedom, not merely about their security or dignity.

(6) Coercive state action always reduces the freedom of the person coerced, even when it is undertaken to prevent domination by others.

(7) The concept of freedom is unified enough that these various claims — about negative liberty, positive liberty, and republican liberty — are claims about the same thing, not merely different things that happen to share the word 'freedom'.

Please go through these pairwise and identify which are in genuine tension, which are only apparently in tension, and which are fully compatible. For each genuine tension, rank it by severity, explain precisely what would have to give to resolve it, and estimate the philosophical cost of each revision. Then tell me which single revision to my set of commitments would resolve the most tension at the lowest cost.
