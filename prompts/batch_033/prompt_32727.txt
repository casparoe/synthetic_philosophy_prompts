A friend of mine who took a political philosophy course described luck egalitarianism to me roughly as follows:

"Luck egalitarians hold that inequalities are unjust only when they result from brute luck — things like your genetic endowments, your family's wealth, or accidents that weren't your fault. But if you end up worse off because of choices you freely made — what they call 'option luck' — then the resulting inequality is perfectly just. You chose to take the risk, so you own the consequences. The state has no business redistributing to help you. That's why the theory faces the 'harshness objection': it tells us to abandon people who make bad choices, even when they end up destitute. And since any plausible theory of justice has to protect the vulnerable regardless of how they got there, luck egalitarianism is basically a non-starter."
