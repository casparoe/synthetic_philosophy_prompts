(1) The core of the argument is right: there can't be a language whose words refer to something only the speaker has access to, where in principle no one else could ever learn or check the use of the words.

(5) Someone could have a genuinely novel sensation that no one else has ever had, and could coin a word for their own use to keep track of whether the same feeling comes back.

Which of these are actually in tension with each other, and which just look like they are once you draw the right distinctions? For the real tensions, rank them by severity and say what I'd have to give up and what it costs. Then tell me the single cheapest revision that leaves the overall position most defensible.
