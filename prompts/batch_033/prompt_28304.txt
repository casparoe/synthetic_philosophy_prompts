Construct a thought experiment in which procreative beneficence — the principle that parents should select, among the possible children they could have, the one whose life can be expected to go best — and liberal eugenics — the view that parents may use genetic and reproductive technologies to shape their children's traits, constrained chiefly by the child's welfare and autonomy rather than by a state-imposed conception of a good life — diverge in the direction that is harder to produce. Specifically, the liberal eugenicist should judge impermissible a reproductive choice that procreative beneficence would require, rather than the more familiar case in which procreative beneficence demands a selection and liberal eugenics merely says the parents are free to decline it. The case should involve a couple using preimplantation genetic diagnosis to choose between two viable embryos, and it should be one a real clinic could face. Constraints: the parents are fully informed and act from ordinary parental concern; neither embryo has a condition that makes its life less than worth living; the liberal eugenicist's objection must not be the stock expressivist objection that selecting against a condition devalues people who have it; and the objection must not depend on the parents' being bigoted, selfish, or otherwise bad people. It should fall out of a commitment a liberal eugenicist actually holds — for example, a limit on selecting a child's traits as a means to the parents' or a sibling's ends, or a limit drawn from the child's interest in an open future. The point of the exercise is to isolate a structural feature of the two views, so a case that simply stipulates the verdicts does not count. After presenting the scenario, walk through what procreative beneficence says and why, what the liberal eugenicist says and why, identify which feature of the case does the work in making liberal eugenics the stricter view, explain what happens to the disagreement if that feature is removed, and give the strongest reply a defender of procreative beneficence could make.
