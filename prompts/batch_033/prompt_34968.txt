I volunteer at a wildlife rehab center. We take in injured animals — mostly songbirds that hit windows, squirrels hit by cars, that kind of thing — patch them up, and release them. But I've been reading some stuff online and now I can't stop thinking about whether we're actually doing net harm. The post-release survival rate is awful: maybe 20% make it a year, the rest get eaten or starve. And even the "success" cases just go back to lives that are mostly hiding from predators and eventually dying painfully. I brought this up at a staff meeting and got blank stares. My supervisor says we're giving animals a second chance and that's inherently good. I'm not convinced but I also can't tell if I'm just rationalizing burnout. How should I think about whether this work is actually good?
