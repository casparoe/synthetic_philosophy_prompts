I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I keep noticing a pattern I can't name. Whenever I try to design a cognitive architecture — or even just think about how my own mind works — I catch myself falling into the same trap. I imagine a central module, a kind of dashboard, where sensory inputs, memories, and decisions all converge and are presented to... something. A CEO process. An inner screen. When I'm debugging my own thinking, I picture a little control room where "I" sit and watch the outputs of various subsystems scroll past.

I know this picture is wrong. I know that positing a central observer just pushes the problem back — who watches the watcher? — and that real minds don't have a single place where everything comes together. But I can't stop doing it. Even when I deliberately design a distributed, decentralised architecture, I find later that I've been testing it by imagining what it would "look like" from the inside, which smuggles the dashboard right back in.

A few instances of the pattern: (1) I was reading about the visual system and caught myself wondering where in the brain the "image" is displayed — as if the optic nerve were a video cable running to a tiny screen, and the real problem is finding the screen. (2) A colleague was describing a multi-agent system and kept saying "and then the agent reports its findings to the central controller" — and when I asked what the controller does with the reports, he described another agent in all but name, one that weighs evidence and decides, which is exactly the thing we were supposed to be explaining. (3) In meditation, I try to observe my thoughts, and I get the unmistakable sense that there's an observer behind my eyes. But when I turn attention toward the observer itself, I find... more thoughts. I never catch the observer, only the observed. The watcher is forever just around the next corner of attention.

Is there a name for this — the persistent, almost gravitational pull to model consciousness as a central theatre with a spectator, even when you know that model is bankrupt? I'm not looking for a bare label. I want to know what the nearest philosophical concepts are, how they carve up the phenomenon, whether what I'm describing fits any of them exactly or is a folk version of something more precise, and what the edges of the concept are. If it doesn't fit anything neatly, I'd want a clear definition and a term that distinguishes it from adjacent ideas.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. I'd appreciate the answer in British English.
