I keep noticing a pattern in philosophy discussions and I'm wondering if there's a name for it.

The pattern is: two philosophers disagree about whether some kind of thing "really exists." Could be numbers, could be holes, could be tables (vs. particles arranged table-wise), could be the average taxpayer. You listen to them go back and forth for a while, and eventually you realize: they actually agree on every single empirical fact. They agree on what particles are where, they agree on what mathematical systems are consistent, they agree on what you'd observe in any experiment. They even agree on what follows from the existence claim — like, if tables exist then such-and-such is true about perception or whatever. They just disagree on whether to *call* it "existing."

So you say: isn't this just a verbal dispute? And they get annoyed and insist no, it's a substantive ontological disagreement, the word "exists" is doing real work, it's not just a choice of vocabulary.

But here's what I can't figure out. Sometimes when people call something a verbal dispute, they mean "you're using words differently and once you disambiguate there's nothing left to argue about." That seems like it would apply here. But other times, people seem to use "verbal dispute" to mean something narrower — like, a dispute that turns on a straightforward ambguity (e.g., "bank" meaning river bank vs. financial institution). And these ontology cases don't feel like that; the disagreement persists even after everyone agrees on definitions.

So I guess my question is: is there an established term for this specific situation — a dispute where all the non-linguistic facts are agreed on, and all the downstream implications are agreed on, but the parties still insist the disagreement is substantive? How does it relate to the concept of a verbal dispute — is it a subspecies, a borderline case, or something that exposes a problem with the verbal/substantive distinction itself? And if there's no standard term, how would you carve this up? I'm less interested in getting a label than in understanding what exactly could make such a dispute count as "real" if nothing factual hangs on it.
