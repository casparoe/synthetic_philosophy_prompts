In a reading group on time travel, someone cut off a debate about the grandfather paradox by saying: "You can't change the past — so if you went back to kill your grandfather, you'd simply fail; that's the whole resolution." I think this is several different claims wearing one sentence, and I want them pulled apart. Lay out the distinct precise theses "you can't change the past" could be asserting — including what "change" would even mean when the time traveler is already part of the earlier period's history, whether the past is supposed to be fixed in a way the future is not, and whether the time traveler keeps ordinary local abilities while the global history stays consistent. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and then say which reading or readings, if any, actually support dissolving the grandfather paradox in the way the speaker used it — and which readings merely restate the paradox rather than resolving it. I want a menu of readings I can bring back to the group, with verdicts.
