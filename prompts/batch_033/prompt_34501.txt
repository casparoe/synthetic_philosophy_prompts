People in ML twitter keep saying that LLMs have "knowledge-that" without "knowledge-how" — like, GPT-4 can tell you everything about how to ride a bike but couldn't actually ride one, and this supposedly shows there's something fundamentally missing from its understanding, that it's just a stochastic parrot with declarative knowledge and no procedural grasp. They're leaning on Ryle's distinction between knowing-that and knowing-how to argue that scaling up text prediction can't get us to general intelligence.

But I'm not sure the analogy between what the LLM is doing and "knowledge-that" even holds up — like, is what the LLM has actually knowledge-that at all, in Ryle's sense, or is the whole thing a category error in the other direction? And even if the analogy holds, does the conclusion (LLMs can't be generally intelligent) follow?

Can you audit this: what maps onto what between the LLM case and the knowing-how/knowing-that distinction as Ryle drew it, where the analogy breaks, what it smuggles in, and whether the argument survives the disanalogies?
