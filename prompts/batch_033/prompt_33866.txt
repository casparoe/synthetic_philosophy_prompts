I'm a PhD student in moral philosophy and I've been tearing my hair out over this argument for about a month. It feels airtight but the conclusion is bananas. I'd love a careful diagnosis of where it goes wrong — or, if it doesn't, which premise I should bite the bullet and reject and what that costs.

(1) An action is wrong only if there is some particular person who is wronged by it. (No victim, no wrong.)

(2) A person is wronged by an action only if the action makes them worse off than they would have been if the action hadn't been performed.

(3) Consider a policy choice that affects the far future — say, whether to burn through our remaining fossil fuels as fast as possible ("burn") or transition to clean energy at significant short-term cost ("transition"). The lives people will have two centuries from now are dramatically better under transition than under burn.

(4) But here's the thing: the identity of the people who will exist two centuries from now depends on fine-grained details of what we do today. Different policy, different people meeting, different conceptions, totally different population. The people who exist under burn wouldn't exist under transition, and vice versa.

(5) So the people who live under burn are not made worse off by our choice of burn — because if we'd chosen transition, they wouldn't have existed at all. And their lives under burn, while much worse than they could have been, are still worth living.

(6) Therefore, by (2), the burn policy wrongs no one.

(7) Therefore, by (1), the burn policy is not wrong.

(8) But that's absurd. The burn policy is clearly wrong. And the reasoning generalizes: almost any long-term policy choice is identity-affecting, so if (1) and (2) are true, we have essentially no moral obligations to future people as long as we don't make their lives not worth living.

I'm most reluctant to give up (1) — the idea that wrongs need victims feels bedrock. But (2) also seems like the standard account of harm. And (4) is just a fact about how identity works. So where's the mistake? And what do I have to give up if I reject whichever premise you think is the culprit?
