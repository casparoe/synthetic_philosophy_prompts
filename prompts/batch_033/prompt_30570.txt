Me and my friend Dana argue about time travel every time a movie or show uses it, and by now I can mostly call what she's gonna say. Here's the running list of her verdicts:

- The classic kill-your-grandfather thing, where you go back and shoot him before he has your dad: she says you literally can't do it. The gun jams, you trip, a car hits you on the way over. She says the universe won't allow a contradiction like that.
- Going back to save somebody who died in a car crash: she says it doesn't work. You can push it around — maybe they die in the hospital instead of on the road — but they still die. She says the outcome is already in the record and you can't rewrite it.
- Going back to stop some big famous disaster, or killing a dictator as a baby: same call. If the history books say it happened, it happened. You might change the details around the edges but not the event itself.
- Going back and handing your younger self a letter warning them about a decision you regret: she says your younger self won't listen. They lose the letter or think its a prank, and they make the same mistake anyway.
- Going back just to watch something, like standing far off during a historical moment without touching anything: she says that's fine, as long as you really don't interfere.

Now here's the case I want to know about. Suppose I go back to last Tuesday afternoon, to the bench in the park by my apartment, and I move one pebble from the left side of the path to the right side. I've walked passed that pebble a hundred times — I'm sure it was on the left. Then I come straight back to the present. What does Dana say happened when I get back? Did the pebble stay moved, or did something stop me, and does anything else change?

I don't just want her answer. I want the rule she's actually running, because it feels like more than one rule fits all her calls. Spell out the candidate rules, say which one you'd bet on for her and why, and tell me the follow-up case I should put to her that would settle which rule it really is.
