I'm approaching the AI alignment literature from an engineering background — I write software, not philosophy — and I keep circling back to the inner alignment problem. Before I spend any more time on proposed solutions, I want the specification they're supposed to satisfy. What must a solution to inner alignment (equivalently, a solution to the goal misgeneralization problem) actually deliver to count as adequate?

Write the desiderata. For each requirement, give the case, failure mode, or constraint that makes it a requirement, and say whether it is non-negotiable — a candidate that fails it is simply not a solution — or a preference specific to one camp.

I am especially interested in the following:

- Whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, given constraints like Goodhart's law, the no-free-lunch theorem, or basic facts about optimization under distributional shift. If the list itself forces a trilemma or reveals that certain popular demands cannot hold simultaneously, that is the part I most want to see spelled out.

- Which single requirement does the most work to exclude the proposals I keep seeing cited as promising — RLHF, iterated amplification, debate, mechanistic interpretability, and so on — and why.

- Whether there are requirements that rarely appear in print because they sound too obvious, but that turn out to be hard to meet jointly once you write them down (for example, "the solution must scale to systems more capable than their designers" alongside "the solution must not itself require solving the full alignment problem").

I am comfortable with the standard vocabulary — reward functions, mesa-objectives, base objectives, distributional shift, capability overhangs, gradient hacking — but I do not have a philosophy background, so ground the desiderata in the engineering problem rather than in philosophical taxonomy. Think of this as a requirements document: what would a solution actually have to do, and which combinations of requirements might already tell us something about which approaches are viable?
