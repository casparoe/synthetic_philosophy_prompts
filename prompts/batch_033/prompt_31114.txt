So I'm putting together a handout for our philosophy of physics reading group on the substantivalism/relationism debate, and I want it in the form of a decision tree rather than a prose summary. The idea is that someone should be able to walk through it and end up at the position they actually hold, without knowing in advance which answer routes them where. The branch nodes should all be concrete questions: about Newton's bucket (does the water's concavity show that there's a fact about absolute rotation, or can it all be cashed out in relations to the fixed stars?), about Leibniz's shifted world (is a universe translated five feet to the left a distinct world or the same one described twice?), about the hole argument (if two models agree outside a hole but differ inside it, is determinism genuinely broken, or is the right move to treat them as the same physical possibility?), about whether spacetime points have primitive identity/thisness over and above their pattern of metrical relations, and about whether the metric field should be counted as part of spacetime or as part of the matter side. Please build the tree from the logical space first — lay out the questions and the combinations of answers they allow — and then locate the actual positions in that space. Leaves I'd like to see: the haecceitistic manifold substantivalism that bites the bullet on hole-argument indeterminism (mostly treated as a reductio these days), sophisticated substantivalism, relationism (split into Leibnizian and Machian if the tree supports it), supersubstantivalism, and ontic structural realism applied to spacetime. Aim for maybe six to eight decision nodes and five to seven leaves. Where the tree can't seperate two leaves, say so instead of pretending the question does more work then it does; and flag any combinations of answers nobody seems to occupy, with a one-line diagnosis of whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just underexplored.
