So I'm a PhD student working on AI governance and I've been in this reading group / discussion circle for like a year and a half and the debate about the competitiveness constraint and the alignment tax just will not move. You probably know the terrain: one side says competitive pressure makes it basically impossible for labs to spend serious resources on safety unless everyone does — if you're in a race and you spend 20% of your compute on alignment research while your competitor spends 0%, you lose, end of story. The alignment tax is real and the market selects against whoever pays it. The other side says this is way too reductive — safety can be a competitive advantage (moat, reputational effects, talent attraction), the empirical claim about a fixed capability-safety tradeoff isn't well-established, and in practice we've seen that some safety work improves capabilities anyway. They also point out that a catastrophic accident would be existentially bad for the company that caused it, so there's a business case for not cutting corners.

And I just keep watching the same moves get made. The realists say "yeah but in the limit, when the stakes are high enough, someone will defect" and the other side says "but we're not in the limit and institutional factors matter." Nobody budges. I've started to suspect this isn't actually an empirical disagreement — or that the empirical part is embedded in something deeper. My working hypothesis is that the realists are doing something like an econ-style equilibrium analysis (assume rational actors, look at the attractor state, comparative statics) and the other side is doing institutional or historical analysis (path-dependence, specific mechanisms, governance solutions are possible). So maybe they're not even answering the same question. But I could also see it being a disagreement about where the burden of proof lands when the stakes are catastrophic, or a deeper temperment divide about how dangerous AI actually is, with the competitiveness argument being the surface-level proxy for that.
