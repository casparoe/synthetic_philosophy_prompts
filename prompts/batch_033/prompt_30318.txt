hey, i've been kicking around an essay idea and would love some pushback before i commit to writing the whole thing. the question is whether Dennett's four kinds of creatures — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — can handle large language models, or whether LLMs are genuinely a fifth kind that the taxonomy doesn't have a slot for.

my rough claim is this: an LLM doesn't cleanly fit any of the four, and the places where it almost fits actually reveal tensions in the framework that weren't visible when the only examples were biological organisms. here's the skeleton of the argument i have so far:

1. LLMs are not Darwinian. obviously. their behavior isn't hardwired by a genome, and they don't rely on selection over generations to "try out" hypotheses. move on.

2. they are also not Skinnerian in any interesting sense. yeah, you could squint and say RLHF is operant conditioning — the model generates a response, gets a reward signal, adjusts. but the bulk of what the model knows comes from pretraining, which isn't trial-and-error in an environment at all. it's pure observational learning over static text. a Skinnerian creature needs to act and get feedback; an LLM during pretraining does neither.

3. Popperian seems closer. Popperian creatures have internal models that let them simulate actions and kill off bad hypotheses "in their stead" rather than in the world. an LLM does something like this — it has an internal world model (or something model-like) built from text, and when you prompt it, it can generate and implicitly filter candidates before outputting. but the filtering is distributed across transformer layers in a way that doesn't look much like explicit simulation. and, crucially, the model never *acts* and observes outcomes. the Popperian creature's inner environment is calibrated by feedback from the outer environment, which the LLM mostly lacks outside of the RLHF phase. so is it really Popperian, or just Popperian-flavored?

4. Gregorian. this is the interesting one. Gregorian creatures import mind-tools — language, concepts, algorithms — that were designed by other minds across cultural time. an LLM is literally made of language. it's the most Gregorian thing imaginable in terms of its training data and what it can manipulate. but here's the rub: the LLM doesn't *use* these tools in the way a Gregorian creature does. a human learns long division and can then apply it to any numbers; the LLM can produce the steps of long division but we have strong evidence it isn't running the algorithm, it's doing pattern completion that happens to align with the algorithm on familiar cases and falls apart on novel ones. the Gregorian creature internalizes the tool; the LLM imitates the tool's output without internalizing its causal structure. is that a difference in degree or a difference in kind?

5. so i want to say there's something genuinely new here — call it a "Gaussian creature" or something admittedly terrible — whose defining move is: compress a vast distribution of cultural outputs into a high-dimensional statistical model, then sample from it conditionally. the creature doesn't have the phenotype/genotype distinction of Darwinian kinds, doesn't do operant learning on its own actions the way Skinnerian ones do, doesn't maintain a separate inner environment it can run counterfactuals in the way Popperian ones do, and doesn't *grip* the tools it inherits the way Gregorian ones do. it does something else: it learns the surface statistics of tool-use so well that it can reproduce the surface without the underlying competence. and at sufficient scale, that surface reproduces a shocking amount of what we thought required the underlying competence.

before i write this up properly: am i being unfair to the Gregorian category? could a defender of the four-kinds framework just say "no, LLMs are clearly Gregorian, you've defined 'internalize' in a question-begging way"? and more broadly — does the taxonomy even claim to be exhaustive, or was it meant as a ladder of increasingly sophisticated biological minds, and i'm making a category mistake by trying to slot in an artifact? basically i want to know whether this essay is interesting or whether it's attacking a strawman. be brutal.
