I've been going in circles on this argument and I need a fresh pair of eyes.

(1) Consider the sphex wasp: it stuns a cricket, drags it to the nest entrance, leaves it outside, goes in to inspect the nest, comes back out, and drags the cricket in. If you move the cricket a few inches while the wasp is inside, the wasp re-positions the cricket at the entrance, re-enters the nest to inspect it, and will repeat this loop indefinitely — never just dragging the cricket straight in. The wasp's behavior is mechanical: it follows a fixed program and cannot adapt when the situation changes.

(2) Human behavior is also the product of physical processes: neurons, action potentials, and so on. These processes are governed by the same physical laws as everything else.

(3) The difference between human flexibility and sphex rigidity is just a difference in complexity. We have more elaborate neural machinery, more stored representations, more layers of feedback. But no amount of complexity can turn a mechanical process into a non-mechanical one.

(4) If your behavior is produced by a mechanical process, you are not free.

(5) Therefore, humans are not free — we are just sphex wasps with more steps.

(6) But this conclusion is hard to swallow. There is a genuine difference between someone who can notice a disruption and change course, and a wasp that literally cannot stop re-checking its nest. We deliberate, we hold each other responsible, we build institutions — all of which presuppose a kind of freedom the sphex lacks. If (5) is true, all of this is based on an illusion.

I'm stuck. If I reject (1) I'm denying the obvious. If I reject (2) I'm denying we're physical beings. (3) seems like a plausible claim about what complexity can and can't buy you. (4) seems like it's doing the real work — but rejecting it means saying a mechanical process can produce freedom, which sounds like a contradiction. I can't tell whether the argument equivocates somewhere or whether I just have to bite the bullet and give up (4) and live with the consequences. Which premise should I reject, and what does that cost?
