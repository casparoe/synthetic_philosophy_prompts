I serve on a city board that reviews proposed communications and choice designs intended to influence residents' decisions, and I would like your judgment about a case we are about to decide. Our recent record is as follows. We approved a water bill insert that told each household how its use compared with the neighborhood average, because it stated a true fact and left residents to draw their own conclusion. We rejected a parking-fine reminder that urged payment within forty-eight hours when no such deadline existed, because it misrepresented the situation. We approved a public health poster that paired an accurate description of smoking-related disease with a graphic photograph, on the ground that the image did not misstate anything and concerned the viewer's own health. We rejected a property tax payment portal that pre-selected the largest installment and made choosing a smaller one require navigating to a separate page, because the design made declining the pre-selected option costly and easy to miss. We approved a library app that placed newly available e-books at the top of search results while leaving the full catalog unchanged, on the ground that reordering options is not a misrepresentation. The proposal now in front of us is a recreation center membership renewal form on which the box authorizing another year's payment would arrive pre-checked, would say in plain language on the same page that the box has been pre-checked, and would allow the member to decline with a single click there. One faction reads our precedents as forbidding pre-checked choices as such, on the principle that a public body should never arrange a choice so that the resident's option is chosen for them. Another reads them as forbidding only defaults that are hidden or burdensome to undo, on the principle that transparent, easily reversible defaults are legitimate influence. What should we decide if we want to be most consistent with our own record, which of our earlier cases is the closest analog, and does the record genuinely force one answer? If it does not, tell me what principle we would be adopting under each decision and what further case would most cleanly settle which principle we are actually committed to. Please answer in continuous prose, as an email or short memo, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
