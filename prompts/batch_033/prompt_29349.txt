I'm taking an upper-division seminar on American pragmatism, and I'm writing a term paper comparing Peirce and James on truth. The way the course readings present it, Peirce and James both defend a pragmatic theory of truth according to which a belief is true just in case acting on it works, and the only real difference between them is that Peirce emphasizes what the community of inquirers would eventually settle on while James emphasizes what works for an individual. If that is right, I do not understand why Peirce goes to such lengths to describe truth as the opinion destined to be agreed upon at the end of inquiry rather than simply saying that a true belief is a useful belief. Why would he prefer that convergence-based formulation over the more straightforward claim that truth is what works? Please answer in American English.
