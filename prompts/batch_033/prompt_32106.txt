People routinely reassess the value of past experiences when they learn they were deceived about the facts. A man who thought he had a happy marriage says "those thirty years were wasted" when he learns his wife was unfaithfull the whole time. A woman who loved raising her child says "none of it was real" when she discovers the child was switched at birth. These aren't just expressions of grief — they seem to claim that the experiences themselves were less valuable than they felt at the time. What theory of well-being does this practice presuppose? Does it commit us to thinking that the value of a life depends on more than the quality of conscious experience? And if we take these judgments as warranted, are we implicitly committed to rejecting Nozick's experience machine?
