I am a novelist developing a cast of characters who work in forecasting and policy analysis. I want each character to hold a distinct, internally coherent position on two intertwined questions: how to weigh the inside view (case-specific reasoning, models, narrative detail) against the outside view (reference-class base rates, historical distributions), and how epistemically modest one ought to be when one's own analysis conflicts with the judgments of peers, experts, or markets.

Please construct a decision tree or taxonomy of the positions in this space. Each branch node should be a concrete diagnostic scenario or claim that forces a choice—for instance: "You have missed your deadline on the last five projects by at least thirty percent. Your inside view says the current project is better-scoped and will take four months. What is your stated forecast?" or "Three colleagues whose judgment you respect, and whose track records are comparable to yours, reach a different conclusion from the same evidence. Do you move your credence more than halfway toward theirs?" The leaves should carry position names (coined if necessary) and a one-sentence summary of each position's most uncomfortable commitment.

I also want you to note: (a) any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate given the questions you chose; (b) any logically possible combination of answers that appears unoccupied, with a brief diagnosis—is the view incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored? The purpose is not scholarly exhaustiveness but dramatic usefulness: I need a map of live intellectual options vivid enough to hang distinct characters on.
