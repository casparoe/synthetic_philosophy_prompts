I'm giving a department colloquium next month defending Frankfurt-style cases against the claim that they fail to refute the Principle of Alternative Possibilities. My view is that the cases succeed: moral responsibility does not require alternative possibilities, and the two main lines of resistance — the flicker-of-freedom strategy and the dilemma defense — both fail. The audience will be a philosophy department of about twenty, including three people who work directly on free will and moral responsibility: one who has published in favor of the flicker strategy, one incompatibilist who favors the dilemma defense, and one Strawsonian who thinks the whole framing in terms of alternative possibilities is a mistake. The rest of the department are philosophers from other areas — ethicists, epistemologists, a historian of early modern — who know the broad outlines of the debate but not the recent literature. I need a Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I will actually get from this audience, ordered by likelihood and potential damage, phrased the way each audience member would phrase them, with a two-minute reply for each and a candid note on where the reply is weakest. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks. And for your overall assessment, tell me which argument or consideration would most change your mind about where my position is most vulnerable, or where your own reasoning about the ranking of objections is most likely to fail.
