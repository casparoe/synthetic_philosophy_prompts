I cover cognitive science and neighboring fields for a general-interest magazine, and I am planning a long feature on the question whether the brain is literally a computer. Before I report the piece, I want the specification of the debate rather than a survey of it: what would an adequate answer to that question have to get right in order to be more than a metaphor or a merely verbal dispute? Write the desiderata for such an answer — the cases it must handle, the distinctions it cannot collapse, the constraints from computation theory and neuroscience it must respect, and the definitional traps it must avoid — with the consideration behind each requirement. In particular, make sure to address the difference between a system's being computationally described and a system's actually computing, and whether the content of a neural computation is fixed by the system itself or assigned by an observer. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are one camp's preference, flag any subset of requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify the single requirement that most cleanly separates the main positions. Hard limit: 900 words.
