ok so i'm a novelist, been at it about fifteen years, mostly literary fiction with morally complicated characters. i've got this set of beliefs i've acumulated about evil and character and responsibility, partly from the work and partly from reading around it (arendt, some moral philosophy, etc.), and i want to know if they actually hang together before i build another book on top of them. here's what i think:

(1) most real-world evil isn't sadism or malice — it's ordinary people doing their jobs inside systems that make the harm invisible to them. the banality thing, basically.

(2) but i also think individuals are fully morally responsible for what they do, even when the system is doing most of the causal work. "i was just following orders" is never an excuse.

(3) as a novelist, my job is to render interiority — to make a character's actions intelligible from the inside. if i can't do that for a character who does terrible things, i've failed as a writer.

(4) but i also think there's a line where making someone intelligible starts to feel like exculpation. like, if i write a novel that makes readers feel "there but for the grace of god go i" about a perpetrator, i've done something morally wrong — i've betrayed the victims by making the perpetrator too human.

(5) and yet i also believe no character should be written as a monster. a monster is a failure of imagination. everyone has reasons, everyone has a story they tell themselves.

so those are the five. i can feel the tensions but i can't sort out which ones are real and which ones dissolve if i'm more careful. can you go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, rank them by severity, and say what i'd have to give up to fix each one? and is there a single revision that cleans up the most?
