The debate over the knowledge argument — the Mary's room thought experiment introduced by Frank Jackson — has now run for more than forty years, and in my reading of the literature it has settled into a pattern that neither side finds satisfying. The core moves are well rehearsed. Mary, a neuroscientist raised in a black-and-white room, learns every physical fact about color vision. Upon leaving the room and seeing red for the first time, she seems to learn something new: what it is like to see red. The anti-physicalist concludes that not all facts are physical facts. The physicalist replies with the ability hypothesis (Mary gains a skill, not a proposition), the acquaintance hypothesis (she gains direct acquaintance with a property she already knew under a physical description), or the phenomenal concept strategy (she acquires a new concept for a fact she already knew). The anti-physicalist counters that each reply concedes that there is something Mary lacked before her release, and that redescribing it as a skill, an acquaintance, or a concept does not make it physical. The literature then cycles through refinements of these moves without visibly converging.

I am not asking who is right. I am asking what kind of disagreement this is. My working hypothesis is that the two sides are answering different questions: the physicalist is asking whether the complete physical truth logically entails all truths about conscious experience, while the anti-physicalist is asking whether any physical description can convey what conscious experience is like from the inside. If these are distinct questions, the apparent impasse may reflect a failure to specify the target rather than a genuine standoff.

Is this diagnosis defensible, or does it misrepresent what the two sides are actually claiming? If it is defensible, what would count as progress — a distinction that dissolves the appearance of disagreement, a formal result about the limits of physical entailment, or something else? If my diagnosis is wrong, what is the real structure of the disagreement, and what, if anything, could move it?
