ok so im taking this philosophy of intellectual property class and we've been going through locke and hegel and all the standard justifications but honestly im trying to figure out what i actually think by just running my gut reactions on a bunch of cases. heres where i've landed so far:

- indie game designer spends two years making a board game, brings it to a convention, and six months later a major publisher drops something almost identical after their reps played it at the booth. feels like straight-up theft to me, no hesitation.

- a mathematician proves a theorem but sits on it, then a year later someone else publishes the same proof having never seen the first guy's work. i don't think the second mathematician did anything wrong, even though the first one was earlier. independent discovery just doesnt feel like stealing.

- pharma company patents a drug after isolating the active compound from a plant that an indigenous community has used medicinally for centuries. the company did real lab work but something about this still feels deeply wrong to me.

- a physicist goes to a colloquium, hears the speaker toss out an offhand suggestion during q&a, and then publishes a paper developing that exact idea without citing the speaker at all. this bothers me a lot, even though the speaker never wrote it up or published anything.

- someone tweets a neat premise for a screenplay, a stranger sees it and writes and sells the actual script. my first instinct is that the twitter person has a legitimate grievance, but then i think — they put it out there publicly, it was like three sentences, the stranger did all the actual work. i genuinely dont know where i stand on this one.

- musician samples a two-second horn stab from an old record, processes it to the point where youd never identify the source, and builds a new track around it. i dont feel like this is theft, even though technically they used someone elses recording without permission. but i cant explain why it feels different from, say, lifting a whole hook.

the two cases that keep tripping me up are the physicist/conference one and the twitter/screenplay one — they seem structurally similar (someone floats an idea in a semi-public setting, someone else runs with it) but i react way more harshly to the conference case and i cant articulate why. is there a principle or a small set of principles that captures most of these verdicts? and if some of them have to go for the sake of coherence, which ones should i reconsider? also what does whatever principle you land on say about fan fiction, which i keep going back and forth on depending on the day?
