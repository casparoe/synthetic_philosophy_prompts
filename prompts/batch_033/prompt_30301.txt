I'm the best man at my brother's wedding next month and I keep putting off writing the toast. Years ago he told me about a story from a philosopher—Williams, I think, or someone like that—about a man who sees his wife and a stranger drowning, saves his wife, and when he starts explaining his reasoning afterward, the wife is hurt: the fact that he needed to reach for a reason at all is already one thought too many. The part that stuck with my brother was that the person you are, the things you care about, are supposed to be enough; having to justify them from the outside changes them. He got weirdly attached to this, and it's become a running joke between us—whenever I overexplain something, he says 'too many thoughts.' I want the toast to actually be built on that idea, not just mention it as an inside joke.

The room will be half his old philosophy friends, who'll know exactly where the story comes from, and half our family, who won't and who will get bored fast if it sounds like a seminar. Its got to land as a warm, funny best-man speech first. The idea should shape it in the background: I shouldn't list reasons he loves her, or talk about how he weighed things and made a good decision, or frame getting married as something he concluded was rational. But I also don't want it to be empty, like he just loves her and that's all there is to say. Can you write the toast, about four or five minutes long? Please no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables; just continuous prose I can read aloud, like a letter.
