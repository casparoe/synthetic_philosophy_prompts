In a graduate seminar on the foundations of statistical mechanics, a student defended a view I have encountered repeatedly in popular writing about thermodynamics. On his account, Maxwell's demon is best read as a claim about mind: an intelligent agent can produce order — a temperature difference, a net decrease in entropy — from nothing but information, so information and intelligence must lie partly outside the jurisdiction of the second law. The demon is being made to work as an analogy: the demon is to the box of gas as a mind is to the material world; the trapdoor is the will; the act of sorting is intelligent action; the temperature gradient that results is negentropy obtained for free. The conclusion the analogy is meant to license is that the second law constrains only unthinking matter, and that a sufficiently well-informed agent amounts to a loophole.

I would like a careful audit of this analogy. Please give an explicit table of correspondences — what in the demon scenario plays the role of the sensory apparatus, the memory, the decision procedure, the act, the energy source, and the information channel — and then identify where the mapping breaks. Which features of mind or intelligence does the analogy quietly import into the thermodynamic situation, and which features of a physically embedded mechanism does it quietly leave out? In particular, assess whether the demon's measurement and its memory are thermodynamically innocent, and whether the information it acquires is best understood as cost-free, as paid for at the moment of acquisition, or as paid for at the moment of erasure. Finally, state whether the conclusion — that information or intelligence supplies an end-run around the second law — survives once the demon is treated as one physical system interacting with the rest, and whether some other comparison, such as an information engine or a feedback-controlled ratchet, would carry the intended lesson more faithfully.
