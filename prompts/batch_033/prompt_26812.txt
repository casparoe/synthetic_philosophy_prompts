I spent most of my twenties with a fairly standard compatibilist picture of free will — the kind where you're responsible for what you do as long as you're acting from your own desires and values, without coercion or certain kinds of impairment. Then last year I served on a jury for a serious criminal case, and I found myself unable to shake the thought that the defendant, given everything that had formed him, could not have done otherwise than he did. That sent me down a rabbit hole that ended with Galen Strawson's Basic Argument, which I now find myself accepting despite my best efforts not to.

The argument, as I understand it: you act as you do because of the way you are — your character, dispositions, and values. To be truly morally responsible for what you do, you would have to be truly responsible for the way you are. But you cannot be responsible for the way you are, because to become responsible for your character, you would need to have already chosen to form it in a certain way, which would require already having a character that makes that choice — and the regress never bottoms out in a self-created self. So no one is ultimately morally responsible for anything. I've been sitting with this conclusion for months now, and it has reshaped how I think about blame, punishment, and credit.

I'm not asking for the standard compatibilist replies — I've read Fischer, Watson, and the rest, and I find them responsive to other arguments but not quite to this one. What I want is something different: a map of what would genuinely count against the Basic Argument. Which of its premises are weaker than they appear? What empirical discoveries or conceptual developments would undermine it? Which of its implications — about pride, about gratitude, about the reactive attitudes — would, if shown unacceptable, put pressure back on the argument itself? And has the argument been qualified in ways that insulate it from counterevidence, and if so, is that a problem for an argument of this kind? I'm trying to be honest about whether I've talked myself into a position that has no real exposure to the world, and if it does have exposure, what it would take to change my mind.
