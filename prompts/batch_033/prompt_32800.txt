I've spent the last few months working my way through the teleosemantics literature — Millikan, Papineau, Neander, the usual crowd — and I've ended up fairly convinced, almost against my will. The idea that mental content is fixed by biological function, where function is understood in terms of selection history, just seems to handle the naturalisation problem better than anything else on offer. My belief that there's a pint in front of me has the content it does because the relevant neural mechanisms were selected (phylogenetically or through learning, depending on your flavour of the view) to track the presence of beer-shaped objects, not because of any irreducible intentionality. It's elegant, it's naturalistic, and it doesn't leave me with the queasy feeling I get from instrumentalist or purely inferential-role accounts that seem to be describing something else entirely.

But I've noticed that the more time I spend with a view, the harder it gets for me to imagine what would actually make me abandon it, and that strikes me as a bad sign. So I want to know, straight: what would count against teleosemantics as a theory of content? Not the standard objections that already appears in every survey article — the swampman, the indeterminacy of frog content, the distality problem — unless you genuinely think one of those is fatal rather than just a puzzle the view has the resources to handle. I mean: what would we have to discover about the world, or what argument would we have to accept, that would make a reasonable teleosemanticist say 'alright, this programme doesn't work'? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want the distinction between things defenders would concede as counting against them and things only critics think are problems. And I'd like an honest assessment of whether the view has been insulated by qualifications to the point where it doesn't genuinely risk anything — and if so, whether that's a problem for a theory of this kind, or just what mature philosophical theorising looks like.
