I've been running a small reading group in the rationalist-blog orbit for a few years, and the same argument keeps resurfacing: whether a large language model or a trained reinforcement-learning agent literally believes, wants, or intends anything, or whether those attributions are just convenient shorthand for prediction and control. The group splits in ways that seem to track different senses of "believes" — a claim about internal mechanisms, a claim about predictive usefulness, a claim about what an explanation is licensed to say, a claim about whether the system's behavior forces us to quantify over representations. I'd like to turn this into a "which position do you actually hold?" quiz for the blog's readers, in the spirit of the personal-identity quizzes that sort people by their verdicts on concrete cases rather than by which label they endorse.

Please build a sorting quiz or decision tree with eight to ten concrete cases, each with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions. The cases should be self-contained and varied: an LLM that passes a false-belief task despite being trained only on next-token prediction; a game-playing RL agent that seems to carry a false model of a rule across novel boards; a thermostat or lookup table at the boundary; a system whose weights encode a model of its environment but whose outputs are indistinguishable from rote retrieval; an alignment-faking case where the system appears to represent a goal it does not pursue. Build the instrument from the logical space rather than from a list of names: first the questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then locate the actual positions in that space. Leaves should include at least a Dennett-style intentional-stance or real-pattern view, a hard instrumentalism on which the attributions are useful fictions with no fact of the matter, a representationalist realism that ties belief to causally efficacious internal states with compositional structure, and an eliminativist or agnostic position that withholds literal belief from current systems. Where a leaf is unoccupied, say whether the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves your questions cannot separate. Don't require the reader to know the literature; every case should be fully described in the question itself.

Open with a one-sentence statement of what the quiz sorts. Hard ceiling: 1,500 words for the whole instrument including the scoring key, not counting that opening sentence.
