I've been going in circles on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism and I feel like I must be missing something, because otherwise the whole utilitarian/EA edifice would have collapsed decades ago and nobody seems to have noticed.

The setup: pleasures don't seem to share any common felt quality. The pleasure of morphine, the pleasure of solving a math problem, the pleasure of eating when you're hungry, the pleasure of sex, the pleasure of seeing your kid take their first steps — when you actually introspect on these, what's the one thing they all have in common? It's not like there's a "pleasure quale" that's the same across all of them the way there's (arguably) a "red quale" shared by stop signs and tomatoes. They feel different. Really different. Sometimes they're not even in the same ballpark — the pleasure of a warm bath is sensory and diffuse, the pleasure of accomplishment is more like a cognitive satisfaction, and the pleasure of morphine is this weird bodily heaviness.

Here's the thing that's bugging me. If "pleasure" doesn't carve nature at the joints — if it's not a unified natural kind — then hedonism as a theory of well-being seems to be saying: "the good is this gerrymandered disjunction of experiences that we happen to have grouped under one word." But that can't be what makes a life go well! The good is supposed to be the fundamental thing that explains why certain states are worth pursuing. If the best you can say is "well, all these states are good because they're all... y'know... pleasurable" and then you can't say what that means except by pointing at the disjunction, you haven't explained anything. You've just given a label. It's like saying "all these things are good because they're all blargs" and then defining "blarg" as "the set of things that are good."

I've tried to bring this up with friends who are into EA and utilitarianism and the responses I get are either (a) "we just define pleasure as whatever people prefer," which feels like changing the subject, or (b) "look up attitudinal hedonism," which from what I can tell just redefines pleasure as a propositional attitude rather than a feeling — but then why call it hedonism at all?

I'm a programmer, not a philosopher, but I read a fair amount of LessWrong and the philosophy-of-mind stuff that gets linked there. So I'd like you to engage as a philosopher who actually knows the terrain: where exactly does my reasoning break down? Am I wrong that the heterogeneity problem is a real problem, wrong about what it implies, or wrong about what hedonism actually claims? Or is this actually a much bigger problem for hedonism than people admit, and I should just update accordingly? Think through it step by step and don't give me a hand-wavy answer.
