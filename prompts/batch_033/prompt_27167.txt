I have been working through the accuracy-first programme in formal epistemology — the epistemic utility programme — and have become convinced that it is broadly on the right track. On this view, the one fundamental epistemic norm is that our credences should be as accurate as possible, where accuracy is closeness to the truth-values of the relevant propositions at a given world, and the other epistemic norms are supposed to fall out of this single demand. In particular, probabilism is meant to be vindicated by the result that every non-probabilistic credence function is accuracy-dominated by some probabilistic credence function relative to any additive, continuous, strictly proper inaccuracy measure, and conditionalization is meant to be vindicated by the result that it maximises expected accuracy relative to such measures. I have in mind the familiar machinery: strictly proper scoring rules such as the Brier score and the logarithmic score, accuracy dominance, and expected-accuracy maximisation.

What I want is not a list of standard objections but a map of what would count against the programme. Which formal results, possible cases, or comparative judgements about epistemic value would have to be true for the accuracy-first story to be wrong or importantly incomplete? For instance, how damaging is the fact that different strictly proper scoring rules can disagree about which credence functions dominate which, and is the choice of scoring rule a free parameter or an unargued assumption quietly doing the real work? Could there be rationally permissible credences that are accuracy-dominated on every strictly proper measure, or probabilistically coherent credences that are rationally impermissible for reasons that accuracy cannot register? Would results about imprecise credences, non-additive measures, or the role of accuracy in practical deliberation undermine the claim that accuracy is the sole fundamental epistemic value rather than one value among several? I would like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with an honest account of which of them a committed accuracy-first theorist would themselves concede as counting against the view. Please also say whether the programme has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and, if it has, whether that is a problem for a research programme of this kind.

I would find it particularly useful if you separated defeats of the formal core — the theorems and their assumptions — from defeats of the philosophical gloss, namely the claims that accuracy is the sole epistemic goal and that the theorems amount to a genuine vindication rather than a technical curiosity. Please write your answer in British English.
