alright so i've been going down the nomic rabbit hole — you know, the game where changing the rules is a move, and the rules for changing the rules are themselves up for grabs — and i'm realizing that before i try to make sense of any particular formalization or theory, i need to know what i should even be demanding of one.

so: write me the desiderata. what does an adequate theory of self-amending rule systems have to deliver? i want a list of requirements — the cases it has to get right, the paradoxes it has to dissolve or at least not make worse, the distinctions it can't collapse, the results from logic or law it has to be consistent with. for each requirement, give me the case or consideration that motivates it.

some specific things i want you to address (but don't just limit yourself to these):

- the self-amendment circularity: when a rule governs its own amendment, what does it even mean for the amendment to be "valid"? if rule 17 says "rule 17 may be amended by a two-thirds vote," and we amend it by two-thirds vote to say "rule 17 may be amended by a simple majority," was the procedure valid? the amendment seems to retroactively authorize the lower threshold that it itself was adopted under — or does it?

- the immutable rule problem: can a rule make itself or another rule unamendable? if rule 3 says "rule 4 is immutable," and then rule 4 is amended to say "rule 3 is mutable," which rule wins? does the system need a notion of entrenched vs. non-entrenched rules, and if so, what makes a rule entrenched?

- the self-repeal paradox: can a rule repeal itself? "rule 12 is hereby repealed" — if it's repealed, then the repeal took effect, meaning the rule was valid when it repealed itself, but now it's gone. if it's not repealed, then it's still in force. is this a genuine paradox or just a category mistake?

- the temporal problem: does amendment happen at an instant, or over an interval? if the amendment procedure is amended while an amendment is in progress, what happens to the in-flight amendment?

- the completeness problem: what happens when the amendment procedure is silent on a case? does the system have an answer, or is it just indeterminate? if it's indeterminate, is that a bug in the system or a feature of all rule systems?

then, after the list: flag which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable — if you drop them you're not even talking about self-amendment anymore — and which are more like theoretical preferences. and here's the thing i'm most curious about: are any subsets of these requirements jointly unsatisfiable? like, is there an impossibility result lurking here, analogous to Arrow's theorem or the liar paradox, where some perfectly reasonable-sounding desiderata can't all be met at once? if so, which ones conflict and why.

if you want to briefly score a few approaches against the list — the metalevel approach (rules stratified into object and meta), the social-practice view (validity is grounded in acceptance not formal derivation), the deflationary view (self-amendment is just replacement of one rule-set by another, no special logic needed), and the fixed-point approach (treating rules as self-referential sentences and amendment as a revision operator) — that's fine, but keep the weight on the criteria. the list is the deliverable here.
