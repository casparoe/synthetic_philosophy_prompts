I'm a PhD student in moral psychology, mostly working on blame and the conditions under which we withhold it, and I've ended up pretty convinced by a strong version of Strawson's reactive-attitudes view. The version I mean isn't the modest one where the reactive attitudes are just useful or hard to shake; it's the claim that the participant stance — holding one another to demands and being open to resentment, indignation, gratitude, hurt feelings — is constitutive of ordinary interpersonal life, so the question whether we should abandon these attitudes in light of determinism isn't answerable from some neutral standpoint. The objective stance isn't a rival account of what persons are like; it's a different practical orientation, one that can't be made global without ceasing to relate to persons as persons, and even if it could be made global it wouldn't be a correction of an error. So on my view the responsibility debate gets settled not by metaphysics but by something like the non-detachability of the participant stance. I'm not asking for a survey of the whole free will literature; I want this view's exposure mapped seperately from the standard compatibilism debate.

What I want is an honest map of what would count against this, in the strongest form a critic could state — not the usual incompatibilist objection that desert requires the ability to do otherwise, but the stuff that should actually move me. For example: there's experimental work suggesting people's retributive impulses change when they're induced to believe in determinism, and some people report sustaining something close to a global objective stance for long stretches. Do those cut against the insulation claim, or just against a strawman? What conceptual tensions does the strong view have — e.g., does it quietly presuppose the very desert thesis it's meant to displace? I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with a sense of which ones a committed Strawsonian would concede count against them rather than dismiss, and a straight verdict on whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and whether, for a view of this kind, that's a bug or the point. I'd personally be most moved by evidence that the objective stance can be a stable, default orientation toward people rather than a local episode, since that would attack the 'not a live option' premise directly.
