The dad gets into arguments with it about baseball. You get the idea. So I've been going down a rabbit hole reading philosophy of mind, and Dennett's intentional stance keeps coming up — the idea that we attribute beliefs and desires to a system when doing so is the best way to predict its behavior, and that's really all there is to it. I get the broad strokes. But here's what I actually need for the novel: I want to write a scene where two characters have a genuine, meaty argument about whether the AI "really believes" something specific (like, believes that the daughter is upset, or believes that it has a history with this family). And I dont want one of them to be obviously right. I need a concrete conceptual puzzle — some kind of edge case or ambiguous scenario — where the intentional stance gives you an answer that's genuinely non-obvious, or where reasonable people applying the stance could come to different conclusions. Not the thermostat example (I've seen that one a million times). Something messier, maybe involving a system that passes some tests for belief-attribution and fails others in a way that doesnt cleanly resolve.

If you could lay out a specific scenario like that and walk through what the intentional stance says about it — and especially flag the points where the stance itself might underspecify the answer — that would be incredible.
