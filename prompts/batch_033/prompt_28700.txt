Two people in my philosophy of science reading group got stuck on the problem of old evidence, and neither would budge. Here's roughly the exchange.

(1) Maya said old evidence can't confirm anything on the standard Bayesian picture. If you already know E — like the anomaly in Mercury's orbit, which was known long before Einstein — then P(E)=1, so conditionalizing on E can't move anything: P(H|E)=P(H). So either old evidence never confirms, or confirmation isn't probability-raising.

(2) Dan said that can't be right, because general relativity really was supported by explaining Mercury. So if the formalism says otherwise, we're formalizing the wrong thing. What's new isn't E; it's the fact that the theory entails E. Before Einstein, no accepted theory entailed the anomaly, so learning that H entails E is new information, and it can raise P(H) even while P(E)=1.

(3) Maya: fine, but then the evidence is the logical fact that H entails E, not E itself. So you've changed the subject. You haven't shown old evidence confirms; you've shown new evidence about a logical relation confirms. P(H|E)=P(H) still holds.

(4) Dan: that's exactly the point — the standard story is too narrow. It only counts newly observed facts as evidence, but we often get confirmation by discovering that things we already knew hang together. Making confirmation depend on whether we happened to learn E before or after the theory is too hostage to biography.

(5) Maya: but if you let logical relations confirm, you owe us an account of what the new evidence is and why it raises P(H), and that's notoriously slippery. And maybe saying old evidence confirms is just a loose way of saying E would have confirmed H had we not already known E — a counterfactual, not actual confirmation.

(6) Dan: the counterfactual move is worse; it pretends away the one thing that's true, which is that we did already know E.

They asked me to referee and I honestly couldn't. Could you go through it move by move and say who's right about what, including anything both of them are missing?
