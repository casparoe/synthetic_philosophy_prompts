I been thinking about advance directives a lot — like, if I write down now that I don't want to be kept alive if I get severe dementia, but then when I actually have dementia I seem happy and content, whose wishes should count? Mine now, or mine then? I keep going back and forth depending on the details of the case, and I want to make a quiz that helps me and some friends figure out what position we actually hold, not just what label we like.

Can you make me a sorting quiz — like a "which theory of advance directives do you actually hold?" thing — with maybe 8 to 10 questions? Each question should be a concrete scenario (dementia and changed preferences, Ulysses contracts, someone who writes a directive when depressed and then recovers, religious conversion, a teenager who writes a directive and then changes their mind as an adult, that kind of thing), with 2 or 3 answer options per question. Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the main positions in the debate.

The positions I've roughly gathered are: (1) the earlier self's directive should basically always be followed, (2) the current self's preferences should always win, (3) the directive should be followed only when the current self is incompetent to decide, (4) the directive should be followed unless the current self's preferences changed in a deep or authentic way, and (5) it depends on whether the earlier and later self are the same person in a narrative-identity sense. But I'm not sure these are all real positions or if some of them collapse into each other — if I've got that wrong, please fix the taxonomy.

The most important thing: no question should just ask "do you think the earlier self's wishes should always be followed?" The questions should be about concrete cases where the positions genuinly come apart, so the quiz sorts people by what they say about cases, not by which label they prefer.

Also, if there are answer combinations that no actual position maps onto, flag those and say why nobody's there — is the combination incoherent, or just unattractive, or has nobody bothered to defend it? And if two positions can't be seperated by any case you can think of, flag that too rather than pretending the quiz tells them apart.
