I'm trying to get clear on what I actually think about when breaking the law is justified, so I wrote down my gut reactions to a bunch of cases. (1) In a city with segregated buses, a Black passenger refuses to give up their seat to a white man and is arrested: justified. (2) A doctor in a state that just criminalized abortion keeps providing abortions and tells the sheriff she won't stop: justified. (3) A climate group blocks a six-lane highway during rush hour; an ambulance is delayed and a patient later dies, and the organizers say the delay wasn't foreseeable: not justified, or at least much less justified than the first two. (4) A pacifist who thinks a war is illegal refuses to pay the portion of their taxes that funds it and puts the money in a public escrow account: I want to say justified, but it feels different from the bus case and I can't say how. (5) A government employee leaks documents showing the military has been lying about civilian casualties: justified. (6) A different employee leaks documents that also expose the identities of local informants, several of whom are then killed: not justified, even though both leaks exposed real wrongdoing. (7) Someone smashes the window of a military recruiting office and spray-paints the word murderers on it: not justified. (8) A family hides an undocumented child whose asylum claim was denied but who will likely be killed if deported: justified, even though it's secret. (9) A restaurant owner stays open during a lockdown because they think the order is illegal overreach and they'll go bankrupt otherwise: I don't think this is justified, but I can't say what makes it different from the doctor, becuase on paper it looks similar.

I keep testing a principle like 'you may break the law only when the law itself is seriously unjust, and only openly and nonviolently, and you accept the penalty' — but that seems to fail the highway case (the traffic law isn't the injustice), the asylum case (it isn't open), and maybe the tax case. Is there a principle, or a small set of them, that gets all or nearly all of my verdicts? State it precisely, say which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and which I should revise, and apply it to a case I haven't listed: a nurse who breaks a court gag order to tell a reporter that patients are being denied treatment as a cost-cutting measure.
