My philosophy of mind prof summed up teleosemantics roughly like this: a mental state's content is just its biological function, so the frog's fly-detector means fly because that neural response was selected for catching flies. That's supposed to be the beauty of the view — it explains error without mystery, since if the same cell fires at a bee or a drifting speck, that's just the mechanism doing its job on the wrong input and the content stays fly. And it gets you intentionality for free from natural selection, no spooky intrinsic aboutness. I like how clean this sounds, but I've also heard people mutter that the view can't pin down content uniquely and can't handle concepts that were never selected for, and I cant tell whether those are external objections or whether the summary itself is already papering over them. Give me a committed verdict: is this a fair summary of what teleosemanticists actually claim? Go claim by claim, say what a careful defender would accept, what they'd qualify or flatly deny, and whether the elegance survives the corrections.
