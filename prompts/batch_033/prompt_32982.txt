I'm a software engineer, and I got into this through modal logic for CS—Kripke structures, state graphs, model checking—and then fell down the metaphysics rabbit hole. The dispute I keep going in circles on is the one between counterpart theory and transworld identity about what could have been true of particular individuals (de re modality, I think people call it). As I understand it: Lewis's counterpart theory says an object exists in exactly one possible world, and a sentence like "I could have been a musician" is true because a counterpart of me in another world is a musician, where the counterpart relation is a matter of similarity, not identity. Transworld identity (Kripke, Plantinga, and much of the mainstream) says no—the very same individual exists in many possible worlds and just has different properties in them, so the modal claim is about me, not about some similar guy. Kripke's objection is basically that Humphrey cares whether he himself wins, not whether someone resembling him wins in another world. Lewis's reply is the problem of accidental intrinsics: if the same object is wholly present in two worlds and has six fingers in one and five in the other, that looks like a contradiction unless you index the properties to worlds or drop identity across worlds in favor of counterparts.

Here's what bugs me: this fight has been running since the 70s, and as far as I can tell both sides can account for all the same modal truths—there's even a translation between the two frameworks, so its not obvious what's left to argue about. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is partly verbal, turning on what "exists in a world" is supposed to mean, and partly a proxy for the separate fight about whether possible worlds are concrete or abstract. But I could be wrong: maybe it's a substantive disagreement about what makes de re modal claims true, or about whether similarity or identity is the right semantic primitive. I'd like a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement rather than a verdict on who wins. Given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress—a formal result, a sharper distinction, a reframing of the question, or a reason to think it's just not well-posed? And please say how confident you are in the diagnosis and flag the part you're least sure about.
