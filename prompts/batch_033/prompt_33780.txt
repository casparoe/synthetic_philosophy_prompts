I keep running into people who cite Chesterton's fence as if it settles any debate about institutional reform — you shouldn't change something unless you understand why it was put there in the first place. But here's what I don't get: doesn't this principle basically rule out all reform? Every institution that exists has some historical reason for existing, even if it's a terrible reason. The monarchy exists because some guy won a battle in 1066. Does Chesterton's fence really say we have to keep the monarchy unless we can fully reconstruct the medieval mind that created it? How do people who take this principle seriously draw the line between reformable and unreformable institutions?
