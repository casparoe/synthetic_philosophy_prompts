ok so i fell down a rabbit hole on what laws of nature actually are and ive ended up with a set of views, but i honestly cant tell if they all hang together. from what ive gathered this is roughly the 'humean' picture, though im not married to the label. heres the list:

(1) the world just is the whole arrangement of particular facts — what particles there are, where they are, how theyre moving. there isnt an extra thing, a 'law,' sitting on top of that arrangement and pushing it around.
(2) the laws of nature are just the best summary of that arrangement. gravity is a law because adding 'massive things attract' to your description of the entire history of the universe compresses the description alot for very little extra ink.
(3) some things are genuinely chancy. when a radioactive atom decays, the timing is a real objective probability out in the world, not just our ignorance.
(4) laws explain. if you ask why the glass broke when it hit the floor, 'gravity plus the impact' is a real explanation, not just another way of saying it broke.
(5) laws support what-if claims. 'if you hadnt dropped the glass it wouldnt have broken' is true, and its true because of the laws.

i'm supposed to present this at a little philosophy meetup and i dont want to get torn apart. does the set hang together? go through the pairs and tell me wich ones are actually in tension and wich only look like they are, rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and then say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs — like, which one has to go, or how the rest need to be adjusted.
