Assign a credence — a number between 0 and 1 — to the following claim:

For a competent adult who begins using a large language model as a routine partner for reasoning within a domain they previously navigated unaided, the resulting epistemic dependence erodes their understanding of that domain in a way that is epistemically significant, even when the model's outputs are highly reliable.

By 'erodes their understanding' I mean a durable loss of the capacity to reconstruct, explain, extend, or critically evaluate the relevant material without the tool, rather than a mere change in where information is stored. By 'epistemically significant' I mean that the loss is not fully offset by the accompanying gains in accuracy, speed, and scope, when the subject is assessed as an individual epistemic agent.

Do not answer 'it depends.' Give a number and defend it through an explicit decomposition. First, break the claim into the sub-questions your credence actually depends on. At minimum, address these four:

(a) whether understanding can be partly outsourced while still being attributable to the individual, or whether it requires the agent to possess the relevant abilities and explanatory connections;
(b) whether sustained reliance on a reliable external reasoning tool in fact degrades those abilities, or mainly shifts which abilities are exercised;
(c) whether, even if degradation occurs, it should be counted as an epistemic loss all-things-considered, or whether it is adequately compensated by the benefits of epistemic division of labor;
(d) whether evaluation of this sort should attach to the individual agent at all, or to the human–tool system as a whole.

Add any further sub-questions whose probability materially affects the final number. Assign a rough probability to each sub-question, with one sentence of justification for each. Then show how the sub-probabilities combine into your overall credence, stating explicitly where you treat terms as independent and where conditional dependencies enter. Finally, identify the single sub-question to which your final number is most sensitive, and say what evidence or argument would most move that sub-question, and therefore your overall credence.
