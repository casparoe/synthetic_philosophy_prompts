I host a small podcast at the intersection of formal epistemology and the rationalist blogosphere, and I've booked a guest who has spent the last decade defending the position that the Kelly criterion — or more precisely, the principle of maximizing geometric expected growth — is the uniquely correct normative standard for individual decision-making under uncertainty. His view is that geometric rationality is more fundamental than expected utility theory, not merely a special case of it, and that the standard EU framework gets the direction of explanation backwards: we should derive utility functions from growth-optimality considerations, not the other way around.

I want to go well beyond the introductory exposition that most of my audience has already encountered on LessWrong or the EA Forum. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press on the view's genuine pressure points. Specifically:

- I want at least one question that forces him to address the problem of specifying the relevant time horizon and aggregation unit. If I face a one-shot career choice, or a medical decision with irreversible consequences, what exactly is being "repeated" such that the geometric mean is the right thing to maximize? How does he individuate the class of bets over which the product of returns is taken, without making the theory vacuous?

- I want a question about the relationship between Kelly betting and log utility. His view claims that geometric rationality is not merely expected utility maximization with a logarithmic utility function, but the standard objection is that the math collapses the two. I want a question that makes him draw the distinction sharply, and a follow-up that asks whether there is any empirical prediction on which the two views come apart.

- I want a question on Ole Peters' ergodicity-economics program and whether he sees his own position as equivalent to Peters', or as a distinct philosophical claim that happens to share mathematical machinery — and if distinct, what the practical difference is.

- I want a question about the multi-agent and market-level implications. If all agents are Kelly bettors but have different time horizons or different assessments of what constitutes the relevant ensemble, does the market pricing kernel still aggregate coherently? What happens to the standard no-arbitrage arguments?

- I want a question about the normative status of the view in genuine edge cases: a literal final bet with no further decisions, or a bet with a positive arithmetic mean but negative geometric mean due to the distribution of outcomes. What does the theory say about an agent who knows with certainty that she will face exactly one more decision in her life — and what grounds the normativity of the geometric criterion in that case?

- I want a question about the empirical psychology literature on whether human risk preferences are better captured by something like geometric-rationality models than by prospect theory or standard EU, and whether he thinks the descriptive adequacy of the view matters for its normative force.

For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the response he is most likely to give, so that the interview doesn't stall on a rehearsed answer. The goal is to produce a conversation that moves the debate forward rather than re-litigating the opening statement.
