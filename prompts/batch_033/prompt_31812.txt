The question I'm centering the essay on is this: does Ridley Scott's repeated public insistence that Deckard is a replicant settle the interpretive question, or is the film itself the only evidence that matters? I've been reading about the intentional fallacy — the argument from Wimsatt and Beardsley that the author's intention is neither available nor desirable as a standard for interpreting a work — and the responses from intentionalists like E.D. Hirsch, who argue that textual meaning is constituted by the author's intended meaning. I want the essay to do more than just take sides; I want to use Blade Runner to test the strengths and limits of each position.

What I'm hoping you can help me with is a careful philosophical reading of the film that does the following. First, lay out what a committed anti-intentionalist would say about the Deckard question and what a committed intentionalist would say, giving each position its strongest formulation. Second, and more importantly, show where the film itself complicates this debate. The film's world is one where the boundary between human and replicant is unstable, where memories are implanted, and where characters themselves are uncertain about their own origins. If the film's subject matter is precisely the unreliability of origins and the constructedness of identity, does that make Scott's own claim to authority over the film's meaning look suspect in a way the film itself seems to anticipate? That is, does Blade Runner dramatize something about the intentional fallacy that the philosophical arguments alone don't capture?

Third, I want to know where the film resists being reduced to an illustration of either position. Are there scenes, details, or ambiguities in the film that don't fit neatly into an intentionalist or anti-intentionalist reading, and that an honest critic would have to acknowledge? What does the film as a work of art add to the philosophical debate that the theoretical literature misses?

Finally, please separate what is settled in the philosophical literature on authorial intention from what is genuinely contested among people who work in the field. I know the debate is live, and I don't want to be told that one side obviously has it right when the experts themselves disagree. I want to understand where the real fault lines are.
