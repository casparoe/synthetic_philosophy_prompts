I run my school's philosophy club, and next week I'm leading a session on whether moral status comes in degrees rather than being a simple on/off property. The members are bright but have no formal ethics background — they have discussed animal ethics in previous meetings, they will grant that causing pain to a dog matters more than causing pain to a beetle, but they have not examined whether those two judgments can sit inside a single coherent framework, nor have they considered what it would mean if the gradations run all the way down to entities we do not normally think of as sentient at all.

I have forty minutes. Please do not explain the view to me — I have thought about it. I need you to plan how I should teach it.

Specifically:

- What distinction needs to be in place before the question even makes sense? I am thinking the difference between something mattering less (a quantitative claim) and something mattering in a fundamentally different way that resists trade-offs (a structural claim). Is there something even more basic they need first?
- What one example or thought experiment should anchor the whole session? My instinct is to lay out a spectrum — human, dog, mouse, beetle, and then maybe a plant or a thermostat — and ask where they stop caring. But I am worried that framing leads them toward a binary-cutoff discussion rather than the graded-structure idea I actually want them to see. What is a better choice?
- What misconceptions will they walk in with, what new ones will form as I go, and at which points in the sequence should I head each off? I am expecting some version of "a human life is obviously worth more than any number of insects" as an unexamined starting point, and I need to decide whether to treat that as the thing to be scrutinized or as a premise I can work with.
- There is a particular derailment risk I want to manage: the session turning into a debate about whether insects can feel pain. Should I grant insect sentience for the sake of argument early on and move past it, or should I let the disagreement sit there and use it to illustrate something about the structure of the question?
- What question, asked in the last three minutes, would tell me whether they have understood that graded moral status is a claim about structure — about whether the units of moral weight are all the same kind of thing — rather than the weaker claim that some creatures just count for less?
- What should I explicitly flag as "we are not resolving this today" and set aside?
