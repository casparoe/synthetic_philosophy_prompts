So our company is going through this big automation push — AI tools, RPA, the whole thing — and I got put on the committee that decides which roles and tasks get automated and which stay human. At first I thought it was just a cost-benefit thing, but the more I've been reading about the philosophy of work — like, what actually makes work meaningful versus just a paycheck — the more I realize we don't have any way to factor that in. Like, sure, automating data entry saves money, but if that data entry job was the only thing giving someone structure and social contact and a sense of competence, maybe we're making a mistake that looks great on a spreadsheet and terrible in people's lives.

I want an actual decision procedure I can bring to the next committee meeting. Something step-by-step that takes a task or role we're considering automating and runs it through the philosophical criteria for meaningful work — autonomy, mastery, social connection, contribution to something beyond yourself, whatever the literature actually supports. It needs to be concrete enough that my colleagues (who have not read a page of philosophy and don't want to) can use it. It should say what questions to ask, in what order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, and — this is crucial — where the procedure has to stop and say "a human being needs to make a judgment call here, and here's why the procedure can't make it for you."

Then I want you to run the procedure on two cases: automating the monthly report generation that three analysts currently spend a week on (tedious but they say it gives them a deep understanding of the business), and automating the first-pass resume screening in HR (which everyone hates doing but which currently forces hiring managers to actually read what candidates write).

And be honest: tell me where this procedure would give me an answer I'm not going to like, or where it would tell me to preserve work that seems obviously pointless from a buisness perspective. I need to know what I'd be signing up for before I walk into that meeting.
