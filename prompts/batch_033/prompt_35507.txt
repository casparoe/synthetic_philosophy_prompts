I'm in a reading group that's spent the last few months on Kripke's reading of Wittgenstein on rule-following, and two of us have ended up in opposite camps. One of us is convinced by the sceptical solution: there is no fact of the matter about what a speaker means by a term like plus, and talk of meaning has to be understood in terms of assertability conditions and agreement within a community. The other is convinced a straight solution has to exist: there is some fact about the speaker, or the speaker plus her environment or underlying mechanisms, that constitutes her meaning addition rather than some deviant function, even if no one has yet stated that fact fully convincingly.

We've tried to write a joint statement and every draft has come out either vacuous or slanted. Write the document we should have produced. It should include a numbered section of claims both camps can sign: about the set-up (a speaker who has added finitely many numbers, all below 57, and is now asked 68 + 57), about what any account has to explain (why 125 is the correct answer, why she can be wrong about what she means, why meaning is normative), and about what the sceptical challenge does and does not establish. Then include a disagreements table, where each row gives a precise residual disagreement, with each side's position phrased so that side would accept the wording. Then a cruxes section: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of us. For each crux, specify a case or arguement both sides agree bears on it (for example an isolated speaker who has never been part of a linguistic community, an idealised dispositional account and the problem of error, the wrong-community objection, the quus function on a previously unseen sum, or whether the challenge can be applied to the word means itself), and record in advance what each side predicts that case will show. If part of the disagreement is merely verbal, say which part and why. Don't adjudicate: the point is a text both of us could sign, with the real disagreement made exact.

Oh, and please write it in British English, and no reading lists or citations - we're after the substance, not the bibliography.
