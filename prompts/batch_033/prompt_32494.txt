hey — I'm a philosophy PhD student trying to nail down my metaethics views before I have to write a prospectus, and I'm worried my commitments on moral relativism might not actually hang together. I'd love an audit from someone who can spot tensions I'm missing.

here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm a relativist about moral truth. Moral claims are true or false only relative to a cultural framework — there are no framework-independent moral facts.

(2) The strongest argument for relativism, in my view, is the argument from persistent moral disagreement. Across cultures and history, people disagree deeply about moral matters, and these disagreements don't seem resolvable by appeal to shared standards or further evidence. The best explanation is that there are no objective moral facts to converge on.

(3) I want to hold onto the idea that moral progress is genuine. When a society abolishes slavery or extends the franchise, that's not just change — it's improvement. I think I can cash this out in terms of a tradition's own internal standards: a society can come to better realize its own commitments over time or correct inconsistencies in its framework.

(4) I'm not bothered by the fact that some moral norms (like prohibitions on indiscriminate killing of in-group members) show up in virtually every society. I think these are explained by shared human nature and common practical problems, not by objective moral facts.

(5) I think relativism has the attractive feature that it supports tolerance. Not that relativism logically entails tolerance — I know that's a terrible argument — but that relativism undercuts the main justification people reach for when they're intolerant, which is the conviction that the other side is just objectively wrong. If you can't claim that kind of epistemic authority, you're more likely to actually listen.

the thing is, I've got a nagging feeling that some of these are pulling against each other in ways I haven't fully thought through. I can't tell which tensions are real and which are just superficial. If you could go through these, flag which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, rank them by severity, and tell me which single revision would do the most to clean things up, I'd be really grateful. I'm not attached to any particular one — I just want to know where the problem is.
