ok so I'm a software engineer, never studied philosophy formally, but I've been reading GEB (Gödel Escher Bach) on my commute and the strange loop thing has been living rent-free in my head. The way Hofstadter sets it up: Gödel figures out how to make a formal system talk about itself — you encode statements as numbers, then construct a formula that basically says "this statement has no proof." And that's the incompleteness theorem. But then Hofstadter's like, what if consciousness is the same kind of trick? Symbols in the brain that start referring to their own reference, tangling up into an "I" that feels like it's outside the system even though it's made of the system.

I want a villanelle about this. The form is literally perfect: two refrains that alternate and then collide in the final couplet, which is exactly how the strange loop works — the mechanical level and the emergent self keep circling each other and at some point you can't pull them apart. One refrain should be the "Gödel" side (the formal system, the unprovable sentence, the rules that can't ground themselves) and the other should be the "consciousness" side (the felt self, the inner voice, the thing that seems to transcend the substrate). The villanelle's structure should do the philosophical work: the refrains taking turns is the two levels of description passing each other, and the final meeting is the strange loop closing.

I don't need it to be a perfect villanelle in terms of strict meter or whatever, but the rhyme scheme and refrains should be recongizable. And it should be accurate enough that someone who's read GEB would nod along, not wince at some mangled version of the idea.
