ok so we're doing this unit on liberty in my gov class and we read excerpts from Berlin's "Two Concepts of Liberty" and then my teacher threw in this third thing called republican liberty — the idea that freedom isn't just about whether someone's interfering with you right now, but whether anyone has the power to interfere arbitrarily. and i've been trying to figure out what i actually think by going through cases and seeing where my gut lands.

here's my verdicts so far. law says i can't buy alcohol till i'm 21 — honestly doesn't feel like a freedom violation. law says i can't read certain books — that feels like a real freedom violation even if i wasn't gonna read them. my friend's dad works an at-will job where he can be fired anytime for no reason; he says he's free because he chose it and can quit, but i feel like his boss has a kind of power over him that makes him unfree, even if the boss is nice and never actually fires anyone. government takes part of my paycheck for taxes to fund schools and roads — doesn't feel like a freedom violation. government says i have to work a specific job they assign me — massive freedom violation, even though it's also interference like taxes. someone living under a lazy dictator who doesn't bother interfering with anyone's life but could at any moment — that person isn't free, even though nothing's actually happening to them. seatbelt laws — i feel like this IS a freedom violation but a really small and justified one, like i can't get worked up about it. law requiring businesses to serve everyone regardless of religion — i'm stuck on this one because it restricts the owner's choice but protects the customer's freedom, and my gut says it's not a freedom violation overall but i can't articulate why.

i feel like there's gotta be a principle that captures most of these, or at least shows me which ones fit together and which ones i have to give up. like, interference alone can't be the thing — the lazy dictator and the at-will job both feel like unfreedom without any actual interference. but not all interference is equally bad either, and some interference (taxes, seatbelts) barely registers. is there a theory of liberty that gets all or most of these, and which of my verdicts does it force me to abandon? also, what would that theory say about a case i keep going back and forth on: a parent who homeschools their kid and tightly controls everything the kid learns about the world — is that kid free?
