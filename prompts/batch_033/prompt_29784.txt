I've been trying to sort out what I think about transitivity of 'better than' and I want a consistency check before I embarass myself at a workshop. Here are my commitments:

(i) 'Better than' is transitive. If A > B and B > C, then A > C. I take this to be almost definitional — if you've got an intransitive ordering, you don't have a 'better than' relation, you've got something else.

(ii) Spectrum arguments like Parfit's repugnant conclusion and Temkin's tradeoff cases show that something is deeply weird about how we compare value across large differences. I don't think these are just Sorites cases in disguise.

(iii) When I compare adjacent items on a spectrum, my pairwise intuitions feel reliable. When I compare the endpoints directly, I don't trust that judgment nearly as much — the gap is too big.

(iv) The money pump argument shows that intransitive preferences are practically irrational. But I think the problem with intransitivity is deeper than pragmatics — it's metaphysical. If 'better than' were intransitive, there'd be no fact of the matter about which of three options is best.

(v) I think the right response to spectrum arguments is probably to reject one of the pairwise comparisons somewhere along the chain, even if I can't say which one. The chain has a weak link even if I can't find it.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. I'm especially interested in whether (iii) and (v) are actually compatible — they feel like they might not be. And I want to know whether (ii) is doing any work or whether I could drop it and keep everything else. Rank the problems by severity and tell me what the cheapest fix is.
