In my school's debate about replacing first-past-the-post with ranked-choice voting for student council elections, a classmate ended the discussion by saying, "Every voting system can be gamed, so there's no point comparing them." I know there is a theorem in social choice theory—the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem—behind this slogan, but I suspect he is overreading it. Could you lay out, as a list, the distinct precise claims that sentence could be making—about which voting methods are covered, what counts as "gaming," how often and by whom, and what follows for choosing among systems—and then say for each reading whether it is trivial, substantive but defensible, or false, and whether it would actually support his conclusion that comparing voting systems is pointless?
