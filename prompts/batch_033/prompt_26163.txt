so i'm putting together the last session of a grad seminar on epistemic justification and i want to close with something that isn't just another paper. we've spent weeks on foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism — and honestly the students get the arguments but i dont think they feel the regress, you know? like the way it just keeps going, the way every stopping point invites the same question again. so i had this idea: a villanelle. the form is basically the regress problem in verse — two refrains that keep returning no matter what you do, and then in the last two lines they finally have to face each other directly.

here's what i'm picturing. the first refrain should be the skeptic's question — something like "And what's your reason for that?" or "But how do you know?" — the thing that keeps the chain moving. the second refrain should be the foundationalist's answer — something like "Some things are simply known" or "It stops at what is given." they alternate through the poem, and in the closing couplet they land side by side in a way that shows the foundationalist's answer is itself vulnerable to the very question it was supposed to stop. someone who knows the literature should recognize the structure of the problem in the structure of the poem, not just in the vocabulary.

a couple constraints: dont name-drop philosophers or -isms in the poem itself — let the refrains and the imagery do the work. and please make it scan. i'd rather have a slightly looser villanelle that reads like real poetry than a mechanically perfect one that sounds like a mnemonic device. oh and if you can work in the coherentist move somewhere — the idea that maybe the whole thing is a circle and thats fine — as a kind of shadow third position that the form itself hints at but doesn't state, that would be perfect.
