I spent most of last night arguing with a friend who is a committed type-identity theorist — the kind who insists that pain just is C-fiber firing, full stop, and that talk of multiple realizability is philosophers making a fetish of logical possibility. I kept pushing the standard cases: the silicon-brain Martian who winces and withdraws and says "that hurts" in all the same circumstances we would, the octopus with its radically different neural architecture, the gradually-replaced-neuron thought experiment where each neuron is swapped for a silicon functional equivalent one by one. He kept coming back to the claim that none of these would feel the way pain feels to us, that there is something essentially biological about the qualitative character, that the stuff matters and not just what the stuff does. At some point I realized we were talking past each other because I was pointing at the causal role and he was pointing at the substrate, and neither of us had a way to make the other see that the pattern is what matters, not the material. It occurred to me on the way home that a sestina might be the right form to make the point. The sestina takes six end-words and recycles them through six stanzas in a fixed permutation, so the same words keep reappearing in different syntactic and semantic contexts, doing different work each time while remaining recognizably themselves. That is almost exactly the functionalist claim about mental states: the same functional role, realized in different substrates, is the same mental state. The form itself says what the argument couldn't.

I would like you to write a sestina about functionalism and multiple realizability where the six end-words are drawn from the vocabulary of the debate — words like "pain," "fire," "same," "stuff," "role," "feel," or whatever set you think works best — and where the poem's movement through its stanzas dramatizes the idea that what matters is the causal pattern, not the material it runs on. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of mind would recognize the position and its standard moves (the Martian, the octopus, the replacement argument, the charge of substrate chauvinism) rather than merely name-dropping them. Along with the poem, I would like a short note in continuous prose — no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, just paragraph form the way one would write a letter — that takes a definite position on where the poem takes liberties with the philosophical ideas or leans on a contested reading of functionalism, since I know the sestina form will force some compromises and I want to know honestly where the metaphor strains and where it holds.
