I'm a high school student who has been reading about ethics on my own, and I keep running into a cluster of puzzles that I can't find a settled name for. I'm hoping you can help me identify the right terminology — but more importantly, I want to understand how the different pieces of this cluster fit together and where their boundaries are.

The basic problem appears whenever you try to apply utilitarian reasoning to a universe that contains infinitely many people. Here is one version: suppose the universe is infinite and contains infinitely many sentient beings. You have the opportunity to take an action that makes a billion of them slightly happier. The total well-being in the universe was infinite before you acted, and it is infinite after — infinity plus a billion is still infinity. So, if you judge actions by their effect on the total sum, your action seems to have made no difference at all. But that conclusion feels absurd; surely helping a billion people matters.

Here is a second version that feels related but might be a different problem underneath. Suppose you are comparing two possible futures, both containing infinitely many people. In Future A, the well-being levels across the population go 1, 2, 3, 4, and so on. In Future B, the well-being levels go 2, 4, 6, 8, and so on — each person in B is exactly twice as well-off as the corresponding person in A. Both sequences diverge to infinity, so if all you can do is compare total sums, the two futures come out equal. But Future B dominates Future A person by person: for every individual position, B delivers strictly more well-being than A. It seems obvious that B is better, yet the summing framework cannot capture that.

I have seen people online dismiss this whole area with a shrug and the phrase "infinity is weird," but I am fairly sure that professional philosophers have carved it up more carefully than that. Is there an established name for this family of problems? If the two versions I described belong under different concepts, I would like to know what those concepts are and how they relate to each other. And if there are standard approaches to resolving them — for instance, ways of comparing infinite sets that go beyond just adding up totals — I would like to know what those approaches are called and what distinguishes one from another. I am less interested in a bare label than in understanding the conceptual landscape: what the core difficulty is, which cases it applies to, and where its edges are.
