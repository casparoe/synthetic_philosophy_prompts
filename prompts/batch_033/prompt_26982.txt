Write a dialogue between two people who disagree about the torture vs. dust specks problem. The setup: you have to choose between (A) one person being tortured intensely for 50 years, or (B) a truly enormous number of people—billions, maybe more—each getting a single dust speck in their eye, a momentary irritation. One character thinks the dust specks are worse because enough tiny bads add up; the other thinks torture is worse and no pileup of specks can touch it. Neither should be a strawman. I want them to actually respond to each other's moves, not just state positions. At some point they should dig into whether the disagreement is about the metaphysics of value (can harms be summed across persons?) or about the epistemology (are we just bad at imagining huge numbers?) or about something else entirely. Keep it tight—maybe six to eight exchanges.
