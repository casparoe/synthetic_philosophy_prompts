so i've been going down rabbit holes about conspiracy theories for a while now — not the theories themselves but like, how we should think about them. and i've ended up with a set of views that i *think* make sense together but i'm not totally sure. wondering if someone can check my consistency here.

here's where i'm at:

(1) most conspiracy theories are false, and the people who get deep into them are usually making some kind of reasoning error — cherry-picking, ignoring base rates, that sort of thing.

(2) but i also think that calling something a "conspiracy theory" shouldn't do any argumentative work. like, the fact that a belief gets labeled that way isn't itself evidence against it. each claim should be evaluated on the actual evidence.

(3) when a government or powerful institution has both the means and a clear motive to cover something up, we should raise our evidentiary standards for accepting their denials. not reject them outright, but require more before we take their word for it.

(4) i think the official story about the JFK assassination is probably wrong. not necessarily that i know what happened instead, but i don't buy that Oswald acted alone. there was likely either a second shooter or some kind of cover-up beyond what the Warren Commission concluded.

(5) i also hold that it's irrational to believe something mainly because the alternative — the official story — feels too tidy, too convenient, or too self-serving for the people in power. "it's exactly what they'd want you to believe" isn't a good reason on its own.

(6) when expert communities reach near-unanimous concensus on something, that's very strong evidence that it's true, even if i personally can't follow all the technical details. expertise matters and i should defer to it in areas i don't understand.

so that's the set. i can feel some friction between some of these but i can't quite put my finger on which pairs are actually in tension versus which just *seem* like they are. if you could go through and flag the real tensions, rank them by how serious they are, and tell me what i'd have to give up to fix each one, that'd be really helpful. and if there's one revision that would clean up the most problems at once, i'd want to know that too.
