I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on rationality and practical reason — about 16 students, one 80-minute session. I want an in-class game that makes them feel the force of the idea that it can be rational to make yourself irrational, along the lines of Parfit's hitchhiker case (you are stranded in the desert; a driver will rescue you only if you promise to pay later; but once rescued, paying is against your interests; so the driver does not stop — unless you can somehow bind yourself to keeping the promise even when keeping it is irrational at the moment of action).

Design a multi-round game. Early rounds should trap them in the bad equilibrium — no trust, no cooperation — so they experience the problem directly. Then introduce a mechanism: a commitment device, a character-selection stage, or something equivalent, that lets players voluntarily constrain their future choices in a way that would be irrational at the moment of action but rational from the ex ante perspective because it changes how others treat them. The mechanism should be optional, so students can see that those who use it do better than those who do not, and also better than players in the baseline rounds.

Give me rules, materials, a payoff scheme that does not need real money (points or tokens are fine), and a debrief. The debrief should map what happened in each round onto the distinction between synchronic rationality (what it is rational to do at the moment of choice) and diachronic rationality (what it is rational to do across time, including what dispositions to cultivate). It should also address: what does the game leave out that matters for the philosophical argument — in particular, the difference between a penalty that changes the payoffs (making cooperation rational at the moment of action) and a genuine case of acting against your interests because of a disposition you chose earlier? And what will predictably go wrong when I run this — will students cooperate out of social pressure in the baseline rounds, and if so, how should I handle it?
