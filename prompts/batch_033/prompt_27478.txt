ok so I've been going back and forth on the realism debate in mechanistic interpretability and I think I need to step back and get clear on what the criteria even are before I pick a side. 

the question is basically: when we find a feature or a direction in a neural network using some interpretability method (SAEs, probes, whatever), are we discovering a representation that's really "in" the network, or are we just imposing structure that's useful for us to talk about the network but doesn't correspond to anything the network itself is doing?

I want the desiderata. What does an adequate theory of neural network representations have to deliver? Not a theory itself — I want the spec that any candidate theory has to meet before I even evaluate it. 

Specifically I want:

- The cases it has to handle. Like, a single neuron that fires for multiple unrelated concepts (polysemanticity), features that only show up in superposition, the case where a probe finds something but ablation doesn't change behavior, the case where two different methods find incompatible features and both seem well-supported. What are all the awkward cases the theory can't just wave away?

- The distinctions it has to respect. For example, the difference between "this feature predicts behavior" and "the network actually uses this feature." Or the difference between a representation being real versus being useful to talk about. What distinctions does the theory collapse at its peril?

- What counts as evidence for or against realism here? If I say "yes, the features are real representations," what kind of thing would prove me wrong? And if I say "no, they're just our projections," what would prove me wrong? A theory that can't be wrong isn't a theory.

- Which of these desiderata are negotiable and which aren't. I suspect some of what people demand from a theory of representations is actually just their favorite interpretability method's assumptions dressed up as requirements. Flag those.

- And the big one: are any pairs of these requirements jointly unsatisfiable? If the list itself contains a contradiction, I want to know that before I spend six months trying to satisfy all of it.

Don't score existing views against the list — I'll do that myself. I just want the list, with the case or argument behind each item, and a clear statement of which ones would actually rule out which kinds of positions.
