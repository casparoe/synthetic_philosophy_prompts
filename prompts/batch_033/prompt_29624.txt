I had a long argument with a friend last night about her moral views, and I still cannot state the rule behind her verdicts. Over the years she has given firm answers to a series of trade-off cases, all involving one person being made to suffer so that others receive a benefit or are spared a harm. Her answers were as follows.

1. Inflicting a mild five-minute headache on one person in order to give one other person five minutes of mild pleasure: impermissible; the pleasure is too small to justify the headache.
2. Inflicting a mild five-minute headache on one person in order to give a billion people an hour of intense pleasure each: permissible.
3. Inflicting extreme torture on one person in exchange for any amount of pleasure, however large and however widely distributed: impermissible; no amount of pleasure could justify it.
4. Inflicting a broken arm, a moderate and clearly non-torturous pain, on one person in exchange for preventing a million other people from suffering comparable broken arms: permissible.
5. Inflicting a severe hour-long migraine, intense but not torture, on one person in exchange for an enormous amount of pleasure for billions of people: permissible, though she hesitated before giving the answer.

She describes herself as a negative utilitarian and insists that her verdicts all follow from one simple principle, but I am not convinced that they do.

The new case is this: inflicting extreme torture on one person in exchange for preventing two other people from being tortured in exactly the same way. When I raised it, she said she needed to think and would not answer on the spot.

What should we expect her to say if she is being consistent with her earlier verdicts? What are the candidate rules that fit those five verdicts, which of them diverge on the new case, and what further case would most cleanly settle which rule she is actually running?
