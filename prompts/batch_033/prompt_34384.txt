I'm stuggling with a passage from Wittgenstein's Philosophical Investigations that Kripke makes a huge deal out of in his book on rules and private language. Here's the passage, from §201:

"This was our paradox: no course of action could be determined by a rule, because every course of action can be made out to accord with the rule. The answer was: if everything can be made out to accord with the rule, then it can also be made out to conflict with it. And so there would be neither accord nor conflict here."

I get the basic idea—that any finite set of applications can be interpreted to fit multiple incompatible rules—but I'm stuck on what Wittgenstein means by "the answer" in the second half. Who is giving this answer? Is it Wittgenstein's own response to the paradox, or is it the skeptic doubling down? And when he says "there would be neither accord nor conflict here," is he dissolving the problem or just restating it in a way that makes it look even worse? I've heard people say this passage is the key to the whole Kripkenstein reading but everytime I read it I feel like I'm missing the move he's actually making. Can you walk me through what's happening in these sentences, what the "answer" is supposed to be doing, and whether the paradox is being embraced or defused?
