I spent my career in condensed matter physics, and since retiring I've been reading around in philosophy of science. The reductionism versus emergence debate has me stuck in a way I didnt expect. The positions have been stable for decades as far as I can tell: one side says everything is in principle fixed by the microphysics and higher-level talk is just convenient shorthand; the other side says some phenomena — phase transitions, superconductivity, maybe eventually consciousness — are genuinely novel and not just compressed descriptions of the parts. What bothers me is that both sides seem to accept exactly the same physics. Nobody thinks you can actually derive a phase transition from the Schrödinger equation for 10^23 particles, and nobody denies that the renormalization group gives a principled story about universality. So why has the philosophical argument not moved? Is it a verbal disagreement about the words reduction and emergence, a disagreement about what an explanation has to do, or are the two camps answering different questions — one ontological, one about our epistemic limits? Or is there a hidden empirical question doing the work? I want a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement rather than a survey, and an honest answer about what, if anything, would count as progress.
