Before I read any more theories of psychiatric classification, I want the full specification of what such a theory must get right. Write the desiderata: the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must handle, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the results from other disciplines it must be consistent with. I am particularly interested in how the list handles Hacking's looping effects — the way classified people respond to classifications, altering the very phenomena the classifications were meant to capture — and whether this forces any requirement that a purely realist theory of psychiatric kinds cannot meet. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and flag which are non-negotiable and which reflect commitments of particular camps (e.g., a commitment to value-free taxonomy, or to the claim that psychiatric kinds are natural kinds in the same sense as chemical elements). If some subset of the desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied — for instance, if respecting looping effects rules out the stability one wants from a diagnostic category — say so. A table at the end is fine, but I want the reasoning behind each entry.
