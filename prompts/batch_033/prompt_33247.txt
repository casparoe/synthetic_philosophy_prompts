A friend and I spent last evening going back and forth about the MU puzzle, and by the end he had cornered me in an argument I cannot find my way out of. The puzzle: begin with the string MI and try to reach MU using four rules — append U to any string ending in I; double any string after its initial M (Mx becomes Mxx); replace III with U; delete UU. The standard solution says MU is unreachable because the number of I's starts at 1 and, modulo 3, never becomes 0 under the rules, whereas MU has zero I's.

His argument runs like this. (1) A proof that MU is underivable is a claim about every possible derivation in the MIU system. (2) The MIU system itself consists only of strings and rewrite rules; it cannot express facts about numbers or divisibility by 3. (3) The standard solution proves MU unreachable by appealing to arithmetic that lies outside the system. (4) If a proof appeals to resources the system itself cannot express, then it is not a proof about that system but about a larger system. (5) So the standard solution does not prove that MU is underivable in the MIU system. (6) By the same reasoning, no external metamathematical argument could establish a fact about a formal system, and the whole subject would collapse. That last step seems absurd, but I cannot see which earlier step to reject. Which step goes wrong, and what does rejecting it cost? Tell me how confident you are in your diagnosis, and single out the point you are least sure of.
