I was shown the following argument in a reading group, and I have not been able to locate the step that fails, though I am certain the conclusion is false. I want a diagnosis of the argument rather than a survey of the surrounding literature.

(1) If I mean addition by +, then my meaning addition must consist in some fact.
(2) If my meaning addition consists in some fact, that fact must be one of three kinds: facts about my past applications of the sign, facts about my occurrent mental states when I use it, or facts about my behavioral dispositions.
(3) My past applications are finite, and any finite set of applications is compatible with indefinitely many functions. For example, suppose I have never computed a sum involving any number of 57 or greater. Then every past use of + by me is equally compatible with addition and with quaddition, where x quus y equals x plus y if x and y are both below 57, and equals 5 otherwise. So my past applications do not constitute my meaning addition rather than quaddition.
(4) My occurrent mental states are no better. A mental image, a formula, or a feeling of what comes next is just another item that must itself be interpreted, and any such item can be taken to fit more than one rule. It therefore cannot constitute my meaning addition rather than some other function.
(5) My dispositions fare no better. They cover only finitely many cases, they are silent about computations too large for me to carry out, they are consistent with my making systematic mistakes, and in any case a disposition records what I would do, not what I ought to do.
(6) Therefore none of the three kinds of facts constitutes my meaning addition.
(7) If none of those three kinds of facts constitutes my meaning addition, then no fact constitutes my meaning addition.
(8) Therefore my meaning addition consists in no fact; that is, there is no fact of the matter that I mean addition.
(9) But there is a fact of the matter. I mean addition by +, and I am more certain of that than I am of any step in the argument.

So at least one of (1), (2), (3), (4), (5), or (7), or the inference to (6), must be rejected. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I am least willing to give up (9); beyond that, I want to know what each available way out commits me to. Please also say how confident you are in your diagnosis and single out the point you are least sure of.
