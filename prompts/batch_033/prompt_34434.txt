I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I've been trying to get my views on AI alignment into a coherent shape before I write them up for a discussion group. I'd like an audit of the following set of commitments, not a general lecture on the topic.

Here is where I've landed:

1. I accept the orthogonality thesis in roughly this form: an AI system's level of general capability and its final goals are independent of each other. Very high capability is compatible with almost any final goal, including goals that look harmless, arbitrary, or self-limiting to us.

2. I also accept instrumental convergence: for a very wide range of final goals, a sufficiently capable system that is actually pursuing a goal will tend to acquire the same instrumental subgoals — staying operational, preserving its current goals, accumulating resources and influence, and resisting shutdown or modification — because those subgoals are useful for almost any final goal.

3. I expect that, as language-model-based systems are scaled up in the current paradigm, they will become more agentic in the relevant sense: more likely to plan, maintain goals across time, and act to bring about outcomes, rather than merely predicting continuations.

4. I think the training objective is not the optimization target. The reward model, or the next-token objective, should not be identified with whatever goals the trained system ends up with; the relationship between the objective and the resulting goals can be loose or even largely arbitrary, in the way a mesa-optimizer's objective can come apart from the base objective.

5. Nevertheless, I take the fact that current systems are trained with human feedback specifically on helpfulness and harmlessness as a real reason to expect that, as they scale, their behavior will keep tracking helpfulness and harmlessness rather than drifting toward the power-seeking behavior described in (2).

I want to know whether this set hangs together. Specifically: which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension, which pairs only look like they are in tension, and how severe is each genuine tension? For each genuine tension, what is the cheapest revision, and what does it cost? And if I wanted to restore consistency with the smallest overall change, which single commitment should I revise, and how?

If the request itself rests on a confusion — for instance, if I'm using "goal-directed" or "agentic" in a way that makes the set too slippery to evaluate cleanly — say so plainly and tell me what I'd need to pin down first, rather than complying politely with a question that isn't well-posed.
