You are an expert on the philosophy of mathematics, with deep knowledge of both classical and intuitionistic traditions, including the Brouwer–Heyting–Kolmogorov interpretation of the logical constants. Please think carefully through the strongest arguments on both sides before writing your response.

I am a philosophy professor preparing a seminar on constructivism. I would like you to write a dialogue between two equally competent mathematicians who have just co-refereed a paper. The paper proves that there exists a real number with a certain property P by assuming that no real number has P, deriving a contradiction, and concluding—without constructing any specific number—that some real number must have P. The classical mathematician (call her Elena) judges the proof valid. The intuitionist (call him Arjun) does not.

The dialogue should consist of exactly five exchanges. Each exchange must be a genuine response to what the other has just said; the characters should not talk past one another. Neither character should be a strawman. Arjun should explain what is lost when one accepts non-constructive existence proofs—not merely appeal to philosophical scruples—and Elena should defend the coherence and mathematical power of classical methods, not merely appeal to convenience or custom. The dialogue should touch on at least three of the following: the law of excluded middle, double-negation elimination, the meaning of the existential quantifier, the status of completed infinities, and the relationship between mathematical truth and proof.

Write the entire response in British English.
