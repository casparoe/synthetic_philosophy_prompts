So I joined my department's hiring committee this year and I keep noticing this pattern that's driving me nuts. We have twelve people, all smart, all well-intentioned, all reading the same files. But when we discuss candidates, the group somehow lands on worse judgments than what I'd expect if you just averaged everyone's private rankings before the meeting. It's like the discussion makes us dumber, not smarter. The weird part is that adding more people seems to make it worse — we had a subcommittee of four that made better calls than the full group of twelve. I vaguely remember something from undergrad about a theorem that says more voters equals better decisions, but that's clearly not what's happening here. Is there a name for this — the thing where adding more people to a group decision makes the outcome worse even though each person is competent? And I don't just mean groupthink or conformity pressure, because we actually disagree a lot. It's more like the information gets pooled in a way that amplifies our shared blind spots rather than canceling out our individual errors. Does this have a name, and how does it relate to that theorem I'm half-remembering?
