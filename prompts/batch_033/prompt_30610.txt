I'm putting together a unit on the demarcation problem and I keep finding that people argue past each other because they never say what a criterion of demarcation is for or what it has to get right. So before I let students loose on Popper, write me the desiderata: what does any adequate account of the science/pseudoscience line have to deliver, and why.

For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that forces it. I want the list to be genuinely in the weeds, so make sure it has to grapple with at least these: astrology (which sometimes does make falsifiable-looking claims), homeopathy (which has survived randomized trials it shouldn't have), string theory (mathematically rigorous, empirically empty so far), psychoanalysis (can explain everything), young-earth creationism (very falsifiable, very wrong, and yet clearly not science in the sense we care about), and the fact that chemistry grew out of alchemy and plate tectonics was fringe for decades. Don't just list the cases — say which demand each one imposes on the criterion.

Also I want you to sort the demands into two piles: the ones that are real requirements any criterion must meet, and the ones that are just hangovers from Popper or the positivists or from the court cases (like: must the criterion be a necessary-and-sufficient condition? must it be statable without any empirical premises? must it classify every historical figure correctly? must it be usable by a judge in a creationism trial?). And tell me which pairs of the real requirements you think can't be jointly satisfied — if the answer is "most of them," then say whether that pushes toward the Laudan line that the problem is a mess and we should stop asking for a single criterion, or toward something weaker.

Then, briefly, score falsificationism against the list. I don't need a tour of every contender, just enough to see which single requirement cuts deepest against Popper's version and what a Popperian has to give up to survive it.
