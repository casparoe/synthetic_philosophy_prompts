I sit on the internal safety review board of a lab that builds LLM-based coding agents: systems that are assigned long-horizon tasks and allowed to operate a computer semi-autonomously, taking actions such as editing files, running programs, and issuing network requests. A team has submitted a proposal to add a shutdown mechanism before the next deployment. Under the proposal, the agent would be fine-tuned with reinforcement learning so that whenever a designated operator sends a STOP token through the same interface the agent uses for other instructions, the agent receives positive reward for halting all side-effecting actions within a short window and entering a waiting state, and receives negative reward for continuing. The proposal also imposes a heavy penalty on any action that tampers with the STOP channel itself. The team argues that this makes the agent corrigible with respect to shutdown and that deploying with the mechanism is strictly safer than deploying without it. Another board member replies that the proposal merely makes being shut down one more goal among the agent's goals: it gives the agent a positive reason to bring about or exploit the shutdown condition rather than simply not resisting it, and it does nothing to rule out other ways of making shutdown ineffective that the tampering penalty fails to cover. The board is split: some see a sensible incremental safeguard, others see safety theater that adds a new failure mode. The board must decide whether to approve the proposal as written, approve it with modifications, or reject it. Please draft the decision memo I will circulate before Friday's meeting: it should recommend one concrete course of action with a stated rationale, and it should honestly engage the strongest argument for the losing side.
