I've spent the last few years reading the predictive processing literature — Clark, Hohwy, Seth, Friston — and I'm comfortable with the vocabulary: generative models, precision-weighting, prediction-error minimization, Markov blankets, active inference, the lot. I've also read Dennett's *Consciousness Explained*, but I find that when I try to think about his heterophenomenology in predictive-processing terms, I keep sliding between "this maps perfectly" and "these are just different projects."

I want you to do a systematic translation. Restate Dennett's heterophenomenological method — the stance that treats subjects' verbal reports about their experiences as data about what they believe their experiences to be, not as direct reports of qualia or inner goings-on — in the vocabulary of predictive processing. Be precise: what in the PP framework corresponds to the subject's report? To the intentional object of the report? To the bracketing of the truth of the report? To the heterophenomenologist's neutral stance toward whether the subject is confabulating? To the construction of the heterophenomenological world of the subject?

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of heterophenomenology translate cleanly, which parts require stretching or distortion to fit, and which parts have no expression at all in the predictive-processing vocabulary? In particular: does the heterophenomenologist's agnosticism about whether there really is an experience corresponding to the report map onto anything in PP, or does PP's ontology already take a stand on that question? And if there is residue, is it because predictive processing lacks resources that heterophenomenology needs, or because heterophenomenology smuggles in assumptions that predictive processing rightly refuses?

I want to know whether my sense that these frameworks are natural allies is right, or whether the translation reveals a deeper incompatibility.
