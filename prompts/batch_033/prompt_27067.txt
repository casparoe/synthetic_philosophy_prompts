I'm on my university's sexual misconduct hearing panel and we're stuck on a case. Two students hooked up at a party. The respondent told the complainant they were single — flat-out lied, they've actually got a long-term girlfriend back home. The complainant says they never would've consented if they'd known, and that the lie makes the whole encounter nonconsensual. The respondent's defense is basically: people lie during hookups all the time — about their job, their age, whether they're "looking for something serious" — and we don't call that sexual assault, so why is this different?

Our university policy says consent has to be "informed, voluntary, and freely given," but it never defines what "informed" actually requires. And that's the whole fight. Half the panel thinks any deception about a material fact — anything the other person would've considered a dealbreaker — vitiates consent. The other half thinks that standard is insane and would turn a huge fraction of ordinary sexual encounters into policy violations, and that only deception about the sex act itself (like lying about a condom, or about what act is being performed) should count.

I'm supposed to draft the panel's decision memo by Friday. I need a clear recommendation — uphold the complaint or dismiss it — with reasoning that takes both sides seriously. Don't give me a survey of options; I need a commited call. And write it in normal American English, not like a legal brief.
