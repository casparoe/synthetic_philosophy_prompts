Draft the joint statement that a representative of the "control-safety" camp (which holds that AI safety efforts should prioritize measures that assume models may be misaligned — containment, monitoring, capability restrictions, and scalable oversight — and that these measures can be made robust enough to secure risky deployments) and a representative of the "alignment-necessity" camp (which holds that safety efforts should prioritize making models themselves aligned, and that relying on control measures to contain a misaligned system is a dangerous bet that will fail against sufficiently capable models) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The statement should include:

1. A numbered list of claims both parties can endorse, including claims about the difficulty of the alignment problem, the nature of the risks, and the role each approach plays in a complete safety portfolio.
2. A disagreements table with columns: (a) the exact claim at issue, phrased neutrally; (b) the control-safety view, phrased so its own proponents would accept it; (c) the alignment-necessity view, phrased so its own proponents would accept it; and (d) whether the disagreement is substantive or partly verbal, with a note on any terminological slippage the collaboration uncovered.
3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would shift at least one side's position. For each crux, state what kind of evidence or argument both sides agree would bear on it, and register each side's prediction about what that evidence would show. Cruxes to consider include: whether control measures exhibit sharply diminishing returns above a capability threshold; whether alignment generalizes under distributional shift in ways that can be verified before deployment; and whether the incentives of developers make control-without-alignment an unstable equilibrium in practice.
4. A closing paragraph identifying any residual disagreement that the collaboration could not resolve, and the conditions under which one side's position would become untenable by the other side's lights.

Aim for formulations that are precise enough to guide research allocation, not vacuous enough to paper over the conflict. If any shared claim turns out to be hollow when pushed, say so rather than preserving it.
