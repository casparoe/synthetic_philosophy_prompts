In my AP Gov class we read pieces of Hannah Arendt’s Truth and Politics and her Pentagon Papers essay Lying in Politics, and the discussion has kind of stalled in a frustrating way. One side of the room says Arendt’s point is that there is a difference in kind between the old political lie, where a politician knows a checkable fact and hides or distorts it, and modern political lying, where the whole goal is to construct an alternate image of reality that people can live inside and even the liar can get absorbed in. The other side says that is overreading her, and that she is really just saying lying has always been a normal tool of politics, with modern PR and image-management making it faster and bigger but not new in kind. We keep talking pass each other, and my teacher won’t settle it.

My working theory is that the real disagreement is not about Arendt at all but about how to define lying: narrowly, as a false statement about some particular fact, or broadly, as any organized attempt to manage what people take reality to be. If that is the real disagreement, then the Arendt debate would not move until we settle the definition thing. But maybe I’m flattening things.
