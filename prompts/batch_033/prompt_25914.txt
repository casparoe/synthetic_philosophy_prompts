I'm a PhD student in metaphysics, and I've been spending the last few months working through the literature on the unity of the proposition — specifically the problem that F.H. Bradley raised about how a proposition's constituents manage to combine into a truth-evaluable whole rather than remaining a mere list. If I say "Socrates is wise," I seem to have at least two constituents, Socrates and wisdom, plus whatever connects them. But if that connector is itself a relation, we need further relations to connect it to the original constituents, and we're off on a regress. The central dispute is whether this regress is vicious or benign, and what kind of metaphysical machinery — if any — can stop it.

I've narrowed the live options to roughly three families: (a) the regress is vicious and we need a non-relational "tie" or mode of combination that is not itself a further constituent of the proposition (the line associated with early Russell and later with Bergmann, though I'm not asking for exegesis); (b) the regress is benign because it merely unpacks what is already implicit in the proposition's unity — the relation relates, and that's the end of it (something like Armstrong's response, or the view that the regress is harmless because each step is just a restatement of the same fact); (c) the unity of the proposition is primitive and unanalyzable, so the regress never gets started — propositions are fundamental unities and there is no deeper story about how their parts cohere (a primitivist view, sometimes associated with the idea that propositions are sui generis abstract entities). I'm aware there are further positions — instantiation as a non-relational tie, the identity theory of truth that dissolves the problem by identifying true propositions with facts, various Fregean approaches where the concept is unsaturated and the problem is thereby avoided — but I'm most interested in how you'd distribute credence across these three.

I'd like your credence that option (a) — the non-relational tie — is ultimately the right account of propositional unity, or the closest to right among the live options. Please decompose the number: how much weight do you put on the regress being genuinely vicious (rather than benign), on there being a coherent and non-mysterious notion of a non-relational tie, on the primitivist alternative being unacceptable for independent reasons (e.g., because it abandons the project of explaining unity rather than solving it), and on whatever other sub-questions you think the final credence turns on. Give me a number for each term, a brief justification, and explain how they combine. Then tell me which sub-question your overall credence is most sensitive to — the one where a convincing argument would shift your number the most — and what that argument would have to establish. Finally, say what evidence or consideration would most likely change your mind about the whole question: what would it take for you to move your credence by, say, twenty points or more?
