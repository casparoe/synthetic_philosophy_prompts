After forty years in a molecular genetics lab I've finally got time to read philosophy, and I keep coming back to the Kripkean idea that you couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg — that your particular origin is essential to being you, not just a contingent detail. Last weekend my grandkids made me watch Back to the Future, and now I can't unsee the two together; I've been thinking about it alot. The whole plot hangs on Marty McFly restoring his parents' courtship: he's interrupted their first meeting, his siblings fade out of a photograph, and during the Enchantment Under the Sea dance Marty himself starts to go transparent until George finally kisses Lorraine. The film is clearly dramatizing something like origin essentialism — Marty's existence depends on getting his origin back on track. But the Kripkean view is more specific than anything the film tracks. The film treats the crucial origin as a kiss at a high school dance, years before Marty was even conceived. It assumes that if the same two people get together, the same children result — at the end the family's circumstances are changed in all sorts of ways, but the same three kids are still there. A Kripkean should squint at that: if the evening went differently, or the courtship afterward ran differently, a different sperm might have met the egg, and the child would be a sibling of Marty's, not Marty.

So I'd like a careful reading of Back to the Future through origin essentialism. Where does the film genuinely dramatize the view — the fading, the urgency that the original events be restored — and where does it fall back on a weaker same-parents-same-kids assumption that origin essentialism actually rejects? Is the altered 1985 at the end compatible with Marty still existing, and does the film even have it's own resources to answer that? And does the movie, as a piece of popular storytelling, get at something about how we feel our own origins that the philosophical arguments don't — or does it just muddy the distinction between social origins and biological origins? I'm not after a bibliography or citations, just a close reading that takes both the film and the philosophy seriously.
