I'm writing a novel about a guy who takes a job at a payday-lending company because he's figured out he can donate most of his salary to bed-net charities where the impact is pretty rigorously measured. His sister, a community organizer, says he's rationalizing: the job does concrete harm to poor people, and by being good at it he helps the company expand. She also thinks the real problem is the laws that let such companies operate, and that his obsession with measurable results makes him ignore bigger, messier changes like policy fights. I want their argument to feel evenly matched. Is she actually right that his plan is self-defeating? How should he answer her without just changing the subject?
