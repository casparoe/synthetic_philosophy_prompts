I've been working through the two-envelope paradox for a paper I'm writing after a colleague brought it up at a workshop last month, and I've hit a wall. The paradox, in its standard form, runs as follows: you are shown two envelopes and told that one contains twice as much money as the other. You pick one, and before you open it, you reason that the other envelope contains either half or double what yours contains, each with probability one-half, so your expected gain from switching is positive — and since this reasoning applies symmetrically regardless of which envelope you picked, you would switch, and then switch again, ad infinitum. If you do open your envelope and find, say, ten dollars, the same reasoning seems to recommend switching, and then the same reasoning applied to the new envelope would recommend switching back. The literature is full of proposed resolutions: some locate the error in the assumption that the other envelope is equally likely to contain half or double, some argue that the expected-value calculation uses the wrong probability space, some appeal to the need for a proper prior distribution over the amounts, some argue that the paradox dissolves once you correctly model the conditional expectation, and some bite the bullet about the unopened case but deny that the opened case is paradoxical once you conditionalize correctly. I have read enough of these proposals to feel that they are often talking past each other, because they disagree — often implicitly — about what a resolution is even supposed to do.

Before I evaluate any particular resolution, I want to lay out the desiderata: what must an adequate dissolution of the two-envelope paradox accomplish? I am not asking for a solution to the paradox itself. I am asking for the specification of what a solution must deliver to count as having actually resolved the thing, rather than having changed the subject or solved a different problem. What are the phenomena a resolution must explain or explain away? For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement — why does a resolution that fails this test not count as a resolution at all? Which desiderata are non-negotiable, and which are optional or reflect the preferences of a particular interpretive camp? Are there pairs of desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied, such that the list itself reveals a tension at the heart of the paradox? And finally: which single desideratum does the most work in ruling out proposed resolutions, and why?
