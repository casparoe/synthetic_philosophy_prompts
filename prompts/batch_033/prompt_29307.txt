ok so for a reading group on suffering-focused ethics I've been asked to write down where I've landed, so the others can poke holes before I turn it into a term paper. I want a consistency check on the whole package, not a summary of the debate. Here's the list:

(a) Suffering has lexical priority over happiness. No amount of added well-being can justify creating or allowing even a small amount of suffering, so preventing and eventually abolishing suffering always comes first.

(b) The long-run goal should be the hedonistic imperative: use biotechnology — germline engineering, designer drugs, eventually rewriting motivational systems — to phase out the capacity for suffering across all sentient life, humans included, and replace the pain/pleasure axis with information-sensitive gradients of well-being so that motivation and salience are preserved without the capacity to suffer.

(c) We shouldn't get there by coercively rewriting people's brains against their will. Consent and autonomy matter, and a world where we override people's values to make them happy would be, in a morally important way, worse than one where we don't.

(d) Predation is one of the biggest sources of suffering on the planet, and we should eventually intervene to phase out predator–prey dynamics, even though that means altering the bodies and minds of nonhuman animals without their consent.

(e) I'm not an antinatalist. I think bringing new happy lives into existence can be a good thing, and the mere fact that a life will contain some suffering doesn't mean it wasn't worth starting.

(f) I don't buy the worry that a world without suffering would be shallow or meaningless. Art, love, curiosity, and achievement don't depend on pain, and gradients of well-being can preserve urgency, salience, and the difference between better and worse outcomes.

(g) I also reject wireheading: directly stimulating reward circuits, or putting everyone in a simulation that just feeds them pleasant experiences, is not what I'm after. The engineered states should track reality and keep their normal functional roles.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look like they're in tension, and how severe each real tension is. For each real tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision is, and then tell me which single change would clean up the most. I don't need a reading list or citations, just work with the views as stated. I'd rather have the problems ordered by severity then get a flat list, and I genuinely don't know where the weak spot is — my current feeling is that its a pretty tidy package and the main challenge is just people outside suffering-focused ethics thinking it's unhinged.
