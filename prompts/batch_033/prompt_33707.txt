I sit on my university's biological safety committee. We're reviewing a proposal from a lab that wants to synthesize and distribute a panel of gain-of-function influenza variants to accelerate vaccine development. The principal investigator argues that the work is responsibly designed, that the containment protocols meet the highest standard, and — this is the part I can't get out of my head — that if we block the project, a lab in a jurisdiction with looser oversight will do it instead, and we'll have forgone any chance to steer the science or set norms. Last night I had a long argument with a friend about the unilateralist's curse, and it's made me see the committee's situation in a new light: we are one node in a distributed network of institutional gatekeepers, each of whom can independently say yes, and the PI is effectively asking us to be the lowest-threshold node on the grounds that someone else will be if we aren't. Please draft the written opinion I'll circulate before Friday's vote. It should recommend whether to approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and it should engage the unilateralist's curse explicitly as a framework for thinking about the decision — including whether the curse cuts in favor of approval (better us than an unaccountable actor) or against it (we should not be the mechanism by which a collectively imprudent action is realized). I want an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side, and a bottom line the committee can actually act on.
