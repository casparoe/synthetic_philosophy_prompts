For a metaphysics seminar on modality, construct a decision tree that takes a respondent from their pre-theoretic judgments about particular modal claims to a named position on de re modality and transworld identity. The tree should include at least these leaves: Lewisian counterpart theory, ersatz counterpart theory, haecceitism (transworld identity via primitive thisness), qualitative essentialism (transworld identity via origin or kind-essence criteria), modal fictionalism, and one further position of your choice.

Each branching node must be a concrete case or a claim the respondent can assess without knowing the theoretical labels. At least three of the following cases (or close variants) must appear as nodes:

- Kripke's Humphrey objection: "Humphrey could have won the 1968 election" — is this claim literally about Humphrey, or is it about someone else who resembles him?
- A world qualitatively identical to ours except that you chose a different career: is the person in that world you?
- Two worlds that are qualitative duplicates but where the individuals swap qualitative roles — are these one possibility or two?
- The "I might have been a poet" case: if you say this, are you referring to yourself or to a counterpart?
- A world where you were never born but your parents had a different child with all your qualitative properties — is that child you?

Build the tree from the logical space: identify the key yes/no questions that carve out the possible positions, locate the named views within that space, and flag any logically possible combinations that no one defends, with a brief note on why (incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored). Also flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate and state what additional commitment would distinguish them.
