Question: "Explain the Sleeping Beauty problem. Present the halfer and thirder positions, giving the strongest argument for each. Then explain what each position says about Beauty's credence in heads after she is told that today is Monday, and why that matters."

Student answer:

"Sleeping Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday. A fair coin is tossed. If heads, she's woken Monday and interviewed, then sleeps till Wednesday. If tails, she's woken Monday, interviewed, then given an amnesia drug so she forgets that waking, and woken Tuesday and interviewed again. She knows the whole setup. When she wakes during the experiment, she's asked: what's your credence the coin landed heads?

The halfer says 1/2. David Lewis defends this: when she wakes, she learns nothing new about the world—she already knew she'd be woken at least once regardless of the coin. So her credence should stay at her Sunday value of 1/2. The coin is fair, after all.

The thirder says 1/3. Adam Elga argues that there are three possible awakening situations: heads-Monday, tails-Monday, and tails-Tuesday. These are subjectively indistinguishable, so by a principle of indifference she should give each equal credence. Since only one of them is heads, P(Heads)=1/3. Another way to see it: if the experiment were repeated many times, she'd be right about heads in only one-third of her awakenings; a rational bettor would break even wagering at 1:2 odds for heads.

If Beauty is told it's Monday, the halfer would still say 1/2—after all, the coin is fair and it's Monday either way, so the news doesn't discriminate between heads and tails. The thirder would also say 1/2 on Monday, since heads-Monday and tails-Monday are equally likely from the thirder perspective. So both camps agree on 1/2 when told it's Monday. That's important because it shows the disagreement is entirely about what happens to credence on Tuesday awakenings."
