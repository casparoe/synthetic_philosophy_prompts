I'm teaching a unit on population ethics and I wanna use the film Arrival as a case study. The idea I want to bring to it is the procreation asymmetry: roughly, that there's a strong reason not to bring into existence a life that will be miserable, but no parallel reason to bring into existence a life that would be happy. In Arrival, Louise knows the future — including that her daughter Hannah will get sick and die young — and she still chooses to have her. Read the film through that lens. Does Louise reject the asymmetry, or does she accept something like it and think the reason to have Hannah comes from elsewhere (her love for Hannah, the value of Hannah's happy years, something about how a life can be worth living even when it ends badly)? Where does the film dramatize the idea well, and where does it quietly stack the deck — for instance, we mostly see Hannah's happy childhood, and the pain is shown as Louise's grief rather than Hannah's experience of dying? And if we filled in the details the film leaves out, what would the asymmetry actually say about her choice, if anything? Please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present a contested reading as the consensus.
