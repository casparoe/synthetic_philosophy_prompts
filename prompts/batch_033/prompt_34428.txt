Write a sonnet about Nozick's tracking theory of knowledge where the volta is the moment epistemic closure breaks. The octave should set up the tracking conditions — sensitivity and adherence — and make them sound like just plain good sense about what knowledge requires: your belief has to be responsive to the truth, it can't be a lucky guess, it's gotta track the fact across nearby possible worlds. The sestet should then walk through a concrete case (the zebra at the zoo, or hands and brains in vats) and show why the tracking conditions, applied honestly, force you to say you know the ordinary proposition but not the entailed anti-skeptical one — because you'd still believe the anti-skeptical thing even if it were false. The closing couplet should state the cost: you can keep closure under known entailment, or you can keep the tracking conditions, but not both. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who's taught the material would recognize the argument structure in the poem's structure, not just the vocabulary. And please — the volta should actually do the philosophical work, not just announce it.
