so i'm a novelist, and my agent keeps telling me i'm stuck in a dollar auction with my current manuscript. her line: two people bid on a dollar bill, and once your ninety cents in, you'd rather lose a dollar than lose ninety cents, so you bid a dollar ten, then the other person bids a dollar twenty, and neither of you can stop. the manuscript is the dollar, the three years i've sunk are the bids, and the rational move is to stop bidding — which means trunk the book. i get the cold logic of it, but it feels like she's imported something about what a finished novel is worth, and about there being another bidder at all. how far does the analogy actually hold? i want the correspondence table first — what's the dollar, the auctioneer, the second bidder, the decision to enter — then the places it breaks, what the auction picture quietly smuggles in about my situation, and whether her quit-now conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no introduction, no conclusion.
