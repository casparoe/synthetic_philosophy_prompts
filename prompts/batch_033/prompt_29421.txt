Kierkegaard's Abraham suspends the ethical out of a private, absurd relation to God that cannot be mediated or justified to others. Where does the parallel genuinely illuminate, and where does it break down? Please attend especially to: the role of the singular individual before God in Kierkegaard versus Raskolnikov's rationalistic, utilitarian justification; the silence of Abraham versus Raskolnikov's inability to stay silent; and the question of whether the teleological suspension requires a telos that Raskolnikov's theory cannot supply.
