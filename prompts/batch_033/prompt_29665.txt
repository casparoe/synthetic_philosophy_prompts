I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on Rousseau's political and aesthetic thought, and I am drafting the final examination. I want one essay question on Rousseau's case against the theater in the Letter to M. d'Alembert on the Theatre (1758). The question should not be answerable by reciting the stock summary that Rousseau thought the theater inflames the passions or corrupts actors. It should force students to reconstruct the argument: why Rousseau denies that theater can improve a people's morals regardless of the moral content of the plays performed, what work is done by the spectator's distance and by amour-propre, and why he contrasts the theater unfavorably with the civic festival. It should also require students to explain how the Genevan political context shapes the argument rather than treating the Letter as a detached piece of aesthetics. Please write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain; in particular, the scheme should distinguish a student who merely reports that theater fails to edify from one who can explain why Rousseau takes that failure to be structural to the theatrical situation itself.
