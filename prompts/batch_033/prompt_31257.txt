I've never taken a philosophy class but I went down a rabbit hole about free will and responsibility a while back, and I'm supposed to write up where I stand for a small discussion group I joined. I've ended up with four things I beleive, and I want to know if they actually hang together before I show them to anyone. Here they are:

(1) I'm pretty convinced by those Frankfurt cases — the ones where a guy decides to do something on his own, but there's a failsafe wired into his brain that would have made him do the exact same thing if he'd started to chicken out. I think he's still fully responsible, even though he couldn't have done otherwise.

(2) But I also think free will requires being able to do otherwise. If your options were really fixed before you chose, I don't see how the choice is real.

(3) And I think you can't be morally responsible for something unless you did it of your own free will.

(4) Seperately, I think determinism — the idea that everything that happens is caused by prior stuff — doesn't by itself let people off the hook. If someone does something awful, the universe made him do it isn't an excuse to me.

I keep feeling like all four can't be true at once, but when I try to find the problem I just go in circles. So: which of these are actually in tension with each other, and which only look like they are? Rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and tell me what the cheapest fix would be — what I'd have to give up and what it would cost me.
