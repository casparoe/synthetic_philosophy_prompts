I've been trying to make sense of a pattern in behavioral genetics that seems, to me at least, to pull in two directions at once. The standard practice in the field is to report heritability coefficients — numbers like 0.4 for educational attainment or 0.5 to 0.8 for general cognitive ability in adults — as the headline result of a study. These numbers are then picked up by journalists, policy commentators, and sometimes by the researchers themselves in their broader writing, and they are treated as telling us something important about how much genes matter for a trait. Yet when you look at the fine print, the same researchers insist that heritability is a narrow population statistic: it describes the proportion of phenotypic variance in a particular sample at a particular time that is statistically associated with genetic variance, and it carries no implications about malleability, no claims about specific genes, no application to individuals, and certainly no license to draw conclusions about differences between populations.

So here is what puzzles me. If heritability really is just a local description of variance structure under one set of environmental conditions, why is it treated as a discovery worth reporting — let alone as the central finding of a research program? What philosophical commitments about genetic causation does this practice presuppose? Specifically, does the practice of partitioning variance into "genetic" and "environmental" components presuppose that these are separable causal factors — that you can meaningfully ask what the genetic contribution to a trait is, as though genes did their work independently of the environments that trigger and shape their expression? And when heritability estimates are invoked in social policy debates — for instance, to suggest that educational interventions face biological limits — what view of the relationship between within-group variance patterns and between-group differences is being taken for granted? I am trying to figure out whether the practice is philosophically coherent as it stands, or whether it implicitly relies on a causal interpretation of heritability that the technical definition explicitly disavows.
