Act as an expert in formal epistemology and probabilistic belief updating, and think carefully. Start by brainstorming candidate desiderata before you commit to a list, because I’m less interested in the standard bullet points than in the reasoning behind them.

I keep going in circles on the Judy Benjamin problem, and I want to step back and get clear about what an update rule would have to accomplish before I compare the usual proposals. Judy is dropped at night into a region divided into four equal quadrants: Red vs Blue territory, and within each, Second Company area vs Headquarters area. She initially assigns each quadrant probability 1/4. Then a radio message tells her something whose whole content is about a conditional chance: say, roughly, that if she is in Red territory, then she is three times as likely to be in Second Company area as in Headquarters. Nothing else is learned. Standard Bayesian conditionalization can’t directly handle this, Jeffrey conditionalization on the material conditional gives results that strike me as wrong, and minimum cross-entropy/InfoMin gives a very different answer that leaves the probability of Red unchanged. People argue about which of these is right, but I want the prior question answered first.

So please write the desiderata for an adequate theory or rule for updating probabilistic beliefs when the new information concerns a conditional probability or a conditional dependence. For each desideratum, explain the case or intuition that makes it a requirement, and say whether it’s non-negotiable or more like a preference of a particular camp. Then flag any pair or subset of these desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied in general — that last part is what I most want to see. Don’t just score the known update rules against the list; I want the criteria themselves argued for, and I want to see where the criteria might pull against each other.
