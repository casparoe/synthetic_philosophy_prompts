I write about health and medicine for a general-interest magazine, and increasingly my work sits at the boundary between science reporting and conceptual commentary: I get assigned a piece on a condition, and the first decision I face is what to call it. Is it a disease, a disorder, a syndrome, a risk factor, or a normal variation? The choice is not cosmetic. Calling something a disease can lead people to seek treatment they may not need; calling a disease a risk factor can give insurers grounds to dismiss a serious problem; calling a difference a disorder can stigmatize a whole group. I make this call several times a month, usually on deadline, and I have no explicit procedure for it.

I am aware that philosophers of medicine have worked on exactly this question, roughly divided between naturalist accounts, on which health and disease are matters of biological function and statistical typicality, and normativist accounts, on which the concepts cannot be cleaned of value judgments about suffering, flourishing, and social expectations. I do not want a summary of that debate. I want it translated into a reusable editorial procedure.

Please write me a decision procedure I could actually run in about twenty minutes before filing a story, with no more than a working journalist's background in philosophy. It should be a sequence of questions in order; for each question, say what would count as a sufficient answer, where the procedure stops and returns a definite label, and where it must stop and say that this step is a values judgment rather than a factual test. Then run the procedure on three cases I have actually faced: a reader whose blood pressure sits in the range some guidelines call prehypertension or elevated and who asks whether they have a disease; a postmenopausal woman whose bone-density score falls below the diagnostic cutoff for osteoporosis but who has never had a fracture; and an article about autism in which I must choose between describing it as a disorder and describing it as a difference. Include at least one case where the procedure returns an answer I will not like, and be explicit about what the procedure cannot settle.
