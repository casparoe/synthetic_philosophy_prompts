ok so i've noticed this pattern that keeps showing up and i'm wondering if it has a name in philosophy or economics or whatever, cause it feels like a specific thing and not just "people are sheep."

here's the situation. you've got a group of people making a decision in sequence, one after another. each person has a little scrap of private information — not the full picture, just their own clue about what the right choice is. the first person goes and picks based on whatever their clue says. the second person sees what the first person did and also has their own clue, so they weigh both. but here's where it gets weird: if the first two people happen to pick the same thing, the third person can rationally decide to ignore their own clue entirely. because what are the odds that two people in a row both got misleading clues? so they just copy. and then the fourth person sees three people all doing the same thing and at that point it's basically locked in — everyone piles on, and nobody's private information matters anymore.

the crazy part is the whole group can end up doing the wrong thing even if most people's private clues actually pointed the other way. if the first two just got unlucky, the cascade goes off a cliff and nobody can stop it, and everyone is being perfectly logical the whole time. it's not peer pressure or wanting to fit in — it's just that other people's actions are a rational source of information, and once enough of them point the same way, your own info looks like noise.

i first noticed this with something dumb at my school. a bunch of seniors started applying to this one college that nobody had really talked about before, and i asked around and it turned out the first couple people had specific reasons (one had a sibling there, another had visited for a summer program) but everyone after that just assumed there must be a good reason if so many people were applying. by the end like thirty-something people applied and most of them couldn't even articulate why beyond "well everyone else is." i've also seen it in online reviews where the first three or four ratings basically determine the whole trajectory of the score.

so my question: does this exact thing have a name? i know about "groupthink" and "bandwagon effect" and "herd mentality" but those all feel too broad — they're usually about social conformity or emotional contagion, whereas what i'm describing is people doing rational inference from limited evidence and it just happens to go wrong. is there a term that picks out this specific mechanism? and also, is there a distinction in the literature between cases where the early people actually had good information and cases where they were basically random? cause those feel importantly different — in one case the cascade is kinda unlucky but the reasoning was sound, in the other the whole thing is built on noise. if there's no established name for the pattern i'm describing, what would you call it and how would you draw the boundaries so it doesn't just collapse into the broader "people follow the crowd" stuff?
