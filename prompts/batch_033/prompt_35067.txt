I sit on the standing review panel of a research consortium whose member institutions sometimes want to act alone on work that other members regard as risky — a recurring instance of the unilateralist's curse, in which any one lab can act but the group might collectively prefer that no one did. Our precedents so far: (1) we permitted a lab to publish a routing-anomaly dataset over another lab's objection that it would aid attackers, on the ground that the data were already effectively public and the feared harm speculative; (2) we blocked a lab from unilaterally beginning gain-of-function research on transmissible mammalian viruses, requiring full consortium assent despite the lab's own favorable safety assessment; (3) we permitted a lab to run a small, time-limited, and fully reversible marine cloud brightening field trial over one member's objection, citing the intervention's bounded and reversible character; (4) we blocked a lab from unilaterally releasing the weights of a large general-purpose model, holding that an irreversible release with significant potential for misuse required a consortium vote, which then went against release.

A new request has arrived: a lab wants to begin, on its own, a small-scale ocean iron fertilization experiment in international waters. It argues that the ecological risk is low and that effects would dissipate within months, and it commits to monitoring and a pre-registered stop rule. A majority of member institutions, while conceding that the physical risk is probably modest, believe unilateral geoengineering would let one lab set a norm the consortium has not agreed to, and they would vote against the experiment if it came to a vote.

What should we decide if we are to be consistent with our own precedents, and which precedent is the closest analog? I am less interested in the bottom line than in the reasoning: reconstruct the rule or rules that fit our four past decisions, say what each rule predicts in this case, give your best judgment about which rule we are actually running, and identify the further case that would most cleanly force us to choose between them.
