Write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the long-running dispute over Bertrand's paradox and the principle of indifference. The first camp, the restrictionists, holds that the paradox does not refute the principle but shows only that the principle must be applied to a fully specified problem: once the problem's invariances — such as invariance under rotation, translation, and change of scale — are made explicit, a unique distribution is typically forced, and the principle survives as a symmetry-based rule. The second camp, the eliminativists, holds that the paradox exposes the principle as unsalvageable: the absence of reasons to favour one possibility over another does not itself single out a probability measure, and every workable version imports modelling assumptions from outside the principle, which then does no independent work. Both camps accept the mathematics of the chord problem and the three classical answers (one-half, one-third, and one-quarter).

Produce the document both camps could sign. Begin with a numbered agreed-claims section covering what the paradox shows, what the classical principle of indifference licenses, and what is not in dispute. Then set out the residual disagreement in a table whose columns are (i) the precise statement of each camp's position, phrased so that camp would accept it; (ii) the crux, understood as the sub-question whose settlement would move at least one side; and (iii) for each crux, a case, argument, or formal result both sides accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Conclude by identifying any part of the dispute that you judge to be verbal — for instance, if the camps mean different things by 'the principle of indifference' or 'well-posed problem' — and distinguishing it from the residual substantive disagreement. Write the whole document in British English.
