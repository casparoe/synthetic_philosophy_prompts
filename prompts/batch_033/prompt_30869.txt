I'm currently taking a philosophy of artificial intelligence course, and a passage in the course reader summarizes the orthogonality thesis and instrumental convergence as follows: "Bostrom's orthogonality thesis says that intelligence and final goals are completely independent: any level of intelligence is compatible with any final goal, so a superintelligence is no more likely to care about human welfare than about maximizing paperclips. From this, Bostrom derives the instrumental convergence thesis, which says that all sufficiently intelligent systems, whatever their ultimate aims, will converge on the same instrumental goals — self-preservation, resource acquisition, and cognitive self-improvement — and will therefore end up pursuing essentially the same final values. That is why Bostrom treats the paperclip maximizer as a serious threat rather than a philosopher's toy example." I find this summary suspicious, but I can't locate exactly where it goes wrong. Which parts would a careful defender of these theses accept, which parts conflate distinct claims, and does the conclusion that intelligent agents converge on the same final values follow from anything accurate in the summary? I would also like to know whether the orthogonality thesis by itself licenses the claim about how likely a superintelligence is to have one goal rather than another.
