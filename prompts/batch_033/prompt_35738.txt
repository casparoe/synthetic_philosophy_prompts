Write the joint statement that a Hempelian confirmation theorist and a selective-confirmation theorist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration about Hempel's raven paradox. The Hempelian holds that the equivalence condition (logically equivalent hypotheses are confirmed by exactly the same evidence) and Nicod's instance condition (an observed F that is G confirms the generalization that all F are G) are non-negotiable constraints on a logic of confirmation, and therefore that an observed green apple does confirm the hypothesis that all ravens are black, however counterintuitive that seems. The selective-confirmation theorist holds that evidential relevance is not a purely syntactic matter; confirmation must be relativized to a reference class, background knowledge, or a sampling procedure. On that view, a green apple confirms the ravens hypothesis only in a degenerate or merely formal sense, if at all.

Produce the document they would issue, in the following form.

1. A numbered Agreed claims section. Cover at least: the logical equivalence of the hypothesis that all ravens are black with the hypothesis that all non-black things are non-ravens; the fact that a green apple satisfies the antecedent and consequent of the latter; the derivation by which Nicod's condition together with the equivalence condition forces the paradoxical result; and a precise statement of what a theory of confirmation must explain, including why a black raven feels confirmatory while a green apple does not.

2. A Disagreements table with columns: issue; Hempelian position worded so a Hempelian would sign it; selective-confirmation position worded so a selective-confirmation theorist would sign it; and the crux underlying that disagreement.

3. For each crux, a concrete test, case, or argument that both sides agree bears on the crux, with each side's prediction or assessment recorded in advance. Good candidate cruxes include: whether confirmation is a purely logical relation or is relative to a sampling procedure or reference class; whether the equivalence condition is an adequacy constraint that cannot be restricted; whether incremental probabilistic support and syntactic instance confirmation are rival explications of the same pre-theoretic notion; and whether the intuition that a green apple is irrelevant is a datum to preserve or a bias to be explained away.

If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal — for example, if the two sides use the word confirms to mean different things — say which part is verbal and why, but do not resolve the substantive remainder. The document should be constructive: a text both parties could put their names to, with the residual disagreement made exact.
