I'm on the board of a small private foundation that funds philosophy of mind research. We've got a grant proposal from a philosopher who wants to spend a year defending epiphenomenalism about phenomenal consciousness — the view that conscious experiences are caused by brain processes but have no causal powers of their own. The proposal is sharp, but one board member says we should reject it outright because of the paradox of phenomenal judgment: if epiphenomenalism is true, then our judgments about our own experiences (like "I'm in pain right now") aren't caused by those experiences, which seems to undercut the very evidence we have for believing we're conscious. The applicant addresses this, but the board member thinks the response is hand-wavy. Other members think the paradox is exactly the kind of thing a good grant should work on, not a reason to reject. We're split down the middle. I need to draft a short decision memo with a recommendation — fund, reject, or fund with conditions. The memo should engage the paradox seriously, not just say "let the peer reviewers sort it out."
