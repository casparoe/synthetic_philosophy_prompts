heres one ive been stuck on and i think itd help me to see it played out. a friend of mine keeps saying hedonism is dead because pleasures have nothing in common — the heterogeneity problem, basically. the pleasure of a warm bath, finishing a proof, scratching a mosquito bite, watching your rival humiliate himself, the relief of finding your keys: they all feel completely different, so (he says) 'pleasure' isnt one kind of thing and a theory built on it is empty. i dont buy that it kills hedonism, but it does force you to say what pleasure actually is. write a dialogue between two hedonists who both agree pleasure is the only intrinsic good but disagree about what it is. one holds the felt-quality view: pleasure is a single distinctive feeling, a hedonic tone — the same 'feels good' quality present in every pleasant experience, with all the variety coming from the non-hedonic content the tone attaches to (warmth, relief, triumph, whatever). the other holds the attitudinal view: pleasure isnt a sensation at all, its taking pleasure in a state of affairs, an attitude of enjoyment directed at different objects — wich is exactly why the cases look so heterogeneous: theres no common feeling, only a common attitude. make both of them genuinely sharp and have them respond to each others actual moves rather than trading speeches. the attitudinal one should press the heterogeneity cases and ask the felt-quality one to locate the common tone in gloating vs an itch; the felt-quality one should press back that infants and cats have pleasures without anything like propositional attitudes, and that 'enjoyment' is doing all the work unless it secretly contains a felt quality. neither should win outright — i want the reader to feel how much hangs on what youll count as a feeling. american english please, and a typo or two is fine.
