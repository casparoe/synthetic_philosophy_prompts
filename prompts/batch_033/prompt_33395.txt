here's where i've landed:

(1) i'm sold on putnam's twin earth argument. "water" means H2O here and XYZ on twin earth, and that difference is fixed by the external environment, not by what's in oscar's head. meanings just aren't in the head, full stop.

(2) i also think burge is right about social externalism — the arthritis case shows that what i mean depends on the linguistic practices of my community, even when i'm ignorant of the details. so content is externally determined by both the physical and the social environment.

(3) despite all that, i think i've got privileged access to my own thoughts. when i think "water is wet", i know what i'm thinking without needing to investigate my environment or ask anyone else. this isn't just a brute intuition — i think it's something like a constitutive condition on being a thinker at all.

(4) the slow-switching problem genuinely bothers me. if you swap me with twin-oscar without my knowing, my "water" concept now refers to XYZ, and my old memories involving "water" (which were about H2O) are now somehow about XYZ too. that seems like i've lost track of my own past thoughts, and i don't see how any externalist has fully dodged that bullet.

(5) i still think there's a notion of narrow content worth hanging onto. there's something psychological that me and twin-oscar share — some cognitive state, perspective, or functional role — that's the same across worlds, and that's what does the work in explaining behaviour. wide content is for semantics; narrow content is for psychology.

(6) lastly, i'm not a naturalist about content. i don't think representational content can be reduced to causal or informational or teleological relations. i think meaning is — i don't know — sui generis, or maybe normative in a way that doesn't bottom out in natural facts.
