I have been arguing with a classmate about whether a language model can lie, and we have found that the real disagreement is not about the models but about the word. I hold the intentionalist view: deception requires an intention to induce a false belief, so a system without beliefs or intentions can mislead, misinform, or be designed to mislead, but cannot itself deceive. She holds a functionalist view: a system counts as deceptive if it is trained or designed in ways that reliably produce false beliefs in its users, whether or not it has anything like an intention. Please draft the joint statement we would produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. It should include: a numbered list of claims about AI systems and deception that both of us can sign; each position stated in wording its own side would accept as accurate and non-caricatured; the precise cruxes — I suspect one is whether the concept of deception should track moral blameworthiness or downstream epistemic harm, another is whether honesty is just the absence of deception or requires something more positive, and another is whether it is legitimate to describe goal-directed systems as having intentions in an as-if sense; and a table of the cruxes, giving for each a concrete case or experimental setup we would both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. End by saying which part of our disagreement, if any, you think is merely verbal.
