Write a dialogue between two philosophers who disagree about what to make of Fitch's paradox of knowability. Both of them know the proof cold. One thinks the derivation—take an unknown truth, p and no one ever knows that p, show it can't be known, then use the knowability principle to conclude there are no unknown truths—is a knockdown reductio of the idea that all truths are knowable. The other thinks the knowability principle is fine and the proof just exposes a mistake in how we formalize knowable or in applying the principle to a sentence like that. Make them argue it out propery: each should press the other on the weakest steps, neither should be a strawman, and they shouldn't just talk past each other. It's fine if they end without agreeing.
