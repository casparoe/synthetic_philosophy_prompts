ok so i'm running a metaphysics reading group and we're about to do a unit on bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition. i want to start the session with a "which position do you actually hold?" quiz, partly as an icebreaker and partly so people can see that their intuitions already push them toward some solution before they've read the canonical papers. could you write one for me?

i want maybe seven or eight questions. each should be a short concrete scenario or a claim, with two or three answer options, and none of them should just ask "do you think relations are real?" or "are you a russellian?" — the whole point is to sort people by what they say about cases, not by what label sounds cool to them.

the positions i want as leaves are: the regress-is-vicious monist (bradley's own view), the regress-is-benign relational realist (early russell, broadly), the states-of-affairs-as-fundamental view (armstrong-ish), the non-relational-tie view (bergmann's nexus), the unsaturated-constituents view (fregean), and the deflationary "there's no real problem here" view. if the logical space of answers forces you to include other leaves i haven't thought of, add them and name them if they have names or flag them as "unnamed" if nobody defends them.

the questions should force people to take stands on things like: does the regress also infect ordinary physical relations (like "the mug is on the desk") or only propositional unity? is instantiation itself a relation? would you be satisfied with a solution that works for propositions but not for facts, or vice versa? that sort of thing.

please also include a scoring key that maps answer patterns to positions, and flag any pairs of positions your quiz can't cleanly seperate. also flag any logically possible combinations of answers that don't correspond to any defended position, and say why nobody's there if you can (incoherent? just unattractive? underexplored?).
