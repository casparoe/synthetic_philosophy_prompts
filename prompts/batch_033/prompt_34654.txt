A friend of mine summarized tranquilism for me after reading about it online. He said: "Tranquilism is the ethical view that the only thing that matters, morally, is the absence of craving and suffering. Pleasure is irrelevant — it has no intrinsic value, and a life of quiet contentment is exactly as good as one filled with joy, because what makes a state valuable is just that you don't want anything to change. As a result, tranquilism is basically a version of negative utilitarianism: the only moral imperative is to eliminate suffering, and creating happiness doesn't count at all. It also seems to follow that the ideal state is death or unconsciousness, since those involve no craving whatsoever."

I'm skeptical that this is a fair summary — it sounds like a view that would be easy to dismiss, and if it really implied that death is the ideal state, I doubt anyone would take it seriously. But I don't know enough about tranquilism to say where the summary goes wrong. Which parts are accurate, which are misrepresentations or overstatements, and do the conclusions he draws — that it collapses into negative utilitarianism, and that it implies death is the ideal state — actually follow from the accurate parts?
