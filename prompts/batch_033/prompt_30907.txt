I'm in a Chinese philosophy class and we just did the School of Names, and I got a bit obsessed with the White Horse Dialogue — the one attributed to Gongsun Long where the thesis is usually rendered as: a white horse is not a horse. I tried to write out the reasoning so I could explain it to my roommate, and the reconstruction I got seems to prove way too much, because it would also show black horses arn't horses, tall men aren't men, and so on down the line. Here it is:

(1) If someone asks for a horse, a yellow or a black horse would satisfy the request. (2) If someone asks for a white horse, a yellow or a black horse would not satisfy it. (3) So the things satisfying the two requests are not the same. (4) If the things satisfying two terms are not the same, the two terms do not pick out the same thing. (5) So the terms 'horse' and 'white horse' pick out different things. (6) If two terms pick out different things, then a white horse is not a horse. (7) Therefore a white horse is not a horse — and the same pattern shows no black horse is a horse, no tall man is a man, no red apple is an apple. But that last part is absurd, since a white horse is exactly a horse that is white, not something other than a horse.

I'd like to keep (1)–(3), because they look like the part most faithful to the dialogue's own example about seeking a horse versus seeking a white horse.
