I hold the following views about decision-making under uncertainty:

(1) For a sequence of independent, identically distributed bets, the uniquely rational policy is to maximize the expected logarithm of wealth — the Kelly criterion — because it maximizes the asymptotic growth rate of capital.
(2) The correct normative framework for all decisions under uncertainty is expected utility theory: an agent should maximize the expectation of some utility function defined over final outcomes.
(3) There is no deep normative discontinuity between one-shot decisions and repeated decisions — the same principles should govern both settings, even if their practical upshots differ.
(4) In a single unrepeatable bet offered at favorable odds, it can be irrational to stake one's entire bankroll even when doing so maximizes expected monetary value.

Are these four commitments jointly consistent? Identify any tensions among them, distinguish genuine conflicts from pairs that only appear to be in tension, and explain why the apparent tensions dissolve. Rank the genuine tensions by severity and state what the least costly revision would be — which commitment should give, and what does giving it up cost the overall view?
