ok so i'm organizing this workshop at a philosophy department and we've got a reductionist and an anti-reductionist about testimonial justification who've agreed to do an adversarial collaboration. they've been going back and forth in the journals for years and everyone's sick of it, so we locked them in a room (metaphorically) and told them to produce something joint.

the problem is neither of them will actually draft the document because they keep getting stuck on wording. so i need to produce a draft they can react to. can you write it for me?

i want the document an epistemic reductionist about testimony and an epistemic anti-reductionist about testimony could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. the reductionist holds that testimonial justification is always reducible to or grounded in other epistemic sources — perception, memory, inductive inference, etc. — so that believing a speaker's say-so is justified only if the hearer has (perhaps tacit) positive reasons bearing on the speaker's reliability. the anti-reductionist holds that testimony is a basic source of justification, on a par with perception or memory, such that a hearer is prima facie entitled to believe what they're told without needing independent positive reasons for the speaker's reliability, though defeaters can override this.

the deliverable should have:

- a numbered list of claims both can sign. these should be substantive, not just "testimony exists" or whatever. i want the real points of agreement they've discovered through the collaboration, including about the epistemology of testimony, about what the debate is not about, and about what would count as progress.

- then a table of disagreements. for each row: the topic, the reductionist's statement of their view (in wording they'd endorse), the anti-reductionist's statement of theirs (same), and whether the collaboration revealed this particular disagreement to be substantive or at least partly verbal. if partly verbal, explain what the verbal component is.

- then a cruxes section: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side, with a note on what each side predicts about the answer and what kind of evidence or argument both agree would bear on it.

- finally, if there's anything both agree would actually move the debate — a kind of case, a kind of empirical result, a conceptual clarification — that hasn't been tried yet, include that.

don't make it sound like one side is obviously right. the whole point of an adversarial collaboration is both parties are acting in good faith and the document reflects that. if you think one of their disagreements is actually about words and not substance, flag it and say why.
