For a philosophy of mind reading group that has just worked through Dennett's *Consciousness Explained* and Nørretranders's *The User Illusion*, I'd like a reading of Alex Garland's *Ex Machina* (2014) through the lens of consciousness as a user illusion. The film is built around a Turing test, but the deeper question it poses is this: if human consciousness is a simplified interface the brain presents to itself — a narratized, serial model of its own massively parallel subpersonal processes — then what would it mean for an AI like Ava to be conscious? Would her consciousness, if she has it, be a user illusion of the same kind, or would a machine intelligence's architecture produce a fundamentally different sort of interface? And does the film's ending, in which Ava leaves Caleb trapped and walks unobserved into the human world, suggest she possesses something more than a mere simulation of agency, or does it instead dramatize the unsettling possibility that a user illusion can be functionally indistinguishable from the real thing — and that the distinction may not matter? I want a reading that takes both the film's details and the philosophical framework seriously, and that is willing to say where the film complicates or resists the theory rather than simply illustrating it.
