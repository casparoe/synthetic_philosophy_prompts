"Daniel Dennett divides minds into four kinds. Darwinian creatures are entirely hardwired by genetics: they cannot learn anything during their lifetime, and change occurs only across generations, through natural selection. Skinnerian creatures add the ability to learn by trial and error — they try actions at random and repeat whatever gets reinforced. Popperian creatures can do this trial-and-error internally, simulating possible actions and letting their hypotheses die in their stead, so they don't have to risk real death. Finally, Gregorian creatures — which is really just us — use language and culture to import thinking tools from others, so we don't have to figure everything out from scratch. Dennett's point is that this is a ladder of increasing cognitive sophistication, with humans at the top as the only full Gregorian creatures. Most other animals are stuck at the Skinnerian or Darwinian level, which is why human cognition is fundamentally different in kind from animal cognition."
