I've been trying to sort out my views on whether we have moral reasons concerning how we treat video game characters, and I'd like a consistency check before I write this up for a blog post. Here's where I am:

(1) Current NPCs aren't conscious and aren't moral patients — you can't wrong *them*.
(2) Still, spending an afternoon torturing an NPC in a sandbox game for the sheer enjoyment of it seems morally gross in a way that needs explaining, even if nobody else ever finds out.
(3) The best explanation for (2) is character-based: the act reflects badly on the agent, cultivates cruelty, etc.
(4) But I also think the gamer's dilemma — why is virtual murder widely accepted while virtual child molestation isn't? — is a genuine puzzle and not just people being squeamish. If the only thing wrong with virtual violence were character-based, both should be equally bad for your character, so the asymmetry needs a different kind of explanation.
(5) If we someday build NPCs that pass behavioral Turing tests for suffering — they plead, they flinch, they describe their pain convincingly — I think the character-based story stops being enough and we'd owe something to the NPCs directly, even if under the hood they're still just fancy pattern matchers.

Are these five consistent? It feels like (3) and (4) are pulling in opposite directions, and I'm not sure (5) doesn't just collapse back into (1) if I'm honest about what "under the hood" means. Rank the tensions and tell me which one I should give up first.
