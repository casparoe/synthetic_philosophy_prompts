I'm teaching a grad seminar on perception, and next week we're hitting the cognitive penetration debate. One of my students keeps insisting that perception is "just a camera" — you point it at the world, light comes in, and it records what's there. Beliefs, desires, expectations, they're all in the post-processing, not in the image itself. From this he concludes the whole cognitive penetration debate is overblown: top-down effects might influence judgment or report, but they can't touch perception proper. I want to give the class a rigorous walkthrough of this analogy. What maps onto what? (Lens = ? Sensor = ? The image file vs. what the photographer does with it = ?) Where does the mapping hold up, and where does it break? Which breaks actually matter for the cognitive penetration claim, and which are just irrelevant dissanalogies? And once we've put all the disanalogies on the table, does the conclusion — that top-down effects can't reach perception proper — survive, or was the camera doing all the work?
