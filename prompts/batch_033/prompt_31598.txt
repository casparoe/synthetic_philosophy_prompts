I am a novelist. I want a reusable decision procedure, grounded in Kant's universalizability test as developed in the Groundwork, that I can run on any morally significant action my characters contemplate. The procedure should take a situated description of a proposed action and yield a verdict: permissible, impermissible, or unsettled by the test alone. It must walk me through (a) extracting a maxim from the description, including how to handle the level-of-description problem; (b) testing for a contradiction in conception; (c) testing for a contradiction in willing; and (d) identifying the points where judgment cannot be eliminated and must be handed off to something outside the procedure. The procedure should be usable by someone who has read the Groundwork but is not a Kant scholar.

Then run the procedure on two maxims from my current work: (1) a character lies to a dying parent, telling them that their estranged child has forgiven them, in order to let the parent die in peace; (2) a character breaks a promise made to a now-dead friend because keeping it would harm an innocent third party the friend could not have known about when the promise was made.

Begin with the procedure itself, no preface or commentary. Be honest about where the procedure gives answers I will find uncomfortable, and flag each step that is a disguised judgment call rather than a mechanical operation.
