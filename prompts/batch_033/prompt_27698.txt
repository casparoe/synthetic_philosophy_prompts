I'm trying to determine what I actually think about which events are real, on the basis of my intuitive verdicts about a series of cases, and I would like to know whether any coherent view of time captures those verdicts.

My verdicts are these. (1) There is a determinate fact about whether, right now, a particular star in Andromeda has already exploded, even though the light from the explosion won't reach us for millions of years. (2) If two observers pass each other at high speed and disagree about which distant events are simultaneous with their meeting, neither is wrong: simultaneity is frame-relative, and there is no privileged frame. (3) What I will decide next week is not already real in the way that last week is; the future is open, the past is fixed. (4) Last week is fully real even though I can no longer affect it. (5) A friend on a high-speed journey is just as real while the journey is under way as I am, even if our clocks disagree; being far away and moving fast does not make them less real.

The only half-formed principle I can articulate is this: an event is real for me just in case it is in my past light cone. That principle handles (3) and (4), but it seems to make the Andromeda explosion unreal until the light arrives, which I find hard to accept, and it makes the traveling friend unreal to me during the trip, which seems absurd. It also does nothing to explain why I want to say there is a fact of the matter about Andromeda now.

So: is there a principle — or a small set of candidate principles — about what it is for an event to be real, about what simultaneity is, and about what temporal becoming consists in, that captures all or most of these verdicts and does better than my past-light-cone idea? If no principle captures all of them, which verdicts should I give up, and why those rather than the others? Please also say what the resulting view predicts about a further case: suppose there is a distant spacecraft moving away from Earth at high speed, so that events I would place next year are simultaneous, in its frame, with an event happening here now; what should I say about whether those next-year events are already real? Finally, say how confident you are in the view you propose, and single out the point you are least sure of.
