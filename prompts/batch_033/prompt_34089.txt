In a metaphysics seminar, a colleague defended the claim that Bradley's regress is a pseudo-problem by leaning on an analogy. He said that thinking of instantiation as needing a further relation to tie it to its relata is like thinking that glue needs a further layer of glue to stick to the surfaces it joins. Just as glue is the kind of thing whose job is to stick, instantiation is the kind of thing whose job is to relate a particular to a universal; to ask what makes it do that is to misunderstand its role. How far does the glue analogy actually hold? Please give the correspondence table—what corresponds to the glued surfaces, the glue, the act of sticking, and so on—then identify where the analogy breaks down, say which disanalogies matter for the anti-Bradley conclusion and which don't, and say whether the conclusion survives on other grounds or was being carried by the analogy. Structure the response as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
