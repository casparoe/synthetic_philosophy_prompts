For a paper I'm writing on the paradox of tragedy — the puzzle of why we deliberately seek out artworks that reliably produce sadness, pity, and fear when we ordinarily avoid situations that arouse those emotions — I want the full specification of what an adequate solution must deliver before I evaluate any candidate.

Write the desiderata: every phenomenon a theory of tragic pleasure must explain, every case it must get right, every distinction it must respect, and every empirical result it must be consistent with. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it.

Then classify each requirement as non-negotiable (any theory that fails it is dead) or as a preference of a particular camp (and name the camp).

I especially want to know whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — if satisfying some requirements forces you to violate others, that is the part I most want to see.

Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most work to exclude the standard solutions — catharsis, conversion, compensation, control, and meta-response theories — and briefly why it eliminates each one it eliminates.
