I keep noticing a pattern in arguments about whether it is right to bring new people into existence, and I cannot find a label for it. Take a possible child whose life would contain both genuine happiness and genuine suffering. If we imagine never conceiving that child, people seem willing to count the absence of that suffering as a point in favor of not conceiving — as something good about the child's never existing — even though there is no actual person who is spared anything. Yet the same people do not count the absence of that happiness as a point against not conceiving; nobody says the never-existing child was deprived. The avoided pain does argumentative work, while the forgone pleasure does none. And this seems specific to people who never exist: for an existing person, we would normally count both the pains avoided and the pleasures lost. Is there an established name for this exact pattern? I want more than a label. I want to know how it is best formulated — whether it is a claim about value, about reasons, or about the psychology of how we in fact judge these cases — how it connects to the stronger view that coming into existence is a harm, and what it actually licenses as opposed to what is read into it. Please separate what is settled among people who work on this from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested formulation as the consensus.
