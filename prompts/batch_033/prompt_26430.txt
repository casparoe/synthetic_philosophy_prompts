"Explain the distinction between epistemological reduction and ontological reduction. Use the relationship between thermodynamics and statistical mechanics as your running example throughout. Then answer: does the fact that we cannot define 'temperature' in the language of statistical mechanics without loss of meaning give us reason to think temperature is a strongly emergent property? Why or why not?"

here's the student's answer:

"The distinction between epistemological and ontological reduction is about whether our theories can be reduced (epistemological) versus whether the things themselves can be reduced (ontological). Epistemological reduction means we can derive the laws of the higher-level theory from the lower-level theory plus bridge principles connecting the two vocabularies. Ontological reduction means the higher-level entities and properties are nothing over and above the lower-level ones — they're identical to, or wholly constituted by, lower-level entities and properties.

In the case of thermodynamics and statistical mechanics, we clearly have ontological reduction: temperature just is mean molecular kinetic energy. But we may lack full epistemological reduction. The bridge principle connecting temperature to mean kinetic energy holds straightforwardly only for ideal gases. For real substances, the relationship between temperature and the microphysical state is more complicated, involving potential energy contributions. So we can't derive all thermodynamic regularities from statistical mechanics in the strict Nagelian sense, since the bridge laws aren't simple biconditionals that hold across all systems.

Now, does this failure of epistemological reduction give us reason to think temperature is strongly emergent? No, it doesn't. Strong emergence requires that the higher-level property have novel causal powers that cannot be explained by or derived from the lower-level properties, even in principle. The fact that we have a messy relationship between our concept of temperature and the underlying molecular description doesn't show that temperature has any causal powers over and above those of the molecules themselves. It just shows that our concepts don't map neatly. The irreducibility here is purely epistemological, and it provides no support whatsoever for strong emergence."
