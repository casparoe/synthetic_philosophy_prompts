Write the joint statement that a committed internalist about normative reasons in the Williams tradition and a committed externalist about normative reasons could both sign at the end of a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The internalist holds that an ascription of a reason to an agent is true only if a sound deliberative route from the agent's subjective motivational set could yield a motive to act accordingly; the externalist holds that some true reason ascriptions outrun any such route. The statement should contain: the claims both can sign, made substantive rather than vacuous; the residual disagreement, stated first in neutral wording and then in wording each side would accept for its own view, with neither side caricatured—in particular, the internalist's wording should allow that agents can have reasons they do not yet recognize, and the externalist's wording should allow that sound deliberation matters to what an agent has reason to do; at least two cruxes, sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and for each crux, a case or argument both sides accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Also state whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal and, if so, which part and what substantive disagreement remains. Format the response as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion, used in order for: (1) the agreed claims; (2) the neutral statement of the disagreement; (3) the disagreement in each side's own wording; (4) the first crux, its case, and each side's prediction; (5) the second crux, its case, each side's prediction, and the note on what, if anything, is verbal.
