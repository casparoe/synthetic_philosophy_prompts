I'm a retired epidemiologist — I spent thirty years doing survival analysis for clinical trials — and since I stopped working I've gotten into the philosophy of probability. There's something about life insurance underwriting that's been nagging at me, and I can't tell whether it's a real philosophical problem or just me being confused.

Here's the thing. A life insurer will quote a 45-year-old nonsmoking man one annual premium. It'll charge him more if he smokes, more if his blood pressure is high, more if he flies small planes or works on a fishing boat. It sorts him into buckets — age, sex, smoking status, occupation, maybe zip code — and out comes a number: 'your ten-year mortality risk is 1.7%.' But the probability that this particular man dies in the next ten years doesn't have any single obvious value. It depends entirely on which bucket you put him in. Put him in 'American men aged 45' and you get one number; add 'nonsmoker' and you get a lower one; add his exact medical history and you get another; and if you made the bucket small enough to contain only him, the ten-year frequency would be either zero or one, depending on whether he makes it. So what is the practice of underwriting actually presupposing about probability? That there's a fact of the matter about this man's single-case chance of dying, and the actuary's job is to approximate it with the right reference class? Or that 'risk' is just the insurer's degree of belief given the information it's allowed to use? The industry talks as if risk is an objective property of the person — your premium reflects your risk — but when challenged it defends it's choice of classes as a business decision, and the law forbids using some classes that are plainly predictive (race, and in health insurance genetic markers). That looks like a tension to me.

Reconstruct the implicit theory of probability behind underwriting, say whether the practice is coherent, and tell me what would actually change in how a policy gets priced if the insurer went strictly subjectivist about risk — and whether the legal bans on certain classes make more or less sense under each view. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate the question back to me.
