I'm giving the best man's toast at my brother's wedding next month. He's an evolutionary biologist who works on costly signalling and the handicap principle — Zahavi, Grafen, honest signals, the lot — and his new wife is a behavioural ecologist, so about half the room will be people who'd catch any slip about whether the signal has to be costly at equilibrium or just differentially costly. The other half are my parents' friends and our cousins, who wont have any idea what any of that means. Write me the toast. It should be built around the handicap principle and it should actually use it: the idea that a wedding — the expense, the public vows, everyone travelling to be here — looks like a pointless waste unless you see it as an honest signal, and thats exactly why it matters. I want it to land as genuinely affectionate, not cynical, and not as a seminar. If my grandmother thinks for one second that I've said marriage is just a peacock's tail, she'll never forgive me. Five minutes, and please write it in British English.
