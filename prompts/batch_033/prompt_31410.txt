I am a high school student in a course that pairs Renaissance political thought with Shakespeare. We have been reading Machiavelli's The Prince, and I have been stuck on the pair of terms he works with throughout: fortuna, the force of chance and circumstance, and virtù, the prince's capacity to impose himself on circumstances through boldness, foresight, and adaptability rather than through Christian moral goodness. We are now reading Shakespeare's Macbeth, and I am trying to write an essay that reads the play through those two concepts. I do not want to produce the standard paper that simply calls Macbeth a Machiavellian villain, because the more I look at the play the less that label seems to fit.

Read Macbeth as a dramatization of, and an argument with, Machiavelli's account of fortuna and virtù. Where does Macbeth actually display virtù in Machiavelli's sense, and where does he fail at it in ways Machiavelli's framework would predict are fatal? Are the Weird Sisters best understood as fortuna—something a bold prince might master—or as a force Machiavelli's categories cannot contain? Does Lady Macbeth begin with more virtù than her husband, and what does her collapse imply about Machiavelli's view that boldness and adaptability, not caution, win fortune's favor? I keep returning to the way Macbeth tries to force the prophecies to come out on his own terms, and to the final movement from Birnam Wood to Dunsinane. Finally, does the play end up endorsing, complicating, or rejecting Machiavelli's confidence that a flexible and ruthless prince can master fortune—or does it present a tragic order in which that whole project is doomed? I want a reading that is faithful in both directions: do not reduce Macbeth to an illustration of The Prince, and do not turn Machiavelli into a moralist. Where Shakespeare resists the Machiavellian framework, say so, and say what the play adds to the philosophical debate that the treatise does not.

Write the essay in American English. Begin with the essay itself: lead with your central claim in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preface and no restating of the question.
