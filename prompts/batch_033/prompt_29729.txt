My friends got into this argument at lunch last week and I still can't figure out who's right. It started because Marcus told his parents he was "studying at the library" when he was actually at a party — but he technically did stop at the library for like ten minutes first, so the sentence itself wasn't false. His parents assumed he meant the whole evening. When he told us this, Chloe said that's basically lying and just as bad. Marcus said no, he never said anything false, he just let them draw their own conclusion. Then it spiraled.

Chloe said the whole point of saying "I was at the library" in that context was to make his parents believe something false, so morally it's the same as lying — the intention and the effect are identical, and the fact that the words happen to be technically true is morally irrelevent. Marcus said that can't be right because if you treat misleading as lying, then tons of ordinary behavior becomes lying, like when someone asks how you are and you say "fine" even though you're having a terrible day. Chloe said that's different because there's no intent to deceive — the person asking isn't actually trying to find out the truth, it's a social script. Marcus said okay but what about when your grandma gives you a sweater you hate and you say "I love it" — that is a lie with intent to deceive, and everyone thinks it's fine, so maybe the whole lying/misleading distinction isn't doing the moral work Chloe thinks it is.

Then our friend Dev jumped in and said Marcus was mixing up two different things. The question isn't whether lying is always worse than misleading — it's whether there's any moral difference at all between them in cases where the intent to deceive is the same and the stakes are the same. And Dev said there is a difference, because when you lie you assert something false, which means you've made a kind of commitment to the other person that you broke. When you mislead, you made true assertions and the other person drew a false inference — you might have set them up to draw it, but you didn't actually break your word. Marcus said that's exactly what makes misleading worse, not better: you're being manipulative and also giving yourself deniability at the same time — you get to deceive someone and then say "well I never actually said that" if you get caught. Chloe said she agreed with Dev that there's a difference but she thought it went the other way — lying is worse because it's a more direct violation of trust, and at least the misleading person left true statements on the table that a more careful listener could of worked with.

At this point I was totally lost. They all made sense at different moments and I couldn't tell if they were actually disagreeing about the same thing or just emphasizing different parts of the situation. So I guess my question is: can you go through their arguments and say who's right about what? Like, is there a moral difference between lying and misleading when the intent to deceive is the same? And if there is a difference, which one is worse, or does it depend? I don't need a final winner exactly, more like I want to understand what each of them got right and wrong and whether the disagreement itself is even well-formed.
