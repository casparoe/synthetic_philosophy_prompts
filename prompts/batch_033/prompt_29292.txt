I'm working on a long-form piece about the metaphysics of reproduction and the way people think about the contingency of their own existence, and I'd like to get the underlying philosophical issue straight before I translate it for a general audience. As I understand it, the position known as origin essentialism holds that a particular human being could not have developed from a different sperm and egg than the particular ones from which they actually developed: if those two gametes had never met, that person would never have existed, even if the same parents had conceived another child around the same time. That part seems straightforward to me.

What's giving me trouble is a case my editor raised. Monozygotic twins begin as a single zygote, formed from one sperm and one egg, which then splits into two embryos. So here are two numerically distinct people who trace back to exactly the same sperm and egg. If origin essentialism says that a person's origin is essential to being that person, doesn't the twin case force the theory to say that both twins are the same person — or at least that origin alone cannot distinguish them? And if origin alone cannot distinguish them, what exactly is the essentialist claim doing? I'm trying to figure out whether identical twins refute origin essentialism, or whether I'm misunderstanding what the theory asserts.
