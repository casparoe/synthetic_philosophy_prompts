I'm teaching a political philosophy unit and want to pair Ursula K. Le Guin's short story "The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas" with Rawls's veil of ignorance and the difference principle. The story describes a city whose happiness and prosperity depend on the deliberate, ongoing misery of a single child locked in a basement. Most citizens, once they learn of the arrangement, come to terms with it; a few walk away. Read the story through the lens of Rawls's theory of justice. Would parties behind the veil of ignorance choose the Omelas arrangement? Does the difference principle condemn it, permit it, or is it the wrong tool entirely for capturing what is wrong with Omelas? And what should we make of the ones who walk away — are they acting on something Rawls's framework can account for, or are they pointing to a moral consideration that Rawlsian justice leaves out? I would like a reading that takes both the story and the philosophy seriously, and does not simply treat the story as a counterexample to be dismissed.
