Write an essay on whether the bootstrapping problem (aka the easy knowledge problem) is fatal for process reliabilism about epistemic justification. Lay out the problem: reliabilism says a belief is justified iff it's produced by a reliable cognitive process, but then it looks like you can bootstrap your way to knowing your faculties are reliable just by using them — look at a red wall, form the belief that it's red, note that it's in fact red, repeat, and inductively conclude your color vision works. This seems viciously circular, but reliabilism apparently licenses it. Weigh the main responses: the "bootstrapping reasoning is independently fallacious" reply, the "reliabilism can be patched to block it" reply, and the "easy knowledge isn't actually a problem, just accept it" reply. Argue for one. Don't just survey — take a side and defend it.
