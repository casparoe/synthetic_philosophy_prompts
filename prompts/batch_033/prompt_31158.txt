Write the joint statement that a lexical threshold negative utilitarian and a throughgoing commensurabilist about pleasure and pain would both sign after an adversarial collaboration. By "lexical threshold negative utilitarian" I mean someone who thinks some range of suffering (above a certain intensity, or of certain kinds) is lexically prior to all positive welfare: no finite amount of pleasure, preference-satisfaction, or other good can in principle outweigh it, so any world containing such suffering is worse than any world without it, no matter what else differs. By "commensurabilist" I mean someone who thinks pleasure and pain lie on a single cardinal scale and any pain can in principle be outweighed by a sufficiently large amount of good, even if the exchange rates are steep and ordinary heuristics treat the trade as obscene. The document should have: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign, including what the dispute is not about (e.g. both can agree intense suffering matters enormously, that many proposed tradeoffs are morally repugnant, that diminishing returns are real, and that folk judgments are messy), phrased so neither side would wince; (2) the precise residual disagreement, with each side's view worded in a way that side would accept and the other would recognize as the real position; (3) a disagreements tabel of cruxes: sub-questions such that resolving one would move at least one side. For each crux give a concrete case or kind of evidence both agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. Pay attention to the spots where the fight is partly verbal—e.g. what "outweigh" means, whether a threshold is a claim about value or about what choices are permitted, whether "finite" is doing real work—and say which parts you judge to be verbal and which genuine. Don't adjudicate who's right; produce the document both could put their names to.
