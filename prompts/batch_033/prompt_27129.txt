I've been building a small automated theorem prover as a hobby and I keep runing into a wall I don't have a word for. The system can prove plenty of things about arithmetic, and about its own code treated as data. But whenever I ask it to prove the general principle 'if the system proves P, then P' — the very thing that would let it trust its own conclusions — it gets stuck, unless it already has a proof of the particular P. If I add that principle as an axiom, the system goes haywire and can derive anything at all, which I'm told means it's inconsistent. It feels like a structural obstacle, not just a missing lemma. I know this is somewhere near Gödel, but it doesn't feel identical to the usual point about a system being unable to prove its own consistency. Is there a name for this specific obstacle, and can you say exactly how it differs from Gödel's second incompleteness theorem and from the halting problem? I'm not after just a label; I want to know what the obstacle actually is, where its edges are, and which of those nearby things it really is.
