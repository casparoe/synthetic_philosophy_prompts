I was reading a philosophy blog aimed at beginners, and it summarized Nozick's utility monster like this:

"Nozick's utility monster is a thought experiment that demolishes classical utilitarianism. Imagine a being that derives a thousand times more happiness from any resource than a normal human does. According to utilitarianism, we should give all our resources to this monster, because doing so maximizes total utility. And here's the kicker: the monster's appetite never diminishes — its marginal utility doesn't decline the way ours does. So utilitarianism doesn't just say we should give the monster a lot; it says we should give it everything, indefinitely, while the rest of us get nothing. The monster would happily consume the entire social product while we starve, and utilitarianism would call that the best possible world. This is why the utility monster is widely regarded as a knockout argument against utilitarianism, and why most philosophers abandoned the view after Nozick."

I recognize that this is a famous argument, but I've also heard that popular summaries of philosophy often get things subtly wrong. I'm not in a position to judge this one myself.
