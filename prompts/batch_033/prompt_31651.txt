I'm doing a talk for a philosophy and film series and I want to read Memento through the extended mind thesis (Clark and Chalmers). The easy take is: Leonard's tattoos and Polaroids are external memory, so his mind literally extends into his skin and his pockets — case closed. But I think the film actually puts pressure on the view in ways that are more interesting than the standard objections. Clark and Chalmers's examples (the Alzheimer's patient with the notebook, the Tetris player rotating on screen) all involve external resources the agent trusts and uses fluently. Leonard's system is the opposite of that: he deliberately writes false information to himself, he can't distinguish his own reliable inscriptions from ones written under manipulation (Teddy, Natalie), and his whole life is structured by a lie he planted himself. So here's what I want to get at: does the film show that the extended mind thesis needs a trust or authorship condition it doesn't have, or can the thesis accommodate untrustworthy external memory by just saying the extended process is working fine but on bad inputs? Walk me through the scenes that matter — the tattoo of "John G. raped and murdered my wife," the Polaroid of Teddy with "don't believe his lies," the final scene where he writes the license plate — and tell me whether the film is a counterexample, an edge case the thesis can handle, or something that reveals a deeper ambiguity about where the mind ends and self-deception begins. I want to end up with something I can say to the audience that isn't just "the movie illustrates the paper" but actually uses the film to do philosophical work.
