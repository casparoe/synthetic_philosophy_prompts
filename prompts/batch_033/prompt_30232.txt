Some friends and I have been reading through the old debates about the unity of the proposition, and we've gotten completely stuck on Bradley's regress. The two camps in our group are basically these. One side says the regress is vicious: if you analyze 'a is F' or 'a stands in R to b' into some constituents plus a relation, you then need something further to connect the relation to its terms, and so on forever, so no finite analysis ever captures what makes the proposition a unity instead of a mere list. The other side says the regress is harmless, because at each step you're just redescribing the same fact, and anyway instantiation isnt a relation like the others — it's more like a primitive tie that holds things together without itself needing to be held. We've been at this for months and nobodys budged. I have a hunch that the two sides just have different pictures of what an analysis is supposed to do: one wants a construction out of independently given parts, the other only wants a true description that doesn't have to build the unity from scratch. Is this a substantive metaphysical disagreement, a verbal one, or two different projects talking past each other? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — or should we expect the impasse to just sit there?
