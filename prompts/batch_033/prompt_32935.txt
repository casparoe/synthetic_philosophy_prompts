I'm trying to work out whether my views in population ethics hang together and I'd appreciate a consistency check. Here's where I've landed:

(1) I'm a negative utilitarian: reducing suffering has moral priority over promoting pleasure, even when the quantities of each are equal.

(2) I hold a lexical threshold view: above a certain intensity of suffering — the kind that undermines a being's capacity for agency or meaning-making — no amount of pleasure, however vast, can compensate for even a single instance of that suffering.

(3) I think pleasure and pain are commensurable in the sense that they can be located on a single cardinal welfare scale; I reject the view that they are incommensurable goods.

(4) I think the threshold in (2) is not merely a matter of our attitudes or a pragmatic heuristic; it tracks a genuine axiological joint in the nature of suffering.

(5) For pains below the threshold, I think ordinary trade-offs with pleasure are permissible, and the negative utilitarian priority in (1) is a matter of greater weight on the same scale rather than lexicality.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how seriously. For each tension, say what would have to give to resolve it, and identify which single revision across the set would do the most to restore consistency at the lowest philosophical cost. Please write your answer in American English.
