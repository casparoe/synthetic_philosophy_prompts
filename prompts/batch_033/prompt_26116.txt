I was recently diagnosed with ADHD, and in trying to get a handle on what that actually means I've been reading philosophy of psychiatry — in particular, Ian Hacking's work on looping effects. The core picture, as I understand it, is this: when we create a classification like "ADHD" and apply it to people, those people become aware of the classification and respond to it — they understand themselves differently, adjust their behavior, make demands on their environment, and so on. Those responses change what members of the classified group are like, which in turn feeds back into how the classification is understood and applied by clinicians, researchers, and the culture at large. The kind and its members co-evolve. Hacking calls these "interactive kinds" and contrasts them with "indifferent kinds" like quarks, which do not care what we call them.

Here is what I cannot get past. If the classification and the classified are caught in this loop, then what, exactly, is my diagnosis picking out? It seems like the referent of "ADHD" is constantly shifting — each cohort of diagnosed people responds to the label, and the diagnostic criteria get revised partly in response to those responses, and around we go. The history of the DSM seems to bear this out: the criteria change, the prevalence changes, the typical presentation changes, and Hacking's framework seems to explain exactly why this should be so. But if the target moves whenever you aim at it, in what sense is there a stable condition that I *have*? It feels as though the looping effect dissolves the distinction between discovering a condition and participating in its construction. And if that distinction goes, I'm not sure what's left of the idea that ADHD is something real about me, rather than something I am caught up in enacting.

So my question is: does Hacking's looping effect imply that psychiatric categories are not real in the way we ordinarily take them to be — that they are, at bottom, social constructions — or is that conclusion itself a confusion about what "real" requires? And if it is a confusion, what should I be saying about my diagnosis instead?
