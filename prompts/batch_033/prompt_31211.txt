I'm a novelist working on a scene in which a character has a hallucination that is, by stipulation, subjectively indistinguishable from a genuine perception. I've been reading about disjunctivism and I keep getting stuck. Disjunctivists insist that a veridical perception and an indistinguishable hallucination are fundamentally different kinds of state. But if the two are genuinely indistinguishable from the inside, then what the character is aware of must be exactly the same in both cases. Doesn't that concession hand the argument from illusion exactly what it needs — the conclusion that the character is never directly aware of the external world, only of something mind-dependent?
