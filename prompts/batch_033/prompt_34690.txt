I was talking with a friend who just finished Gödel, Escher, Bach and he summarized the MU-puzzle chapter for me like this:

"So Hofstadter gives you this little formal system with the string MI and four rules for transforming strings, and the puzzle is to get to MU. You mess around with it for a while and you can make all kinds of strings but never MU. Then he shows you that the only way to prove MU is unreachable is to step outside the system and use number theory — specifically, you notice that the number of I's in any reachable string is never a multiple of 3, and MU has zero I's, which is a multiple of 3. He calls this 'jumping out of the system,' or JOOTSing. The big idea is that every formal system has blind spots that you can only see from outside, which is just what Gödel proved — any consistent system rich enough to do arithmetic contains true statements it can't prove, and you can only see their truth by stepping outside the system. So the real lesson of the MU-puzzle is that consciousness is the ability to jump out of whatever system you're currently operating in. That's why computers can't be conscious: a program is stuck inside its own code, so it can never JOOTS. Human minds, on the other hand, can always reflect on their own rules and transcend them."
