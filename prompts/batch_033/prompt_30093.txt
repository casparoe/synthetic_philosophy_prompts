I'm taking a course on free will and moral responsibility this term, and I'm trying to sort out whether my own views form a coherent package before I write my final paper. Here's where I've landed after the first two-thirds of the course:

(a) I'm a determinist. Every human action is the causal upshot of prior states of the world plus the laws of nature, and there's no room for agent-causal gaps in that chain.

(b) I think the kind of free will that matters — the kind worth caring about — is compatibilist free will: roughly, the capacity to act on one's own reasons and values, free from coercion, manipulation, or incapacitating pathology.

(c) I think moral responsibility — the kind that licenses blame, punishment, and the reactive attitudes — requires that the agent could have done otherwise in a robust sense, not merely the conditional sense (where "she would have done otherwise if she had chosen differently" is the only kind of alternative possibility on offer).

(d) I find Frankfurt-style counterexamples convincing. It seems to me that an agent can be morally responsible for an action even if a counterfactual intervener ensures she couldn't have done otherwise, so long as the intervener never actually intervenes and the agent acts on her own reasons.

(e) I think P.F. Strawson was broadly right that the reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, indignation, and the rest — are justified and inescapable features of human life, and that the truth of determinism doesn't undermine them.

I have a nagging sense that some of these pull against each other, but I can't quite articulate which tensions are real and which are merely apparent. Could you go through these five commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only seem to be, and how severe each genuine tension is? For any real tensions, what would be the least costly revision to restore consistency, and what would I lose by making it? Finally, please indicate how confident you are in each of your assessments and single out the point you're least sure of.
