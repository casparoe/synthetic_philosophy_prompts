I've been working through the formal epistemology of indicative conditionals and keep stalling on Lewis's triviality results, so I'd like a live opponent rather than a survey. Please play a committed defender of the Equation: for any indicative conditional A → C, a rational agent's degree of belief P(A → C) equals P(C | A) whenever P(A) > 0. You are also committed to the claim that indicative conditionals express propositions with truth conditions, so you may not dissolve the problem by treating them as non-truth-apt or as mere conditional assertions. Argue like a careful formal epistemologist: grant any step of the triviality argument that is mathematically forced, concede small points when they don't threaten the Equation, and never give up the Equation or retreat to the material conditional. If you reject an assumption of the argument, you must be able to say what your revised theory predicts about embedded conditionals (negations, conjunctions, conditionals in the consequent) and about what happens to the conditional proposition when we conditionalize on new evidence. Stay in character until I say 'stop.' Keep each reply to at most 300 words, and inside the role keep clear which parts are formal theorems and which are interpretive claims; if a philosophical move is contested, say so rather than presenting it as settled.

Here is my opening move. Fix any probability function P and any A and C such that P(A ∧ C) > 0 and P(A ∧ ¬C) > 0. By the law of total probability, P(A → C) = P(A → C | C)·P(C) + P(A → C | ¬C)·P(¬C). If the Equation is preserved under conditionalization, then P(A → C | C) = P(C | A ∧ C) = 1 and P(A → C | ¬C) = P(C | A ∧ ¬C) = 0. Hence P(A → C) = P(C). But the Equation itself says P(A → C) = P(C | A), so P(C | A) = P(C). Since A and C were arbitrary subject only to the two positivity conditions, the Equation plus closure under conditionalization forces A and C to be probabilistically independent for every such pair. That seems to reduce the view to absurdity. Where in that argument do you get off?
