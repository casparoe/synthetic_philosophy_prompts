Right, I've been reading about mind uploading for a while now (mostly blog posts and the odd paper, so go easy on me) and I've ended up with a cluster of views that each feel right on their own, but I've never actually checked wether they fit together. I'm a backend engineer, so I keep reaching for systems metaphors, which is probably part of the problem. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) The mind is what the brain does: if you implement the same computations, you get the same mind, regardless of whether the substrate is neurons or silicon. (2) If you replaced my neurons one at a time with little chips that perform exactly the same function, I'd still be me at the end — like hot-swapping components in a running server without ever taking it down. (3) But if you scanned my brain and booted up a copy, that copy wouldn't be me. It'd be a fork with my memories, and if the original was destroyed in the process I'd be dead. (4) Whether I survive shouldn't depend on the speed of the swap or on whether it's done piecemeal — that feels like an implementation detail. (5) And the thing that actually matters for survival is psychological continuity: enough overlap of memories, plans, values, that sort of thing.

I have a feeling there's a contradiction or two hiding in there, but I can't pin down which pair is the real problem and which pair only looks like one. Could you act as a professional philosopher working on personal identity and philosophy of mind and give this a proper audit? Please start by brainstorming the possible trouble spots, then go through the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension and which dissolve on inspection, ordered by how bad they are, and for each real tension say what I'd have to give up and what that would cost me. Don't just tell me I can fix it all by changing definitions unless you actually think that's the right move.
