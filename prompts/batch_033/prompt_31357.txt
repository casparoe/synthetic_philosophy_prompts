Consider a social media platform whose hate-speech policy prohibits posting a particular racial slur regardless of context. Enforcement removes the slur when it appears inside a quotation, in a news report about the word itself, in a first-person reclamatory use by a member of the targeted group, or spelled with symbols replacing letters. Yet the same policy leaves up posts that convey the same derogatory attitude toward the same group by euphemism, circumlocution, or image, so long as the banned string never appears. What account of slurs does this policy presuppose? In particular, does it presuppose that the slur's derogatory force is part of the word's standing linguistic meaning, rather than something a speaker does with it in context? Reconstruct the implicit semantics/pragmatics, distinguish what the policy strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and say which rival views of slurs could equally license the written rule. Then explain whether the enforcement pattern is coherent under the presupposed view and how enforcement would have to change if the platform adopted a consistently use-based view on which conveying the same derogatory attitude by euphemism is just as sanctionable as using the slur.
