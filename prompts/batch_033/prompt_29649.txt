I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, so bare with me if I'm missing something basic here. Lately a bunch of people I follow have been sharing this line from Simone Weil: "A political party is a machine to generate collective passions." They use it as if it settles the argument against political parties — like, parties are basically factories for manufacturing outrage and tribalism, and once you see that, it's obvious they should be abolished. But when I actually try to parse the sentence I realize I don't know what it's claiming. "Machine" — literal, like a deterministic mechanism? "Generate" — does it mean parties create passions from nothing, or just amplify and channel passions that already exist? "Collective passions" — does that cover all group emotions, including solidarity and hope, or just the ugly in-group/out-group stuff? So what I'd like is a menu of the different precise claims this one-liner could be making, from the tame reading a defender of ordinary party politics could shrug and accept, to the radical reading that actually does the work my friends want it to do. For each reading, say whether it's plausibly true, whether it's trivial or substantive, and whether it actually gets you to Weil's conclusion that parties should be abolished. I want to be able to go back and say "look, that sentence can mean five different things, and the only reading that's both true and radical isn't the one you're defending."
