I've somehow ended up giving a talk at our local EA meetup next month, and its turned into a whole thing. The title is something like "Why Kelly betting, not EV maximisation, should be our default for repeated decisions under uncertainty." I got into this after a long arguement with a friend about why we shouldn't just take every positive-expected-value bet we're offered, even when the stakes are small — I kept saying the log of your bankroll is what you actually care about if you plan to keep playing, and he kept saying that's just risk aversion with a utility function you don't have. The talk is a cleaned-up version of that: for repeated bets, maximising expected log wealth beats maximising expected wealth over time, the time-average growth rate is the thing that actually compounds, and a policy of taking all positive-EV bets is eventually ruinous, so Kelly (or fractional Kelly) is the sensible default, with geometric rationality as the underlying norm.

The audience is mostly hardcore expected-value maximisers — the kind of people who'll say the St Petersburg paradox isn't a paradox and you just pay the expected value — plus a couple of people who are into the ergodicity/geometric-rationality literature and will be sympathetic but picky, and one philosopher who keeps asking what the long run even means when you only live once. I want a Q&A prep sheet, not a talk outline. What are the eight or so objections I'll actually get, roughly in the order they'll come, phrased the way this audience would phrase them? For each: how much damage it does, what I should concede, what I should hold, and a reply I can actually deliver in two minutes. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks — I have a feeling it's about whether Kelly gives any guidance at all when the probabilities are made up, but maybe it's something worse.
