I work in the engagement team at an NHS trust, the bit that runs public consultations on what the trust should keep funding and what it should cut when the budget won't stretch. Every few months we run a meeting where residents tell us what they think should be prioritised, and afterwords I write a note for the board saying which views we took seriously and which we set aside. The thing I keep getting stuck on is that I can't explain, even to myself, where the line is between someone's considered view and someone's bias. People say things like 'why should my taxes pay for people who smoked' or 'mental health isn't a real illness like cancer' or 'we should look after our own before people who've only just got here.' Some of that is clearly out of order, but the line between 'that's a moral view about responsibility and desert' and 'that's just prejudice' feels like it moves depending on who said it and whether I happen to agree. I don't want our consultation to be fake, where we only count the views that match what the trust was going to do anyway, but I also don't want to dress up every nasty comment as a legitimate preference.

Could you write me a procedure my team could actually run on a comment that comes up in one of these meetings? There are four of us, none of us with any philosophy background, and we usually have about two weeks between the meeting and the board paper. I'd like it as a set of questions we ask in order, with some idea of what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure says 'set this one aside,' and where it says 'this is a genuine value judgement, a person has to make it, don't pretend the checklist made the call.' Be honest about which steps are really judgement calls dressed up as procedure, and about the cases where the procedure is most likely to misfire. Then run it on three examples: (1) someone saying the trust shouldn't fund IVF because having children is a luxury not a medical need, (2) someone saying we shouldn't pay for interpreter services because if you live here you should speak English, and (3) someone saying dementia care should be funded before cancer screening because people with dementia have no voice and cancer already gets all the charity money. And make sure at least one of the worked examples is a case where your procedure tells me I have to count a view that I'm going to hate putting in front of the board.
