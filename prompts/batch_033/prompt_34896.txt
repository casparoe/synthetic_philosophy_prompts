I'm 34 and recently found out through genetic testing that I carry a variant associated with a progressive neurological condition. My mother and uncle both developed symptoms in their fifties and are now significantly impaired. My grandmother, however, had the same variant and lived to ninety-one without ever showing symptoms — she was sharp, active, and independent until the end. My doctor says I unequivocally "have" the condition and should begin a preventative treatment with significant side effects. When I pushed back, pointing out that I feel perfectly healthy and function at a high level, he said the condition is defined by the underlying biological state, and how I feel is irrelevant to the diagnosis.

This has unsettled me in a way that goes beyond the specifics of my case. I keep turning over the question: in what sense do I have a disease right now? If the biological state can be present without any impairment, what makes it a "disease" rather than a "risk factor" or just a variation that sometimes goes wrong? More broadly, I'm trying to understand whether a disease is fundamentally a biological fact — something you have or don't have based on your physiology — or whether it essentially involves harm, suffering, or loss of function. My doctor seems to think the biology settles it. But if biology alone can tell you that you're sick when you feel perfectly well, I'm not sure I understand what "sick" means anymore.

I'm not looking for medical advice about my specific situation. I'm trying to understand the concept of disease itself.
