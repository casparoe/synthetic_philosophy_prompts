I host a philosophy podcast focusing on metaethics, and my next guest is a philosopher who has spent much of her career defending the internal reasons thesis — the view, originating with Bernard Williams, that an agent has a reason to φ only if there is a sound deliberative route from the agent's existing subjective motivational set to a motivation to φ. I want to go beyond having her restate the view. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press the genuine pressure points in the literature. I want at least one question on each of the following: the charge that the "sound deliberative route" condition is too vague to do real work; the objection from agents who seem to lack even prudential motivations (the apathetic or self-destructive agent who does not care about their own future); McDowell's challenge about conversion experiences that bypass deliberation entirely; and the worry that internal reasons cannot ground the categorical force we expect moral reasons to have. For each question, include the follow-up I should ask if she gives the standard reply her position is known for.
