So I just spent like three hours going in circles with my friend about sphexishness and free will — and this is maybe the fourth time we've had basically this same argument — and I'm honestly not sure what kind of argument we were even having. You know the setup: there's this wasp, Sphex, that does this rigid routine — stings a cricket to paralyze it, drags it to the burrow, leaves it at the entrance, goes inside to check, comes back out, then hauls the cricket in. If you move the cricket a couple inches while it's inside checking, it drags it back to the entrance and checks again, and you can do this over and over and it never just skips the check. My friend's take is that humans are exactly the same, just with more loops and better camouflage. He says we have our scripts — morning routines, social rituals, arguments we rehearse — and when the world interrupts a script we just restart it from an earlier step, and the feeling of "noticing" is itself just another scripted step that can loop. So free will, on his view, is a story we tell about machinery we can't see.

I kept pushing back that it's a difference in kind, not degree. The wasp can't step back from the routine; we can. The whole reason the wasp's rigidity looks funny to us is that we can represent the routine as a routine and ask whether to keep doing it. That's not one more loop, it's a different capacity — the thing wich you'd need for free will, or at least for the kind of flexibility that matters.
