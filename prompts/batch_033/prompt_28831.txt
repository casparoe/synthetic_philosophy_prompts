Here's what the student wrote:

"An epistemic bubble is when a person is cut off from relevant information or viewpoints, but not because anyone is actively deceiving them — it's just a gap in their exposure. Eli Pariser's work on epistemic bubbles shows how algorithmic curation can leave people underinformed without anyone intending to deceive them. An echo chamber, on the other hand, is when a person has been trained to distrust sources outside their group. The key difference is that a bubble is an omission, while an echo chamber is an active attack on trust. This means they respond differently to attempts to fix them. You can pop a bubble fairly easily: just show the person the missing evidence or diversify their media diet. An echo chamber is harder, because the person won't accept outside information as credible. But even an echo chamber can eventually be broken through if you repeatedly expose the person to contrary evidence from credible outside sources, so long as the evidence is strong enough. Nguyen's point is that echo chambers are more serious than bubbles because they corrupt the very faculty we'd use to evaluate the evidence."
