I'm a painter, and a foundation director is the sole judge of a grant that would keep my studio open. He awards the grant only to artists he believes sincerely admire his own style, and he is good at telling real admiration from performance. A friend argues: the fact that the director will fund me only if I admire the style is a genuine reason to admire the style, and if I come to admire it on that basis, my admiration is appropriate, not phony. I feel the force of the first point, but the second unsettles me — the paintings themselves haven't improved just because the director controls the grant. Is the grant a reason to admire the style? If it is, what kind of reason is it, and does it make the style more admirable?
