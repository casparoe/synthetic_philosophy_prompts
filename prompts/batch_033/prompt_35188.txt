I'm a journalist working on a piece about the conceptual foundations of AI alignment, and I want to understand where the real uncertainties lie. Please give me your credence — a number between 0 and 1 — that the problem of low-impact AI can be solved to a satisfactory degree without first solving the problem of value specification. By "low-impact AI" I mean the challenge of designing AI systems that achieve their assigned goals without causing unintended, large-scale side effects on the rest of the world. By "solved to a satisfactory degree" I mean that we can build systems whose side effects are reliably small enough that we would be comfortable deploying them in high-stakes settings. And by "without solving value specification" I mean without giving the system anything close to a full or near-full representation of what humans ultimately value.

Decompose your credence into at least the following terms, assigning a probability and a one- or two-sentence justification to each:

(1) The probability that the very concept of a "side effect" is inherently value-laden — that distinguishing a side effect from an intended effect requires a substantive normative standard, and that no value-neutral definition can capture what we actually care about.

(2) The probability that, even if a value-neutral definition of side effects is possible in principle, any such definition would be too weak or permissive to be practically useful — for example, because it would rule out only trivial perturbations while allowing harmful ones, or because it would be so computationally expensive to enforce that it offers no real safeguard.

(3) The probability that a satisfactory solution to the low-impact problem requires only a partial or approximate specification of human value, rather than a full one — that we can get most of the benefit with a rough sketch of what counts as a side effect, without needing anything close to a complete account of human preferences.

(4) Your conditional credence that the problem is solvable without value specification, given that the first two obstacles are surmountable — i.e., given that a value-neutral and practically adequate definition of side effects exists.

Then tell me how these terms combine to produce your overall credence. Finally, identify which of these terms your final number is most sensitive to — the one where a small shift in your probability would produce the largest shift in your overall credence — and describe what evidence or argument, if it were to materialize, would move that term by the largest margin.
