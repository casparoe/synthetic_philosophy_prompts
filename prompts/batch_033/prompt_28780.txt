i've been teaching the MU-puzzle from hofstadter's GEB to my formal systems class and i keep thinking about how the puzzle *is* a poem, or should be one. for those who don't know: you start with the string MI, and you have four typographic rules (add U after an I, double everything after M, replace III with U, delete UU). the goal is to produce MU. you can't — but proving you can't requires jumping out of the system (JOOTSing), mapping strings to numbers and noticing that the number of I's is never divisible by 3 for any reachable string.

here's what i want: a poem that IS a tiny formal system. maybe 14 lines where lines 1-4 set up the "axioms," lines 5-12 apply the "rules" (each stanza or couplet is a rule application, so the poem feels like it's grinding through derivations), and lines 13-14 are the JOOTS — they break the metrical or formal constraint the rest of the poem obeyed, and by breaking it, they reveal why the goal (MU / the poem's closure) was impossible within the constraint. the breaking of the form IS the argument. the content should be about trying and failing to reach something from inside a system, not just name-dropping hofstadter. someone who knows the puzzle should grin; someone who doesn't should still feel the claustrophobia and then the release. you can pick the meter and the constraint, but whatever rule the first 12 lines follow, the last two have to violate it in a way that feels earned and not sloppy — the reader should realize the rule was always a cage.
