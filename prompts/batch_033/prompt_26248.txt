I'm giving a talk next month at a workshop on formal epistemology where I'm arguing that Solomonoff induction can't ground a substantive version of Occam's razor—like, not just "there are some technical issues," but that the whole project is circular once you take the reference machine problem seriously. My line is that the choice of reference machine isn't a parameter you can handwave away as asymptotically irrelevant; it encodes exactly the simplicity judgments the framework was supposed to derive, and there's no way to pick one without already having views about which hypotheses are simpler. The audience is a weird mix: maybe half philosophers of science (a lot of them already think formalizing simplicity is a dead end), a third CS/ML people who basically think the universal prior has solved induction and philosophers are just being fussy, and some physicists and biologists who heard the word "induction" and showed up. I need a Q&A prep sheet: the eight or ten objections I'm going to get, grouped by which camp they're from, phrased the way those people would actually say them, with a roughly two-minute reply for each and a candid note on where my reply is weak. And include the one or two questions I'm really hoping nobody in the room thinks to ask.
