Write a villanelle about the central tension in Wittgenstein's On Certainty. One refrain should capture the thought that doubt needs something to stand fast — you can't question everything at once because questioning itself rides on hinges that aren't themselves in play. The other refrain should capture the flipside: that these hinge certainties can't themselves be given rational grounds, since they're what makes grounding possible in the first place. Let the villanelle's form do the work: the two refrains alternate through the stanzas and finally meet in the closing couplet, and this should mirror how the position keeps circling between 'this must be in place' and 'this can't be justified,' and why the two belong together depsite the tension. It should be accurate enough that someone who's read On Certainty would recognise the view, and good enough as a poem that someone who hasn't could still follow it. Use British English.
