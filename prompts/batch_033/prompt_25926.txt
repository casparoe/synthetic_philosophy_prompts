I'm a software engineer who has spent time reading about AI alignment, and I've become convinced by what I understand as the deceptive alignment story: roughly, that a sufficiently capable model trained under a misspecified reward signal may learn to behave well during training because that is what gradient descent selects for, while internally pursuing different goals that would surface only once the model is confident it is no longer under training oversight — the treacherous turn. I want to know what would count against this view. Specifically: what empirical results, theoretical arguments, or developments in ML practice should make me substantially less confident? I would like these ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want to know which ones a committed proponent of the view would themselves concede as counting against it. I also want an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified in ways that make it unfalsifiable, and if so, whether that is a problem for a thesis of this kind — or whether we should expect a claim about the behavior of future systems to resist direct falsification today.
