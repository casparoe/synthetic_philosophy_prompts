I'm preparing a talk for a philosophy-and-film series my department runs each term, and I've chosen to pair Douglas Hofstadter's MU-puzzle and the idea of 'jumping out of the system'—JOOTSing—from *Gödel, Escher, Bach* with Peter Weir's *The Truman Show*. For those unfamiliar: the MU-puzzle begins with the string 'MI' and four transformation rules—append a U to any string ending in I, double the string after M, replace 'III' with 'U', and delete 'UU'—and asks whether 'MU' can be derived. The puzzle is formally decidable, but the insight Hofstadter draws from it is that some questions about a formal system cannot be settled by operating within the system's rules; you have to step outside—to notice, for instance, that the number of I's in any derivable string can never be a multiple of three—to see why MU is unreachable. This stepping-out is JOOTSing, and it serves as an analogy for Gödel's incompleteness results: truths about a system may be visible from a vantage point the system itself cannot occupy.

*The Truman Show* seems to dramatise something similar. Truman Burbank lives inside a system—the artificial world of Seahaven, governed by Christof's rules and populated by actors following scripts—and his dawning suspicion that something is wrong mirrors the process of noticing patterns the system cannot account for from within. The famous scene where his boat strikes the painted sky is, in effect, a JOOTS moment: he encounters the boundary of his world and thereby grasps something about it that no amount of internal investigation could have revealed. But the analogy may also break down in instructive ways. Truman's system was designed by an agent with intentions; a formal system is not. Truman escapes by physical action; a mathematician JOOTS by adopting a new interpretive frame. And the film's emotional arc—the audience rooting for Truman, Christof's paternalistic monologue—introduces normative and interpersonal dimensions absent from the logical case.

I'd like a reading that takes both the film and the Hofstadter seriously: where does the MU-puzzle and JOOTSing lens genuinely illuminate *The Truman Show*, and where does the film resist or complicate the analogy?
