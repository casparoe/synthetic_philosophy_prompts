I work as a software engineer at a company that builds supply-chain and compliance tools for the seafood industry. We're developing a new module for aquaculture certifications — basically, instead of just checking whether a farm follows a static checklist of practices (like "stocking density below X kg/m³"), we want the software to help auditors and farm managers actually reason through whether a proposed operational change improves animal welfare. The idea is to embed some kind of decision procedure that a non-philosopher can follow step by step, and that I can turn into a guided workflow in the app.

The domain is finfish and crustacean aquaculture — salmon, trout, shrimp, that sort of thing. I've been reading enough to know that the question of whether fish and crustaceans even feel pain or suffer is genuinely contested, and that even if you grant they do, it's not obvious what "welfare" means for a shrimp versus a salmon — totally different sensory systems, different needs, different behaviors. So the procedure can't just assume that whatever matters for a pig matters for a prawn.

Here's what I need: a decision procedure for evaluating a proposed change to aquaculture practices from the standpoint of animal welfare. It has to be something I can code into a questionnaire-style workflow. The auditor or farm manager opens the module, describes the change they're considering, and the system walks them through a series of questions that determine whether the change is a welfare improvement, a welfare regression, or ambiguous, and why.

The procedure has to handle the hard cases. For instance:

- What if a change improves one dimension of welfare but worsens another (e.g., switching to a slower-growing strain that has better health outcomes but lives longer at higher density)?
- What if there's genuine scientific uncertainty about whether a particular species can suffer in a relevant way — do we default to "assume they can" or "wait for evidence"?
- What if the change benefits a large number of animals slightly but harms a small number severely?
- At what point does the procedure say "we don't know enough, you need to bring in an expert" rather than giving a verdict?

I want the procedure to be honest about where judgment enters. Don't dress up value choices as technical questions. Flag the steps where a human has to make a call that the software can't make for them, and say what kind of call it is — a factual one, a values one, or a tradeoff one.

Then, run the procedure on two concrete proposals so I can see how it works in practice:

1. A shrimp farm considering switching from eyestalk ablation to a non-ablated breeding method (more expensive and slightly less productive but eliminates a painful procedure).
2. A salmon farm considering reducing stocking density by 20% (more per-fish space, probably less stress and disease, but requires more total pens, more handling events, and more infrastructure).

I know this is asking a lot. Also, at the end, please tell me how confident you are in each major part of the procedure you've given me, and single out the step or claim you're least sure about — I need to know where the weak points are before I build this into production code and someone's audit result hangs on it.
