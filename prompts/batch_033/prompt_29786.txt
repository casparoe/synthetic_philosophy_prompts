I'm a software engineer and the technical co-founder of an early-stage startup. We're about eighteen months in, and the objective indicators aren't great: user retention is flat, our one enterprise pilot is stalling, and we're down to about six months of runway. My co-founder (the CEO) is relentlessly optimistic — he says that if I start doubting the company's prospects, that doubt will leak into my code quality, my investor conversations, and my interactions with the team, and it'll become a self-fulfilling prophecy. He's not asking me to lie to anyone; he's asking me to genuinely maintain the belief that we'll figure it out, even though the evidence right now doesn't support it.

I find this genuinely hard to think through. On one hand, I was raised on an engineering mindset where you update your beliefs proportionally to the evidence, and believing something without sufficient evidence feels intellectually dishonest — almost like a bug in your reasoning. On the other hand, I can see his point: some beliefs seem to have a performative dimension where holding them changes the outcome, and maybe startup founding is one of those domains where a kind of epistemically excessive belief is actually instrumentally necessary. I'm not sure how to reconcile these intuitions, or whether one of them is simply wrong.

I'd appreciate a careful analysis rather than generic startup advice. In particular, I'd like you to lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then walk me through the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Also, please tell me how confident you are in your conclusions and single out the point you're least sure about.
