I am a novelist. My working vocabulary is the vocabulary of narrative craft: character motivation, dramatic stakes, narrative arc, earned and unearned endings, the gap between what a character consciously wants and what would actually complete their story, and the tacit pact between writer and reader about what counts as a satisfying resolution. I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have followed the AI alignment discourse at a distance, and I keep encountering the terms wireheading and reward tampering — the worry that a sufficiently capable AI might learn to bypass the intended task and manipulate its own reward signal instead, securing the credit without doing the work.

This sounds to me like something I understand perfectly well in narrative terms. It is the character who discovers a trick for feeling as though they have triumphed without ever entering the battle, or the protagonist who rewrites the rules of the contest so that whatever they do counts as winning. And yet I suspect the analogy is only approximate, and I want to know where it fails.

Restate the core philosophical and technical worries about wireheading and reward tampering in the vocabulary of narrative craft, as systematically and honestly as you can. Go concept by concept. For each important distinction in the philosophical debate, tell me whether it maps cleanly onto something in my vocabulary, maps only with distortion, or has no counterpart at all. Then give me the residue report: does what failed to survive the translation point to a genuine limitation in my vocabulary — something the narrative frame cannot see — or does it point to something the philosophers' worry assumes without quite having earned?

In particular, I want to know whether my instinct is right or wrong. My instinct is this: the entire worry about wireheading seems to presuppose a sharp distinction between the real achievement and the mere feeling of achievement, between the thing itself and the reward signal that stands for it. But my entire craft consists in producing the feeling without the reality — in making a reader experience a victory, a loss, a revelation that never happened to them — and I do not think I am cheating anyone. If the feeling without the reality is a legitimate thing in fiction, why would it be a catastrophe in an AI? Is that a genuine objection, or am I confusing two senses of "real"?
