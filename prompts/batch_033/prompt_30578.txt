I keep seeing the raven paradox treated as a puzzle with a clever fix, where each proposed solution makes a different move and nobody states the standard by which the moves should be judged. I want that standard first. Write the desiderata that an adequate account of confirmation, as tested by Hempel's raven paradox, must satisfy, with the case or consideration behind each: the equivalence condition and what it costs to reject it; Nicod's criterion and whether it is a genuine requirement or a pre-theoretic prejudice; the datum that a black raven evidently confirms the hypothesis that all ravens are black while a red herring or a white shoe at most negligibly confirms it; the role of background information about the relative sizes of the relevant classes; and the link to actual scientific practice, such as why ornithologists examine ravens rather than compile inventories of non-black objects. For each desideratum, mark it as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp. Then identify which subsets of the list cannot be jointly satisfied — this is the part I most want to see, since it would show what the paradox really is. Finally, name the single requirement that does the most to exclude the standard candidate solutions, and briefly note how the Bayesian, the Hempelian, and the projectibility or natural-kind responses fare against it.
