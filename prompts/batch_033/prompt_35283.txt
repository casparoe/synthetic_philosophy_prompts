I'm drafting a novel whose protagonist, Elena, is a morally reflective person who works out her positions through concrete cases rather than by articulating abstract principles. Over the course of the manuscript, she has expressed the following verdicts:

- When a friend serves her factory-farmed chicken, she quietly declines and later reflects that eating it would have been wrong, even though her single meal wouldn't have changed how many chickens are raised.
- During an election chapter, she doesn't vote. When challenged, she says she lives in a district that isn't competitive; her vote wouldn't decide anything, so skipping it isn't wrong.
- She confronts a neighbor who dumps used motor oil down a storm drain, calling it wrong even though one more quart in an already-degraded watershed makes no measurable difference.
- She throws a plastic bottle in the trash rather than walking it to the recycling bin, and when a friend calls her on it she says it isn't wrong — one bottle is a rounding error.
- She refuses to cross a picket line to shop at a grocery store whose workers are striking, saying it would be wrong even though her single purchase wouldn't affect the strike's outcome.
- On a weekend away, she takes a very long shower in a region under drought restrictions, and when her host mentions it she says it isn't wrong — her shower isn't what's draining the reservoir.

In the chapter I'm drafting now, she's traveling and wants to buy a bar of chocolate. She learns that the brand uses child labor in its cocoa supply chain. The chocolate is already on the shelf; her individual purchase won't change the company's practices or affect any specific child's working conditions.

I need to know what Elena would say. I'm less interested in the answer itself than in the rule or rules that could jointly explain her earlier verdicts, which of those rules would say what about the chocolate, and which one best captures who she is. If her earlier verdicts genuinely underdetermine the answer, I'd like to know what principle I'd be committing her to with each choice — and what further case I could write into a later chapter that would settle which rule she's actually running.
