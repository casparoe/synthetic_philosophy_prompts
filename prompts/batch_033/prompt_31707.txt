"The bootstrapping problem, also known as the easy knowledge problem, is an argument against reliabilism about justification. Imagine you believe your gas gauge is reliable because you've observed over many drives that when it reads 'full,' the tank is full, and when it reads 'empty,' the tank is empty. But here's the thing: to know the tank was full on any of those occasions, you had to rely on the gauge reading in the first place — you didn't have independent access to the fact. So you're using the gauge to confirm the gauge, which is circular reasoning. Reliabilism says a belief is justified just in case it's produced by a reliable process. So it seems to license this: the process of reading the gauge and forming the belief is reliable, so the belief about the tank is justified, and then the process of tracking those readings over time is also reliable, so the belief that the gauge is reliable comes out justified too. But this is clearly illegitimate — you can't earn knowledge through a circle. Therefore reliabilism has to be rejected."
