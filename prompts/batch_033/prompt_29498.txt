I keep running into the same argument in alignment circles and it never goes anywhere. One side says an aligned AI should just aggregate everyone's values into something like CEV or a big utility function, and that the usual objections—Arrow, population ethics, moral uncertainty—are just engineering problems to be solved well enough. The other side says the whole project is illiberal: the AI shouldn't pick any particular weighting of people's values or conceptions of the good, it should stay neutral between them, maybe act as a facilitator or let each person's values govern their own corner. Both sides seem to accept the same formal results and the same broad liberal premises, but nobody moves. My working theory is that this is really a disagreement about what it means to "respect" someone's values—whether respect means counting their values in one common currency, or refusing to trade them off against anyone else's—and that the two sides are answering different questions under the single label "alignment." Is that right, or is there a real factual or formal disagreement hiding in here? I want a diagnosis of the structure of the impasse, not a survey of positions, and an honest answer about what if anything would actually move it: a distinction, a theorem, a change of question, or just accepting that it's bedrock.
