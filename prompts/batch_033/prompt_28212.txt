As a retired evolutionary biologist who has only recently started reading philosophy of biology in earnest, I've been turning over the debate between etiological and causal-role accounts of biological function. The causal-role view—that a trait's function is simply whatever contribution it makes to some system-level capacity we choose to analyze—strikes me as too promiscuous and too dependent on our explanatory interests to capture what biologists actually mean when they say the function of the heart is to pump blood. But I worry I'm just defaulting to what feels congenial given my training. I'd like you to make the strongest argument you can for the etiological account: that the function of a trait is what it was selected for. In doing so, please address what you take to be the most serious objection to the etiological account and explain why it does not defeat the view. I'd also like you to tell me which argument or consideration would most likely change your mind about the superiority of the etiological account—where your reasoning is most vulnerable to failure. Please write in American English.
