I am a software engineer working on causal inference for product experimentation, and I have no formal background in philosophy, so I may be using some of these terms loosely. A colleague recently laid out an argument for me, and I have been unable to find the flaw. It goes like this.

(1) On the interventionist account of causation, X causes Y just in case there is some possible intervention on X with respect to Y such that, if the intervention were performed, the value of Y or its probability distribution would change while an appropriate set of other variables is held fixed. (2) But whether a manipulation counts as an intervention is defined relative to a causal model: the manipulation must be exogenous to that model, must override the other causes of X, and must affect Y only through X and variables downstream of X. (3) Consequently, whether a given manipulation counts as an intervention, and therefore whether X causes Y, depends on which variables, functional relationships, and background conditions the model includes; two models of the same system can apparently return conflicting verdicts about the same physical process. (4) Yet whether X causes Y is an objective fact about the world, not a fact about how we choose to represent the world. (5) So the interventionist must either deny the objectivity of causal claims or supply a principled, interest-independent rule for selecting the one correct model. (6) The interventionist account supplies no such rule; its central notion is explicitly model-relative. (7) Therefore the interventionist is committed to saying that causal facts are model-relative, and that sounds like a euphemism for saying that causation is subjective. But that conclusion seems to prove too much, since it would make the causal claims we rely on in randomized experiments less objective than they appear.

I would prefer to keep step (4), but I also do not want to give up interventionism, because the Lewis-style counterfactual alternative seems to face a parallel problem about the choice of events and similarity relations. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If more than one response is available, I want to know what each major option gives up.
