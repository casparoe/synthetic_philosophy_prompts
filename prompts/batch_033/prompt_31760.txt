ok so I'm a few years into a phil mind PhD and I've been trying to sort out what I actually think about belief attribution to AI systems. Here's where I've landed, roughly, and I'd like someone to tell me whether it hangs together:

(1) I'm basically a Dennettian about belief: the intentional stance is all there is. You attribute beliefs when doing so buys you predictive or explanatory leverage, and there's no further fact about whether the system "really" believes.

(2) Current LLMs are in the sweet spot where the intentional stance is clearly useful — if I want to predict what GPT-4 will say next, treating it as having beliefs about the world works better than any other stance I've got.

(3) But I also think there's a difference between the kind of belief attribution we do to LLMs and the kind we do to humans, and it matters. Humans have a unified, temporally extended agency — plans, commitments, things they care about across time — that makes belief attributions hang together in way they don't for a system that gets reset each conversation.

(4) I think we should be pretty reluctant to attribute a belief to a system that can't update that belief through interaction with the world. If the system can't go out and get new evidence, the attribution feels shallow in a way I want to take seriously.

(5) I also think the whole debate about "does the AI really believe" is mostly a distraction — what matters is what happens downstream when we treat it as a believer, not whether the attribution is metaphysically kosher.

So that's the set. I can feel that some of these pull against each other but I'm not sure which tensions are real vs. which are just me being sloppy. If you had to rank the tensions and tell me which one or two commitments I'd need to revise to get a clean package, what would you say? I want to know what the cheapest fix is and what it costs.
