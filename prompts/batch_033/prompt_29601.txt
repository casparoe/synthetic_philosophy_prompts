ok so i've been going down a rabbit hole on the unilateralist's curse — the situation where a bunch of people each have the power to unilaterally do some big, irreversible thing, and they disagree about whether it's net good, and because any one of them can act without the others' permission, the person whose private estimate is most optimistic is the one who ends up deciding for everyone, even if nine of ten would have stood down. i want a villanelle about this. the two refrains should be the two halves of the curse: one line for the structural fact that a single yes can override every no, and one line for the private judgment each person trusts ('one yes is all it takes' and 'my own estimate says go' are the kind of thing i mean, but you can reword them). as the refrains take turns through the stanzas it should feel like a series of reasonable people each checking there own numbers, mostly abstaining, and the final couplet where the two refrains meet should be the moment the curse actually lands — the group realizing that the most optimistic estimate has already decided for all of them.

set it around something concrete, like publishing a dangerous genome sequence or a geoengineering field test or releasing a capable model, so the stakes are felt, but don't lecture. it needs to be right enough that someone who knows the problem would recognize the mechanism, and good enough that someone who doesn't would still get the logic from the poem itself.
