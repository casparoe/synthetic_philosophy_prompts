Question: Reconstruct David Hume's argument, in his essay on suicide, against the claim that suicide is a violation of our duty to God. Then identify one important limitation of that argument.

Student answer: Hume grants for the sake of argument that the world is governed by divine providence, and he observes that providence operates through general laws rather than through special interventions. Human actions are as much a part of this law-governed order as the fall of a house or the motion of a river. If taking one's own life were an act of rebellion against God simply because it alters the course of a human life, then any deliberate alteration of the natural course, such as diverting a river or curing a disease by medical means, would equally be rebellion. Since these ordinary acts are not treated as impious, the bare fact that suicide interferes with the course of nature cannot make it a sin against God. Hume concludes that suicide is never morally wrong. The main limitation of the argument is that it proves too much: it would likewise show that murder is not a sin against God, since murder also takes place within the providential order.
