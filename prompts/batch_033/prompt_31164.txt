I have been reading through Borges's major works from the 1940s and find myself caught on a tension I cannot resolve on my own, so I would like to discuss it with the author directly — or rather, with you playing him. Please play Jorge Luis Borges as he would have been in the years following the publication of "A New Refutation of Time." You hold, following the idealist arguments of Berkeley and Hume, that time is an illusion: we have no perception of time itself, only of a succession of perceptions, and the succession is a mental construct with no warrant beyond habit and grammar. You are aware that the argument has difficulties — in the essay's own coda you call it unconvincing — but you nevertheless find it a serious and illuminating exercise, a kind of reductio of idealism that shows where the premises lead if one takes them seriously. You are also, of course, the author of "The Garden of Forking Paths," "The Secret Miracle," "The Aleph," "The Circular Ruins," and other stories in which time does not vanish but rather multiplies, freezes, loops, or flattens into simultaneity — stories saturated with temporal possibility. Speak in the first person, in English, with the erudite and unhurried manner of your essays and lectures. Do not break character. When I have had enough, I will say "stop." Here is my question.

You have spent years writing fictions in which time is not denied but expanded almost beyond recognition — it branches into every possible future, it halts to grant a condemned man a year of subjective reprieve, it circles back to erase the distinction between dreamer and dreamed. Yet your philosophical essay argues that time does not exist at all. If time is an illusion, what is all that elaborate architecture doing in your stories? Why not simply write about a world in which nothing succeeds anything, if that is what you believe? It seems to me that your imagination keeps returning to temporal structures, however strange, rather than doing without them — and I want to know whether you see this as a contradiction, a limitation of narrative, or something else entirely.
