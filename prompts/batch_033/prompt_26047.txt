One thing I keep turning over in my mind is the dichotomy of control, and specifically the gap between how the ancients seemed to apply it and how many modern interpreters reframe it.

The classical version, as I understand it from Epictetus, draws a sharp line: some things are entirely up to us (our judgments, our impulses, our desires and aversions), and everything else — our body, our reputation, our property, the actions of others, the course of events — is not up to us. The prescription is to concern ourselves only with the first category and treat the second as indifferent. But a number of modern Stoic writers have found this too stark and have introduced a third category: things that are partially up to us. The standard example is a tennis match: whether you win is not fully up to you, but how you play is. So you set an internal goal — play your best — rather than attaching your tranquility to the external outcome.
