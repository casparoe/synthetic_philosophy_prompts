I've been turning over Fitch's paradox for a fortnight and I can't see the join, because the premises all look modest and the conclusion is obviously mad. I'm doing an epistemology module, and the anti-realist line we were given is that every truth is knowable: not that anyone has actually got round to knowing it, just that for any true proposition it's possible in principle for some suitably placed inquirer to know it. That seemed a fairly modest slogan — it's just the thought that truth can't outrun our cognitive capacities. But then there's a derivation, which I gather is due to Fitch, that seems to show the modest slogan collapses into the arrogant one, namely that every truth is actually known. It runs like this:

(1) Assume every truth is knowable: for any proposition p, if p is true, then it is possible that someone knows p.
(2) Now suppose, for reductio, that there is a true proposition p which nobody in fact knows, and consider the proposition q: p is true and nobody knows p.
(3) q is true, given what we supposed about p, so by (1) it is possible that someone knows q.
(4) Knowing q would mean knowing both conjuncts. So a possible knower of q would know that p, and would also know that nobody knows p.
(5) Knowledge is factive, so if they know that nobody knows p, then nobody knows p. But they themselves would know p — contradiction.
(6) So q cannot possibly be known.
(7) By (1) and (6), q must be false. That is, there is no truth of the form q.
(8) But p was arbitrary, so there can be no unknown truths; and since 'there is no unknown truth' is just another way of saying every truth is known, we get the absurd conclusion.

I can't find the step to give up. (4) only unpacks what it is to know a conjunction, and (5) is non-negotiable if knowledge is meant to be knowledge rather than lucky belief. That leaves either restricting (1) somehow, or blaming something in the logic — the move at the very end from 'there is no unknown truth' to 'every truth is known' feels a bit quick, but I can't say why, and anyway a modus tollens shouldn't be the culprit. Which premise or step should the anti-realist reject, and what does each option cost them? In particular, is there any way of keeping the spirit of 'every truth is knowable' without the conclusion that every truth is known, or is the slogan just sunk?
