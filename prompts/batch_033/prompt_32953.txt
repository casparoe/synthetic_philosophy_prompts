I've been reading about the debate over actual infinities — whether there can be a completed, actual infinite collection (like the set of all natural numbers existing as a finished totality) or whether infinity can only be "potential" (an unending process, like counting, that never reaches completion). On one side you have Cantorians and mainstream set theorists who work with transfinite numbers and completed infinite sets; on the other you have finitists who think this is a mistake and that only finite mathematical objects are legitimate.

I'm curious what a good-faith adversarial collaboration between these two camps would produce — not a debate, but a document both sides could sign. Please draft it.

Include:

- A numbered section of claims both sides agree on. These should be substantive, not just "we agree that 2+2=4." Cover things like: what is not being claimed by either side, what the formal relationship is between finitistic and infinitary mathematics (e.g., consistency results, reversals), and what practical consequences (if any) the disagreement has for the working mathematician or computer scientist.
- A disagreements table: for each point of disagreement, give the precise formulation of each side's position, phrased so that a proponent of that side would accept the wording.
- The cruxes: the sub-questions whose resolution would actually move at least one side. For each crux, describe what kind of argument or evidence both sides would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction.
- If any part of the dispute turns out to be verbal or terminological, identify it and explain why.
