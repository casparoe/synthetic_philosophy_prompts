I've sort of talked myself into a view about pain and pleasure and I want to know what could actually move me off it. I don't have any training in philosophy, I've just been reading essays online and arguing with friends. The view is that once suffering gets bad enough, no amount of mild pleasure can make up for it. A whole planet of people enjoying nice afternoons, good food, falling in love — none of it outweighs one person being tortured, and it would be wrong to press a button that trades the one for the other no matter how many pleasant afternoons you stack on the other side. I think it's because past a certain point pain and pleasure stop being on the same scale. I've seen people call this a lexical threshold, where the bad thing just wins regardless of quantity. I got here by thinking about smaller versions first: I wouldn't give someone a migraine so a hundred strangers could each get a free ice cream, and then I kept scaling up the ice cream side and the answer never flipped. But lately I've noticed the view is suspiciously easy to protect, because whenever someone describes a pleasure that seems to tip the scale I can just say the suffering was worse than they think or the pleasure was shallower than they think.

So what I'm actually asking is: what would count against this view? I don't want strawmen or the standard objections I can already bat away. I want the cases, arguments, or considerations that should genuinly move me, ranked from "this basically kills the view" to "this is just awkward." What would someone who really holds the lexical threshold view concede counts against it, and which objections would they shrug off? I'd personally find it most persuasive if someone could show the threshold isn't really a threshold — that pleasure and pain are the same kind of quantity after all and I've just been bad at comparing very large numbers. And be honest about whether the view has been insulated so much that nothing could touch it, and whether that's a problem for a moral view or just how moral views work.
