I was reading a blog post about voting theory and it summarized Arrow's theorem like this: "Arrow proved that no voting system can be truly fair. Any method of combining individual preference rankings into a group ranking must fail at least one of three basic conditions: transitivity of the group ranking, non-dictatorship, or independence of irrelevant alternatives. Majority rule fails transitivity because of Condorcet cycles, and ranked-choice voting fails IIA because a third candidate can spoil a race. So all real voting methods are either secretly dictatorial or produce irrational group preferences, and the search for a fair voting method is doomed in principle."

I'm inclined to beleive most of this because the Condorcet cycle thing is definitely real and I've heard people say ranked-choice has spoiler problems. But the last sentence feels too strong, and I have a vauge memory that Arrow's theorem has more conditions than the three listed.
