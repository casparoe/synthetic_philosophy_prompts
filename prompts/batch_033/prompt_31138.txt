I'm working on a piece about whether technologies have politics, and I keep tripping over the same exemple. The story everyone tells is that Robert Moses built the overpasses on the Long Island parkways low enough that buses couldn't pass under them, which meant that Jones Beach stayed effectively off-limits to poor and Black New Yorkers who relied on public transit. In the standard telling, the clearance itself is the racist policy, poured into concrete. But I've also read historians who say that's mostly myth: the low clearances were normal for parkways at the time, or about cost, or about keeping commercial vehicles out, not a deliberate plan to keep anyone off the beach. So now I'm stuck. If the famous example is shaky, doesn't the whole idea that artifacts have politics collapse into the much weaker claim that designers sometimes have political intentions? And if the politics of a bridge is just whatever the designer meant by it, why say the bridge is political rather than the designer? Am I missing something, or is that one bridge example doing all the work in the whole debate?
