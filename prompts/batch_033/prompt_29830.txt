I'd like your actual credence in the following proposition: Landauer's principle is a substantive physical constraint, one that does independent explanatory work in resolving Maxwell's demon, rather than a restatement of the Second Law in information-theoretic vocabulary. By substantive I mean the following: there is a coherent description of the demon-plus-environment in which the Second Law would fail unless erasing one bit of the demon's record costs at least kT ln 2 of entropy exported to the environment, and that erasure bound is not already implied by Hamiltonian evolution plus the Second Law itself. If the bound is a theorem of those ingredients alone, with information entering only as bookkeeping, the proposition is false.

I don't want a survey of positions or a hedge about definitions; I want a number and the decomposition that produces it. Decompose the question into at least these terms:

(a) The probability that erasure, rather than measurement or the demon's own operation, is the load-bearing step in the standard resolution — that is, that a mechanically coherent demon can be specified whose only remaining route to a Second Law violation is costless erasure.

(b) Conditional on erasure being load-bearing, the probability that the kT ln 2 bound constrains the dynamics in a way not already implied by Hamiltonian evolution plus the Second Law — that is, that the direction of explanation runs from the erasure bound to the Second Law in this case, rather than the reverse.

(c) The probability that the measurement step can be made thermodynamically reversible in the relevant sense, so that erasure really is the only place left for the entropy bookkeeping to close.

(d) The probability that the slogan that information is physical carries more than the truism that information must be encoded in physical degrees of freedom.

For each term, give a number and one or two lines of justification. Then show how the terms combine to your headline credence, including any interactions if the combination is not multiplicative. Finally, say which term your headline credence is most sensitive to, which term you are least confident in, and what specific result — a simulation, a theorem, a sharper formulation of the demon — would move that term most.
