I've been reading about Kepler and his weird relationship with astrology — he cast horoscopes for money and seemed to genuinely believe in some kind of astral influence, but he also spent a lot of energy reforming astrology and throwing out the parts he thought were superstitious nonsense (like most of the zodiac stuff). So he was neither a true believer nor a debunker. Here's what I'm wondering: if Kepler were around today and saw what's happened to astrology — on the one hand, it's been completely rejected by science and lumped in with pseudoscience, and on the other it's evolved into this whole psychological, Jung-influenced thing where people talk about it as a tool for self-knowledge rather than literal prediction — what would he make of it? Would he be on the side of the debunkers, or would he see modern psychological astrology as a step in the direction he was already heading (away from literal prediction and toward something more symbolic or harmonic)? And what about the scientific rejection — would he accept it given his own empirical bent, or would he argue that science is missing something he was trying to capture? I'm trying to get a sense of how his actual views, as expressed in his writings, would map onto the contemporary landscape.
