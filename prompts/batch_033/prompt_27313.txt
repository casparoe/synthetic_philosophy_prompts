I recently inherited a painting from my grandmother that I had grown up admiring in her sitting room. She always described it as an original by a minor but respected nineteenth-century landscape painter, and over the years I came to love it — not as a financial asset, but as an object dense with personal and aesthetic meaning. Last month I had it appraised for insurance and learned that it is in fact a forgery: a highly skilful one, executed in the same period, materially and visually indistinguishable from the artist's genuine work. The appraiser told me its market value is negligible. I still find the painting beautiful, and it still hung on my grandmother's wall for six decades. Yet I cannot shake the sense that the painting now means less to me, and I am troubled by this reaction — the physical object has not changed in any respect, so why should my attachment to it have changed? Is my response rationally defensible, or am I merely being snobbish about provenance? Please respond with exactly five points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
