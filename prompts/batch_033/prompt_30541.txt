I'm a philosophy professor, and I keep ending up in the same argument with a former student who now builds distributed systems for a living. He's smart and knows his field cold — nodes, message passing, shared state, consensus, leader election, gossip protocols, partial failure, latency, the whole toolkit — but he's never read the socialist calculation debate, and he's convinced its just a bunch of pre-computer people reinventing the wheel badly. I know enough distributed systems to follow a careful translation and to call you out if you're hand-waving, but not enough to do the translation myself.

I want you to act as a philosopher of economics who also understands distributed systems well enough to do a real translation rather than an analogy. Restate the debate between Mises and Hayek on one side and Lange (and Lerner if you want) on the other in his vocabulary. Treat prices as some kind of message or coordination signal, enterprises as nodes, the central planning board as a central coordinator or scheduler, the economy as the system, and so on. Do Mises and Hayek first — their argument that a planned economy can't solve the calculation problem — then Lange's reply that the planner can just set prices by trial and error and adjust them based on reported shortages and surpluses.

Then give me the residue report. What translated cleanly? What did you have to fudge to make it fit, and where did the fudging change the claim? What parts of the debate have no counterpart in distributed-systems vocabulary at all? I especialy want to know whether Hayek's point about dispersed, tacit knowledge survives the translation or turns into something weaker, like nodes having local state they can't fully report. And whether Lange's trial-and-error price setting is doing real work in the translation or just assumes the thing it's supposed to show.

Start by brainstorming the mapping on your own before you write the translation out, and think carefully about where you might be importing assumptions from distributed systems — truthful messages, nodes that know their own state well enough to report it, convergence guarantees — and calling it a faithful translation. If part of the debate doesn't survive, tell me whether that's because the vocabulary is missing something or because the original argument was less precise than it looked.
