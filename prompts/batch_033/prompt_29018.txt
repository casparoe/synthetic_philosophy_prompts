I'm teaching a grad seminar on rationality and epistemic game theory next term, and I want to spend one session on Kubrick's Dr. Strangelove — specifically the doomsday machine. People always trot it out as a cute illustration of commitment, but I want to use it more seriously than that. The machine is supposed to be the perfect deterrent because it takes the human decision out of retaliation: nobody has to choose to end the world, the world just ends. But the film also makes clear the thing was kept secret, so the Americans didn't know it existed and couldn't be deterred by it. I'd like a reading of the film through the lens of common knowledge and rationality: where it gets the difference between mutual knowledge and common knowledge right, where it runs together "automatic" with "credible" or "known," and whether the later scenes of everyone reasoning their way through the crisis rely on rationality assumptions that the epistemic game theory literature would reject. What does the film dramatize that the formal papers miss, and where does it get the game theory wrong? I'm going to teach it, so I'd rather you say that honestly than make the film fit the theory, wether it makes for a neat class or not. And at the end, tell me how confident you are in the reading and which point you're least sure of.
