I'm writing up my views for a seminar on idealization in ethics and I'd like a consistency check before I present. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) What I ought to do is fixed by what I would want after an idealization procedure that corrects factual ignorance and reasoning errors, not by my current, unreflective wants.
(2) The procedure is meant to be conservative: it cleans up my factual picture and my reasoning, but doesn't rewrite my values.
(3) Coherence constraints are part of the standard the procedure enforces — no cyclic preferences, no inconsistent beliefs — and where my values conflict with coherence, coherence wins.
(4) In at least some cases the procedure branches: there are two equally coherent, equally legitimate idealized descendants of me who want different things, and no fact of the matter which one is "really me."
(5) If my idealized counterpart would want X, then X is a reason for me now, even when X feels alien to my current self.
(6) Some value changes are not self-discovery: if the process replaces one final end with another, that's not my values becoming clearer, it's a change of subject.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and which single revision would clean up the most? Rank the tensions by severity and say, for each, what has to give. Keep it under 500 words.
