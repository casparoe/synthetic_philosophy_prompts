I've been chewing on the moral equality of combatants for a term paper and somehow talked myself into an argument that seems to prove no war can ever be unjust. That obviously can't be right, but I can't find where it derails. Here's the chain:

(1) If MEC is true, then a soldier fighting for an unjust cause acts permissibly when she kills an enemy combatant, so long as she follows jus in bello — distinction, proportionality, all that.

(2) If an act is permissible, the agent isn't blameworthy for it.

(3) So unjust combatants who respect jus in bello aren't blameworthy for killing just combatants.

(4) Just combatants aren't blameworthy for killing unjust combatants either — they're defending a just cause and following the same rules.

(5) So if everyone follows the rules, nobody on either side is blameworthy for any combatant-on-combatant killing.

(6) Now, a war just is the sum of acts that constitute it. If none of the individual killings are blameworthy, it's weird to say the war itself is blameworthy — collective blame can't just float free of individual blame.

(7) But if the war isn't blameworthy, then the label "unjust war" doesn't pick out any moral failing by those who fight it. And if the injustice doesn't cash out in anyone doing anything blameworthy, what's left of the distinction between just and unjust wars?

I think the weak spot is probably (1) — maybe MEC doesn't actually say the unjust combatant's killings are "permissible," maybe it says something narrower about symmetrical liability to attack without implying full permissibility. But then I'm not sure what MEC actually amounts to, or wether whatever's left is still interesting. I'd also wonder about (6): maybe unjust wars are blameworthy in a way that doesn't distribute to individual soldiers?

Walk me through which step you'd reject, what the fix costs, and most importantly: what argument or consideration would most likely change your mind about the diagnosis? If you're confident about your answer, tell me what would make you switch to a different one. Keep it under 350 words.
