I'm taking a course on democratic theory, and we recently spent a week on the justification of different selection mechanisms for public officials. A puzzle has been nagging at me that I hope you can help me think through.

In most common-law jurisdictions, criminal juries are selected by lot from the citizenry. The state compels people to serve, and the resulting body — a group of randomly chosen laypeople — is treated as a legitimate decision-maker with the power to deprive someone of liberty. Yet if someone proposed selecting members of Congress or Parliament by lot instead of by election, the proposal would be met with incredulity, even alarm. Both juries and legislatures make decisions that bind the rest of us; both exercise state power. So the practice of using sortition for one and elections for the other seems to presuppose that there is a principled difference between the kind of task a jury performs and the kind a legislature performs — a difference deep enough to make random selection legitimate in the first case but illegitimate in the second.

What I am trying to work out is: what exactly is that presupposed difference? Is it a claim about the nature of the two tasks (juries apply pre-existing law to particular facts, while legislatures create new law), or about the kind of representativeness each body needs (juries represent the community's moral judgment, while legislatures represent the people's political will), or about the epistemic demands of the roles (jurors need ordinary powers of judgment, while legislators need specialized expertise), or something else entirely? And is the practice internally consistent? After all, we do sometimes use sortition for bodies that blur the line between applying norms and creating them — citizens' assemblies charged with drafting constitutional proposals, for instance. If the asymmetry rests on a sharp distinction between mere application and genuine lawmaking, does the existence of such hybrid bodies undermine it? Finally, if we concluded that the presupposition behind the asymmetry was false — that whatever legitimates sortition for juries also applies, in principle, to legislatures — what would have to give way? Would we need to start selecting legislators by lot, or would we need to stop selecting jurors that way?
