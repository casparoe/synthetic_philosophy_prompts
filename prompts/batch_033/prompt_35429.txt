I've been reading about the history of PR and propaganda for a course, and I keep circling back to Edward Bernays's Torches of Freedom stunt — you know, Easter Sunday 1929, he had a group of women light up Lucky Strikes on Fifth Avenue during the parade, framed it as a feminist gesture, tipped off the press so they'd cover it as news, and the whole thing was bankrolled by the American Tobacco Company. The women weren't all in the dark, either — some of them knew it was a PR operation, though the public certianly didn't. I get why people call it manipulation, but every time I try to nail down exactly what makes it manipulation rather than just clever persuasion, the line gets fuzzier than it seems like it should.

I want to use this episode to stress-test the manipulation vs. legitimate persuasion distinction properly. Go through it systematically. First, map the episode: who's influencing whom, through what channels, with what kind of intent, and what did each party — the marchers, Bernays, the press, the public — actually know or believe? Then take the main accounts of the manipulation/persuasion boundary (deception-based views, autonomy-based views, views that turn on whether you're appealing to reasons or bypassing rational capacities, or whatever you think the real contenders are) and apply each one to the features of the stunt: the hidden sponsorship, the staged spontaneity, the co-opting of a genuine political symbol, the fact that the women who lit up weren't all dupes, and the downstream effects on the newspaper readers who saw the coverage. Where do the accounts agree on a verdict, and where do they come apart?

Then flip it around: what does this case do to the distinction? Does it sharpen it, expose a gap, or suggest that the distinction is more of a spectrum with no principled cutoff? I'm less interested in whether Bernays was a bad guy — he obviously was — and more interested in whether the episode reveals something about the manipulation concept that a cleaner hypothetical wouldn't. And be honest about where the theory just doesnt have anything to say, rather than making it pronounce on features it was never built to handle.
