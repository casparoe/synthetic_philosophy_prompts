I'm the chief of staff for a city councilmember in a mid-sized city, and I've been going back and forth on wether I did something wrong in a vote last month. We passed a deal where a developer funds a long-delayed flood wall for a low-lying neighborhood that floods every few years, and in exchange the council rezoned a parcel so the developer could put in a big box store. The rezoning meant the lease of a small family-owned hardware store on that parcel wasn't renewed, and the owner — a guy who'd run it for thirty years — had to shut down. I wrote the memo recommending the yes vote, and I knew the rezoning would kill his store. I still think the flood wall was worth it, and I'd vote the same way again.

But I can't shake the feeling that I wronged him, and it's not just that I feel bad about a bad outcome: it feels like I owe him something I can't repay, even though I don't know what I could have done differently. My colleagues tell me that's just politics and that if I'd do it again, feeling guilty is incoherent — either it was the right call or it wasn't. A friend from grad school says the guilt just means I'm not cut out for this work. I'm not looking for therapy; I want to know whether those two thoughts can both be true — that the vote was the right thing to do and that I wronged the owner — and, if they can, what I'm supposed to do with the guilt.
