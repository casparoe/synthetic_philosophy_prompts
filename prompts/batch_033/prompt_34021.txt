I'm a novelist, and I'm writing a book about a guy who starts out ordinary and slowly gets pulled into a world of suspicious patterns — the kind of person who either uncovers something real or ends up loosing his grip. The trouble is my own gut reactions to conspiracy claims are all over the map, and I'd like them to be consistent so my character's arc isn't just me flipping a coin from scene to scene.

Here's where I land, just going by gut:

- The moon landing being faked: not reasonable. Same shelf as flat earth.
- Flat earth: not reasonable, full stop.
- Tuskegee — the US government letting syphilis go untreated in Black men just to watch its course — is true, and I think someone in the 60s who heard credible rumors about it would have been reasonable to believe it before the story broke.
- The tobacco companies knowing smoking causes cancer and coordinating to bury that: reasonable to believe long before the documents leaked. The incentive structure alone made it close to obvious.
- MKUltra — the CIA dosing unwitting people with LSD — is true, and I think a person who believed it on scattered testimony in the 60s was right, not just lucky.
- Watergate: reasonable to believe on early reporting, before it was fully proved.
- 9/11 as an inside job: not reasonable, even though the same government has done genuinely monstrous things.
- The 2020 election being stolen: not reasonable. I don't think I'm just being partisan here; I'd want the principle to say why it's different from Tuskegee or tobacco.
- A drug company burying negative trial results: reasonable to suspect in general, and reasonable in particular cases before the evidence is public.

Then the one I keep going back and forth on for the book: the claim that the Covid lab-leak hypothesis was suppressed for political reasons — not just that the virus may have escaped from the Wuhan lab, but that there was a coordinated push to make the zoonotic story stick and to discredit the lab theory. Some days it feels like the tobacco case (institutions protecting themselves), other days it feels like 9/11 trutherism (too many people, too tidy a story).

So my question is: is there a single principle — or a small set of them — that sorts all of these the way I already sort them? State it precisely, and be honest about which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and which ones are load-bearing. Then tell me what the principle says about the lab-leak case, and about a case or two I haven't listed where it would push me somewhere I might not expect.
