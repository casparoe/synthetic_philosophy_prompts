I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on concepts and categories, about 18 students, one 80-minute session. Design a hands-on classroom activity that makes them feel the force of the problem at the heart of Hofstadter's Letter Spirit project: what makes every 'a' an 'a', given the enormous variety of shapes that qualify and the fact that no checklist of necessary-and-sufficient features seems to work. The activity should force participants to try to specify recognition rules, watch those rules fail against carefully chosen counterexample shapes, and discover for themselves that something more elusive — a spirit or style — appears to be doing the work. They should not hear the name Letter Spirit or Hofstadter until the debrief. Give me the rules, the materials I need to prepare (including a set of challenge shapes described in enough detail that I can draw or print them), a timeline for the 80 minutes, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the philosophical terrain: the limits of classical definitional theories of concepts, family resemblance, prototype and exemplar theories, and the specific challenge Letter Spirit poses that none of those fully address. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be candid about which aspect of the problem the activity cannot reproduce — I suspect the generative side, creating new 'a's in a given style, is one.
