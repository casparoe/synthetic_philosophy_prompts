I'm a game engine programmer. I've been reading about Berkeley's idealism — the view that physical objects are just collections of ideas in minds, that to be is to be perceived — and his "master argument," which says you can't conceive of something existing unconceived because the moment you try, you're conceiving it, so it's not unconceived after all.

This sounds to me exactly like how a game engine handles world state. Objects outside every player's view frustum don't get rendered; their visual properties aren't computed. If no one is looking at a tree, that tree has no rendered appearance. And if I try to imagine what an unrendered tree looks like, I'm necessarily rendering it in my imagination — I can't picture an unpictured tree. So Berkeley's argument seems to map perfectly onto frustum culling: you can't ask what's on screen when nothing is on screen, because the question puts something on screen.
