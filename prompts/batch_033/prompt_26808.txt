Write an essay on whether the Judy Benjamin case shows that standard probabilistic updating rules are incomplete and that a rule such as minimizing relative entropy is needed. Spell out the case: Judy Benjamin is dropped in a region divided equally into Red and Blue territories, and each territory is divided equally into Second Company and Headquarters Company areas, so she assigns probability 1/4 to each quadrant. She then receives a radio message whose only relevant content is that, if she is in Red territory, the odds are 3 to 1 that she is in Headquarters Company rather than Second Company area. Because no unconditional proposition is learned, ordinary Bayesian conditioning does not apply. Weigh the main responses: treating the message as conditioning on a material conditional, applying Jeffrey-style updating over some partition, and minimizing relative entropy subject to the new conditional-probability constraint. Pay particular attention to the widely discussed feature that the relative-entropy update also changes her probability of being in Red territory even though the message says nothing unconditional about Red, and to the charge that minimizing relative entropy implicitly imports a principle of insufficient reason. Consider whether the right conclusion is that conditioning is incomplete, that the proposed alternatives misrepresent what was learned, or that the message itself is ambiguous between different constraints. Reach a definite, justified verdict about which response is most defensible. Your answer should be exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion beyond the five points.
