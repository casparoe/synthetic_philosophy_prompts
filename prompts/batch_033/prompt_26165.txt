I've recently started working on a project about how ordinary people attribute intentionality to autonomous systems — think of a self-driving car that executes a maneuver knowing it will produce a harmful side effect — and I keep finding that the experimental philosophy literature on the Knobe effect is both directly relevant and frustratingly unsettled. For those unfamiliar: in the classic vignettes, a corporate chairman approves a profit-increasing program that also harms the environment in one condition or helps it in another; his stated attitude toward the side effect is identical in both cases, yet most subjects judge the harm as brought about intentionally and the help as not. The asymmetry has been replicated many times, but its interpretation remains disputed.

I'd like you to draft the joint statement that a defender of a normative account (roughly: moral or evaluative considerations partly constitute our concept of intentional action) and a defender of a non-normative account (e.g., the trade-off hypothesis, the counter-normality view, or a conversational-pragmatics explanation) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

The document should include: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can agree on — about the experimental findings themselves, about what has been ruled out, about the structure of the debate; (2) a table of disagreements, with each side's position stated in wording its own proponents would accept; (3) the cruxes — sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, the kind of evidence or argument both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Please separate what is genuinely settled from what remains contested among specialists, and do not present a contested interpretation as if it were the consensus.
