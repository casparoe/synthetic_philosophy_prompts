I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently taken up moral philosophy. There is a recurring puzzle in the consent literature that I have not seen given a satisfactory name or taxonomy, and I would like to know whether one exists.

The puzzle concerns the role of deception. It seems clear that some deceptions invalidate consent while others do not, and the standard move is to distinguish deceptions about the nature of the act from deceptions about collateral matters — its consequences, the identity or motives of the other party, and so on. But I find that this distinction collapses under pressure in ways that are rarely acknowledged.

Take three cases. (1) A surgeon tells a patient she will perform a biopsy but instead performs a sterilization. Consent is clearly invalid. (2) A surgeon accurately describes a procedure but falsely claims it is medically necessary to prevent cancer when it is in fact elective and performed for profit. Many would say consent is not vitiated, or at least not in the same way. (3) A person pretends to be a licensed physician, performs an examination identical to what a real physician would do, and the "patient" consents to being examined by someone they believe is a doctor. Is this closer to (1) or to (2)? One could say the patient consented to a medical exam and got one — the deception was about credentials, a collateral matter. Or one could say the patient consented to an exam by a doctor and got something else entirely — an exam by an impostor — so the deception went to the nature of the act itself.

What interests me is not the legal answer (I understand the law has categories like fraud in the factum and fraud in the inducement) but whether moral philosophy has developed a principled way of drawing this line, what it is called, and what criterion does the work. If the leading view is that there is no sharp line and that consent comes in degrees or is context-sensitive in a way that resists a single criterion, I would want to know that too, and how that view handles the impostor case.
