My line is that the argument is stronger and more interesting: it follows from his view that moral judgment is grounded in sentiment, and since there's no stable sentiment backing a supposed duty to oneself to keep living at all costs, the very idea of such a duty dissolves on Humean grounds. Suicide isn't just permissible on balance — the category of "duty to oneself not to commit suicide" is incoherent once you accept Hume's picture of where moral judgments come from, in a way that mirrors his broader skepticism about duties to oneself across the board.
