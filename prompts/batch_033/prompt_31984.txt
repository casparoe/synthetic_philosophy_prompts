I'm teaching an upper-level undergraduate seminar on interpretation and the philosophy of literature. I want to give my students a decision tree that takes them from a few basic commitments about how meaning works to a named position in the debate over authorial intention. The tree should be built from the logical space of possible answers rather than from a list of names: first the diagnostic questions, then the positions located in that space, with unoccupied combinations noted and briefly diagnosed.

Each branch node should be a question a student could answer without already knowing the labels — phrase them in terms of concrete cases or claims. A question like "Can a text mean something its author did not intend it to mean?" is fine; a question like "Are you an actual or hypothetical intentionalist?" is not.

The tree must include the following positions as leaves: actual intentionalism, hypothetical intentionalism, conventionalism (or textualism, the view associated with the intentional fallacy), value-maximizing theory, and at least one version of reader-response theory. If there are other positions you think belong in the tree, include them and explain why.

Two additional requirements:

First, the tree should address the distinction between meaning and significance (associated with E.D. Hirsch). One way to handle this is to ask whether the tree is tracking meaning or significance, or both. I want you to decide how best to incorporate this distinction into the tree's branching structure — for instance, whether it functions as a meta-question that reframes earlier answers, or as a branch of its own.

Second, please flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate (e.g., because they differ on a matter of degree rather than kind, or because the diagnostic question that would split them would be too theory-laden). Also flag any logically possible combination of answers that no one defends, and say briefly whether that position is unoccupied because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
