my jurisprudence professor has spent the whole term on Hart and Devlin, and i honestly can't tell which side she's on. (quick version: Hart thinks the criminal law should only stop people harming each other; Devlin thinks it can also enforce a society's shared morality, even when nobody's harmed.) here's her track record on the cases she uses:

- laws against private, consensual adult same-sex conduct: she'd repeal them. no one's harmed, and she said the law shouldn't be in the business of enforcing a sexual morality.
- laws against possessing cannabis for personal use: she'd repeal those too. what you put in your own body is your own affair.
- a ban on selling your kidney: she'd uphold it, but her reason was that desperate people would get exploited, not that the body is sacred or anything.
- a ban on consensual dueling: she'd uphold that too, and here she sounded much more Devlinian — she kept saying a society can't let people agree to kill each other, because of what that does to the value the law is supposed to protect.
- a ban on consensual incest between adult siblings: she'd uphold it, but only for the risk to possible children and family roles; she said the disgust isn't the reason.
