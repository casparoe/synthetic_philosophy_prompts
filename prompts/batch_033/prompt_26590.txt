I've been following the debate between virtue ethicists and situationist critics since graduate school, and by my lights it has barely moved in twenty years. The situationist side, drawing on the social psychology of the 1970s and after — Milgram on obedience, Darley and Batson on helping behavior, Isen and Levin on mood effects — argues that putatively robust character traits are routinely swamped by minor situational features, and that virtue ethics therefore rests on an empirically false picture of moral psychology. The virtue-ethical side replies that the experiments target a caricature of virtue (global traits that issue in behavior regardless of circumstance), that cross-situational inconsistency does not entail the absence of stable dispositions any more than it does for non-moral traits like irritability or generosity, and that virtue ethics was never in the business of making the kinds of empirical predictions the situationists attribute to it. Both sides have heard these replies. Neither side has conceded. The same arguments reappear in the journals with slightly different framing, and the needle does not move.

I want a diagnosis of the impasse. Specifically: is this a substantive empirical disagreement about what the experiments actually show, a verbal disagreement about what counts as a character trait, a disagreement about what standards a theory of moral psychology must meet, or something else — for instance, two disciplines talking past each other about different questions under a single heading? Or is it a clash of bedrock methodological commitments that neither side can argue for without begging the question? Give me a committed diagnosis, not a survey of the positions, and support it by saying what each side would have to be right about for the other to be wrong. Then tell me what, given your diagnosis, would actually count as progress — a new kind of experiment, a conceptual distinction, a formal model of the situationist inference, a change of the question, or something else — or, if you think no progress should be expected, explain why the impasse is structural and what that implies about the terms in which the debate is conducted.
