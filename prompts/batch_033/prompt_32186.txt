Here is an argument I built for a seminar on moral worth, and I cannot find the exit. I will lay it out in numbered steps.

(1) An action has moral worth only if it is done from duty: the agent must perform it because they take the action to be morally required, and not merely in accordance with duty. This is the familiar contrast with the honest shopkeeper, who gives correct change because doing so is good for business; his action conforms to duty, but it has no moral worth.

(2) When someone helps a stranger out of spontaneous sympathy, they act because of the stranger's distress and their own concern, not because they take the action to be morally required.

(3) So, by (1) and (2), the sympathetic helper's action — however right, and whatever other merits it has — lacks moral worth.

(4) Merely adding a dormant recognition of duty changes nothing about what the action is done from: if the agent is in fact moved by sympathy, and the thought that the action is required is present but does no motivational work, then the action is still not done from duty.

(5) So a person who both feels sympathy and recognizes the duty to help, but whose sympathy is what actually moves them, also acts without moral worth.

(6) But that seems to strip moral worth from exactly the actions we most want to call morally worthy — the parent who acts from love, the bystander moved by compassion — and it does so merely because the agent's concern was not mediated by the thought of duty.

(7) Therefore (1) is false: moral worth cannot require being moved by the thought of duty in this way.

I would much rather keep (1) than accept (7), because giving up (1) seems to make moral worth depend on the quality of the agent's feelings, and I do not see how to do that without collapsing the distinction between the shopkeeper and the sympathetic helper. But I also refuse to say that the loving parent's care is morally worthless. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If I keep (1), what exactly must I say about the overdetermined case in (4) and (5)?
