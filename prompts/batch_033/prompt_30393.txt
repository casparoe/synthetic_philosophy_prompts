I'm teaching an upper-level epistemology / phil mind seminar this semester and I'm stuck on the take-home final. We spent like three weeks on the knowing-how vs. knowing-that debate — started with Ryle, then the Stanley & Williamson intellectualist comeback, then some of the back-and-forth (Noë, Fridland, etc.). The problem I'm having is that every year students write perfectly adequate summaries of both positions — they can rehearse Ryle's regress argument, they can state that Stanley thinks knowing-how is just knowing-that under a practical mode of presentation — but they never actually evaluate whether the intellectualist genuinely escapes the regress or whether the regress just pops up again inside the "practical mode of presentation" itself.

So I want one question that forces them to do that evaluation. Specifically, I want them to reconstruct the regress argument as an argument *against intellectualism* (not just as a Rylean position statement), then assess whether the practical-mode-of-presentation move answers it, and if it doesn't, say what the intellectualist's remaining options are. The question should be answerable in 90 minutes by someone who's done the reading and thought about it, but it shouldn't be answerable by someone who just memorized the glossary entries.

Please write the question, a model answer of around 800 words, and a marking scheme out of 25 that separates A-range (20–25), B-range (15–19), and C-range (10–14) answers by the specific analytic moves each band must include. Also flag the single most common mistake with a one-sentence note — in my experience it's students saying "practical modes of presentation avoid the regress because they're not propositions" without explaining what a practical mode of presentation actually is or why it wouldn't itself require know-how to grasp.
