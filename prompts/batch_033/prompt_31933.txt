I'm doing a podcast episode on Nozick's tracking theory of knowledge and I've got a guest coming on who's one of the few people still willing to defend it in print. The audience is mostly rationalist-adjacent — they've read some epistemology but aren't specialists. I want to skip the "what is the tracking theory" intro and go straight to the hard stuff.

Can you draft me 8–10 questions that push on the view's actual weak points? Specifically:

- The closure denial. This is the thing everyone bounces off of. I want at least two questions here that don't let him just say "well, closure is intuitive but false." Push on whether there's any weaker closure-like principle the tracking theory can salvage, and on what it means for ordinary reasoning if we literally can't chain deductions.
- The lottery case. If I believe my ticket will lose, the tracking conditions are satisfied — if it were a winner, I wouldn't believe it would lose — so the theory says I know it'll lose. But that seems crazy. I want him to steelman the objection and then respond.
- Method individuation. How do you carve up "methods" finely enough to avoid counterexamples without making the whole thing ad hoc? Doesnt this just inherit the generality problem from reliabilism?
- The brain-in-a-vat. The theory says we don't know we're not BIVs, which is supposed to be a feature not a bug. But doesn't this mean we also fail to know a huge number of ordinary propositions that entail not being envatted? How does he actually live with that?

For each question, include a follow-up that anticipates his most likely reply — I want to push past the standard moves. And no "what drew you to epistemology" type questions, please.
