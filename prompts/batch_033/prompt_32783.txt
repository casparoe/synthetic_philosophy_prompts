I'm a journalist working on a long-form piece about the problem of divine hiddenness — the argument, associated most closely with J.L. Schellenberg, that if a perfectly loving God existed, God would ensure that every person capable of a relationship with God was in a position to enter into one, and since many people sincerely seek God and find nothing, such a God does not exist. My piece aims to go beyond the logical back-and-forth and assess how the major philosophical responses to this problem fare when measured against the lived experience of divine silence — the felt sense of abandonment, the erosion of trust, the sheer existential weight of reaching out and encountering nothing.

I would like you to sort the following families of response into tiers based on how existentially adequate they are. By "existentially adequate" I mean: if someone in the grip of divine silence were to take this response seriously, could it genuinely address their distress, or does it merely solve a logical puzzle while leaving the experience itself untouched? Use at most four tiers, and for each response give a sentence or two explaining why it lands where it does.

Here are the responses:

1. The cognitive-limitations response (skeptical theism): we are in no position to judge whether God has good reasons for remaining hidden; our epistemic situation is too constrained to draw conclusions from the fact that we cannot discern those reasons.

2. The free-will response: God remains hidden to preserve our ability to choose or reject a relationship freely, without the coercive pressure that unambiguous divine presence would exert.

3. The soul-making response: divine hiddenness is a necessary condition for developing virtues such as trust, perseverance, and spiritual maturity that could not develop in the same way if God's presence were manifest.

4. The post-mortem response: whatever relationship with God is missing now will be made available after death, so no one is ultimately denied the opportunity for a relationship with God.

5. The resistance response: God is not hidden; the problem lies in human resistance, self-deception, or sin, which prevents us from recognizing a divine presence that is in fact available.

6. The atheist response: the experience of divine silence is not a problem to be solved but evidence to be accepted — it is precisely what we should expect if there is no personal God, and the honest response to it is to conclude that theism is false.

Please rank these into tiers, not a strict linear order — ties are fine — and give your reasoning for each placement.
