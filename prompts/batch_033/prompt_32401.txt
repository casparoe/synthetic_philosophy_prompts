I've got this friend Maya who is weirdly good at predicting, in those nature-documentary thought experiments, whether a cooperative behavior will catch on or get swamped by cheaters. She's not a biologist or anything, she just reads alot of pop science and argues with people online. I started writing down her calls to see if there's one rule behind them. So far: she says that in a well-stirred flask where some bacteria spend energy making an enzyme that helps everyone, one round only, nobody meets twice, the freeloaders win. She says vampire bats that roost together for years and share blood, where a bat who got helped is more likely to help back later, the sharing sticks around. She says ground squirrels that screech when a hawk shows up, warning their nearby sisters and cousins even though it makes the screecher more likely to get eaten, and it's not a tit-for-tat thing, the alarm call evolves. She says in a big anonymous city where people play a one-shot prisoner's dilemma with a stranger they'll never see again and there's no reputation or gossip, everyone defects. And she says if you play many rounds but the computer shuffles the partners randomly every round and wipes everyone's memory so you can never recognize or remember who did what to you, cooperation never gets going.
