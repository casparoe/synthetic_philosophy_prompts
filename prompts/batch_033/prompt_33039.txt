A friend and I have been stuck on this for way too long. She's a continuity theorist about personal identity: if you replace my neurons one at a time with functionally identical artificial ones, I survive the whole way through, because at each step there's enough psychological connectedness with the previous step. I'm a substrate theorist: at some point the original me is gone, replaced by a copy, and the gradualness is just a fancy way of making it feel less like dying.

We want to write an adversarial-collaboration document. Draft it for us. It should have:

- A numbered list of claims we can both sign. These should cover the metaphysics (what personal identity is or isn't), the facts of the procedure (what happens to the brain, the phenomenology, the behavioral output), and the prudential question (what you should fear or anticipate).
- A disagreements table. Each row should state exactly where we differ, in wording each of us would accept for our own side. No straw men.
- The cruxes: the sub-questions where, if we could settle them, at least one of us would change our mind. These should be concrete enough that we could imagine evidence or arguments bearing on them.
- For each crux, a thought experiment or case we both agree is relevant, plus what each of us predicts about it.

If you think some of our disagreement is verbal — we're using "survive" or "same person" in different ways and talking past each other — say which part and why. And if you think the whole setup is confused in a way that makes this exercise pointless, tell me that flat out instead of pretending it works. I'd rather hear that you're uncertain about parts of this than get a confident-sounding answer that papers over the cracks.
