ok so I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but I've been asked to give a talk at a writing conference about the ethics of persuasion in fiction, and I've been trying to get my views straight. I've ended up with a set of commitments that I'm not sure all fit together, and I'd like a consistency check before I stand up in front of a room full of people.

Here's where I've landed:

(a) Manipulation is wrong because it subverts the target's autonomy — specifically, it gets someone to act (or believe, or feel) on the basis of motivations they wouldn't endorse if they were reflectively aware of how those motivations were produced.

(b) Legitimate persuasion, by contrast, works by offering the target reasons they can assess and either accept or reject on their own terms.

(c) As a novelist, I regularly use pacing, free indirect discourse, and other narrative techniques to make readers feel things — grief, sympathy for a character they'd normally despise, unease — that they didn't choose to feel and that bypass their deliberate scrutiny while they're reading. I think this is not only legitimate, it's basically what fiction is for.

(d) The difference between manipulation and persuasion can't be read off the mechanism alone; intent matters. If a doctor lies to a patient to get them to take a life-saving medication they'd otherwise refuse out of confusion, that's not manipulation in the morally loaded sense, even though it bypasses the patient's rational capacities.

(e) I think most advertising that deliberately exploits cognitive biases — anchoring, framing effects, decoy options — is manipulative, even when the advertiser is just trying to sell toothpaste and bears no ill will.

So my question is: does this hang together? I can feel some tensions but I'm not sure which ones are real and which are just me being sloppy with terms. I'd like you to act as an expert on moral philosophy and the ethics of influence — think through each pair carefully before answering, maybe start by brainstorming all the possible conflicts and then narrow down. Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, and which ones only look like they are. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. And for each genuine tension, tell me what the cheapest revision would be and what it cost.
