I am a novelist writing a book whose central character is a demographer who has become convinced by the doomsday argument and has quietly reorganized her life around the expectation that humanity is much nearer its end than ordinary planning assumes. She is not a crank; I want her to hold the strongest, most careful version of the position, and I want the novel to put real pressure on it rather than use it as set dressing. The version she holds is roughly this: conditional on treating her own birth rank as a random draw from the sequence of all humans who will ever live, and on an indifference principle that makes her place in that sequence unremarkable, she judges it improbable that the total number of humans will be many orders of magnitude larger than the number who have already lived, and she therefore expects a far shorter future than common sense does. She draws practical conclusions from this about what is worth doing now, and about how much weight to give to the distant future.

What I would like is a map of what would count against this position, written the way a novelist rather than a debater needs it: the objections, possible observations, and formal considerations that should move her, sorted from fatal to merely awkward; which of these a committed defender of the argument would herself concede as counting against it and which she would reasonably dismiss; what, if anything, the position predicts that a rival reading of the same evidence would not; and whether the most defensible form of the argument has been qualified, in its choice of reference class, in the prior it assumes, or in how the indifference principle is applied, in ways that insulate it from any possible evidence, and whether that insulation should be treated as a defect. Where an objection can be dramatized, say what the scene would look like: what someone would have to show her, concretely, to make her change her mind. I am not looking for citations or a reading list. Please say how confident you are in the overall map and single out the one point you are least sure of. Keep the response under 1,100 words.
