I manage a research programme on scalable oversight, and I need to get clear on what the obfuscated-arguments problem actually implies for the debate approach to AI safety. The debate proposal, roughly, is that two AI systems argue opposing sides of a question before a human judge, and the adversarial structure is supposed to surface the truth even when the judge cannot independently evaluate the arguments. The obfuscated-arguments worry is that a dishonest debater might construct arguments that are false but that a human judge finds persuasive — not because the arguments are good, but because the human cannot detect the flaws hidden in their complexity. Please describe the most important implications of this problem. Organise the implications into three categories: those that are fatal to the debate approach altogether, those that merely restrict the conditions under which debate can work, and those that motivate concrete fixes within the debate framework rather than abandoning it. For each implication, say whether it is settled or still contested, and if contested, what the disagreement turns on.
