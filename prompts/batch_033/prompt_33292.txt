I'm putting together a paper on the epistemology of thought experiments, and I've been trying to nail down where I actually stand before I start writing. I'd like an audit of whether my commitments form a coherent package, because I suspect I'm papering over a tension or two without realising it.

Here's where I've landed:

(1) Thought experiments are a legitimate and indispensable method in philosophy; eliminativism about them is a non-starter.

(2) The difference between a legitimate thought experiment and a misleading intuition pump is that the former isolates a genuine conceptual or modal distinction, while the latter exploits a cognitive bias or an imaginative limitation of a kind that has no probative value.

(3) Thomson's violinist is a legitimate thought experiment. It isolates the distinction between having a right to life and having a right to the use of another person's body, and the vividness of the scenario is incidental to the conceptual work being done.

(4) The footbridge version of the trolley problem is a misleading intuition pump. It generates a different verdict from the switch case not by isolating a morally relevant factor but by triggering an aversion to physical contact — a cognitive bias dressed up as moral insight.

(5) Searle's Chinese room is a misleading intuition pump. It persuades by encouraging the reader to imagine a system too small to do the work and then to generalise from that impoverished image.

(6) The philosophical zombie argument is a misleading intuition pump. It exploits the explanatory gap — our inability to see a priori how physical facts could necessitate phenomenal facts — and treats that imaginative limitation as a guide to metaphysical possibility.

(7) Nozick's experience machine is a legitimate thought experiment. It isolates something real about the value we place on authentic contact with reality, and the scenario does not rely on any particular cognitive bias to do its work.

(8) Dennett's own intuition pumps — the vim, the Multiple Drafts model, the Cartesian theatre critique — are epistemically virtuous. They are self-aware about their status as intuition pumps; they function by exposing the cognitive machinery that makes rival pumps persuasive, rather than by creating new unexamined imagery.

I want you to go through these and identify which pairs (or larger subsets) are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each tension is. For each genuine tension, tell me what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me philosophically. Please rank the tensions by severity and be specific about which commitment forces which other commitment to give way. I'm particularly interested in whether (2) can do the work I'm asking it to do — that is, whether it actually delivers the verdicts in (3) through (7) without begging the question or being applied ad hoc. The answer should be in British English.
