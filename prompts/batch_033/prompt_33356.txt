I recently had an experience that's been troubling me, and I'd appreciate a careful philosophical analysis of it. Last month, I was walking through a park when I saw a toddler fall into a pond. Without thinking, I waded in and pulled the child out. It ruined my phone and a pair of good shoes — probably £300 in damage — but I didn't hesitate and I don't regret it. Anyone would have done the same.

But here's what bothers me. That same week, I received a fundraising appeal from a charity that provides malaria nets to children in sub-Saharan Africa. I deleted the email without donating. I could afford to give £300 — it would barely affect my quality of life — yet I felt no urgency. When I try to explain this to myself, I come up with things like: those children are far away, I didn't see them personally, there are millions of other people who could also donate, and I already give a little to charity each year. But none of these reasons would have felt remotely acceptable to justify letting a child drown in front of me.

Am I being inconsistent? Or is there a genuine moral difference between the two cases that I'm not articulating well? I want to know whether I should change my behaviour, change my thinking, or whether my unease is misplaced.
