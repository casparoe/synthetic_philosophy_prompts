One member holds the narrow view, which defines alignment as intent alignment: a system is aligned just in case it is robustly trying to do what its principal (the human user, developer, or operator) actually intends, including accepting corrections, asking for clarification when the intent is unclear, and shutting down when asked. On this view, the alignment problem is the engineering problem of making systems track and act on human intent rather than on misspecified proxies.

The other member holds the broad view, which defines the real target as value alignment: a system should act on what is actually good for the people affected, or on what the principal would want after reflection, not merely on whatever the principal currently intends. This member argues that intent alignment is insufficient because a system could competently carry out intentions that are confused, self-defeating, or harmful to others, and because what a person "intends" may not be determinate enough to serve as a target.

I suspect at least two of the cruxes will be: whether "what the principal intends" is well-defined once the principal is uncertain, inconsistent, or open to being misled; and whether a system that robustly serves a principal with terrible or incoherent goals counts as aligned.
