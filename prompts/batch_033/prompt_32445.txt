I keep returning to a disagreement with a colleague about whether a life can be bad for the person who lives it, and our argument always stalls on the same point: the procreation asymmetry. As I understand it, the asymmetry comprises four claims. First, the presence of pain in a life is bad. Second, the presence of pleasure in a life is good. Third, the absence of pain in a possible life that never comes into existence is good, even though there is no one who enjoys that good. Fourth, the absence of pleasure in such a possible life is not bad, because there is no one who is thereby deprived. On this picture, when we compare existence with nonexistence for a possible person, the two sides of the ledger are not symmetric: nonexistence receives credit for the pain avoided but incurs no debit for the pleasure forgone. I find this asymmetry intuitively compelling, and it seems to me to do much of the work in arguments that coming into existence is a net harm. My colleague regards it as a framing artifact: he suspects that I am applying an impersonal standard to pain while switching to a person-affecting standard for pleasure, and that once a single standard is applied the asymmetry vanishes.
