I want to stop adjudicating the debate over whether large language models understand language case by case, and instead fix the standard against which any account in that debate should be judged. Write the desiderata: what an adequate account of LLM understanding has to deliver, whether it ultimately lands on the side of the stochastic-parrot charge or on the side of genuine comprehension. I want the criteria themselves, not a brief for either camp.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and say what it rules in and rules out. The list must address, at minimum: the difference between linguistic competence — producing well-formed, contextually appropriate text — and understanding, if you take that distinction to be real; the behavioral evidence now routinely cited, including compositional generalization, in-context reasoning, following novel instructions, and apparent theory-of-mind performance, and what, if anything, such evidence establishes on its own; the octopus-style eavesdropper scenario and what it is supposed to show about a system trained only on the form of language; the fact that pretraining is supervised by next-token prediction over text, and whether that fact constrains what can be learned in principle; the grounding problem and whether an account is permitted to require it; the gap between a model's capacities and a human interpreter's tendency to attribute understanding; and the role of scale, given that qualitatively new behaviors appear as models grow.

Flag which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a particular camp, and mark any pair or subset you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — for example, a requirement that the bar be set by ordinary human conversational criteria together with a requirement that the account not be substrate-chauvinist. End by saying which single desideratum does the most to separate the positions, and whether the list as a whole is neutral or already tilts the debate.
