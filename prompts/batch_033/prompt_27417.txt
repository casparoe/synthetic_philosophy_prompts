So my friend has been on this Nietzsche kick for like two years now — she's read the Genealogy like four times and has gotten really into classifying moral outlooks as either master morality or slave morality. I've been keeping a running list of her verdicts, partly because I find it interesting and partly because I want to figure out what rule she's actually using. Here's what I've got:

She says the Homeric warrior ethic (glory, prowess, pride in one's strength) is obviously master morality. Christian charity and turning the other cheek: slave morality, no question. She says modern humanitarianism — caring about the suffering of people you'll never meet — is slave morality too, which suprised me. The self-made entrepreneur who takes pride in building something and doesn't apologize for winning: master morality. Guilt as a foundational moral emotion: slave morality. The artist who's single-mindedly devoted to their work and doesn't care if it's socially useful: master morality. Ascetic self-denial, like monasticism: slave morality. And here's one that threw me — she says the ancient Greek value of hospitality, xenia, is master morality, because it's about the powerful displaying their magnificence through generosity, not about pity for the vulnerable.

Now I've got three cases I want to pin her down on, and I'm trying to figure out what she'll say before I ask her. First: a political revolutionary who fights for the oppressed out of righteous anger and a fierce desire for justice — is that the ressentiment of slave morality or the active, value-creating force of master morality? Second: the Stoic ideal of rational self-mastery and indifference to external fortune — it's about strength and self-discipline but also about suppressing the passions, so where does it land? Third: the modern effective altruist who maximizes charitable impact through cold cost-benefit calculation — they're helping the weak but they're doing it in this detached, almost aristocratic way that doesn't seem to come from pity.

I want a committed answer: don't just lay out the possibilities, tell me what you think she'll actually say on each one and why. More importantly, tell me what rule or rules best fit her track record so far, which of these new cases would most cleanly distinguish between the candidate rules, and whether you think her own framework forces her into a position she might not be comfortable with on any of them. Please write in American English.
