For a bioethics seminar handout, I'd like a decision tree that takes a student from reflecting on a single case to a named philosophical position on the authority of advance directives. The case: a competent adult, A, signs a clear advance directive refusing life-sustaining treatment if they ever develop advanced dementia. Years later, A has advanced dementia, seems content moment-to-moment, enjoys simple activities, and accepts food. A develops pneumonia that will be fatal without antibiotics. A's current behavior does not indicate distress, but A cannot express a considered preference about treatment. Should A's advance directive be followed?

Each branch node should be a yes/no question that genuinely separates positions on the case, phrased so that a student can answer it without already knowing the labels. Leaves should include at least: the precedent-autonomy view, the current-interests view, the Ulysses-contract view, a best-interests view, and a sliding-scale or proportionality view. If the tree has logical space for positions that nobody actually defends, flag those unoccupied leaves and briefly say why they are empty (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored). Also flag any pair of positions your tree cannot separate — do not paper over that they converge on the case.

Keep the entire decision tree and accompanying notes to 500 words maximum. Use American English.
