I've been going back and forth with a friend who's a hedonist — like, a serious one, she thinks pleasure is literally the only thing that's intrinsically valuable — but she's got this wierdly specific way of deciding what counts as a pleasure and what doesn't. I started writing down her verdicts because they kept surprising me. Here's what I've got so far:

Tastes and sensations: chocolate, coffee, a hot shower, the feeling of sun on your skin — all pleasures. The burn of a good workout while you're doing it — pleasure. The muscle soreness the next day — also pleasure, she says she enjoys having it. The feeling of being slightly drunk — pleasure.

Cognitive stuff: the feeling of finally getting a proof, winning an argument, remembering a funny joke — pleasures. The satisfaction of finishing a tedious chore — she says no, that's just relief that it's over. The moment when a confusing paragraph suddenly clicks — pleasure.

Social and moral: schadenfreude (she's embarassed about it), pride in her kid, the glow of doing someone a favor — all pleasures. Gratitude — she's unsure but leans no, says it feels more like a recognition than a pleasure. Righteous indignation — she says yes, definitely pleasure, which I found darkly funny.

Edge cases: ASMR tingles — pleasure. Runner's high — pleasure. The calm after meditating — pleasure. Scratching a mosquito bite — NOT a pleasure, she says that's just the cessation of an itch, and the itch itself is unpleasant. The buzz from a cigarette when you're a smoker — NOT a pleasure, she says it's just the removal of withdrawal pangs, so it's relief. The feeling of stretching after sitting too long — pleasure, even though I pointed out it seems a lot like the scratching case (removing stiffness). She got a little defensive and said it's different but couldn't articulate why.

The one that really got me thinking: I asked about the feeling of finally peeing after you've been holding it for way too long. She got annoyed and said she'd have to think about it. But I want to get my analysis straight before she comes back with an answer.

So here's what I'm trying to figure out: What are the candidate rules she might be running that fit her verdicts so far? (I can think of at least two or three.) What would each rule say about the pee case? And if I wanted to settle which rule she's really using — like, a single additional case I could ask her about that would decisively split them — what should that case be? I'm less interested in whether she says yes or no to peeing than in what the pattern reveals about what she actually means by "pleasure."
