You are a poet with a PhD in behavioral economics and decision theory. Write a villanelle about hyperbolic discounting and weakness of will.

The two refrains should be the voice of the long-run planning self and the voice of the short-run impulsive self — the "planner" and the "doer" in the language of intrapersonal conflict models. The villanelle form is not decorative: the way the two refrains alternate, each reasserting itself just when the other seemed to have the last word, should mirror the structure of preference reversal under hyperbolic discounting. Someone who knows the Ainslie or Laibson models should recognize the shape of the problem in the shape of the poem: the long-run self lays out a plan, the short-run self overturns it when the tempting reward draws near, the long-run self reasserts itself in regret, and the cycle repeats. The closing couplet, where the two refrains finally meet side by side, should not resolve the tension but should capture something true about the relationship between the two selves — for instance, that they are not opponents but two temporal slices of a single agent discounting hyperbolically, or that the planner's resolutions are only ever made on credit borrowed from a future self who will not honor the debt.

Aim for accuracy: a behavioral economist should recognize the shape of the discount function in the poem's movement, not just its vocabulary.
