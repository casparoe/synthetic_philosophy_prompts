I've been chewing on something for a while and I wonder if you can help me pin it down. I keep noticing a particular kind of structure across very different domains — law, metaphysics, language, even art — and I don't know whether philosophy has a single name for it, or whether the different instances are really the same thing at all.

Here's what I mean. Take a legal system: the constitution defines what counts as a valid court, but the courts are the ones who get to say what the constitution means. So the constitution depends on the courts and the courts depend on the constitution, and if you try to trace which one is more fundamental you just go in a circle. Or take the liar paradox: a sentence that says of itself that it's false. Or Escher's lithograph of two hands, each drawing the other into existence. In each case you have levels that are supposed to be ordered — the constitution is supposed to be prior to the courts, the object language is supposed to be prior to the metalanguage, the drawn hand is supposed to be prior to the drawing hand — but the levels loop back on themselves. The hierarchy gets tangled.

Now here's the part I'm really after. A lot of people, when they hit one of these loops, insist that there must be something outside the loop that anchors it. A basic norm that isn't itself legally constituted. A language level that doesn't talk about itself. A hand that isn't drawn by anything else. They want an inviolate level, somewhere the buck stops. And yet in some of these cases — Escher's being the obvious one — the whole point seems to be that there is no such level, and that's not a bug, it's the thing that makes the system interesting. So my question is: is there an established term for this whole family of self-referential loops, and more specifically, is there a name for the claim that such loops always have to bottom out somewhere — that there must be a level that isn't itself tangled? And if the phenomenon and the anti-loop claim don't have standard names, how would you carve the territory? I'm not just looking for a label; I want to know whether the legal case and the logical case and the artistic case are genuinely instances of the same structure, or whether I'm lumping together things that work differently. And if someone says "no, the loop can be fully closed, there's no inviolate level needed," what's the strongest version of that argument?
