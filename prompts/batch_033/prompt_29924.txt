You are a historian of economic thought with a specialty in the socialist calculation debate. I am preparing a unit for an upper-level seminar on the history of economic planning, and I want to give my students a sharply argued position paper that treats the debate as two distinct exchanges with different targets rather than a single dispute. Please write a careful, tightly reasoned essay defending the following thesis: Oskar Lange's trial-and-error market socialism is best understood as a response to Ludwig von Mises's original claim that rational economic calculation is impossible in the absence of genuine market prices for capital goods; yet even if Lange's model succeeds on its own terms against that challenge, it does not answer F. A. Hayek's later objection. Hayek's worry is not chiefly whether a central planning board can solve the allocation equations once it has the relevant data, but whether the relevant data—dispersed, local, often tacit knowledge of particular circumstances—could ever become available to planners in the first place.

In developing the argument, distinguish explicitly between the computational or equilibrium problem that Lange addresses and the epistemic or discovery problem that Hayek raises, and explain why a planning board that adjusts parametric prices in response to shortages and surpluses might be responsive to the first while leaving the second untouched. Consider at least one strong Langean reply—for example, that Hayek's criticism proves too much because it would undermine any general-equilibrium model, or that trial-and-error pricing itself creates incentives to reveal the needed information—and explain why you think the reply does not ultimately rescue the position. Before you begin the essay proper, brainstorm briefly: list the core commitments of Mises's calculation argument, the assumptions Lange's model makes about what the planning board knows, and the claims about the nature of knowledge that Hayek needs for his objection to go through. Then write the argument itself. Please do not include citations or a bibliography; the paper should stand on its own as an exercise in philosophical reconstruction and argument.
