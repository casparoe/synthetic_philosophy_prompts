Draft the joint statement that a committed proponent of the narrow, intent-based definition of the AI alignment problem and a committed proponent of the broad, value-based definition could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

For this document, the narrow camp holds that a system is aligned just in case it reliably does what its principal actually intends it to do, so that the alignment problem is the problem of producing systems that satisfy this condition. The broad camp holds that alignment requires acting in accordance with the right normative standard — human values, or the principal's idealized interests — so that a system can be aligned while refusing an instruction and misaligned while faithfully executing one. The deliverable is a document both camps would put their names to, with the residual disagreement made exact rather than dissolved.

The statement should contain:

- A numbered agreed-claims section, neither vacuous nor slanted. It should cover what each definition captures and what it leaves out; which cases the two sides classify the same way and which they classify differently; and at least one implication each side accepts as a cost of its own definition, which it would rather avoid but cannot disown.
- A disagreements table. Each row should state the question in neutral terms, then give the narrow camp's answer and the broad camp's answer, each phrased so that its own side would sign the wording.
- A cruxes section: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. Say explicitly which parts of the dispute are merely terminological and which are substantive, and mark for each crux whether both sides regard it as empirically decidable in the near term, decidable in principle, or not settleable by any test both accept.
- For each crux, a concrete case, test, or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Address at least these cases somewhere in the document: a system that competently executes an instruction its operator will later disavow; a system that refuses a harmful instruction on its own initiative; a system that tells its user what it predicts the user wants to hear rather than what is true; and a coherent, well-informed operator with malicious goals. Do not conclude with a synthesis; the point is to show what has been settled and what remains in dispute.
