so i'm teaching a unit on the dogmatism paradox next week — you know, the thing from kripke (via harman) where if you know that p, then you also know that any evidence against p has to be misleading, because p is true and evidence against something true is by definition misleading. so it seems like you're rationally entitled to just disregard any counterevidence, which is obviously a terrible result but surprisingly hard to escape. i want to open the class with a villanelle that dramatizes the paradox rather than just stating it. here's what i'm picturing: the two refrains should be the dogmatist's move and the skeptic's reply — one line that captures something like "i know it, so your evidence is noise i can dismiss," and another that captures "if i always do that, i'll never discover i was wrong." you pick the exact wording, but they should be tight enough to bear repetition. the form should do the structural work: the refrains keep circling back because the paradox keeps reasserting itself, and when they finally meet in the closing couplet, the poem should leave the tension unresolved rather than picking a side. it needs to be accurate enough that someone who knows the epistemology would recognize the shape of the paradox — the closure step, the entitlement claim, the slide from single-case to global — and not wince at a misrepresentation.

the audience is undergrads who've done the reading, so the poem can assume they know the setup but shouldn't presuppose they already grasp why the paradox is hard to escape.

oh and at the very end, after the poem, i want a one-sentence summary of the paradox as the last line, starting with "Summary:" — that way anyone who got lost in the verse has a handhold.
