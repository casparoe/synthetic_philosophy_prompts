"Löb's theorem shows that any agent smart enough to reason about itself hits a brick wall: it can't prove that its own reasoning is reliable without begging the question. The moment you try to build an AI that can check whether its future decisions will be safe, Löb's theorem says the system would need to assume its own soundness to prove its own soundness, which is circular. This is why self-improving AI is impossible in principle — any agent that tries to modify its own code or design a smarter successor will eventually face a point where it can't verify that the new version won't do something catastrophic, and it'll either freeze up or take a blind leap. The whole project of corrigible AI — agents that can reason about and constrain their own future behavior — is basically ruled out by the math. People still working on it are just hoping the theorem has a loophope that it doesn't have."
