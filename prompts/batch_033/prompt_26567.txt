But I've also read that Block himself thinks the spotlight picture was built to explain access, not what-it's-like, and that the theater quietly imports an audience, which starts to look like a homunculus with good lighting. I'm trying to figure out how far the analogy actually carries the global workspace conclusion. Can you audit it? I'd like an explicit correspondence table first — stage, spotlight, actors, audience, broadcast, maybe the director if that's part of the standard picture — then the places where the mapping breaks, and then a judgment about which disanalogies matter for the specific claim that there's no phenomenal consciousness beyond what gets broadcast. Does the conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the theater doing the argumentative work the whole time? And if it's a bad fit for phenomenality, is there a better analogy, or should global workspace people just stop reaching for one?
