I was reading a popular science book that summarizes Daniel Dennett's ideas about evolution and reductionism. The author writes something along these lines:

"Dennett distinguishes between cranes and skyhooks in evolution. Cranes are ordinary physical processes like natural selection that build complexity step by step from the bottom up. Skyhooks are supernatural explanations — essentially, appeals to God or an intelligent designer — that try to explain complexity by dropping down from above. Dennett's point is that Darwin showed we only need cranes, not skyhooks. Relatedly, Dennett warns against 'greedy reductionism,' by which he means the mistake of trying to reduce everything to physics. He thinks the sciences can't all be reduced to fundamental physics and that we should resist reductionist programs in general, because higher-level explanations in terms of beliefs, desires, and functions capture real patterns that physics misses."

I'm a software engineer and I find these ideas interesting — they remind me of debates about whether higher-level abstractions in code are real or whether everything just reduces to machine instructions. But I'm not sure if this summary gets Dennett right. Could you go through it and tell me which parts are accurate, which are subtly off, and what Dennett would object to? I'm particularly curious about whether "greedy reductionism" really means what the author says it means, and whether Dennett actually opposes reductionism in general.
