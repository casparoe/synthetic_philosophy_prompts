We've been arguing about whether our city should use conditional prediction markets to decide things like zoning changes and pandemic restrictions. I keep bringing up specific objections — who writes the "values" metric, what counts as success, cant traders manipulate the market that then becomes official policy — and he just replies "vote on values, bet on beliefs," as if that dissolves the whole problem.

I suspect the slogan is doing a lot more work than one sentence can bear, but I'm having trouble pinning down exactly what it claims. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that sentence could be expressing — about what "values" are, about what "betting" is supposed to add over polling or deliberation or expert judgment, about why markets should get to be authoritative for beliefs but not for values — and give a verdict on each reading? Specifically: which readings are basically platitudes, which are substantive and maybe empirically true, which are false or overreach, and which reading actually does the work in an argument for using futarchy to set zoning or pandemic policy.

And finally, which reading do you think my friend is implicitly using when he says it as a shutdown move, and does that reading actually answer the metric-writing and manipulation objections?
