I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but I've been working on a book that involves time and infinity, and I fell down the Thomson's lamp hole and now I can't get out. The setup is simple enough: a lamp starts off, you turn it on after half a minute, off a quarter minute later, on an eighth later, and so on, halving the intervals, until the minute is up. Thomson's claim was that at the end the lamp can't be on (it always got turned off after it was on) and can't be off (it always got turned on after it was off), so the lamp is impossible, and that's supposed to tell us something deep about supertasks. The standard reply, which I think is Benacerraf's, is that the story just doesn't determine the state at the limit: the instructions only specify the state at times before the minute, and "do this infinitely many times" doesn't settle what's true when the minute is reached. So one side sees a contradiction and the other side sees an underspecified story. And as far as I can tell this standoff has been sitting there since the sixties with nobody moving.

What I want isn't a verdict about who's right. I want to know what kind of disagreement this is, because it doesn't feel like the two sides are even arguing about the same thing. Is it a verbal dispute about what "possible" means — logical, physical, metaphysical? Is there a hidden formal question doing the work, like whether the lamp's state has to have a limit as you approach the minute? Is one side asking "do the instructions force an answer?" while the other is asking "is the story coherent?", two seperate questions being run together as one debate? Or is it just bedrock intuitions about whether "after infinitely many steps" is a place you can stand at all?

And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction that dissolves the standoff, a formal result that settles the hidden question, a reframing of the lamp that both sides would accept as putting the real issue up front, or an honest argument that the two sides can't be made to meet and the debate is just a stuck artifact of the word "task"? I'd rather have a diagnosis of the shape of the disagreement than a survey of the positions.
