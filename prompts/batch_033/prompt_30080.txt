I've been dipping in and out of the embedded agency literature for a couple years now — the MIRI stuff, the logical induction paper, some of the follow-up work — and I honestly can't tell if the conversation has stalled because the problem is just that hard or because people are talking past each other. On one side you've got people saying we need fundamentally new decision theory and a theory of logical uncertainty before we can even think clearly about embedded agents. On the other side you've got people saying this is all elegant but ultimately a distraction — that we can build systems that reason about themselves being part of the world without solving the deepest versions of these problems. And then there's a third group that thinks the whole embedded/Cartesian distinction was drawn wrong from the start. I'm not asking who's right. I'm asking: what kind of disagreement is this? Is it about what "counts" as a solution? Is it a hidden bet about how future AI systems will actually be built? Or is it three different research programs that happen to share some vocabulary? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that could actually settle it?
