I have been trying to assemble a coherent package of views about simplicity and inductive inference, and I would like you to audit the package for internal consistency rather than argue for or against any single commitment. Here is where I have landed.

1. Simplicity is a truth-conducive virtue. Other things being equal, a hypothesis that posits fewer independent parameters, entities, or ad hoc clauses is more likely to be true, not merely easier to test or more convenient to use.

2. Solomonoff induction is the correct formal vindication of this virtue. The rational prior over hypotheses should be proportional to 2^{-K}, where K is Kolmogorov complexity under some fixed universal reference machine, and updating should proceed by conditioning on observed data in the usual way.

3. The choice of universal reference machine is not a serious problem for this program. It changes K only by an additive constant, and although that constant can be large for finite samples, it is swamped as data accumulate, so for realistic inference the machine-dependence is mostly a theoretical worry.

4. The grue problem is dissolved by this framework. A hypothesis stated in terms of grue has to specify a time-indexed shift in color, while the green hypothesis does not, so grue is more complex in the algorithmic sense and rightly receives a lower prior.

5. I do not think the world itself is likely to be simple in any ordinary sense. The true data-generating process could be enormously complex, and yet I take induction along these lines to be the right policy.

Please go through these commitments and identify which are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension, and order the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say exactly what would have to give and what the revision would cost. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to restore coherence. I want an audit, not a lecture: do not use my commitments as a pretext for presenting your own preferred account of induction.
