heres roughly where I've landed on Grice and I want a consistency check before I present it in a seminar. I'm not sure where the problem is, so don't just tell me everything is fine or everything is broken — go through the pairs and seperate the real tensions from the fake ones.

(a) speaker meaning is a matter of reflexive communicative intentions: the speaker intends the audience to form some attitude, intends the audience to recognize that first intention, and intends that recognition to be part of the audience's reason for forming the attitude.

(b) everything a speaker communicates on an occasion is either said or implicated — those two boxes exhaust the space of communicated content.

(c) what is said is fixed by conventional meaning plus disambiguation and reference assignment, and it's the input to implicature. you figure out what was said first, and only then run the Cooperative Principle and the maxims to get at what was implicated.

(d) conversational implicatures are calculable from the Cooperative Principle and the maxims, and the two standard diagnostics hold for them: cancelability and non-detachability.

(e) conventional implicatures are real. words like "but" and "therefore" contribute something over and above truth conditions — the contrast in "but", the inferential link in "therefore" — and that something is part of conventional meaning, not something the maxims generate on the fly.

(f) scalar implicatures like the "some" to "not all" step are conversational implicatures, generated by the Maxim of Quantity.

(g) metaphor and irony are conversational implicatures too, arrived at by flouting the Maxim of Quality.
