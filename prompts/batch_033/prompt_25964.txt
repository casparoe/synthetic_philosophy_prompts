I've been reading about the Sphex wasp—the digger wasp that performs an elaborate, seemingly intelligent routine of bringing a paralyzed cricket to its burrow, inspecting the burrow, and then dragging the cricket inside. The famous finding is that if an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches while the wasp is inside inspecting, the wasp will drag the cricket back to the threshold and inspect again, and will repeat this indefinitely without ever just dragging the cricket straight in. The behavior looks flexible but turns out to be rigid—a fixed action pattern that cannot adapt to a simple perturbation.

This example gets used by Hofstadter, Dennett, and others to raise an uncomfortable question: to what extent are we the same? Our behavior feels flexible and responsive to reasons, but the Sphex makes you wonder whether that feeling is itself just another layer of the machinery—whether what we call free will or genuine flexibility is simply what it feels like from the inside to be a sufficiently complicated Sphex.

What puzzles me is that this framing has been around for decades, and yet the underlying debate does not seem to have moved. On one side, you have people who think the Sphex story generalizes: given enough complexity, you get something that looks like free choice but is still deterministic, or indeterministic but no more free for it. On the other side, you have people who think there is a difference in kind—that human cognition involves something, whether self-reflection, normative responsiveness, agent causation, or some other feature, that breaks the Sphex pattern in a principled way rather than just by adding more subroutines.

I want a diagnosis of why this debate has stalled. Is it a disagreement about facts about what the brain does, about concepts—about what flexibility or free will even mean—or about what would count as evidence? Are both sides actually answering different questions under the same vocabulary? And if you had to say what it would take to move the debate—an experiment, a conceptual distinction, a formal result—what would it be? Please separate what is genuinely settled among people who work on this from what remains contested, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.
