Construct a spectrum argument aimed at making the strongest possible case that the relation all things considered better than is not transitive. The scenario itself should be presentable in one paragraph; the analysis may be as long as needed. The case must satisfy all of the following specifications.

1. The items being compared should be ordinary social states or individual life-prospects — options of the kind a person might face in choosing between jobs, treatment plans, neighborhoods, or institutional policies — not entire possible worlds, and not the familiar pair of intensity of pain and duration of pain. Choose two different evaluative dimensions.

2. The spectrum must contain at least five items in a chain. For each adjacent pair, the later item must be judged better than the earlier one, and each such judgment must be defensible with reasons. No adjacent step may rest on a difference too small to notice, on a vague boundary, or on a stipulation that the two items are equal in value. The intransitivity must be generated by the way the two dimensions interact, not by sorites-style vagueness.

3. The endpoint judgment must reverse: the first item must come out all things considered better than the last. This reversal must be shown rather than stipulated; it should arise from the same kind of comparative reasoning that drives the adjacent judgments, applied to the two endpoints.

4. The case must be robust against the objection that the phrase better than shifts its meaning somewhere along the spectrum. Make the comparative predicate as univocal as you can, and state explicitly what remains constant across all the comparisons.

After presenting the scenario, do the following. (a) State the full chain of verdicts, with the reason for each adjacent step and for the endpoint reversal. (b) Identify the single structural feature of the case that does the work of breaking transitivity, and explain what happens to the argument if that feature is removed. (c) Give the strongest reply available to someone who insists that all things considered better than must be transitive: the reply should identify which judgment or assumption the defender should reject, and what theoretical cost that rejection carries. (d) State what the case is for, and what would have counted as cheating in constructing it — in particular, explain why stipulating the endpoint reversal or letting the predicate change meaning would have defeated the purpose.
