hey, i work at a foundation that makes largish grants — think $2M to $10M, maybe six or seven decisions a year. i've gotten convinced by the moral uncertainty stuff (mainly through the EA grapevine, macaskill and ord and that crowd) that we should be taking our uncertainty about moral theories seriously when we allocate money, rather than just picking whichever theory the ED happens to believe and running with it. but i have literally no idea how to operationalize this. our team is five smart generalists, not philosophers, and we have maybe an afternoon per decision. 

i need a procedure — a checklist or a decision tree or something — that walks us through how to actually do moral-uncertainty-aware grantmaking. it should cover: how to identify which moral theories we should even consider (we can't consider all of them), how to assign credences without it turning into a seminar, how to compare options across theories when the theories don't share a common currency, and what to do when one theory says the stakes are astronomical and another says they're modest. i need to be able to defend this to a board that is skeptical of philosophy.

then run the procedure on two actual decisions: (1) we're choosing between a global health charity with strong RCT evidence (looks great on a narrow welfarist view, less obviously great if you care about structural justice) and a criminal justice reform group where the evidence is messier but the theory of change appeals to a broader set of moral considerations; (2) we're picking between two climate projects, one framed around preventing future human suffering (which looks different depending on whether you count future people as having full moral weight) and one framed around protecting ecosystems for their own sake. flag the steps where the procedure breaks down or where we're just dressing up a gut call as a methodology.
