I've been following the debate about legalizing kidney sales for maybe five years now, reading papers on both sides, and I've gotten to the point where I can predict every move. The pro-market side says: prohibition kills people on waiting lists, consenting adults should be free to contract, and the exploitation argument is paternalistic because it denies poor people an option they'd otherwise choose. The anti-commodification side says: putting a price on body parts degrades the meaning of the body and our shared humanity, markets in kidneys would exploit the desperate even if they "consent," and the long-run effect would be to erode norms of altruistic donation. Both sides have heard all of this a hundred times and nobody budges.

I'm starting to think this isn't really an empirical disagreement — like, if we ran a careful pilot program in some country and got data on exploitation rates and donation crowding-out, I'm not sure either side would change its mind, because each would interpret the data through its own lens. So what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it a clash of foundational moral axioms that can't be argued for? Is it a disagreement about what counts as harm? Or is it a verbal dispute where "exploitation" and "dignity" mean different things to each side? And given your diagnosis, is there any move — a distinction, a new kind of argument, a reframing — that could actually break the impasse, or should I just accept that this one is permenantly stalemated?
