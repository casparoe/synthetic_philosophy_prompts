I've been teaching a philosophy of physics seminar this term, and we've spent the last three weeks on Maxwell's demon and its afterlife in the physics of information. I've come to accept what I'll call the standard resolution: roughly, the Bennett-Landauer view that the demon doesn't violate the second law because erasing the information it acquires necessarily dissipates at least kT ln 2 of heat per bit, which exactly compensates for the entropy reduction from sorting. On this picture, information is physical in a deep sense — it's not that acquiring information is costly (measurement can in principle be done reversibly), but that discarding it is, and the demon must eventually discard its record to complete a cycle. This is the story I teach my students, and it's the story I see repeated in the literature as the settled answer to the paradox.

But I've realized I don't have a clear map of what would actually overturn it. I want to know what would count against this position — not just what skeptics have said, but what the position's own defenders would have to concede as genuine evidence against it. I'm thinking of things like: a clean experimental violation of the Landauer bound, a theoretical demonstration that the demon can operate in a closed cycle without ever erasing its memory, a result showing that measurement itself carries an irreducible entropy cost (contra Landauer), or a quantum demon that demonstrably beats the classical bounds. I'd like these defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with an honest assessment of whether any of them are live possibilities given the current state of physics, or whether the position has been insulated into unfalsifiability. If it has been insulated, tell me whether that's a problem for a view of this kind — after all, it's a conceptual claim about the foundations of thermodynamics, not a typical empirical hypothesis.

Please approach this as an expert in the foundations of statistical mechanics and the physics of information. Think carefully about the logical structure of the Bennett-Landauer argument, distinguish its separable commitments, and say which ones are exposed to empirical or theoretical challenge and which are near-tautological given the rest of physics. I'm especially interested in whether the quantum-information version of the story — with Szilard engines and the like — changes the exposure map in any interesting way, and whether any of the escape routes open at the quantum level are genuinely live rather than merely logically possible.
