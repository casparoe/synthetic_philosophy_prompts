I'm putting together a two-page proposal for a summer stipend and I want it to be on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism, but not the standard version of the problem. The standard version says the things we call pleasures — the warmth of a bath, the relief of finally solving a puzzle, schadenfreude, the burn of a hard run — don't share any single felt quality, so hedonism about well-being is trying to build a theory out of a category that has no unity. Fine. But I half-suspect that version is too quick, and that the more interesting question is comparative: does the heterogeneity of pleasure cause more trouble for hedonism than the heterogeneity of desire causes for desire-satisfaction theories, or the heterogeneity of "objective goods" causes for objective list theories? Every theory of well-being seems to have a central category that starts to fall apart when you squint at it. So the question I want the proposal to state precisely is something like: is there a version of the heterogeneity problem that is special to hedonism — one that bites hedonism even if analogous problems bite its rivals — and if so, what exactly is the special feature? Is it the demand for a common felt quality, the fact that hedonism treats pleasure as the sole primitive, something about aggregation, or something else? The usual responses (the felt-quality view, the attitudinal view) are answers to "what is pleasure?", but they don't obviously address whether the heterogeneity worry is special to hedonism, so I think the comparative angle is the part that's open.

Turn that into a two-page proposal. I want: the question stated so it has a determinate answer, why the existing positions leave it open, what I'd actually do month by month (this is a philosophy project, so the "method" is mostly distinctions, arguments, and test cases — but be concrete about which cases you'd use and why this comparative approach beats the obvious alternative of just re-litigating whether pleasures share a felt quality), what a successful result would look like, and an honest statement of what would count as the project failing. Also, please seperate the parts that are settled from the parts that are genuinely contested — I don't want to write a proposal that quietly treats a contested reading as the consensus. And don't bother with citations, I'll add them myself.
