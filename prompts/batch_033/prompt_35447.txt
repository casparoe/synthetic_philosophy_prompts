Below is the exam question and the student's full answer.

Exam question: "Egalitarians are often said to face the leveling-down objection. State the objection in your own words, using an example. Then explain how the priority view (prioritarianism) differs from egalitarianism, and evaluate whether the priority view succeeds in avoiding the objection while still capturing the thought that benefits to the worse off matter more."

Student answer: "Egalitarianism, at least in the version the question is about, says that equality is valuable for its own sake, not merely as a means to something else. The leveling-down objection is usually illustrated like this: suppose two people are at welfare levels 100 and 50. If we 'level down' by lowering the better-off person to 50, nobody gains and one person loses. The egalitarian seems committed to saying that this change is better in one respect, because the outcome is perfectly equal. The objection is that it is implausible to count a change that harms someone and helps no one as better in any respect at all. Prioritarianism, by contrast, denies that equality itself is valuable. It says that the moral value of a benefit depends on the absolute level of well-being of the recipient: the same-sized benefit counts more when the recipient is worse off. So prioritarianism would not judge the leveling-down change to be better, since the worse-off person gains nothing. This is how it avoids the objection. Still, prioritarianism can be seen as a form of egalitarianism with equality treated as derivatively valuable, because any move toward a more equal distribution will benefit at least one worse-off person. That is why many people think prioritarianism captures the egalitarian intuition while avoiding the objection."
