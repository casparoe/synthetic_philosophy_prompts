So my friend Dave and I got into it over some lottery choices he put to me at a bar. He gave me two decisions:

First: pick between (A) $900 guaranteed, or (B) an 89% chance of $900, a 10% chance of $5000, and a 1% chance of nothing.

Second: pick between (C) an 11% chance of $900, or (D) a 10% chance of $5000.

I took A in the first one and D in the second. Dave said I'm being inconsistent. His argument was: look, in the first choice, the only difference between A and B is that B swaps the sure thing for a mix where most of it (89%) is the same $900, some of it (10%) is way better, and a sliver is nothing. In the second choice, C and D are just A and B with that 89% chunk of $900 carved out of both. So if I really prefer A over B, I should prefer C over D too — otherwise I'm flip-flopping based on how the options are packaged.

I told him that's ridiculous. In the first choice, the certainty of $900 feels totally different from a gamble, even if the gamble is mostly $900. In the second choice, neither option is certain, and the odds are so close (11% vs 10%) that I'd rather swing for the $5000. He said I'm violating something called the independence axiom and that my preferences can't be represented by any consistent utility function. I said maybe that's a problem for the axiom, not for me. He said if I think that, I'm gonna get money-pumped someday. I don't even know what that means.

Can you walk through our argument and say who's right about what? I want to know if Dave actually has me cornered logically, or if there's a defensible way to make the choices I made.
