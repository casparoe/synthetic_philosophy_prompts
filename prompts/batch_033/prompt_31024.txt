write me a villanelle about Hempel's raven paradox. the two refrains should be the hypothesis that all ravens are black and it's contrapositive that all non-black things are non-ravens (or close paraphrases that scan, but keep the logical relation obvious). the form has to do the work: since the refrains are logically equivalent, the poem should let them trade places stanza by stanza the way the two formulations trade places in the paradox, and the final couplet should bring them together on the green apple or the white shoe that confirms the raven hypothesis by confirming the contrapositive. someone who knows the paradox should recognize it, including the standard Hempelian point that this confirmation is real but vanishingly weak — don't write the poem as if the apple is obviously irrelevant and that's the whole story. after the poem, add a short note saying how confident you are in the philosophical content and which bit you're least sure of.
