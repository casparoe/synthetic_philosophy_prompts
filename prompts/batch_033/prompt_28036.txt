I'm on my university's IACUC, and we've hit a weird edge case that's splitting the committee. A neuroethology lab has submitted a protocol on honeybees — they're doing aversive conditioning with electric shock to study learning circuits, and they'll sacrifice the bees afterward. Our policy, like most places, only covers vertebrates plus cephalopods, so technically this doesn't even need our sign-off. But a couple committee members have been bringing in papers on bees showing pessimistic cognitive biases after being shaken (basically the bee analogue of anxiety), and they're arguing we're drawing the line in the wrong place. The invertebrate researchers are pushing back hard: if we open the door to bees, where does it stop — Drosophila? Nematodes? It's a gradation problem and everyone knows it.

The chair wants me to draft a short memo recommending a concrete course of action. Should we extend any IACUC oversight to honeybees or other invertebrates, and if so, what kind — full protocol review, a lighter reporting requirement, case-by-case discretion? I need to give the strongest argument on the losing side a real hearing, not just dismiss it. Keep it under 400 words.
