I have been reading about supertasks — tasks with infinitely many steps completed in a finite time. The standard cases I can keep in view at once are these four. Zeno's dichotomy, in which a runner covers half the remaining distance, then half of what remains, and so on. Thomson's lamp, in which a switch is flipped at 1/2 minute, then at 3/4, then at 7/8, and so on, so that at the end there seems to be no fact about whether the lamp is on. The Ross-Littlewood urn, in which at each stage ten numbered balls go in and the lowest-numbered one comes out, so the pile grows without bound at every finite stage yet is empty after all the stages. And a collision chain, in which infinitely many tiny objects lined up in a row set off an endless sequence of impacts in a finite time, and the setup seems not to fix a unique final state. I suspect I have forgotten one more standard sort of case, or else a fifth genuinely distinct one could be built to stand next to these four. If there is a missing standard case, tell me what it is; otherwise, offer a new supertask that is not a rewording, special case, or combination of the four I listed, and say what question it isolates better than they do.
