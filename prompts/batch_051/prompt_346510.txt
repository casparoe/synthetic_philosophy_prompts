I've been drafting a paper on deontological constraints and, to get my head straight, I wrote out the commitments I actually hold in plain English. I have a nagging feeling they don't fully hang together, but I can't put my finger on where the crack is. Could you audit this set for me?

1. There are genuine agent-relative constraints: I may not kill an innocent person even if killing them is the only way to stop two other people from each killing an innocent person. The number of killings I'd prevent does not, on its own, make my killing permissible.

2. The ground of these constraints is respect. Persons are inviolable, and I'm suspicious of the idea that aggregate outcomes could ever license me to violate somebody.

3. Still, when my only options each involve violating a constraint, I think I should violate the fewest and least serious ones. If the only two live options are to break one serious promise or break two equally serious promises, I should break the one; likewise, if my only two ways of preventing a disaster would each foreseeably kill someone, I should pick the plan that kills fewer.

4. I also accept the intend/foresee distinction (the Doctrine of Double Effect, more or less): a death brought about as a merely foreseen side-effect of saving people can be permissible, whereas bringing about the same death as an intended means is not. I would redirect the trolley but not push the bystander.

5. And I take that distinction to be grounded in respect too: what's fundamentally wrong is using a person as a mere means, not causing a bad outcome as such.

Which pairs are in genuine tension, and which are only apparently in tension? For each genuine tension, I'd like a sense of how bad it is, what the cheapest fix would be, and what that fix costs; then tell me which single revision would leave the set most coherent. Don't assume I'm willing to give up any particular one.
