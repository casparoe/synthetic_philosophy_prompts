I am a retired atmospheric chemist who has spent the past few years reading philosophy of science. In two months I will speak to the climate modeling division of the institute where I spent my career: scientists who know the relevant physics and accept that stratospheric aerosol injection (dispersing reflective sulfate aerosols into the upper atmosphere to reduce incoming sunlight) would very likely cool the planet, but who consider any outdoor work—even small, heavily instrumented field experiments—politically reckless and scientifically premature. Most of them have no formal philosophical training, and they share a professional worry that experimental work will tarnish climate science's credibility and turn their modeling results into ammunition for deployment advocates. I will defend the thesis that a staged, internationally observed program of small outdoor experiments is now the more responsible path, and that treating such research as off-limits is itself a moral-political decision being made quietly by funders and peers rather than by any accountable body. My case turns on three claims: (1) models are least reliable exactly where the highest-stakes uncertainties lie, especially aerosol microphysics and regional precipitation response; (2) the moral-hazard worry and the termination-shock worry (rapid warming if injection were stopped abruptly) are best understood as arguments for strict governance and explicit stopping rules rather than for a research ban; and (3) an unlegislated de facto moratorium has no more democratic legitimacy than a governed, transparent experiment. Draft me a Q&A preparation sheet: the eight or so objections I will actually receive from this audience—not from environmental activists and not from deployment enthusiasts—ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased as these scientists would phrase them, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is weakest, and indicating which segment of the room each objection is most likely to come from. Tell me what I should concede up front, what I should refuse to concede under any pressure, and include the question I am hoping nobody asks.
