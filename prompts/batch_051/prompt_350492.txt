Suppose that in 2030 a major national science agency adopts a funding rule under which every multi-year project must present itself as a Lakatosian research programme: a hard core of commitments the group may not abandon during the grant, a protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses that may be revised, and a criterion that a revision to the protective belt is admissible only if the revised programme has since successfully predicted at least one novel fact that was not used in constructing the revision. Renewal decisions turn on demonstrated progressive problem shifts rather than on publications or citations. Predict how the funded research landscape changes over the following twenty-five years: which fields and kinds of project thrive, which quietly lapse, how scientists adapt their behaviour to the rule, and whether the rule actually suppresses ad hoc theory-saving. Give exactly five numbered predictions, each no more than two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Begin each prediction with a confidence level, state the main mechanism behind it, and end with the one condition most likely to reverse it.
