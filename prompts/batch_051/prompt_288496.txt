At work we've got this ancient lint rule that keeps failing CI for certain patterns, and everytime a new person tries to figure out why it exists they come up empty. The person who added it left three years ago and the comment next to the rule just says "leave this, it prevents the incident from 2019" but the incident write-up got lost in a wiki migration. One of my coworkers wants to delete the rule. His reasoning is: a rule is only as good as the reason someone currently has for it, and if nobody on the team can reconstruct the reason anymore, then for all practical purposes there is no reason, so we should get rid of it. My gut says that's to convenient — the reason might still be true of the code even though no one is holding it in their head anymore, like the rule is carrying a rationale that's detached from any actual reasoner. Who's right? And is there a principled way to decide which inherited rules to keep when the person whose reason it was is gone but the rule lives on?
