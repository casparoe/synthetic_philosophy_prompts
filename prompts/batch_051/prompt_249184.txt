I retired a few years ago from a career in parasitology and evolutionary ecology, and I've been reading moral philosophy since then, mostly to keep busy. The discussions of the free rider problem keep striking me as missing a set of distinctions we use all the time in biology, so I've been kicking around a little homemade taxonomy and I want someone to tell me honestly whether it's doing any work or their just dressing up ordinary intuitions.

In ecology you can split organisms that live off another's effort into roughly three groups. Commensals take a benefit without harming the host much, if at all — the mites on your skin, a remora hitching a ride. Parasites take a benefit and do harm the host, but usually not enough to kill it — a tapeworm, a tick. And brood parasites trick the host into doing the work of raising them — a cuckoo chick in a warbler's nest, fed by parents who've been manipulated into treating it as their own. My thought is that human free riding falls into the same three boxes. There's commensal free riding: the person who reads the neighborhood association's newsletter every month but has never once written or delivered it; they gain, and the people doing the work aren't really worse off. There's parasitic free riding: the housemate who eats the communal food and leaves the kitchen worse than he found it; the host system is actively degraded. And there's brood-parasite free riding: the colleague who contributes almost nothing to a project but frames things so everyone ends up investing in him and he walks away with the credit, like the cuckoo chick being fed.

The claim I want checked is that the moral wrongness of taking without contributing tracks this gradient: commensal free riding is at worst a minor vice or no vice at all, parasitic free riding is wrong because it leaves others worse off, and brood-parasite free riding is worst because it doesn't just take — it subverts the host's agency and makes them complicit in the taking. I'm not sure whether the manipulation part is doing independent moral work or is just parasitic free riding plus lying. And I keep worrying that a rich person enjoying public goods they don't pay for looks commensal on my scheme but might still be wrong on fairness grounds, which would mean the gradient leaves out a whole dimension. Is the taxonomy coherent, does it already exist under another name, and what are the strongest objections to it — is any of them fatal?
