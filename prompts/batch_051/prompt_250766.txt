so ive been trying to write up my philosophical profile on whole brain emulation for a workshop, and someone told me the commitments are in tension but wouldnt tell me where. i cant find it and i want someone to actually audit the set rather than give me a survey. heres roughly what i believe:

1. functionalism about the mind: mental life supervenes on causal/computational organization. if you implemented my brains organization in silicon youd get the same conscious experiences and thoughts; neural tissue isnt doing anything over and above implementing computations.
2. that also means fidelity has a fairly high tolerance for noise. an emulation that kept track of spike rates and synaptic strengths, and maybe slow plasticity-ish changes, would be enough, because molecular and subcellular detail is just noise for cognition. i dont think you need atom-perfect or quantum anything, and that's part of why i take the whole thing to be feasible in principle.
3. in the classic gradual replacement scenario, where each neuron is swapped one at a time for a functionally identical chip, id stay conscious throughout and at the end the all-silicon system would still be me, not just somebody qualitatively like me.
4. but a destructive scan-and-instantiate upload is different: it would kill the original me, and the resulting emulation would be a copy with my mental life, a different person. even though something exactly like me carries on, id be dead.
5. i also hold that in a non-destructive duplication case, at most one of the two resulting people can be me. identity is one-to-one, so mere psychological continuity cant be the whole story because it branches.
6. and i think identity can survive the emulation being fully paused and restarted, like a computer being shut down and booted back up with the same state. surviving that kind of gap seems fine to me.

where im really unsure is whether these hang together, and if they dont: which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look like it once the right distinction is drawn, and what the cheapest fix is. order the tensions by severity. make it a committed audit, not a menu: tell me which of these i should revise, or defend the set if it actually is consistent, and make the case. if you think the real problem is that im equivocating on one word somewhere, say so plainly and show me what breaks when i keep using the word two ways. and tell me which one revision would do the most cleanup, even if it leaves me unhappy.
