ok so I'm trying to work out if I actually have one principle about voting rather than just a pile of opinions. my gut calls:

- compulsory voting is fine with me, like a small fine if you don't show up. if only the super motivated people vote, candidates just pander to that slice and ignore everyone else, so making everyone turn out seems like it forces politicians to represent the whole population.
- lowering the voting age to 16 for everything: no. 16-year-olds mostly borrow their opinions from parents or tiktok, so adding them doesn't make the electorate more representative, it just adds a bunch of copied votes.
- noncitizens who live here and pay taxes should be able to vote in city council and school board elections. those choices run their neighborhood and their kids' school, so it seems wrong to shut them out.
- but noncitizens voting in national elections: no. the country's overall direction should be decided by citizens.
- I also don't like a civics quiz as a condition for voting. even a fair one feels too much like the old literacy tests.
- and 16-year-olds in local elections specifically: I still lean no, even though it makes me a little less sure of the noncitizen-local thing.

is there a single principle that captures all or most of these, and what does it say about: 16-year-olds in school board elections only, noncitizens in state elections (between city and national), and compulsory voting for 16-year-olds if the age were lowered? please state it precisly, say which of my verdicts it can't keep, and give the verdicts a principle-following person would have on the three extra cases.
