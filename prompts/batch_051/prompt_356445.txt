Here's a parallel I've been turning over and can't decide about, between impact measures for AI and environmental impact assessment. In the AI safety world there's this low-impact agent idea: you don't give the agent a full account of human values (since nobody has one); instead you give it a baseline — usually the world as it would have been if the agent had done nothing, or had taken some narrow default action — and you penalize it for making the world diverge from that baseline. The selling point is that you can define "avoid side effects" without first solving the whole value-specification problem: you don't need to say what's good, only what counts as change from the default.

Environmental impact assessment looks to me like the same move, worked out for ordinary projects decades earlier. A dam or a highway doesn't have to show it maximizes social welfare; it has to characterize the environment as it is, compare against the no-project alternative, and show that significant changes are avoided or mitigated. Big departures from the status quo need justification; small ones don't. So both regimes seem to substitute "minimize divergence from baseline" for "maximize value" as a way to govern interventions under deep uncertainty about the good.

My question is whether this is a real identity of structure or just a loose analogy. Here's the specific thing that nags me. In the environmental case, the values come back in through the back door: you have to decide which parts of the environment are worth assessing in the first place, and what counts as a "significant" impact, and those are substantive value judgments. Does that mean the AI analogue has to make the same kind of choice when it picks a distance metric over world states — i.e., low impact is just value-laden in disguise, and the baseline trick never actually avoided the problem? Or is there a version of the AI idea that's genuinely value-free in a way the environmental process isn't? I want to know where the mapping holds, where it comes apart, what would have to be true of both sides for the back-door-value problem to transfer, and whether "change from baseline" is a way of not needing a full value function or just a way of hiding one.
