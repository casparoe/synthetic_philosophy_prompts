My brother has stopped calling our mother. She wasn't cruel, just cold, and she played favorites, and at this point he genuinely doesn't want her in his life. His argument is: 'I don't want to call, nothing in my current motivations would be served by calling, so I have no reason to call. A reason has to connect to something I already care about. If you can't point to a desire or goal of mine that calling would serve, then you're not giving me a reason — you're just telling me a fact about the world and hoping I'll have feelings about it.' I want to say he has a reason anyway: she's our mother, he's the only one she'll listen to about her health. But he says those are exactly the kind of bare facts that can't generate a reason unless I already care about them. I can't seperate the suspicion that he's got a real philosophical point from the feeling that something has gone wrong if a theory says you can never tell someone they should care about something they don't. Is he right? If he is, what follows for ever criticizing someone for not caring about what they should care about? If he's wrong, where's the flaw — and what would a reason be, if it doesn't have to hook into what he already wants? Don't let either of us off the hook.
