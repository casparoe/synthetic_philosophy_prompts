Heres something I keep going back and forth on. I want a working definition of whats a reactive attitude, in the Strawsonian sense, that actually sorts hard cases rather than just saying "an emotion about another person." For each case below, tell me reactive attitude, not a reactive attitude, or no determinate answer, then give me the definition and run it through.

1. A flash of resentment when someone steps on your foot, before you realize it was because the train lurched.
2. Sustained resentment toward someone who deliberately shoulder-checks you in the street.
3. Indignation on behalf of a stranger you see getting scammed.
4. Indignation at a company for an exploitative policy that no single person ever sat down and decided on.
5. Gratitude toward a friend who skipped a big event to sit with you in urgent care.
6. Gratitude toward a voice assistant that reminded you about a meeting, where you have no illusion it has intentions.
7. Annoyance with a dog that barks outside your window all night.
8. Anger at a drunk driver who hit your car, before you learn anything about him.
9. The same anger after you learn he was blackout and has no memory of driving.
10. Guilt for breaking a promise to a friend.
11. Awe at someone who free-soloed a hard climb, where the feeling is about their skill and nerve, not their character.
12. The calm, detached concern you feel when managing someone in a psychotic episode who is currently insulting you.

My suspicion is the definition will have to say something like: you attribute good or ill will to the person and you hold them in the participant stance, not the objective stance. That would put 2, 3, 5, 8, and 10 clearly inside and 6, 7, 11, and 12 clearly outside. But 4 and 9 are the two I actually want you to stress-test, and 1 is odd because the will is innocent but the feeling still rises. Propose the defintion, run it through all twelve, and tell me which of your verdicts is least secure and whether you'd revise the definition or just bite the bullet.
