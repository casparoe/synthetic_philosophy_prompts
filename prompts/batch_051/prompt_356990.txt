Here is Locke’s canonical statement of the labour theory of appropriation in the *Second Treatise of Government*, ch. V, sec. 27:

> Though the earth, and all inferior creatures, be common to all men, yet every man has a property in his own person: this no body has any right to but himself. The labour of his body, and the work of his hands, we may say, are properly his. Whatsoever then he removes out of the state that nature hath provided, and left it in, he hath mixed his labour with, and joined to it something that is his own, and thereby makes it his property. It being by him removed from the common state nature hath placed it in, it hath by this labour something annexed to it, that excludes the common right of other men: for this labour being the unquestionable property of the labourer, no man but he can have a right to what that is once joined to, at least where there is enough, and as good, left in common for others.

Now consider a variant of Locke’s acorn-gathering case. A solitary forager finds an oak that produces acorns. But the tree is not merely a common resource; it is the only mature oak in a deforested region, and the forager’s labour is not restricted to picking up fallen acorns. He clears undergrowth, diverts water, and grafts branches, thereby making the tree vastly more productive. In doing so, however, he uses up the remaining groundwater and prevents any second tree from being cultivated by anyone else who later enters the region. So there is no longer “enough, and as good” left in common for others, and his labour itself is partly responsible for the disappearance of the “as good.”

Does the labour-mixing argument by itself give him property in the improved tree and its acorns, or does the proviso’s failure defeat the title he would otherwise have acquired? If the title is defeated, what should happen to the labour he has mixed in—does it become part of the common stock, does he acquire a claim to compensation from later appropriators, or does the labour simply cease to matter for the property analysis? Work through what the passage commits Locke to, and say exactly where the variant forces a choice between the labour-mixing rationale and the enough-and-as-good condition.
