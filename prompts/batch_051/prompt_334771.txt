I'm going to open with an interpretation-of-Kant challenge.

Right after the section in the Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals where Kant introduces the formula of universal law, he considers a person short on money who wonders whether he may borrow by promising to repay, while knowing he won't repay. Kant's thought is that the maxim, roughly that whenever I believe myself in need of money I will borrow and promise to repay it though I know I never will, could not hold as a universal law because the practice of promising would destroy itself: everyone would know such promises are not to be trusted, so the promise would become impossible as a means to the loan. That is not an empirical prediction for Kant; it is supposed to show a kind of practical contradiction in the very willing.
