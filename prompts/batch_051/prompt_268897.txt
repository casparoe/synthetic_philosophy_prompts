I have been trying to pin down what Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism would look like if it were written up as a causal model, and I want you to do the translation carefully. My working summary of the position is this: God is the only genuine cause; what looks like fire causing the burning of cotton is not a real productive relation between creatures; the regular order we observe is God's habit, which he can in principle vary, and this is what makes miracles possible. I know modern causality reasonably well — structural causal models, directed edges, structural equations, exogenous variables, interventions, do-expressions, counterfactuals — but I only know the occasionalist side at the level of that summary, so flag places where my summary is wrong or already question-begging.

First, give the translation. Restate occasionalism as a thesis about the true causal graph of the world: where God enters the model, whether the fire node has a directed edge to the burn node, how the observed regularity between them is represented, and what a miracle or a violation of the customary order amounts to in the formal vocabulary. Be explicit about which structural equations you would and would not include, and about whether the fire is best treated as a cause of the burn, a common cause shared with the burn, an intervention target, a context variable, or something the SCM language cannot state without distortion.

Second, give the residue report. Which parts of Al-Ghazālī's view survive the translation cleanly, which survive only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in structural causal models at all? I specifically suspect two stress points. An occasion — fire as the occasion for God's action without being its cause — seems to require a conditioning relation that is explicitly not a causal relation, and God's standing power to vary the usual order seems to sit badly with the idea that a structural equation defines a well-behaved counterfactual. Assess those, and then say for each failure whether it is a limitation of the SCM vocabulary or a sign that the original position was less precise than it looks. Spend at least half of the answer on the residues rather than on the parts that translate smoothly.
