I've been reading about low-impact AI — the idea that rather than fully specifying an agent's goals, we can make it safe by penalizing it for changing the world relative to a baseline in which it never acted. I tried to make this precise and ended up with an argument that seems to show the approach is either circular or arbitrary, which I find hard to accept because avoiding side effects feels like ordinary engineering to me. Here's the argument.

(1) Calling something a side effect is only meaningful against a distinction between what the agent was supposed to bring about and everything else.
(2) That distinction comes from the agent's task or goal specification.
(3) But an actual task specification picks out only a few intended consequences — "clean the floor," "maximize this metric" — and does not determine, for the indefinitely many other consequences of the action, which are extraneous.
(4) Since the world itself doesn't mark a consequence as extraneous, the intended/side-effect boundary has to be supplied from elsewhere.
(5) The only candidate elsewhere is a full specification of value: a ranking of the relevant outcomes that says which changes are acceptable at which cost.
(6) So any well-defined notion of avoiding side effects presupposes a full specification of value.
(7) But the low-impact program is motivated precisely by the fact that we can't supply such a specification; that's why an agent with a simple cleaning objective might repaint the walls while maximizing exactly the reward we gave it.
(8) Therefore, low-impact constraints either secretly assume a full value specification or draw the intended/side-effect line arbitrarily. In the first case the approach doesn't solve the problem; in the second, "avoid side effects" is only an ad hoc patch.
(9) So "avoid side effects" cannot be defined without a full specification of value.

This seems to prove too much, because I constantly engineer against side effects — pure functions, no mutation of global state, limiting the blast radius of a refactor — with only an informal, partial spec of what the system is supposed to do. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd most like to keep (7), since underspecified goals are the normal condition of real software.
