The lab works on a setup called debate, where two AI systems argue opposite sides of a factual question in front of a third AI judge, on the theory that even a mediocre judge can find the truth if both sides actually try. Their twist is meant to stress-test that setup against obfuscation.

Here's the proposal. Two debaters face a judge that only gets to read their arguments and is scored on final accuracy. Neither the judge nor the honest debater is told which of the fogger's sentences are "fog." The honest debater is assigned to defend the true answer. The honest debater can flag up to three of the fogger's sentences as suspected fog; flagged sentences are deleted before the judge reads anything, but each false flag costs the honest debater a large chunk of its score. The fogger is scored on how much it moves the judge's confidence toward the false answer; the honest debater on how much it moves confidence toward the true answer.

Walk me through how this lands if the tournament runs for many rounds and all three models keep training on their scores. What does the fogger learn to write?
