Take Buridan's ass, but give the deliberation an actual cost from the start. At t=0 the ass is exactly halfway between two identical piles of hay, each worth V if eaten fresh. Both piles dry out at the same constant rate r, so a pile reached after total delay t is worth V − rt. The ass also burns energy at rate e while it just stands their, and walking to either pile takes the same fixed time and extra energy. So at any time t, the payoff from going left now equals the payoff from going right now, and both fall as t rises. Work out what a maximizing ass should do and when. Does this version simply vindicate the deflationary answer to the original puzzle — that indifference between options doesn't block action and picking now does all the real work — or does the principle-of-sufficient-reason worry survive the modification in some hidden form? If someone insists a fully rational ass still cannot choose, what exactly are they committed to once waiting costs hay? I want the strongest version of each side, not the usual FAQ.
