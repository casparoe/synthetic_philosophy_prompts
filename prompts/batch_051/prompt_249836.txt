I've been trying to get Wang Yangming's claim that knowing and acting are one straight, and instead of reading more commentary I want to reverse-engineer it from my own gut on some cases. Here's where I land, roughly.

My cousin knows all the health facts about smoking — she can recite the risks in her sleep — and she keeps smoking. I want to say she does not really know smoking is harming her; she has the information but not the knowledge, or at least not the kind of moral knowledge the doctrine cares about. Meanwhile my brother-in-law is on a diet and, as he's cutting himself a slice of cake, says "I know I shouldn't." I want to say he does know, he just doesn't do it. That already feels like a tension, and I can't tell whether I'm being inconsistent or whether the two cases are supposed to come apart.

Then there's my old roommate who volunteers every Saturday out of pure habit. She never thinks about whether it's good, and if you asked her she'd say she does it because she's always done it. I want to say that's action without knowledge, so it's not the unity either — it's missing something on the knowing side.

I once saw a man hesitate at a lake, clearly trying to work out whether saving a struggling kid was worth ruining his phone, and then he went in. In the seconds before he moved, did he know he should save the kid? I want to say yes — it seemed like he knew the whole time and just swallowed the cost. But on the unity claim, maybe he only came to know at the moment he moved? That feels wrong too.

I also had a coworker who cheated on an expense report and was genuinely surprised when I suggested it was stealing. She said the words "stealing is wrong" and meant them sincerely, but couldn't see that her report was an instance of it. I want to say she didn't really know stealing is wrong — she knew a slogan.

And a stranger one: my friend grew up in a small apocalyptic religious community, left as an adult, and now says he "knew" as a child that the end-times was coming. He acted on it fully — gave away his toys, fasted, the works. But the thing was false. Does the unity of knowledge and action apply to false beliefs? I want to say he had the knowledge-action link but didn't know anything, because there was nothing to know. Or maybe the unity only cares about the link, not the truth.

So: is there one principle that makes sense of all this, or close? Which of my verdicts does it force me to give up? And the case I keep going back and forth on: a severely depressed friend who says "I know I should call my sister back" and wants to, but lies in bed for two days anyway. Is that a genuine gap between knowing and doing, or would the doctrine say the knowledge is incomplete somewhere?
