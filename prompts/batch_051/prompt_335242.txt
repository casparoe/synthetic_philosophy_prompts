I am the recording secretary of a small workers' cooperative. We make decisions in monthly meetings under a loose 'consensus' practice: we have no written rules of procedure, yet the minutes I write are treated as binding on every member whenever money, labor, or membership status is at stake, and they may be challenged at the next meeting. I need a reusable post-meeting procedure for deciding whether the members have actually made a collective decision, or merely had a conversation after which individuals happened to have separate personal intentions. Write it as a checklist I can apply in about twenty minutes, without legal or philosophical training, after any meeting: the questions to ask in order, what counts as sufficient evidence at each step, where the procedure stops and records 'no decision,' and where it hands the matter back to the whole membership because the question is a matter of values rather than fact. Ground the checklist in whatever the philosophy of joint action and collective commitment actually shows, but present it as a checklist, not an essay; say which distinctions a layperson would dismiss as pedantic must survive and which can be dropped. Then run the checklist on these three cases: (a) several members said 'I'm in if everyone else is in,' and no one gave an unconditional yes; (b) members stayed silent while the chair laid out a plan and said 'hearing no objection, we'll proceed'; (c) a member nodded when asked 'can you commit to this?' but now says she meant only 'I can live with it if the rest of you go ahead.' Be explicit about the cases the checklist cannot settle, and make sure one of the worked cases yields an outcome I would not personally have recorded.
