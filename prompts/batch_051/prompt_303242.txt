I've been kicking around a theory of work I call the friction account. The core claim is that the felt meaning of a job tracks how much unnecessary resistance it makes you overcome, not the value of what you actually produce and not how skilled it makes you feel. A miserable spreadsheet reconciliation that fights you all afternoon can feel more meaningful than a genuinely useful analysis an AI assistant turns out in one click, because the fight was the work. Automation, then, doesn't mainly destroy jobs; it de-frictions them, leaving jobs that survive as husks that feel empty even when the pay's fine. My slogan: meaning is the scar tissue of inefficiency. I know this is somewhere near effort justification and the generic "struggle gives life meaning" stuff, but I want to know if there's an actual theory here or just a vibe. Give me the strongest case it handles, the strongest case it gets backwards, and tell me whether I've just reinvented an existing position under a worse name. Start with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning.
