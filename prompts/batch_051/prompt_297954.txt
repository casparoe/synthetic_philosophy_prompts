I've been rereading the Ant Fugue in Hofstadter's *Gödel, Escher, Bach* after a long forum argument about whether an ant colony could be conscious. The part I keep getting stuck on is when Aunt Hillary, who is a whole colony, tells the anteater something like the individual ants don't have thoughts or awareness, but the colony-level pattern does. She seems to treat the trail-following and signal-passing as a self, even though no single ant is thinking "I am Aunt Hillary." I've been trying to turn that into a working definition of "mind" or "consciousness" that doesn't just beg the question.

Here are the cases I want it to sort: an individual ant (clearly not), a human (clearly yes), a dog (probably yes), an octopus, a chess engine, a corporation, a thermostat, and Aunt Hillary herself. The definition should give a definate answer for Aunt Hillary, and it shouldn't reduce to "anything that processes information" or "only things with neurons," because both of those feel like dodging the hard part. Bonus if it explains why mental terms seem to come in degrees instead of a clean on/off. And if the right answer is that the concept is just confused, say that.
