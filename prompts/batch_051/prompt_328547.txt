In Stoic ethics, and especially in the modern Stoic revival, the dichotomy of control is often treated as a simple split between what is up to us and what is not. The label is familiar; the boundary is not. Give me a working definition of what is up to us that gets the right results on these cases: (1) a calm person who, after an insult, deliberately decides to answer in an even tone; (2) flinching at a door slamming before one can think; (3) the hot flash of anger in the first instant after an insult, before any decision; (4) a tic one can suppress for a while with effort but cannot make disappear; (5) a belief formed on good evidence that one cannot now make oneself surrender; (6) the mood one wakes up in tomorrow; (7) whether a colleague judges one's latest work favorably. I am confident that (1) is up to us and that (2) and (7) are not, and I have no settled view about (3), (4), (5), and (6). Propose a definition, run it through every case, flag where it returns an uncomfortable or indeterminate verdict, and revise or defend the definition in light of that. Then say which of your final verdicts you would most want to hedge.
