I've been reading the Firestone and Scholl stuff about whether cognition penetrates perception, and I've ended up confused about what would even count as evidence, let alone a strong case. They hammer on the point that top-down effects on vision aren't automatically cognitive penetration, because things like attention, expectation set, and response bias could explain the results, and they keep bringing up the El Greco fallacy—roughly, the idea that if El Greco's elongated figures were caused by astigmatism, the same distortion would apply to the canvas he was copying, so the painting should've come out normal anyway, which is supposed to show that you can't infer a distorted percept from a distorted output. Fine. But then when I look at the other side of the debate, people point to cases like the backpack-hill effect or memory-color making a banana look yellower, and I think: if a belief or even just a bodily state changes what you see, isn't that exactly cognition reaching into perception? If we accept that these top-down effects are real, why does everyone keep protecting the idea of perception as a separate, encapsulated system? Is the early-versus-late vision distinction a real empirical boundary, or just a goalpost people move when they dont want to accept that seeing is shot through with thinking?
