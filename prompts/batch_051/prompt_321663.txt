Suppose a mid-sized country decided, starting tomorrow, to move all its main legislation into a new chamber run on something close to Habermas's ideal speech situation. The standing orders are roughly these: every affected party and social group gets a guaranteed seat and equal speaking time; debate on a bill continues until no one can offer a new argument; party whips are banned inside the chamber, so no one can be threatened with deselection for changing their mind; contributions are ruled out of order if they involve threats, deals, appeals to authority or status, or rhetoric that doesn't engage a previous argument; and the aim is explicitly consensus rather than a majority. Predict how this chamber looks after a decade: which kinds of bills get through, which quietly stall forever, how power and money re-enter through the cracks, whether genuine consensus ever occurs, and which of the five conditions is doing the real work (or can't be institutionalised at all). I want the chain of reasoning, with each step marked as robust or a guess, and an explicit statement of what would have to be true for the cynic's prediction to be wrong.
