I keep seeing people gesture at Locke in debates about abandoned software, but the actual application is usually left vague. Let's make it concrete. A proprietary program was written in the late 1980s by a company that folded in 1994; the copyright passed through a chain of holding companies, the last of which no longer exists, and no identifiable person or entity can now claim title. A hobbyist patches and redistributes the program so it keeps running. Suppose Locke—who wrote before modern copyright law and before software existed—were given an accurate briefing on how software copyright and enforcement work today. Use his labor-mixing account of property, his proviso that one leave enough and as good for others, and his spoilage condition to work out what he would say: has the code fallen back into the common stock, and if so, when and why? Take a definite view about what the Lockean framework actually entails here. If you think it has no stable answer, say exactly where it splits, and defend the amendment that best repairs the gap. Keep the response to at most 500 words.
