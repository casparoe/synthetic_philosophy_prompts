"Grice's starting point is that what a sentence means and what a speaker means by uttering it come apart, and the gap is implicature. Take the classic case: if I say 'some of the students passed,' I implicate that not all of them passed, since I'd have said 'all' if that were true. The literal sentence doesn't say it; you reconstruct it by assuming I'm following the Maxim of Quantity. The maxims — Quality, Quantity, Relation, Manner — are basically the unspoken conventions of cooperative talk that we pick up socially, and implicatures are what you get whenever a speaker breaches or exploits one of them. The crucial upshot is that implicatures are never part of the sentence's truth conditions: they're pragmatic through and through, always cancellable, and the literal meaning is already fixed before this context-sensitive inference gets going. So semantics handles the fixed context-free part, pragmatics handles everything else, and the two never mix."
