I am an undergraduate currently taking an upper-level philosophy of mind course, and we have just finished Alan Turing’s 1950 paper “Computing Machinery and Intelligence.” In our next tutorial I have to produce one practice exam question for the group, together with a model answer and a marking scheme. I would like the question to turn on the following passage from section 2 of the paper, and to be unanswerable by merely reciting how the imitation game works:

“The question and answer method seems to be suitable for introducing almost any one of the fields of human endeavour that we wish to include. We do not wish to penalise the machine for its inability to shine in beauty competitions, nor to penalise a man for losing in a race against an aeroplane. The conditions of our game make these disabilities irrelevant.”

I want the exam question to push students to say what work the phrase “these disabilities” does in Turing’s argument: which capacities he is deliberately setting aside, why he thinks a behavioral test is strengthened rather than weakened by setting them aside, and where a critic might reply that the test has now been protected from counterexample only by changing the subject. Please write the exam question, a model answer of around 800 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain and the specific misunderstanding that should cost marks. Do not use any headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables; write in continuous prose, the way one would write an email or a letter.
