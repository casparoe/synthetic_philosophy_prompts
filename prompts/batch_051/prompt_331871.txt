Friend and I got into a long argument last night about whether "same mechanism" is a real relation or just a way of saying "same input-output behavior." His claim: two systems can answer identically on every test you can throw at them, including all the same errors, and still be using different mechanisms; and two systems can disagree on some inputs yet still share a mechanism that just got disturbed. I said that until he can tell me what observation would seperate those, "mechanism" is doing no work. We kept coming back to one case: two classifiers that both fail on the same images with watermarked backgrounds. He says the shared failures are real evidence they're using the same shortcut; I say it only shows they landed on the same decision boundary, and a dozen different insides could produce it. So: how would you actually test whether those two systems share a mechanism rather than merely compute the same function? Design the best test you can, say what result would push you each way, and be honest about what the test couldn't settle even in principle.
