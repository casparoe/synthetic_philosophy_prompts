A friend and I spent most of an evening arguing about whether a sufficiently large amount of pleasure can ever outweigh intense pain; we kept returning to four positions and could not settle on one. I left the argument unsure whether the positions themselves were well formed. I would like you to adjudicate the comparative question by rating the positions rather than discussing them at large.

Rank the following four views from most to least defensible. Assign each view a fraction of your credence, with the four fractions summing to 1, and give one or two sentences of reasoning for each placement. Answer in American English and keep the whole response to no more than 400 words.

1. Smooth commensurability: pleasure and pain are quantities on a single scale, so any finite amount of pain can in principle be outweighed by a sufficiently large finite amount of pleasure; pain is simply negative pleasure.
2. Lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: below some threshold of intensity (or intensity-times-duration), pain and pleasure trade off roughly as the first view says; at or above that threshold, reducing or preventing pain takes absolute priority over producing or preserving any amount of pleasure, and no amount of pleasure can outweigh it.
3. Global pain priority: any pain, however mild, takes priority over any amount of pleasure; minimizing or preventing pain always wins, and promoting pleasure is never a substitute for it.
4. Incommensurability: pleasure and pain are different dimensions of value that cannot be placed on a common scale; talk of compensation or trade-off ratios is a mistake, and choices across the two dimensions need some further deliberative judgment that neither dimension alone settles.
