I retired from chemical research a few years ago and have been reading philosophy in a somewhat disorganised way, mostly for my own interest and for a small local reading group. Recently I got stuck on a passage in Descartes's First Meditation where he entertains the possibility that an immensely powerful deceiver is arranging all his perceptions, so that even the most obvious things could be false. A friend told me this is basically an old version of the brain-in-a-vat scenario, but I'm not sure the two are as close as he thinks. Anyway, I tried to write out my current commitments on the subject, and I'd like someone to tell me whether they actually hang together before I present them.

Here is roughly where I've landed:
(1) I am a fallibilist: I can know ordinary empirical things, such as that I have hands, without having certainty.
(2) I also accept something like a same evidence, same epistemic standing principle: if a scenario is subjectively indistinguishable from my actual situation, and in that scenario my belief would be false, then I don't know the belief in the actual situation either.
(3) I find Putnam's semantic externalism plausible: the meaning of our words is fixed partly by the world we are actually in, so an envatted brain could not think "I am a brain in a vat" with the content I now attach to that sentence.
(4) Even so, I think the question "How do I know I am not a brain in a vat?" is a real question when I ask it, not just a pseudo-question.
(5) I accept closure: if I know that I have hands, then I can know (or at least could easily come to know) that I am not a handless brain in a vat being fed hand experiences.
(6) But I also think my evidence does not give me a way of ruling out the brain-in-a-vat scenario, if it were true.

Could you go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only appear to be? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, say what would need to give in each case, and then suggest the single cheapest revision that would clean things up the most. Please answer in British English.
