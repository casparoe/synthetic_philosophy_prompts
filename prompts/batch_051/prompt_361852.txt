Two friends of mine got into an argument I couldn't referee, and I'd like it adjudicated point by point. The background: a neighborhood in our city wants to incorporate as its own municipality. Before the incorporation vote, someone has to decide who gets to vote on whether to incorporate at all. The residents pushing incorporation drew a proposed boundary. Some people just outside the line say they should vote too, because they use the same streets, shops, and parks and will be affected by the decision.

Priya said only the people inside the proposed boundary should vote, since the question is whether this group may govern itself. Every democratic body has to draw its own line somewhere, and the mere fact that a line is disputed does not give outsiders a claim to help draw it.

Tom said that is circular. Who "the people" are for this decision is exactly what is at issue, and you cannot settle it by pointing to a line one of the parties drew. If the proposed members define the electorate, the result is fixed in advance, because the people inside the line are the ones who want to leave.

Priya answered that Tom's fix is circular as well. If you let everyone affected vote, someone still has to decide who counts as affected, and that decision has to be made by some existing body. There is no non-circular starting point, so the only workable rule is to begin from the group people already recognize.

Tom replied that this just concedes the starting point is arbitrary, and an arbitrary line should not be allowed to entrench itself. The decision is not purely internal: it would redirect tax money and change services for those left outside, so the electorate should be drawn to include those outsiders.

Priya came back that widening the circle does not remove the arbitrariness; it only moves the line out. And if outsiders can always demand a vote, boundaries become permanently contestable and no group can ever make a first decision.

I genuinely could not tell who was right about what, or whether they were partly talking past each other. Please adjudicate the exchange step by step: say what each is getting right, what both are missing, and who has the better case overall.
