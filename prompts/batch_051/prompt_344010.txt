A group in my city wants to turn the zoning appeals board into what they're calling a discourse chamber, explicitly modeled on Habermas's ideal speech situation. The mechanics: (1) everything runs as anonymized text so status cues can't leak through; (2) anyone can introduce any claim or question any claim, but every proposal must come with a reason the speaker says all affected parties could accept; (3) a filter automatically strikes sentences that appeal to credentials, wealth, tradition, or majority support; (4) moderators can time out participants for interrupting, dominating the floor, or arguing in obvious bad faith; (5) three timeouts and you lose your vote on the final recommendation. Treat this as a mechanism, not a question of whether the ideal is worth pursuing. Walk me through what it actually incentivizes, who ends up with influence, where it gets gamed first, and what the second-order effects are. I'd like a committed take: name the failure mode you think is most damning, defend why it's the one, and say whether the scheme can be patched or is better abandoned.
