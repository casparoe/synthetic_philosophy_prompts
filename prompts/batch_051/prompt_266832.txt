I'm a backend engineer at a small lending startup. Next week I'm presenting to our executive team to argue that we should drop zip code from our underwriting model, even though it's currently our single most predictive feature for default risk. My argument, roughly, is that zip code is doing a lot of the predictive work because neighborhoods are still so segregated by race and class, so the model is letting that history quietly make the decision for us; using it is wrong for the same kind of reason using race directly would be wrong, even though zip code isnt a protected category. In the arguments so far I keep hearing three different tests: a feature is okay if it's genuinely predictive, or okay if it's something a person can change, or okay as long as it's not a legally protected class. I don't think any of those is exactly right, but I can't say what the better test is. The room will be: our CTO, a former data scientist who's going to want exact numbers on how much accuracy and revenue we're giving up; our head of risk, who thinks a model that ignores real default risk is actuarially irresponsible; a product VP who suspects this is PR-driven and will say we'd just be cross-subsidizing high-risk borrowers; and our general counsel, who cares mostly about what survives a fair-lending review and will push back if I go beyond what the law requires.

I don't need a legal memo, and I don't need the speech itself. I need a Q&A prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get, who raises each, roughly the order they'll come, how much damage each one does, what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and the question I should be praying nobody asks. Please write the answer in American English.
