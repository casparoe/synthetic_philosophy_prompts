Exam question:

In Lewis Carroll's "What the Tortoise Said to Achilles," the Tortoise says the following after Achilles has tried to force him to accept Z:

"If A and B and C are true, Z must be true," the Tortoise thoughtfully repeated. "That's another Hypothetical, isn't it? And, if I failed to see its truth, I might accept A and B and C, and still not accept Z, mightn't I?"

Explain exactly what the Tortoise is doing here, and then answer: does this passage show that deductive reasoning rests on principles that cannot themselves be supplied as premises? If so, what does that tell us about the idea that logic can force someone to accept a conclusion?

Student answer:

The Tortoise is refusing to apply modus ponens. He accepts A, B, and C but won't move to Z because he insists that the move itself must be written down as another premise, D. Each time Achilles gives him the required conditional, the Tortoise treats the next inferential step as still another conditional. So the regress is generated. This shows that every deductive argument needs an extra premise that says its premises entail it's conclusion, and that premise will need the same support, so no deductive argument can ever be completed. Therefore logic cannot compel someone who accepts all the premises, because they can always ask why the conclusion follows. The only fix is to take modus ponens as a premise at the start, but that just restarts the regress one step later.
