I'm in a philosophy of perception course, and I've been trying to reconstruct the argument from illusion in a way clean enough to see exactly where disjunctivism enters. Here is the version I have. I want a diagnosis of it: is the argument valid, and if not, which step fails? If it is valid, which premise should a disjunctivist reject, and what does rejecting it commit them to?

(1) Let V be a veridical visual experience as of a bent stick, and let H be a matching hallucination as of a bent stick. By hypothesis, V and H are indistinguishable from the inside: no reflection on the experience alone would tell the subject which kind of experience she is having.
(2) If two experiences are indistinguishable from the inside, then they are the same fundamental kind of mental state.
(3) So V and H are the same fundamental kind of mental state.
(4) In H, the subject is directly aware of something; and since no mind-independent bent stick corresponds to the hallucination, what she is directly aware of is a mental particular — a sense-datum or appearance.
(5) Therefore, in V as well, the subject is directly aware of a mental particular, not the bent stick itself.
(6) Therefore, ordinary veridical perception never puts us in direct awareness of mind-independent objects; direct realism is false.

My instinct is that the move from (3) and (4) to (5) is suspicious: two states' being of the same fundamental kind doesn't by itself force their objects to be of the same kind. But I also don't see how to repair the argument without adding exactly the bridge principle disjunctivists reject, and I can't tell whether the weight then falls on that bridge principle or instead on (2). I'm also unsure whether (4) is independently problematic. Is the argument valid as stated? If it isn't, identify the first step at which the inference fails, and say whether the argument can be repaired by adding a further premise — and if so, whether a disjunctivist can still reject the repaired argument. If it is valid as stated, say which premises are least stable and what rejecting each would amount to.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning; no preamble, and don't restate the question. After your verdict, tell me where your diagnosis is least secure: which alternative reading of 'indistinguishable from the inside' or 'same fundamental kind' would most plausibly change your mind about where the argument fails.
