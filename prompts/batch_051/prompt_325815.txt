I am a PhD student in cognitive neuroscience, and I am repeatedly confronted with a claim I do not know how to evaluate. On this view, attention is identical to precision-weighted prediction-error minimization: the brain assigns different gains to different prediction-error signals so as to minimize free energy, and this is meant to explain selective report, endogenous orienting, and distractor suppression, not merely to describe them. The difficulty is that any attentional behaviour can be redescribed, after the fact, as the brain having assigned high precision to one source and low precision to another. Design the strongest experiment you can that would distinguish the causal claim that precision weighting is what attention is and does from the weaker claim that precision weighting merely summarizes attentional effects. Specify the result that would favour each reading, how an opponent could reinterpret either result, and what the test could not settle even in principle — and whether that residue is an empirical limitation or a defect in the claim's content.
