For an ethics unit on what the present generation owes to future people, I'm drafting a position paper and have found that I accept several claims that my teacher says are in tension, though I can't see exactly where. I want to submit the most defensible version of my view, so before I decide what to revise I'd like an audit of the set as it stands. I hold all of the following:

(1) An action is permissible only if it does not wrong any particular person.
(2) To wrong a person is to harm that person, and to harm a person is to make that person worse off than they would otherwise have been.
(3) Which particular people will exist in the future depends on what we do now: under a low-emissions path, different people would meet, different children would be born, and so most of the individuals alive a century from now would not have existed had we taken the other path.
(4) The individuals alive a century from now under a high-emissions path would mostly still have lives worth living.
(5) Even so, continuing on a high-emissions path would wrong the people who end up living in that future.
(6) These choices should not be evaluated simply by totaling well-being across possible populations; which particular people come to exist matters in itself.

Please audit these six claims by giving exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Order the items from most to least severe, and have each say what conflicts with what and what would have to give in the least costly revision.
