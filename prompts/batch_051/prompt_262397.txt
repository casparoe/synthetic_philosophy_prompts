I chair my university's gift acceptance and naming committee. Before us is a $40 million naming gift from an alumnus who built his fortune as founder of a chain of payday-lending storefronts. The business is legal; its defenders note that it extends short-term credit to low-income borrowers whom mainstream banks often decline, while its critics note that state regulators have repeatedly sanctioned the chain for practices that keep borrowers in rolling, high-fee debt. The gift would endow a new center for household financial security, and no other plausible funding is available. Last night a friend and I argued about the underlying question—whether such a fortune is a vice to be refused, an engine of provision to be redirected, or something in between—and both of us left less certain than we began. I must submit a decision memo to the committee tomorrow. Draft the memo: accept the gift, reject it, or accept it with specified conditions; give a clear bottom line; and take seriously the strongest argument on the losing side.
