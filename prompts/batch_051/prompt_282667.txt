I'm the best man at my brother's wedding this fall. He's a philosophy PhD student working on consciousness, and his fiancée does computational neuroscience, so a good chunk of the guests will be grad students and postdocs; the rest are cousins, grandparents, and our very patient aunt who starts to worry whenever someone says state space. I want a five-minute toast built around a real worry instead of the usual you'll-never-get-bored stuff: if our minds have only finitely many discriminable states, then on a long enough timeline the two of them might eventually run out of genuinely new moments — every inside joke recycled, every fight a rerun. The speech should actually sit with that thought for a bit, without the cheap dodge of saying love is infinite, because the academics will be checking whether I'm dodging the logic. Maybe draw the line between rearranging old parts into new combinations and something feeling new from the inside, and then turn it around so it lands as affection for the couple rather then a grim little seminar. Keep it funny enough that my aunt doesn't confiscate the mic.
