I recently joined my town's landmarks commission, and we must decide the fate of a shuttered textile mill that anchored the town for generations. Some members want it preserved exactly as it stands, others want it cleared for new housing, and still others want only a plaque honoring its founder. I have been reading the old records obsessively to make the right decision, but the more I learn, the harder choosing becomes, and I have begun to suspect that my deepening knowledge of the town's past is itself making action difficult. Could you help me think through how honoring the past should relate to our ability to act well in the present?
