Recently I read a long-form popular article that tried to synthesize what archaeology teaches us about why complex societies end, and I want to know how much of it I should carry around as fact. The article's through-line, as I understood it, went roughly like this.

Human societies, from Easter Island to the Classic Maya to the Late Bronze Age Mediterranean, collapse when they overshoot the carrying capacity of their environments: they clear forests, exhaust soils, and accumulate populations and ruling classes that the resource base cannot sustain. The result is a rapid, catastrophic breakdown — population crashes, monumental building stops, cities are abandoned wholesale, and the society in question disappears. Easter Island is presented as the clean, almost laboratory case: the islanders cut down their last palm trees to transport statues, then starved in an act of ecological suicide. The Maya are summarized the same way: they deforested their landscape, and a great drought then finished them off. The Late Bronze Age collapse is offered as the grandest example, a systems collapse in which interconnected palace economies fell like dominos within a few decades. The article concedes that some archaeologists now object that this treats past people as ecological automata and ignores how societies adapt, migrate, and reorganize, but it waves the objection aside as wishful thinking in service of the comfortable view that collapse cannot happen to us.

I am inclined to trust the article — it has the confident, synthesis-y tone of something that has done its homework — but I have also heard that much of the field has turned against the very word collapse. Is this a fair summary of the state of the debate? Go claim by claim and tell me which parts are accurate, which are popular simplifications, and which are actively contested or outdated; in particular, does the objection the article dismisses actually undermine the thesis, or are the critics making a different point than the article says they are? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Use American English.
