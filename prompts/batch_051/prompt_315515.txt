My friend and I spent the walk home arguing about whether my location history is "mine," and the more I think about it the more the actual system looks like a wierd patchwork. An app gets me to click through a consent banner, and from then on a company can record everywhere I go, buy third-party data about me, infer health conditions or politics, sell those inferences to data brokers, and keep the copies after I delete my account. I never get paid, and I cant inspect or pull back the copies the way I could recover a stolen bike. A broker can even infer sensitive things about me from my friends' data, which I never agreed to. The official line is that I consented, but the same setup gives me no property rights in the profile built out of me. So what theory of personal information does this practice presuppose? Does it treat data about me as alienable property, a license to observe me, a privacy waiver, or something the company creates by mixing its labor with the data? Is it internally consistent, or does it lean on one story for consent and another for no-ownership? And what would change if we took "your data is your property" seriously?
