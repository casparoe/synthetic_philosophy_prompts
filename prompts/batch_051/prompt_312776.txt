I've been thinking about this on my own for a few months and I've boxed myself into a corner. It started with a thought experiment: imagine floating in the dark with my body and senses completely shut off — no sight, no sound, no touch, no memories, not even the feeling of having a body — but there's still a bare awareness that I am. It seems obvious I'd still know I exist, maybe more plainly than ever. That part feels fine.

But here's the argument I built on it:

(1) I can imagine myself in that state: body shut off, no senses, no memories, no awareness of having a body — just direct awareness that I exist.
(2) If I can be directly aware of my self without being aware of some feature, then that feature is not what my self is; it's just something I have.
(3) So my body, my senses, and my memories are not what I am. The real me is just the thing that is aware.

Then the same reasoning wouldnt stop:

(4) Pick any particular thought, feeling, or trait I have — I can be aware of my self without being aware of that particular one, so by (2) it isn't what I am either.
(5) Since each particular feature can be removed one at a time while my direct self-awareness stays, I can remove all of them at once and still have a self aware of itself — but now it's a self with no features at all.
(6) But a self with no features is indistinguishable from nothing. There'd be nothing left to be aware, or to exist.
(7) So the same reasoning that showed I'm not my body also seems to show there's no me at all. That's absurd.

I want to keep (2) and (3), because it genuinely feels like the thing that has my experiences is not the same as the experiences themselves. But I cant see which step I'm allowed to reject, or what rejecting it costs. Which step goes wrong, and what do I give up by rejecting it?
