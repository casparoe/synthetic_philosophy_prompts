Say Ali has cyclic preferences: he prefers a scarf to his hat, gloves to the scarf, and the hat to the gloves. A broker runs him through the three swaps back to back, charging a dollar each time; Ali ends up with his original hat and is three dollars poorer. Everyone agrees Ali's preferences are irrational, and the money pump is the proof.

Say Berta has perfectly transitive preferences at any given moment, but they drift on a predictable yearly cycle. In January she prefers the scarf to the hat, so she swaps for a dollar. In May she prefers the gloves to the scarf, so she swaps for a dollar. In September she prefers the hat to the gloves, so she swaps for a third dollar. She's back to the hat she had in January, three dollars poorer, and the same broker knew the whole cycle in advance.

Most people, im included, are way more reluctant to call Berta irrational than Ali. We want to say Ali has a defect in his preferences while Berta is just changing her mind, or being seasonal, or whatever. Is there a principled difference between the two cases that justifies this asymmetry — for instance, a reason why intransitivity at a single time is a rationality flaw while predictable preference change over time is not, even when it's just as exploitable? If there is, spell it out and test it on a case where Berta's drift carries no new information and isn't something she'd endorse from any stable perspective. If there isn't, tell me which verdict I should flip. And whichever way you go, say what would change your mind.
