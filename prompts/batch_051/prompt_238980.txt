So my kid came home from a math circle saying 2 is an element of 4. I thought he'd gotten mixed up with subsets, but he wrote it out for me: with von Neumann ordinals, 2 = {0,1} and 4 = {0,1,2,3}, so yep, 2 ∈ 4. I've been turning this over for days now. In ordinary arithmetic, numbers dont have elements; "is 2 inside 4?" isnt even the kind of question you can ask. So how should I parse the set theory claim? Is it a truth about the numbers 2 and 4 themselves, or just a truth about one particular way of coding them? And if its just an artifact of the coding, then why do people state it like a theorem instead of a convention? I've heard structuralism is supposed to dissolve this whole thing, but I dont see how saying "only structure matters" settles whether 2 ∈ 4 is something I should believe.
