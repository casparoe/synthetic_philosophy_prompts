I keep getting sucked into the same argument about whether there's such a thing as the scientific method, and the weird part is that the shape of the fight hasn't changed in decades and neither side ever gives an inch. The pro-method side says: sure, the history is messy, but there's still a recognizable core — you form hypotheses, derive predictions, try to falsify them, prefer the ones that survive, demand controls, replication, and some kind of calibration against error. It's not one rigid algorithm, but it's not nothing. The anti-method side (Feyerabend, with a certain reading of Kuhn in the background) says the history of science is a graveyard of proposed rules: for basically every methodological principle you can name, some decisive bit of good science violated it — fudged the data, ignored anomalies, leaned on rhetoric or taste or a charismatic lab head. So there's no fixed method; science advances by whatever works.

I'm not asking you to settle who's right. The strange thing is that both sides already know the other side's canonical cases — Galileo, Newton, Einstein, the quantum revolution, all the usual exhibits — and they just keep re-describing them. So I want a diagnosis of why the debate has stalled. Is 'method' doing two different jobs here (a decision procedure you can state in advance versus a loose set of community-level virtues)? Is the descripive/normative distinction doing all the work — one side talking about how science actually proceeds, the other about how it would have to proceed to be trustworthy? Or is it a genuine disagreement about whether the historical record gets to veto an epistemological theory?

My hunch is that the defenders are mostly talking about mature, local, puzzle-solving science and the critics are mostly talking about discovery and revolutionary episodes, so the two camps are characterising different phases of the same process and both calling the whole thing 'science.' But I don't trust that hunch, since both camps would probably reject the redescription, and anyway it might just be a way of not noticing the real conflict. So: diagnose the disagreement, and tell me what would count as progress given that diagnosis — a distinction, a formal result, a better-framed question, or an argument that the whole debate is ill-posed.
