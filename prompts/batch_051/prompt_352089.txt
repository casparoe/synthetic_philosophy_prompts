ok so im a novelist, currently working on a novel about a couple going through preimplantation genetic testing, and I keep getting tangled in the bioethics of whether they're allowed—or maybe even obligated—to pick the embryo with the best expected life. I rewatched Gattaca last week and realized I might be conflating two positions. On one side, procreative beneficence: roughly, that if parents are already selecting among possible future children, they have a moral reason, other things equal (maybe a strong one, maybe even an obligation), to choose the child whose life can be expected to go best. On the other side, liberal eugenics: genetic enhancement and selection can be permissible, even good, provided the state stays neutral, parents choose according to their own plural values, and no single vision of the good life gets imposed. Gattaca pretty clearly goes after the second—a society-wide, genetically sorted, credentialist dystopia—but I want to know whether its nightmare also counts against the first view, which is a claim about parental selection rather than state coercion.

Could you put on your bioethicist / philosopher-of-medicine hat and give me a careful reading of Gattaca through those two lenses? Where does the film actually land a blow against procreative beneficence, and where is it only attacking the illiberal implementation that procreative beneficence doesnt require? Start by brainstorming a few different candidate readings, then settle on the one you think is strongest and think carefully about the details. In particular, I want you to press on a few things: whether the film trades on genetic determinism in a way the procreative beneficence view isn't committed to; whether the harms it shows to people classified as genetically unfit are harms to existing people, which embryo selection never directly causes; and whether a society could practice procreative beneficence without Gattaca's hierarchy and contempt following. I don't need a reading that flatters the film or the philosophy—it's fine to conclude the film misfires, or that it exposes something the philosophy misses, or both. What does the film add to the philosophical debate that argument alone tends to leave out?
