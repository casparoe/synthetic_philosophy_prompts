I teach a Sunday school class and keep running up against the old problem about petitionary prayer: if God already knows exactly what God is going to do and cannot be changed by anything, then asking for things looks either redundant or like trying to nag an unchangeable plan. I'd rather work out what I actually believe by going case by case, because my gut seems to have a view that my head hasn't caught up with yet. Praying for a friend's cancer surgery on Tuesday: clearly fine, maybe the clearest case I have. Praying for a parking spot before a job interview: feels a little gross, like turning God into a valet, though I catch myself doing it anyway. Praying that my estranged brother's heart will soften so we can reconcile: feels right and urgent, even though its about another person's will. Ending a long list of specific requests by handing it all back over to God's will feels necessary, like the one part I can't omit. Once I found myself praying that a cruel text I had sent would somehow not have hurt the person, even though I knew it already had; that felt incoherent, almost like a category mistake, but I couldn't stop. Praying to win the lottery or for a coworker I envied to fail: feels like corruption, a misuse of the whole practice. When I was depressed and asked my small group to pray for me, it felt like their prayers added something beyond just me knowing they cared. Praying for more faith, or for help with my unbelief: that's the paradigm, the one I'm most sure about. And praying for good weather for the church picnic feels silly and a little embarrassing but not exactly wrong.

My tentative rule is that petitionary prayer is really about reshaping the person who prays rather than moving God. I can see how that covers the paradigm case and maybe the handing-it-over case, but it doesn't obviously cover the intercessory ones, where I feel like my asking is doing something for someone else, or the small group case. Is there one coherent account of petitionary prayer—consistent with God being omniscient and immutable—that gets all or most of these verdicts, and which of them does it force me to give up? And what would the account say about the case I keep going back and forth on: praying over a big life decision like whether to take a job, where I both want God's guidance and don't want to outsource the choice. Please write your answer in British English.
