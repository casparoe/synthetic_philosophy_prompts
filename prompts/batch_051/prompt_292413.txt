For the past two months I have been assigned to a safety-review team at my company, and my job is to say how much weight we should put on interpretability reports before deciding whether to deploy a model in a high-stakes setting. I am not a philosopher; I trained as an engineer, and I keep running into the same pattern. The interpretability researchers hand us claims of the form: the model has a feature for deception, this attention head attends to syntactic agreement, a probe decodes the concept of danger from layer 18. The workflow behind these claims is standard, and I have watched it happen. They train a linear classifier (a probe) on the network's hidden activations to predict some externally labeled property such as truthfulness or sentiment; if the probe does well, they report that the network encodes or represents that property. They remove or zero out a head, a neuron, or a learned direction and observe how the model's behavior changes; if a task degrades, they call that component causally responsible for the behavior. They inspect the inputs that most activate a component and name it after the common element they see. I do not doubt that these techniques are useful for prediction and for making targeted edits. But I want to know what view of representation this practice presupposes. Does running this workflow commit someone to realism about the model's internal states — the view that the network literally has representations with those contents — or can the whole thing be read instrumentally, as compressed summaries and editing handles that carry no commitment to whether the model has those contents in any deeper sense? Does the probing step presuppose something different from the ablation step, or from the naming-by-inspection step? Reconstruct the implicit theory of representation the practice embodies, distinguish what it strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and say what, if anything, would have to change in how the work is done and reported if the presupposed view were replaced by a deflationary one.
