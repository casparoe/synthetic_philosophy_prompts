As a journalist covering science and ideas, I keep returning to the great-filter argument and suspect it is less a single hypothesis than a family of claims about different possible bottlenecks. The usual claim is that somewhere between prebiotic chemistry and a galaxy-spanning civilization there is at least one transition with an extraordinarily low probability, and that this hidden step explains why the sky looks empty. I want to know whether that claim, especially the question of where the filter sits, is testable at all given that we have exactly one data point for some transitions and none for others. Design the best empirical program you can for locating the filter. Be concrete: say which observations would bear on which step — exoplanet demographics for the abundance of candidate worlds, evidence for a second origin of life within our own solar system for the abiogenesis step, laboratory work on prebiotic chemistry, and the contrast between biosignature and technosignature searches for the intelligence and longevity steps — and say what pattern of results would push the filter toward one step rather than another. Then be equally concrete about what a positive or negative result would not settle: which parts of the argument remain invisible to observation even in principle, and why. Please answer in American English.
