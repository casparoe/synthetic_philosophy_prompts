Most people in WEIRD societies — Western, educated, industrialized, rich, and democratic — hold two moral judgments that sit uneasily together. An official who uses her position to get her unqualified nephew hired is condemned as corrupt and thought to deserve punishment; a parent who spends her savings on private tutoring so that her mediocre child wins a place at a selective school is admired as devoted, or at most mildly criticized. Both cases convert an advantage into an unearned benefit for a family member; what differs is only whether the channel runs through a public office or a private market. Many non-WEIRD communities find this public/private line far less morally significant: they treat family obligation as natural in both settings, or treat both as favoritism. Is there a principle that justifies the WEIRD asymmetry, or is it merely a local folk morality that mistakes its own habits for the moral truth? Give the strongest justification you can find, test it against nearby cases, and say honestly whether the asymmetry should be kept, revised, or abandoned.
