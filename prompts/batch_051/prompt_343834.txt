I am a working Bayesian statistician, and I think best in terms of priors, likelihoods, posteriors, model comparison, and observation selection effects. Please translate the main lines of argument in the Fermi paradox into that vocabulary. Treat three things as candidate models of our observations: abundance reasoning in the style of the Drake equation, the Great Filter hypothesis, and the bare inference that any sufficiently advanced civilization would already have arrived here. For each, specify what belongs in the prior, what belongs in the likelihood, what is being conditioned on, and how the fact that we observe from a life-bearing planet enters the analysis, if at all. Then give a residue report: which moves in the informal debate translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression in this vocabulary. In particular, assess whether the Great Filter is best understood as a claim about parameters, about priors, or about evidence, and how the observation of silence updates each model. End with your judgment: does the residue expose equivocation in the informal debate, or does it expose a limitation of Bayesian machinery for this problem? Keep the translation honest; do not attribute to the informal arguments assumptions that only the Bayesian framework introduces.
