I've been going back and forth about modal metaphysics, and I keep noticing that arguments between the two main pictures end with each side accusing the other of failing a requirement it never stated up front. So before I read any further, I want the desiderata made explicit. The two contenders, as I understand them: counterpart theory (an object exists in only one world, and a de re modal claim about it is really a claim about its counterparts — other objects in other worlds that resemble it in the right respects) and the transworld identity picture (one and the same object exists in many possible worlds and has properties there directly). Don't give me a bibliography or a history; give me the specification of what any adequate theory of how an individual occupies possibility-space has to do, each requirement tied to a concrete motivating case rather than a slogan, plus a verdict on which requirements are negotiable and which are not.

To force the list to do real work, it should at least take positions on the following and may add more: the de re / de dicto distinction and which cases force one reading rather than the other; essential versus accidental properties and whether a theory may leave either notion unexplained; the necessity of identity and distinctness; tolerance chains, where a sequence of possible individuals each differs only slightly from the last and the final member is wildly different from the first, and what that does to any transitivity assumption about being-the-same-as-across-worlds; a symmetrical world containing two qualitatively indistinguishable spheres and whether it is legitimate to ask which actual sphere is which possible one; claims to the effect that some actual thing could have been of a radically different kind (say, a given human's having been something inanimate), including whether such claims are meaningful and what would make them true; and how the view treats individuals that fail to exist in some worlds without leaving de re claims about them truth-valueless. Flag any subsets of the desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied — if the list itself is an impossibility result, I want to know that rather than silently rigging it for one side.

Finally, score counterpart theory and the transworld identity picture against the list you defended, but keep that scoring to at most a quarter of the response; the weight belongs on the criteria and their cases, not on the standings. Hard cap: 1,200 words.
