im strongly pulled toward the everettian line: treat the schrodinger equation as applying to everything all the time, and measurement stops being special — it's just decoherence entangling the apparatus and environment, after which the observer-in-a-branch sees one definite result. that strikes me as the only story with no ad hoc collapse or extra variables. but i also can't shake the intuition that when i measure the spin and see up, there's then a single fact about the electron in my lab — it's up — and talk of "another branch where i saw down" doesn't seem to reach that fact. i dont want a taxonomy of interpretations. i want a negotiated verdict: is the single-outcome intuition a classical hangover that should be debunked, is everett quietly positing a lot of real worlds it hasn't earned, or is there a reading of "the world" / "i" / "fact" on which both survive? say what would justify each move, not just which side wins. and please flag what's settled (unitary dynamics, the decoherence formalism) versus genuinely contested (branch realism, the probability problem) instead of presenting one interpretation as the consensus.
