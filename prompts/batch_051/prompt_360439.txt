She has set a short practice question on Harsanyi's aggregation theorem, and I would like an independent, expert second opinion on the answer before I return a mark and comments. The question on the sheet reads:

"Does Harsanyi's aggregation theorem establish utilitarianism? Set out the theorem's assumptions and conclusion, and assess what, if anything, the theorem shows about the normative standing of utilitarianism."

The student's answer reads:

"Harsanyi's aggregation theorem shows that any rational social evaluator must be a utilitarian. Each individual's preferences over lotteries obey the von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms, so each can be assigned a cardinal utility function, unique up to positive affine transformation. The evaluator's own preferences over lotteries also obey those axioms, so they can be represented by a social utility function. If we add the Pareto condition — that the evaluator is indifferent between two options whenever every individual is indifferent between them — then the social utility function must be a weighted sum of the individual utility functions. A weighted sum of individual utilities is precisely a utilitarian social welfare function, so the theorem converts utilitarianism from a controversial moral doctrine into an axiomatically forced one. The only serious way to resist the conclusion is to reject the Pareto condition, but that condition is very modest, since it merely requires the evaluator to respect unanimous indifference."
