Ok so I've been going back and forth on the careenium/simmballs stuff and I want a consistency check before I present this in a seminar. The setup, as I understand it: there's a simulated world full of tiny bouncing balls (the simmballs), and at the micro level it's all just physics, but sometimes their patterns implement a whole mind with symbols, beliefs, that kind of thing. The thing nagging me is whether the high-level symbols do any causal pushing of the balls, or whether all the pushing is bottom-up. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) The base-level physics of the simmballs is causally closed: given the full microstate and the laws, the next microstate is fixed, with no extra causal force coming from the symbol level.
(2) But I also think the high-level symbols are real patterns, not just useful fiction. If there's a mind in there that forms the belief "I'm thirsty," that belief is a real thing in the system, not an illusion.
(3) I'd say that belief caused the agent to reach for the water in the ordinary sense of "caused" — not just as an "as if" story.
(4) I also don't think the simmballs need to be "pushed around" by the symbols; the causal work happens entirely at the ball level. Saying the symbol pushes the balls is a category confusion.
(5) I'm convinced that if the simmballs ran a full simulation of a human brain, it would be geniunely thirsty, not just simulating thirst — but only if its high-level intentional states are causally relevant to what it does.
(6) And I think qualia in this picture are epiphenomenal, but intentional states like beliefs and desires aren't. That distinction feels right to me, though I'm not sure I can defend it.

Is this actually a consistent package? I can feel something pulling against something else but I can't pin down which one is the problem. Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, and tell me which pairs are only apparent tensions that dissolve once you keep the levels straight. And if something has to go, I want to know what it costs.
