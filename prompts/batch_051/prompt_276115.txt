Write an essay on the question whether dreams are experiences. Frame the controversy carefully: one side claims that dreaming is not something that happens to a subject during sleep, but only the waking act of telling or recalling a story, so that talk of a dream as a felt event with a real duration is a confused projection from waking testimony. The other side claims that dreams are genuine experiences that occur during sleep, with qualitative character and temporal structure, and that the fact that an outsider cannot check them while they occur no more disqualifies them than it disqualifies pains. Weigh the strongest version of each side rather than caricatures, and use concrete puzzles to test the positions, such as a dream that incorporates a real stimulus (an alarm becoming a bell within the dream) or a lucid dream in which the dreamer seems to deliberate. Also address how far empirical facts could settle the dispute: would a record of organized perceptual and motor activity in the sleeping brain decide it, or would the philosophical question survive any such finding? Reach a definite conclusion, even if it is a qualified one such as that the question has a determinate answer only under some interpretations, and justify it. Write in continuous prose only, as you would in a letter or a journal essay, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
