I somehow ended up running a reading group this term on regress arguments, and it's started to feel like we're stuck in a way that says something about the debate itself rather than about us. We keep going over two stock regresses: the justification regress, where every belief needs another belief to justify it and the chain never bottoms out, and the truth regress, where if p is true then it's true that p is true, and it's true that it's true that p is true, and so on forever. One camp says the justification regress is vicious because the justification is never completed — you can't cash out something infinite. The other camp says that's just what an infinite series is, and the truth regress shows a regress can go on forever without anything going wrong, so 'never completes' can't be the whole story. Then someone proposes that the real difference is in what the earlier steps do for the later ones: in the truth regress each later claim is trivially guaranteed by what was already there, whereas in the justification regress each belief is supposed to receive positive support from the link below it and an infinite chain might never actually deliver that support to the top. And then we're back to square one.

I've started to suspect the debate about when a regress is vicious is stalled because the word is doing several jobs at once. Sometimes 'vicious' seems to mean incomplete — the original item is never fully spelled out. Sometimes it seems to mean circular — each step just re-poses the original problem. Sometimes it means nontransmitting — a status that was supposed to flow up from below never actually arrives. I've seen the same slide in a metaphysics discussion about whether the regress where a relation needs another relation to glue it to its terms is fatal or harmless: one side means 'the original fact is never fully specified', the other means 'each stage is perfectly fine and the iteration is just redundant.' So I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself rather than a verdict on whether the regress is vicious: is this a verbal dispute about the word 'vicious', a substantive dispute about what a justification or explanation is supposed to do — terminate, transmit, ground, or just be extendable — or are the two sides answering seperate questions under one heading? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a sharper taxonomy, a formal criterion, or just noticing that different questions got bundled under one label?
