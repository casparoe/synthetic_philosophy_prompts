I run a small philosophy seminar where we interview people about a view they've actually defended, and next week's guest is a philosopher who defends the moral-trade model of compromise: the thought that when different value systems conflict, we can get real moral progress through trades where each side sacrifices something it cares about less for something it cares about more, so each side sees itself as better off by its own lights, without any shared ranking of values. Draft eight to ten interview questions. At least one question each should press on: whether you can even talk about gains from trade without a common measure of value across the two systems, how the model differs from logrolling or buying off your conscience, what it says about deontic constraints that the holders refuse to treat as tradable, and whether iterated trades ratchet the baseline so that a series of individually Paretian compromises amounts to slow value drift. For each, include a follow-up keyed to the answer the speaker is most likely to give.
