I'm putting together a handout for my intro to ethics course, and I want it to be a "which position do you actually hold?" quiz rather than another survey of -isms. The topic is the old moral saints debate: whether the morally best possible life — someone who always does the right thing, gives away everything beyond bare necessity, and has no overriding ambition except being good — is a life we should admire, or whether there's something a little stunted about a person like that. My students all nod along when I describe the saint, and then they split four ways as soon as I press them on cases, so I'd like the quiz to do the pressing.

Could you write me a ten-question quiz, two or three answer options each, where every question is a concrete case. Give me cases like: a donor so aggressive that a close friendship collapses under it; a person who has never done anything wrong but only because they have never been tempted or tested; someone whose acts are faultless but whose motives are just compulsive rule-following with no real concern for others; someone maximally beneficent but with no humor, no aesthetic sense, no idle play; a person who is privately resentful the whole time; a parent devoted to a global cause at obvious cost to their own kids; and whether you'd want your own child to grow up like any of these. The questions should be answerable by someone who knows no philosophical labels, and none of them should just ask "do you think we should be moral saints." Then give a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto, say, four positions: the sainthood-is-the-ideal view, the one-ideal-among-many view, the positively-unattractive view, and a deflationary view that the whole category is too artificial to praise or blame. Tell me which pairs of positions the quiz cannot cleanly seperate and which questions are doing the real sorting work. The angle I care about is whether the debate is really about what a person does, what a life is for, or how the person feels about the sacrifice, so build the key with that in mind.
