Back when people were still optimistic about online communities, the standard argument for karma/reputation systems was that they solve the trust problem: if your history of contributions is visible, posting junk or scamming people now has a long-term cost, so a one-shot prisoner's dilemma turns into a repeated game and cooperation becomes the rational move. I spend my days working with LLM agents, and its pretty obvious you can spin up a hundred accounts that post plausibly helpful answers for near zero cost, farm karma, and then cash it in. Does the old argument survive that, or did it depend on reputation being expensive to fake? If it survives, say exactly why cheap automated contributions don't break it. If it doesn't, say which premise died and what the remaining problem should be called.
