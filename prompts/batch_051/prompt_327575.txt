I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am working on an op-ed about Nozick's utility monster. I don't want the usual treatment in which it is either presented as an instant refutation of utilitarianism or dismissed as an eccentric fantasy. Write me the argumentative core of the essay. Begin by setting out the objection in its strongest form: not merely a creature that enjoys resources more than we do, but one whose utility is stipulated to be free of the usual empirical constraints, especially diminishing marginal utility. Then take seriously the standard utilitarian reply that actual sentient creatures display diminishing marginal utility, and explain why that reply may evade the thought experiment rather than answer it. State clearly what a committed utilitarian should say if a utility monster really can be stipulated into existence — including the possibility that the utilitarian should simply embrace the conclusion — and assess whether this response is a principled defense or a retreat into intuition. Consider whether the deepest damage the monster does is to utilitarian aggregation as such or to the failure to respect the separateness of persons. End by defending a definite verdict: does the utility monster refute utilitarianism, expose a repairable weakness, or fail as an objection? Do not end on a non-committal note.
