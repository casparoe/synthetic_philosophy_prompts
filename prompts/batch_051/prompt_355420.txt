In my free will seminar last week a grad student dismissed the whole idea that quantum indeterminacy could create room for free will with the line "chance isn't choice." It landed, but I've been turning it over since and I'm not sure what principle was actually doing the work—and I doubt he was, either. I'm putting together a handout for my determinism unit and I want a menu of precisifications of that slogan: what "chance" and "choice" could mean, e.g. chancy events aren't actions, adding an objective randomizer to a deliberation doesn't add control or authorship, mere alternative possibilities without an agent determining the outcome fall short of freedom, that sort of thing. For each reading, say wether it's trivial, defensible, or too strong. Then say which of these readings would actually undercut the libertarian who thinks quantum-level indeterminism is where free will gets its foot in the door, and which leave that position standing.
