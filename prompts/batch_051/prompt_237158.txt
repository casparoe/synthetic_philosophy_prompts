My university's examination ordinance states: "No candidate shall permit any other person to draft, compose, or correct any part of the work submitted for assessment." I am a computer science student. Rather than use a pretrained large language model, I followed Alan Turing's proposal in *Computing Machinery and Intelligence*: I built a small learning machine with no initial linguistic ability and trained—or, as Turing would say, educated—it over several months through conversation, worked examples, and corrective feedback, until it produced a polished 6,000-word chapter on Turing's own views about learning machines. I edited it only lightly. The ordinance predates such machines and was written with human essay mills and ghostwriters in mind. Am I in breach? I want less the practical answer than an account of what the text, the purpose of the clause, what the drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable reader would understand each require, and which approach ought to govern when they diverge.
