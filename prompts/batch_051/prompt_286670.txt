I'm a software engineer at a mid-sized company, in charge of defining how we supervise an internal LLM-based coding agent that reviews and refactors a large Python codebase. The team is split. One camp argues that we should score the agent only on outcomes: whether its final diff passes CI and the test suite, or whether the refactor matches the change a senior engineer actually merged. Another camp argues that outcomes alone are too coarse and too easy to game, and that we should also score the intermediate reasoning—whether the agent states the invariants it is preserving, whether it explains why a change is safe before making it, whether it plans its edits in a defensible order. I have no formal philosophy background, and I can tell the two camps are smuggling in very different assumptions about what a training signal can tell us, but I can't articulate the space of positions clearly.

Could you write a decision tree—in the spirit of a "which supervision position do you actually hold?" instrument—that takes a reader from concrete commitments about their own setting to a named position on process-based versus outcome-based supervision? Each branch node should be a question a working engineer could answer without already knowing the taxonomy: for example, whether the task has a cheap and reliable final score, whether an external check can be automated, whether good intermediate steps can be distinguished from bad ones by a human or by another model, how much the deployment distribution may differ from the training distribution, and how strongly we worry that whatever signal we reward can be gamed. Build the nodes around concrete cases my team actually faces—a refactor guarded by an existing test suite, changes that touch critical invariants with sparse tests, a greenfield feature where no reliable final score exists, and a setup where a stronger model can critique a weaker model's plan—rather than around the abstract labels. Keep it usable in practice, so aim for roughly eight to ten decision nodes.

Please build the tree from the logical space of those questions first rather than from a list of labels, and include, as leaves, at least: pure outcome-based training on a scalar reward, outcome-based training with a hard programmatic verifier, process supervision from step-level human labels, process supervision from a learned process reward model, and a debate-style setup where one model critiques another instead of a static signal being collected. Each leaf should carry the name of the position and one sentence stating its most awkward commitment—the assumption or cost it is easiest to ignore. Where two positions fall into the same combination of answers, flag that the tree cannot separate them and say why; where a combination is logically possible but no one actually defends it, note that too, with a brief diagnosis of whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. I would rather the instrument sort people by what they would do or accept in a concrete case than by which label they like, so no node should simply ask whether someone favors process or outcome supervision.
