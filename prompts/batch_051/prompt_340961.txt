I hold roughly a 0.75 credence in the following proposition; a colleague of mine puts it at 0.15: a homeowner who discovers a paper-wasp nest under the eave of her porch and must resolve the situation within the week has a pro tanto moral obligation to arrange live removal rather than spray an insecticide, where live removal is equally effective at keeping the porch usable, costs about twice as much, and takes an extra hour. Give me your own credence in that proposition as a number — not a hedge about how it depends on one's theory — but derive it by decomposing the claim into the three or four sub-questions that carry the weight. I would expect at least: the probability that wasps have valenced experience; the probability that the interests of short-lived invertebrates aggregate into a claim strong enough to defeat ordinary convenience; and the probability that the obligation survives the homeowner's not having created the conflict, the appeal to naturalness, and our uncertainty about insect sentience itself. Assign each sub-question a number with one line of justification, mark which term you are least confident in, show how you combine them, and identify which term the final figure is most sensitive to and what evidence would shift it most. Finally, say where my 0.75 and my colleague's 0.15 most likely diverge.
