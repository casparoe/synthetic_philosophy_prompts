I keep running into a tension between two things that both feel right to me, and I'd like an expert in the philosophy of perception and cognitive science to help me work through it carefully. I'm not coming at this from an academic background; it's just something I've been turning over on my own.

On one side, there is a picture of perception I find very attractive for its tidiness. On this view, the early sensory part of perception is an informationally sealed system: what you believe, want, or expect cannot reach in and reshape the experience itself. That is exactly why the Müller-Lyer lines keep looking unequal even after you have measured them and know they are the same length, and why seeing can act as an honest referee that sometimes corrects your beliefs instead of just mirroring them. The theory appeals to me because it explains the stubbornness of illusions, keeps perception fast and stable, and carves out a special role for perception as evidence.

On the other side, I have a firm and very concrete intuition that knowledge really does change how things look. After someone pointed out the hidden figure in one of those images where, once the pattern snaps into a new organization, you cannot go back to seeing it the old way, the picture genuinely looked different to me—I could not recover the naive look I had before. The same thing happens with music: after I learned to hear where the beat falls in a syncopated rhythm, the sound itself seemed transformed, not just my later interpretation of it. It feels as though the information has become part of the experience itself, not something added on top of it.

So I am torn. Should I keep the sealed-module picture and explain my intuition away as attention, or as a change in judgment that happens after the fact? Or should I give up the idea that perception is sealed off and accept that cognition penetrates perception—that beliefs really can alter what we see and hear? Or is the right move to redraw the boundary some other way, so both sides end up partly right? Please start by laying out the strongest version of each position, then give me a negotiated verdict: what would justify debunking my intuition, what would justify amending or abandoning the theory, and how could I tell, in my own cases, which of these is really going on? Think carefully, and feel free to distinguish different kinds of influence before committing.
