In American law, a criminal statute can be struck down as void for vagueness when it fails to give fair notice of what it forbids or invites arbitrary enforcement, yet the civil law routinely runs on imprecise standards — a negligence standard of care, good faith, unconscionability — without anyone treating them as invalid. Most people accept both practices without strain, and the courts have long applied their strictest vagueness scrutiny primarily to criminal prohibitions. Is there a defensible principle that makes an imprecise rule worse when the penalty is criminal, or is the asymmetry merely a historical accident we have stopped noticing?
