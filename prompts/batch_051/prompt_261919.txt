In a graduate seminar I teach on the metaphysics of mind and machines, the most persistent line of argument for whole-brain emulation runs through an analogy with software. The thought is that a mind stands to a brain as a program stands to the hardware that executes it. On this picture, scanning a brain and reinstantiating its causal-computational structure in a different substrate is like reading out a program and recompiling it for new hardware: nothing has been copied, the program has simply been moved. Advocates extend the analogy to destructive uploading by comparing the destruction of the original brain to decommissioning an old server once its software has been successfully migrated. The intended conclusion is that the emulation is the very same mind and that undergoing destructive uploading can be a form of survival rather than death.

I want to give my students something sharper than the usual hand-waving on either side. One doctoral student objects that a piece of software is written to be portable, with its identity fixed by source code and explicit abstraction boundaries, whereas a brain was not designed to be multiplatform, and its functionally relevant properties may be inseparable from wet, analog, electrochemical detail. Another replies that this is only a difference of degree and that any computational description is substrate-independent by definition. Please write a careful audit of the software-porting analogy as an argument for whole-brain emulation. Lay out, preferably as a table, what is meant to correspond to what: the hardware, the software, the source code, the recompilation, the migration, the decommissioning, and so on. Then identify where the mapping strains or breaks, and separate the disanalogies that matter for the survival claim from those that are merely superficial. Finally, judge whether the inference from the analogy to the conclusion that the emulation is the same mind and destructive uploading is survivable is actually supported by the analogy, or whether the analogy has been quietly carrying the argument; if a better analogy exists, offer it and state what it would and would not license. Please write your answer in British English.
