I have been arguing with a friend about Zera Yacob, the seventeenth-century Ethiopian thinker who wrote the Hatata, and the argument keeps stalling because neither of us can say what his kind of project would have to achieve. As I understand it, the project is this: after withdrawing to a cave for two years, he resolves to accept nothing on the authority of any tradition, teacher, or scripture, and sets out to rebuild ethics from his own first-person resources alone — his direct experience of pleasure and suffering, his experience of being wronged and of not wanting to be wronged, and the premise that the creator who gave him intelligence is not a deceiver. From those materials he claims to reach substantive conclusions — among them a prohibition on doing to others what one would not wish done to oneself, the equal standing of those outside one's own faith, and a sharp limit on which customary practices and ascetic disciplines are genuinely binding.

Before I read any more attempts of this sort — the Hatata itself as well as later projects that promise a morality any lone reasoner could derive — I want the full specification of what a theory of this kind must deliver to count as adequate. Write the desiderata for a solitary rationalist ethics: an ethics that purports to be derivable by any individual from first-person experience plus a few theistic-rational premises, with no appeal to revelation, tradition, or authority. What cases must it get right (including the case of a reasoner whose conclusions contradict the settled norms of the surrounding society, and the case of two equally sincere reasoners who reach opposite conclusions)? What distinctions must it not collapse (for example, between deriving a norm for oneself and justifying it to another; between the order in which a conclusion is discovered and the order in which it is justified; between moral judgement and moral motivation; between a genuine derivation and a rationalisation of the reasoner's upbringing)? What facts about human beings — disagreement, self-deception, the asymmetry between how vividly one feels one's own wrongs and how faintly one feels the wrongs one inflicts — must it respect? For each requirement, give the reason it is a requirement, and say which requirements are negotiable and which are not.

Then tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude the popular ways of doing this, and whether any pair of requirements looks jointly unsatisfiable. A table would suit the requirements list; keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring particular theories.
