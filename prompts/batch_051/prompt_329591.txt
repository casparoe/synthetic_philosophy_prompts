I run a philosophy-and-probability reading group, and after several sessions on Kelly betting we keep returning to the same impasse. One camp holds that the rational objective in a wager is expected wealth, or expected utility more generally, with logarithmic utility as just one admissible taste among many; on this view the Kelly fraction has no special normative status, and the mere fact that wealth compounds across repeated bets does not privilege any particular utility function. The other camp holds that once bets are repeated and returns multiply together, the rational objective is the long-run growth rate of a single capital path—equivalently expected log return—and that maximizing expected wealth is an ensemble-average mistake that prescribes stakes no actual decision-maker should take. I would like you to draft the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Anchor the statement in a running example: each round an agent may stake any fraction f of current capital on a biased coin; heads has probability 0.6 and adds 80% of the stake, tails has probability 0.4 and loses 50% of the stake, so capital is multiplied by 1 + 0.8f on heads and 1 − 0.5f on tails. Structure the document as follows: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims both camps can sign, including the mathematical claims about this example—the stake that maximizes expected wealth per round, the stake that maximizes expected log growth, and the long-run fate of each policy as the number of rounds grows; (2) a disagreements table giving each side's residual position in wording that side itself accepts, kept non-vacuous; and (3) a cruxes section in which each crux is a sub-question such that settling it would move at least one camp, paired with a calculation, case, or argument both camps agree bears on it and with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If you conclude that some part of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why.
