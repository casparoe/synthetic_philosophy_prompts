A reading group I'm in has been stuck on echo chambers for three meetings now, and every discussion has the same shape. One side insists the problem is informational: people in epistemic bubbles just don't see enough of the outside, so the fix is exposure — more sources, more links, maybe a better feed. The other side keeps saying that misses the point, because an echo chamber isn't mainly about missing information; it's about who gets trusted, and in a real chamber the people inside already know the outsiders exist and have been trained to discredit them. Both sides agree on the paradigm cases, and nobody moves. My hunch is we're not having one argument: the first side treats escape as an empirical problem about information access, the second treats it as a problem about trust and social dependence, and maybe each side has a different picture of what counts as "seeing the other side." Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, empirical, normative, or two questions sharing a label — and say what would count as progress, or whether we should stop expecting the two sides to converge.
