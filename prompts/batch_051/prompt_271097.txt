I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background. I have been asked to encode the decision logic used by our island's harbour board for what it calls excess-carrying cases: when a rescue launch may take aboard more people than its rated capacity. The board works from precedent rather than a written policy, so I need to infer the rule from its rulings. In every case below the launch is the same 12-person vessel and is already carrying 12 people, the sea state and water temperature are comparable, and the board has never permitted more than one extra person aboard.

The board's rulings so far:

1. Two adult swimmers. A capsized dinghy leaves two healthy adults in the water. The board ruled that neither may be taken aboard; both are to be held alongside on lines with lifejackets and floats until the lifeboat arrives.

2. A child and an adult. A capsized dinghy leaves a six-year-old child and a healthy adult. The board ruled that the launch may exceed its rated capacity by exactly one, for the child; the adult is held alongside.

3. A paramedic needed ashore. Two adult swimmers: one is a paramedic urgently needed ashore for a cardiac arrest, and the other is an ordinary adult. The board ruled that the launch may exceed capacity by exactly one, for the paramedic; the other adult is held alongside.

4. The culpable coxswain. Two adult swimmers: one is the coxswain whose negligence capsized the dinghy, and the other is an uninvolved adult. The board ruled that no exception applies; both are held alongside.

5. Two children. Two six-year-old children are in the water. The board ruled that the launch may exceed capacity by exactly one, not two, and did not specify which child should be chosen.

The new case: the launch is carrying 12 and comes on two swimmers, a healthy adult and a 78-year-old man with a hip injury who cannot keep a firm hold on a line in these seas. The lifeboat is 40 minutes away. What should the board decide if it is to remain consistent with its own rulings? Say which precedent is the closest analog, and if the rulings underdetermine the answer, say what principle the board would be adopting with each option.

Please answer in British English. Begin with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and no restating of the question.
