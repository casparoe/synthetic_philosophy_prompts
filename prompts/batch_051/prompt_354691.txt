I run a small effective-altruism group, and I keep hitting the same wall in my own life: whether to give a windfall of about $40,000 to a couple of charities I'm convinced do enormous good, or use it to get out of debt and start a small business. The weird thing is that both feel like the same kind of rational demand, and I can't find a principled trade-off between them. Someone told me this is basically Sidgwick's dualism of practical reason in miniature. If I take that seriously, what should it actually change about how I deliberate?

Specifically: Does the dualism imply there is no fact of the matter about what I most rationally ought to do here, or only that I can't know it? What would it imply about whether hedging — say, splitting the money — is rationally required, permissible, or just a cop-out? And does it change what it even means to act rationally when your aims pull in two directions, beyond the usual advice to just decide which aim you care about more? I want implications I can actually use, not a close reading of Sidgwick.
