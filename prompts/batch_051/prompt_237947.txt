I cover science and ideas for a living, and for a long piece I'm working on I've been reading the metaethics literature on evolutionary debunking. The rough story is familiar: natural selection built our moral intuitions—disgust at incest, loyalty to kin, anger at free-riders—because they helped our ancestors survive and reproduce, not because they were tracking independent moral facts. The debunker says that once you accept this genealogy, believing in objective moral truths is like trusting a compass you know points toward food, not north: your moral beliefs may keep you alive, but you have no reason to think they're true. Realists push back by saying moral beliefs could be both fitness-enhancing and truth-tracking (pain, kin, and reciprocity are morally relevant on plenty of realist views), and that the debunking argument seems to cut against all our beliefs, including the belief in evolution itself, so it proves too much. Both sides have been making these same moves since roughly the early 2000s, and nobody, as far as I can tell, has moved.

What's striking to me is that the two sides don't even seem to agree about what would settle the matter. Is this a dispute about the empirical facts of how flexible or truth-tracking moral cognition actually is? About the epistemic principle that a belief's origins can undermine it? About what moral realism is even supposed to deliver? My working theory is that it's mostly a disagreement about standards of evidence—specifically, about what it takes for a genealogy of a belief to count as a debunking explanation—and only secondarily about the science or first-order morality. But I'm not sure I can tell weather that's right or whether there's a hidden empirical or formal question underneath.

So I'd like a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is, not a survey of the arguments. Is it verbal, substantive-but-about-standards, a standoff between starting intuitions, or an ill-posed question? And given the diagnosis, what would real progress look like—a better account of debunking, an empirical result about moral cognition, a formal argument, a cleaner distinction—or should I accept that nothing is going to end it? I want the structure of the impasse, and an honest answer about what could actually break it.
