You serve on a five-member city ethics commission that must recommend to the city council whether to adopt a small-donor public matching system for city elections. Under the proposal, contributions of $50 or less from city residents would be matched six-to-one with public funds, individual contributions would be capped at $500, and contributions from corporations, labor unions, and city contractors would be prohibited. The alternative before the commission is a disclosure-only rule: no new contribution limits, but all donations over $100 and all independent expenditures over $500 would be posted online within 48 hours. One commissioner argues the matching system forces taxpayers to subsidize political speech they oppose and will simply shift money into outside groups; another argues that without the stricter system, incumbents will answer chiefly to their largest donors and challengers will be unable to compete. Draft the commission's written recommendation to the council: choose one option, note any modifications you would make, give your reasons, and address the strongest argument for the option you reject.
