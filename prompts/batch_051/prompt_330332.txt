I'm a PhD student in early modern philosophy, and I've been trying to get clear on what Berkeley's master argument actually commits him to. I've come to think the crucial move is not the slogan that sensible things are ideas, but a stronger claim about the limits of conception that appears in George Berkeley's A Treatise Concerning the Principles of Human Knowledge, section 5:

"But my conceiving or imagining power does not extend beyond the possibility of real existence or perception. Hence, as it is impossible for me to see or feel anything without an actual sensation of that thing, so is it impossible for me to conceive in my thoughts any sensible thing or object distinct from the sensation or perception of it."

I want to treat the principle expressed here as a live philosophical position and ask what would count against it. I am not asking only for the stock criticisms. I want a genuine map of its exposure. Which intuitions, possible cases, and results in philosophy of mind or the theory of perception would bear on the claim that I cannot conceive any sensible thing distinct from the sensation or perception of it? For each item, distinguish whether it is a decisive counterexample, a serious difficulty, or only an awkward implication. Where a committed Berkeleyan could respond, specify the response: would they have to revise the principle, reject the case as question-begging, or introduce a distinction that absorbs it? Also tell me which possible defeaters are live rather than merely logically possible, and what a defender would themselves concede as counting against the principle. I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Finally, consider whether the position can be insulated by saying Berkeley is only making a claim about what a finite mind can conceive, rather than about being or possibility outright, and say whether that insulation is itself a cost. Conclude by saying where you think the real vulnerability lies: in the conceivability premise, in the inference from conceivability to possibility, or elsewhere.
