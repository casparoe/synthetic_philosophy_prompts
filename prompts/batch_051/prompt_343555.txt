I sit on my denomination's committee on doctrine and practice. A proposal before us would make head coverings mandatory for women during worship, on the strength of a scriptural instruction that appears to command it. Our committee has long treated scriptural instructions about slavery and dietary practice as culturally bound and not binding on us, but we have never stated the principle that sorts one class of commands from the other. The debate is turning on that missing principle: supporters of the proposal argue that refusing to apply the head-covering text is arbitrary selectivity, while opponents reply that applying it would equally arbitrarily impose a first-century Mediterranean custom while ignoring other texts we already read as bound. After a long argument with a friend last night about whether any principle can distinguish transcultural commands from culturally bound ones without begging the question, I have to circulate a short decision memo before our Thursday meeting. Draft the memo: it should recommend adopting, rejecting, or modifying the proposal; formulate a criterion our body could actually apply to this and future cases; and engage the strongest consideration on the losing side.
