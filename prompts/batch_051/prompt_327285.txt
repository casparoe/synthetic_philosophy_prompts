I'm trying to get my views straight before a discussion group on wild animal welfare and conservation, and I want a consistency check more than a lecture. Here's where I've landed.

1. Suffering is bad wherever it occurs, and having feathers or scales or fur doesn't by itself make pain count for less. What matters is whether the individual can suffer, not what species it belongs to.

2. I think a healthy wild ecosystem is, on the whole, net-negative in terms of felt welfare. Most wild animals die before they reach maturity, usually by starvation, disease, predation, or exposure, and being eaten alive or slowly starving is about as bad as anything a human endures. The accumulated suffering in a functioning marsh or woodlot seems to me to outweigh whatever enjoyment is going on in it.

3. So, bracketing effects on humans and on future nonhuman animals: when a wetland is drained or a lot is cleared and the animals who would have lived and suffered there never come into existence, I think that's a welfare improvement. I don't have to like the look of it or the motive behind it to think that.

4. Nonetheless, I believe we should generally preserve intact ecosystems, biodiversity loss is a real bad, and I'd sign most conservation petitions without hesitation.

5. I also believe we're bad at predicting what happens when we intervene in complex ecological systems, and that uncertainty should make us strongly conservative about doing anything. Lots of interventions that look like they'll reduce suffering blow up in practice, so even when an action would be good if it worked as planned, it can still be the wrong thing to do.

6. And I think the interests of sentient beings who don't exist yet matter, including future wild animals, not just the ones alive now.

Go through these and tell me: which pairs are in genuine tension, which only seem too be, and how bad each tension really is? Then tell me what the least expensive revision is, and what it would cost me. I have a vague feeling there's a snag somewhere, but when I try to locate it I just go in circles.
