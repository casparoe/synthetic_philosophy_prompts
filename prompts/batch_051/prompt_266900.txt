I have been working through formal epistemology on my own, and I keep getting stuck at the point where strict Bayesian conditionalization runs into evidence that is less than certain. I understand the standard story: if you come to be certain of some evidence E, your new probability for a hypothesis H should be your old conditional probability P(H | E). But the cases that actually interest me are ones in which an observation does not make any proposition certain. A cloth looks bluish under doubtful lighting; a test result is only moderately reliable; a witness seems probably but not definitely trustworthy. In each case, the experience merely redistributes confidence instead of proving anything. Jeffrey conditionalization is presented as the rule that handles this, but I want the machinery, not the slogan.

Please lay the explanation out in five titled sections, in this order:

1. The problem (roughly 150 words). State why strict conditionalization cannot represent uncertain evidence, and say what an adequate update rule would have to deliver in such cases.

2. The rule and its commitments (roughly 200 words). State Jeffrey conditionalization precisely. Define the partition over which the evidence acts, give the update formula, and explain the rigidity or invariance condition — the claim that the conditional probabilities of hypotheses on members of the partition remain fixed through the update. Explain why that condition is doing the real work and what would go wrong without it.

3. A worked example (roughly 200 words). Construct a small numerical case — for instance, a diagnostic hypothesis with two or three possible test outcomes — and apply the rule step by step, showing how the new probabilities over the partition combine with the unchanged conditional probabilities to yield a new unconditional probability for the hypothesis.

4. The order-dependence objection (roughly 250 words). Present and diagnose the objection that Jeffrey conditionalization is not generally commutative: if you first update on one uncertain observation and then on a second, you may arrive at a different final distribution than if you had updated in the reverse order, even though nothing has been learned with certainty. Explain where this order-sensitivity comes from and what a defender of the rule should say about it. Is the non-commutativity a genuine flaw in the rule, or is it tracking something about the evidence itself?

5. Your assessment (roughly 200 words). Step back and judge whether Jeffrey conditionalization solves the original problem of uncertain evidence or mainly relocates its difficulty — for example, into the question of where the new probabilities over the partition are supposed to come from. Include what you take to be the deepest unresolved issue the rule leaves open.
