I spent most of my working life as a research scientist, and I still cannot think about an unsettled empirical question except by decomposing it into the components that would have to be true for a conclusion to hold, assigning each a rough probability, and seeing where the final estimate is most fragile. I have recently been reading philosophy of religion, and the argument from divine hiddenness — in roughly the form associated with J. L. Schellenberg — strikes me as more probative than most arguments on either side. As I understand it, the argument runs: a God who perfectly loves every human person would ensure that each person is at some point in a position to enter a conscious, personal relationship with God, so that widespread sincere, nonresistant unbelief would be deeply surprising; yet such unbelief plainly exists across times and cultures. Those nonresistant nonbelievers are the evidence, and the claim is that their existence is much more likely if no such God exists than if one does.

I would like your credence, as a number between 0 and 1, that the argument from divine hiddenness succeeds in the following precise sense: taking the observed distribution of belief and nonbelief as evidence, it makes the existence of a perfectly loving God less probable than not, once you have stated your own prior. Do not stop at the number. Decompose it: the probability that a substantial fraction of actual nonbelief is genuinely nonresistant rather than culpable, self-deceived, or insincere; the probability that a loving God could have adequate reasons to permit widespread nonresistant nonbelief, for example to preserve an uncoerced free response or to allow a kind of moral and spiritual development that certainty would foreclose; the probability that, conditional on such a God existing, the observable patterns of religious experience and conviction would look very different from what we in fact find; and any further component you think the estimate turns on. Give each component a rough probability with a one-line justification, show how they combine into the final number, and tell me which component the result is most sensitive to and what new evidence would move your credence the most. I have my own working estimate, but I am more interested in seeing where a careful person can defensibly place the numbers than in whether ours happen to match.
