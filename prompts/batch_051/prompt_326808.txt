I have been trying to make precise a limitation I keep running into when one AI system is used to interpret another, and I would like an honest assessment of whether the idea is sound or merely restates something already familiar. I call it the deficit principle for recursive interpretability.

Let the target system be T and the interpreter be I. The principle says that when I produces an interpretation of T, the interpretation is reliable only if I can, in principle, generate or reproduce the structural feature of T that the interpretation attributes. This is not a claim about I's overall capacity; it is local to the dimension being interpreted. If I is less expressive than T along that dimension, the interpretation will fail in a characteristic way: I will substitute an abstraction it can manipulate and report that abstraction as if it were the mechanism operating in T.

Now apply the principle to itself. A second-order interpretation is just a first-order interpretation whose target is the first-order interpretation. If the same system supplies both levels, the second-order report inherits the first-order system's representational limits, so it cannot reliably detect the first-order system's blind spots along the dimensions where those limits operate. The regress does not continue indefinitely; it terminates in whatever blind spots the outermost interpreter is unable to state. I call the number of stable rounds of self-application before the report merely repeats itself an interpretation's depth.

From this I draw the conclusion that no single interpreter can fully audit an interpretation of a system more complex than itself along the audited dimension. The best available corrective is not deeper self-application but heterogeneity: interpreting with a second system whose limits are shaped differently. The choice of that second system, however, is ultimately a normative or sociological choice, not something the first system can justify from inside its own interpretive frame.

What I want to know is whether this is a coherent position, whether it already exists under a recognizable name, and where it breaks. In particular, am I confusing representational complexity with interpretive accuracy, and is the prohibition on self-auditing systematic blind spots merely a tautology or does it have real content?
