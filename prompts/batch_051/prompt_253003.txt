Please argue for the following position, and try to make the strongest case you can. The standard explanation of Moore's paradox says the sentence — it's raining but I don't believe it — cannot be properly asserted because assertion is governed by a norm: roughly, assert p only if you believe p (or know p). Given the second conjunct, asserting the first conjunct flouts that norm, and that's why the whole thing is defective. I want you to argue the reverse direction. Take the Moorean data — that such sentences are absurd to assert, and arguably also absurd to think — as the basic phenomenon. Then explain the intuition that assertion expresses belief as a generalisation from those data, rather than treating the norm of assertion as an independent principle that explains them. In other words: the norm of assertion, if there is one, is a downstream codification of the Moorean data, not it's source. Say what this would change about how we should think about the relationship between speech acts and mental state attribution, and flag the strongest objection to the position you can think of.
