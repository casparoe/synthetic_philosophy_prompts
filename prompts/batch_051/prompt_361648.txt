I follow a standing rule when asked to attach a probability to a state of affairs on the basis of information that only fixes a rough symmetry among the alternatives. Before I give a number, I test whether my answer would change under an equally natural redescription of the same alternatives. If it would — if the judgment that these options are equally likely is true under one partition but false under another partition of the same possibility space — I refuse to assign a single probability. Instead I report either a range or a set of conditional probabilities: under partition A the probability is p, under partition B it is q, and the bare question is under-specified.

The rule takes its motivation from Bertrand's paradox. Ask for the probability that a randomly drawn chord of a circle is longer than the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle, and seemingly legitimate readings of "randomly drawn" give one-third, one-half, or one-quarter, because each reading applies the principle of indifference within a different parameterization of the same drawing. I take the paradox to show not that the principle of indifference is hopeless, but that it requires a preferred partition; my rule is simply a refusal to pretend that such a partition exists when none has been supplied.

A concrete case where I applied the rule: a colleague asked for my probability that a gain parameter exceeded a certain threshold, offering only that we both regarded the prior as "uninformative." I noticed that taking the parameter as uniformly distributed, taking its logarithm as uniformly distributed, and taking its odds as uniformly distributed produced materially different tail probabilities, though each sounded like a way of saying we had no information. I declined to give a single number and instead reported the competing numbers and a range.

Is this policy defensible, or is it a bias against precision dressed up as a principle? Specifically:

(4) Can the rule be restated so that it avoids the illusion of precision in genuinely under-specified problems without leaving me unable to adopt harmless uniform priors in ordinary settings?
