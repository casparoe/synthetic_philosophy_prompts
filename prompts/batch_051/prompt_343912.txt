Lately my kids keep using boomer and gen z as if they were categories with actual edges, and I caught myself doing the same thing at work—there's now a training module on generational differences. So I tried to turn it into a real definition instead of a vibe. Here's what I've got so far. A generation is an age cohort that has become a self-fullfilling kind: no cohort starts out as a generation, but it becomes one when enough people begin treating it as real—marketers segmenting by it, employers hiring through it, politicians courting it, and members themselves planning their lives around what their generation is supposed to be like—and that collective treatment is part of what makes the supposed shared traits actually show up, or at least makes members adopt and act them out. Before the loop, it's just people born in the same window; after it, maybe it's a real social kind, because the belief is doing some of the making.
