Ryle's regress argument against intellectualism about know-how assumes that the only alternative to his dispositional view is a picture on which the agent must first contemplate a proposition about how to act, then a further proposition about how to apply it, and so on. That picture now looks dated: we routinely attribute skilled performance to subpersonal representational states—motor commands, forward models, trained networks—that guide action without ever being entertained or applied by an agent. I want to know whether Ryle's regress argument survives this shift. Which exact premise does the shift put pressure on? Is there a repaired form of the regress that still reaches an intellectualism built on subpersonal, automatically deployed representational states, or has the intellectualist simply moved out of the range of the regress? If the regress cannot be repaired, say what an anti-intellectualist should now say about the representational states that seem to be doing the work in skilled performance.
