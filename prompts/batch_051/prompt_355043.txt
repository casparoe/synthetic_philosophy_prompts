I have been trying to work out what I actually think about sweatshop labor and similar cases of exploitation in which the person taken advantage of still ends up better off than before. I have recorded my instinctive verdicts on a series of cases, but I cannot find a single principle that obviously fits them all, and I would like you to help me find one.

1. A garment factory in Dhaka pays the prevailing local wage, which is far below any reasonable living wage. The workers are not deceived, the hours are long but legal, and the job is the best option most of them have. Verdict: permissible, though it leaves me uneasy.

2. The same factory, same wage, same conditions. It then emerges that the firm could double wages without raising prices or meaningfully affecting margins, because labor is only a small fraction of the retail price. The firm declines. Verdict: not permissible. The refusal is what tips it for me.

3. A local workshop with thin margins pays its workers the identical wage the factory in case 1 pays. Verdict: permissible, or at least much less wrong than case 2.

4. A factory recruits workers from a poorer region, pays them below the going local rate but well above what they earned at home, and does not tell them what local workers are paid. Verdict: not permissible.

5. A family needs cash for urgent surgery and sells its home to a developer for half of its market value. The developer did not create the emergency, and the family understands the price and accepts. Verdict: not permissible.

6. A mining company in a region of high unemployment pays slightly above the local going rate and meets local safety law, which is weak. Its workers describe it as the best job they have ever had. Verdict: permissible. I cannot get this case to come out wrong.

The case I keep going back and forth on is this: a multinational offers twice the local going rate, provides free meals, and engages in no deception, but its superior bargaining position allows it to retain ninety percent of the value the workers create, and it could share more without raising prices. I cannot decide whether this is permissible.

Is there a principle that captures all or most of these verdicts? Please state it as precisely as you can, or, if no single principle will do, state a small set of candidate principles with the trade-offs between them. Show which cases motivate each part of the principle, apply it to the unresolved case and to one or two further cases I have not considered, and say honestly which of my verdicts, if any, it cannot accommodate. If some of my verdicts have to be revised, tell me which ones and why.
