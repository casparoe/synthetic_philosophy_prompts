I'm a retired geochemist who has gotten into philosophy over the last several years, mostly ethics and metaethics, and I keep coming back to the Frege–Geach problem without ever feeling I have it straight. I recently read a piece — an essay in a general-interest magazine, not an academic journal — that tried to explain the problem for expressivism, and I'd love a sanity check on whether its summary is fair. Here's roughly how it went, as I remember it:

"Expressivism is the view that moral sentences don't describe the world and aren't really in the business of being true or false. Instead, saying 'lying is wrong' is first and foremost a matter of expressing a non-cognitive attitude toward lying. In its older, simpler form this was glossed as the idea that 'stealing is wrong' works more or less like 'stealing, boo!', and the article treats that boo/hurrah picture as the core of the view. The Frege–Geach problem arises when these attitude-expressions get embedded in larger sentences. Consider 'if lying is wrong, then getting your little brother to lie is wrong.' In the antecedent, the speaker isn't actually expressing disapproval of lying; she might be arguing against lying or just exploring a possibility. So it seems 'lying is wrong' does something different inside the conditional than it does on its own, and the expressivist is caught in an equivocation in the argument 'lying is wrong; if lying is wrong, then getting your little brother to lie is wrong; therefore getting your little brother to lie is wrong.' Worse, attitudes aren't the sort of thing that can stand in relations of validity or contradiction, so the expressivist can't explain why that argument is a good one. The article concluded that although Blackburn and Gibbard have offered elaborate technical fixes, the received view among philosophers is that expressivism never really solved the problem, and that Frege–Geach still effectively refutes the view."

I do know the boo/hurrah bit is an old caricature of an early view, but I'm less clear on how much of the underlying complaint survives once you modernize the view. Please also seperate what's genuinely settled from what's still contested among people who know the area, and don't present a contested reading as if it were the consensus.
