I write about economics for a general-audience magazine, and I keep getting stuck on something basic. Everyone in central banking says printing money doesn't create wealth — it just changes who holds claims on the same real stuff. And yet when a crisis hits, the central bank creates hundreds of billions in new reserves to buy government bonds (they call it quantitive easing), and everyone treats the new liquidity as if it's genuinely valuable, because without it solvent institutions would collapse and real output would be destroyed. So the practice seems to be: new money can't make us richer, but swaping one asset for another with newly printed money can save us from becoming poorer. What theory of value, or of money, does that practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view, say which parts of the practice strictly require it versus merely fit with it, flag any place where the practice and the view come apart, and tell me what a central bank would look like if it actually presupposed that printing money creates wealth.
