I'm writing a short memo on pre-AGI bargains — treaties, equity splits, shared-control deals that people try to hammer out before anyone builds the thing that could renegotiate everything — and I've got a list of positions I seem to hold. Before I show it to anyone I'd like a consistency check, because I can't tell if I've asembled a contradiction or just a few views that each sound fine on their own.

Here they are:

(1) A pre-AGI bargain should bind the parties after the transition. Not just "it would be nice" — if the deal says control or compute or money is split such-and-such a way, then later failing to perform is a real wrong, not merely bad PR.

(2) But a bargain only binds in the normal way if both parties had a genuine alternative to signing. If one side couldn't walk away without catastrophe, the agreement is closer to a forced settlement, and the duty to honor it is weaker.

(3) I'm sceptical that what we write down before the transition actually controls much after it. A post-AGI party that can outthink the authors of the deal will keep it only if it happens to suit them, so "locking in" terms against the later, stronger party's interests is close to fantasy.

(4) Even so, I think it's legitimate to bind your own later self under uncertainty. You agree to give up half the shares before the valuation, and you don't get to relitigate it when the number comes in.

(5) I also think many pre-AGI bargains matter mostly before AGI — by coordinating current players, buying time, making defection legible — rather than by actually constraining the AGI itself.

There's a concrete case where I find myself wanting all three of the following at once: a lab trades a nontrivial slice of post-AGI control to a coalition of small states in exchange for them not pushing for a shutdown moratorium now. I want to say the lab should perform the deal later; I want to say the small states didn't have a real walk-away option; and I want to say the deal was still worth making. I honestly don't know whether that's the place where my views are in trouble or whether it all comes apart once I've applied (1)–(5).

So: which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and which single revision would buy me the most consistency for the least cost? Please rank the tensions by severity and say what each would force me to give up.
