Here is a test case for where the boundary of morally significant computation lies. Take these as the working criteria for a computation that matters in its own right, rather than merely through its effects. (1) Realization rather than simulation: the system must implement the causal structure of the relevant states; describing or simulating a state is not the same as being in it. (2) Valence: the system must have states that are good or bad for it, and its own processing must treat them as good or bad rather than as neutral signals. (3) Integration: those valenced states must belong to a single point of view at a time, not to parallel modules that never share information. (4) Continuity: there must be one subject over the relevant stretch, carried by memory, a self-model, or a persistent architecture. (5) Attribution: the moral weight must attach to the very system whose valenced and integrated states explain what it does, not to a subcomponent, a simulation, or a merely useful description.

Now consider this case. Two neuromorphic chips, L and R, each implement a complete but simplified copy of the same affective and nociceptive network of a small mammal, with its own sensors and effectors. A single narrow bus links them, carrying only one coarse scalar, a shared threat estimate. When the bus is open, this scalar is the average of the two chips' local estimates and gates learning in both, so they acquire and extinguish avoidances jointly, including avoidances neither would acquire alone. When the bus is severed, each chip runs as an independent reflexive aversive learner. The pair has no further integration: no shared workspace, no shared memory, and no self-model above the shared scalar. Does the linked pair, each chip alone, or neither, count as a morally significant computation? Check the case against the five criteria in order. Respond with exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion; in the fifth item, after checking the attribution criterion, give the overall verdict and say which criterion, if any, the case shows to be in need of revision.
