I intend to approach the question of whether video-game characters matter morally by first fixing the standards of success, so that whichever author is most vivid does not do the argumentative work. Before I read further, I want a specification of what any adequate theory of the moral status of simulated or game characters has to deliver. Write the desiderata: the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it has to respect (including the ones it must not let do all the work), and the results it has to be consistent with, each requirement accompanied by the case or consideration that motivates it. Then flag any subset of the desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and finish with a short note on how the main families of view — substrate-independence or functionalist accounts, biological chauvinism, sentience-only views, and relational or contractualist accounts — fare against the list. Keep most of the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring.

Two issues should be handled explicitly rather than assumed. First, the difference between a character that depicts suffering and one that implements whatever process pain or fear turns out to be: a pixel-art enemy that plays a canned hit animation presumably has no claims on us, while a whole-brain emulation of a mouse plausibly does, and the theory should say what does the separating work. Second, the reset problem: a character whose internal state is wiped or overwritten between sessions, so that it may have short, disconnected episodes but no diachronic identity. Say how a theory should treat the moral difference between twenty million separate one-minute resets of a pain-capable agent and a single agent that suffers for twenty million minutes, and whether that difference belongs among the desiderata or merely among the applications. I also want the list to respect a constraint of epistemic modesty: it should say how we ought to act when a system is behaviourally indistinguishable from a subject along the dimensions that matter, even though we cannot yet establish whether anything is happening on the inside.

Please write your answer in British English.
