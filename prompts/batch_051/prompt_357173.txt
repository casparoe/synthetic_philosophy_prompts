Here's a claim people treat as either obviously true or obviously false: that an endless afterlife, even a pleasant one, would eventually become boring in a deep way — not just between hobbies, but the way Bernard Williams's Makropulos case suggests, where after 300 years everything repeats and the projects that give life shape are all completed or abaondoned. The other side says that's a failure of imagination: a god (or a sufficiently advanced future) could keep refreshing our desires, and 'boredom' under those conditions is just a word for our finite psychology. I want to know whether the Williams-style claim is a real claim about how human motivation works or just a mood dressed up as an argument. Design the strongest test you can of the claim that an indefinitely extended life under our current psychology would necessarily go stale — use empirical evidence where you can (longitudinal studies of hedonic adaptation, how time perception changes with age, people who report time passing faster or slower, whatever fits). Tell me what result would count for the Williams side, what would count for the other side, and what the test could never settle even in principle. And if the question as I've set it up rests on a confusion — maybe 'under our current psychology' or 'necessarily' is doing all the work — say so plainly instead of playing along.
