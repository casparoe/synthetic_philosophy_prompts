I write about the history and philosophy of science, and I am preparing a piece on how evidence that has been sitting in the literature for years can suddenly confirm a new theory. The classic Bayesian puzzle here is the problem of old evidence: if a datum is already known with certainty, it cannot raise the probability of a new hypothesis, because its probability is 1; yet everyone agrees that Einstein's general relativity was confirmed by Mercury's anomalous perihelion advance, which astronomers had known for decades. Now vary the case: suppose the anomaly is not known with certainty but is a marginal, disputed result to which the field assigns a probability of only 0.6, and it is only after the new theory is formulated, and found to entail the anomaly with certainty, that the data are reclassified as solid. Does the old-evidence problem now dissolve, or does it simply move somewhere else? Work through the formal situation and the philosophical residue, and say which of the standard diagnoses survives this variation and which does not.
