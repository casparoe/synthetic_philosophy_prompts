Ok so I'm not someone with a background in philosophy—I just got interested in lying because I kept noticing that people say "he didn't technically lie" and then other people say "no, he lied in his heart" and I wanted a clean way to think about it. I ended up reading Augustine's On Lying, and I got stuck on this part:

"that man lies, who has one thing in his mind and utters another in words, or by signs of whatever kind. Whence also the heart of him who lies is said to be double; that is, there is a double thought: the one, of that thing which he either knows or thinks to be true and does not produce; the other, of that thing which he produces instead thereof, knowing or thinking it to be false."

So his idea seems to be that lying isn't really about whether the sentence is false, and it isn't only about whether someone gets deceived. It's about the doubleness: the mismatch between the inner state and the outer expression. That feels right to me, but then I started wondering: is that the kind of claim you can actually test, or is it just a defintion?
