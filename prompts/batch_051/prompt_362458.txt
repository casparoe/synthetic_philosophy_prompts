I've been part of a small rationalist-adjacent forum where we keep noticing the same failure mode: threads are captured less by refutation than by framing. The first person to write a confident longpost can presuppose something in the background of nearly every argumentative move, and because no individual reader wants to derail the thread by objecting to an assumption nobody else has flagged, the assumption quietly enters the thread's common ground by the third page. By the time someone finally objects, half the participants have built on it and the objection reads as a non sequitur. A typical case: someone argues that the interesting question about AI timelines is not whether scaling trends continue but which downstream capabilities track them, and the thread silently inherits a specific view of what scaling is without anyone ever having asserted or defended it. I know this is just accommodation and scorekeeping as a description of how language games already work; what we are considering is turning the description into explicit mechanics.

Here is the proposal. Every thread gets an explicit presupposition ledger. A post may introduce at most one proposition not already in the ledger, and it must be tagged as a presupposition. Tagged propositions enter a quarantine of either forty-eight hours or five replies, whichever comes first. During quarantine any participant may challenge the proposition on one of three grounds: it is false; it is true but not appropriately shared background for this thread; or it is too equivocal to carry the uses being made of it. On a challenge of the first or third kind, the original author must either defend the proposition from the ledger using only premises and inference rules the thread already accepts, conditionalize the entire downstream argument as an assumption branch, or withdraw the post. On a challenge of the second kind, the author may conditionalize immediately. If no challenge lands in the window, the proposition is automatically accommodated into the ledger and later speakers may rely on it without tagging. To prevent slow-motion frame capture, an author deposits a fixed amount of their forum reputation for each presupposition they introduce; the deposit is refunded once the proposition has been relied on by at least two distinct participants without a successful challenge, and is forfeited if the proposition is demoted. Any ledger entry can be reopened later by a motion requiring three seconders and a majority vote of active thread participants.

Walk through how this would actually behave. Who would get skilled at working it, and who would quietly stop posting? What does a well-formed post come to look like once writers optimize for the ledger, and does the ledger itself become the object of meta-dispute? Where does it get gamed first: presuppositions dressed up as definitions, one large presupposition split across consecutive posts, waiting out the quarantine, or something else? And which failure modes are fixable by adjusting the deposit, the window, or the challenge grounds, as opposed to being intrinsic to any attempt to make common ground explicit?
