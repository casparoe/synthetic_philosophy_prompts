In my introductory philosophy textbook, reflective equilibrium is summarized roughly as follows: "You begin with your considered judgments about particular cases, then formulate general principles that would account for them, and adjust either the principles or the judgments until the two fit together. Because the only inputs are the intuitions you already happen to have, the method cannot genuinely justify the resulting view; it merely rationalizes your starting point. Reflective equilibrium is therefore best understood as a form of intuitionism." I am inclined to believe the chapter's skeptical conclusion, but I do not know enough to assess the reasoning. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what is accurate, what is a simplification or conflation, and what a careful defender of reflective equilibrium would reject, and then say whether the skeptical conclusion survives the corrections.
