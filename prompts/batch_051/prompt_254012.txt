I recently read a piece by a political theorist that summarises the communitarian critique of liberalism, and I want to know how much of it I can trust. The author writes, roughly:

'Communitarians often reach back to Aristotle, who held that the polis is prior to the individual. In the Politics (I.2, 1253a) he says: "For as man is the best of the animals when perfected, so he is the worst of all when sundered from law and justice. For unrighteousness is most pernicious when possessed of weapons, and man is born possessing weapons for the use of wisdom and virtue, which it is possible to employ entirely for the opposite ends." On this view, a person outside political community either cannot be fully human or becomes morally monstrous. That is why communitarians reject the liberal idea that individuals are bearers of rights independently of any community. The author then concludes that communitarians are committed to claiming that liberal neutrality is incoherent: if the state is prior to the individual, no state can stand back from rival conceptions of the good.'

How fair is this as a summary of either Aristotle or the communitarian position? Go sentence by sentence and tell me what a political theorist working in this debate would accept, what they would wince at, and what is just wrong. In particular, does the quoted Aristotle passage support the inference the author draws from it? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Please write in British English.
