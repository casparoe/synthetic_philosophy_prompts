Ok so this came up in adult Sunday school. We're in Romans 9, stuck on what Paul means about election, and the conversation starts sliding toward the usual "well, then who's to say which of us is right?" Mark—he's the perspicuity guy—cuts in with "Scripture interprets itself." His move was: you don't need a church or a scholar or a fancy hermeneutic to settle it; the Bible is self-interpreting, so the unclear passages get read in light of the clear ones and the text resolves its own ambiguities. I've heard the line a lot in Reformed-ish circles, and it's clearly doing real argumentative work: it's supposed to stop the regress of interpretation dead.

The more I try to cash it out, though, the less sure I am that it's one claim rather than a grab-bag. I want to tabboo the phrase and see what's actually under it. What are the distinct precise theses "Scripture interprets itself" could be making? For each, mark whether it's trvial, substantive but defensible, or flat-out false, and say whether it actually gets Mark his conclusion—that no external interpretive authority is needed and the regress ("who decides what the text means?") dissolves. I'd also like at least one reading where the slogan just restates the problem, and one where it's quietly smuggling in the conclusion. Which is the weakest reading that still does the job he needed it for? Give it as a menu of readings, not a defense of the one you think he meant.
