I'm a novelist. The book I'm drafting centers on a retired ultramarathoner who now coaches a high-school crew team, and I keep catching myself giving every character the same unexamined default about effort: hard work is good, struggle is meaningful, quitting is bad. I want my people to differ, but I want the differences to be real joints in how someone could think, not just personality noise. So I'd like you to build me a sorting quiz: "which theory of effort does this character actually hold?" It would be for me to run on my characters (and later maybe on readers), so every question has to work when answered on behalf of a fictional person who knows no academic philosophy.

Make it eight questions, each a concrete little scene, with two or three answer options each. No question should just ask whether effort is valuable in itself. The whole point is to sort by what the person says about cases, so write the cases to pull apart positions that usually blur together. At minimum these leaves should come out: effort as pure cost (only results count); effort as part of what makes an achievement worth having; effort as grounds of desert; effort as training or excellence of character even in failure; struggle sanctified as good in itself; a flow/anti-effort view where felt effort is a sign something is wrong; and the luck-egalitarian critique where the capacity to sustain effort is itself an unchosen lottery. If a costly-signal view (trying hard is valuable mostly because other people see it and feel cared for) earns its place, add it, but not as a forced extra.

Build from the diagnostic axes first, not by reverse-engineering the labels: what questions or dimensions actually split these views, then where the positions sit. Include a note on combinations of answers on the axes that no live position occupies, and whether each is empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Flag any two positions the eight questions can't actually seperate and say what a ninth question would need to do to split them. Let a character profile come out as a primary position plus a runner-up; most people are mixed.

For the deliverable, give me JSON rather than prose, with this schema:

- `title`: string.
- `diagnostic_axes`: array of `{axis, what_it_separates}`.
- `questions`: array of `{id, case, options: [{id, text}]}`.
- `scoring_key`: array of rows `{option_id, weights: {position_id: number}}`.
- `positions`: array of `{id, name, one_sentence_blurb, telltale_pattern, awkward_commitment, can_be_confused_with}`.
- `blank_regions`: array of `{axis_values, diagnosis}`.
- `inseparable_pairs`: array of `{positions: [position_id, position_id], what_would_split_them}`.
