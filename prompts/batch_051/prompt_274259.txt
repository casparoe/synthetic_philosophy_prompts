Write the desiderata for any adequate account of where the burden of proof lies in disagreements about the existence of God. I mean "burden of proof" as an epistemic norm governing who must produce arguments, evidence, or reasons before rational inquirers may treat their position as the default in a debate—not merely as a rhetorical convention or a rule of thumb for public policy. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and say whether it is non-negotiable or could be traded off against another requirement. Cover at least the following: the asymmetry, if any, between affirming a proposition and denying it; the difference between lacking a belief, suspending judgment, and believing the negation; whether a default position can be inherited from something like epistemic conservatism, doxastic innocence, or the mere absence of positive reasons; and how the account should handle agnosticism understood as a positive thesis about the limits of our cognitive capacities rather than as a personal failure to decide. Then briefly score the view that atheism is the default because it is merely the absence of theistic belief against your list, and identify which single requirement does the most to exclude that view—and which pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied.
