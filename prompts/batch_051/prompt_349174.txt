I'm a PhD student in philosophy of math and I've been trying to get my views on the continuum to cohere for a chapter I'm drafting, but I'm to close to it to see where the load-bearing claim is. Here's the package I keep defending, depending on which paper I'm reading that week:

(1) I'm a structuralist about mathematical objects in general: numbers have no identity beyond their positions in a structure.
(2) I think the reals are defined, not discovered. Dedekind cuts and Cauchy-sequence completions are just two ways of building a complete ordered field, and the continuum has no content over and above whatever that specification pins down.
(3) I think second-order categoricity is doing real work here: because any two complete ordered fields are isomorphic, (2) shouldn't make the reals fuzzy—the definition gives us one structure up to isomorphism.
(4) I take the full power set of the naturals as a definite totality. Talk of "arbitrary subsets of N" is meaningful, and classical analysis is committed to that.
(5) I think the continuum hypothesis has no determinate truth value—it's one of those questions that only gets an answer relative to a set-theoretic universe, and no principled "new axiom" project will settle what it is about.
