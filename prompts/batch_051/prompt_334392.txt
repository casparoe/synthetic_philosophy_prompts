The thesis I'm defending is roughly: if you already grant that personal identity is constituted by the brain's physical and functional organization rather than by a soul or by bare biological continuity, then signing up for cryonics through a life-insurance policy is a rational bet even at a revival probability around one in a few hundred; refusing to sign up is mostly status quo bias dressed up as an expected-value argument.

For Q&A prep, give me the eight or nine objections I'll actual get from this audience, not the LessWrong-flavored ones. Order them by how likely and how damaging they are, phrase them the way these particular philosophers would (use their jargon), and for each give me a two-minute reply plus a note on where the reply is soft. The two I'm dreading are: the personal-identity person asking why the revived person isn't just a duplicate of me, and the decision theorist pressing that I'm not entitled to a precise credence like one-in-a-few-hundred about such a weird, one-off event. Also say what I should concede up front rather than trying to defend, and include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
