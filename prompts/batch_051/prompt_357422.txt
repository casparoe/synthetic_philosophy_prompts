Propose a working definition of 'constitutional interregnum,' as it bears on the question of who governs when the rules run out, and test it against the following situations, revising it until you can sort them in a way you can defend: (a) a king dies and the old order states that the crown passes at once to the heir, but the heir has not yet been proclaimed and the capital is in confusion; (b) a president and vice-president die in the same accident, the constitution is silent on succession, and the speaker of the legislature claims power; (c) after an election with no majority, a caretaker cabinet holds office under a clause permitting it only routine current business; (d) a president is briefly incapacitated under general anaesthesia and the vice-president acts under an explicit succession article; (e) a coup suspends the constitution and military officers rule by decree, claiming they are merely holding order until elections; (f) a territory becomes independent and is governed provisionally before any constitution is adopted; (g) an incumbent president's term expires, the election result is disputed, and the incumbent stays in office while the courts and legislature are deadlocked. Say whether the term merely reports an absence in the constituted order or already carries a normative judgment that whoever acts must justify, identify the cases on which that distinction turns, and state which of your classifications is least secure.
