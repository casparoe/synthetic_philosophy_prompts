I want to actually write X down. Give me the desiderata for a theory of commemoration in public space — statues, monuments, and street names — good enough for a city council that has to decide something rather than just be right in the abstract. I need the full requirements list: the cases it has to get right and the case or consideration behind each, the distinctions it can't collapse (like between not celebrating someone and erasing that they existed, between a statue and a street name used for navigation, between judging the person and judging the act of honoring them), and the uncontroversial facts about how cities and memory actually work that it shouldn't contradict. The list should at least cover: mixed-legacy figures (Colston, Rhodes, Confederate generals, also local nobodies like my hospital guy), statues put up decades after death by people with an obvious political agenda, neighborhoods that never consented to being named after someone, the fact that almost everyone looks worse the closer you look, and the range of options — plaque, removal, renaming, keeping-with-a-counter-monument, leave-alone — as something the theory itself has to justify distinguishing. For each requirement say whether it's non-negotiable or negotiable, then tell me which single requirement knocks out the most popular positions people actually hold, with maybe a one-line score of the usual remove-all / leave-all / contextualize camps.
