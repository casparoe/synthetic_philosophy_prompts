I am a high school student, and I have been trying to figure out what I actually think about when a piece of music is itself sad, as opposed to merely being associated with sadness or making me feel sad. I have collected my gut reactions to a set of cases, and I would like to know whether there is a single principle behind them, or close to one.

Here are the cases.

1. A slow, quiet string melody in a minor key built from falling phrases. This is the clearest case: the music itself is sad, and it would still be sad even if I learned it had been written as a dry technical exercise with no sad story behind it.
2. A single minor chord struck once on a piano and held. I would not call the chord itself sad; it is dark or heavy, but there is no sadness in it yet.
3. A recording of a real person sobbing. The sound is unmistakably sad, yet I would not call it sad music; it is not music in the relevant sense, and its sadness feels like a different kind of fact.
4. A ballad in which the singer deliberately stylizes sobs into the melody. The music does sound sad, even though the sobbing is plainly performed rather than spontaneous.
5. The same melody as in case 1, transposed into a major key and played at a walking tempo. It no longer sounds sad to me; it sounds nostalgic or content.
6. A fast, bouncy, staccato tune written in a minor key. The key alone does not make it sad; at most it feels tense or mischievous.
7. A song whose lyrics recount a devastating loss, set to an upbeat, danceable accompaniment. I want to say the song as a whole is sad because of the words, but the music on its own is not sad.
8. A descending, wailing violin line that resembles crying. This is sad music.
9. A fire alarm with a descending wail whose pitch contour resembles the violin line in case 8. It is not sad music, even though the contour is similar.

I keep going back and forth on a further case. A computer program generates a melody by tracing the average pitch and rhythm contour of people speaking while severely depressed. I find the result oddly sad; a friend says it is no more sad music than a printed spectrogram of a sad voice. Is there a principle that captures all nine verdicts, or nearly all of them, and what does it predict about the generated melody? If no one principle fits, which of my verdicts must I abandon, and what does the cleanest remaining principle look like? Please state the principle precisely, say which case motivates each of its clauses, give verdicts on cases I have not listed, and be explicit about which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate.
