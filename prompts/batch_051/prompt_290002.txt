I am a journalist covering constitutional and political-theory debates for a magazine of ideas. A national constitutional commission is considering an amendment that would abolish political parties as legal electoral entities: no candidate could stand under a party label, no parliamentary group could operate with party whips, and any organisation that coordinated candidate endorsements or campaign financing across constituencies would be classified as an unlawful electoral conspiracy. The commissioners explicitly invoke Simone Weil's essay On the Abolition of All Political Parties, and in particular a passage in which she argues, as I recall it, that a party is not a vehicle for expressing pre-existing convictions but a machine for manufacturing a collective passion stronger than individual judgement, so that once the party exists its members' first loyalty is to the party's survival and growth rather than to truth or justice. I am paraphrasing from the essay and do not have the exact wording to hand. The commission's sponsors take this passage to show that parties are structurally incompatible with honest political deliberation and therefore cannot be tamed by regulation, only abolished. I would like you to analyse how such an abolition would actually behave in practice, rather than assess whether Weil's argument is sound as a piece of political philosophy. What would happen to the formation of stable legislative majorities? How would voters, lacking party labels as a heuristic, infer what a candidate would do in office? What sorts of informal factions, media blocs, or donor networks would emerge to fill the vacuum, and would they be worse than the parties they replaced? Would the ban simply push party-like coordination underground, and if so, which observable features of political life would change? Are there less drastic mechanisms, such as banning whipped voting or party discipline rather than party labels, or funding independent candidacies, that could preserve the benefits the commissioners claim to want while avoiding the more predictable failure modes? Please write your analysis in British English.
