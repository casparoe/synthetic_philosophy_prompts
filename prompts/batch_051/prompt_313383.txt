weve been going back and forth on the forum about the dark room objection to predictive processing, and i want a sonnet that actually argues it instead of gesturing at it. octave: set up the theory cleanly — the brain as a hierarchical generative model, perception as top-down prediction corrected by bottom-up error, all of it in service of minimizing variational free energy, a bound on surprise. the volta has to be the objection: if surprise is the enemy, than the best life is the dark room, no sensory input, no prediction error, just homeostasis in the quiet. the sestet has to be the actual reply, the one Clark and Friston give in their different ways but the poem has to merge them: action is the same minimization run the other direction, expected free energy not instantaneous surprise, and the dark room is brutally surprising for a system with priors on being a living body in a world. make the turn land hard; someone who knows active inference should nod at the sestet, and someone who doesnt should still get a real poem, not a lecture with line breaks.
