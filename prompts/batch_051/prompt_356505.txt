I have been working on a small argument in the philosophy of time, and I would be grateful for an honest assessment of the principle at its center. Standard statements of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument observe that two observers in relative motion, momentarily coincident, will disagree about which distant events are simultaneous with their meeting: an event that one regards as present the other regards as future. The usual conclusion is that the future must already be real, because reality cannot be relative to a frame of reference.

I want to defend a stronger form of the argument by isolating the principle that seems to me to do the real work. Call it the Transitivity of Coexistence, or TC. Let x, y, and z be events, and write x ~ y to mean: there exists some inertial frame in which x and y are simultaneous. TC says that ~ is transitive: if x ~ y and y ~ z, then x ~ z. This seems to me almost a truth of logic, because the middle term y functions like the shared item in a chain of identities. If y is present with x, and y is present with z, there is no coherent way to place y that leaves x and z on different sides of the present. If TC holds, the Rietdijk–Putnam conclusion can be iterated: starting from my here-now, any event reachable by a finite chain of co-present events is real, which I take to be the whole of Minkowski spacetime. That would give a considerably cleaner route to the block universe than the usual two-observer argument, and it would also answer the indexical reply that "now" is merely like "here," since TC concerns the underlying relation rather than any particular localization of it.

I am aware that simultaneity itself is not transitive across frames; three events can fail to be simultaneous in any single frame. But I am not claiming that x is simultaneous with z in the same frame in which either pair is simultaneous. I am only claiming that an appropriate frame exists.

What I want to know is this. First, on the reading just defined, is TC true or false? If it is false, give the cleanest counterexample in 1+1-dimensional Minkowski spacetime, and identify exactly where the "shared middle term" reasoning goes wrong. Second, if TC is restricted or reinterpreted so that it comes out true, does it still yield the iterated Rietdijk–Putnam conclusion, or does it become a triviality that no presentist would resist? Third, does my claim that the iterated relation collapses all of spacetime into a single real block overstep what even a B-theorist should say, or is that simply what B-theorists are already committed to?
