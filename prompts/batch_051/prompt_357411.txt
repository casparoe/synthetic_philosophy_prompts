In a graduate seminar on political violence, I got into a disagreement about whether a particular non-state group's attack on a crowded government building should be classified as terrorism or as an act of legitimate armed resistance. A classmate ended the exchange by saying, "One man's terrorist is another man's freedom fighter." His intended upshot seemed to be that the word terrorism just picks out whichever side the speaker opposes, so our disagreement had no factual substance, and trying to settle whether the attack was really terrorism was a waste of time.

That sentence is surely doing too much work, but I also suspect there is a real insight buried in it: the labels do seem to track allegiances more reliably than they track features of the violence itself, and a state that bombs a city square is rarely called terrorist while a militia that does the same thing is. I want to know what the slogan could actually be claiming. Please lay out the distinct precise theses someone might be asserting with it — for example, claims about how the word is used, about the distribution of the label across states and non-state groups, about the moral symmetry of the two sides, about whether a neutral definition is possible, or anything else you see — as a menu. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or false; where a reading is genuinely contested among reasonable people, say so rather than forcing a verdict. Then tell me which reading or readings would actually support the conclusion that the classification dispute is merely perspectival, and whether the step from that reading to my classmate's conclusion is valid.

One caveat: if this request is itself confused — if the slogan is better understood as a rhetorical move rather than as a claim, or if it cannot be precisified until we have already answered the very definitional question it is being used to dismiss — I would rather you say that plainly than produce a tidy menu.
