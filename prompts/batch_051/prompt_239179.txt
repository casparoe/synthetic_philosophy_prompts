I am a researcher in reinforcement learning and robust control. My working vocabulary includes policies, environments, reward functions, transition models, uncertainty sets, adversarial or worst-case environments, minimax and minimax-regret objectives, domain randomisation, and priors over environment parameters that get updated to posteriors. I know almost no moral or political philosophy.

I keep hearing the Rawls–Harsanyi debate summarised in my own terms: Rawls behind the veil of ignorance is said to be doing maximin — choosing the social arrangement whose worst possible outcome, for the person one might turn out to be, is best; Harsanyi is said to be doing Bayesian expected utility over an equal-probability assignment to every person's position; and there is also a minimax-regret proposal in the vicinity, which chooses the arrangement that minimises the worst shortfall from what would have been best for each possible person. I would like this mapping checked rather than assumed.

Restate these three positions — Rawls's original position and maximin rule, Harsanyi's impartial-observer argument for expected utility, and the minimax-regret criterion for choice under ignorance — in my vocabulary, as three different objective functions that a single planner could optimise when selecting a social policy without knowing which person it will become. Be precise enough that I could implement each of them in code.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the Rawls–Harsanyi dispute translate cleanly into my vocabulary, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart at all? In particular, tell me whether the disagreement is really just robust control versus Bayesian inference under a uniform prior, and what becomes of the separateness of persons, the difference between uncertainty and fairness, the agent-relativity of risk, and the distinction between a society of many distinct persons and a single agent uncertain about its own reward function. For each piece of residue, say whether it exposes a limitation in my vocabulary or a place where the robust-versus-Bayesian reading of the debate is too quick.

Please write the answer in British English.
