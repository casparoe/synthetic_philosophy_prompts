I'm working through Woodward's interventionist account of causation, and I keep losing my footing on the difference between the manipulation condition and the invariance condition. On the usual gloss, the manipulation condition says that X counts as a type-level cause of Y only if there is some possible intervention on X that changes Y or its probability distribution; the invariance condition says the generalization connecting X and Y must remain stable under a range of interventions on X, not just produce a change at a single isolated setting. I want a case built to specification in which these conditions come apart in the direction I find hardest to construct: the manipulation condition is satisfied, but the invariance condition is not, so that a Woodward-style interventionist should withhold the type-level verdict that X causes Y even though an admissible-looking intervention on X changes Y on the occasion in question. Make it an ordinary, realistic case — a medical, engineering, or social intervention is fine — with no demons, no perfect predictors, and no wildly improbable coincidences. The scenario itself should be at most a paragraph, and it should generate the verdicts from the structure of the variables rather than stipulating them.
