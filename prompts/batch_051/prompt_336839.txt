I've been trying to get my head around the whole carbon tax versus regulation versus degrowth argument, and I keep coming back to the yellow vests thing in France. In 2018 Macron's government raised fuel taxes to try to cut emissions, and it blew up into this huge protest movement of people who needed their cars to get to work and couldn't absorb the extra cost, and in the end the government backed down and scrapped it. I get the standard economist story: a carbon tax is the cheapest way to cut emissions because it lets everyone decide for themselves what to give up, and you can hand the money back as a dividend so most people come out okay. But the yellow vests really dont fit that story, or at least the story seems to miss why it blew up. What I want is a careful breakdown of the episode through the three lenses. Who were the actual players and what did they want? What would the carbon-tax camp, the regulation camp, and the degrowth camp each say should have been done, before and after the protests? Then tell me what the carbon tax model gets wrong about why it failed: is it just that the rebate was botched or never convincing, or is there something deeper about how people experience fuel prices, trust, and being told to change? And does the episode actually count as evidence for regulation, or for the degrowth people who say you have to plan for less driving rather than just price it? Don't give me a history paper; use the episode to stress-test each of the three positions.
