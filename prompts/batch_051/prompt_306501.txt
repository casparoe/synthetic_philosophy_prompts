I have a policy in my own moral reasoning: I treat my judgments about concrete cases as fixed unless I can point to a recognized bias or distorting condition, and I treat abstract judgments and principles as up for grabs. So if a principle fits most of my concrete case judgments but clashes with some abstract judgment I hold—say about what a person deserves—I revise the abstract one rather than the case ones, and if two case judgments conflict I keep the one formed in a calm, concrete, non-hypothetical situation and drop the more abstract one. This is supposed to be reflective equilibrium, but it's a version where I only ever revise one class of judgments, and I stop as soon as the whole set feels coherent to me. Is this a defensible version of the method, or have I rigged it so it always outputs the moral views I started with? How would I tell from the inside whether I'm doing reflective equilibrium rather than just protecting my starting points?
