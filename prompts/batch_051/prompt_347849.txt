I'm putting together a decision tree for an upper-level undergraduate metaphysics unit on whether reality has a fundamental level, and specifically on whether the one is grounded in the many or the many in the one. The tree should sort students by the claims they accept rather than by the labels they like. The leaves must include at least: priority monism, atomistic metaphysical foundationalism, infinitism about ground (no bottom level, because every ground is itself grounded without loops), coherentism about ground (some chains of ground loop), and a deflationary view on which talk of being more fundamental is at best a bookkeeping device. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from the names: branch on claims a student could assess before learning any position, such as whether grounding talk tracks a real relation; whether every fact has a ground or some facts are ungrounded; whether a chain of grounds may loop; and, for those who accept ungrounded facts, whether those facts belong at the level of the smallest parts or at the level of the whole cosmos. For each combination of answers, state which named view occupies it, and for each empty combination say whether no one is there because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Finally, flag any two leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate, and supply one further diagnostic question that would separate them.
