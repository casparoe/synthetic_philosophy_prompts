I'm drafting the position statement for a dissertation chapter on reduction and emergence, and before I circulate it I'd like an audit of whether my commitments actually cohere. Here is the set of views I hold, each of which I find individually plausible:

(1) The physical realm is causally closed: every physical event has a complete physical cause, and we never need to invoke non-physical factors to account for physical goings-on.

(2) Nothing in nature is strongly emergent: higher-level properties and patterns are, in the end, nothing over and above the physical facts that realize them, and they do not add novel causal powers that push the microphysical world around.

(3) Yet special-science explanations—why a bank run unfolded, why an allele frequency shifted in a population, why a firm changed its investment strategy—are autonomously explanatory. They state genuine higher-level causal or explanatory generalizations whose point would be lost if we translated them into microphysical terms; the microphysical story would miss what does the explaining.

(4) Mental states such as beliefs and desires genuinely cause our actions.

(5) Psychological and biological kinds are multiply realizable: the same kind can be realized by indefinitely many distinct physical configurations, so these kinds cannot be reduced to or identified with physical kinds.

(6) Higher-level patterns are objective features of the world—real patterns in populations, markets, and organisms—not merely convenient fictions or artifacts of our epistemic limitations.

I do not see the tensions in this set, if there are any. Please go through it and give me an audit: which pairs of commitments pull against one another and how severely, which pairs are only apparently in tension and why, and what the cheapest revision would be to restore joint tenability. For each genuine tension, please say exactly which commitment would have to be weakened or given up and what that would cost me elsewhere, and then tell me whether there is a single revision that would clean up the greatest number of problems at once.
