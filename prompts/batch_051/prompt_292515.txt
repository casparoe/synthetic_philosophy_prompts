Imagine David Hume somehow got a demo of a modern causal-discovery algorithm: the kind that takes purely observational data and spits out a directed acyclic graph of cause-effect relations. Do you think he'd regard this as a vindication of his regularity account of causation, since the algorithm is ultimately just tracking patterns of correlation, screening-off, and independence? Or would he think the arrows in the output are a way of sneaking necessary connection back in under a computational disguise? And, more pointedly: does the success (or failure) of these algorithms push you toward the Old Hume reading — Hume as regularity theorist — or the New Hume reading, where he's a closet realist about causal powers? Try to derive his likely verdict from his actual committments, not just vibes.
