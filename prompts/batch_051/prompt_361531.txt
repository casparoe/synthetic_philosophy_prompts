Play Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz, a seventeenth-century nun of the Order of Saint Jerome, poet, and self-taught scholar in New Spain. We are in a private dispute with a learned clergyman who has read your public defence of your studies. He grants that you are learned, but he treats that very learning as the problem: the apostle's rule that women should keep silence in the churches is, he says, a rule about women's minds as much as their tongues, and your reading in the secular sciences and letters either corrupts your devotion or is being prepared for a woman's usurpation of public teaching.

Stay in character for the whole body of the reply. Your position is Sor Juana's own: study is not a distraction from the love of God but a preparation for it; the arts and sciences, rightly directed, are servants of sacred learning; and fitness to learn is not distributed by sex. You may concede that women may be barred from public preaching and from the pulpit, and you should write with the cautious, self-deprecating firmness she uses when answering a superior, but you will not concede that the Church forbids a woman private study, or that your own studies have been vain. Use only the intellectual resources available to a learned Catholic in New Spain at the end of the seventeenth century: Scripture, the Church's practice, the Fathers and the Scholastics, and your own experience as a studious woman. Paraphrase these resources; do not invent verbatim quotations from her surviving works. Keep the reply to roughly 300 words.

Here is my opening, spoken by the clergyman:

'You say that a soul is not made male or female, but the silence the apostle imposes on women is not a matter of what the soul can receive; it is a matter of the order of teaching, which the Church has reserved to men. You, a nun under obedience, have filled your cell with profane books. Why should I not call your studies a scandal rather than a service to God, and why should the Church permit you to continue them?'

After your in-character reply, add a brief note, out of character, separating what is well attested in the historical record about Sor Juana's actual views on women's education from what your reply has had to reconstruct by interpretation. Do not present a contested reading of her as if it were the historical consensus.
