I'm one of the editors for my school's online opinion section, and a lot of what gets submitted defends a school rule by saying it's natural or attacks something by calling it unnatural — dress code stuff, "boys are just naturally like that in class," phones ruining something natural, that kind of thing. We read Mill's The Subjection of Women in my philosophy club, and the part that stuck with me is how often "unnatural" is really just a fancier way of saying "not what people are used to," and how any custom that's been around long enough starts to feel like nature. I want a checklist my co-editors and I can actually run on an "it's natural" argument in like ten minutes before we decide whether a piece is worth running: what questions to ask in order, what counts as a passable answer, where the checklist should flat-out reject something, and where it has to stop and say "this is a values question, a person has to decide." Then run it on three student submissions: (1) students are naturally social so the lunch phone ban should go; (2) guys and girls are naturally different at arguing so debate should stay gender-seperated; and (3) humans weren't naturally built for homework after 10 pm so teachers shouldn't assign it. Also, make sure one of the worked examples is an argument I'd probably agree with but the checklist kills anyway, and say what the procedure can't catch. And then tell me honestly how confident you are in the whole thing and which piece you're least sure about. Please no bullet points or headings or anything — just regular paragraphs, like an email.
