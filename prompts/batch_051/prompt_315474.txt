i've been toying with something i call the finite palette view. the claim is: for any particular human mind there is a finite set of basic experiential atoms — the smallest qualitatively distinct flavors of seeing, feeling, wanting, remembering, bodily sensation, whatever — and at some point fairly early in life you have already had all of them. everything after that point is recombination. the way every sentence you will ever read is built from the same small alphabet, so you can get an endless number of new sentences without ever getting a new letter. i want to say life after about 25 is just new arrangements of a finished palette, and that this, not lost energy or responsibilities, is why adolescence feels so much more vivid. i also suspect it implies something mildly sad about a long life: you can get more instances of the existing kinds of value, but no genuinely new kinds.

is this idea trivially true, meaningfully false, or interesting and salvageable? has someone already got this under a better name and i've just rebuilt it badly? where exactly does it break — is the trouble with the very idea of an 'atom of experience'? give me the strongest version you can and then tell me what it still can't capture.
