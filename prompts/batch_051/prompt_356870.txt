I'm an undergrad taking a seminar on constitutional theory this term (we just did Marbury and a pile of readings on the counter-majoritarian difficulty), and I also serve as one of the appointed members of my university's Constitutional Review Board. The Board is appointed, not elected; the Student Assembly is elected. Last week the Assembly passed Bylaw 14, which says any future Review Board decision is only "advisory" unless the Assembly votes within ten days to adopt it. A student group has petitioned us to strike Bylaw 14 down. Our student constitution says the Board has "final authority to interpret and enforce" the constitution, but it also has an amendment procedure requiring a two-thirds vote plus a referendum, and the Assembly passed this as an ordinary bylaw by a simple majority. So there's a threshold issue about whether Bylaw 14 is a disguised constitutional amendment. The constitution is silent on whether the Board can hear challenges to its own jurisdiction, and half the board says we shouldn't touch it because its unseemly for an unelected body to protect its own power; the other half says declining to hear it is exactly how the review power dies. This feels like the course's whole question — should unelected judges overrule legislatures — except here the legislature wrote a law about the judges.

I've been asked to write the decision memo we vote on Monday evening. Act as an expert on constitutional theory and the counter-majoritarian difficulty. Before you write the actual memo, brainstorm the two or three strongest arguments on each side, and only then draft the memo. The memo needs a clear bottom line — uphold Bylaw 14, strike it down, or uphold it with specific conditions — and it has to engage the self-dealing worry as a live argument rather than waving it off.

One more thing: I'll have to defend this in seminar, so either in the memo or in a short note after it, seperate what's genuinely settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on judicial review, and don't present a contested position as if it were the consensus. Don't pad it with citations or obscure cases I can't check; just reason from the facts and the theory.
