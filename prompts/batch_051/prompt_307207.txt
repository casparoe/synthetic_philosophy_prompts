I cover science and ideas for a living, and there's a recurring argument I can't seem to escape: whether Sweden or Norway is "more liberal." I hear it among expats, in comment sections, occasionally on panels, and it never moves an inch. It's not because there's no data. The Sweden side points to earlier secularization, a more permissive public culture around gender and family forms, and a refugee intake that was historically more open. The Norway side points to the oil fund as a near-socialist institution of collective ownership, tight labour protections, and a political culture that wasn't as eager for market-style reform. Both sides can pull out an index that ranks their pick higher on something. Then they accuse each other of cherry-picking, and the whole thing resets.

My working theory is that "liberal" is doing too many jobs at once — economic liberalism, social permissiveness, civil liberties, welfare universalism — and the two sides are really comparing different dimensions under one word, so it looks like an empirical question but is secretly a fight about which dimension matters. But I can't tell whether thats right, or whether it's a badly specified factual disagreement, or just a proxy for wich flavour of social democracy people prefer.

So diagnose this one. What kind of disagreement is it, exactly — and support the diagnosis with what each side actually says, not a survey of Nordic politics. Given your diagnosis, what would count as settling it: a dataset, a definition, a time horizon, an agreement to stop asking? Be honest about whether the question is even well-posed, and if you think nothing would ever settle it, say why.
