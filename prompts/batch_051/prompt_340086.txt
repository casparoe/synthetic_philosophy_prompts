I'm putting together a one-year grant proposal on corrigibility and I need something that sounds like a real research plan rather then the usual rehash of the off-switch problem. My half-formed idea: everyone frames corrigibility as a property of the agent's preferences over world-histories, specifically the agent has to be roughly indifferent between 'I finish the task' and 'I get shut down mid-task.' But that seems to conflate two different things: not resisting correction, and not minding bad outcomes. A corrigible agent should let the operator shut it down, but it shouldn't be indifferent to the shutdown button being pressed by accident, or to a failure that just happens to look like shutdown. An agent fully indifferent to its off-switch would, as far as I can tell, leave the button where a breeze can press it, or steer toward whatever path lets it stop acting sooner. So the question I want funded is roughly: can you get shutdown-passivity without shutdown-indifference, by making the shutdown signal an input the agent treats as outside the scope of its world-model instead of an outcome it assigns utility to? Turn this into a two-page proposal: state the question precisely, say why existing indifference-based approaches leave it open, make the case that this is conceptual/formal work worth funding rather than just another benchmark, give the method month by month (I'm imagining toy gridworlds and hand-specified utility functions), say what success looks like and what would count as the approach just failing, and keep it scoped to one year.
