After a long argument with a friend about whether the current enthusiasm for grit, fortitude, and resilience is best understood as a revival of the virtues or as an ideology, he offered what he called a fair statement of the skeptical side: "The research began as empirical psychology about why some people persist, but it has become a moralizing discourse that blames the disadvantaged for not being tough enough. Its core scientific claim is weak: grit predicts only a few outcomes, modestly, largely because it is conscientiousness relabeled, and once conscientiousness is controlled for it adds almost nothing. Everything else is instruction to reinterpret hardship as an opportunity for growth, which conveniently spares institutions and structures from scrutiny." I pushed back during the argument, but I am not sure where his summary is accurate, where it overstates, and what a careful defender of the literature would concede or reject. Could you audit it claim by claim? I especially want to know whether the ideological conclusion survives if the empirical criticisms are granted, and which part of his summary is doing the real argumentative work.
