Had a long argument with a friend last night about a version of the Platonia dilemma / Luring Lottery he wants to build as an app, and now I want the mechanism itself analyzed, not whether it's a good idea or an ethical one.

The app: once a month, anyone with a phone number can enter for free. The pot starts at $10,000 and doubles every month it isn't won; if it is won it resets to $10,000. On the last day of the month, for sixty seconds, every entrant gets one button that says Claim. If exactly one person presses it, they get the whole pot. If two or more press it, nobody gets the pot, every person who pressed it is charged a $20 penalty, and the pot rolls over and doubles for next month. If nobody presses it, the pot also rolls over and doubles. Before the window opens, entrants can see the current pot size and the total number of entrants, but not who has pressed or how many have pressed; after the window closes, the number of claims is public. There's a pseudonymous chat for entrants, multi-accounting is banned but only enforced via SMS and device fingerprints, and the company keeps the penalties and pays the pot out of its own reserves.

Assume risk-neutral, self-interested players. My friend says the thing will degenerate into everyone claiming every month and nobody ever winning; I pointed out that if everyone else claims, you're better off not pressing, but he says the pot doubling makes it weirder. How does this actually play out? Give your best guess at the claiming distribution month to month, whether it ever pays out, how people would game it (side-payments, fake accounts, coordination attempts, the company's incentives), and which failure modes are fixable. Lead with the bottom line in your first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, no restating the question.
