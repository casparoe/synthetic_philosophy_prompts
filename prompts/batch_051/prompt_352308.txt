Me and my friend just spent three hours arguing about whether memes are actual replicators or just a metaphor, and I walked away with a distinction I want checked before I start using it everywhere.

I'd seperate cultural things into copy-memes and cue-memes. Copy-memes spread because the token itself gets copied: a chorus melody, a chain-letter formula, a punchline where the wording is the whole joke. Change the surface form and it stops being that meme. Cue-memes spread because the same stable input keeps re-triggering the same idea in each new head, so it looks like copying but each person reconstructs it independently. The earth orbiting the sun, the seasons being caused by axial tilt, the Pythagorean theorem: nobody needs the original sentence, and if all the wordings mutated the idea would still show up.

My claim is that only copy-memes satisfy the fidelity condition Dawkins needs, so cue-memes arent replicators at all — they're more like a recurring reinfection from a stable environment than descent. And here's the part I actually care about: cumulative cultural evolution should only need copy-memes; cue-memes can stick around forever without anything piling up, so they shouldn't be explained by the same theory.

My friend says I've just reinvented Sperber's cultural attractors and that Dawkins never required verbatim copying anyway. I think he's dodging, but the argument got too heated for me to be sure.

So: is the distinction real, or am I carving a joint that isn't there? Has someone already made this exact version with the reinfection point? Give me the clearest case that misclassifies, and tell me whether the kicker about cumulative evolution actually follows or if I smuggled it in.
