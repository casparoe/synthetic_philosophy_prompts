Ok so I'm in a seminar on the ethics of partiality and we read MacIntyre's Lindley Lecture, *Is Patriotism a Virtue?*, and it left me frustrated. His argument, as I understand it, is roughly this: if morality is understood as requiring an impersonal, impartial standpoint, then patriotism can't be a virtue because it means loyalty to one's own nation's particular projects even when they conflict with what the neutral standpoint would require. But if you instead ground morality in the particularity of a community and its shared goods, then patriotism looks central but you've seemingly given up on the idea of a universal morality. The lecture ends in a dilemma rather than a full account, and my classmates have just been arguing about whether his dilemma is even real.

What I want, before I read any more theories, is the specification. What does any adequate theory of the moral status of patriotism have to get right? I want the desiderata: the cases it must handle, the distinctions it can't collapse, the features of real patriotic attachment it cant just idealize away, and the normative constraints it has to respect. For instance, it must distinguish patriotism from nationalism, and probably from mere preference for your own country's interests. It has to handle both loyalty to a country with a decent regime and loyalty to a country whose regime you oppose (exiles, dissidents, internal critics). It has to say something about whether feeling proud of something you didn't do is even coherent, and whether love of country is more like love of a person or love of a place. And it can't simply assume that partial loyalty to a nation is morally on a par with partiality toward a friend. Tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's prejudice, and flag any pair that you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. I especially want to know which single requirement knocks out the most popular theories, including MacIntyre's own communitarian leanings. A table or a bulleted list is fine, but give the reasoning behind each requirement.
