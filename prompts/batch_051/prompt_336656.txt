For the past several years I have worked as a software engineer on the reputation and ranking features of a discussion platform, and I keep encountering a configuration I do not have a precise term for. We give each user a single public karma score, as these sites generally do, and display it next to everything they post. The score is presented as an output: a summary of how much the community has valued a person's contributions. But in practice it has quietly become an input to its own production.

Our internal tests show that identical content is judged very differently depending on the author's existing score. A thoughtful-but-ordinary post from a high-karma account is read charitably, upvoted quickly, and given the benefit of the doubt in disputes; the same post from a new or low-karma account is more likely to be skipped, read ungenerously, or downvoted. Early bad luck compounds, and moderately good work from established users receives credit that the work itself would not earn. The raters are not cheating or strategically gaming the metric. They seem to be using the score exactly as it was designed to be used, as a shortcut for whether a contribution deserves attention. Yet the score is also the quantity their votes produce, so the system gradually loses the ability to distinguish between the quality it was meant to track and the standing that the score itself confers.

Is there an established name for this precise loop, in which a quality metric becomes a premise in ordinary quality judgments without anyone deliberately optimizing against it? It is more specific than a generic feedback loop, and it does not seem to be a case of gaming or strategic exploitation. I am less interested in a label than in the carving: whether this is one phenomenon or several, what distinguishes it from nearby concepts, what counts as inside versus outside the boundaries, and, if there is no established term, how one should define it. A bare name would not satisfy me; I want to know exactly what the thing is and where it ends.
