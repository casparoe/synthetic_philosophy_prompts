Title: Replication Without Replicators? Reconstructing the Meme as a Unit of Selection

I am planning a short essay on whether talk of memes as cultural replicators is more than a loose metaphor, and I would like critical comments on the argument before I draft it. Below is a précis of the position I intend to defend, followed by the specific points on which I would most value pushback.

The essay's central claim is that debates about memes often conflate two distinct questions: first, whether cultural items are copied with enough fidelity to count as replicators in a gene-like sense, and second, whether cultural change is genuinely a selection process among discrete variants. I want to separate these questions by introducing a distinction between high-fidelity replication and what I call recurrence-with-modification. In high-fidelity replication, error is a rare perturbation around a largely faithful copy, so that most of the information in a token is preserved from one generation to the next. In recurrence-with-modification, by contrast, a token is reconstructed each time from a compressed or gappy representation—a recipe, score, rule, or remembered gist—that underdetermines the surface form. The variation between tokens is therefore not so much copying error as the normal result of underdetermined reconstruction.

My argument is that many stock examples of memes, such as tunes, phrases, craft techniques, and rituals, are primarily cases of recurrence-with-modification rather than high-fidelity replication. What is transmitted is not a copy of a performance or utterance but something more like a reconstruction rule that only probabilistically produces any particular surface form. From this I draw an intermediate conclusion: if we make replication the criterion for being a meme, then very few cultural items qualify. But I then want to resist the further conclusion that selection talk is merely decorative. Selection requires heritable variation in fitness, and recurrence-with-modification can supply heritable variation provided the biases governing reconstruction are themselves transmitted and differ across lineages. The essay therefore ends by proposing that the relevant cultural unit of selection is not the surface variant but the reconstruction rule—a disposition, norm, or generative schema—that produces it.

I would especially appreciate comments on the following:

1. Is the replication/recurrence distinction stable, or does it collapse under the observation that all copying involves some reconstruction and all reconstruction involves some constraints? If every copy is a reconstruction, is "high-fidelity replication" just a limiting case of recurrence-with-modification rather than a different kind of process, and does that matter for the argument?

2. Does my central example do the work I claim? I argue that a tune transmitted via standard notation is reconstructed from a compressed score, and that the score underdetermines timing, ornamentation, and accent, so the performances are not copies of one another. A natural reply is that the meme is then the score, not the performance, and scores can be copied with high fidelity. How damaging is that reply to the distinction I want to draw?

3. Is the constructive move available? I claim that a reconstruction rule can vary, be inherited, and differ in fitness, so selection can operate even when surface tokens are not faithful copies. But is a reconstruction rule the sort of thing that can be individuated and inherited in the way selection requires, or does this reintroduce a gene-like picture through the back door?

4. Does the essay's terminus actually improve on the standard view? If the unit of selection is a reconstruction rule rather than a surface variant, is the word "meme" doing any work beyond what is already captured by talk of norms, skills, or dispositions? In other words, does the account make "meme" more than a metaphor, or does it quietly abandon the metaphor while keeping the word?

5. Are there internal tensions in the argument as summarized, or obvious objections I have not anticipated, that would need to be addressed for the essay to be persuasive to someone who already doubts that cultural variants replicate in any instructive sense?
