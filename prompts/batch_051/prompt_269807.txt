Okay here's an argument I keep going back and forth on, and I can't tell if I've smuggled something in. (1) In Principia Ethica, Moore says the two greatest intrinsic goods are the pleasure of personal affection and the enjoyment of beautiful objects. (2) Now both of those are just states of consciousness. (3) And the intrinsic value of a state of consciousness is fixed entirely by how it feels from the inside. (4) A Nozick-style experience machine can give you states that feel exactly the same from the inside: the same warmth toward your friends, the same delight in what seem to be beautiful paintings and landscapes. (5) If two states of consciousness feel exactly the same from the inside, there is no value-relevant difference between them. (6) So, by Moore's own lights, life in the machine contains just as much of the two greatest goods as real life. I find that conclusion weird, because Moore seems like exactly the kind of person who would hate the machine. So: is this argument valid? If it is, would Moore really have to accept it? And if not, which step exactly should he reject, and how should it be restated to capture his actual view?
