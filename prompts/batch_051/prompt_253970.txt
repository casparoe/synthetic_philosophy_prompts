Consider the following legal counterfactual. In 1995, a mid-sized common-law democracy amends its criminal code to permit penal substitution. The change is simple: any person sentenced to a term of imprisonment may apply for the sentence to be served instead by a single adult substitute, provided that the substitute was not a party to the offense, has full mental capacity, gives informed consent in open court, and has no conviction for a serious offense. The substitute's service fully discharges the offender's sentence, and the substitution itself creates no criminal record for the substitute. The offender remains formally convicted and bears the stigma of the conviction. Private payment to the substitute is neither prohibited nor regulated. The statute survives constitutional challenge, and, while later amendments are permitted, the basic provision remains in force.

David Lewis pressed a question in this vicinity when he examined the doctrine of penal substitution: he considered whether there is anything conceptually or morally incoherent in the idea that one person might freely bear another's punishment, and he floated the thought that the usual obstacles to allowing substitution in human penal practice are contingent features of those practices rather than an intrinsic defect in the idea itself.

Give me a reasoned prediction of how this country's criminal justice system, and the surrounding social norms about punishment, look fifty years later, in 2045. I want the chain of reasoning, not a story. Address at least the following: who becomes a substitute, and for what reasons; whether a market in substitution emerges, and how offenders of different means fare; what happens to deterrence, retribution, and the public meaning of a criminal conviction once substitution is permitted; how courts and the legislature respond over time, including what restrictions they add and why; and whether the fifty-year outcome supports Lewis's suggestion that the objection to penal substitution is contingent on human arrangements, or instead reveals intrinsic defects that appear as soon as the practice is allowed at scale. For each link in your argument, indicate how confident you are and state an assumption whose failure would break that link.
