Question: A government directs the central bank to finance a large one-off transfer to households by printing new money. Evaluate the claim that this makes the economy, as a whole, wealthier in real terms. In your answer, distinguish real wealth, purchasing power, liquidity, and seigniorage.

Student answer: The claim is mostly false. The economy's real wealth is its stock of goods, services, and productive capacity, and printing money doesn't add to that. What it can do is create purchasing power for the transfer recipients, who can buy real resources before prices fully adjust, and that is a real gain to them. It can also create liquidity if the central bank buys less liquid assets, since cash is more liquid and liquidity is valuable; by removing the liquidity discount on the assets it buys, the central bank adds real wealth rather than merely redistributing it. The government also gains seigniorage, revenue it can spend without levying taxes. The cost shows up as inflation, which falls on existing money holders. So on net the economy is not wealthier in real terms, except for the liquidity gain, which is a genuine addition.
