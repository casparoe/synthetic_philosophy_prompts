Over the past year I have been responsible for a forecasting program that scores forecasters by strictly proper scoring rules, and the experience has moved me from treating those scores as merely pragmatic incentives to accepting something like accuracy-first epistemology. The position I now hold is this: the sole fundamental epistemic good is closeness to the truth, and the central norms of rational credence—probabilistic coherence, conditionalization, and perhaps the Principle of Indifference in some settings—are rational because failing to satisfy them leaves one accuracy-dominated or lowers one's expected accuracy by the lights of any strictly proper measure of inaccuracy. I know this is a research program rather than a single result, and I want to know what I have actually staked. What would have to be true for the accuracy-first picture to be false, or true only in a way that no longer earns its keep? I would like a map of its exposure, with defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. In particular, address at least these: (i) how much the orthodox arguments depend on the choice of scoring rule and on structural assumptions such as additivity, and whether different strictly proper scores could reverse practical verdicts once higher-order evidence or extreme probabilities are in play; (ii) whether the vindications survive when credences are imprecise, when the space of possibilities is infinite, or when an agent must reason about the very algebra of possibilities it currently uses—for example, when inquiry could reveal a new hypothesis not yet in the partition; (iii) whether closeness to the truth really is the sole fundamental epistemic value, or whether understanding, explanatory fit, and responsiveness to higher-order evidence resist reduction to expected accuracy; and (iv) whether the accuracy theorems are the genuine source of the norms or an elegant formal redescription of them, so that the program either collapses into its rivals or has been qualified into unfalsifiability. For each, say which parts a committed accuracy-first epistemologist can absorb as refinements, which parts count as genuine counterexamples that defenders would themselves concede, and which parts would show the program is hollow or objectionably insulated. End by being explicit about which of these considerations would most change the overall verdict you reach here, and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
