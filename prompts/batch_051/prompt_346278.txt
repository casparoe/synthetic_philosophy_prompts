On their view, even though the future is open in the sense that there are many possible continuations from now, one of those continuations is singled out as the actual future, and future-tensed statements are true or false because they line up with that actual history, not because the future is necessary. I've read some of the Belnap-and-Green-style objections, and I don't want the conversation to just repeat the standard exchanges.

Could you write eight to ten interview questions that push on the view from different angles? For each question, include a follow-up that anticipates the most likely reply. And if they answer that the red line is actual but not necessary, have a follow-up ready about whether this simply collapses into an Ockhamist view where God already knows the actual future.
