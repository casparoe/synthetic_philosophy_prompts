I am a journalist who writes about law and ideas, and I am trying to establish whether the position on the rule of law that I have settled into over the past several years is coherent before I commit it to print. I am familiar with the usual debates running from Fuller through Raz to Waldron, and the following is roughly where I have landed. I would be grateful if you would audit the package for tensions rather than expound or attack any one of its parts.

1. At bottom I take the rule of law to be a formal ideal: public, prospective, clear, stable and general rules, reliably and impartially applied. Its point is that law should be capable of guiding conduct. I do not think the ideal has any necessary substantive content, and I accept that a broadly wicked legal order could conform to most of it.

2. Yet I cannot treat the ideal as morally flat. A regime that rules through secret decrees or retrospective criminal laws does not merely govern badly; it wrongs those it governs, and I want to locate that wrongness inside the rule-of-law ideal itself, not in independent principles of justice brought in from outside.

3. I also regard retrospective criminal legislation as categorically unacceptable, even when it is enacted through scrupulously democratic procedures to punish conduct that was plainly blameworthy when it was done.

4. I support strong-form judicial review: courts should be able to strike down duly enacted statutes on fundamental-rights grounds, and I count this as part of the rule-of-law ideal rather than a compromise with it.

5. The deepest justification of the ideal, as I see it, is that ordinary people should be able to stand before the law as responsible agents: able to understand and predict it, addressed by it as equals, and able to see themselves, through their representatives, as authors of the legal order rather than merely its subjects.

Do these five commitments hang together? Please rank whatever tensions you find by severity and say what would have to be given up to remove each; and please also flag any apparent tension that dissolves on inspection, since I need to know which of my own worries to discount.
