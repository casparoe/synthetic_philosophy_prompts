I chair the Rules Committee in my faculty senate, and we've hit a genuinely strange procedural question I need a written opinion on before Thursday. Our bylaws say any amendment needs written notice to every senator at least two weeks before the vote. A group now wants to amend that same amendment clause: the replacement would let the Executive Committee amend the bylaws by simple majority with no notice at all, and it includes the sentence "This clause applies to its own adoption." They circulated it 48 hours ago, skipping the old notice rule entirely. Their argument is two-fold: the notice defect doesn't matter because the rule they're passing is the one that abolishes the notice requirement, and in any case a body has to be able to replace its own rule for changing rules, otherwise the old rule gets a permanent veto over every future change. Opponents say that's letting an invalid act make itself valid by its own content. The Senate President has asked our committee whether the motion is in order.

Draft the short opinion I'd circulate, using these sections in this order:

- The question (about two sentences: exactly what we're ruling on)
- The bootstrapping problem (one paragraph: why applying the new rule to its own adoption is circular)
- The case for the motion being in order (one paragraph: the strongest version of the sponsors' position)
- The case against (one paragraph: why a body can't retroactively cure its own procedural defect)
- My recommendation (one paragraph: whether to rule the motion in or out of order, with a concrete course of action and timing for the sponsors)

Keep it under 900 words, and make sure the recommendation engages the sponsers' position seriously rather than just citing the old notice rule back at them.
