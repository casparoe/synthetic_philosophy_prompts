I just came from a long argument with a friend about whether Dennett’s multiple drafts model actually eliminates the Cartesian theater or only relocates it into an abstract contest among drafts. We kept returning to article 31 of Descartes’s *The Passions of the Soul*, where he argues that the soul, though united to the whole body, has its primary seat in the pineal gland, since that is where the body’s incoming impressions can be gathered and presented to it. My friend thinks Descartes made a purely anatomical mistake: remove the gland, allow parallel processing, and the theater disappears. I doubt it. Please argue that Dennett’s multiple drafts model, as set out in *Consciousness Explained*, still requires a functional counterpart of the theater—something that does the work of singling out the draft that will be reported as the subject’s current conscious content—even though it is not a place in the brain and has no precise finishing time. Use the Descartes passage as the test case: separate the bad anatomical bet from the deeper functional demand, and show that Dennett has given up only the former. Make the strongest version of this case rather than listing standard objections.
