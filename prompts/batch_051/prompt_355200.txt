I chair the Algorithmic Equity Review Board for the City of Hartley. The housing department has begun using a Bayesian model to rank households for emergency rental assistance from a fixed pool of $4.2 million, with roughly the eight hundred highest-scoring households receiving aid. Our technical advisers have shown that in the small-sample districts where the most precarious applicants live, the scores move enough under different priors that about ninety households cross the cutoff either way. The department's data science lead proposes to resolve this by directing her team to use the maximum entropy prior, on the grounds that it is the uniquely rational and neutral representation of ignorance, and that standardizing on it would insulate the city from charges of bias. A minority of the board contests this. They argue that a maximum entropy distribution is flat only relative to a chosen way of parameterizing the model and partitioning the possible outcomes, and that calling one prior objective may simply conceal substantive assumptions made elsewhere. They want the board to require instead that every consequential model be fit and reported under a panel-approved set of competing priors, with the resulting spread displayed as a sensitivity range.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend one concrete course of action—adopt the single prior, adopt the competing-priors rule, or a third alternative—together with an implementation timeline. It should engage the strongest consideration on the losing side honestly rather than caricaturing it, and it must carry five of nine board members who have no quantitative training, so the reasoning should be in plain language rather than formulas. The document will not cite external literature, so there is no need to include references or footnotes.
