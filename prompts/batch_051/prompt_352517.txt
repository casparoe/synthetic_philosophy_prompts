I'm a novelist, and most of my books come down to characters stuck in hard choices where the things they care about don't seem to sit on one scale — betraying a friend vs telling a truth that saves a stranger, staying to care for a parent vs doing the work your actually good at. I've ended up with an actual view about it: my credence that some values are genuinely incommensurable (not just hard to compare because we lack information or nerve, but such that no common scale of value can represent the trade-off even in principle) is around 0.7, while a friend who does decision theory is at 0.2 and thinks its always just an epistemic problem. Give me your number, but mostly give me the derivation: decompose it into subquestions like what 'incommensurable' would have to mean to be interesting, whether rational choice presupposes a single scale of comparison, whether the regret people feel afterward is evidence of anything beyond uncertainty, and whatever else you think belongs. Put a rough probability on each subquestion, show how they combine, and say which subquestion is doing the most work in the gap between 0.7 and 0.2 — and what evidence would move your number most.
