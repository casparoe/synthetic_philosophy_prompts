I want to make sure my character's reasoning is actually sound before I put it in his mouth.

Here's the argument I want him to give. He faces a symmetric one-shot prisoner's dilemma against a copy of himself who is running the same deterministic decision procedure, and both know all of this. (1) Because the procedure is deterministic and their information is relevantly identical, whatever one does the other does, and vice versa; so mixed outcomes where one cooperates and the other defects are off the table. (2) So his real choice is between the pair of actions cooperate-cooperate and the pair defect-defect. (3) In a prisoner's dilemma, cooperate-cooperate gives each player a higher payoff than defect-defect. (4) Therefore he should cooperate. I suspect the trouble is in step 1 or step 2, but I can't state exactly where the slide happens. Is the argument valid, and if not, which step fails?
