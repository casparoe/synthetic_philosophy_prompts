I'm a novelist and I've been reading about the physics of time for a book I'm trying to structure non-linearly, with a couple chapters running backward. The Rietdijk–Putnam argument has me a little tangled up. The way I understand it: two observers moving relative to each other will disagree about which far-off events are happening "now," so an event that hasn't happened yet for me — on a distant star, say — might already be in someone else's present. If it's already real for them, dosen't that mean the future is already fixed? That the ending of my novel is sitting there in the block before I've written it? And if so, why does writing it feel like I'm deciding anything, and why does suspense work at all? Is the feeling that a plot could still go another way just my ignorance of what's already the case? I keep coming back to this: the physics seems to be telling me every choice in the book was settled before I started typing. Where am I actually going wrong here?
