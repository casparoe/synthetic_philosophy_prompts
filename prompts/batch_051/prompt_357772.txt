Two people who study democratic backsliding have agreed to write a joint statement instead of continuing to talk past each other: one treats backsliding as a real, institutionally measurable process; the other thinks the label mostly does partisan work. Write the document they'd produce, and keep it concrete by making them work through Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the US rather than stay in the abstract. Begin with a numbered agreed-claims section: what both sides are willing to call unambiguous erosion, what the usual democracy indices actually track and where they diverge, and where any measure of democratic quality has to take a stand on contested policy questions. Then give a disagreements table, with each remaining claim stated twice — once in wording the institutionalist camp accepts, once in wording the skeptic camp accepts — so it's clear which disagreements are substantive and which are verbal. Finally, pick three cruxes: sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one side. For each, say what case or evidence both sides agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance.
