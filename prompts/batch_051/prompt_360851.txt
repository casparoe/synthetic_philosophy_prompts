I cover philosophy of science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and for several months I have been moderating a written exchange between two philosophers who disagree about the structure of epistemic justification. One is a foundationalist: she holds that chains of justification must terminate in basic beliefs, which are justified without being inferred from further beliefs. The other is a coherentist: he holds that there are no privileged starting points, and that a belief is justified by how well it fits with the rest of a person's belief system. The exchange keeps stalling at the same place. The foundationalist argues that coherentism turns justification into a circle, and therefore into no justification at all; the coherentist argues that foundationalism cannot explain how a basic belief earns its regress-stopping status without quietly relying on something like an inference. In private, each has told me they suspect part of the dispute is verbal and part of it is a disagreement about what would count as resolving a question about the structure of justification.

I would like you to draft the document they should have produced after a genuine adversarial collaboration. Open with a numbered section of agreed claims: statements both can sign about the regress problem itself, about what any satisfactory account of justification must explain, and about what the third option, infinitism, gets right or wrong by their shared lights. Then provide a disagreements table. For each residual crux, state the crux in neutral language; give the foundationalist's own formulation of her view and the coherentist's own formulation of his, each worded so that side would accept it; and identify a concrete case or argument both sides would treat as bearing on the crux, such as a perceptual belief under non-ideal conditions, a memory belief, a mathematical axiom, or an inference about one's own reliability. Record in advance what each side predicts that case will show. Conclude by flagging any disagreement you believe is merely verbal, and restate it in a way that removes the apparent conflict.
