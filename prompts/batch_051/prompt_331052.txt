I'm a novelist, and the book I'm drafting turns on a congregation that splits after a teenager's sudden remission from an aggressive leukemia is proclaimed a miracle: one faction holds that such signs ceased with the age of the apostles, the other that they continue today. Rather than a survey of that debate, I need a working definition of "miracle" — one a careful central character could defend and apply consistently, and one that forces me to think rather than recite. Please test it against these cases: the teenager's remission, which her oncologist cannot explain but which would not be described as a violation of a known law; a person born blind whose sight is recovered at a healing service, with clinical records on both sides; a stage magician who appears to move spoons at a distance; a routine, uneventful flight after prayers for safe travel; a healing later shown to have been staged; a solar eclipse that halts a battle and is read as an omen by both armies; a private message taken to be from God that produces no observable change in the speaker's life; and the resurrection of Jesus. Propose the definition, run it through all eight cases, and then tell me where it strains hardest and which verdict you would least want to defend to a character who disagrees.
