I am a retired physicist who came to philosophy late, and I have been reading Schopenhauer's aesthetics with particular interest. I find myself drawn to a stronger and more precise claim than the familiar observation that music is the most immediately moving of the arts. As I understand the position, it is this: the other fine arts present the Platonic Ideas, the grades at which the will objectifies itself, and therefore remain bound up with the phenomenal world, while music alone does not present Ideas at all but presents the will itself, directly and without the intermediary of representation. Music is supposed to hold its unique rank precisely because it gives us the thing-in-itself in a form unmediated by the principle of sufficient reason.

I would like to stress-test this position the way I would have stress-tested a physical theory. Please map what would count against it. That is, identify the phenomena, arguments, conceptual distinctions, or empirical findings that would be decisive against the thesis, and distinguish them from those that would be merely awkward. Which of these would a committed Schopenhauerian concede as genuine costs, and which would they dismiss as irrelevant or question-begging? Where is the position most exposed, and has it been insulated by qualifications? I would especially like your assessment of the following pressure points: whether the view can handle programmatic and representational music; whether purely formal or rule-generated music, including music assembled by a machine with no felt will of its own, fits the account; whether the phrase the will itself smuggles in a person-level desire that is not the same as Schopenhauer's metaphysical will; and whether the central claim can be distinguished from the more modest psychological claim that music bypasses conceptual processing and acts directly on affect. Finally, tell me honestly whether, under sharp scrutiny, the claim collapses into something less distinctive, and whether its immunity to empirical refutation would be a virtue or a defect.
