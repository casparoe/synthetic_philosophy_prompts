Just got done arguing for an hour with a friend about that scene in The Matrix where Neo gets kung fu uploaded and immediately announces he knows it. My friend thinks it's basically a decisive point for intellectualism about knowing how: the movie treats a whole skill as information that can be copied into a brain, no practice, no trained dispositions — like knowing how is just knowing a proposition under a practical mode of presentation. I don't buy it, and I think the dojo fight right after is the more interesting evidence, because the uploaded skill doesn't really click until Morpheus talks him into a different state of mind. Read the movie through the knowing how/knowing that debate for me. Where does it treat know-how as a kind of knowing-that, where does it resist that reading, and what would a Rylean have to say the upload would need to include to actually work? Give me a careful reading that takes the film and the philosophy equally seriously; if the movie takes a side, say which, and also say where it paperes over the hard part.
