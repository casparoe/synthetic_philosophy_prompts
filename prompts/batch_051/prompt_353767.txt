I sit on the Allocation Committee of the Meridian Community Fund, which each year divides a fixed flexible pool among local organizations running housing, addiction-recovery, and disability-support programs. Our standing procedure is to rank allocations by total expected benefit, using a shared scale of wellbeing points that staff assigns to each proposal.

We are facing a recommendation to direct the entire pool to a single applicant, the Threshold Center. The argument runs as follows. On staff's numbers, each additional dollar given to Threshold produces more wellbeing points than a dollar given to any other applicant; moreover, unlike the other applicants, Threshold's marginal returns do not taper off as the grant grows—staff model them as essentially flat across the full range of feasible awards. If that is right, every dollar placed elsewhere is a dollar doing less good than it could have done. One colleague calls this a utility monster; another replies that if our decision rule always produces this corner solution, that is a reductio of the rule, not a reason to eliminate every other program. A staff note adds that the flat marginal returns may be an artifact of the rubric, which awards points per unit of service delivered rather than estimating the marginal contribution to actual wellbeing; the proposal's supporters answer that abandoning the shared scale now, under pressure, would invite arbitrary exceptions.

I must circulate a short written opinion before next Thursday's vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action—adopt the all-in allocation, reject it, or adopt a specific alternative such as a cap, a floor for other applicants, or a revision of the scoring rubric—and give a time frame for anything we would ask staff to do. It should also engage seriously with the strongest argument on the side I recommend against: if I reject the all-in allocation, I should say what the maximizing framework gets right; if I accept it, I should say what is mistaken about the reductio intuition. Draft the opinion I will circulate, as a document the committee can actually act on, not a survey of positions.
