I've been kicking around an idea about politics and I want to know where it breaks. It started with me being annoyed by the term limits debate. The usual pitch is: career politicians are corrupt or out of touch, so kick them out after a few terms. But that always seemed like solving the wrong problem, because incumbency advantage is the symptom, not the disease. The disease is that elections select for people who are good at campaigns—fundraising, soundbites, looking normal in a diner—and those people are not necessarily any good at writing law. A term limit just makes the same type of person serve for less time, with less knowledge, and more dependence on the staff who actually run the place. So far so good, I think.

Then I got to the crazier part. What if, instead of term limits, we filled maybe a quarter of a legislative chamber by lottery from the voter rolls, like jury duty (I think the fancy term is sortition)? Pay them properly, give them a real staff budget, make the term three years, and no reelection. The point is not that random people are smarter. It's that they dont have a career to defend and they don't owe anyone money. The obvious objection is capture: they'd be clueless and would just get led around by whoever explains the bill to them. But here's where I keep going back and forth. A career politician is also captured, just by a different set of people (donors, party leaders, future employers), and he's better at hiding it because he knows the jargon and can make the thing he's doing for donors sound like a matter of principle. A randomly picked welder or nurse is at least honest about not knowing, so they'd ask questions like "why is this line even in the bill?" That seems valuable, because the actual job of a legislator isnt to be the expert; it's to be the person the experts have to explain things to. An expert can bamboozle another expert in a different field, but it's hard to bamboozle a normal person who just won't let go of a simple question.

So the thing I want comments on is whether that last move actually works. Is it true that a lottery-selected amateur is harder to capture than a career politician, or did I just drown the problem in a nice story? And is my "legislator as the person experts have to explain themselves to" idea doing real work, or is it just a slogan dressed up? I'd rather you be harsh than polite, because I'm trying to figure out whether this is a real idea or just something that sounds good at a bar.
