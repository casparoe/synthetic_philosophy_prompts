I keep going back and forth on whether this is a real structural parallel or just me pattern-matching. There's an old thought that if a mind is a finite physical system, then it has finitely many possible states, so over an infinite life the stream of conscious states must eventually repeat, novelty runs out, eternal recurrence via pigeonhole. That sure looks like the finite-automaton fact that a DFA with n states, fed an infinite input, must revisit states and settle into an ultimately periodic pattern — the kind of counting argument behind the pumping lemma. The mapping would be: brain state = automaton state, and your sequence of experiences over time = the sequence of states the machine visits as it reads the tape. What I can't decide is whether this is a genuine structural identity or whether it breaks somewhere important. The obvious disanalogy: an automaton's next state is a function only of (current state, next input), but the *content* of an experience is plausibly relational — same internal state, different object in the world, different experience — and content is usually individuated by meaning/reference, not by physical state. So: is the 'finite mind exhausts experience' argument basically an automata theorem in disguise (which would make it's conclusion stronger, a theorem rather than a vibe), or does it fail precisely because mental states don't compose like automaton transitions? If it fails, pinpoint the exact place where the formalization has to smuggle in the world or semantic content, and whether that dependency could itself be folded into a bigger finite state space.
