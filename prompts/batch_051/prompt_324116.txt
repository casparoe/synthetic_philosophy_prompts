I am a high school student preparing an independent research essay on the claim that the world is becoming less violent, and I keep finding that sources answer in opposite directions because they seem to be measuring different things. I do not want another chart or book that simply asserts an answer; I want the specification of the question that any serious answer must satisfy.

Write out the desiderata for an adequate account of long-run trends in violence, as a requirements list. For each requirement, give the concrete case or consideration that makes it binding. At minimum, work through: the unit of analysis (per capita rates versus absolute numbers, and how the comparison behaves as population grows); the numerator problem (what gets counted as an instance of violence — deaths in interstate war, civil war, homicide, state killings, famine, structural violence, nonfatal assault — and how the overall verdict changes if the definition is narrowed or widened); the denominator and the time horizon; the problem of missing or inconsistent historical records; and the question of whether there is a single fact of the matter here at all, or whether the sentence 'the world is becoming less violent' is definite only once we commit to a weighting of the different kinds of violence.

For each requirement, say whether it is non-negotiable or only one camp's preference. Flag any pairs of requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied; I especially want to know whether a measure can make per-capita and absolute trends always move together, and if not, which one must yield. Then, briefly, score the familiar broad approaches — counting absolute fatalities, tracking per-capita war death rates, and adopting a wide definition of violence — against the list, and identify the single requirement that disqualifies the most of them. Keep the weight on the requirements and their motivating cases rather than on the scorecard.
