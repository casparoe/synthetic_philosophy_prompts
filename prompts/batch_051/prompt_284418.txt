I am a sixth-form student, and I have been reading about collective intentionality while preparing for an essay on how people manage to act together. I came across an encyclopaedia entry that sums up the debate between Michael Bratman and Margaret Gilbert in the following way.

Bratman's planning theory says that for two people to share an intention to do something, such as painting a kitchen together, it is enough that each privately intends that the two of them paint it. Shared intention therefore adds nothing over and above the individual intentions of the participants; the only thing genuinely shared is the overlapping content of those intentions. Gilbert objects that this cannot explain the way partners in a joint action are answerable to one another: if one stops halfway through and the other demands an explanation, no story built purely from personal intentions can account for the standing to make that demand. Joint action, she argues, requires a joint commitment, which she describes as a commitment of the parties as a body rather than a conjunction of personal commitments. Because a joint commitment cannot be reduced to the personal intentions of the parties, the entry concludes, Gilbert treats the group itself as an additional agent standing behind the action.
