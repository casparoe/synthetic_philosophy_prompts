A colleague of mine has been arguing that psychopaths do not actually know that what they do is wrong, even when they calmly say that it is. On his account, moral knowledge requires an affective, empathic grasp of the victim’s harm — not merely the ability to recite the rule — so a psychopath’s fluent use of moral vocabulary is parroted convention rather than knowledge. I am sympathetic, but I worry he has quietly defined “knowledge” so that no study could count against him. How would one test whether the psychopath lacks moral knowledge, as opposed to knowing perfectly well and lacking the motivation to act on it? Give your strongest concrete design — drawing, for instance, on the moral/conventional distinction, physiological measures, or learning tasks — specify what each possible outcome would show and what residue no experiment could reach, and then take a definite position: does the available style of evidence settle whether the psychopath knows it is wrong, and which side does it actually favour?
