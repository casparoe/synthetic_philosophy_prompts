I cover the philosophy of science and mathematics for a general-interest ideas publication, and I am working on a long feature about the distinct senses in which a deterministic system can be called 'unpredictable'. Public discussion of the subject is still mostly organised around the butterfly effect, and I want the piece to move beyond it towards the finer-grained claims: sensitive dependence on initial conditions, the role of strange attractors in constraining how much finite-precision data can be reconstructed, the contrast with quantum or genuinely stochastic indeterminacy, and the more contested suggestion that chaos yields a kind of objective randomness. Before moving into journalism I trained as a statistician, and I still reach for a particular technical vocabulary — aleatoric versus epistemic uncertainty, identifiability, predictive versus inferential targets, model misspecification, and the distinction between a model's noise term and the unmodelled degrees of freedom in the world. My working intuition is that the philosophical distinction between ontic and epistemic unpredictability is partly, but only partly, expressible in that vocabulary: deterministic chaos seems to produce uncertainty that is neither cleanly aleatoric nor cleanly epistemic, since it is in principle resolvable but in practice explosive.

I would like a systematic translation rather than a loose analogy. Restate the central claims about what makes chaotic systems unpredictable — the ones I listed and any others you think belong on the list — in the statistical vocabulary above, as faithfully as that vocabulary allows. Then give me a residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which had to be bent or quietly redefined in order to fit, and which have no counterpart at all in a statistician's talk of noise, uncertainty, and misspecification. For each item of residue, tell me whether it points to a limitation in my own framework, a fudge in the philosophical claim it came from, or a genuine insight that the statistical vocabulary cannot house. I am less interested in a survey of chaos theory than in knowing what the translation cannot carry, and in whether my suspicion — that some of this dispute is a disagreement about the word 'random' rather than about the world — survives contact with the details.
