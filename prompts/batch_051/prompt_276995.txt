My grandmother, the last fluent speaker of our language, died in March, and ever since I've been having the same fight with my cousin about whether anything was actually lost besides her. She's an anthropologist and says a language is its own way of slicing up the world; we should mourn it almost like a species. I keep saying nobody was made worse off: the grandkids are fine in English, and a language nobody bothered to learn was already dieing. Neither of us just wanted to win — we kept talking past each other. Could you write the argument out as a real dialogue, with an intrinsic-value view and a harm-only view, where each character has to answer the other's best point? And make them actually dig into what 'loss' means here rather than trading speeches.
