In an argument about whether generative models are creative in any sense worth caring about, a colleague kept deflating every appeal to human originality by repeating that creativity is just knob-twiddling. His only elaboration was that to create is to take an existing theme or structure and vary its parameters, and that machine systems do precisely that. The line is carrying real weight in that argument, so I want to know exactly what it would have to mean. Separate the distinct claims it might be asserting: for example, that all creation draws on antecedent material; that creative change always consists in shifting values along dimensions that are already given; that novelty is always a new point in an existing space of possibilities rather than the introduction of a new dimension; and that the variations produced by machines and by human artists are of the same kind. For each, say whether it is a trivial truth, a substantive but defensible claim, or false. Then identify which reading the dismissal actually requires, and whether that reading can bear the weight.
