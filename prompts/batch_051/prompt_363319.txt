I've been mulling over an analogy a meditation teacher of mine uses, and I can't decide how much work it can actually do. He says the mind works like a still lake: the water reflects things accurately only when its surface is undisturbed, so every emotion is a ripple, anger, grief, even delighted excitement, and each ripple makes the image waver. His conclusion is that the real goal is to be free from disturbance altogether, not merely to stop being swept around by strong feelings but to treat any stirring at all as a distortion to be quieted down. I keep thinking the picture is attractive but suspect it turns the mind into something merely passive and makes every disturbance look like an error. Could you audit this analogy the way you'd audit an argument that seems to be doing too much work? I'm after a table of correspondences, the water, the ripples, the wind, the clarity of the image, then the points where the mapping breaks, what the lake quietly imports about whether disturbance is always distortion, whether accurate seeing requires the absence of feeling, and whether the mind is a reflector rather than an actor, and finally whether the conclusion, that freedom from disturbance is an unqualified good, survives once the disanalogies are laid out.
