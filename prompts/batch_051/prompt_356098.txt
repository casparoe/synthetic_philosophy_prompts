Over the past few months emergency powers have stopped being an abstract thing for me: I've lived under a night-time curfew, and my partner was stuck on the wrong side of a border when the government shut its borders with about six hours' notice. Everyone around me keeps saying the measures are or aren't "legitimate," and I've realised I don't actually know what work that word is doing. It clearly isn't the same as "legal" — I can think of illegal things that were probably the right call, and legal things that were an abuse.

So here's what I want. You're a political philosopher who works on the rule of law and emergency powers. Please think carefully, and before you commit, brainstorm two or three candidate definitions of a legitimate emergency measure and say what each get's wrong. Then give me a working definition and run it through all of the cases below. Where your definition gives a verdict you weren't expecting, or gives no verdict at all, say so, and either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict.

The term to define: a legitimate emergency measure, as opposed to a merely legal one.

The cases:

A. After a major earthquake, the health minister declares a 72-hour curfew to stop looting and keep roads clear for rescue vehicles.

B. During a pandemic, the government closes the border to everyone for three weeks, including asylum seekers, with no exceptions or exemptions.

C. The government invokes emergency powers to suspend parliament and pass an unrelated budget, on the grounds that the public finances are in a state of emergency. This is lawful under the country's constitution.

D. A regional governor commandeers a hotel to use as a field hospital during a flood, even though the legal basis for doing so is shaky and the owner objects.

E. The interior ministry imposes a curfew on only two neighbourhoods during protests, with no sunset clause and no parliamentary vote.

F. During a virus outbreak, the health minister renews a stay-at-home order every three weeks under an emergency law, with no parliamentary debate, even though cases have fallen sharply and the order still has broad public support.

G. The army is used to break a port strike under emergency powers, without a parliamentary vote, to keep fuel and medicine moving.

I'm confident A is legitimate and C isn't, despite C being legal. D is the one that really bothers me — I suspect it might be the right thing to do and illegal, and I don't know what a definition should say about that. For the rest I genuinely don't have a settled view. Tell me where your definition surprises you and which of its verdicts is the one you'd defend least confidently.
