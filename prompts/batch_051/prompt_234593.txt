Ok so I've been down a Church-Turing rabbit hole for the last few weeks and I've ended up with a pile of commitments that all feel right individually, but I want someone to check whether they actually hang together before I go repeat them in an argument and get owned. I'll number them so you can point at specific pairs.

(1) The original Church-Turing thesis — the claim that the effectively computable functions are exactly the Turing-computable (or lambda-definable, or general recursive) ones — isn't the kind of thing a single clever counterexample could refute. It's more like a sharpening of a fuzzy pre-theoretic notion, so I want to say it's true by something like successful stipulation or convention, not by empirical discovery.

(2) The physical Church-Turing thesis is a different claim entirely: no physical process computes a non-Turing-computable function. That one is a real empirical claim about the actual world, and it's very probably true.

(3) Hypercomputation is still mathematically coherent. Oracle machines and infinite-time Turing machines are perfectly well-defined objects; the concept of a machine that solves the halting problem isn't confused. Its only confused if you think one exists in our universe.

(4) The convergence of lambda calculus, general recursive functions, Turing machines, and a pile of other formalisms on the exact same class is strong evidence that we've found the right notion, not just a historical accident or a selection effect.

(5) If something like a Malament-Hogarth spacetime actually existed — roughly, a general-relativistic setup where one observer can, in finite proper time, have an infinite computation play out along another worldline and then observe the result — that would refute the physical thesis from (2). But it wouldn't touch the original thesis from (1), because an infinite-run process isn't an effective procedure in the relevant sense.

So: which pairs are in tension and how badly? I specifically want you to separate pairs that are only apparently in tension and explain why they dissolve, from the ones that genuinely pull against each other. For each real tension, say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs, and then say which single revision would clean up the most.

And one meta thing: please mark which of these are things people who actually work on this treat as settled, versus which are live disputes. Don't take my view and present it as the consensus if it isn't.
