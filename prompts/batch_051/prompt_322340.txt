A substantivalist about spacetime and a relationist about motion have spent a year arguing past each other about Newton's bucket experiment. Write the joint statement they should have been able to produce at the end of a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Treat both positions sympathetically: the substantivalist holds that spacetime structure exists over and above matter and is what distinguishes genuinely absolute rotation from merely relative motion; the relationist holds that all motion is ultimately relative and that inertial effects, such as the water's concave surface, are to be explained by relations to the rest of the material universe.

The document should begin with a numbered section of claims both sides can sign. Include the observational core of the bucket experiment stated carefully: (i) while the bucket rotates but the water has not yet taken up the bucket's rotation, the surface stays flat despite relative motion between water and bucket; (ii) once the water has caught up and rotates with the bucket, the surface is concave despite water and bucket being at relative rest; (iii) therefore the concavity is not settled by motion relative to the bucket. Both sides should also be able to sign a precise statement of what these observations show, what they leave open, and what explanatory burden any adequate account must meet.

Then state the residual disagreement in neutral wording each side would accept for its own position: whether the concavity requires a primitive absolute space, whether the needed structure can be analyzed as relations among matter, and whether the fixed stars enter the explanation as physically relevant to inertia or only as a convenient reference system. Identify the cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, give a concrete scenario, argument, or empirical signature that both sides agree would bear on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. Reasonable candidates include: whether inertial-frame structure reduces to the matter distribution; what a materially specified universe containing a single water-filled bucket and no other matter would look like, or whether that case is well-posed; and whether one could in principle tell apart a rotating bucket in a universe with the fixed stars at rest from a fixed bucket with the stars rotating around it. Finally, identify any part of the dispute you think is verbal, and say what substantive disagreement would remain.

Format the result as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table whose rows each list a crux, the test or case both accept as bearing on it, and the two sides' predictions. Do not adjudicate the dispute; the deliverable is the constructive document both parties could sign.
