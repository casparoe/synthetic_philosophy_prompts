Okay here's one. I want a thought experiment built to order, working inside Anscombe's objection to Truman and the atomic bombs. The purpose of the case is to isolate whether the intention/foresight distinction is really the engine of the absolutist verdict, or whether it just tags along behind an expected-value calculation that would hand down the same judgment anyway.

Use two views:

(1) an Anscombe-style absolutist: intentionally killing noncombatants as a means to your ends is always murder, full stop; foreseen-but-unintended collateral deaths can sometimes be okay, but only if they are genuinely not part of the mechanism you are relying on, and only if they are proportionate to the good achieved.

(2) an act-consequentialist: all that matters is expected consequences; noncombatant deaths count equally whether intended or merely foreseen, and the right act is the one with the best expected tradeoff.

Now construct a pair of cases, First Strike and Second Strike, close enough to Hiroshima and Nagasaki that the reader doesn't need extra historical background, but not so tied to the record that someone can nitpick dates to death. The cases have to satisfy these specs:

- In First Strike, the two theories diverge: the consequentialist defends the drop (e.g., the expected invasion-plus-blockade death toll is worse, and the shock is what ends the war), while the absolutist condemns it, because the civilian deaths are the intended mechanism for producing surrender.

- In Second Strike, they converge on condemn, but by different routes: the consequentialist says the marginal expected utility was negative—for instance it came too soon after First Strike for the enemy to respond, or a diplomatic off-ramp hadn't been tried—while the absolutist says the timing is beside the point, since it's still deliberate killing of the innocent as a means and is ruled out before the ledger gets consulted.

Then do the full demonstration: what each theory says about each case and why. Identify the single structural feature that is doing the seperating work in First Strike, and show what happens if you remove it: rewrite First Strike so the very same number of civilian deaths is a foreseen side effect of a strike on a genuinely military bottleneck, holding expected consequences fixed, and say whether the absolutist flips, digs in, or has to do some awkward work to avoid flipping. Finally, give the honest note: which theory comes off looking worse in your setup, and how would a smart defender of it attack the cases? Don't just stipulate the verdicts; set the case up so they get pushed out of the two theories.
