Here's a setup for a ranking task on Abelard's consent-only account of sin. Eight people live at an abbey, and the forbidden outer act — call it T — is taking the silver kept for the poor from the strongbox and keeping it for yourself. I want you to rate and rank each person for sinfulness as an Abelardian should, not as a modern consequentialist would.

Quickly state, in your own words, the grading standard your using: desire, pleasure in the thought, the physical act, and consent, and where exactly Abelard locates the sin. I'm using the standard reading where neither the desire nor the act is itself the sin; sin is the considered inner "yes" to wrong, and actually carrying out the act adds nothing to the guilt. One point you'll have to settle: is the "wrong" consented to the act as the person understands it, or as it really is? That bites in G's case. And in H's case, decide whether an earlier clear-headed choice (to drink, knowing what it does to you) can carry the guilt of a later act you were too foggy to consent to.

The cases:

A: Alred has spent months turning over the thought of taking the silver. The scenes play in his head most evenings, and he enjoys them. But he has never said an inner yes to actually doing it, has never formed a plan, and has arranged his duties so he is never alone with the strongbox. He takes nothing.

B: Berengar, alone at night and fully awake, says to himself: "If I could be certain no one would ever find out, I would take it." He means it in that moment. He forms no plan. Ten minutes later he is disgusted, repents, and firmly resolves never to do it. He takes nothing.

C: Cenwulf fully consents, works out a plan, and waits for a rainy night. On the appointed night he is halfway to the strongbox when a loose flagstone gives way; he falls, breaks his leg, and is carried to the infirmary. He never touches the silver.

D: Dunstan performs T while sleepwalking. He had never consented to it while awake, has no waking memory of it, and when he finds the coins in his cell the next morning he is horrified and returns them.

E: Eadgyth consents to T only because a blackmailer has threatened to reveal an old humiliation unless she brings him exactly that amount. She hates the plan, sleeps badly, and finally gives a reluctant inner yes; absent the threat she would never do it. She takes the silver and hands it to the blackmailer.

F: Frithuswith, under no pressure at all, gives a clear and untroubled inner yes, takes the silver, and hides it for her own use. She feels no compunction afterward.

G: Godric gives the same full inner yes as Frithuswith and sets out to steal. In the dark he opens the wrong chest and actually takes a bag containing only his own wages, which the treasurer had moved there earlier that day. Godric believes he has taken the poor fund's silver.

H: Hild knows from long experience that three cups of mead strip away her self-command and make her steal. One feast night she drinks them anyway, thinking only of the pleasure. Later, in the blur, she takes the silver. At the moment she was not clear-headed enough to deliberate or say yes, but she admits afterward that it is exactly what she would have consented to.

Rate each person 0–10 for sinfulness under the standard you stated, then rank them 1–8. Allow ties only where you are confident Abelard's view actually forces a tie, and say why. Don't just float considerations: commit to a ranking.

Then, after your reasoning, give me one JSON object with this schema (no markdown fence needed, just the JSON):

- "grading_standard": one sentence restating the standard including how you handle the subjective-vs-objective point.
- "cases": an array with one entry per case; each entry is an object with "case_id" (A–H), "name", "score" (0–10, number), "rank" (1–8, with shared ranks for ties), "verdict" (one of "full_sin", "partial_sin", "no_sin"), and "rationale" (one sentence tied to Abelard's distinctions).
- "ties": an array of strings explaining each tie.
- "overall_confidence": "low", "medium", or "high".
- "least_sure_point": the specific call you are least sure about, and why.

Also, in the prose before the JSON, say how confident you are in the overall ranking and single out the point you're least sure of.
