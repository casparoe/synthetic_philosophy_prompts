After a long argument with a friend who is convinced that talk of "the fundamental" is just a projection of our explanatory preferences, I've come away holding a definite package of views. I hold that grounding is a real, objective, hyperintensional relation of metaphysical explanation between facts; that it is asymmetric, transitive, and well-founded, with every chain of ground terminating in something ungrounded; and that the fundamental facts are exactly the minimal collection of facts on which all other facts supervene with metaphysical necessity. I want to know what would count against this package. Which possible cases — infinite descent, holistic or self-referential grounding, contingent ungrounded facts, or a minimal supervenience base that pulls apart from the ungrounded facts — would undermine which component? Which formal or methodological considerations should move me? Distinguish defeaters that are fatal to the whole package from those that are merely awkward for one part of it, say which of them a defender of my view would have to concede as genuine counter-evidence rather than dismiss as question-begging, and rank the threats from decisive to survivable. Finally, is the position in danger of being insulated against revision, and would that itself be a problem?
