I head the content moderation policy team at a mid-sized social platform with several million active users, and I am trying to replace our current ad hoc, moderator-by-moderator handling of race-related speech complaints with a single written decision procedure. Our rules prohibit racist content, but the term is undefined, and our moderators — who are not philosophers and have roughly two minutes per report — disagree in predictable ways. Some will only act when they believe the speaker intended to demean or exclude, and they are reluctant to penalize ambiguous or coded statements. Others act when a statement predictably harms a racial group or reinforces racial hierarchy regardless of the speaker's intent, and they think an intent test lets bad actors hide behind plausible deniability. A third group keeps invoking the idea of a dog whistle but cannot operationalize it without either over-policing innocent speech or letting every statement with an innocent reading pass. The stakes are concrete: we remove content and sometimes suspend accounts, users can appeal and demand a plain-language explanation, and our decisions are summarized in a public transparency report.

Write the reusable procedure our moderators should follow to decide whether a reported statement counts as racist under platform policy. Ground it in the best available philosophical accounts of what makes an utterance racist — including the roles of speaker intent, the content and foreseeable effects of the utterance, audience uptake, and coded or deniable speech — but translate that into a checklist or decision tree a non-expert can apply in about two minutes, with the steps run in a fixed order and each decision recorded in terms of the step that settled it. The procedure should (a) distinguish between a statement being racist and a speaker being racist, and say which one our policy should track given that we remove content and sometimes sanction accounts; (b) give clear tests for when a statement with a plausible innocent reading is nonetheless racist, including the dog-whistle case, without requiring moderators to read minds; and (c) identify the cases the procedure cannot settle and must escalate to a senior reviewer, and say what that reviewer should be trying to determine. Then apply the procedure to three worked examples: one, a user posts a fabricated crime statistic about a racial group without further comment and defends it as merely sharing data; two, a user replies to a Black public figure with an emoji sequence whose literal content is innocuous but which moderators report is being used in a coordinated harassment campaign; and three, a user says in a housing-policy thread that a proposal will destroy the character of the neighborhood, in a context where the phrase has a known coded history. At least one worked example should reach a conclusion you expect platform leadership or the moderators themselves to resist, and you should say explicitly why the procedure nonetheless yields it.
