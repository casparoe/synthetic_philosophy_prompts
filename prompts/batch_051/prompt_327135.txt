I need a working definition of "coincidence" that gets all of these cases right, or at least tells me what's doing the work in each one: (1) two people in a 23-person seminar turn out to share a birthday; (2) you think of a song you haven't heard in years and it comes on the radio a minute later; (3) a county-level study finds that ice cream sales and drowning deaths rise and fall together; (4) someone wins a major lottery twice; (5) your new coworker grew up in the same town of 2,000 people you did; (6) you and a friend, without any planning, get seated next to each other on a train in a country neither of you has visited before; (7) a dream you have matches the next morning's news in one specific detail.

I want the definition to keep three things apart: genuine coincidences, mere common causes (which I don't think should count), and events that are just plain chance and not even worth noticing. The birthday case is there to force you to deal with the fact that a match out of many possible matches is much more likely than it sounds, and the dream case is there to force you to deal with how much we select stories after the fact. After you run the definition through all seven, tell me which verdict is the least secure, and whether you'd want a second defintion if we treat the word as doing a psychological job rather than a metaphysical one. I'm writing this up for a piece about why people see signs in random noise, so I care more about the epistemic side than about saving any particular philosophical label.
