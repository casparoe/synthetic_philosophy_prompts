Last night a friend and I spent the evening arguing about what it means to say that a fair coin has a fifty percent chance of landing heads, and I came away genuinely divided. I found myself defending a subjectivist line: probabilities are graded degrees of belief, and the only constraints on them are the usual coherence axioms and whatever updating rule rationality imposes; beyond that, there is no mind-independent chance in the world that credences are answerable to. I like this view because it gives a clean story about prior probabilities, it handles one-off events such as an election or a single throw of a biased die just before the die is destroyed, and it ties probability directly to decision theory through expected utility. But I also cannot shake the conviction that a fair die has a one-in-six chance of showing a four whether or not anyone ever forms beliefs about it, and that someone who assigns credence nine tenths to that die showing four is not merely unorthodox but wrong — wrong in a way that seems to outrun the fact that he will lose money in the long run. My friend insists this is exactly the kind of objective propensity I was trying to avoid, and that I cannot keep the subjectivism while indulging the intuition; he may be right, but discarding the intuition feels like denying something plain rather than revising a theory.

I want to be walked through what each option actually costs, not told which side wins. If I stay a subjectivist, what should I say the intuition is tracking — stable physical symmetries or relative frequencies that rational credences may take as evidence, or is that already objective chance sneaking back in under another name? If I move to a propensity or frequency view to save the intuition, what do I give up in explaining single-case probabilities without arbitrary reference classes, and what becomes of the link between probability and rational action? I want the strongest version of both positions and a negotiated verdict: what would it take for the intuition to be the thing that gets debunked and dropped, what would it take for subjectivism to be amended or abandoned, and whether there is a reading of there having been a real chance on which both can survive.
