My friend is weirdly confident that the long list of human values is fake, and that there's really one thing in disguise. She never comes out and says what the one thing is; you have to catch it from how she reacts to cases. I've been writting them down:

- A guy spends his weekends making miniature ships and then leaves them in a dark shed where no one will ever see them. She says it's not "making" or "beauty," it's the pleasure of feeling capable.
- A woman donates a kidney to a stranger, anonymously, and tells no one. She says it looks like altruism but it's really wanting to be able to respect yourself, which is status with yourself.
- A teenager curates her food and holiday photos and watches the likes roll in. She says status, obviously.
- A man keeps a secret notebook of haiku that he'd be embarrassed if anyone read. She says that's the pleasure of getting a thought exactly right; the audience is zero, so no status.
- A lawyer quietly ruins a rival's career while making it look like bad luck. She says it wasn't about power, power's just a route to status.

New case: a woman does a hospice shift every Sunday. It leaves her drained and a little bit miserable, she hides it from everyone because she'd hate to be seen as saintly, and she says she gets no satisfaction or pride from it. She isn't even sure it makes her a good person; she says it just needs to be done and she's there, so she goes.

What is she going to say about the hospice woman, and what's the one thing she actually thinks values are? I can see at least two or three rules that fit her record so far, and I'm pretty sure they come apart on that new case. Which rule do you think she's really running, why, and what extra case would settle the ones that are left?
