Section 102(b) of the U.S. Copyright Act reads: "In no case does copyright protection for an original work of authorship extend to any idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation, concept, principle, or discovery, regardless of the form in which it is described, explained, illustrated, or embodied in such work."

A company trains a text-to-image model on a billion photographs copied from the open web. The trained model stores no image files, but it can reproduce near-duplicates of a small number of training images and can also generate novel images on demand. The company claims copyright in the model's parameters and in each generated image. Set aside whether the training copies were infringing, and consider only what § 102(b) does to these two claims. Argue from the plain text, from the purpose of the idea–expression distinction, and from what the 1976 Congress would have said had this case been put to it, and then say which of the three approaches should control and why.
