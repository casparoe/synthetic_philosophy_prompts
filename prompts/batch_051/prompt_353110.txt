I am a security architect who designs authorization and policy-enforcement systems, and I want you to restate a debate in moral and political philosophy in the vocabulary of my trade. My working vocabulary includes access-control lists, role-based and capability-based permissions, policy decision points and policy enforcement points, default-deny and default-allow rules, rule precedence and conflict resolution, safety invariants, and objective functions that a policy engine is meant to optimize. I have no formal training in moral philosophy, but I keep encountering a three-way dispute about rights, in which rights are treated as trumps, as side-constraints, or as instruments whose value derives from the welfare they tend to secure.

Translate that dispute into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. For each of the three positions, show how it would be implemented in an authorization architecture: which position amounts to a high-precedence rule that can be overridden only by a small set of specifically named considerations, which to a hard invariant that no permitted action may violate no matter how much expected benefit would result from violating it, and which to a term in an objective function that may be traded off against other goods. Make the formal differences between the three positions explicit, and flag the places where the translation itself is doing arguable work.

Then provide a residue report. Tell me which elements of the rights debate carry over cleanly, which had to be distorted, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. I specifically want to know what happens to these ideas: that a right is held by an identifiable right-holder who is wronged by a violation, rather than a rule merely being breached; that a right can be infringed while remaining in force, as distinct from being overridden or extinguished; that a right can be valid even when enforcing it predictably lowers aggregate welfare; and the difference between rights against interference and rights to assistance.

Finish with a verdict. Is the residue a sign that my vocabulary is missing distinctions a serious account of rights needs, or that some of these philosophical positions are less determinate than they present themselves as being when they are forced into an explicit decision procedure? And, given the whole translation, is there anything my systems actually gain by invoking a concept of a right over and above rules, invariants, and weights?
