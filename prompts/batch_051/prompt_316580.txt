You are a professional philosopher specialising in the structure of moral reasoning and the design of decision-making institutions. Think carefully, and begin by brainstorming the main failure modes before you write your analysis.

Imagine that a national medical ethics board abolishes its written code of principles. Henceforth, a committee decides each disputed case solely by analogy to a public register of previously decided cases. A written decision may cite several earlier cases as positive or negative analogies, but it may not state any general rule, maxim, or test; any sentence that reads as a general principle must be rephrased as a remark about a particular case. After each decision, the new case is added to the register by majority vote, and any older entry can be retired by majority vote as no longer informative.

Analyse how this institution would actually behave. Treat it as a mechanism to be analysed rather than a policy to be praised or condemned. What does it incentivise among the committee members, the parties who bring cases, and those who lobby for the addition or retirement of particular cases? How would a sophisticated party try to steer the register, and with what second-order effects? Can the system remain stable, or will it drift—and drift toward what? Does the prohibition on general principles genuinely remove principle-based reasoning, or merely relocate it? Where useful, connect the analysis to the philosophical debate over whether sound moral judgement is a matter of applying general principles or of discerning what is salient in a particular case. Write your answer in British English.
