I am the community representative on my hospital's patient-safety committee, and I need to circulate a short memo before our vote next month. The committee is deciding whether to approve a six-month pilot of a commercial large language model that would draft replies to non-urgent patient portal messages, with a nurse reviewing and editing each draft before it is sent. The case for the pilot is speed and reduced backlog. The case against it is that the system is, in the strongest sense, a stochastic parrot: it writes fluently without understanding what the patient is asking or going through, and a confident, well-phrased draft could slip past a hurried reviewer or subtly steer a nurse's wording. Our real disagreement is not just about error rates; it is whether a system that produces language without understanding has any place in drafting communication with patients. Please write the memo with a clear, actionable recommendation and a time frame.

Please use exactly these section headings, in this order:

1. Recommendation — roughly 150 words. State the decision and any conditions upfront.
2. The proposal — roughly 200 words. Describe the pilot and what is at stake.
3. The case for approval — roughly 250 words.
4. The case against approval — roughly 300 words. Include the strongest version of the no-understanding objection, not a caricature of it.
5. Weighing and conditions — roughly 250 words. State what would have to change, or what safeguards would have to be in place, for the opposite decision to be worth reconsidering.
