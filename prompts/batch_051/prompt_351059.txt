So I just got done with a long, circular argument with a friend about the predictive processing / free energy principle thing, and I'm starting to think we never actually disagreed about any fact. He's in the camp that says FEP is the first real unification of mind and life — perception, action, attention, and homeostasis all fall out of minimizing free energy (or surprise, as he puts it) — and that anyone who calls it unfalsifiable just hasn't done the math. I'm in the camp that says it's closer to a framework than a theory: any system that persists in a changing environment is going to look like it's minimizing something like free energy, so the claim can't really be false, and the slogan-y parts — perception as controlled hallucination and all that — don't generate risky, detailed predictions. My working theory is that the actual disagreement isn't empirical; it's about what a theory of the mind is supposed to do, unify a lot of phenomena or make mechanistic predictions that could fail. Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a genuine empirical or formal dispute hiding underneath? And if I'm right, what would actually count as progress — a specific experiment, a formal constraint, a distinction — or should we just stop calling it a theory?
