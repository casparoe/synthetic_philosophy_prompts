Okay, so I teach an upper-level political philosophy course and I'm writing the final. I want one essay question on Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain argument, but I want it pitched quite a bit harder then the usual explain-the-difference-between-historical-and-patterned-principles question. The students know the basic story; what I want to force them into is the question of exactly what the argument is supposed to prove. Does it show that any patterned principle of justice would require continuous interference with people's lives, or only the weaker claim that voluntary transfers will tend to erode a pattern if nobody intervenes? Those are different claims and I think a lot of students slide between them.

So here's what I'd like from you. Draft the question, then a model answer of maybe 600-800 words, then a marking scheme out of 20. For the question, give them a small twist so they can't just rehearse the example: for instance, ask whether Nozick would have to call it just if a society started from a distribution that is just by his own standards and, through a long series of clearly voluntary trades and gifts, ended up with a distribution that exactly matches Rawls's difference principle. Then ask whether that possibility undermines Nozick's larger claim that liberty and patterns conflict, or whether it actually leaves the claim untouched because the real issue is the legitimacy of enforcing patterns rather than a pattern happening on its own. A top answer should, among other things, notice that Nozick's principle that just steps preserve justice seems to commit him to calling the resulting patterned distribution just, and then say something sharp about what that implies for his actual target. I'd also like the marking scheme to separate an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain, and to include one or two typical mistakes—like thinking the argument is a direct argument for libertarianism rather than an argument against end-state or patterned principles, or thinking that because the transfers are voluntary the justice of the starting point doesn't matter. Make it feel like a real final-exam question, say 90 minutes, and don't require any secondary literature or citations.
