But I don't think anyone has cleanly separated those proxies from the underlying moral question of whether being farther from a wrong in time changes what the wrongdoer owes, or what victims can claim, just because time has passed. Suppose evidence were perfectly preserved and a wrongdoer has lived a decent life for fourty years. Does the wrongdoer owe less on day 14,600 than on day one? Or is the moral ledger untouched by time, and limitation rules are purely second-best institutional compromises?

Also include a short paragraph on whether the answer should move actual limitation policy, since "time itself does no moral work" might in principle license much longer limitation periods but maybe it doesn't.
