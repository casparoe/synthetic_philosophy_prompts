I'm compiling a list of candidate answers to the question: why would an AI want anything at all, if nobody evolved it and nobody may have successfully put a goal into it? So far I have:
- fully designed goals (utility functions, reward channels, setpoints);
- RL training on a scalar reward, where the learned policy acts like it has preferences even though the reward number isn't the goal;
- instrumental convergence, where any final goal generates the usual subgoals (self-preservation, gathering resources, self-improvement);
- the mesa-optimization story, where the trained policy does internal search and ends up with its own objective that can come apart from the training objective;
- homeostatic/cybernetic drives, where the wanting is just corrective behavior that keeps some variable in bounds;
- the deflationary answer, where wanting is a predictive stance toward behavior rather than an internal fact;
- value/preference learning, where it picks up goals from humans or operator feedback.

I think that covers the usual families, so I need one more candidate answer that isn't a rewording, a special case, or a blend of those, and ideally one that doesn't lean on a designer's goals or on a reward/training signal. Give me the new candidate, say in a sentence or two how it differs from the closest two entries above, and then tell me whether you think it's a real mechanism or just a redescription, and how confident you are that it's genuinely distinct. If you think the question itself is mishapen — for instance that the word wanting is doing too much work, or that my families are secretly the same thing at different levels — say so plainly and fix the taxonomy instead of forcing a new entry.
