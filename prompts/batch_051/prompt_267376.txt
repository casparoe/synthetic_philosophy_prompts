His summary, as best I can reconstruct it: Kant wrote the essay during the French revolutionary wars and basically argued that lasting peace requires every state to become a democracy, that the state of anarchy between nations has to be replaced by a single world government with real enforcement powers, and that once those are in place peace is pretty much automatic, because in a democracy the voters who pay for and fight the wars will never vote themselves into one — so democracies by their nature won't attack each other. He also said Kant called for abolishing standing armies and for no country interfering in another's internal affairs, and that he supported a universal right to travel and trade anywhere.

I have no idea how much of this is Kant and how much is my friend tightening the screws, because the essay is old and I've only seen memes about it. It all honestly sounds like a just-so story for why a liberal international order should work, which makes me suspicious. I'd like an accuracy audit: go through his summary piece by piece, say what a careful reader of Kant would accept, what they'd push back on, and in particular whether Kant actually wanted a single world state or something less like that.
