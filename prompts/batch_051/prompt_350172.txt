I'm one of five faculty members on my university's academic integrity board, and we are deadlocked on a case that seems to be about complicity more than about who copied from whom. A third-year student, M., ran a large study group for a difficult required economics course. Three days before the take-home final, M. uploaded to the group's shared folder a document of fully worked solutions to the previous year's final exam, with a note telling members to treat it as a study aid and to check the derivations carefully. The final was open-book and take-home, but its instructions explicitly prohibited collaboration and stated that incorporating another student's written work would count as cheating. Three group members copied passages from M.'s document into their submitted finals, one nearly verbatim, and all three have admitted doing so. M. never saw their submissions, did not tell anyone to copy, and received nothing in exchange; M. says the file was meant for studying and that others' misuse of it is not M.'s responsibility. Half the board wants to find M. equally responsible for the cheating; the other half wants no finding at all.

I need to circulate a short decision memo before our meeting next week. It should recommend one concrete disposition for M. from the available range — no finding; a written warning; a reduced finding for facilitating academic dishonesty; or the same finding and sanction as the students who copied — and it should defend that recommendation with reasons the rest of the board can actually vote on. Please do not simply rest on our conduct code's definition of facilitation; several members regard that definition as exactly what is in dispute. Instead, work through the distinction between causing wrongdoing and participating in it: a person can enable, invite, or join wrongdoing without making a counterfactual difference to whether it occurs, and a person can make a causal contribution without sharing in the wrong. Say where M. falls, and give the strongest argument for the losing side its due before explaining why the recommendation is nonetheless the right one.
