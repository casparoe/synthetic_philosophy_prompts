Write a villanelle in British English, in the standard nineteen-line form with the usual rhyme and refrain scheme, titled 'An Antimeme'. Its subject is an antimeme: an idea whose content actively frustrates stable memory of it, so that the more clearly one grasps it, the less one can hold it — not an ordinary forgotten thought but a self-concealing one. Do not merely describe such an idea; enact one. Use the two refrains as a thought that keeps returning each time the mind lets it go, and let each repetition subtly shift or erode the apparent sense of the refrains, so that a reader's hold on them slips across the poem. In the final quatrain, where the refrains meet, stage the moment the idea becomes fully explicit and therefore begins to disappear. A reader familiar with antimemes should recognise the mechanism as accurate rather than decorative, and a reader who is not should still find the poem coherent and quietly unnerving. Do not allude to any existing fictional universe or quote from any real text.
