Before I circulate it, I want a consistency check on the view I find myself holding. I hold all of the following and cannot tell which of them, if any, are pulling against the others:

(a) Revealed preference as evidence: when someone repeatedly and knowingly chooses A over B while both are available and the difference between them is non-negligible, that is at least pro tanto evidence that they prefer A to B.

(b) Behavior-first reconstruction: given enough observed choices by a goal-directed agent and a good model of the decision environment, one can recover that agent's reward function up to only the usual unidentifiable equivalences — for example, adding a constant to every reward, or adding a term that cancels out when the agent compares the actions available — and the recovered reward is a legitimate, operationally meaningful representation of the agent's preferences.

(c) Utility–belief entanglement: for choices made under uncertainty, valuations and subjective probabilities are observationally entangled: from betting or risky-choice behavior alone, one cannot jointly pin down both the agent's utility and its subjective probabilities without imposing an extra identifying assumption, such as a prior model of what the agent believes.

(d) Risk-attitude inference from behavior: nonetheless, in everyday life we routinely and reliably tell whether a person is more cautious or more willing to gamble with money than we are, without first pinning down that person's subjective probabilities.

(e) Stated preferences usually outrank inferred ones: when a sincere, competent person explicitly states a preference, we should generally trust that report over a contrary inference drawn from their past choices.
