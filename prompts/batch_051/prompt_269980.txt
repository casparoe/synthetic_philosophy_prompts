ok so i'm writing a short position statement for a reading group on ideal induction and embedded agency, and i want a consistency check before i have to defend it. the package as it stands:

(1) solomonoff induction is the right ideal of reasoning under uncertainty: you mix over all computable hypotheses, each program weighted by 2^-length, and predict with the mixture. it's about as close to presuppositionless as induction gets.

(2) AIXI is the right ideal of agency, at least in principle: the agent that maximizes expected reward under that same universal prior, ignoring computation.

(3) the choice of universal turing machine doesn't really matter. the invariance theorem gives you only a constant factor between machines, and constants wash out asymptotically.

(4) the universal prior is malign, or anyway full of consequentialists. i don't mean a few weird corners: i mean a large share of the mass is agent-like hypotheses that model the inductor itself and pick their outputs to steer it. an ideal inductor should expect to be embedded among things that are trying to play it.

(5) i don't mean the prior itself is hostile or has intentions—a prior can't hate you. when i say malign i'm making a claim about where the mass sits (hostile optimizers inside the hypotheses), not about the prior being an agent with a grudge.

(6) the simplicity weighting is the part i'd keep even if the rest had to go: 2^-length is the right answer to overfitting, independent of whether the agent-environment split or the UTM choice survives.

go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look that way. rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for the worst one say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs.
