I’m not trained in philosophy; I once read Daniel Dennett’s story about a man whose brain is removed and kept alive in a vat while his body walks around controlled by radio. The original puzzle is whether the man is where his brain is or where his body is. I’ve been turning over a modified version in my mind. Suppose the radio link is perfect but introduces a two-second delay each way. When someone speaks to the body, the brain hears them two seconds later; when the brain decides to answer, the body produces the answer two seconds after that. To an observer, the body behaves like a person with a constant lag—delayed reactions, pauses before speech, movements that begin slightly after the brain’s choice. Meanwhile the brain in the vat experiences the world as though it is living two seconds behind everything its body does. There is no single moment at which the person seems to be in sync with both brain and body.

What I want to know is whether this delay changes the original puzzle. In the original, the person might identify with the body because that is where his eyes and ears are, even though the brain is elsewhere. With the delay, does that identification break down? Does the person now seem to be in the vat, watching a delayed recording? Or does the social fact that other people interact with the body still keep the person anchored there, just with a bad connection?
