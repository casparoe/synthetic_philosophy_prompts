I've been co-running a little weekly discussion group on stories and philosophy, and last month we did Le Guin's "The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas." The announced topic was utilitarian — can the city's happiness justify the child in the basement — but the session dissolved into something else. I noticed that nobody really argued for or against the Omelas deal on it's own terms. People just felt the story's pull and then either trusted that feeling or tried to explain it away. I've reread it twice since then, and I've become a bit suspicious of how the thing works on me. The city is made gorgeous and warm before we learn the price, so by the time the child shows up I'm already invested in thinking of Omelas as a good place. The child is an innocent human being rather than a number. The story simply stipulates that there is no third option — no reframing the deal, no rescuing the child and keeping the city — only accept it or leave. That's not an argument, that's a machine for manufacturing an intuition.

So: I want you to read "Omelas" as a case study in intuition pumps, in Dennett's sense and in the skeptical literature about them (how vivid cases persuade and mislead). Give me a fine-grained account of what judgment or emotion the pump is built to produce, and which specific narrative choices do the pumping. Be honest about where the story rigs the case instead of earning its conclusion. Then take the other direction: where does the story resist being reduced to a rigged intuition pump? I'm thinking especially of the walkers, who don't actually do anything for the child — that seems like Le Guin quietly admitting her own story can only produce a gesture, not an argument, and I want to know what that does to the trust we should place in the intuition. Finally, tell me what Omelas teaches about when a thought experiment is a legitimate tool and when it's just vivid rhetoric, because a friend of mine keeps deploying the story in arguments about how much we owe strangers, and I want to know whether it's giving me a reason or just grabbing me by the feelings. No citations needed, I care about the mechanics.
