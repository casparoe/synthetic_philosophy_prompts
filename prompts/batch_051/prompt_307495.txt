I'm on the terminology committee at a mid-sized AI safety org, and I need to send around a short written opinion before our Thursday vote. Here's the situation. A working group is proposing that we stop using "AGI" in all official output — papers, grant applications, policy briefs, job ads — and replace it with "high-impact general systems." Their pitch has two parts that I can't quite get apart. The descriptive part says "AGI" doesn't track a natural kind: it bundles capability, generality, autonomy, and a bunch of implied moral-status baggage, and different people in the field use it to mean different things, so it's making our internal disagreements and our policy asks fuzzier. The ameliorative part says the term is doing actively bad work with funders and policymakers — it sounds sci-fi and triggers the wrong comparisons — and a flatter term would make containment and coordination asks land better. The proposal is explicitly not a new benchmark or a capability threshold; it's a change of official vocabulary, with possible consequences for which scenarios the org treats as "the thing we're worried about."

I keep wavering on the philosophically weird bit. The authors slide between "this is what AGI actually means, if you look at usage" and "this is what AGI should mean, given our aims." Those feel like different projects. Analyzing a concept is more or less trying to get it right about what's already there; engineering one is deciding what would be useful and then trying to get a community to adopt it. The proposal seems to want the second while justifying itself with the first, and I suspect our committee can't make a clean call until those are seperated — including whether it's even legitimate for a safety org to deliberately push an engineered term rather than trying to describe the thing everyone else calls AGI.

Can you draft the committee's decision memo? It should recommend one concrete course of action — adopt the new term in all official comms, adopt it for some audiences/docs, keep "AGI" but publish an internal definitional standard, or reject the proposal outright — with a time frame for review. It also needs to give reasons the working group could actually respond to, and it should spend real time steelmanning the strongest consideration for whichever option loses. I'd particularly like the memo to address the fix-versus-analyse issue head-on, rather than quietly picking one of the two framings.
