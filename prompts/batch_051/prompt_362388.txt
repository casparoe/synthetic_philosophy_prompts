I retired some years ago from a career in field biology, mostly ornithology, and have been spending my retirement reading philosophy of biology alongside the animal-communication literature I once took for granted. My current preoccupation is the debate around the handicap principle and costly signaling: the claim that a signal's very costliness is what keeps it honest, and the dispute over whether that claim, in its strong form, is a general account of honest communication or merely one mechanism among several. Before I weigh the competing accounts, I want to fix what an adequate theory in this area would actually have to deliver.

I would like you to write the desiderata: the requirements any adequate theory of how animal signals are kept honest must satisfy. Please include at least the following. The distinction between signals, cues, and coercion, and why the theory's claims about honesty are answerable to it. The difference between a signal's being costly in the aggregate and its cost being differentially distributed, so that only low-quality or low-need signalers would pay more than the benefit of the signal is worth—the logic without which the handicap idea collapses into pointless self-punishment. The distinction between efficacy costs, paid by every signaler simply to produce and transmit the signal, and strategic costs, paid disproportionately by those who would benefit from exaggerating, with an account of why only one of these can carry the burden of keeping signals honest. The cases a theory must handle: mate-choice displays such as the peacock's train, stotting in gazelles, begging calls, status badges, warning coloration, and low-frequency deceptive mimicry. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and mark whether it is a non-negotiable demand on any adequate theory or a preference of one school. I am especially interested in two things. First, whether any subset of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied—for instance, whether one can coherently demand both that a theory put differential cost at the center of honesty and that it explain cheap yet stable honest signals. Second, which single requirement does the most to exclude popular but loose versions of the handicap idea. A short scorecard of the main existing accounts against your list is welcome, but keep the weight on the criteria rather than the contenders.

Two further constraints. Throughout, keep separate what is settled among those who work on animal communication from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus; I want to know where the agreement ends. And end with a single machine-readable last line, \answer{...}, containing a one-sentence summary of your main conclusion.
