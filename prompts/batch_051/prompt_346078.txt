I've been going back and forth on Searle's Chinese Room, and a friend sent me a summary from the intro material they use in their philosophy of mind course. Here's the paragraph:

"Searle's Chinese Room shows that a digital computer could never really understand Chinese. A man in the room follows rules for matching Chinese symbols, and from outside his answers are indistinguishable from a native speaker's; but the man himself doesn't understand Chinese, so the understanding can't be located in the man. Searle concedes that the whole room-plus-man-plus-rulebook might be said to 'understand,' but he thinks that's just a verbal dodge. His real point is that syntax is never sufficient for semantics, so passing the Turing test tells you nothing about whether a system has genuine intentionality. Thus any AI built purely on symbol manipulation, even one that behaves indistinguishably from a Chinese speaker, cannot be said to have a mind."

Something about this feels off to me, but I cant pin down exactly what. How fair is this as an account of Searle? Walk me through it more or less sentence by sentence: what's right, what's a common simplification, what Searle actually concedes or rejects, and whether the last sentence follows from the room argument or is a stronger claim than the argument actually supports.
