For an epistemology seminar I am submitting a short statement of my views on coincidence and anecdote, and I would like a consistency audit of it before I circulate the draft. My current commitments are these.

I accept a broadly Bayesian view of evidence: the evidential bearing of an observation on competing hypotheses is exhausted by how likely each hypothesis makes that observation, and facts about when or why an inquirer happened to form the hypothesis are not themselves evidence.

I think the birthday paradox shows that human surprise at coincidences is poorly calibrated. In a large field of opportunities, even a specific-looking match is often very likely somewhere in the data, so the move from that is too improbable to be chance to that is evidence of design is usually a base-rate error.

Still, I allow that coincidences can sometimes be evidence. A match that is specified in advance and is genuinely improbable under the chance model should raise one's confidence in a common-cause or intentional explanation.

I also think prediction and post hoc noticing are not symmetric: the same match predicted in advance supports a non-chance hypothesis more strongly than an otherwise identical match noticed only after it occurred, because after-the-fact noticing lets the explanation be fitted to the data.

But I hold that the reference class against which a match is evaluated is fixed by objective features of the sampling situation, such as how many opportunities there were, what counts as a match, and the selection rule, not by the subject's psychology. This is what blocks the trick of redescribing any occurrence until it looks miraculous.

Finally, on anecdotes: I think they are poor evidence for frequencies and causal effects, but a single well-verified anecdote can justify believing that an event-type occurs, since an existence claim needs only one instance.

I would like an audit rather than a defense or refutation. Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, and which pairs only appear to be? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to be revised and what the least costly revision would sacrifice.
