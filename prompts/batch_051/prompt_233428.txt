I teach an upper-level seminar in formal epistemology with about fourteen students, and I have one ninety-minute session to spend on van Fraassen's Reflection principle. Design a structured classroom activity that makes students feel the force of the principle as a coherence constraint on their own beliefs over time, rather than as an abstract formula. The activity should get them to first form a current credence about a concrete proposition, then predict the credence they will hold later after receiving a specified piece of information, and then discover through a betting mechanism that a divergence between the two makes them vulnerable to a sure loss across time. I want the rules to make the divergence tempting—for example, by making the later information psychologically salient but, by the participants' own lights, uninformative, so that some students expect their future selves to overreact. The betting should use poker chips or points, not real money, and should be arranged so that a student who predicts a gap between current and future credence can be shown to have accepted a set of bets that loses no matter what. The debrief should map what happened onto the principle: roughly, that a coherent agent's current credence in a proposition should equal the agent's current expectation of their future credence in it, except where the future credence is expected to be shaped by causes the agent does not currently count as evidence. The facilitator should not state the principle up front; participants should run into the problem themselves. Please include the propositions or materials to use, the timing for each phase, the exact rules of the betting scheme, what the facilitator should say during the debrief, a note on what will predictably go wrong the first time it is run and how to handle it, and an honest note on which feature of the Reflection principle the activity cannot reproduce and must be supplied separately.
