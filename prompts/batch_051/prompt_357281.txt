I've been trying to figure out whether my views on humanitarian intervention amount to anything more coherent than liking some wars and hating others. Going by my raw calls on the actual cases:

Kosovo 1999. NATO bombed Serbia for two and a half months with no Security Council authorization, framed as stopping the ethnic cleansing of Kosovar Albanians, not toppling Milošević, though it did force him to pull his forces out and accept terms. I think it was justified, outright, even though it was formally illegal.

Rwanda 1994. No one intervened. Clearly, monstrously wrong not to have. No hesitation.

Iraq 2003. Not justified. The WMD case collapsed, there was no Council authorization, and the humanitarian justification was pasted on afterward to a war whose results speak for themselves. But I can't make my rule be that Council authorization decides it, because that would make Kosovo wrong and I don't think it was.

Libya 2011. The Council authorized force to protect civilians as Gaddafi's forces moved on Benghazi, and at first I supported exactly that. Then the operation slid into de facto regime change from the air — hitting retreating convoys, effectively fighting for one side in a civil war — and the country came apart. My call: the initial protection was justified; the slide into regime change was not. But I also can't shake the thought that stopping mid-air would have just let Gaddafi massacre the rebels city by city.

Syria 2013. After the chemical attacks near Damascus, the US drew its red line and then did not strike. I did not support strikes, since a few cruise missiles were not going to fix anything, but the non-response still felt like a moral collapse. I genuinely do not know the right call, and I do not know what principle would rule on it.

I keep reaching for a two-clause version of something like this: you may intervene, without Council authorization if you have to, to stop or prevent mass atrocities, when you have a realistic shot at doing more good than harm; and you may not intervene to change a regime as such. On paper that handles my whole list — but that is exactly what makes me suspicious. Maybe I have just back-fitted the principle to the wars I happened to like. The forcing Milošević to accept terms part of Kosovo looks like regime change under another name, yet I was fine with the whole operation. Libya had authorization and a protective script, yet I got uneasy exactly when the goal became removing Gaddafi. Syria seems to satisfy the first clause more than Iraq ever did, and I still cannot endorse it. So maybe the principle is right and my unease is noise, or maybe something else is doing the work: legality, likely outcome, whether the interveners really had humanitarian motives, or whether the intervention had a built-in incentive to expand until the leader fell.

What I want from you: the best principle you can extract from my calls, stated precisely, with each clause tied to the case that makes it necessary. Then tell me which of my calls it cannot accomodate, or which ones it forces me to give up. And test it on something I did not list: a state is about to massacre a minority; intervention could stop it in weeks with low risk, but the intervening coalition announces it will install a new government with its own bad human-rights record. Does your principle intervene or not — and does that outcome make me want to revise any of my real-case calls?
