Ibn Khaldun gives us a cycle of dynasties: a hardened group from the periphery with strong asabiyyah (roughly group solidarity / esprit de corps) seizes power, builds a state, then over a few generations gets softened by luxury and city life, loses its asabiyyah, and gets displaced by a fresher group from the margins — and the cycle repeats, each dynasty lasting something like three or four generations.

I vaguely remember Polybius treats a mixed constitution like Rome's as a way of exiting the cycle, whereas Ibn Khaldun seems to think the dynastic cycle is just how history works, with religion or renewed asabiyyah maybe delaying it but not abolishing it. Is that reading right, and if it is, is that a real theoretical difference or just Polybius being more of a political reformer?
