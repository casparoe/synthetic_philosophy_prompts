Write me an ironic op-ed, maybe 650 words, in the voice of a committed self-indication assumer who is annoyed that philosophers keep using Bostrom's phrase "the presumptuous philosopher" to describe his position. The piece should be deadpan and readable to someone who's never heard of SSA or SIA, but a specialist should recognize the actual argument. The target is the charge of presumptuousness, not any named living person.

Here's the setup: your character thinks SIA is correct — roughly, that when two otherwise decent theories differ only in how many observers they predict, the one predicting more observers is more likely, because your own existence is evidence. He's sick of being called arrogant for this. Every complaint he makes has to be one he can actually make without contradicting himself — don't let him slip into SSA halfway through unless that IS the joke. By the end, the reader should have felt the real question: does SIA's license to update on your own existence force you to be epistemically presumptuous, and is that a bug or just a feature?

The humor has to come from the actual content: he should keep insisting he's humble, but his humility is exactly what forces him to endorse grand cosmological claims about vast numbers of observers. Maybe he complains that the real presumptuousness is thinking small — that assuming the universe is sparsely populated is just as much a guess, but with worse anthropic hygene. Keep it in first person, a bit cranky, like an op-ed in a philosophy blog.

Before you write, think through: what exactly is the presumptuous philosopher thought experiment, and what would a defender of SIA actually say about it? Then write the op-ed in that voice.
