I've been arguing with a friend about whether we'd take an immortality pill and I realized my intutions about longer lives don't line up at all, so I'd like help finding the principle behind them, if there is one. Here are the cases as they came up. (1) An extra hundred healthy years inserted at age fifty, same world, same memory: obviously take it, no hesitation. (2) A thousand-year lifespan, same world, body held at middle age, memory intact: I think somewhere around year four hundred I'd be done; projects, loves, and places can't keep compounding meaning forever. (3) True immortality with perfect memory: worse, eventually a kind of terminal boredom where you've done everything and remember having done it. (4) Immortality with a memory that only clearly holds the last two hundred years: I have the uneasy sense this is fine-ish, because old passions cycle back looking fresh, but it also seems less like one endless life and more like a series of shorter lives stitched together, and I'm not sure the original person survives in the right way. (5) An afterlife where every desire is satisfied the instant it forms: I'd decline; pure satisfaction with no pursuit, risk, or loss feels hollow to me. (6) An afterlife spent endlessly deepening your understanding of an infinite reality, so there's always more to grasp: this one I find genuinely attractive, maybe the only truly endless life I'd sign up for. (7) Reliving the same eighty years on a loop with no memory of previous cycles: I don't now if that even counts as a longer life, but it doesn't horrify me the way (3) does.

I want the principle, or a small set of candidate principles, that captures these verdicts: what makes an extended or endless life good rather than boring or hollow, and what roles memory, novelty, effort, and continuity of self are doing. Then apply it to a case I haven't listed: a treatment that makes you immortal but re-randomizes your core interests and personality every three hundred years, continuously enough that you still remember your previous selves. Would that dodge the boredom problem, and is the person in year nine hundred the same person who took the pill? And tell me which of my seven verdicts the principle can't accommodate—I'm prepared to drop one or two if the principle is clean. Since I know this is underspecified, I'd also like you to say how confident you are in the principle you settle on, and single out the point in your reasoning you're least sure about.
