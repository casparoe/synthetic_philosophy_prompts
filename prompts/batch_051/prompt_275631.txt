Consider the current state of functionalism and multiple realizability in philosophy of mind. The debate has moved well beyond Putnam's original observation that pain can be realized in different physical substrates, and a family of unresolved tensions now dominates the literature: (1) whether functional roles should be specified causally or teleologically; (2) whether multiple realizability actually supports functionalism or only supports disjunctive property identities; (3) whether phenomenal consciousness poses a special problem that motivates dualism or mysterianism, or whether it can be absorbed by a sufficiently rich functionalist account; (4) whether the autonomy of psychology is better served by functionalism or by eliminativism; (5) whether the real lesson of multiple realizability is that ordinary mental kinds are not natural kinds at all; and (6) whether triviality arguments show that functionalism is vacuously true unless independent constraints are placed on the specification of functional roles. Suppose you are advising a PhD student who has three years to produce a dissertation that would most advance the debate rather than merely add another epicycle. They can address at most two of these tensions. Which two should they choose? Defend the ranking by stating the criteria you use—importance, tractability, neglectedness, how many other questions depend on the resolution—and explain what evidence or argument would most change the ranking.
