I've been back at my aunts church group lately because she asked me to drive her, and last week the priest spent most of the meeting consoling a woman with awful intrusive thoughts. He kept saying the sin is in the consent, not in the act — Abelard, apparently — and it sounded exactly right. But when I asked him afterwards what consent has to be for that to work, it went fuzzy fast. He gestured at three stages: the thought arriving, taking pleasure in it, and then consenting. He could not explain where taking pleasure ends and consenting begins, and now I'm stuck there.

I want a working definition of consent, in that Abelardian sense (the thing that makes a sin a sin), that correctly sorts all seven of these cases into sin / not sin:

1. A married man gets propositioned, a sexual fantasy flashes up, and he feels a wave of excitement for maybe two seconds before he snaps out of it, shuts the thought down, and doesn't go back to it.
2. A woman decides in her heart to commit adultery with a coworker, agrees to meet him at a hotel, and drives there; he dies in a crash on the way, so nothing physically happens.
3. A man is drugged without knowing it and, barely conscious, is made to mouth the words of a renunciation of his faith. He has no idea what he is saying.
4. A man spends an hour deliberately replaying a sexual fantasy about a coworker, enjoying every image, though he has no intention of ever acting on it.
5. A woman renounces her faith out loud because a gunman says he will shoot her child if she doesn't; she hates every word as she says it.
6. A man honestly thinks he is adding sugar to his friend's tea, but it is actually poison and the friend dies. He never meant any harm.
7. A devout man has violent blasphemous thoughts that disgust him; he fights them all day and never accepts a single one.

Im fairly sure 1, 3, and 7 are not sin and that 2 is, but any rule I cook up to keep those four in place ends up swallowing 4 or 5 or 6, or else it makes consent impossible to ever be sure about. Propose the definition, run it through all seven, tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and then either revise it or tell me which of my intuitions I should drop.
