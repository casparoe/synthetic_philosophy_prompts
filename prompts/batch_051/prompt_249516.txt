Write me the joint statement that a deflationist about truth and a correspondence theorist about truth would sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want the kind of thing they'd actually bring to a reading group, so make it concrete and a little uncomfortable for both of them, not diplomatic paste.

Start with a numbered section of claims both can sign. Cover at least: what they both accept about particular truth ascriptions, for example that the sentence snow is white is true just in case snow is white; whether either side needs a special ontology of facts to make sense of the word true in I didn't hear what she said, but whatever she said is true; and what they both accept about blind generalizations like everything he says is true. Then have them state which of their long-running arguments turned out to be verbal, and say exactly what was verbal about it.

Then give a disagreements table. For each row: a neutral statement of the question, then the maximal wording the deflationist would own, then the maximal wording the correspondence theorist would own — strong enough that the other side would flinch, but not a caricature. Include rows on (a) whether the equivalence schema exhausts the concept of truth or is just an adequacy condition on something deeper, (b) whether truth does any causal or explanatory work in why acting on true beliefs tends to go better, (c) whether being true consists in some truth-bearer's matching or corresponding to mind-independent facts, and (d) whether the norm of truth in assertion and belief adds anything over and above the schema.

Then give the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would get at least one side to revise. For each crux, specify a concrete case, armchair test, or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts the case will show. One crux has to be built around a thought experiment I'll call the sealed oracle. A box displays a sentence with a light marked TRUE or FALSE next to it; the box always lights TRUE just when the sentence matches what's actually going on in a room the subject can check at any time. The only way a child in the experiment ever hears the word true is by watching this box, and she soon goes around correcting people with no, that's not true. The deflationist and the correspondence theorist each write down in advance wether the child's predicate will behave like our word true, what the child's corrections would show about the schema, and which possible outcomes would count against their own view.

Close with a short paragraph on what each side would have to see in the other side's theory before calling it empty or circular, and whether the old fight over whether sentences or propositions are the primary truth-bearers was carrying any real weight.
