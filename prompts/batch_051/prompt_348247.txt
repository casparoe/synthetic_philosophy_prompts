I serve on the compensation and governance committee of a mid-sized nonprofit. A pay-equity review has just concluded, and the committee must decide whether to adopt full salary transparency: publishing each employee's salary and the formula by which pay is set. One faction supports publication, arguing that secrecy protects the organization rather than employees, and that a defensible pay structure should be defensible in the open. Another faction opposes it on different grounds: whatever the schedule's merits, making salaries public will predictably breed envy and resentment among colleagues, corrode working relationships, and turn private compensation into a standing occasion for invidious comparison. The reply is that this treats envy and resentment as personal vices to be managed, when envy can also be excusable — even a signal of injustice — where it tracks a disparity that makes it hard for the worse-off to regard their position as acceptable; if the pay structure cannot withstand being seen, suppressing that signal is worse than the discomfort of hearing it. Draft the decision memo I will circulate before the vote. Recommend a concrete policy (publish, do not publish, or publish with specified safeguards), give the reasons for it, and engage honestly with the strongest consideration on the losing side. At the end, add a short note to me stating how confident you are in the recommendation and the single point on which you are least sure.
