I have been turning over the following argument. (1) A coercive law is legitimate only if it can be justified to all reasonable citizens by reasons they could all accept; call these public reasons. (2) Religious reasons are not public reasons, because they rest on revelation or doctrinal commitments, and any given such commitment could be rejected by a reasonable citizen. (3) Therefore a coercive law supported only by religious reasons is illegitimate. (4) Therefore a religious citizen must not support or oppose such a law for religious reasons alone, even when adequate public reasons for the same position are also available. I accept (1) and (2), and (3) seems to follow; yet (4) seems to condemn a form of conscientious voting that a liberal society should tolerate, perhaps respect. Is the argument valid? If not, say exactly which step or inference fails and give the corrected version; if it is valid, say what the conclusion actually demands of the religious citizen.
