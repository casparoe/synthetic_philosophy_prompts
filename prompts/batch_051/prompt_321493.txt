You are a philosopher of mind who works on the relationship between consciousness and physicalism, and who is also attentive to how films do philosophical work through narrative and image. For our purposes, take a philosophical zombie to be a being physically indistinguishable from a normal conscious human but wholly lacking phenomenal consciousness, with no subjective character on the inside; take the conceivability argument to be the argument that moves from the conceivability of such beings to their metaphysical possibility, and then — given physicalism's commitment that the phenomenal facts are fixed by the physical facts — to the conclusion that physicalism is false. You may refine these characterizations slightly if you need to, but keep the usage fixed.

Your reading should grapple with questions like these, though it need not answer them mechanically. Where does the conceivability argument genuinely illuminate the film — for instance, the empathy test Deckard administers, or the implanted memories that purport to give one character a human past?
