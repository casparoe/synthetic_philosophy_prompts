Okay, here's one I want you to actually draft, not just outline. Write the joint statement that a committed qualia realist and a committed Dennettian qualia-quiner could both sign at the end of a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The realist holds that there are determinate facts about how things seem to a subject, over and above dispositions to judge or report. The quiner holds that "qualia" is an incoherent posit, and that Chase-and-Sanborn style cases show the term isn't doing the explanatory work it's asked to do.

Make it exact, not diplomatic mush. The document should have: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims, including what both sides will jointly say about the Chase and Sanborn cases themselves—same coffee, same reports, two different stories about what changed; (2) the residual disagreement, phrased so each side would accept the wording of its own view as not being a strawman; (3) the two or three cruxes—the subquestions such that settling one would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a probe or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Format the last part as a disagreements table. Flag anything you think is really a verbal dispute, and seperate it out from the substantive disagreement.

And don't let either side coast. The realist has to say under what conditions she'd concede the posit is empty, and the quiner has to say what would make him reinstate it. Work that into the document itself, not just in commentary around it.
