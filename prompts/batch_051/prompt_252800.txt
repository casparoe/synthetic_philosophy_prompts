I'm in a 400-level seminar on Hegel's Phenomenology of Spirit and we just spent two weeks on the lordship–bondsman section. The prof kept stressing a point I find genuinely weird: Hegel seems to think that recognition is only worth something if it comes from a self-consciousness you can recognize back, and that the lord's recognition is therefore hollow because it comes from a bondsman he treats as a thing. A friend of mine keeps saying this maps exactly onto influencers, where the influencer is the lord and the followers are the bondsmen — or maybe the platform is the lord and everyone else is its bondsmen. I'm not convinced, since nobody is actually risking their life over a follow. But there is something similar: influencers become kind of dependend on an audience whose taste they openly despise, and followers do all this invisible work (scrolling, engaging, giving attention) that the platform monetizes.

For a seminar paper I want to make the strongest case for what Hegel, dropped into the present, would have said about the whole economy of likes, followers, and verified status. Would he count a follower's one-tap recognition as genuine recognition in his technical sense? Why or why not? And would he see the same unsatisfied, unstable struggle he diagnoses in lordship and bondage, or would he say this is a different phenomenon — recognition from an anonymous aggregate with no actual confrontation? I care less about whether social media is good or bad than about what the internal logic of the dialectic forces a Hegelian to say, so keep it tied to the specific beats: the life-and-death struggle, one-sided recognition, work, and fear.
