i've been tinkering with what i call degeneration debt, and i want it checked. Lakatos gives us hard cores, protective belts, positive and negative heuristics, and progressive vs degenerating problemshifts. My proposed addition: a research programme incurs a unit of debt whenever it handles an anomaly with a belt adjustment that predicts nothing new, and it pays down a unit whenever a belt adjustment leads to a confirmed novel prediction. So far that's just bookkeeping. The substantive claim is that the debt is causal. High-debt programmes start behaving differently from the inside: they stop working the positive heuristic and mostly produce belt patches; they retell old patches as if the predictions had been their all along; they get defensive about the belt rather than the core. I think this is what a degenerating programme feels like to the people inside it, and I think Lakatos's official story misses it because it has no path-dependence—two programmes that look identical this year, one old and debt-heavy and one young and clean, are actually in different states.

The difference shows up in two kinds of fix. One team hits an anomaly and adds a knob that just absorbs it: debt up. Another team adds an auxiliary hypothesis that also predicts some independent observation that then gets confirmed: debt down, and the fix becomes part of the positive heuristic. The first team's knob gets renegotiated every few years, and each renegotiation is another unit of debt.

Where I'm honestly unsure: am I just renaming 'degenerating problemshift' with a finance metaphor, or does the stock/flow distinction plus the insider phenomenology add something real? And if this is already a thing under another name, what's it called? Give me the three strongest objections, and tell me whether any of them is fatal rather than just uncomfortable.
