In a philosophy of psychiatry seminar I am preparing, we will assess diagnostic classification not as a neutral catalogue but as an interactive institution. The starting premise will be Hacking's looping effect: those who receive a psychiatric classification often respond to it — as patients, as service users, as persons explaining themselves to others and to themselves — and their responses can alter the very phenomena the category was intended to describe. A category can stabilize the pattern it names or drive it in new directions; either way, the objects of classification are not fixed specimens waiting to be catalogued.

Before we argue about whether this or that taxonomy is an improvement, I want the standard of improvement itself worked out. Give me the desiderata list for any adequate account of psychiatric classification under this looping constraint. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that justifies it. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of a particular camp — reliability-minded clinicians, researchers who want stable natural kinds, service-user movements who want first-person authority over their own self-description, health systems that want billable and administratively tractable categories.

I am especially interested in where the list conflicts with itself. Tell me which pairs of desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied while the loop is doing genuine work — for example, where a classification stable enough to function as the same instrument across clinicians, studies, and years is precisely a classification that misdescribes or harms the people it classifies — and say whether the conflict is a real contradiction or a negotiable trade-off. Also identify which single requirement does the most to exclude the picture of psychiatric categories as fixed biomedical kinds.

You may close with a compressed scorecard of a purely biomedical taxonomy and a looping-sensitive alternative against the list, but the desiderata and their motivating cases are the deliverable; do not turn this into a literature review or a history of any particular manual.
