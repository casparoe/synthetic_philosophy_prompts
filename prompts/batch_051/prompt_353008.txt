I am drafting a short statement of my present commitments for a seminar on Buddhist ethics and its relation to consequentialism, and I would like a consistency audit before I present it. Here is what I currently hold:

(1) The basic criterion of rightness is suffering and its relief: an action is wrong to the extent that it causes or risks dukkha for sentient beings, and right to the extent that it prevents or reduces dukkha.

(2) Nevertheless, in Buddhist terms the moral quality of an action is settled by cetanā—volition or intention—rather than by what actually results from it. Outwardly identical conduct can be kusala or akusala depending on the state of mind behind it.

(3) The three poisons—lobha, dosa, and moha—are unwholesome in themselves, not merely in virtue of their likely downstream effects; their badness is not something I infer case by case from what they produce.

(4) There is no self who acts or who suffers. A person is a causally continuous stream of aggregates, and moral credit or blame attaches to links in that stream rather than to some owner standing over and above it.

(5) Karma and rebirth are true in a robust sense: intentional actions shape future experience, including across lives, through a causal process that is not merely a metaphor for habit formation within a single life.

(6) The highest good is not simply the reduction of suffering within the round of rebirth, but release from that round. Nirvana is categorically different from any state of the aggregates that could be reached by maximizing welfare.

Please audit this set for me. I want to know which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension, and how the genuine tensions should be ranked by severity. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the smallest repair would cost. Then tell me which single revision of the set would do the most work toward restoring consistency. I would rather have a discriminatory audit than a list of every possible conflict.
