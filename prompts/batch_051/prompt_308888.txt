I run a reading group on the philosophy of social science, and two of us have been arguing for months: one is a committed methodological individualist, the other a committed holist about social explanation. Rather than keep talking past each other we've agreed to write an adversarial-collaboration document, and I want you to draft it for us.

The individualist thinks explanations of social phenomena should bottom out, at least in principle, in facts about individuals — their beliefs, preferences, choices, and interactions — and that any explanation that doesn't show how the macro pattern arises from those is at best a place-holder. The holist thinks some social facts — institutions, norms, network structures, positional distributions — are explanatory in their own right and can't be restated one-individual-at-a-time without losing whatever does the explaining; structure constrains individuals, not just the other way around.

Write the document both could sign. It should contain:
1. A numbered list of agreed claims, substantial enough not to be vacuous and worded so neither feels it conceded something.
2. A disagreements table: each row gives the sub-dispute in neutral language, then the individualist's formulation of their view, then the holist's, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording.
3. A section on cruxes — the sub-questions where settling one would actually move at least one of them. I suspect one crux is whether social facts are ever more than shorthand for complex individual-level facts, and another is whether macro-to-micro causation makes sense, but check whether that's right rather than taking my word for it.
4. For each crux, at least one concrete case or piece of evidence both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Use cases like a bank run, a checkerboard-style segragation model, or an institution that survives complete turnover of it's members.

Also, if part of their dispute is just verbal, say which part and why. And if a crux isn't testable in practice, say what the document should record instead of a prediction.

Don't give me a literature summary or reading list — I want the actual joint statement, with the residual disagreement made exact.
