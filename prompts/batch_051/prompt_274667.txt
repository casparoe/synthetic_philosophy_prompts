I work as a software engineer on applied machine learning products, and a proposal has just reached my team: build a griefbot feature that lets users upload a deceased relative's saved messages, letters, and voicemails and then converse with a bot that replies in the dead person's style. I have no philosophical training, so I would like an answer that argues from ordinary cases rather than from terminology. The contrast I cannot shake is this. The people around me reread letters from the dead, replay their voicemails, and watch old videos of them; this is treated as normal, healthy, even sacred. But when the same materials are used to power something that answers back, many of the same people call it creepy, exploitative, or a refusal to let the dead rest. Stated plainly, the asymmetry is: replaying a record the dead person actually produced is widely accepted, while generating new speech attributed to them from the same record is widely condemned. Does anything justify treating these two so differently? For example, is the difference that the bot invents words the dead person never chose, that it manufactures an impression of present responsiveness, that it uses someone's archive without their consent, that it introduces commercial incentives into mourning, or something else? Or is our comfort in the one case and revulsion in the other simply an inconsistency that ordinary grief lives with? Please find the strongest candidate justification, test it against nearby practices such as an actor improvising in a dead relative's voice at a memorial or speaking aloud at their grave, and say whether the asymmetry should be kept or given up.
