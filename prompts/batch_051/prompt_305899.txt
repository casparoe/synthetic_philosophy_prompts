I'm in a philosophy of mind reading group, and last week I tried to reconstruct the causal exclusion argument from scratch so I could explain it to a flatmate who does biology. The group is broadly non-reductive physicalist, so we came in wanting to keep both mental causation and the idea that mental properties aren't type-identical to physical ones. The version I came up with runs on rails, but then I noticed it doesn't actually care about the mental at all, which makes me think I've either botched the reconstruction or the argument proves way to much.

The argument goes: (1) Mental properties are distinct from physical properties, and every physical event has a sufficient physical cause. (2) If a mental property caused some bit of behaviour, that behaviour would have two sufficient causes — the mental property, and the physical state that realises it and is already sufficient for the behaviour. (3) That looks like systematic overdetermination: every alleged case of mental causation would involve two sufficient causes at once, which is too high a price to pay, so mental properties never cause behaviour. (4) Nothing in (1)–(3) turns on the property being mental rather than biological, chemical, geological or economic, as long as it is distinct from but realised by physical properties. (5) So a hurricane's being a hurricane doesn't cause the damage, only its constituent particles do; a gene's being for blue eyes doesn't cause the blue eyes, only the base-pair sequence does; a recession's being a recession doesn't cause the job losses, only the underlying transactions do. (6) But that is absurd, or at least it abandons the causal talk of every science above fundamental physics.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? If it doesn't go wrong, is the conclusion just something we have to live with? I'd like a diagnosis that names the exact step to reject and prices the alternatives — I'd rather keep mental properties as causes and keep biological causation than give up the exclusion principle, but I want to know what I'm actually paying. Oh, and could you write the answer in British English?
