I'm a novelist, and for the novel I'm writing I need an in-world document: a decision memo from the independent oversight board of an AI lab. The situation: the lab wants to deploy an 'embedded' agent — one that can observe and modify parts of its own training setup and reward model, so it's part of the environment it's acting on rather than a sealed-off mind with a clean boundary. The board has to recommend whether to allow the deployment, block it, or allow it with conditions, with a concrete time frame. I want the memo to take the philosophical issue seriously: when an agent is embedded in the world it's optimising, the usual distinctions between the system, its model of the world, and the world itself get blurry, and the agent can reason about how its own actions change the training distribution it's part of. Does that create a risk a real oversight board should treat as decisive, or is it just a formal curiosity? Draft the memo in British English, with a clear bottom line and an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side.
