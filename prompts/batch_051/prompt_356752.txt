I'm a water manager for a mid-size city, and every couple years I have to decide whether to put real money into flood protection — raising a levee, upsizing culverts, buying out a few houses. I'm not a climate scientist and can't afford a custom study, so I work off the same publicly availible downscaled climate model ensembles everyone uses: twenty to forty models that disagree about whether our big storms get 10% wetter or 40% wetter by mid-century, and sometimes disagree about the direction of change entirely. I keep reading that the ensemble spread isn't a probability forecast, that models share code so they aren't really independent, and that robustness across model families matters more than the mean — but I still have to pick a number to design to. Can you write me a decision procedure I could actually run, like a checklist: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer, where it says stop and don't build yet, where it says build to this level, and where it has to hand the choice to me or the board because it's a values call rather than science. Then run it on two cases: a levee sized for the 100-year event where the models split between 10% and 40% wetter, and a culvert where a third of the models get drier, a third stay about the same, and a third get wetter. Be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and say where the whole framework is most likely to fail or what would change your mind about it. End with a one-sentence summary as the very last line.
