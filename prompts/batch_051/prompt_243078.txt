I have been tutoring first-year writing this term, and a disagreement with the course director has forced me to think more carefully about what we mean when we say something is bad grammar. She wants me to mark constructions as errors whenever a good style manual would, but I have started to suspect that the manuals are doing several different jobs at once. So I made myself three criteria. A construction counts as bad grammar if (1) it would not appear in the unplanned speech of any native dialect, so it is not a legitimate regional or social variety; (2) it actually gets in the way of understanding, not merely fails to match a school rule; and (3) it is not established in edited standard prose, whatever happens in ordinary speech.

These criteria felt clear until I met the phrase "between him and I" in a student essay. My colleague would correct it reflexively. But the construction is used in speech by many educated native speakers, often when they are trying to sound careful; it does not impede comprehension in the least; and it is nonetheless rejected by most style manuals as a hypercorrection. Does "between him and I" count as bad grammar under my criteria? Go through each criterion and say where the phrase falls. Where the criteria leave the answer underdetermined, tell me whether that means the criteria need changing, or whether my idea of bad grammar was doing something else all along—register, not grammar. If the case forces a revision, state the revised criteria and test them once more on this example.
