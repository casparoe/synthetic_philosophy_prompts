I write a small rationalist-adjacent blog and want to build a "which theory of deplatforming do you actually hold?" quiz for my readers. The idea is that most people come in with a label — free-speech person, harm-reduction person, whatever — but their actual views are a mess of case-specific calls, and I want a quiz that sorts them by the calls they make rather than the label they like. So no questions that just ask "is deplatforming censorship?" or "do you believe in free speech?"

Please write 8–10 questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key. Use at least these leaves: (1) a classical-liberal free-speech-prior view that counts deplatforming as very hard to justify, (2) a self-defense/incapacitation view where the question is whether the person is actively making others unsafe and proportionality doesn't really apply, (3) a proportionality/desert view where the sanction has to fit the wrong, (4) a power/structural view where deplatforming the powerful is different in kind from deplatforming the marginal, and (5) a restorative view that cares whether there's a path back. The tricky split I want the quiz to force is between (2) and (3): I'm pretty sure half my readers think they hold both, and they cant. So include at least two cases where a self-defense logic says "ban, and stop worrying about fit" but a proportionality logic says "not for *that*," or the reverse.

Case types I want in the mix: a Substack or Patreon demonetizing a writer for edgy-but-not-threatening material; a mob trying to get someone fired from their day job over an old tweet; a university disinviting a speaker; a Discord server kicking a member for off-server posts; a mass-reporting campaign against someone whose views the campaigners find genuinely frightening; and a person deplatformed years ago who has since apologized and wants back on. Vary the power asymmetry across the cases.

Also flag any two leaves the quiz can't actually seperate cleanly, and if there are answer combinations that are coherent but have no named position, note whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
