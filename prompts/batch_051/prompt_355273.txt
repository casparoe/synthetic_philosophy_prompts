I've been keeping a running list of what a friend of mine will pay for different hypothetical bets, because she has strong opinions about long-shot gambles and I can never work out the rule behind them. The question is always the most she'd pay, in cash, to play once. For a coin flip that pays $200 on heads and nothing on tails, she'd pay $80. For a 1-in-10 chance of $1,000, she'd pay $50. For a 1-in-100 chance of $10,000, she'd pay $20. For a 1-in-1,000 chance of $100,000, she'd pay $8. And for a 1-in-10,000 chance of a million dollars, she'd pay $3. The average payout on all five is the same hundred dollars, so she can't just be going by the average.

Last week I ran into someone pushing a coin game and I realized I couldn't predict what she'd say. The game is: flip a fair coin until it lands heads. If the first heads is on the first flip you get $2, on the second flip $4, on the third flip $8, on the fourth flip $16, and so on, doubling each time it hasn't happend yet. The guy running it claimed the average payout is infinite, since the coin could keep landing tails forever, and was asking a $100 entry fee. What would my friend make of that? Based on her five prices, what are the plausible rules she could be running, what does each one say about the $100 entry fee (or the most she'd pay for that game), and what extra bet could I offer her to tell the rules apart? I don't just want a guess about the hundred dollars; I want to know which rule she's actually using.
