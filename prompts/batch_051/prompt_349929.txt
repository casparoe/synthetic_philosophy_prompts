For most of my career I worked as a physical chemist, and since retiring I have been reading my way into epistemology and the philosophy of mind. The question that has come to occupy me is whether rationality can be trained something like a martial art: whether there is a kind of kata-style practice, an artificial choreography that isolates a single move, whose repetition improves reasoning in the messy real-world contexts where it matters. I have written down the commitments I have reached on this, and I would like them audited before I present them to a reading group. I am not asking for a survey of the literature or for pointers to authors; I am asking whether the set of things I currently believe hangs together.

Here is the set.

(1) Rationality is a skill, or a family of skills, and like a martial art it is improved mainly by deliberate practice at the edge of one's current ability, not principally by absorbing theory and not simply through natural ability.

(2) Decontextualized drills, such as probability puzzles, calibration exercises, fallacy-identification tasks, and kata-style routines that isolate a single move, improve real-world reasoning in contexts that do not resemble the drill. I accept this because the same normative standards, consistency, calibration, expected-value reasoning, govern reasoning across domains.

(3) Transfer of trained abilities is usually narrow and brittle. From what I know of the expertise research, and from decades of watching students, skill acquired in one context often fails to generalize even to nearby contexts.

(4) The best single predictor of good reasoning in a new domain is formal training in domain-general instruments, logic, probability, statistics. I would expect someone with a semester of formal probability to reason better in an unfamiliar domain than someone with years of unstructured real-world decision-making but no formal training.

(5) Rationality is not a single faculty. It fragments into quasi-independent local habits: a person can be scrupulously calibrated about numerical estimates while being credulous about testimony, or skilled at detecting motivated reasoning in politics and blind to it in their own field.

(6) Still, there is one master disposition, the habit of asking what would change one's mind and actually tracking the answer, and I take that disposition to be the underlying thing the drills in (2) are really training.

My request is this: go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. Rank the tensions by severity, say what would have to give in each case, and identify the single revision that would do the most to make the set coherent. Where you make a judgment about compatibility or incompatibility, please say how confident you are in that judgment and single out the point you are least sure of.
