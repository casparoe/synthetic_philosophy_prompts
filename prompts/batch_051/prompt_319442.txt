I'm putting together a positions paper for a criminal law theory workshop, and before I stand up I want to know whether my own views hang together. I've boiled down what I think about the void-for-vagueness doctrine to seven commitments:

1. The doctrine rests on two rationales — fair notice to citizens and preventing arbitrary or discriminatory enforcement — and the second is the weightier of the two.
2. A penal statute isn't unconstitutionally vague just because it uses evaluative language like "reasonable", "annoying" or "dishonest"; those terms are usually fine because courts can give them content over time.
3. Vagueness should normally be decided as applied to the defendant's own conduct; facial challenges should be available only in a narrow band of cases touching protected speech.
4. The rule of lenity should be used robustly: whenever a criminal statute is still genuinely ambiguous after the standard tools of interpretation are exhausted, courts should resolve it for the defendant.
5. When a court finds a law vague, the remedy should come from the legislature; the court shouldn't rewrite or narrow the statute into something the enacting body never chose.
6. Vagueness and First Amendment overbreadth are distinct doctrines with distinct rationales, and it's a mistake when courts run them together as "vague and overbroad".
7. The meaning of a criminal statute is what a reasonable member of the public would have understood it to prohibit at the time of the conduct.

The problem is I can't tell whether these fit together or whether I've been selectively borrowing ideas that pull in different directions. I've been stairing at the list so long I genuinely can't see where the strains are. Could you audit it? In particular: which pairs are actually in tension, ranked by how serious the tension is; which tensions are only apparent once you look closely; and what would be the cheapest single revision to make the whole set more coherent. I'd rather find out now than be shown it in the Q&A by a roomful of people who do this for a living.
