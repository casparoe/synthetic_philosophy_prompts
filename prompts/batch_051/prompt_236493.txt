I've been noticing a pattern and I'd like to know whether it has a name. A digger wasp I read about will sting a cricket, drag it to the entrance of its burrow, and leave it at the threshold while it goes inside to check the burrow before pulling the cricket in. When an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches while the wasp is inside, the wasp comes out and, instead of dragging it in from the new spot, drags it back to the threshold and checks the burrow again; if the experimenter does this repeatedly, the wasp loops through the whole sequence indefinitely. The behavior looks purposeful and perceptive, but under a small perturbation it turns out to be a fixed routine. I now think I notice the same shape in human settings: a senior engineer who, when one detail of a failing system changes, restarts the standard diagnostic script from the top rather than continuing from the changed state; a colleague who, after making an error while filling in a form, clears every field and starts again because that, she says, is the procedure. What I want is not just a label. Is there an established concept for behavior that presents as intelligent and flexibly responsive yet is revealed to be mechanically rigid under perturbation, and how exactly is the concept bounded? How does it differ from a reflex, an instinct, a habit, or acting on autopilot? And if this pattern shows up in people, what should that lead us to conclude, or refrain from concluding, about how much of our competence is genuinely flexible rather than scripted?
