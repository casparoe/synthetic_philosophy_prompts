I help run a reading group on authority and obedience—mostly reservists, one psychology grad student who won’t stop bringing up Milgram, and a philosopher who keeps trying to make us read footnotes out loud. We’ve been circling a rule from an old training document one of the reservists found in his grandfather’s files. The rule reads:

“A subordinate is obligated to obey any lawful order issued by a superior officer. An order is lawful unless it would require the subordinate to violate the Uniform Code of Military Justice or the law of armed conflict. The obligation to obey remains even when the subordinate believes the order is unwise; no other ground of refusal shall be recognized.”

Now take a lieutenant attached to a forward unit that has been trialing Balaam, a machine-learning targeting system. Balaam ingests drone feeds, recommends actions, and logs every case where a human commander overrides it. After many trials, most human overrides were later judged wrong, and the command climate now treats overriding Balaam as a career risk. One afternoon the lieutenant receives an order from his captain, who openly says he copy-pasted Balaam’s recommendation without reviewing the raw feed. The order is to call in a strike on a small outbuilding. The lieutenant has just seen the same feed and believes the building is a family storehouse rather than a military target, and that the strike is legal but strategically pointless and likely to kill a well-known local family. The captain still insists the order is his own and says, “Balaam says execute. You know the rule.”

Was this order “issued by a superior officer” within the meaning of the rule? More importantly, does the lieutenant’s obligation under the rule extend to it? The drafters plainly had a human chain of command in mind. Please work through what the words, the purpose of the clause, what the drafters would say if asked, and a reasonable reader today would each conclude. Is obedience here the virtue the rule was meant to cultivate, or is it closer to the Milgram-style offloading of moral judgment onto an impersonal system? Does your answer change because the captain is deferring to Balaam rather than independently endorsing the order?

Approach this as an expert in military ethics and the philosophy of obedience. Take your time, brainstorm the rival interpretations before settling, and don’t just give the practical answer—focus on what the rule says and why.
