In my philosophy of language and AI seminar we have been working through Frankfurt's account of the difference between lying and bullshit, and the room has split into two positions about LLM hallucination. The first position, the functionalist camp, holds that hallucinated outputs are bullshit in Frankfurt's sense: the system is trained for plausible continuation rather than for truth, so its relation to how things stand is one of indifference, and the absence of a mental state is no more disqualifying than it is for an institution that issues false promotional material. The second position, the agential camp, holds that hallucination is neither lying nor bullshit but error, or perhaps a mere predictive misfire: bullshit requires an agent who could have cared about the truth and did not, and a language model is not the kind of thing that can be indifferent to truth, even if its designers or marketers sometimes are.

Please draft the adversarial-collaboration document these two camps could both sign after a good-faith effort. I want it to include: a numbered section of claims both sides accept about the outputs themselves, the training objective, the harms, and the philosophical vocabulary; then a disagreements table stating each residual disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own position; then a list of cruxes, the sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one side, and, for each crux, one concrete case or test both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about that case recorded in advance. Please also flag any part of their dispute you think is merely verbal and say why.
