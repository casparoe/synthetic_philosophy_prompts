I'm putting together a handout for a reading group on prediction markets and democracy, and we keep arguing about futarchy without ever actually finding out what people disagree about. I'd like a decision tree that sorts readers into their real position on futarchy, where the branch questions are concrete enough that someone could answer them without already knowing the standard labels. The split I care most about for this group is between people whose objection is procedural (markets can't legitimately bind a political community) and people whose objection is epistemic or practical (markets would be too thin, too manipulable, or built on a metric that would get gamed). Please also separate the people who'd accept markets as binding only for a narrow class of decisions from the full futarchists, and the people who'd just make market verdicts advisory input into a normal democratic vote.

Leaves I'd like to see: full/strong futarchy (markets over policies, conditioned on a welfare metric that was chosen with some democratic legitimacy, and the results bind), the democratic-legitimacy rejection, the value-pluralist rejection (not everything that matters fits on one metric), the metric-selection skeptic (the real politics is in picking the metric, and that part can't be depoliticized; any metric used to control policy gets gamed), the market-reliability skeptic (whatever the ideal, real policy markets would be too thin or too easy to manipulate), advisory futarchy, and the scoped version. Use one or two concrete running cases to pose the branch questions — a minimum wage decision and maybe an infrastructure project — and make each node a claim or case someone can answer without knowing where it leads. I'd like between five and seven branch questions and six to nine leaves.

After the tree, write short notes on the combinations of answers that have no one in them: are they empty because they're incoherent, because they're just unattractive, or because nobody has thought to occupy them? Also flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really tell apart with first-order questions, and say what further question, if any, would separate them. Please start directly with the tree itself — root question first — no introduction or preface, since I'm going to hand it out as-is; put the notes section after the tree and keep the whole thing tight, maybe a page and a half alot of people won't read past that.
