Suppose a social media site with users about evenly split between the US and the EU rolls out a new hate-speech policy. Any reported post gets judged by a jury of twelve randomly chosen users from the same country as the poster: a German's post is judged by twelve Germans under German hate-speech law, an American's by twelve Americans under American law. The jury is shown the post, the report, and a one-page plain-language summary of the local standard (for Americans, roughly that speech is protected unless its intended to and likely to produce imminent lawless action; for Germans, roughly the provisions against inciting hatred of a national, racial, or religious group). Removing the post takes nine of twelve votes. If fewer vote to remove, the post stays up but gets a permanent 'jury declined to remove' label, and the vote tally plus each juror's one-line reason is displayed under the post. Jurors get a small fee and can be called at most once a month. Walk me through how this would actualy behave: what it incentivizes posters, flaggers, and jurors to do, what it does to the platform's own legal and moderation incentives, and where it gets gamed first. Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no introduction and no conclusion, hitting those five things in order.
