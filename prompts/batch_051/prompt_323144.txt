Write a decision tree for a one-page handout in a philosophy of physics seminar on the question of what is real in a gauge theory, using classical electromagnetism or Yang-Mills theory as the running example. The tree should begin from the shared observation that two gauge-related configurations are empirically equivalent, and it should sort participants into positions based on how they answer a sequence of diagnostic questions. Each node should be answerable by someone who knows the formalism but does not yet know the interpretive labels, and the tree should be built from the logical space of answers rather than from a pre-given list of positions. Use at least the following splitting questions: whether a gauge transformation changes the physical state or merely redescribes the same state; whether a gauge-variant quantity can represent a physical fact; whether a fixed-gauge or dressed description is merely a choice of representative or is required to make the formalism literally true; and whether boundary or asymptotic data must be treated as physical degrees of freedom distinct from bulk holonomies. The leaves should include, and be located within the resulting logical space: the view that the gauge potential is real and gauge freedom is surplus descriptive structure; the view that only gauge-invariant quantities are real; a holonomy-based view on which loop-like quantities rather than point-localized potentials are fundamental; a dressed-observable view on which gauge-variant quantities become physically meaningful only relative to a gauge-fixing or dressing construction; and an edge-mode or boundary-realist view. Note any combinations of answers that no named position occupies, and briefly diagnose whether each empty cell is empty because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely underexplored. Finally, flag any pairs of leaves that the tree cannot cleanly separate without further argument.
