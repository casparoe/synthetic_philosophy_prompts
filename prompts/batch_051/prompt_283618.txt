I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I have just been assigned a long essay on whether it still makes sense to say that science is getting closer to the truth. In the background reading I keep encountering deflationary theories of truth: the idea that calling a claim true adds nothing over and above asserting the claim itself, and that the word true earns its keep only as a logical convenience — for endorsing something someone else said, for quoting a sentence back as an assertion, or for saying a whole class of claims at once, as in the thought that every sentence in this report is true. I find the deflationary picture attractive because it deflates exactly the grand metaphysical talk my editors want me to avoid, but I do not yet know how much weight it can bear, and I have no settled view about whether it survives contact with the harder cases.

Give me a working definition of true — or, if you conclude the deflationists are right and no substantive definition is available, a precise statement of the rule that governs when I may properly call a claim true — and I will use it while reporting and checking copy. It must classify the following: the claim that snow is white; the claim that phlogiston exists; the claim that electrons exist; the claim that there is intelligent life elsewhere in the universe right now; the claim that it will rain in Boston on the Fourth of July in 2030; the claim that torturing a child for fun is wrong; the claim that every theorem of Peano arithmetic is true; the claim that the present King of France is bald; the sentence that says of itself that it is false; and the claim that the butler did it, made by a detective whose evidence points strongly that way but who might be mistaken. I am confident only about the first two, and in opposite directions.

Propose the definition or rule, run it through every one of those ten cases, and tell me honestly where it surprises you, where it returns no verdict, and which of your classifications is least secure. If the view you recommend treats some of these as not truth-apt, true only in a deflated or merely useful sense, or true only relative to a framework, say that explicitly and explain what follows for the essay I am writing. I do not need names or a survey of positions; I need the distilled idea and the verdicts it forces.
