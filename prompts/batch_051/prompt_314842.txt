My local council is debating whether to ban glyphosate weedkiller from parks and playgrounds. At a residents' meeting, a bloke stood up and said the precautionary principle settles it: we don't know it's safe, so we shouldn't be spraying it where children play. Afterwards I asked him what the principle actually says, and he just shrugged and said 'better safe than sorry.' I can tell that little phrase is doing a lot of work, but I cant pin down what work. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims 'better safe than sorry' could be making here — what kind of uncertainty, how likely, how bad, whose safety, and at what cost — and say for each version whether it's a platitude, a plausible rule, or actually false, which of them would actually support the ban, and which reading he was probably relying on when he used it to shut the discussion down? I'd like a menu of readings rather than one settled interpretation, and I want to know what I'd be signing up for if I backed him.
