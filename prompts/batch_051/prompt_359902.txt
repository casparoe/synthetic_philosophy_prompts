Suppose that, beginning in 2030, a safe, reversible, and inexpensive "experience preview" becomes widely available in a wealthy Western country. A person confronting a transformative choice — having a child, undergoing a religious conversion, transitioning gender, emigrating permanently, entering a monastery or the military, or making an irreversible medical decision — can undergo a two-week, fully immersive preview of the relevant life change. During the preview, the person has the salient experiences and also undergoes the associated change in what they care about, coming to value things roughly as the post-change version of themselves would. The preview is known to be accurate and has no side effects beyond those described.

One feature matters most. When the preview ends, the transformation is guaranteed to reverse completely: the person returns to their pre-preview desires and values. They retain detailed, accurate memories of what the experience felt like and of what they wanted and valued during the preview, but those remembered desires carry no motivational force. You remember, vividly, having loved the simulated child; you do not now love it.

Predict how such decisions are made, experienced, and assessed over the next two decades. I am not asking for a story but for a chain of reasoning about mechanisms, incentives, and constraints. In particular, address these questions. Does the preview dissolve the epistemic problem of transformative choice while leaving the problem of changed values intact? Do people treat their remembered-but-unendorsed desires as evidence about what to do, or as alien states they no longer answer for? And do some transformations depend for their content on being irrevocable, so that a reversible preview necessarily misrepresents them?

Give exactly five predictions, with no introduction or conclusion. Each prediction should be one or two sentences, and should state the change you expect, the mechanism that produces it, and a confidence judgment together with at least one assumption your prediction depends on.
