I want a committed credence from you on the following proposition: Gödel’s rotating-universe solution to general relativity, together with the philosophical reflections in his 1949 paper A Remark About the Relationship Between Relativity Theory and Idealistic Philosophy, gives strong reason to regard the passage of time as mind-dependent rather than objective. My own credence is around 0.3; a philosopher colleague puts it around 0.7. Give me your number, and more importantly the decomposition. In particular, assign rough probabilities to and justify these components: (a) the Gödel metric, with its rotating dust and negative cosmological constant, is a physically meaningful solution of general relativity rather than a formal artifact; (b) within that solution, the existence of closed timelike curves and the absence of a global cosmic-time foliation show that no objective now could be defined even in that model; (c) facts about a merely possible rotating cosmos can carry metaphysical weight for the actual universe; and (d) the idealist conclusion follows only with extra premises about temporal passage requiring a global now, premises that general relativity by itself does not supply. Show how these components combine to your overall number. Then identify the sub-question to which your overall credence is most sensitive, and say what evidence or argument would move it most.
