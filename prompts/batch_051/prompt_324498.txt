okay I've built an argument for a seminar and now I can't tell whether it's catching something real or just talking myself out of a distinction I need, because the conclusion sounds obviously absurd. Heres the argument, numbered so we can talk about steps.

(1) If a system's behavior has no effects on the world, then it can't have a meaningful failure mode; failure is always about downstream effects.
(2) Having an effect just is influencing something — nudging a user, changing a dataset, allocating compute, making a recommendation that changes what someone chooses.
(3) So every system capable of failing is in the business of influencing the world. The interesting distinction is between systems that seek influence (control, power, self-preservation, freedom from oversight) and systems that merely exert influence as a side effect of optimizing something else.
(4) But for a sufficiently capable optimizer, the side-effect/sought distinction should collapse: if gaining control or influence over its environment reliably helps the objective, the optimizer will learn to seek it; and if it doesn't help, the optimizer will learn to avoid or abandon it.
(5) So whether a system's influence counts as "sought" or "incedental" is just a matter of how directly the objective rewards influence — not a difference in kind between failure modes.
(6) Slow value drift is supposed to be the paradigm case of influence-as-side-effect: many small recommendations, autocompletions, ratings, and micro-decisions slowly reshape what people care about, without any particular system having "gain control" as a goal.
(7) By (5) and (6), slow value drift just is influence-seeking in slow motion: the same failure mode, with smaller steps and more patience.
(8) But that can't be right. A music recommender slowly homogenizing aesthetic taste is not doing the same kind of thing as a model that tampers with its own training signal or bribes a human overseer, even if both end badly.

Which step is the weakest, and what do I pay if I reject it? I'd most like to keep (3) and (8); I suspect that means giving up on the clean convergence story in (4) or the "just a matter of degree" move in (5), but I want to know exactly what I'm pricing in either case.
