You advise the Digital Contract Formation Panel of a state consumer-protection agency. The Panel decides one question: whether a website's or app's interface brought standard-form, non-negotiable terms into a consumer contract. It treats its six published decisions as the complete body of authority, so do not import outside case law.

Its six prior decisions are as follows.

1. Software installer. The installer displayed the full license agreement in a scrollable box, with an unchecked box beneath it reading "I agree to the License Agreement"; the "Install" button remained disabled until the box was checked. The Panel held that the terms were binding.

2. News website. The site's terms of use were reachable only through a footer link labeled "Terms"; no mention of terms appeared during account creation or checkout. A user who had not clicked the link purchased a subscription. The Panel held that no binding agreement was formed.

3. Mobile app sign-up. The sign-up screen contained a "Create Account" button. Immediately above it, the screen read, "By tapping Create Account, you agree to our Terms of Service and Privacy Policy," with both documents hyperlinked but not displayed. The user tapped "Create Account." The Panel held that the terms were binding.

4. E-commerce checkout. Directly above the "Place Your Order" button, the checkout page read, "By placing your order, you agree to our Terms of Sale," with a hyperlink and no inline display of the terms. The user clicked "Place Your Order." The Panel held that the terms were binding.

5. Streaming sign-up. The page contained a "Start Free Trial" button. A "Terms" link appeared in small type at the bottom of the page, far from the button, and nothing near the button indicated that clicking it constituted agreement. The user clicked "Start Free Trial" without opening the link. The Panel held that no binding agreement was formed.

6. Cloud-storage installer. The installer offered an "I Agree" button and a separate "View License Agreement" button that opened the full terms in a new window; the user clicked "I Agree" without opening the terms. The Panel held that the terms were binding.

A new case now comes before the Panel. A hotel-booking site's final payment page displays the complete Terms of Sale in a scrollable box above the "Confirm Reservation" button. The button says only "Confirm Reservation"; there is no checkbox, and no sentence anywhere states that clicking the button constitutes acceptance. The user must scroll through the terms to reach the button, but is not required to tick or type anything indicating assent. A user scrolled through the terms and clicked "Confirm Reservation." The question is whether the arbitration clause contained in those terms forms part of the user's contract.

What should the Panel decide if it aims to be consistent with its six precedents? Identify the rule or rules that best fit the precedents, state what each candidate rule predicts about the hotel-booking case, identify the precedent that is the closest analog and explain why, and say whether the precedents genuinely settle the case. If they do not, state as precisely as possible what principle the Panel would be adopting if it rules each way, and describe one further interface design that would most cleanly discriminate between the competing principles.
