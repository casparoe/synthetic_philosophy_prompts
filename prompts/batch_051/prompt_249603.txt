In Brussels-y debates I keep hearing that European integration is like riding a bicycle: you have to keep pedaling or you fall off. It gets wheeled out against anyone who says the EU should pause the deepening, fix the democratic deficit, or claw back sovereignty before going further — the reply is that you cant pause; if the EU stops integrating the whole thing wobbles and falls apart, so the only real choice is more integration or disintegration. I want you to audit this analogy properly. Lay out what corresponds to what — the rider, the pedaling, the bicycle, the falling, maybe the road or the destination — then where the mapping breaks, and especially what the bicycle picture quietly imports that a political union might not have: a single rider, a chosen direction, an actual law of physics connecting stopping to falling. Which disanalogies actually matter for the conclusion, and which are just cosmetic? Does the 'more integration or collapse' conclusion survive once they're on the table, or is the analogy doing all the work? And if there's a better analogy for whatever the real mechanism is, say what it would be. Tell me how confident you are in what you claim, and which point you're least sure of.
