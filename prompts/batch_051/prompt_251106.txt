I teach a small advanced undergraduate seminar on formal models of cooperation and collective action, about sixteen students, in seventy-five-minute sessions. I would like a runnable classroom activity that makes participants feel the distinction between a Pareto improvement that requires trust and a safe Pareto improvement that each individual can adopt without trusting the others. The activity should use a sequence of simple payoff matrices with real but small stakes, such as points toward a course bonus. In the first phase, students face a proposed change from a status quo that is a Pareto improvement if everyone switches but is individually risky: a student who switches while some others stay ends up worse off than under the status quo. The design should let students experience their own reluctance to switch even though the collectively superior outcome is visible and no one has any information that the others will defect. In the second phase, each small group receives a constrained design task: modify the payoffs of the risky proposal so that it remains a Pareto improvement over the status quo, but becomes individually safe in the sense that choosing the new action never leaves a student below the status quo payoff regardless of what the others do. Groups should then test their modified proposals on the class and observe whether uptake becomes immediate. The debrief should occur only after the activity, should name the concept then rather than before, and should map the first phase onto the failure of trust-dependent Pareto improvements and the second onto safe Pareto improvements. It should also discuss where the line of safety lies, such as worst-case payoffs versus expected payoffs under risk, and be candid about what the game cannot reproduce, including heterogeneous status quo payoffs, private information, and non-monetary dimensions of real collective decisions. The deliverable should be a complete facilitator plan with exact payoff numbers, group sizes, timing, the text of the design task, expected student moves, likely failure modes, and a debrief script. No lecture up front, and the term safe Pareto improvement should not be heard by students until the debrief.
