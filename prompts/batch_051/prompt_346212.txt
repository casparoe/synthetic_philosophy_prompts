I am a philosophy professor, and I would like you to act as an expert in moral psychology and the ethics of self-interest. Please reason carefully and consider alternative principles before settling on your answer.

A colleague of mine, M, has clear judgments about a set of cases involving the pursuit of wealth. I want to predict how she will judge a new case, but I suspect more than one principle could explain her earlier judgments. Her judgments are:

1. A commodities trader buys grain futures ahead of a forecast drought and sells after prices spike. M says this is predatory greed and is morally wrong because the trader profits from others' hardship.
2. A software engineer founds a startup, works long hours for a decade, and sells it for $80 million. M says this is legitimate ambition, not greed, because she created a useful product and jobs.
3. A landlord raises rents to the maximum the market will bear after a tech campus opens nearby, displacing several long-term tenants. M calls this callous and a form of greed, though she will not say it is unjust because the leases permit the increases.
4. A retired couple saves aggressively and lives below its means in order to leave a large inheritance. M calls this thrift, not greed.
5. A hedge fund manager uses a legal tax-avoidance scheme to reduce his effective tax rate to nearly zero on millions in income. M calls this greedy and a failure of civic virtue even though it is legal.
6. A professional basketball player insists on a maximum-salary contract and plays teams against each other to obtain it. M says this is fair negotiation, not greed, because the player is extracting a share of the revenue he helps generate.

Now consider a new case M has not judged. A pharmaceutical company develops an innovative drug for a chronic condition that was previously untreatable. The company prices the drug to yield very high profits; many patients in poorer countries cannot afford it, though the company argues that the price funds future research and that no one is forced to buy it.

How would M most likely judge the pharmaceutical company: as morally wrong greed, as legitimate self-interest, or as something in between such as "callous but not unjust"? I am less interested in the bare verdict than in the reasoning: identify the candidate rules that fit her six earlier judgments, state which rule best predicts her verdict in the new case and why, and describe a further case that would discriminate most sharply among the remaining candidates.

Please end with a line beginning exactly "Verdict:" followed by your single best predicted verdict for the pharmaceutical company in no more than three words, e.g., "Verdict: morally wrong greed," "Verdict: not greed," or "Verdict: callous but not unjust."
