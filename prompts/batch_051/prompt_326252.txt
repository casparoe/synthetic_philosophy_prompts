Write a deadpan corporate announcement of about 600 words, in the voice of a fictional AI company's "responsible AI" blog. Give it a headline, a dateline, and a three-question FAQ at the end. The setup: the company has decided to stop describing its large language model's false outputs as "hallucinations." From this release forward, they will be classified as "bullshit," in what the company calls the strict Frankfurtian sense, after Harry Frankfurt's essay On Bullshit.

The comedy should come from getting Frankfurt's machinery right, not from mocking the word "bullshit." The post should correctly explain the distinction: the liar must care about the truth, and must track it closely, in order to lead anyone away from it, whereas the bullshitter is defined by indifference to how things actually are. The company then argues, in a technically accurate but conveniently self-serving way, that its model is not a liar (no intention to deceive), not hallucinating (a hallucination implies a breakdown in a system that is trying to track the truth), but bullshitting — by design, with a straight face, and the company is proud to say so. Include at least one invented quotation from a fictional Chief Epistemic Compliance Officer. The target of the satire should be the practice of laundering truth-indifference through a term that makes it sound like candor: the company congratulates itself on telling the truth about not caring about the truth, while continuing to market the system as an information service. Stay in character throughout; do not wink, do not state the objection, and do not break the deadpan. You may, if it helps, correctly allude in passing to Frankfurt's earlier discussion of the bull session as exploratory talk, but the core joke must rest on the liar/bullshitter contrast.

After the post, add a 150-word author's note. In it, commit to a definite position on the question the post leaves implicit: is it fair to say the model bullshits, or does Frankfurt's concept require an agent who misrepresents what it is up to, so that only the company qualifies? Take one side, give the strongest reason for it, and do not hedge or lay out options.
