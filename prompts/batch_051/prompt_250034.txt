I recently spent a long evening arguing with a friend about what the word unpredictable actually means, and I came away convinced we were using one word for several different properties. He maintained that a double pendulum is unpredictable in the same way next month's weather is. I resisted: for the double pendulum we know the governing equations and have a small, isolated system, so the unpredictability is a matter of sensitivity to initial conditions, whereas for weather the difficulty also includes incomplete data, model error, and an open system with uncertain boundary conditions. He replied that those are merely differences in the source of our uncertainty, not differences in unpredictability itself. The argument ended with a pile of examples and no agreed principle.

I would like a working definition of unpredictable that sorts the following cases correctly:

1. A double pendulum released from a precisely specified initial angle and angular velocity.
2. The weather at a particular location three weeks from now.
3. A fair coin toss.
4. A deterministic pseudorandom number generator whose seed is known to the programmer but not to an outside observer.
5. The exact trajectory of a thrown die under Newtonian mechanics, assuming perfect knowledge of initial conditions and forces.
6. A deterministic cellular automaton whose next state is trivially easy to compute from the current state.
7. The logistic map in its chaotic regime, iterated many steps from an initial value known only to finite precision.
8. What is inside a sealed box the asker has never seen.

My tentative view: (1) and (2) are both unpredictable but for different reasons; (3) is a hard case; (4) is predictable in principle but not in practice, which makes me suspicious of purely epistemic definitions; (5) is predictable in principle, and if it is unpredictable at all it is only because no real measurement is perfect; (6) is clearly predictable; (7) is unpredictable in the interesting sense; and (8) is a case of ignorance, not unpredictability.

Propose a definition, run it through all eight cases, and say where it gives a surprising verdict or no verdict at all. If the definition would not count the double pendulum and the weather as unpredictable for the same reason, say whether that is a feature or a defect. Then revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. Finally, tell me which of these cases or distinctions would most likely make you change your mind about the right analysis, and where you think your reasoning is most likely to fail.
