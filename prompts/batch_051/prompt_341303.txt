I'm putting together a translation exercise for an upper-level philosophy of language seminar, and I want the deliverable to be the translation itself plus a residue report. Take the Lewis/Stalnaker framework of presupposition, accommodation, and conversational scorekeeping — in which presuppositions are requirements on the common ground, assertions update it, and accommodation lets hearers quietly adjust the conversational scoreboard so that a speaker's move counts as felicitous — and restate it in the vocabulary of a distributed systems engineer who has no philosophy background and works fluently with shared state, replication, consensus, caches, API contracts, schema migrations, and optimistic concurrency. Keep the translation honest: do not smuggle philosophical commitments into the engineering vocabulary and then present them as translations. Then report the residue: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only under distortion, and which have no counterpart in the target vocabulary at all. End with a judgment on what the residue shows — whether the engineering vocabulary is missing something the conversational picture genuinely needs, or whether the residue exposes places where the philosophical apparatus is less precise than its defenders assume.
