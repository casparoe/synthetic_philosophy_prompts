Question: "What is the difference between an echo chamber and an epistemic bubble, and why does the distinction matter for attempts to correct people's beliefs?"

Student's answer: "Both describe situations where a person mainly hears views they already agree with. The key difference is that an epistemic bubble is a matter of exposure: certain voices or sources never reach you, often because they have been filtered out of your media diet. An echo chamber is different because it involves active distrust: you have been taught to discredit outside sources, so whilst contrary evidence reaches you, you treat it as a sign of the source's unreliability rather than as a challenge. The distinction matters because different interventions are appropriate. In a bubble, the fix is to expose the person to the missing voices, and this tends to work. In an echo chamber, mere exposure tends to fail because the outsider is already discredited, so you have to work on the underlying trust first. That said, I'd say the practical upshot is basically the same in both cases: you should diversify the person's information environment. An echo chamber is really just an epistemic bubble with a stronger filter, so the only difference is one of degree."
