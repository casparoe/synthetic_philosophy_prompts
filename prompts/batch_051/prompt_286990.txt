my brother in law is a prosecutor, and whenever we argue about sentencing he lands on the same idea: prison is how you pay your debt to society. on his telling, punishing someone has nothing to do with deterring other people or fixing the person; a crime is like taking out a loan you never planned to pay back, and the sentence is the repayment. once you've done your time, your account is back at zero, and at that point keeping you on a registry or refusing to hire you is like a debt collector chasing a bill that's already been paid. but he uses the same picture to argue for harsh sentences too: a lender gets to demand every cent, so a sentence shouldn't be scaled to public safety or the risk of reoffending, it should be scaled to what the offender owes. if you took a year of someone's life you owe a year of yours; if you took a life you owe your whole life.
