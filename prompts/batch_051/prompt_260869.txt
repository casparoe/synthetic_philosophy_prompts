I'm a political science PhD student working on democratic erosion, and I've settled into a rule in my own research that I'm not sure I can defend. I don't code a country as "democratic backsliding" untill something clearly institutional has happened: a constitutional court is packed or stripped of jurisdiction, an electoral commission is captured, term limits are repealed, an election is cancelled through emergency powers, something like that. If the evidence is softer — a government strangling independent media, prosecuting opposition figures on dubious charges, severely lopsided campaign finance, gerrymandering, a president calling the opposition illegitimate or the press an enemy — I'll write "autocratic rhetoric," "polarization," or "erosion risk," but I won't call it backsliding. The habit shows up outside my own datasets too: I find myself quietly discounting papers and conference talks that use the backsliding label without one of my institutional indicators, especially when the author clearly hates the government in question. Last month a colleague argued that a Central European country had crossed into backsliding because the ruling party had captured most of the broadcasters and was prosecuting opposition mayors; I pushed back that no electoral rule had actually changed yet.

I can see the worry about my own rule. Maybe by the time one of my institutional red lines is crossed, the thing I claimed to be tracking has already happened, and the soft stuff is exactly where it happens. Maybe I've built a precise-sounding checklist to avoid making a contested normative judgment, or to protect a country I have priors about. But I also don't want to be the grad student who uses "backsliding" as a synonym for "the wrong party won," and a checklist at least forces me to be falsifiable. So: is this habit defensible as a research policy? Under what assumptions is it epistemically better or worse than the alternatives, and is there a version of the rule — a continuous scale, a probabilistic threshold, different indicators — that keeps the skepticism without the delayed alarm? And how do I tell from the inside whether I'm being disciplined or just protecting my priors?
