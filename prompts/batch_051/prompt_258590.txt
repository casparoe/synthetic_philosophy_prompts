I write fiction for a living, and I keep running into something I don't have a word for. When I'm deep in a scene, it feels like the words show up in my mind first and then get projected, moment by moment, to a small inner watcher who sits behind my eyes and watches the scene before I type it. But when I try to catch that watcher, there's no one there; the sentence just arrives at the same time as the hearing of it, or a beat later.

Worse, sometimes I realize halfway through a paragraph that an image I thought I'd consciously seen two sentences back must have been slipped in later, because only now does the context make it mean something. I genuinly can't tell whether I did consciously see it then and my memory's been edited, or whether I never saw it then and the whole scene quietly rewrote itself so it feels like I did. The two possibilities are indistinguishable from the inside.

Is there a name for the assumption that conscious experience is displayed like a film to a single inner audience? And is there a separate name for that forked possibility, where a later event can either overwrite the memory of an earlier conscious moment or overwrite the conscious moment itself, with no way to tell which? I definately don't just want labels; I want to know how these two possibilities actually differ, if they do, and where the line is between a harmless way of talking about how a story comes to me and a mistaken picture of what the mind is doing.
