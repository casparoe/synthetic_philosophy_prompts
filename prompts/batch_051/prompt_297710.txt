Please give me your actual credence in the following claim: Benatar's asymmetry about absent pleasures and pains is correct—the absence of pain in the case of someone who never comes into existence is good, even though there is no one to enjoy that good, while the absence of pleasure is not bad, because there is no one to be deprived of it—and this asymmetry is enough to establish that coming into existence is always a net harm to the person who comes into existence. Do not tell me it depends on framework; I want a number and the reasoning that produces it. Decompose the claim into at least these terms, each with a rough probability and a one-line justification: (a) the asymmetry itself correctly describes the value of absent pains and pleasures; (b) it is intelligible to compare a life against never existing, rather than only against other possible lives; (c) given the asymmetry, it actually entails that existence is always a net harm, rather than merely an asymmetry about which lives may permissibly be created; (d) the fact that most people judge their own lives worth living does not undermine (a)–(c), for example because such judgments are biased or because a life's being judged worth living does not show that being born was not a harm. Then state how these terms combine into your overall credence, identify the single term your final number is most sensitive to, and say what evidence or argument would move that term most. End with your credence in the form \answer{0.XX}.
