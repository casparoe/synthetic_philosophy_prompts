I'm the best man at my brother's wedding in a few weeks, and I've been told to keep the toast warm, short, and not too political. Problem is I just finished Engels's The Origin of the Family, Private Property and the State and this bit, from the part of the family chapter where he gets to monogamy, wont leave me alone: "It develops out of the pairing family, as previously shown, in the transitional period between the upper and middle stages of barbarism; its decisive victory is one of the signs that civilization is beginning. It is based on the supremacy of the man, the express purpose being to produce children of undisputed paternity; such paternity is demanded because these children are later to come into their father’s property as his natural heirs." I actually buy the basic point—monogamy arrives as property logic and inheritance, not as some timeless moral law—but I don't want to turn the reception into a lecture on historical materialism, and I also don't want to stand there saying stuff about marriage being sacred or divinely ordained, because I think that's false. The room will be half my old reading group and half my very Catholic family. Can you write the toast so it's genuinely warm and lands with both halves, and so every line is something a person who accepts that Engels passage could honestly say? The passage should do the work under the surface; I don't want to quote it or explain it on the night.
