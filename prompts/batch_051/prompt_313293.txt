I am a novelist, and I am drafting a scene in which two characters argue late into the night about the pragmatist theory of truth. One is a younger writer of a practical, experimentalist temperament who admires William James; the other is an older scholar devoted to Charles Peirce. I have written the younger character's central argument as a numbered chain of reasoning, and before I let him present it as compelling in the book, I want to know whether it actually survives scrutiny, because a later scene will have the older character answer it and I do not want either side to be a caricature.

The argument is this. (1) Peirce characterizes truth as the opinion that an indefinitely extended community of inquirers, using the method of science, would settle on in the long run. (2) But no actual inquirer ever occupies that long-run endpoint; one can only ever predict which of one's present beliefs will survive continued inquiry. (3) The only basis available in experience for such a prediction is that a belief has so far survived testing and continues to guide expectation without conflict, which is precisely the Jamesian standard of whether a belief works. (4) Therefore, any element of Peirce's account that goes beyond the Jamesian standard is not something an inquirer could ever use to distinguish true from false beliefs. (5) For a pragmatist, a distinction that could never in principle be used to guide belief or action is no real distinction. (6) So Peirce's account reduces to James's, and their famous disagreement is only apparent.

I would like you to assess this in exactly the way one of my more careful characters would. Is the argument valid as stated, and are the premises true on the most charitable readings of Peirce and James? If any step fails, identify the first step that fails, state the reading under which it is false, and say whether a Peircean could repair that step or whether the collapse retains real force. If you think the argument goes through, tell me which premise is doing the most work and where a Peircean would still push back, so that I can write the rebuttal scene credibly.
