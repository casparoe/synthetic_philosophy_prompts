For a philosophy of religion working group I am drafting my position on the Euthyphro and would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. My view is organized around the moment in Plato's Euthyphro just after Euthyphro proposes that what the gods love is holy. The text (Plato, Euthyphro, trans. H. N. Fowler, 1914) reads:

Euthyphro: "Well, I should say that what all the gods love is holy and, on the other hand, what they all hate is unholy."

Socrates then raises the familiar dilemma—whether the gods' loving a thing explains its being holy, or its being holy explains the gods' loving it—and, when Euthyphro does not follow, Socrates explains:

Socrates: "Then I will try to speak more clearly. We speak of being carried and of carrying, of being led and of leading, of being seen and of seeing; and you understand—do you not?—that in all such expressions the two parts differ one from the other in meaning, and how they differ."

I take this active/passive contrast to be a point about explanatory direction, not merely about grammar: the two expressions pick out one state of affairs, but only one of them gives the explanation of it. With that reading in place, here are the commitments I hold:

(1) Obligation without value voluntarism: an act is morally obligatory because God commands it, but the goodness of persons, and God's own goodness, is not created by divine decree.

(2) Anti-arbitrariness: God could not have made gratuitous cruelty obligatory; the content of morality is fixed by God's essentially good nature rather than by an arbitrary act of will.

(3) Paradigm priority: God's nature is not good because it measures up to an independent standard of goodness. Rather, God's nature is the paradigm of goodness, and creatures are good in virtue of resembling it. The traits God has—love, justice, mercy—are good-making because they are God's, not the reverse.

(4) Explanatory asymmetry: the Euthyphro question demands an asymmetric answer with God on the explaining side. A bare identity of God and the Good cannot dissolve the dilemma, since identity is symmetric and explanation is not.

(5) Answerability: obligation is answerability to a person. An impersonal standard or Form can explain what there is reason to do, but only a person's command can explain what is owed; that is why a personal God does metaethical work that a standard of value alone cannot.

I would like you to audit this set: which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, and which only appear to be? Please order the genuine tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and what the cheapest repair costs, and then tell me which single revision leaves the strongest coherent position. I have no settled view about which commitment I would surrender.
