I am a PhD student in historical theology, and I need a careful interpretive ruling on a case that has split our faculty. Our institution stands in the Augustinian and Calvinist tradition, and its confessional standard includes this article:

"God, before the foundation of the world, did freely and unchangeably ordain who shall be saved and who shall be left to the just consequences of their sin; and this decree is not founded upon anything foreseen in the creature, nor upon any movement of the creature's will, but upon God's mere good pleasure."

A candidate for a chair affirms the article but also teaches a view of election built on middle knowledge: God knows, for every possible person in every possible situation, how that person would freely choose, and God decides which persons and situations to actualize so that the elect come to faith freely and without violence to their wills. The candidate argues that this does not make election rest on "anything foreseen in the creature," because the relevant divine knowledge concerns what a merely possible person would or would not do, not what any actually existing person does; actual faith remains God's gift, and no actual merit enters the decree.

The committee is split. Some say the plain wording plainly covers counterfactual free choices, since the creature is the object of the foresight. Others say the clause was aimed at the Remonstrant teaching that election rests on foreseen actual faith, and everyone concedes the article's authors did not draft it with the middle-knowledge refinement in view.

Please rule on whether the candidate's view violates the article. Give three separate findings—one from the wording alone, one from the evident purpose of the clause, and one from what the drafters would most plausibly have said if asked about middle knowledge—and then state which interpretive approach should govern here and why. I especially want a judgment on whether the distinction between foreseen actual faith and foreseen counterfactual free choices can carry the weight the candidate places on it, or whether it is a distinction without a confessional difference. Please write your answer in American English.
