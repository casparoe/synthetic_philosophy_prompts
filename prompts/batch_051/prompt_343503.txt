I have been trying to think carefully about whether ordinary driving is worth it once the risk of death is made explicit, and I have reconstructed my reasoning as a numbered argument. The conclusion feels wrong, which makes me think some step in the argument is bad, but I cannot see which one, because the arithmetic at each point looks correct on its own. Could you tell me whether the argument is valid and, if not, at exactly which step it fails, and what the strongest corrected version of that step would be?

(1) A micromort is a one-in-a-million chance of dying.
(2) Suppose, just to keep the arithmetic simple, that a short car trip exposes me to exactly one micromort of fatal risk.
(3) The rational cost of bearing a risk of death is the probability of death multiplied by the value one places on avoiding death; so the mortality cost of such a trip is (1/1,000,000) × v, where v is the value I place on avoiding death.
(4) I would not accept certain death in exchange for any finite amount of convenience; the disvalue of death to me is therefore effectively infinite.
(5) If the disvalue of death is effectively infinite, then a one-in-a-million chance of death still carries an infinite expected cost, since (1/1,000,000) × infinity is still infinite.
(6) No finite convenience, such as a quick drive to the store, can outweigh an infinite expected cost.
(7) Therefore no individual car trip is worth its mortality risk.
(8) A practice made up of trips that are each not worth taking is itself not worth taking.
(9) Therefore, once the life risk is counted, driving is not rationally worth it.

I half-suspect the trouble is in the move from refusing certain death to assigning death an infinite disvalue, since people plainly accept small mortal risks for small conveniences without thereby showing they are making a straightforward arithmetic error. But I cannot say exactly why that move is illegitimate, if it is. Be specific about which step fails and why, and say whether the argument can be repaired to support a weaker but still interesting conclusion about the rationality of ordinary driving.
