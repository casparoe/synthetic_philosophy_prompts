A friend who took a course on non-classical logic gave me this summary of dialetheism:

"Dialetheism is the view that there are some true contradictions — sentences that are both true and false, such as the liar sentence. To make room for this, dialetheists adopt paraconsistent logics, which give up the law of non-contradiction so that a contradiction no longer implies everything. But once you give up that law, the truth/falsity distinction collapses, and there's no principled reason to stop at the liar: if the liar is both true and false, why not an ordinary contradiction like 'it's raining and it's not raining'? At that point dialetheism is indistinguishable from trivialism, the view that every sentence is true."

I'm not looking for a reading list; I want the conceptual reasoning.
