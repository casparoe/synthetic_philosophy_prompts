I'm a novelist, and I'm writing a scene I can't get right. A politician's aide is testifying to a committee and says something arranged so that every literal sentence is true but the room walks out beleiving the one false thing she never actually said. I can't decide whether the book should treat what she does as being as bad as a lie, or as a lesser and different wrong — and my own drafts keep flip-flopping, which is making the scene muddy. So I want the conceptual spine first, not a lecture.

Set it up as an adversarial collaboration between two camps on the lying-vs-misleading question: (a) the lying-is-worse camp, who think the explicit lie is a morally distinct and generally worse wrong, and (b) the parity camp, who think the distinction does no fundamental moral work once you equalize intentions and consequences. Draft the joint statement they'd co-sign, not a debate transcript.

The deliverable: (1) a numbered list of claims both camps can sign, stated non-vacuously — about the manipulative intention, about what the hearer is owed, about deniability, about whether misleading respects the hearer's agency or just exploits it, about how deception loads on trust; (2) a disagreement table, where each cell is phrased in wording the relevant camp would accept as its own; (3) the cruxes — the two or three sub-questions such that settling them one way would genuinely move one camp, plus, for each, the test or argument both sides agree would bear on it and what each side predicts that test will show; (4) a note on any part of the fight that looks verbal (different senses of "lie," "deceive," or "warrant") with the reformulation that dissolves just that part. Run the document through at least three cases so it doesn't stay abstract: the committee aide, a doctor who says "the test came back negative" while omitting that a second test is still pending, and a spouse who says "I didn't sleep with her" because the act in question wasn't, technically, that.

I'll put the result in the mouth of a character, so at the end tell me where your own version is shakiest: which claim in the agreed list or the table is most likely to be wrong, and what argument or case would change your mind about where the line between the camps really falls.
