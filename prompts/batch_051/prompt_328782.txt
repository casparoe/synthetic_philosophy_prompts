Ok so I'm on the committee at my university that signs off on 'choice architecture' stuff—dining halls, benefits, clinics. A friend and I argued about this for an hour last night and now I have to actually write the decision, and I'm less sure than I was. Our past calls: we approved auto-enrollment in the student-employee retirement plan, with a genuinely easy opt-out and an annual email stating the contribution rate and how to change it. We rejected a ten-cent surcharge on sugary drinks; some of us said it was because the surcharge would of been added at the register without being posted anywhere, others said it was because we don't do price penalties on personal choices. We approved a cafeteria plan to move fruit and water to eye level in the grab-and-go fridges, but only on condition they put up a sign saying the shelves are arranged to make the healthier stuff easiest to grab. The new proposal is to quietly reorder the dining-hall stations over the summer so the plant-based entrees come first and the meat station ends up last in line. Nothing is removed, nothing is repriced, and there'd be no sign or announcement—students would just show up to the new layout in the fall. So: should we approve, reject, or approve with conditions? My friend thinks the real through-line in those earlier calls is transparency (so the new one should fail without disclosure), and I've been saying the through-line is keeping the disfavored option cheap and easily available (so the new one should pass). Which of us is the better reader of our record, which old case is the closest analog under each reading, and what further case would be the cleanest way to figure out which principle we're actually committed to?
