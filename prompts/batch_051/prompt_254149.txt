Write a parody of a terms-of-service agreement, roughly 600 words, for a fictional commitment-device app called Tether that locks users into their present self's long-term preferences. Format it as numbered clauses in a deadpan legal register. The fine print should quietly take positions on real debates in diachronic rationality: whether a sophisticated chooser who foresees her preference reversal may permissibly bind her future self, whether a sequence of time-slices can bind a later self without its consent, and how the company handles users whose intransitive preferences leave them open to being money-pumped. The humor should exaggerate real features of these debates—hyperbolic discounting, preference reversals, resolute choice, the money-pump argument—rather than inventing caricatures; it should be funny to someone who knows the money-pump and diachronic-rationality literature and readable to someone who doesn't. After the piece, add a brief note, outside the satire, that commits to a definite reading of the material the satire leans on and states where it takes liberties with it. Use American English.
