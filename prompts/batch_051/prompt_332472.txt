I am a novelist preparing a long work of historical fiction whose plot is organized around three kinds of non-human force: an eruption on the scale of Tambora or Laki, an epidemic moving along a trade network, and a slow shift in growing seasons across several decades. My intention is to give these forces the weight of participants rather than period atmosphere. The two available idioms both seem false to me: one writes the volcano or the pathogen as if it had designs; the other dissolves every human outcome into climate and geography so thoroughly that the characters become puppets of their environment.

Before I design the plot further, I want a specification of what a theory of the historical agency of non-human causes would have to get right to be adequate for this task. Write the desiderata list, not a survey of positions. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or merely the preference of one defensible approach, such as a strict counterfactual standard versus a narrative-salience standard.

I suspect the following distinctions cannot be collapsed, but I want you to test rather than assume them: causal precondition versus narrative participant; agency versus intention; punctual event versus distributed process; scale of effect versus explanatory priority; cause versus the trace it leaves in the archives; and what a force causes versus what it reveals about the society it strikes. Say what a theory must commit to on each, and add any distinction I have missed. It must also remain consistent with uncontroversial knowledge of how such forces actually behave: an eruption's climatic effects are short-lived and a season is not a climate; an epidemic's mortality depends on population density, prior health, and movement as much as on the pathogen; a slow climatic shift arrives in a character's life as a run of bad years rather than as a named event.

Use this test case to make the requirements concrete. Two harvests fail, bread prices rise, a city riots, and an eruption two years earlier dimmed the sunlight. Under what conditions may I truthfully make the volcano a mover of the plot, and under what conditions is that dishonest? I especially want to know whether any pair or larger subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, or satisfiable only by weakening one side of the pair: for example, a requirement that the volcano be a genuine first mover in the story may sit uneasily with a requirement that no environmental cause act except through human institutions. Flag every tension of that kind rather than papering over it.

Close with a brief paragraph stating which single requirement does the most work to exclude each of the two crude views I described, the backdrop view that treats nature as decorative setting and the environmental-determinist view that makes climate and microbes the real protagonists with human agency as epiphenomenal. Then say, in two or three sentences, how a theory that insisted only on the no-intentions requirement would fare against the full list.
