I am a high school student who runs a small philosophy-and-technology podcast for my school's philosophy club, and next week I am interviewing a professor of epistemology whose recent work defends a heavily offloading-friendly account of our reliance on large language models. As I understand her position, she holds that depending on an AI to draft arguments, diagnose one's reasoning, or summarize a field is not in principle different from depending on a notebook, a calculator, or a reliable colleague: the competence lives in the extended system of person-plus-tool, and talk of an "erosion of understanding" mistakes a relocation of cognitive work for a loss of it. More specifically, she maintains that a learner who can reliably check a model's output against her own judgment has not lost any understanding worth wanting, even if she could no longer produce the answer unaided.

I would like the interview to press on the joints of that view rather than allow her to restate it, so please draft eight to ten questions that are concrete and adversarial in a friendly way, with no generic openers. I want at least one question on each of the following, and for each I want a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. First, the bare-controller case: a professional who can recognize the model's answers as right but could not reconstruct the reasoning behind them, and whether recognition without regeneration amounts to understanding. Second, the difference between a tool that extends a skill the user already keeps active and a tool that quietly replaces the skill a novice would otherwise have acquired; her likely reply will be that verification itself is the skill worth keeping, so push on where the ability to detect a subtle error is supposed to come from if unaided production never gets exercised. Third, the responsibility problem: if the system rather than the individual is the unit that understands, what becomes of individual expertise, credit, and blame when the tool fails or disappears, and is saying the competence was always distributed merely a redescription of the loss she denies? Write each follow-up as a natural continuation of the reply she would most plausibly give, not as a scripted tag-on question.
