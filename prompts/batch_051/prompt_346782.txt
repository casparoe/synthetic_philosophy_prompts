Question:

Bayesian conditionalization requires that the evidence acquired be a proposition E such that the new credence in E is 1. Jeffrey conditionalization is often introduced as a way to handle evidence that is uncertain or partial. State the formula for Jeffrey conditionalization over a finite partition, explain what rigidity assumption it imposes, and discuss two further issues: first, whether successive Jeffrey updates on different partitions are path-independent, and second, whether the diachronic Dutch book argument establishes Jeffrey conditionalization as the only rational update rule for uncertain evidence.

Student’s answer:

Jeffrey conditionalization applies when an experience directly changes one’s credences in the members of a salient partition {E1, …, En}, without making any single Ei certain. If the posterior credences over that partition are Q(Ei), then for any proposition H, the new credence is given by:

Q(H) = Σi P(H | Ei) Q(Ei).

The rule builds in a rigidity assumption: the conditional probabilities P(H | Ei) are held fixed through the update. That is what allows the shift in the partition to propagate to other propositions. The shift is understood as uncertain evidence because the experience does not force one to assign probability 1 to some evidence proposition.

The diachronic Dutch book argument shows that any agent who violates Jeffrey conditionalization is vulnerable to a bookie’s strategy. Therefore, Jeffrey conditionalization is the only rational update rule for cases of uncertain evidence.

Finally, successive Jeffrey updates on different partitions commute. Since each update uses the same formula and simply multiplies through by the appropriate conditional probabilities, applying an update on one partition and then an update on another gives the same final credences as applying them in the opposite order. Thus Jeffrey conditionalization is path-independent.
