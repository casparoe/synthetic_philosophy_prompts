For my alignment reading group — about 15 people, one 75-minute session — I want a game that makes them feel Goodhart's law, specifically the difference between the two failure modes I keep seeing people conflate. Mode one is plain specification gaming: an agent hits the letter of the objective and misses the spirit. Mode two is reward-model overoptimization: the evaluators are themselves just a noisy proxy for whatever we actually want, so past a certain level of optimization the proxy score keeps going up while true quality falls off a cliff. I don't want another 'give them a points scheme and watch them munchkin it' exercise; they've done that and it only gets them to the boring version. I want rules, roles, a scoring system with no real money, and a timeline where the participants themselves draft the proxy metric, act as the agents optimizing it, and act as the evaluators whose metric gets gamed — and where there's a revision round after they've seen the gaming. The point of the revision round should be that patching the rubric doesn't fix the underlying problem: it just relocates the hole, and that's the thing I want them to feel. The debrief should map what happened to the distinction between regressional, extremal, causal, and adversarial Goodhart, and to the specific moment where a better rubric wasn't the fix. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and which part of real Goodharting a classroom can't reproduce (my guess is that 15 undergrads can't be pushed into the true tails where extremal Goodhart lives) so I know what I'll have to say rather than show. And please dont have the facilitator lecture the distinction first; that would defeat the whole point.
