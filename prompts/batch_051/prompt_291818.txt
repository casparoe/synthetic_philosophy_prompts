Before I settle my view on whether LLM hallucination should count as lying, bullshit, or mere error, lay out the standard an adequate theory of bullshit would have to meet, rather than another survey of positions. Write the desiderata: the cases it must classify correctly, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the structural constraints it must respect — with, for each, the case or puzzle that forces it, and a mark of whether it is non-negotiable or only a preference of one camp. Then flag any pairs or larger sets of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and name the single requirement that does the most work in excluding simple views.

Take as the foil the distinction usually attributed to Frankfurt: lying needs an intention to induce a false belief, while bullshit involves indifference to whether what one says is true. I want the requirements, not an exposition of that view.

The account must make principled sense of at least the following:
- The liar, who asserts not-p while taking p to be true, in order that the audience believe not-p.
- The spin doctor, who selects and arranges claims for effect without caring whether the resulting impression is accurate.
- The honest error, where someone asserts not-p because their evidence misleadingly supports not-p, and would revise if corrected.
- The accidentally true bullshitter, who is indifferent to truth and happens to get it right.
- The self-deceiver, who appears to do to themself what the bullshitter does to an audience.
- The committee report or PR pipeline in which no individual ever believes the false claim, but the organization's output is fluent, confident, and unconstrained by evidence.
- The language model, trained to maximize plausibility or approval rather than truth, which emits fluent, confident falsehoods with no agent intending to deceive about that claim — and arguably no agent who holds any belief about it at all.

For each requirement, say what forces it and why. In particular, I want you to address: whether a theory must choose between locating bullshit in a speaker's mental state and locating it in an utterance's warrant, and what each choice loses; whether a process or institution can literally bullshit; whether bullshit must be compatible with being accidentally true; and whether its epistemic damage is best located in the speaker, in the utterance, or in the shared practice that the output degrades. Mark which of these demands pull against one another. Then, in a short closing paragraph, score three candidate views against the list — the indifference-to-truth view, a no-adequate-warrant view, and the deflationary view that hallucination is just error plus confidence — and say which requirement creates the most trouble for each. Keep the scoring brief; the criteria are the deliverable.
