There's an idea often called a free-floating rationale: a reason for a trait or practice that can be genuine even though no one anywhere represents it. Suppose a funding body adopts a rule built on that idea. Every grant proposal must contain a free-floating rationale section: the strongest statement the applicant can produce of why the work deserves funding that would still count as a reason if the entire team lost interest tomorrow and no one else endorsed the project. Reviewers are told to score the proposal on that section alone, ignoring the applicants' actual motives. How would this rule really behave? What would applicants write, what would reviewers in fact reward, how would it get gamed, and would it select for better work or just for people who are good at writing free-standing justifications? Then say whether the failure modes could be fixed by tweaking the rule, or whether the rule is doing something incoherent.
