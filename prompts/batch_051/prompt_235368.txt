I've been thinking on my own about an old dispute over personal identity and keep returning to a thought experiment in Thomas Reid's Essays on the Intellectual Powers of Man, Essay III, chapter VI. As I understand the passage, Reid asks us to suppose a boy is flogged for robbing an orchard; later, as a young officer, he takes a standard from the enemy and remembers the flogging; later still, as an old general, he remembers taking the standard but has completely lost all memory of the flogging. Reid says Locke's account gives us that the officer is the same person as the boy, and the general is the same person as the officer, but the general is not the same person as the boy, because his consciousness no longer reaches back to the flogging. That seems to make the general both identical with and distinct from the boy.

For each crux, include a case, argument, or test both would accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each side predicts. Please format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
