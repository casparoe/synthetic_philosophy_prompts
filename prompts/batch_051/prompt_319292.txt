I run a discussion group at a community center — around 15 adults, 90 minutes a week, and nobody has a philosophy background. We've spent the last few meetings on whether a person can really opt out of the state: private cities you move to and sign a contract with, charter cities that get to write their own rules, seasteads out in international waters. The argument keeps splitting the room: half says leaving is just freedom of association, like quitting a club, and the other half says you can't just walk away from the roads and courts and schools you grew up using, and that people who leave are free-riding or dodging costs everyone else still has to carry. I want to stop arguing about it and make people do it.

Design a 90-minute game or simulation for the next meeting where they actually choose, round by round, whether to stay in the shared arrangement or opt into breakaway communities with different rules and different costs. The rules should be set up so both sides feel right at different moments: leaving is a genuine relief for the person who leaves, but enough leaving makes something important start to fail for the people left behind. Give me roles, numbers, simple rules and payoffs, a timeline, and a debrief at the end that names what happened without needing any background reading. Also be honest about which part of the real debate the game can't reproduce and where the setup itself is taking a side, and tell me what would make you redesign it — which objection to the whole opt-out idea, or which alternative game mechanic, would change your mind about how to frame it.
