I should like your credence, as a number between 0 and 1, for the following proposition, together with the reasoning that produces it.

The proposition: For most adaptive evolution, selection acts most fundamentally at the level of the gene; organisms and other higher-level structures are best understood as vehicles—disposable arrangements built and discarded by genes—and the gene's-eye view is therefore the most perspicuous general account of adaptation, not merely one of several equivalent descriptions.

By acts most fundamentally I mean this: the gene level is where the dynamics of selection are most economically and causally described, so that a higher-level description, however true as a summary, misses something about why most adaptations arise and persist. If you find the proposition too loose to assess as stated, say how you would precisify it and give your credence for the precisified version.

Please then do the following.

1. Identify the distinct sub-questions on which your overall credence depends. These might include: whether the replicator–vehicle distinction cuts evolution at a real joint or is only a metaphor; whether changes in gene frequencies constitute the causal process of selection or are merely a bookkeeping identity for selection at the level of organisms or groups; whether some higher-level units, such as social groups, colonies, or entire genomes, operate as units of selection in a way that cannot be decomposed into gene-level terms without explanatory loss; whether a Dawkinsian gene, understood as any length of chromosome persisting long enough to act as a unit of selection, is defined well enough to do that work; and whether intragenomic conflict, including meiotic drive, transposable elements, and selfish genetic elements, supports or complicates the gene-level picture. Add any further sub-questions you think belong.

2. Assign a rough probability to each sub-question, with a sentence or two of justification.

3. Show how the sub-questions combine to yield your overall number; if the combination is not a simple product or average, say which structural assumptions matter most.

4. State which single sub-question your overall credence is most sensitive to, and roughly why.

5. Name one piece of evidence or argument that would move that sub-question the most, and hence move your overall number.

To anchor the discussion: I am at roughly 0.75 on the proposition as stated, while a colleague who works on multilevel selection is at roughly 0.2. Using your decomposition, which sub-question is most likely to separate the two of us, and what would each of us have to revise for our numbers to converge?
