I'm trying to help two factions in my Nomic club write up an adversarial-collaboration statement, and I'd like you to draft it. The club has been stuck for years between two positions about self-amendment.

The first group (call them the Suberians) takes Peter Suber at his word when he writes, in the introductory note on his Nomic page:

"For me, it was intended to illustrate and embody the thesis of my book, The Paradox of Self-Amendment, that a legal 'rule of change' such as a constitutional amendment clause may apply to itself and authorize its own amendment."

They think a rule of change can validly apply to itself, and that Nomic shows there's no paradox: amending the amendment procedure is just one more legal move made under the rules that happen to be in force when the move is made. The second group (call them the entrenchmentists) thinks this is exactly where self-amendment falls apart. They say a rule can't pull itself into existence or grant itself its own amending power, so either something sits outside the amendable rules and can't itself be amended by them (the players' standing agreement, the rule that current rules are binding, something like a Kelsenian grundnorm), or the game reduces to whatever the current majority can get away with. They like to point out that real Nomic games usually end up treating some rules as practically off-limits, and they claim that isn't an accident.

What I want is the document the two camps would actually both sign. Give me, in decent prose: first, a section of numbered claims about the Suber passage and the mechanics of self-amendment that both sides can agree to without hedging, without being so watered down they're empty. Then a disagreements section, where each genuine disagreement is stated twice: once in wording the Suberians would accept for their own view, and once in wording the entrenchmentists would accept for theirs. Then a cruxes section: sub-questions where settling one would move at least one side, with each side's prediction recorded in advance, and, where possible, a test, formal model, or describable Nomic game outcome both sides agree would bear on it. And if you think part of the fight is verbal (for example, they mean different things by "rule" or "in force"), say which part and why, and seperate that from the real disagreements.

After the prose, add a JSON object with these fields: "agreed_claims" (list of strings), "disagreements" (list of objects with keys "point", "suberian_wording", "entrenchmentist_wording", "why_it_matters"), "cruxes" (list of objects with keys "question", "suberian_prediction", "entrenchmentist_prediction", "test_or_case"), "verbal_disagreements" (list of objects with keys "term", "suberian_meaning", "entrenchmentist_meaning"), and "residual_question" (the sharpest way to state what's left once the verbal stuff is cleared away).
