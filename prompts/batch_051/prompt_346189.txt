I keep accusing people of arguing in bad faith, but I'm not sure I could pass a test on what I actually mean by it. Give me a working definition of "arguing in good faith" and defend it.

To give the definition something to earn, here are cases I want it to handle. Clear ones: I'd want it to count as good faith my friend who'll change his mind mid-conversation if you show that the thing he's leaning on is false, asks "do you mean X or Y?" before objecting, and can say what evidence would move him. I'd want it to count as not good faith (a) a sealioning account that keeps saying it's "just asking questions" and never stops demanding sources, and (b) someone who's already decided and is performing for an audience, where every reply is addressed to spectators rather than to you.

Now the hard cases:
- someone sincerely believes a false thing and repeats claims that have been debunked five times, but has never checked and isn't deliberately lying; sincere but epistemically lazy.
- a pundit paid to defend a side who never publicly concedes anything; he might privately be convinced, I can't tell from outside.
- someone who deliberately avoids reading the strongest version of the other side because he suspects it would move him. Not dishonest, he just looks away.
- a devil's advocate who argues a position he doesn't hold, says so upfront, but does it to stress-test an idea.
- someone in a group where disagreeing with the local line costs you standing, so they argue for the other side while constantly signaling loyalty and leaving an escape hatch.
- someone who applies strict skeptical scrutiny to the other side's evidence and none to his own, but is unaware of the asymmetry.

I want the definition to give a definet verdict for each hard case, not "it depends." Then run it through all of them and tell me where it gives a verdict you didn't expect, or where you had to revise the definition to keep a case you wanted to come out the other way. I have a hunch good faith is only partly about sincerity — someone can be completely sincere and still be bad faith in some structural sense — but maybe that doesn't survive the cases. If the concept needs to split into sincerity plus something else, say which of the two I should be pointing at when I accuse someone of bad faith.
