I'm writing an essay on Blade Runner and the deepfake problem. The film keeps using photographs as proof of a personal past: Rachael keeps a snapshot of a little girl with her mother as evidence that her childhood memories are hers, and Deckard later tells her those memories are implants and the photo is someone else's past. Read this through the idea of an epistemic backstop — the thought that recordings are what we fall back on when memory and testimony fail, the evidence that would settle what actualy happened. Does the film already anticipate the deepfake worry that the backstop itself can be manufactured, or is it a more old-fashioned story about memory being unreliable while photos remain solid? What exactly would have to be true for Blade Runner to count as a deepfake allegory rather than a memory allegory? Where does the lens genuinely illuminate the film and where does it force it?
