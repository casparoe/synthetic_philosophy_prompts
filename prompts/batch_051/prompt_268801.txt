I cover science and ideas for a living, and I keep coming back to the first weeks of the covid pandemic — roughly January through late March 2020 — as a real-life case of decision under ignorance, not just under risk. I know the broad timeline: the Wuhan lockdown, the WHO emergency declaration, Italy locking down, the UK and US hesitating before their own lockdowns, and everywhere the same underlying choice about whether to impose very costly restrictions on the basis of a virus nobody could yet characterize. What I want is a case analysis through the formal apparatus, not a policy retrospective. Take maximin, minimax regret, and the Rawls–Harsanyi split over how to choose when probabilities are unavailable, and map them onto the situation carefully. Who or what is the "decision-maker" here, and is treating a government as a single agent a modeling cheat? What are the actual options and the relevant states of the world? What does each criterion tell a decision-maker to do if they really refuse to put numbers on the virus's transmissibility and lethality? And where the criteria disagree, say why. Then give me an honest account of where the framework breaks or goes quiet. My hunch is that these tools were built for one-shot choices by a single agent, while real goverments faced a sequence of choices, information that changed faster than any prior, exit problems, and wildly uneven distribution of costs — but I want to know whether that diagnosis is right. Finally, say what the episode does back to the framework: does it vindicate maximin as a public-health heuristic, expose it as too blunt, or show that the situation was never really "ignorance" in the required sense? Please keep seperated what is settled in decision theory (the definitions, the formal results, what Harsanyi actually proved) from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this — for instance, whether Rawls's use of maximin actually works — and don't present a contested reading as the consensus. And I'm after the verdict of the criteria at the time, not what turns out to have been right with hindsight.
