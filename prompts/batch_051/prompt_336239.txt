I spent most of my career in a maize breeding program, and since retiring I've been working through some bioethics. I keep noticing a parallel between the argument for procreative beneficence and the logic of recurrent selection in plant breeding, and I can't figure out whether its deep or superficial.

As I understand procreative beneficence, it says that if you're choosing among possible future children—say, several IVF embryos—you have a strong moral reason to pick the one expected to have the best life, given available information. The liberal eugenics part is that this is meant to be a matter of individual parental choice, not state coercion or improving the species. In breeding, of course, I did something structurally similar for decades: rank candidates by estimated breeding value, set a selection differential, and predict the response in the next generation as heritability times that selection differential.

Both are cases of selecting the highest expected value from a finite set of candidates under an additive genetic model. My question is whether this is a genuine structural identity, a loose analogy, or just me pattern-matching.

If it is real, what transfers? I immediately think of correlated response to selection—in maize, selecting hard for yield drags along all sorts of other traits, which is why we built selection indices. Does procreative beneficence face an analogue: selecting on a genome-wide index for something like educational attainment or life satisfaction drags along other heritable traits that matter for a good life? Do selection limits or genotype-environment interaction transfer as arguments? And where does the mapping break? My own suspicion is that the breeder's equation itself fails to transfer, because an embryo's genome is already fixed—there's no Mendelian sampling, so heritability should enter only through the accuracy of prediction, not as a shrink factor. Is that the real disanalogy, or a sign the analogy was always superficial?

I'm after a structural diagnosis rather than a survey: state the mapping, what would have to be true on both sides for lessons to transfer, and what the parallel predicts that turns out false.
