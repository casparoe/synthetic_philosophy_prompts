I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but at a residency last fall a poet cornered me with something he got from Galen Strawson and I still haven't been able to shake it. His version of the basic argument ran like this. What I do is a function of the way I am — my character, my motives, my hang-ups — together with the situation I find myself in. So to be truly responsible for anything I do, I would have to be truly responsible for the way I am. But to be responsible for the way I am, I would have to have chosen how I am, and to be responsible for that choice I would have had to choose how I chose it, and so on back with no floor. Since I cannot be my own cause, I cannot ever be ultimately responsible for anything. Fine.

The frightening part is that the same machine seems to run, with no new parts, through my whole profession. (1) The novel I write is a function of the writer I am — my ear, my obsessions, my blind spots, my impatience — plus the situation I write in. (2) I didn't choose the writer I am; any choice I made would itself have come from the writer I already was, and so on back. (3) So I am not ultimately responsible for the novel, no matter how many drafts I burn. (4) If that's right, then the entire apparatus of authorship — the review, the prize comittee, the blurb, the reader who says my sentences changed her — is crediting someone who is no more responsible for the book than for his own birth. (5) But I can't accept that the whole apparatus is a mistake. I spent four years making this one book better, and the difference between the dead draft and the published one is something I did, not weather.

So the argument seems to prove too much, and something has to give. If I insist on keeping (5), which premise do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me elsewhere? Specifically, can I reject it for the fiction case while leaving the moral-responsibility version intact, or would that be special pleading? I don't want to end up the writer who claims the good pages are mine and the bad ones are my upbringing.
