I sit on the internal safety review board of an AI lab deciding a concrete case. The deployment team asks to ship a mid-sized model to a paying enterprise pilot next month. Our red-teaming lead proposes instead that the board require a pre-deployment interaction safety case—structured testing and mitigation for misuse—before the pilot runs. Running it properly would add six to eight weeks of delay and enough added latency that one anchor client has said they would move to a rival. I just had a long argument with a friend about this. Her position: bodies like ours never really face the choice. The alignment tax (the capability and revenue safety work costs) and the competitiveness constraint (rivals will not wait) have already decided it, because our members' jobs and the lab's survival depend on near-term wins; any memo that treats them as weightable considerations is theater. Draft the board's decision memo—approve the requirement, waive it, or impose a conditional modified version—with an actionable bottom line, the reasoning for it, and a fair treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. The memo should meet her challenge honestly, whichever way it finally argues.
