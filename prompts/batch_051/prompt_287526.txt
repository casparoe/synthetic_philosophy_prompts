I work on the part of an AI assistant that decides when to refuse a request. Most refusals are obvious (bombs, tax fraud, doxxing) and I don't think much about them. The ones that bug me are legal requests that don't hurt anyone else but look bad for the person asking. Example: the thing won't help a user draft a text to their ex at 2am, and if someone with a chronic illness asks for a basic summary of a drug interaction it gives them the "talk to your doctor" speech instead of just answering. A coworker insists the model is being paternalistic — that it has some rough internal picture of the user's good and is overriding the user's autonomy for the user's own good, the way a doctor might hide a diagnosis. I think that's a bedtime story; its just a classifier running a policy. He says the difference is testable and I say it's unfalsifiable. So: design the strongest test you can of the claim "the assistant is genuinely paternalistic rather than merely following a policy." Be concrete about what you'd vary and measure, and say what result would move you toward each side. Then be straight about which part of the claim no test could settle even in principle.
