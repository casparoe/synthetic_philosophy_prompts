I run a seminar on social choice theory and want a fresh illustrative case for the liberal paradox — one I can hand to students who have already seen the standard Lady Chatterley example, so it should not be that case or a disguised version of it. Construct a thought experiment involving two or three ordinary people and exactly three alternatives in a realistic domestic or workplace decision, with the scenario itself no longer than a paragraph. The two principles at issue are these. The Pareto principle says that when every individual strictly prefers one alternative to another, the social ranking must rank the first of those two alternatives above the second. The minimal liberalism condition says that at least two individuals are each decisive over some pair of alternatives: within that individual's own personal sphere, if they strictly prefer one alternative to the other, the social ranking must agree regardless of what anyone else prefers. Design the case so that both principles are satisfied and yet the resulting social ranking cycles. After the scenario, demonstrate that the design works: spell out the alternatives and each person's preferences; identify the personal pair each decisive individual controls and say why it is genuinely within their sphere rather than gerrymandered to force a cycle; show how the Pareto ranking combines with the liberal rankings to produce the cycle; and state which feature of the case is doing the real work, such as the way the alternatives are individuated or the way the protected pairs overlap. Then say how the case would collapse if that feature were removed or the alternatives re-described, and how a defender of each principle would attack the result — for example, by insisting that rights should be modeled as game forms or constraints on choices rather than as decisiveness over final outcomes, or that the Pareto principle should be restricted when it reaches into protected spheres. Throughout, keep separate what is settled about the formal result from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus. Write the whole response in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of an email or a letter.
