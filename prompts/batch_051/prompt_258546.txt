My church's Statement of Faith (adopted 1976, binding on ministers) has an Article VII that reads:

"God is impassible: he cannot be acted upon, troubled, or changed by anything within the creation, and his affections are eternally the same. We reject every doctrine that ascribes suffering, passion, or mutability to the divine nature."

A minister in our denomination has just published a book about the cross. His claim, roughly, is that in the incarnation the Son assumed a full human nature and in that nature genuinely suffered dread, grief, and abandonment; and since the Son is God, it is true that God suffered — not in the divine nature, but in the person of the Son through the humanity he assumed. Article VII was written after a row about whether God can be grieved by sin or moved by prayer; the incarnation and the two natures never come up in the minutes or the surrounding debate. The denomination is now deciding whether the book is a breach of Article VII.

How should Article VII be applied? Set out the four readings seperately: the words alone; the original purpose; what the drafters would have said if this exact case had been put to them; and what a reasonable member of the denomination today would take the Article to mean. Where those readings come apart, which should govern — and is there a defensible reading on which the minister's book doesn't breach Article VII?
