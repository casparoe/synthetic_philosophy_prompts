I've gotten kind of obsessed with Bongard problems lately—you know, those puzzles with six boxes on the left and six on the right, where you have to find the rule that separates the two sides. I made one for a group of friends, but since I can't draw to save my life I just described each box in words. We ended up with seven proposed rules that all fit the examples perfectly, and now I truly can't decide which one is the "real" rule, or whether some are just kind of cheating. I'd love a second opionion.

The left boxes are: (1) one big red triangle, (2) one big red square, (3) one big red circle, (4) one big red star, (5) one big red cross, (6) one big red diamond. The right boxes are: (1) two small blue triangles, (2) two small blue squares, (3) two small blue circles, (4) two small blue stars, (5) two small blue crosses, (6) two small blue diamonds.

The proposed rules are:
R1: left = exactly one object, right = exactly two objects.
R2: left = big objects, right = small objects.
R3: left = red figures, right = blue figures.
R4: left = one big red object, right = two small blue objects.
R5: left = anything that is red OR big OR single; right = anything that is blue OR small OR paired (yes, I know this one spills over onto new cases, but it does fit all twelve I showed).
R6: left = just the six exact boxes I listed on the left; right = just the six exact boxes I listed on the right.
R7: left = being on the left side of the page, right = being on the right side.

Could you sort these seven rules into tiers by how much you'd trust each one as the distinction that would keep working if I added new boxes later? Give each rule a score out of ten and a sentence saying why it lands where it does. I'm especially torn about R1, R2, and R3: they're equally simple and all fit the data, and I can't tell if there's actually a principled reason count should beat color or size, or if that's just a vibe.
