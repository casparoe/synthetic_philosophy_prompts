I've been trying to run the Euthyphro dilemma against people who ground morality in God's nature rather than God's commands, and I want to know whether my version actually goes through. Here it is: (1) If rightness is grounded in God's nature, then either God's nature has the properties it does because those properties are right-making, or those properties are right-making because they are the properties had by God's nature. (2) On the first option, there's a standard of rightness independent of God, which undercuts the point of nature-grounding. (3) On the second option, God's moral character is arbitrary in roughly the way the original command horn was: if God's nature had been cruel, cruelty would have been right. (4) But a theist can't except either option. (5) So nature-grounding is false. My gut is that step (3) is where this breaks — on classical theism God's nature is necessary, so the 'arbitrariness' charge doesn't transfer from the command version. Am I right, or does the argument fail earlier, maybe already at (1)?
