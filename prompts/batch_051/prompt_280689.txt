I'm a software engineer and I've got into the habit of flagging 'bad grammar' in code review: comments, commit messages, docstrings, user-facing error strings. I'll flag 'less' where I think it should be 'fewer', split infinitives, singular 'they', ending clauses with a preposition, 'who' vs 'whom', passive voice. Half the team reckons I'm being pedantic and says linguists have been descriptivists for ages — there's no such thing as 'bad grammar' beyond what a speech community actually does. I half-believe that, but I also don't want to loose users because our docs look sloppy. So: is the habit defensible? I'm not asking about each individual rule (I know some are shibboleths) but about the policy of enforcing a written standard at all when that standard looks arbitrary from a descriptive point of view. And if some rules earn their keep and others don't, how do I tell which is which without just deferring to a style guide? Please answer in British English.
