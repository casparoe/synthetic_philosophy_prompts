Deciding whether the Twitter link is suspicious is trickier than it looks, because the credibility of the source and the content are only loosely connected. The coefficients are a signal about the user, not about the story: a brand-new account that's mostly reposting political talking points and making typos could be amplifying a real local news article, and a "verified" account could be laundering nonsense. Weighting by coefficients flattens all of that. But the platform's stated goal might not be truth anyway — it might be making engagement look healthier, no superv, or shifting blame to users. The failure that bugs me most is that if coefficients are adjusted based on how often a user gets liked or replied to, then controversy trumps correctness, and the whole thing becomes an outrage-generation machine.
