I keep flip-flopping on hedonism, and I'd rather be shown how to stop flip-flopping than told which side to land on. The theory has a lot going for it: it gives me the tidiest picture of value I know of, because everything good is good in virtue of how it feels from the inside, which means there's a single underlying currency, a clean decision proceedure for comparing outcomes, and no spooky realm of value floating free of experience. The arguments for it feel, to me, like the only ones that don't end up pointing at good-making features I can never actually catch hold of when I introspect.

But I also have a firm intuition that pleasures are just too heterogenous to be one thing. The pleasure of finally seeing through a proof, the pleasure of a hot shower after being freezing, the mean little rush of schadenfreude, the pleasure of a drug, the pleasure of finishing a run you wanted to quit—these don't feel like the same kind of state. They're felt in different places, last different lengths of time, some pull you toward the experience while others just ride on top of it, some I want to call 'mental' and some 'bodily.' If hedonism claims they all share a single property that does the value-work, I want to know whether that property is real or just a word for 'whatever ended up on this list of good-seeming experiences.' So: which yields? Should the intuition be debunked as mistaking surface variety for an underlying shared valence, should the theory be amended (attitudinal pleasure, a functional account, pluralism, something else), or is there a reading on which the heterogeneity is true and yet irrelevant to what makes them all good? Don't just pick a side—give me exactly five numbered candidate moves, each one or two sentences, with no intro or conclusion, and make each one say what it costs and what would justify it. I care less about which move you'd make than about the honest price tag on each.
