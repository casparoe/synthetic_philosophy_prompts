Consider a version of the murderer-at-the-door case in which the murderer does not ask one question and leave, but turns the householder into a live tracking device. The house has three rooms connected by a hidden crawlspace; the friend hiding inside can move through the crawlspace at will, and the householder can watch him through internal cameras. The murderer, standing at the locked front door, announces the following rule: every two minutes he will ask a yes/no question of the form, Is your friend currently in room X? If the householder answers truthfully, the murderer will immediately enter and search that specific room; if the room is empty when he arrives, or if the householder gives a false answer, or if he refuses to answer, the murderer will shoot the householder and then continue searching. The murderer cannot be reasoned with further and is known to follow this rule without exception.

In his 1797 essay On a Supposed Right to Lie from Benevolent Motives, Kant argues that one may not lie to a would-be murderer because truthfulness is an unconditional duty, and because the consequences of a truthful statement are uncertain enough that one cannot justify a lie by predicting a bad outcome. But in this variant the householder is not making one statement with opaque downstream effects; he is making a continuous series of truth-apt reports that function as a sensor for a deliberate killer, and he can time those reports to send the killer away from his friend, because the friend can move through the crawlspace after each answer.

What does Kant's analysis say here? Specifically, after the first two truthful answers have established to the murderer that the householder is cooperative and the friend is evasive, what is the householder required to do at the third question, if he knows that answering truthfully, Is your friend currently in room Y, will result in the killer entering room Y while the friend has just moved to room Z? Can he separate the act of truthful assertion from the act of guiding a search, or does Kant's position force him to become a reliable informant? And if the householder begins answering with true but carefully timed statements that send the killer to empty rooms, is that still compatible with the unconditional duty of truthfulness, or has he found a way to treat truth as a weapon without uttering a lie?
