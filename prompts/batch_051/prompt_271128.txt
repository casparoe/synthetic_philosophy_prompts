I have no academic background in philosophy or statistics, but I have spent a fair amount of time thinking on my own about what it actually means to say that something has a certain probability. A friend who reads a lot of popular science and philosophy and speaks with considerable confidence recently offered me the following rundown of the main interpretations.

Frequentism, he said, is the view that the probability of an event is the relative frequency with which events of that kind occur in a long run of repeated, similar trials. For claims that do not pick out a repeatable setup, such as the claim that it will rain tomorrow or that a particular suspect is guilty, he said a frequentist has to say either that the claim lacks a well-defined probability or that it is really shorthand for a frequency in some larger class of similar cases. Subjectivism, he continued, is the view that probability is just a measure of a person's degree of belief. Because different people can rationally start with different degrees of confidence, he said, there are no real constraints on the probabilities anyone may assign; this permissiveness, he added, is exactly what subjectivists mean by coherence. Finally, he described the propensity interpretation as the claim that probability is an objective physical tendency of a chance setup, such as a coin or a die, to produce a certain outcome. But he dismissed this as frequentism in disguise: the only way to check a propensity claim is to collect frequencies over many trials, so, he concluded, the propensity view adds nothing beyond a metaphorical picture.

This left me with three impressions: that frequentism makes ordinary single-case probability claims meaningless, that subjectivism holds any probability assignment to be as reasonable as any other, and that the propensity view has nothing distinctive going for it. I would like to know whether my friend's summary is fair. Please go through it claim by claim and tell me which parts a defender of each interpretation would accept, which parts are subtle mischaracterizations, and whether the three impressions I drew actually follow from the accurate parts. Please write your answer in American English.
