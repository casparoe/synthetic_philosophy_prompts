I am taking a philosophy of technology course, and we have been working on whether artifacts themselves can be political rather than merely put to political uses. My professor has given a series of verdicts, and I would like to work out the principle behind them before our exam. So far, she has judged as follows: the parkway overpass built too low for buses to pass beneath—political; the mechanical tomato harvester whose introduction made small-farm tomato growing unworkable and shifted power toward large growers—political; the nuclear power plant—political, and in her view the strongest case of the artifact itself being political; a household rooftop solar array—not political; and an ordinary bicycle—not political. Next week's case is a city bike-share system whose docks require a smartphone and a credit card, so that residents without either cannot use it. How should I predict she will judge it? Please identify the candidate principles consistent with her record, say which principle each verdict most supports, state what each candidate principle predicts for the bike-share, give the best-supported prediction, and describe the further case that would most cleanly distinguish the remaining principles.
