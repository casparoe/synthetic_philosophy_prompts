My school's academic integrity policy says a correct answer on a math or science exam is worth almost nothing unless the work is shown: the reasoning has to be on the page, in a form a teacher can inspect. But the same school now encourages us to use AI tools for research and coding, and everyone around me—students and teachers—judges those tools almost entirely by whether the output is right. Nobody can say how the model got there, and mostly nobody tries. That looks like a real asymmetry in ordinary practice: when a person's reasoning is a black box that happens to reach the right answer, we treat the person as not really understanding and grade accordingly; when a machine's reasoning is a black box that happens to reach the right answer, we treat the machine as useful, and increasingly as something to be trusted.

I am trying to figure out whether anything justifies treating the two cases differently. One friend says the cases differ because a student is being trained to become a competent reasoner, while an AI is just a means to an end: the student is the point, the tool is not. But I am not sure that holds, since we are starting to deploy opaque models in settings where the reasoning matters just as much as the result, such as screening applications or suggesting medical follow-ups. A second friend says the real problem is our low standards for machines: we should be putting effort into training models to be legible—to produce reasoning a person can actually audit—rather than accepting accurate-but-unreadable as the best that can be done. My question is whether the asymmetry is defensible, and on what exact grounds, or whether it is just an inconsistency we have drifted into. Whichever way you lean, please construct and test the strongest justification you can find for treating students and AI systems differently, consider what that justification implies for high-stakes uses, and say whether we should keep the asymmetry, reverse it, or give it up. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
