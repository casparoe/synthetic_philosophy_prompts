I keep reading popular science pieces about why an animal has this or that feature, and every author sounds completely sure their story is right. One says the peacock's tail is there because peahens find it attractive; another says male nipples are just a side effect of how embryos get build and don't have a purpose at all; then there's the appendix, which is either useful or leftover depending on who you ask. I've decided I'm not going to be taken in by a clever-sounding story any more. Before I read another one of these, I want to know what an explanation of why a body part or behaviour exists has to get right before it deserves to be believed. Tell me the things such an explanation has to handle, the cases that trip people up, the distinctions it can't flatten (like what a trait was originally for versus what keeps it around now, or being useful versus being neutral), and the traps it has to avoid (like being good at telling a story without good evidence), and for each one say what case or fact makes it a requirement. Then tell me which of these requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's tastes, and which single requirement would finish off most of the pet stories floating around. And if it turns out no account can meet all the requirements at once, say so, because that's the part I'd most want to know. Please write it as continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold text or tables, like an email.
