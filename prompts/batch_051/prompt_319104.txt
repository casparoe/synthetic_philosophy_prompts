I'm a junior majoring in philosophy and currently taking a course called The Ethics of Play, where we've been discussing how games represent morally serious subject matter. A puzzle keeps coming up for me, and I'd like you to help me think it through in a structured way.

Mainstream war games often seem to hold two attitudes toward their subject matter at once. On the one hand, they treat armed conflict as an occasion for entertainment: players are rewarded for kills, weapon mastery is central to the fun, and advertising sells the thrill of combat. On the other hand, many of the same games make conspicuous gestures of seriousness. They hire historical consultants, advertise their authenticity and respect for veterans, include memorial sequences or disclaimers, and sometimes refuse to make certain atrocities available as player mechanics even when the technology would easily allow them. A few games go further and present themselves as anti-war statements, claiming that their gameplay is really a device for conveying how terrible war is. I am not asking whether any particular game achieves what it claims. I am asking what philosophical commitments this whole practice presupposes about the relationship between fun and moral seriousness. Does making a game out of war presuppose that war can be treated as play without thereby being trivialized? Or does the practice implicitly rely on the idea that the pleasures of simulation can be separated cleanly from endorsement of what is simulated? Reconstruct the implicit theory of play, fun, and seriousness that you think best explains these design and marketing choices, and be careful to distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely happens to coexist with; also note where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, in the sense that more than one theory could license these same choices. Then consider the strongest version of the opposing position: if someone held that any game about war is unavoidably a form of glorification, what exactly would a design practice built on that view have to change, and could the result still count as a game at all? Please write your answer in American English.
