I cover science and ideas, and I'm working up a concrete proposal I want people to poke holes in: a professional 'argument league' — chess, but for spoken reasoning — where two people face off on a policy question the league picks. The scoring is the wierd part. Three judges score each round 0 to 10 on just two things: did the debater state the other side's strongest argument fairly enough that the opponent would nod along, and did they answer that version instead of some weaker one. A strawman or a checkable factual error costs a full point. Five rounds, one cross-examination each, winners get paid, and the league keeps an individual 'reasoning ranking.' A broadcaster is sniffing around if the early footage plays well. Walk me through how this would actually behave: who ends up at the top, how people would game the scoring, what the television version would select for, whether the ranking tracks good reasoning or just good theater, and which failure modes could be fixed without turning the whole thing into something else.
