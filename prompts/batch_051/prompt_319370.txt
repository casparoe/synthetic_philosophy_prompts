I have a standing policy about how I handle claims involving grit, fortitude, or resilience, and I apply it often enough that I should check whether its actually defensible. The policy has two settings. When someone explains a social outcome by pointing to grit or resilience as a main cause — why some kids get through college and others don't, why one team survives a bad stretch and another falls apart — I discount the explanation almost to zero. Last week this kicked in when a manager at work said our team missed its targets because the other team just had more resilience, and I found myself treating the explanation as noise before I'd even heard the details. I don't think persistence is unreal; i've seen people get better at hard things by not quitting and by changing how they practice. But when the words get attached to social outcomes, they seem to be doing moral work more than causal work, and the claim looks unfalsifiable from how things later go: if someone sticks it out they're resilient, if they leave they knew their limits, if they break they lacked resilience. Meanwhile, when the same person tells me a close-up story about a particular person practicing something hard over months, with failed attempts, changes of method, and a place where the improvement actually showed up, I take it at face value. My friend says this is incoherent — that the same underlying trait is being asserted in both cases and I'm letting the context chose my credence, or committing a genetic fallacy against the first kind of explanation. He may be right that the asymmetry needs a rationale I don't have. So: is there a defensible version of the two-setting policy, what should it say, and is there a principled rate at which I should discount the social-outcome explanations instead of zeroing them out? To be clear, I'm not asking about the prescriptive versions of the idea, the teach resilience stuff, though I'm suspicious there too — just the explanatory claim that grit or resilience was a main cause of some uneven outcome. And if the whole policy is just a bias protecting my politics, how would I tell from the inside?
