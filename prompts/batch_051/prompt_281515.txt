I keep running into this line in health policy circles and I want someone to take it apart slowly. A friend who works in cost-effectiveness is always saying QALYs are like a common currency for health: the system has a fixed budget, so allocating care is just spending that currency to buy the most health per dollar. From that he concludes a bunch of things at once -- that a $50k-per-QALY drug should be funded while a $2m-per-QALY one shouldn't, that its fine to prioritize a 20-year-old over a 90-year-old because the young person gets more life-years out of the same resources, and that formal triage rules that deprioritize someone whose disability lowers their expected QALY gain aren't discriminating, they're just not overpaying for a smaller health return. He's also weirdly vague about how the value of a statistical life fits in -- sometimes it sounds like its the same currency in a different denomination, sometimes like a second currency with its own exchange rate.

I keep feeling like the currency picture is doing more work than he admits, but I can't tell which parts are load-bearing. Walk me through it like an audit. First give me the correspondence table: in this analogy, what is the currency, the account or budget, the market, the exchange rate, the interest rate, and who is the spender? Then the disanalogies: where does treating QALYs as spendable currency break down, and which of those breakpoints actually threaten his conclusions, as opposed to just nuancing the metaphor? I especially want to know which features of money get quietly imported just by saying the word currency -- fungibility, neutrality about who holds it, a stable exchange rate between quality and length of life, the absence of a morally meaningful zero, the idea that the unit's value doesn't depend on who accrues it -- and whether the moral conclusions are secretly riding on those imports. Then the bottom line: once the disanalogies are on the table, do the three conclusions (fund the $50k one not the $2m one, prefer the young, treat disability-based deprioritization as neutral) survive? Or does the currency analogy fall apart exactly at the points where it was doing the philosophical work? And if there's a better analogy for what QALY-maximizing triage is actually doing, give me that at the end.
