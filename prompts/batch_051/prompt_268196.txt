I work as a senior auditor for a farm animal welfare certification body, and we're getting a surge of applications involving insects — black soldier fly larvae for feed, crickets for protein, mealworms in processing, plus incidental deaths from crop protection. The board keeps citing what they call the scale argument: even if the probability that an individual insect is sentient is very low, the numbers are so huge that the expected amount of suffering could still be enormous. But some of my collegues reply that the probability might be so low that the expected value is negligible, or that we don't even have a well-defined probability to plug in. I need a reusable decision procedure for my team, who are not philosophers, to decide when to treat insects as sentient for certification purposes. It should be a checklist or flowchart in words, usable in about an hour per case, with triggers for different levels of precaution. Include worked examples: one case where the procedure says we should restrict the practice even though it feels over-cautious, and one where it says we can approve it even though insects are being killed. Also flag which steps are judgment calls, not mechanical. Please respond in American English.
