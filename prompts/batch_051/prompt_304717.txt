I am a high school student helping to write a mock final for an upper-level high school philosophy elective. I want one closed-book essay question on John Stuart Mill's views about colonial rule, especially India. In his Considerations on Representative Government, in the chapter titled Of the Government of Dependencies by a Free State, Mill argues something along these lines: when a free and civilized state governs a dependency whose population he regards as too backward for representative institutions, it should not simply impose its own political forms. Despotic rule over such a dependency can be legitimate, he thinks, only if the end is the improvement of the ruled and the means actually bring about that improvement. The question should turn on this idea.

Please supply three things:

1. The essay question, aimed at a 50-minute closed-book final. It should not be answerable by summarizing Mill's position. It should force the student to test the improvement criterion, for example by asking how we could tell that colonial rule was improving the governed in a way that counts on Mill's own terms, and what would show that the criterion was being used as a cover for mere domination.

2. A model answer of about 500 words. It should apply Mill's criterion to British India, name the assumptions about progress and paternalism that do the work, and take a clear position on whether the criterion can do the evaluative job Mill wants it to do.

3. A marking scheme built around five must-hit points. Say how many marks each is worth, make the total 20, and after each point add a one-line note on how students typically get it wrong. No bibliography or further-reading suggestions; the materials should be usable with just the text and the student's reasoning.
