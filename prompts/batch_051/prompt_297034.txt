I keep changing my mind about which English forms count as "the same word," and I would like a single working criterion instead of ad hoc case-by-case judgments. Propose a definition of "same word" for English that lets someone sort each of the following pairs as one word or two, and where the dichotomy itself seems inadequate, say so and explain why. Test the definition against every pair below, mark the places where it gives a clean verdict and the places where it strains, and revise the definition once if that helps. The pairs: "cat" and "cats"; "go" and "went"; "good" and "better"; "sing" and "singer"; "happy" and "unhappy"; the verb "run" and the noun "run"; the financial "bank" and the river "bank"; "do not" and "don't"; "blackbird" and the phrase "black bird." Finally, identify the verdict you are least willing to stand behind, and say which case most exposes the limits of the definition you propose.
