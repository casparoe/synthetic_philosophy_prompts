I am drafting a statement of my views on humanitarian intervention and would like a consistency audit before showing it to anyone. Over the years I have settled into the following six positions, and I want to know whether they can be held together or whether I have been keeping them in separate mental boxes.

(1) Sovereignty is conditional, not absolute: a state's claim against outside military interference is conditioned on its protecting its own population, and it is at least prima facie forfeited when the state commits or permits genocide, ethnic cleansing, crimes against humanity, or large-scale war crimes.

(2) NATO's 1999 intervention in Kosovo was, on balance, justified even though it lacked Security Council authorization, because it was a last-resort response to an ongoing campaign of ethnic cleansing and further delay would have allowed the expulsions and killings to continue.

(3) The 2003 invasion of Iraq was unjustified, and the failure to find weapons of mass destruction is not the main reason. The decisive problem was that the coalition intended to remove a regime by force, and regime change by external force is not a purpose for which war may be used.

(4) The UN Charter's prohibition on the use of force is not a mere rule of thumb. When the Security Council is blocked by a veto, acting without its authorization is itself an intrinsic wrong against the international order — not merely a risk — and that wrong must count in any all-things-considered judgment of the act.

(5) I read the 2011 Libya intervention by the same standards: the initial authorization to protect civilians in Benghazi was legitimate, but the mission became illegitimate once the coalition's goal shifted from protecting civilians to removing Gaddafi as such.

(6) I also hold that the means/end distinction carries real moral weight here: intentionally overthrowing a regime as the goal of a campaign is morally worse than foreseeing that a campaign of civilian protection will weaken or even bring down that regime.

I am not asking for an argument for or against any of these views taken alone, but for an audit of their mutual consistency. Please (a) separate the actual tensions among (1)–(6) from the merely apparent ones, explaining which distinctions dissolve the apparent ones; (b) rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say precisely what would have to be revised, weakened, or given up to restore consistency; and (c) identify where my reasoning is most likely to fail — what argument, objection, or case detail would most plausibly lead me to change one of these commitments, and which one. At the very end, after the full discussion, give a fixed bottom line in the form \answer{...}: either \answer{consistent as stated} or a short phrase naming the commitment that must be revised first, in order of priority, e.g., \answer{revise (4) first}.
