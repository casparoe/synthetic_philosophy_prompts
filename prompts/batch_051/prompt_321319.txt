I'm a novelist. My agent has an offer on the table for the streaming rights to my last book, from a platform owned by a company that also does defense contracting and runs a data-broker business on the side. The money would buy me maybe four years of not teaching. And I can't figure out whether to sign. Whatever I do seems to run off into consequences I can't see the end of: if the show reaches people it might do some good, or it might get mangled into something I regret; if I turn it down, maybe someone else writes a worse version and nothing improves; maybe the royalties help a company that does real harm, or maybe at this scale any publisher or platform is tangled up in the same stuff and my one contract changes nothing; maybe the four years buy my next book, which is the real bet, or maybe that book never matters. My agent says I'm being precious and should just decide on the local facts: the contract terms, the money, the creative control, whether I actually want the show made. He says trying to trace every consequence to the heat death of the universe is just a way to never signing anything. Is he right? Is there a sensible way to make this call without pretending I can add up the whole causal chain, or is refusing to trace it just another guess? I want a way to think about it more than a yes or a no.
