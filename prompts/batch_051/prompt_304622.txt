I have found that I hold my long-standing views to a much higher standard of disconfirmation than my recent ones: the more I have invested in a belief—through reading, teaching, and defending it in writing—the stronger the counterevidence has to be before I will revise it. I defend this to myself by saying that the investment is a proxy for arguments I have since compressed or partly forgotten, so I am deferring to my past evidence, not to the sunk cost itself. Is this a defensible epistemic policy, or is it the sunk cost fallacy applied to beliefs, dressed up as trust in my past self? What would distinguish a version of the policy that preserves genuine information from a version that merely protects my prior commitments, and is there a rule I could actually follow that keeps the first without the second?
