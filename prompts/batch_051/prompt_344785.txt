I'm in a reading group that stumbled into the continuum hypothesis, and I've been surprised by how calm everyone is about a dispute that hasn't moved in half a century. The mathematics itself was settled long ago: everyone accepts that CH is independent of the standard axioms—you can add either CH or its negation to ZFC and get a consistent theory. Yet the camps never budge. On one side are people who think there is a unique intended universe of sets. For them the cumulative hierarchy is a definite object, the power set operation has a determinate meaning, and CH has a real truth value that our axioms are simply too weak to see. They spend their time hunting for new axioms—large cardinal principles, forcing axioms, and other candidate additions—that might settle CH by pinning down how thick the continuum really is. On the other side are people who think the independence result basically ends the matter: there is no privileged universe of sets, "set" is more like "group" or "geometry"—a concept instantiated by many equally good structures—so the only real question is which set theory is convenient or elegant, not which one is true.

What I can't tell is whether these two sides are even having the same argument. One person in my group, who leans pluralist, insists the whole thing is a verbal disagreement about the word "set." Another says that's a cop-out, and that the actual disagreement is over what counts as a legitimate reason to add an axiom in the first place—for example, whether "it decides more questions" is a genuine justification, as opposed to intrinsic plausibility or simplicity. They agree on every formal fact and still don't move. I want a diagnosis, not a verdict: is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, a difference about standards of justification, or two research programs answering different questions under one heading? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress—a consensus on how new axioms get justified, a formal result showing every natural extension of ZFC leaves CH undecided, a worked-out multiverse picture, or something else—or should we just expect it to stay permanantly stalled?
