I've been going back and forth with a friend about how strong Bayesian convergence actually is. The loose line people repeat is that once two rational people see enough of the same data, their priors "wash out" and they end up agreeing. But I know the precise results are more careful than that—Blackwell-Dubins style theorems, for example, are about their predictive distributions over the future merging, not about every hidden thing they have opinions about converging. What I don't have is a vivid, concrete case where predictions genuinely merge while a substantive posterior belief stays split. Can you construct one? I'm writing a short note about what convergence results do and don't license, so the case needs to generate the verdict rather than stipulate it.

Specs. Two observers, one shared stream of ordinary observations, and a substantive hypothesis H. (a) They genuinely start out assigning different one-step-ahead predictive probabilities, so the eventual agreement is produced by the data rather than built into the setup. (b) Both assign positive probability to the true sampling distribution, so the divergence isn't one of them just being wrong in a way that gurantees disagreement. (c) As the data pile up, their predictive probabilities for the next observation come together—total-variation distance going to zero, if you want to be precise—the way the convergence results should make you expect. (d) But their posterior probabilities for H do not wash out, and remain far apart even in the limit. H should be a real claim, something a future experiment or intervention could in principle distinguish, not a bare reparametrization of the likelihood. The scenario should feel like ordinary life (a small observational study, not a textbook urn), and fit in one paragraph.

Then walk me through it: state the models and priors precisely enough to check the verdicts, show why the predictive distributions merge, and show why H stays wherever the priors left it. Then tell me which single structural feature of the case is doing the work, what happens to the case if you remove that feature, and how a defender of "priors always wash out with enough data" would push back on your example.
