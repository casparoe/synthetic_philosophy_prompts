I am designing a short course on writing systems, and our discussions keep sliding into verdicts—whether English spelling is broken, whether Chinese characters "represent ideas"—before we have agreed on what a script would have to do to count as an adequate encoding of a language at all. Before I let the seminar compare theories, I want the specification of the explanatory target. Please write the desiderata that any adequate account of what a writing system encodes, and of how phonemic an alphabet is, must satisfy. For each requirement, give the concrete case or phenomenon that forces it. The list should at minimum contend with: the productivity of a finite signary and why an inventory the size of a syllabary or of a morphographic script such as Chinese does or does not scale; why abjads omit vowels that their language demonstrably has; why so-called deep orthographies persist and resist reform (English, Tibetan, French); what Hangul's featural letters show about units smaller than the phoneme, and what Japanese kana, read moraically, show about units other than the phoneme; morphologically transparent spellings such as a final obstruent written by its underlying voiced form despite devoicing (the German Rad/Rat contrast) or a single written plural marker pronounced differently by phonological context; and mixed systems such as Japanese kanji-plus-kana, where one script seems to divide labor between lexical and grammatical information. The list must also respect the distinctions among phone, phoneme, and morphophoneme, and say explicitly which of those distinctions a theory is not allowed to collapse.

Then assess the list itself. Mark each desideratum as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp (phonemic transparency, morphological transparency, visual distinctness, dialect neutrality, learnability, and the like). Tell me whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable—for example, whether full grapheme–phoneme transparency, morpheme constancy, and dialect neutrality can all be had at once, and if not, exactly why. Finally, score the phonemic-encoding view, the morphophonemic/lexical view, and the view that writing is an autonomous system rather than a transcript of speech against the requirements, but keep that part to a short paragraph per view; the criteria are what I am after, not the scorecard.
