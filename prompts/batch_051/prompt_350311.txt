I'm a PhD student in philosophy, and two people in my department got into it after our seminar on Du Bois's "Criteria of Negro Art." I'm still not sure which of them I agree with. The back-and-forth went roughly like this.

(1) Ramona said Du Bois is basically right that all art is propaganda, because every artwork either reinforces or quietly challenges some arrangement of power, even when it presents itself as apolitical. She said refusing to take a stance is itself a stance, so Du Bois is just being honest about what all art does.

(2) Jonah replied that if every artwork counts as propaganda, then the word has been stretched untill it does no evaluative work. Saying "this is propaganda" can't tell you whether an artwork is good or bad, and Du Bois plainly wanted to judge artworks, not just classify them. He also wanted truth and beauty, so his real criterion must be something narrower then propaganda.

(3) Ramona answered that the evaluative work comes from the content of the propaganda. For Du Bois, she said, good art is propaganda that tells the truth about black life and makes that life lovable rather than merely pitiable or respectable; bad art is propaganda that flatters the white gaze or hides reality. So the criterion isn't empty, it's just external to aesthetics proper.

(4) Jonah came back that this just relocates the problem: now you have to say which political effects count as emancipatory, and reasonable people will disagree about that. He also said Du Bois's own practice shows the criterion underdetermines things, since Du Bois chafed both at the demand for only respectable, uplifting images and at the demand for the grotesque, even though both kinds of work could serve somebody's political ends. So there has to be an unstated aesthetic-political standard that "propaganda" alone doesn't supply.

Adjudicate this move by move: for each of the four moves, say whether it's mostly right, partly right, or mistaken, and briefly justify each rating. Then say what, if anything, both of them are missing about Du Bois's position, and whether the two of them are even disagreeing about the same thing.
