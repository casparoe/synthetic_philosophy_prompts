So I just spent way too long arguing with a friend about Berkeley and we kept coming back to this one line in his *A Treatise Concerning the Principles of Human Knowledge*, where he says: "For can there be a nicer Strain of Abstraction than to distinguish the Existence of sensible Objects from their being perceived, so as to conceive them Existing unperceived?" My friend says that's just obviously outdated, because we can easily conceive of e.g. a Minecraft server keeping chunks loaded while no player is logged in—the world is still there, with its blocks and mobs and timers running, but nobody's perceiving it. I don't care about the practical question (obviously the server's RAM is doing *something*). What I want to know is whether Berkeley's challenge in that passage actually reaches a case like this. Is a game object a "sensible Object" in his sense, and does the server's code or state count as a spirit perceiving it, or as just more unthinking thing? If Berkeley would say the Minecraft case doesn't count as conceiving something existing unperceived, where exactly does his argument bite—what is it my friend is actually doing when he thinks he's conceiving it? And is there a reading of Berkeley on which my friend is just right and this is a genuine case of an unperceived sensible object?
