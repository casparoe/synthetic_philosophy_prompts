I'm putting together my political-obligation unit and could use a piece of satire to assign along side the serious readings. Write a deadpan FAQ page for a micro-nation that claims authority over everyone currently inside its borders, with the wrinkle that its borders are redrawn every night to include anyone who viewed its website that day. The voice should be a cheerfully helpful bureaucrat explaining, in plausible-sounding terms, why simply reading the FAQ counts as accepting its authority. The target should be the idea that political obligation can be generated by mere presence, passive enjoyment of benefits, or 'tacit' consent—and the jokes should turn on the real distinctions: obligation versus merely having reasons to comply, express versus tacit consent, fair play versus benefiting from a scheme you'd rather opt out of. It should be funny to someone who knows the consent and fair-play literature and readable to someone who doesn't. Keep it around 600 words, and dont let it break character to explain the philosophy.
