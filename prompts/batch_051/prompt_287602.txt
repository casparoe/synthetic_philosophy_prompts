My history and philosophy of science working group has two members who keep arguing about multiple independent discoveries. One defends a strong inevitabilist position: once the surrounding mathematics or natural history reaches a sufficient state, major discoveries such as the calculus or natural selection are robustly overdetermined, so the identity of the named discoverer is mostly a matter of being in the right place at a well-prepared moment. The other defends priority particularism: the route, timing, and detailed content of a discovery are highly sensitive to the particular people involved, and the bare fact that someone would eventually have found something similar does not show that credit and priority practices are arbitrary. The two have agreed to produce a joint adversarial-collaboration document, and they have asked me to coordinate. Please write that document for them.

The document should contain: (1) a numbered list of substantive claims both can sign, including at least one claim about what the Newton–Leibniz and Darwin–Wallace episodes actually establish and at least one about what they fail to establish; (2) a disagreements table that states each side's residual position in wording its own side would accept, not in wording the other side would reject; (3) a cruxes section identifying the sub-questions that, if settled, would actually move at least one side, with at least one concrete historical case or counterfactual test attached to each crux; and (4) a short assessment of which parts of the dispute are genuine rather than verbal, flagging anywhere the two sides use key terms such as "same discovery" or "inevitable" in different senses.

Pay particular attention to distinctions the document should keep in view: between the inevitability of the occurrence of a discovery, the inevitability of its timing, and the inevitability of its specific form or content; between a descriptive claim about overdetermination and a normative claim about the fairness or arbitrariness of credit; and between the Newton–Leibniz and Darwin–Wallace cases as instances of multiple discovery and as evidence for a general pattern. The collaborators want a document that someone outside the debate could follow, so any technical terms or historical references should be explained in a sentence rather than assumed.
