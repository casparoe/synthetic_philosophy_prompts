I keep coming back to the information-cascade model because it gives such a clean answer to something that usually gets treated as mere stupidity. A couple of people choose the same thing, the next person rationally reads the actions in front of them, and past a precise point your own private signal is mathematically outweighed — so the model derives banks runs, fads, and one empty restaurant next to one with a line from individually reasonable moves, with no appeal to panic or crowd psychology. And yet I can't shake the intuition that if I'm deciding between the two restaurants, my own information says the empty one is better, and I join the queue anyway, I'm being an epistemically spineless sheep, not a rational agent. The model says my gut is doing bad arithmetic; my gut says the model has dressed up conformity in the language of inference. I don't want a winner. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the part that goes, what it would take for the model to be the part that goes, and whether a line like 'individually rational but collectively ruinous' actually dissolves the clash or just renames it.
