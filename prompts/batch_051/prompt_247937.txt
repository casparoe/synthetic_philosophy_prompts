I run a small podcast where each episode is a pretend interview with a philosopher who's no longer around, and this month's guest is Elizabeth Anscombe. The whole episode is going to be about her attempt, in 1956, to stop Oxford from giving Harry Truman an honorary degree, and the argument she made in the pamphlet Mr. Truman's Degree that dropping the atomic bombs was murder. I don't want a biographcal tour or a set of softballs that just let her restate the view. Write me nine or ten questions that force her to actually operate the machinery of the position.

Specifically, I want questions that push on: (a) the combatant/noncombatant distinction she leans on, including what she would say about Hiroshima and Nagasaki containing war industries or command functions; (b) the intention/foresight distinction and double-effect reasoning, especially whether she can really maintain that the civilians' deaths were intended rather than merely foreseen when the bomb was aimed at a city center; (c) her flat claim that no consequences, however good, can turn the deliberate killing of innocents into anything but murder, with a follow-up ready for the answer she's most likely to give — that the good consequences weren't actually available anyway, because Japan was already effectively defeated; and (d) the more historical part of her argument where she suspects the real point was demonstrating the weapon to the Soviets rather than ending the war. I'd also like a question about what she thinks Truman should have done instead, and a closing question that makes her defend the absolutism itself: is there any set of circumstances in which she would not call the bombing murder, and if not, what does she say to someone who thinks the lives an invasion would have cost make an equally strong moral claim. For any question where she has a canned answer, write the follow-up that anticipates that answer and doesn't let the episode end there.
