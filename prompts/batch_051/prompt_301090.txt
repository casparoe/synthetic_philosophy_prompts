I've been going back and forth with a friend about what "optimizing" actually means, because we both use the word for decisions that feel pretty different. My criteria: a decision procedure is optimizing if (1) the person can say what they're maximizing and how each option scores on it, (2) they compare options against each other on that score rather than just against a pass/fail bar, (3) they wouldn't knowingly take an option when a previously seen alternative scores higher, and (4) their stopping rule is justified by some estimate of what continuing to search would cost and gain.

My other friend does this when apartment hunting. Before she starts, she picks three hard floors: commute under 25 minutes, rent under $1900, and south-facing windows. Then she visits places in whatever order they pop up and takes the first one that clears all three, even if the very next place might have been better. The wrinkle is that she didn't pick the floors at random: she made a spreadsheet that estimates how much a week of searching costs her and how quickly the average quality of available apartments improves, and she kept adjusting the floors untill the expected gain from another week of searching was roughly equal to the expected cost. So the bar is optimized; the individual choice just checks against the bar.

Is she optimizing or satisficing? Go through my four criteria honestly and say where they underdetermine the verdict. If she's not optimizing, is that because my criteria are too strict or mis-focused, or because she's genuinely making a different kind of choice — and does the difference matter for whether we should call her decision rational?
