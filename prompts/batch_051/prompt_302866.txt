I've been going around in circles on a variant of the presumptuous philosopher and I'd like a careful walk-through rather than the usual summary. Standard setup: T1 says the universe contains N observers, T2 says it contains 1000N, and the theories are otherwise empirically equivalent. Self-indication says favor T2 by a factor of 1000, since more observers means more slots for you; self-sampling says that washes out, because under T2 the probability of being any given slot is 1000 times smaller. I can follow that part.

Here's my variant. T2's extra 999N observers are all in the future. The two theories agree that exactly N observers have existed up to and including your birth (you are one of them), but T1 says the species ends fairly soon after, while T2 says the species continues and eventually produces another 999N. You know you are roughly the r-th observer. Now:

(a) Does self-indication still favor T2 by the full factor of 1000, or does that advantage disappear once the evidence is 'I am the r-th observer with this particular life' rather than the bare 'I exist'? Work through the likelihoods, and say what the answer hinges on.

(b) Does self-sampling now favor T1 by a factor of 1000 rather than staying neutral? In the standard case it has nothing to grab, but here my rank is the same under both theories and only the future total differs, so my position looks much more typical under the smaller theory. Is that right, or can the reference class be set up in a principled way to block it?

(c) If (b) is right, does the standard no-update verdict for self-sampling depend on quietly hidding your birth rank from yourself? And is there a stable position in the neighborhood of self-sampling that doesn't immediately lead to the doomsday conclusion that a long human future is improbable? I don't want the presumptuous philosopher's swagger, but I also don't want to end up punishing any theory that predicts a long future.
