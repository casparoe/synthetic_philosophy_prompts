I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am writing an essay about Daniel Dennett's ladder of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. My working question is whether this is best read as a taxonomy of four genuinely distinct kinds of mind, or as a continuum with only one or two principled breakpoints; I would like your help arriving at an appraisal I can defend in print.

Here is the version of the ladder I am working with. The shared engine is generate-and-test, and each rung moves the testing of candidates into a faster or cheaper arena. Darwinian creatures can test only via natural selection, which discards whole lineages across generations. Skinnerian creatures add a within-lifetime loop in which individual behaviors are selected or extinguished by environmental feedback. Popperian creatures add an inner loop: candidate behaviors can be prescreened offline, so that a bad hypothesis is discarded before it is acted out. Gregorian creatures add culturally inherited thinking tools—words, numerals, diagrams, notational systems, explicit rules—which create new virtual environments in which hypotheses can be articulated, compared, and shared.

Please act as a philosopher of cognitive science and work through this carefully rather than defaulting to a textbook progress story. Before settling on a final ranking, brainstorm at least two alternative ways of carving the hierarchy—for example, a version on which the biggest breakpoint falls between Skinnerian and Popperian creatures, and a version on which the middle two rungs collapse into a difference of degree. Then rank the three transitions—Darwinian to Skinnerian, Skinnerian to Popperian, and Popperian to Gregorian—on a four-level scale in which S marks a genuinely new kind of mind, A a major but continuous change, B a minor change, and C a mostly cosmetic relabeling; give one sentence of justification for each placement. Finally, allocate 100 points among the four creature types according to how much independent explanatory work each does in accounting for distinctively human cognition, over and above what the earlier types already provide; the points must sum to 100 and a type may receive zero. End with a one-sentence verdict on whether the tower contains four real kinds, two real breakpoints, or something else.
