Here's a scheme a friend of mine is trying to convince his professional society to adopt, to cut down on free-riding. Once a year every member has to have their net worth independently assessed, then destroy property they own that's worth at least 5% of it, in front of the assembled membership. The thing has to be lawfully theirs, can't be insured in a way that pays out for the destruction, can't be something they secretly wanted to get rid of or rebuild anyway, and three randomly chosen members have to attest to both the value and the destruction. A member's lifetime destroyed value is public and gives them proportionally more voting weight in the society. I told him this sounds like a costly-signaling arms race, and he said yeah, that's the point. Walk me through how this actually plays out: who ends up dominating, what the membership looks like after a decade, where the first cheats and hacks appear, and whether a handicap principle works when the cost is paid in wealth rather than effort or risk. Dont just say "people will cheat" — give me the specific strategies and the second-order effects.
