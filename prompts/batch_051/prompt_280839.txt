I am writing the section of a longer project in democratic theory where I have to state my own position on referendums, and I want a consistency audit before I build an argument on it. My current commitments are:

1. The legitimacy of a democratic decision rests on whether the procedure treated citizens as political equals, not on whether the decision-making was well-informed or whether the outcome is, on the merits, good policy.

2. The Brexit referendum was a legitimate exercise of collective self-government. Although I would have voted Remain, simply overturning or ignoring the result after the fact would have been a kind of anti-democratic paternalism.

3. California's ballot-proposition system is, on balance, a failure as an instrument of government: it allows well-financed interests to draft confusing measures, and it asks voters to legislate without the information, deliberation, or capacity for amendment that a legislature provides.

4. Switzerland's highly frequent use of referendums is broadly healthy and shows that direct democracy can be stable and competent under the right institutional conditions.

5. Courts are right to strike down referendum outcomes that would violate entrenched individual rights, even when a clear majority supported the outcome.

I suspect that some of these are in genuine tension and others only appear to be, but I cannot see clearly which is which from inside the position. Please audit the set. For each tension, state how severe it is, what would have to be revised to resolve it, and what that revision would cost; then say which single change would buy the most coherence overall. Rank the tensions by severity, and do not water the verdict down: if some commitment must be abandoned, name it.
