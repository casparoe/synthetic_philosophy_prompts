Here's an argument a student gave in my bounded-rationality seminar and I can't find the weak spot. (1) A satisficer is just someone with an aspiration threshold: she keeps searching until an option meets that threshold, then stops. (2) For any stopping behavior, we can always construct a utility function plus search costs such that continuing past an acceptable option has lower expected utility than stopping. (3) So every satisficing choice can be redescribed as expected-utility maximization. (4) If it can't be redescribed that way, then the satisficer stops for no reason at all, and stopping for no reason is irrational. (5) So satisficing is either just optimizing in disguise or it's irrational. (6) But the whole point of satisficing was to be a geniune, rational alternative to optimizing, so the view collapses. Which step would you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd like to keep (1), since it's the standard definition, but if that's the real problem, say so.
