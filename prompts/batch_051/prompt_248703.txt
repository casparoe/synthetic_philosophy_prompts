One thing I've never been able to get strait in metaethics is which fight I should actually be tracking. Suppose I've got about a year of evenings to spend reading and arguing, and the goal is to come out with my credence in moral realism moved for defensible reasons, not a survey. Rank the following open sub-debates by how much the rest of the moral realism/anti-realism debate depends on their answer, and tell me which two I should ignore even though they're the famous ones: (a) whether evolutionary explanations of moral judgment debunk realism or just debunk a strawman version of it; (b) whether expressivism about 'ought' can actually solve the problems it was invented for, or collapses into realism-with-extra-steps; (c) whether epistemic reasons sink or swim together with moral reasons, so anti-realism about the latter drags down the former; (d) the queerness/supervenience problem — what objective values are made of and how they could explain anything; (e) the old 'why be moral' question and whether anti-realists have ever given a non-moral answer. Defend the ordering with dependency claims like 'settling X makes Y tractable' or 'Y is downstream of X', say how tractable each actually is for someone doing arguments rather than data, and what would have to happen over the next couple of years to make you swap the top two.
