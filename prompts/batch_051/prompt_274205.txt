For some time I have defended what I will call a Fearon-style account of AI races. In my view, the rationalist framework that James Fearon developed to explain why states go to war despite a shared interest in avoiding it transfers surprisingly well to competitions in advanced AI development. The central drivers of such races, I claim, are not primarily irrationality, nationalist emotion, or bureaucratic accident, but three features familiar from the study of interstate war: information asymmetries, commitment problems, and barriers to divisible bargaining. Of these I have come to think the commitment problem carries most of the weight. A major lab or state may sincerely prefer a slower, safer trajectory to an accelerating race; but it cannot credibly commit to refrain from exploiting any advantage it later acquires, and its rivals know this. The result is a spiral that no party prefers ex ante, yet each has reason to continue.

I want you to produce a systematic account of what would count against this position. Do not merely list external criticisms; tell me what would have to be true for the Fearon-style framework to be the wrong, or a seriously incomplete, explanation of AI races. Which assumptions of the rationalist model are doing real work, and what would their failure look like in the AI case—for example, if the actors are not the kind of unified strategic agents the model presumes, or if the gains from cooperation are structured so that commitments turn out to be credible after all, or if the decisive uncertainty concerns technical facts rather than one another's dispositions and capabilities? Distinguish considerations that would be fatal from those that would merely force me to qualify the view. Where the position makes genuinely falsifiable claims, identify them; where it has been insulated by after-the-fact redescription, say so and tell me whether that insulation is a defect or an acceptable feature of a philosophical model of this kind. I would be especially interested in what a defender of the view should themselves concede as counting against it, rather than what a critic would merely allege from outside the framework.
