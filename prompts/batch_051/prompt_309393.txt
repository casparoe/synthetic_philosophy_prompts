Me and a mate were out last night and got into a long argument about petitionary prayer, and it's still bugging me. He's a fairly serious Christian and said he's been praying that his sister's visa comes through. I asked why: if God already knows she needs it, already decided whether she'll get it, and always does whats best for her, then nothing about the asking can add anything. He said he wasn't trying to change God's mind. Fine. But then he went straight back to talking as if specific prayers for specific outcomes matter. In any other part of life I'd call a request pointless if the person already knows, has already made up their mind, and never changes course. You don't write to the rain asking it to stop. So is there a best justification for that asymmetry — petitioning someone who, by your own account, can't be moved or surprised — or do you have to give up one side? If prayer's only meant to change the person praying, not the outcome, then why does so much of it look exactly like asking for things?
