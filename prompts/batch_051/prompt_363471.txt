That makes it look like the feeling of being one subject is itself just another bit of storytelling produced by the language system, and that unity is something the brain narrates rather than something it detects. And if the unity of consciousness is a confabulation in the split-brain case, why assume it's any more real in the intact case, where the hemispheres simply have better bandwidth?

But I can't shake the intuition that there's a single something-it's-like to be me. When I say "I," I don't feel like I'm referring to a committee or a useful fiction. The theory seems to tell me that this intuition is exactly the sort of thing that would be manufactured whether or not it was true, which makes me suspicious of the theory and of myself at the same time.
