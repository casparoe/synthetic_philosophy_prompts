I am a staff engineer at a company that has spent the last year transitioning to a workflow in which large language models write most of our production code. Engineers now draft natural-language specifications and tests, review machine-generated diffs, and patch or regenerate outputs rather than authoring code line by line. This has made me unsure whether our long-standing debates about programming languages—static versus dynamic typing, expressiveness, readability, tooling—still track anything real. Please write an essay on whether programming languages still matter when machines write the code. Weigh the strongest version of the claim that they have become an implementation detail, a target format no human needs to read, against the claim that they remain the medium through which correctness, maintainability, and reasoning about systems get specified and checked, and that a language's semantics—its type system, ownership rules, purity, or effect tracking—still causally shapes what a machine can reliably generate. Reach a conclusion and defend it.
