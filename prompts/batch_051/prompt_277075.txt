Okay so I'm an undergrad taking a comparative secularism course — we cover England, Germany, France, and the US — and I've also ended up as the intern at a consultancy that's opening small offices in London, Berlin, Paris, and Chicago. HR somehow decided that since I'm the one in the religion course, I should draft the "global religion-in-the-workplace guidance": a one-page checklist for local office managers. Whenever an employee or a team proposes something touching religion — a prayer room, a faith group wanting a weekly conference-room slot, dietary changes in the canteen, shift swaps for a holy day, a chaplain at a staff retreat — the manager runs the checklist and gets a yes, a no, or a "this needs a person."

The reason I don't want this to be a country summary is that the class has been arguing these four aren't just points on a single "how secular" scale. England has an established church with the monarch as its supreme governor, so entanglement with one denomination is the baseline, not the bug. Germany has what we keep calling cooperative separation: the state collects a church tax from members of registered religious bodies and hands it over, so religion ends up looking almost like a piece of public infrastructure. France runs laïcité, an assertive secularism that wants religion kept out of the state's face and, in practice, out of a lot of public institutions. And the US has seperation plus a strong accommodation tradition, so the recurring question is whether a policy endorses religion or just lets private people practice it. A quiet prayer room can read as neutral accommodation in Chicago and as an unwanted entanglement in Paris.

So the deliverable I want is a decision procedure: a workflow in words. The questions in order; wich questions every country has to ask and which branch by country; what counts as a good-enough answer to each; where the procedure stops with "no"; where it says "yes, but only with these changes"; and where it says "this is a values call, escalate to a human." It has to be runnable by a non-lawyer, non-philosophy office manager in about fifteen minutes, maybe once a month, and the stakes are real — we've already had a complaint and a near-lawsuit in the US over a Bible study in the break room.

Then run the procedure on the three proposals actually sitting in my inbox:

1. A weekly Wednesday lunchtime Christian staff group gets the main conference room; no other faith or non-faith group has asked for a slot.
2. In Berlin, payroll will automatically deduct the church tax from any employee who declares a religion, and — for "consistency" — HR wants every office to start asking employees their religion on the onboarding form.
3. A quiet meditation/prayer room in each office, open to anyone, no symbols, first come first served.

I want the procedure to force us to see where the four offices will have to run different policies even though HR wants one global rule, and I want it to be honest about which steps are actual constraints from the country's church-state settlement and which steps are just my own values in disguise as a checklist. If the procedure tells us our pet global policy is bad, tell me anyway.
