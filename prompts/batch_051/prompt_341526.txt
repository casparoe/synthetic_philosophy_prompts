Me and a few people have been running a reading group on forbidden colors and whether a brain interface could ever produce genuinely new qualia, not just a weird recombination of colors we already have but a new quality, like a color nobody has ever seen. We've got two camps that have argued past eachother for a year, and we agreed to do an adversarial collaboration. One side, call them the recombiners, says any experience you can produce by driving a human brain has to be built out of the phenomenal resources that brain already has, so the most you could ever get is something like the old stabilized-image reports of reddish-green or bluish-yellow, and those only count if they are not just artifacts. The other side, call them the expansionists, says that is a failure of imagination plus a slide from current wiring to possible wiring: a new input channel, with the right interface and training, could add a phenomenal dimension that is not a blend or extension of any existing one, and insisting otherwise confuses reportability with possibility. I want the document the collaboration should have produced. Start with the claims both could sign, about opponent processing, cortical plasticity, sensory substitution, and what the current evidence does and does not show. Then give the exact remaining disagreement, worded so each side would accept the phrasing of its own view. Then the cruxes. I suspect one is whether a genuinely new channel, like giving a normal adult a fourth cone type, would produce judgments that cannot fit the old color space, and another is whether any verbal report could ever distinguish new quality from new discrimination. For each crux, give a concrete case or experiment both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Also say plainly if part of the fight is verbal, especially if novel is being used in different senses. No headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just continuous prose like you are writing them a letter.
