Someone in our philosophy department has proposed a rule for our weekly research-in-progress workshop. Every claim of fact made in a talk would have to carry a tag stating which of Peirce's four methods fixed the speaker's belief in it: tenacity (held because you've refused to consider alternatives), authority (held because some institution or text says so), a priori (held because it strikes you as reasonable), or the method of science (held because its been tested against experience and you'd drop it if experience went the other way). Unlabelled claims couldn't be criticised, and only science-tagged claims would count when the workshop writes its collective summary of where the field stands. Treat this as a mechanism, not a moral verdict: what does it incentivise speakers to do, where does the tag system get gamed first, what second-order effects does it have on which topics get discussed, and does it select for genuine adequacy to experience or just for performances of scientific method? Say how the fallibilist point that even science-tagged claims stay revisable interacts with the scheme. Can the failures be fixed without gutting it?
