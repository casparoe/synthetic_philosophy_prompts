One of my friends has gone deep down a Szasz rabbit hole over the last year and keeps insisting that most of what psychiatrists call mental illness isn't disease, its problems in living. I've been keeping track of her actual verdicts on individual cases because I want to know whether shes got a stable criterion or is just pattern-matching. The track record so far:

- Alzheimer's: she says disease, full stop. There's clear neuropathology.
- General paresis, the late-stage syphilis thing: disease. An identifiable infection eating the brain.
- Epilepsy: disease. The brain is doing something straightforwardly physically wrong even when there's no single clean lesion you can point to.
- ADHD: not a disease. She says it's mostly a mismatch between a certain kind of kid and what schools and offices demand.
- After a close friend's father died and the friend couldn't sleep or eat properly for a few weeks: she says that's grief doing what grief does, not depression-the-disease.
- Opioid addiction: not a disease. She says it's a bad solution to other problems, closer to a lifestyle spiral than to a seizure.

Now there's a new case. A 19-year-old has gone over about eight months from being reasonably social and passing his classes to hearing voices most days, barely leaving his room, and failing everything. All the scans and bloodwork come back clean. The psych team want to call it schizophrenia or schizophreniform.

If my friend wants to stay consistent with her own track record, what should she say about this new case? Which of her earlier cases is the closest analog, and what principle is she committing to if she goes either way? If her earlier verdicts genuinely underdetermine the new one, say so, and tell me what additional case would actually settle which rule she's been using.
