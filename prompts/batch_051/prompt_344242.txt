I've been noticing this pattern lately and I can't tell if it's one thing or two, and I don't know what to call it. Where I grew up there's a midsummer festival everyone treats as an ancient national custom—people call it 'our heritage' and get genuinely moved by it. By chance I read a local history that showed the version everyone now celebrates was assembled in 1908 by a regional tourism committee, who stitched together bits of older village practices, made up a few ritual details, and advertised it as a revival of a pagan past. The older village practices were real, but the national meaning people now attach to them isn't. What's more, people point to that festival as proof the nation itself is more than a thousand years old, which on the face of it seems like a bait-and-switch. My question is whether that kind of process has a specific name, and whether it's the same phenomenon as something else I keep seeing: people talking about the nation as if it's a big extended family they have loyalties to, even though they'll never meet more than a tiny fraction of it. Are these two seperate things that just travel together, or two sides of one thing? If you answer with a label, please don't stop there—tell me what exactly falls in the bucket and what stays out. For instance, at what point does a genuinely old local practice become one of these, and does it matter whether the people participating are sincere?
