I've been working out what markets actually tell us, and I've arrived at something I call the Error-Settlement Account of prices. The idea is this: when two people trade an asset purely as a bet on its future direction, the trade just is a disagreement about the future, and at most one of their directional beliefs can turn out to have been right. So every purely speculative trade involves at least one party acting on a mistaken belief. From there I've drawn the stronger conclusion that a market price is not an aggregation of knowledge but a running average of private errors that happen to point in opposite directions, and that liquid markets are evidence of abundant error rather than of abundant information. I realize this cuts against the usual view that prices reflect information. Is this a sound principle? Does something like it already exist as a theorem, and if so under what name? Give me the strongest objections and tell me which, if any, are fatal rather than merely limiting.
