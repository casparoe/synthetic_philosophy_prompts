I'm working through Dharmakīrti for a seminar on Buddhist epistemology and I keep tripping over the same point. As I understand him, he allows only two instruments of knowledge (pramāṇas): perception, which is non-conceptual and takes as its object the unique momentary particular (svalakṣaṇa), and inference, which is conceptual and deals in generalities (sāmānyalakṣaṇa). Those generalities are, on his account, built through exclusion (apoha), so they aren't ultimately real; only the momentary particular is ultimately real.

So heres what I can't shake: if inference never gets at the particular, and the particular is all there really is, why call inference a pramāṇa at all, rather than a useful false picture? The more Dharmakīrti pushes the privilege of perception and momentariness, the more he makes inference look like a lie that happens to work. I keep feeling like he can have the ontology or he can have the epistemology, but not both. Is there a way to read him where inference is genuinely knowledge of the world and not just a stand-in, or am I missing something basic?
