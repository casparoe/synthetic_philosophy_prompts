I'm the grad student rep on our IRB and we've got a weird one. A behavioral science lab wants to run a study with a phone app that passively pulls participants' location, sleep, and social media data to guess when they're stressed or depleted, then fires off just-in-time messages urging them to cut back on drinking. Participants consent to the tracking and to receiving wellness messages, but they're not told the messages are timed to hit when there most persuadable. The messages themselves are factually accurate and the aim is benign. Half the board thinks this is just smart persuasion—true claims, decent consent—and half thinks surgical timing that exploits low self-control is manipulation, because it routes around people's reasoning rather than engaging it. Draft the decision memo I should bring to the board: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a clear bottom line, and do the strongest point on the losing side honestly. Please separate what's actually settled in the manipulation-vs-persuasion literature from what's genuinely contested, and don't present a contested view as the consensus. American English, and no bibliography or citations—just the reasoning.
