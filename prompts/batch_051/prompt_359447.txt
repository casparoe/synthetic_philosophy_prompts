I teach philosophy of law and I keep hitting a wall when I try to justify a judgment I actually hold, so I want you to work through it with me properly. You are a philosopher of criminal law with a low tolerance for boilerplate. Start by brainstorming three or four candidate justifications, then defend the strongest one, then stress-test it.

Imagine two crimes, identical at the time they were committed: same harm, same planning, same state of mind, same offender age, same evidence. One offender is caught within months and punished at full severity. The other is identified only thirty years later, and in between he has lived a completely law-abiding life. At the moment of the crime the two offenders were equally deserving, equally dangerous, and equally plausible targets of deterrence. Yet nearly everyone treats the old crime as less punishable, and the law encodes this with limitations periods, at least for most offenses. The passage of time alone, plus a clean record that is partly just the passage of time, seems to be doing the moral work.

Here is what I want from you. Either this time-sensitivity is defensible or it isnt. If you defend it, name the exact feature that does the justifying and then test it against the following case: the old crime was a brutal murder, the victims family has waited thirty years to see the person caught, and the offender before being caught lived comfortably on the proceeds of the crime. Tell me whether that changes your view, and why. If instead you think the asymmetry cannot be fully defended, tell me what a more consistent sentencing or limitations regime would look like, and what it would lose. At some point, address whether the moral relevance of time tracks changes in the offender, changes in what punishment expresses, or changes in the states standing to punish after delay, and say which, if any, actually carries the weight. And note that many legal systems exempt murder from limitations periods altogether, so think about whether that carve-out is itself justified or just another piece of the inconsistency.
