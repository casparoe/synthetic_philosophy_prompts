ok so I've more or less landed on this: hindsight bias is not really about individuals being sloppy with probabilities. its a structural feature of narrative explanation. the minute you organize events into a story that ends in the thing you're explaining, you select for antecedents that point at the outcome, you quietly drop the dead ends, and the outcome starts to look like it was coming all along. histories of 1914, of the 2008 crash, of company collapses — read them and everything looks overdetermined in a way it couldnt have looked at the time. so the so-called narrative fallacy is a real fallacy of explanation, and hindsight bias is just the individual-level symptom of it.

what I want is the case against that view, laid out straight. not the part about hindsight bias being well documented — I already grant that. I want what would show that hindsight bias is a manageable quirk of individual judgment rather than a structural flaw in narrative explanation, or that narrative isnt really the culprit. for instance: are there results where knowing the outcome actually improves causal judgment or calibration, not just confidence? does the adaptive story (hindsight bias as a mostly rational model-updating process that sometimes overcorrects) undercut calling it a fallacy? do counterfactual historians who write explicit what-if branches actually avoid the inevitability effect, and if they do, does that show the problem is a particular genre rather than hindsight itself? what would a narrative history that preserves genuine contingency have to look like, and do any exist, or do they all smuggle inevitability back in through verbs and periodization? and is the fact that historians constantly flag contingency evidence against me, or just a hedge that leaves the narrative machinery untouched?

rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. say which ones a defender of my view would themselves concede count against it even if they think they can absorb them, and which theyd dismiss as attacking a strawman. be honest about whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability — if every contingency-preserving narrative counts as one that did the thing right, is my view now compatible with anything? and tell me where you'd land. also: what does my view predict that the rival view (hindsight is a minor bias, not a structural one) doesnt — predictions I could actually look for, even if nobodys tested them.
