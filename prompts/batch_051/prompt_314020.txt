ive been chewing on Rawls's treatment of envy and i cant get it to sit right with me. the theory side: in a well-ordered society where inequalities are arranged to benefit the least advantaged, envy mostly shouldnt arise or at least shoudn't be socially necessary, and envy proper is a vice because it wants the other person's good to be less even when that wouldn't make anyone better off. thats a clean, systematic story — it treats envy as a passion a just society can shrink by being visibly fair, rather than as a political tool.

the intuition side: when somebody near the bottom of a deeply unjust society resents the rich, i cant bring myself to call that a vice or a character defect to be managed away. it looks more like a signal that the inequality is not justified and not to their benefit — like the resentment is seeing something real. and i worry that calling this 'envy' lumps a moral response to injustice together with just wanting what someone else has. i dont want a theory that pathologizes exactly the people it claims to help.

so my question is which should yield. is Rawls already making room for this with his idea of excusable envy, or is that still subtly treating the resentment as a defect rather than as a cognitive signal? is there a reading on which the theory and the intuition are compatible, or do i have to either drop the intuition or amend the theory so that some kinds of envy count as fitting responses to injustice rather than vices? and what would justify each of those moves — what would count as a reason to debunk the intuition, and what would count as a reason to amend the theory?
