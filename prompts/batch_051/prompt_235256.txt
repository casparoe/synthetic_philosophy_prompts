Analyze Kitzmiller v. Dover Area School District, the 2005 Pennsylvania trial in which a U.S. federal judge ruled that a school board policy requiring ninth-grade biology students to hear intelligent design described as an alternative to evolution was unconstitutional, and that intelligent design is not science. Use Dennett's distinction between cranes and skyhooks, together with his associated warning against greedy reductionism, as the analytical lens. By cranes, understand stepwise, bottom-up mechanisms that can build complex structures without an unexplained leap to design; by skyhooks, understand explanatory devices that do their work from above by positing a special, non-mechanistic source of order. Greedy reductionism is the overreach of explaining away higher levels of organization rather than merely explaining them.

I want a disciplined mapping in both directions. First, apply the framework to the case: what in the intelligent-design side's central arguments, especially the appeal to irreducible complexity, functions as a skyhook rather than a crane; where the evolution side's explanations operate as cranes; whether either side actually courts greedy reductionism or only makes legitimate reductionist moves; and whether the judge's finding that intelligent design is not science is best read as a rejection of skyhooks in scientific explanation, or as a legal demarcation that does not map neatly onto Dennett's categories. Second, turn the lens around and say what the episode shows about the crane/skyhook distinction itself: which features of the dispute it captures cleanly, which it misdescribes or ignores, and what to make of the fact that one can accept all the Darwinian cranes while holding theistic metaphysical commitments. Keep settled and contested matters clearly separated throughout, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus.
