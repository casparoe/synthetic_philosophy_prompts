I'm a novelist, and I've talked myself into a corner while working on a character, and I could really use someone who does epistemology professionally. The character is a woman whose daughter has been missing for eleven years. Everyone else has quietly moved on; she hasn't. She isn't delusional — she can recite the base rates, she knows what the detective told her — but she keeps hoping, and keeps searching. I wanted her hope to read as a moral strength, not self-deception. Then a friend in a philosphy department showed me an argument, and now I'm seccond-guessing the whole book.

Here's the argument, numbered the way he gave it to me:

(1) A belief is rational only if it's proportioned to the evidence: you shouldn't be more confident than the evidence warrants.

(2) Hope involves more confidence in a good outcome than the evidence warrants. If your confidence just matched the evidence, it wouldn't be hope, it would be flat expectation.

(3) So hope is always a failure of epistemic rationality — wishful thinking with better branding.

(4) But we admire hope. The mother who keeps looking, the patient who stays warm through a grim prognosis, the activist who keeps organizing when the polls are hopeless: those people seem morally better, not worse, than the ones who calmly update and move on.

(5) And a disposition that systematically misreads reality can't be a moral virtue. So if hope is an epistemic vice, it can't be morally admirable.

(6) Therefore either hope isn't a virtue after all, or one of the premises is false. But (1) and (4) both feel unshakeable, and (2) is, um, my protagonist.

So where does this go wrong, if it goes wrong? I'd most like to keep (1) and (4); I'd rather surrender (2) or (5), but I don't know what surrendering them costs. Put on your epistemologist-of-record hat and think carefully before answering — maybe start by brainstorming all the things "hope" could mean, then give me your best diagnosis. I'm not asking for a reading list or citations, just the actual argument work. Which step breaks, and what do I have to give up if I break it? If I reject (2), what's left of hope?
