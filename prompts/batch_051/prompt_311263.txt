Write a villanelle in the voice of a would-be state — think Somaliland — that has the usual trappings of statehood (population, territory, a working government, the ability to deal with other states) but still hasn't been recognized. The two refrains should be the declaratory and constitutive positions on recognition: one line should have the state insist that it already is a state and recognition would only confirm what's already true, and the other should have it concede that maybe it isn't a state until other states say so. Let the villanelle structure do the work: the refrains alternate across the stanzas the way these two positions keep talking past eachother, and they should finally land side by side in the last couplet, where the poem should show whether the standoff can ever resolve or whether each view is answering a different question. Don't name the theories in the poem. Hard limit: 19 lines, plus at most a one-sentence gloss if you want.
