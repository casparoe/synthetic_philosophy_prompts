Some freegans are strict vegans at the register: they will not buy meat, milk, or eggs, because paying for them funds animal farming. Yet many of the same people will eat animal products when no purchase is involved — a block of cheese recovered from a supermarket dumpster, or eggs from rescued hens kept at a sanctuary, eggs that would otherwise rot or be thrown away. The practice seems to treat these foods as clean: the hen is not being exploited by the person who eats her egg, and no demand is sent back to any industry. What theory of the wrongness of animal use does this practice presuppose? Does it strictly require a consequentialist picture on which the wrong lies entirely in the harms one causes or the demand one creates, so that an already-existing byproduct is harmless to use? Or can rights-based and virtue-based views also license it, once using an animal's body is distinguished from being complicit in the system that produced it? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say where the practice is underdetermined between rival theories, and say what a freegan who held that consuming the product is itself wrong would have to change. Also tell me which argument or consideration would most likely change your verdict, and where your reasoning is most fragile.
