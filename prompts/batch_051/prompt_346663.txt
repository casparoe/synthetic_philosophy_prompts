A colleague in climate policy keeps telling me that an ensemble of climate models is basically a committee of experts: where the models roughly agree, the prediction is robust and you should be confident, and where they spread out, that spread is your uncertainty. I'm a philosopher of science, and I have a nagging sense that the analogy is doing real work there, but I can't tell whether that work is legitimate. How far does the analogy actually hold? Spell out the mapping — what is supposed to correspond to the experts, to their independence, to their track records — then say where it breaks, and tell me whether the inference it's used to license, that the spread of an ensemble is a measure of our uncertainty, survives once the disanalogies are on the table.
