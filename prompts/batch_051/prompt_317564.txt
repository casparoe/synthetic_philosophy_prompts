Write the joint statement that a natural-rights intellectual-property theorist and a commons advocate who holds that ideas cannot be owned could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The two camps take as their shared textual starting point Thomas Jefferson's 1813 letter to Isaac McPherson, specifically the passage in which he argues that ideas are less susceptible than anything else to exclusive property: the moment an idea is divulged, it forces itself into everyone's possession, the receiver cannot dispossess himself of it, and no one possesses the less because every other possesses the whole; receiving an idea resembles lighting one taper from another without darkening the first; and inventions therefore cannot in nature be subjects of property, though society may grant exclusive rights to their profits as an encouragement. They have agreed that the familiar idea/expression distinction cannot simply be assumed; if they invoke it, they must show whether and how it follows from or limits Jefferson's claims.

Produce the document this collaboration would yield. It must include a numbered agreed-claims section, followed by a disagreements table. The agreed claims should cover what Jefferson's argument does and does not establish, what follows from the non-rivalrous character of information, and the difference between owning an idea and holding a socially granted right to exclude others from using it. The disagreements table should state each residual disagreement in neutral wording and then give each side's own formulation, phrased so that its own side would accept it. After the table, identify the cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side, and for each crux provide a concrete case or argument that both sides accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Use cases specific enough to be discriminating: one should concern an independent inventor who reverse-engineers a patented machine from a purchased artifact, and another should concern a text-generating model trained on a corpus of copyrighted works without retaining any particular work. Flag any part of the dispute that is purely verbal. Treat Jefferson's argument as a live claim to be tested rather than as an authority to cite.
