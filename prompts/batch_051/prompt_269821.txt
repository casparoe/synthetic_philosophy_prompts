I'm a software engineer, and I spend enough time trusting random samples to stand in for a whole population that jury duty last month got under my skin. We pick jurors by lottery and treat the randomness as a feature: it's suposed to stop anyone from stacking the box and to make the jury a fair cross-section of the community. But if you propose a lottocracy — a legislature picked by random draw — most people, me included, think you're joking, even though it seems to run on the same fairness logic. So the asymetry is: two small groups making decisions for the rest of us, and we treat sortition as obviously fine for one and absurd for the other. Is there a real difference between the jobs, like deciding whether one defendant meets a legal standard versus writing general laws, that justifies the split? Give me the strongest principle you can find that treats them differently, then tell me honestly whether it actually works or whether we should take lottocracy more seriously.
