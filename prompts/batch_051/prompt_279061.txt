I've been going in circles on the absent-minded driver problem all week. The setup as I learned it: a driver will pass two identical exits, but he's absent-minded and can't tell the first exit from the second once he's on the road. If he exits at the first, he gets 0. If he exits at the second, he gets 4. If he drives past both, he gets 1. From the start, the best plan is to continue at each intersection with probability 2/3, which gives expected payoff 4/3. I can follow the math.

But when I imagine actually being the driver at one of the intersections, I can't shake the feeling that the ex ante plan has no authority over me there. I now know I'm at an intersection, and I should update on that. When I try to maximize expected payoff conditional on being at an intersection, the probabilities of being at the first versus the second shift, and the action that looked best before the trip no longer seems obviously right. My roommate says that's the whole point: you should precommit to the 2/3 strategy because the later self is ignorant and will choose wrong. But I don't see why the later self is wrong if he's conditioning on all the information he has. Is this a genuine time inconsistency, or is one of the two perspectives making a subtle mistake? I don't want a side picked for me. I want to know what it would cost to give up the intuition that the current decision-maker should overrule the past plan, what it would cost to give up ex ante optimality, and whether there's a way of framing it where both the planner and the driver at the intersection are rational.
