Suppose I have an ordinary wine glass that I call fragile. A mask is in place: the glass is packed in shock-absorbing foam, so if someone were to strike the package hard enough, the foam would cushion the blow and the glass would not break. A fink is also in place: a small device attached to the glass detects any incoming impact and heats the glass in the instant before contact, so that the glass deforms rather than shatters. In both cases I still want to say the glass is fragile — that fragility is a real property it has — even though the conditional if it were struck, it would break is false. But if a disposition can be present without the corresponding counterfactual being true, I do not see what fragility adds to any explanation: the outcomes seem fully determined by the glass's microstructure, the foam, the heating device, and the laws of nature. More troubling, I do not see what observation could ever count against calling something fragile, since any failure to break can always be blamed on a mask or a fink. Have finks and masks shown that dispositions are either causally superfluous or empirically unfalsifiable, and that we should do metaphysics without them?
