Here are four criteria I was taught make a state a surveillance state: it systematically collects information about identifiable individuals; it does so without individualized suspicion; the information is kept in a centralized or otherwise searchable record; and the information is used to predict, score, or control people. Now consider a state, Lumen, that requires every car and phone to continuously broadcast its GPS position and the registered owner's ID on an unencrypted public band. Police receivers pipe the whole feed through an algorithm that flags suspicious movement patterns in real time and sends an officer the relevant identities; the raw locations are never written to disk, and the only persistent thing is a daily "mobility score" for each person, overwritten every morning. Officials deny Lumen is a surveillance state: the signal is public, anyone can listen, and there is no location database to leak or supoena. Go through the four criteria one by one and say whether Lumen qualifies. Then say whether this case shows that centralized storage, or the fact that the data is public, was doing more work in the definition than it should, and whether a citizen with nothing to hide still has reason to hide from a state like Lumen. Please also say how confident you are in the overall verdict and which part of your answer you're least sure of.
