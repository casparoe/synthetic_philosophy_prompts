Write me a deadpan self-help column, maybe 600 words, with a title and six numbered tips, for people who've finally accepted that an action only has moral worth if you do it from duty, not just because you happen to feel like it. The joke is that the genre is structurally self-defeating: every tip the columnist gives for purifying your motive instantly becomes one more self-interested reason for doing the right thing (you're now doing it so you can be the kind of person who does it for its own sake), so the column has to keep adding meta-tips that make the problem worse. Work in the shopkeeper who gives correct change because its good for business, the worry that you'd have done the thing anyway for self-interested reasons so the whole motive question feels unanswerable, and the weirdness of mentally auditing your motives mid-action. Dont name any philosophers or books. Someone who teaches this stuff should recognize the positions being satirized and find it fair; someone who doesn't should still find it funny.
