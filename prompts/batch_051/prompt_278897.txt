In my government class we have this project where we pick an unusual political system and check it against our teacher's definition of democracy. The definition she gave us is: (1) all citizens have equal say over what goals the government should pursue, (2) the policies passed actually track what the people want, and (3) voters can hold decision-makers accountable when things go badly. I picked futarchy, the thing where elected politicians only choose a national welfare metric (like some mix of life expectancy, income, and pollution), and then prediction markets decide which policies get implemented — whoever bets that a policy will raise the metric the most wins, and the highest-expected-scoring policy becomes law. So the goal-setting part looks democratic, but the actual policy decisions are made by people with money and information betting, not by voters. Does futarchy count as a democracy under our teacher's criteria? Go criterion by criterion, and wherever it fails, say whether thats a problem with futarchy or a problem with the definition. Dont hedge — I want you to commit to a yes or no and defend it.
