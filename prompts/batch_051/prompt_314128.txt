A friend and I stayed up way too late last night going back and fourth about the old Islamic debate between the Mu'tazila and the Ash'arites, and I'm still trying to get it straight. From what he told me, the Mu'tazila held that reason can figure out right and wrong on its own, and that God commands things because they are really good or forbids them because they are really bad — like, murder would be wrong even if God had never said anything about it. The Ash'arites held the opposite, that something only becomes good or bad through God's command, so there's no moral standard apart from or above God. I kept pushing the obvious objection: doesn't the Ash'arite view make morality arbitrary? God could have commanded theft, and then theft would be good, which seems crazy. He shot back that my objection assumes a human standard of decency that God has to answer to, and that the Mu'tazila view ends up limiting God's power. We went in circles.

I want you to compare the two positions and actually adjudicate the dispute, not just report it. Does the Mu'tazila view really escape the arbitrariness worry, or does it just relocate the problem? And is the Ash'arite view stuck biting the bullet that God could make anything good by commanding it? Give the strongest version of each side and then say which one you think is better, and why.
