I'm a backend engineer and I've been going down a philosophy rabbit hole about epiphenomenalism, and there's a parallel I can't shake. Epiphenomenalism says conscious experience is caused by brain activity but has no causal powers of it's own — it just rides on top of the physical stuff, like a read-only side effect. The standard problem, the paradox of phenomenal judgment, is roughly this: if consciousness causes nothing, why do we form beliefs and say things like "I'm in pain" or "that looks red", and why should we trust those reports? If qualia never feed back into the physical story, then our reports about them look like a lucky coincidence, and natural selection couldn't have tuned the reports to track qualia.

Here's the thing, though. In my day job we deliberately build systems that produce accurate self-reports about internal state without letting the reports affect the computation: logging, tracing, metrics. A log line reads a variable and writes it out, and if you're doing it right the logging doesn't change what the program does. Yet the log isn't lucky — the variable's value causes the log entry. So we seem to have a working example of a causally inert (with respect to behavior) but accurate self-report. That sounds a lot like what the epiphenomenalist needs for phenomenal judgment. My question is whether it's the same structure, or whether I'm just pattern-matching on the phrase "side effect."

The disanalogy I can already see: the log reports the variable, and the variable sits in the causal chain that produces the log. But in epiphenomenalism the judgment "I'm in pain" is caused by the physical state, while the pain itself is an extra thing the physical state produces, and the pain doesn't cause the judgment. So the analogous program would have to print something about a "glow" that accompanies the variable, where the glow is caused by the variable but can't influence the print statement. If I hardcode "glow present" to print whenever x == 5, the output tracks x, not the glow — a zombie build with no glow would print the same string. So the analogy seems to break exactly where the paradox bites. Unless the epiphenomenalist is allowed to say that qualia just are the internal state from the inside, in which case the log analogy is closer than the glow story makes it look. I keep flip-flopping.
