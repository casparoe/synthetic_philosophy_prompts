Suppose a democratic, multinational state adopts the following constitutional procedure for secession, designed to separate the desire for independence from the exact terms on which it would occur. A constituent territorial unit whose parliament approves may hold an initiating referendum asking its voters to authorize the territorial government to open independence negotiations with the central government; a simple majority of valid votes cast authorizes negotiation. If authorization passes, the central government must publish, within three years, a complete draft separation agreement covering the division of public debt and assets, citizenship and residence rights, currency and central banking, borders and freedom of movement, military installations, and succession to treaty obligations. The constitution requires the central government to negotiate in good faith but says nothing about how that requirement would be enforced. At least one ordinary general election to the central parliament must occur after the draft is published. The territory then holds a second, binding referendum on the published agreement itself, and a simple majority of valid votes cast makes separation binding on the negotiated terms. If the second referendum rejects the agreement, the territory may not initiate the procedure again for ten years.

Treat this as a mechanism rather than as a position on secession. Walk through how it would actually behave. What incentives does it create for territorial secessionist parties, territorial unionists, the central government, and voters whose preference for or against independence is weak or conditional on the terms? Where does it get gamed first, and by whom? What does the intervening general election do to the negotiations and to the meaning of the two votes? What does the ten-year prohibition after a failed ratification do to the politics that follows? And which of the failure modes you identify could be fixed by adjusting thresholds, deadlines, or enforcement provisions, and which are structural to the two-vote design itself?
