I'm in a formal epistemology seminar and we just went over van Fraassen's Judy Benjamin case — the one where someone is dropped into a region that's equally likely to be Red territory or Blue territory, and each territory is split into a Headquarters Company area and a Second Company area, so all four sub-areas are initially equally likely. She gets a radio message from her own side saying that if she is in Red territory, then she is in Headquarters Company. The message says nothing else.

Here's an argument I can't shake:

(1) Initially, the four cells are equally likely: P(B-HQ) = P(B-S) = P(R-HQ) = P(R-S) = 1/4.

(2) The message says that if she is in Red territory, then she is in Headquarters Company.

(3) What she learns is the proposition: either she is not in Red territory, or she is in Red Headquarters. That is, ¬R ∨ R-HQ.

(4) Bayesian conditionalization says she should update by conditioning on what she learned, so her new credence function should be P(· | ¬R ∨ R-HQ).

(5) Conditioning on ¬R ∨ R-HQ rules out R-S and renormalizes the other three cells to 1/3 each.

(6) Therefore P(R | message) = P(R-HQ | message) = 1/3.

(7) So she should now think Red territory is less likely than Blue territory.

I think (7) has to be wrong. The message only gives her information about where she is conditional on being in Red; it doesn't bear on whether she's actually in Red. Her credence in Red should stay at 1/2. But I genuinely can't tell whether the bad step is (3) — maybe a conditional isn't a material conditional — or (4) — maybe you can't conditionalize on something like this at all. Can you tell me exactly which step fails, why it fails, and what the correct update would look like? And is there a version of Bayesian updating that handles this kind of case without throwing out conditionalization entirely?
