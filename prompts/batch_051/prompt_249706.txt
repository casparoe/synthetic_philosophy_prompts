A colleague and I are preparing a position paper for a workshop on negative utilitarianism, and we need a clean argumentative draft. The topic is lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: the family of views according to which suffering is divided into bands by intensity, and pains at or above a specified lexical threshold dominate all lower-band experiences in moral choice, so that no aggregate of mild pleasure can justify allowing a single above-threshold pain. My colleague thinks this view is unstable because it claims pleasure and pain are commensurable within each band but incommensurable across the threshold, and that the threshold itself is either arbitrary or has to be fixed by intuition. I think the instability is only apparent, but I have not worked out the strongest exact version of the view.

Please argue for the following position: lexical threshold negative utilitarianism is a coherent and plausible account of the commensurability of pleasure and pain, and its threshold discontinuity is defensible rather than ad hoc. Make the argument self-contained: define the view precisely, state the rule for intraband and cross-threshold comparisons, explain what it means for two quantities to be commensurable under this view, and address the arbitrariness objection directly. You need not defend the view against all possible objections, but you should end by stating the residual issue that would give you most pause. No external citations are needed.

After the argument, provide a JSON object with the following fields and nothing else after it:
- "position": one sentence stating the defended claim.
- "view_definition": a three-sentence summary of what the view asserts.
- "comparison_rules": an object with two keys, "intraband" and "cross_threshold", each a one-sentence statement of the relevant comparison rule.
- "key_premises": an array of at least three and no more than five strings, each one core premise in the argument.
- "strongest_objection": a one-sentence statement of the strongest objection considered.
- "reply": a two- or three-sentence reply to that objection.
- "residual_concern": the issue the argument leaves least settled.
- "overall_verdict": one of "strong", "moderate", or "weak", indicating how confident the argument is in the defended position.
