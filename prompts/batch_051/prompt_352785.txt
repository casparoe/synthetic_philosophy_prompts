Consider the claim at the center of the Chinese Room argument: a system can be behaviorally indistinguishable from a competent speaker while operating on symbols without any understanding of what they mean. Treat this not as an established conclusion but as a hypothesis about the limits of behavioral evidence. Propose a concrete test — an experiment, probe battery, or interactive protocol — that would distinguish between the hypothesis that a system understands the language it processes and the hypothesis that it merely tracks formal or statistical regularities in language. Specify the task or tasks, the systems or populations compared, the controls needed, and the outcome that would count in favor of each hypothesis. Then state precisely what a positive result would and would not settle: which version of the dispute the test can genuinely adjudicate, whether each side could reinterpret any outcome within its own commitments, and what residue remains inaccessible to empirical test no matter how the protocols are refined.
