Here's a position I'm thinking of defending at a workshop, and I want you to tell me what would count against it. The claim is that we can make AI systems avoid negative side effects without first writing down full human values, because a side effect can be characterized as a change in the world's attainable utility—roughly, how well any one of a very wide distribution of possible reward functions could still be satisfied from the resulting state. The concrete proposal is this: give an agent its ordinary task reward, but add a penalty equal to how much its actions reduce attainable utility across a big ensemble of randomly generated reward functions, relative to a baseline where the agent doesn't act on the world at all (or acts only in some narrowly specified way). The thought is that a robot should sweep the floor without knocking over the vase, not because we told it to value the vase, but because knocking it over changes what can later be achieved under lots of possible goals, and that change is measurable. That way you get a kind of impact limiter that works without a full theory of the good.

What would have to be true for this position to be wrong, or for the impact measure to be such a bad proxy for what we care about that it's useless as a safety property? I want the evidence, arguments, formal results, and failure modes sorted by how decisive they'd be. In particular I want to hear about: cases where attainable utility is preserved but the action is still catastrophic, or where it drops but the action is completely fine; whether the baseline even makes sense for an agent that's already embeded in the world; whether the choice of the goal distribution sneaks in values through the back door; whether such penalties can be gamed or offset by doing harm and then doing something that raises attainable utility again; and whether the position gets patched after each counterexample until it stops risking anything. Also say what this view predicts that rival views don't, and tell me honestly whether the position as stated is empty, or merely hard to make precise. I'd personally be most moved by a toy environment where the impact penalty reliably produces a result that's obviously not okay. And write the whole thing in American English.
