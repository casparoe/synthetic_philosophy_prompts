I am taking a course on nationalism and self-determination, and for a paper I need a thought experiment that isolates a disagreement among three theories of secession. Please construct a realistic scenario in which the remedial-right-only view—secession is justified only as a remedy for serious, persistent injustice—judges secession permissible, while the plebiscitary view—a territorially concentrated people may secede just in case it democratically votes to do so—judges it impermissible, and a primary-right view—a self-identified nation's right to secede does not depend on any passing vote—also judges it impermissible but on distinct grounds. Constraints: the state is a functioning liberal democracy; the injustice is serious but institutional rather than violent; the would-be state is internally divided about independence; there is no external intervention; and the case should fit in one paragraph, with no stipulated verdicts where the facts should generate them. After giving the scenario, state each theory's verdict and the reasoning behind it, identify the single structural feature that does the work, say what happens to the three verdicts if that feature is removed, and explain how a defender of the plebiscitary view would resist treating the case as a decisive counterexample. The point of the case is to test whether remedial-right theories are, in an underappreciated direction, more permissive than plebiscitary ones.
