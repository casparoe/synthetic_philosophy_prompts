Brandom's whole line is that meaning isn't some separate layer attached to a sentence's inferential connections; to grasp a meaning just is to know what follows from a claim, what it commits you to, what it's incompatible with, and what would count as evidence for it. The standard knock on associationist views was that a parrot can be trained to say "the house is on fire" without being able to do any of that, so it doesn't mean what it says. But now we have LLMs trained only on next-token prediction over a huge pile of text, and they're suprisingly good at exactly the moves that were supposed to distinguish a parrot from a genuine concept-user: they answer "what follows?", they flag inconsistencies, they supply what would count as reasons. I'm not asking whether they're conscious or have intentions. I want to know whether this development kills, weakens, or leaves untouched the inferentialist argument that meaning is constituted by inferential role. Which premise exactly is under pressure—does it hit the idea that inferential role is a normative status, or the idea that an LLM never really undertakes commitments? And is there a repaired version of the argument that survives while claiming something weaker but still interesting?
