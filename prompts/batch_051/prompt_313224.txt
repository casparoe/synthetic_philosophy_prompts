I keep noticing people defend "harmony" or "order" as political values in ways that clearly don't overlap. A conservative relative says a decent society needs a settled, shared way of life; a friend who reads Confucius says harmony is differences held in their proper relations, not sameness; my liberal friends hear either claim as an excuse for shutting people up. I want a working definition of social harmony as a political value that gets the cases below right. Run the definition through all of them, say where it gives a verdict you wouldn't have expected and where it gives none, and either revise the definition or defend the weird verdicts. Then tell me which of your verdicts you're least sure about.

- a neighborhood where nobody complains anymore, because complainers get shunned by the community association
- a pluralist democracy with adversarial parties and loud protests, but shared acceptance of the rules
- a family where one member suppresses a real grievance for decades to keep the peace
- a jazz ensemble improvising successfully around a shared structure
- a society where everyone sincerely affirms the same moral code, because dissenting views were educated out two generations ago
- a coalition where factions compromise so much that none of them recognizes the resulting policy as their own
- a hierarchical workplace where everyone knows their place, producing smooth coordination and quiet resentment
- a ceasefire between two armed factions that both plan to break once they've rearmed

I'm fairly settled that the jazz ensemble is harmony and the rearming ceasefire isn't; the rest I keep flipping on. I also half-suspect the word does one descriptive and one evaluative job, and that "harmony" differs from "order" mainly in whether the parts keep their own character or get filed down to fit. If you agree, give me two definitions and say which cases move between them.
