I am writing a paper on the law's resistance to naked statistical evidence, and I need a pair of thought experiments built to order. The standard cases set up the problem: in the blue bus case, a plaintiff is hit by a blue bus, and the only evidence is that 80% of the blue buses in town are owned by the defendant; in the gatecrasher case, the operator proves that most attendees never paid and sues a randomly selected attendee on that frequency alone. In each, the probability of liability seems high enough to satisfy the preponderance standard, yet courts and most commentators treat the evidence as insufficient. I want a controlled pair that isolates the feature doing the work.

Specifications. Both cases are civil cases governed by the preponderance standard, which I am using deliberately so that the criminal standard's separate debate about how to interpret beyond reasonable doubt is held fixed. In both cases, the probability that the defendant is liable given the evidence is exactly 0.8. In both, the link between the defendant and the injury is supplied entirely by a statistic: no eyewitness, confession, or document identifies the defendant, and no physical trace uniquely matches anything under the defendant's control. Case A should have the structure of the blue bus case: a frequency about a population to which the defendant belongs. Case B should instead be a diagnostic output: a procedure with known error rates applied to a trace from the particular event, where the procedure's output is the only thing connecting the trace to the defendant.

Design Case B so that a sensitivity-based account of legal proof treats the evidence as sufficient while a safety-based account treats it as insufficient. By a sensitivity-based account I mean roughly this: a finding is acceptable only if the factfinder would not have made it had the defendant been innocent. By a safety-based account I mean roughly this: a finding is acceptable only if it could not easily have been false. Case A should be rejected by both accounts.

The point of the pair is to test the claim that the law's resistance to naked statistics is best explained by sensitivity rather than safety; if the pair works, sensitivity lets in a class of diagnostic evidence that safety still excludes. After constructing the two scenarios, show the reasoning for each account in each case, identify the single structural feature that makes the accounts come apart in Case B, and say what happens to the case if that feature is removed. Then address the hard part. A defender of the safety account will say that if the false-positive possibility in Case B is near enough to make the finding unsafe, it should also make it insensitive. Give the best reply available to the sensitivity account, or say plainly that the specification cannot be met as stated and identify which clause has to be weakened.

Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this topic. For example, it is settled that the preponderance standard is more likely than not, and that courts are reluctant to base verdicts on bare frequencies; it is contested why they are reluctant and whether that reluctance is coherent. Do not present a contested account as the consensus.
