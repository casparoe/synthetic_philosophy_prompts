I've been going back and forth on pain asymbolia and I need someone to help me figure out which of two things I should give up.

On one side is a view I find really compelling: pain has two components that can dissociate—a sensory-discriminative component (where it is, how strong, whether it's sharp or burning or throbbing) and an affective-motivational component (the awfulness, the urge to make it stop). Pain asymbolia looks like a clean demonstration of the split. The patients can point to the pain, rate its intensity, and tell you it's sharp or throbbing, but they are completely unbothered by it—they don't flinch, they don't pull away, some of them smile while describing it. Morphine can do something similar: people report that the pain is still there, but they no longer mind it. The two-component view is elegant because it handles all of this with one distinction, and it explains why the informational part of a pain could outlive it's nastiness.

On the other side is an intuition I can't shake: the badness is not an ingredient that could be peeled off. It is what pain is. A pain that isn't at all unpleasant isn't a pain; it's a mere sensation—a stinging or a pressure or a warmth that happens to occur where there's damage. Strip out the awfulness and you haven't removed one component while leaving the pain itself intact; you've removed the very thing that made it pain. So the asymbolic doesn't have pain-without-suffering; they have the sensory shell of pain with the essence gone, and calling it 'pain' at all is loose talk.

I don't want someone to just tell me which side wins. I want to know what it would take for each side to be the one that gives. Is there a version of the two-component view on which full-fledged pain is defined by the affective component, so asymbolia counts as pain only in a thin or derivative sense—and if so, is that a real reconciliation or just relabeling? And is there a way of debunking the intuition—am I only insisting that pain must be awful because the awfulness is the only handle I have on the experience from the inside, so I literally can't imagine it absent? What does each concession cost, and which one costs less? Or is there a reading on which both survive?
