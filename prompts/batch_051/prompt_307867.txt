In intuitionistic propositional logic, the law of excluded middle (p ∨ ¬p) and double negation elimination (¬¬p → p) are interderivable. The student wrote:

LEM and DNE are interderivable. Given LEM, suppose ¬¬p. By LEM, either p or ¬p. In the first case we have p. In the second case, ¬¬p together with ¬p gives a contradiction, and explosion yields p. So DNE follows. Conversely, given DNE, suppose ¬(p ∨ ¬p). If p, then p ∨ ¬p, contradicting the assumption; so ¬p. But ¬p gives p ∨ ¬p as well, again contradicting the assumption; so ¬¬(p ∨ ¬p). DNE then yields p ∨ ¬p. So they are equivalent in intuitionistic logic.

Under the BHK interpretation, a proof of p ∨ ¬p would have to be either a proof of p or a proof that p leads to a contradiction. We have no general method for deciding, for an arbitrary proposition, which of these we can produce, so LEM is not constructively justified. A proof of ¬¬p is a construction that turns any proof of ¬p into a contradiction, and that does not yet give us a proof of p; so DNE is rejected for the same underlying reason.

The schema ¬∀xP(x) → ∃x¬P(x) is intuitionistically valid. If it is not the case that every x has P, then there must be some particular x for which P(x) fails; BHK lets us take that x as the witness for the existential, and its failure of P gives a proof of ¬P(x). The dual schema ¬∃xP(x) → ∀x¬P(x) is valid by the same kind of reasoning, so the two quantifier De Morgan laws both hold.
