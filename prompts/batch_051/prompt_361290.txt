An influential argument for prioritising the prevention of extreme suffering over the creation of new positive lives maintains that suffering has a special moral urgency that happiness lacks, and that its reduction cannot simply be traded off against added happiness. The argument, as usually stated, takes for granted that positive and negative affect are distinct magnitudes, so that one could in principle reduce suffering without touching the positive states alongside it. Now suppose a body of experimental work firmly established that this separability fails at the level of the underlying systems: in any sentient creature, the very structures that support intense joy, attachment, and achievement are also what make possible severe grief, fear, and pain, so that any intervention that dampens the negative range proportionally flattens the positive range. Does the suffering-focused argument survive this development, or is it dead? If it survives, produce the repaired version of the argument and say what work it can still do; if it fails, state precisely which premise has given way and whether any weaker but still important claim remains. Answer in at most 450 words.
