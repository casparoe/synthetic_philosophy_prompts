Before I retired I was in population genetics — mostly models of how small populations lose variation — so I'm comfortable with formal systems, but until last year I'd never read any philosophy. Lately I've been pouring over the AI alignment material, and the idea that's taken up residence in my head is "embedded agency": the point that a real reasoner is a proper physical part of the world it's trying to reason about, so the clean Cartesian split between "agent" and "environment" was always a convenient fiction. Once you give that up, a bunch of things get messy at once: the agent has to include itself, or a model of itself, in its world-model; its own computations take time and energy and are themselves events in the world it's predicting; it can't reliably run through all the logical consequences of its own possible actions before acting; and it may have to trust or delegate to future versions of itself that its present model says will be smarter than it currently is. I've seen these treated as separate subproblems — embedded world-models, logical uncertainty, and reflection or robust delegation — and I can follow each one individually, but I don't have a feel for how they trade off against each other.
