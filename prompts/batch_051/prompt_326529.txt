Write an essay about the controversy over Jeremy Bentham’s dismissal of natural rights. Focus especially on his treatment of Article II of the French Declaration of the Rights of Man and the Citizen, where the preservation of natural and imprescriptible rights is given as the aim of every political association. Bentham argues that such rights cannot be real rights because no law has created them and no sovereign is assigned to enforce them; rights without law are, on his view, nothing but expressions of desire dressed up as political facts. Reconstruct this argument and then consider the strongest objection: Bentham may show that natural rights are not legal rights, but he does not show that they are useless as moral reasons for making or resisting law. Modern human-rights charters can be understood as translating just such moral reasons into positive legal form. Assess that reply and reach a definite conclusion. Does Bentham’s critique undermine only a naive version of natural-rights talk, or does it also create serious problems for mature legal systems that entrench human rights in positive constitutions and international instruments? Defend your verdict. Maximum length: 850 words.
