The philosophical engine behind this is a generic-case argument — unfaithful parameterizations, where two paths cancel exactly or an extra edge fits only via fine-tuned parameters, are measure zero, so we're supposed to be able to ignore them.

What's bugging me is that the last decade of ML looks like it lives precisely in those measure-zero neighborhoods. We train wildly overparameterized models that interpolate the training data (zero loss), they generalize fine, double descent, the whole thing.
