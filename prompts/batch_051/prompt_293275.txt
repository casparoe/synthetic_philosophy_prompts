I am a software engineer who works on systems that search large combinatorial spaces — heuristic search, anytime planners, constraint solvers, and similar machinery — and I am also a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, so I am used to treating rationality as a topic to argue about rather than a term to gesture at. I want to understand what Herbert Simon's notion of satisficing, within his program of bounded rationality, actually adds to the familiar engineering point that exact optimization is often computationally intractable. In my vocabulary, a satisficing agent sounds as if it is just running an anytime algorithm with an acceptance predicate and a computational budget: it takes the first candidate whose heuristic score clears a threshold, and it raises or lowers the threshold as candidates arrive. Read that way, the debate between optimizing and satisficing accounts of rationality seems to collapse into ordinary design questions about objective functions, stopping rules, approximation guarantees, and how much search one can afford. But I have the lingering suspicion that Simon meant something stronger — perhaps that in real choice problems the objective is not given in advance, or that the act of search changes what the agent wants — and I cannot say in my own terms what that residue is.

Restate Simon's satisficing account of rational choice, and the associated critique of maximizing models of rationality, in the vocabulary just described: search spaces, objective functions, heuristic evaluation functions, aspiration thresholds, acceptance predicates, stopping conditions, anytime and approximation algorithms, and computational budgets. Translate each component — the way the choice problem is posed, the agent's goals, the stopping rule, the adjustment of aspirations over time, and the descriptive and normative claims about real agents — as faithfully as that vocabulary permits. Then give me the residue report: which parts translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion and what the distortion loses, and which parts have no counterpart in the engineering framework at all. Conclude with your judgment about whether the residue indicates that my vocabulary is missing something essential, or whether the optimizing-versus-satisficing dispute is, after all, mostly a dispute about bounded computational resources.
