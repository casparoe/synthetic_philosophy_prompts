I'm taking a course this term on social epistemology and online trust, and we've spent a good chunk of it on reputation: indirect reciprocity, costly signalling, gossip networks, and then a unit on platform-specific stuff like Reddit karma, eBay feedback, Uber ratings, credit scores. I'm writing my term paper on what reputation actually is, and I keep going back and forth on the definition. The working definition floating around the readings is something like: reputation is socially stored information about someone's past behaviour, used to decide whether to interact with them. But I'm not convinced it survives its own cases. My flatmate keeps telling me I'm overthinking it, but here's the list I want the definition to get right:

- an eBay seller with 98.7% positive feedback across 4,000 transactions
- a Reddit account with 60,000 karma, almost all of it from one viral post
- someone in a small town who has a name for being reliable but has never actually done anything for you
- a FICO credit score
- an Uber passenger rating that half the drivers don't even glance at
- a dead person (can you damage a dead person's reputation, or is there nothing left for a reputation to be?)
- a person who 'has a bad reputation' in a community entirely because of a false rumour nobody can trace back to anyone

My hunch is that the informational definition gets the eBay case right but the credit score wrong (a credit score feels to me more like a risk forecast than a reputation), and that the small-town case is actually the most central example, not the online ones. Give me a definition that handles all seven — or if you need two definitions, a descriptive one and an evaluative one, that's fine — run it through each case, tell me which verdict you're least confident in, and flag the places where the definition has to stretch or quietly changes meaning. And please seperate what's actually settled in this corner of the literature from what people who work on this stuff still genuinly argue about; I don't want to end up treating a contested reading as the consensus in my paper.
