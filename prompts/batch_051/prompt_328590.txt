Most of us will happily say that foot-binding in nineteenth-century China was wrong or that Roman slavery was unjust, and we don't feel like cultural imperialists when we do. But switch the example to something happening now in another country and a lot of the same people get queasy: it's their culture, who am I to judge. Distance in time seems to license moral judgment while distance in space seems to revoke it. I'm trying to figure out whether there's an actual principle behind that, or whether it's just that the dead can't make us feel rude. If there is a principle, say what it is and test it against the case where your own grandchildren someday regard something you do now as obviously wrong.
