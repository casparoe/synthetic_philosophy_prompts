I've spent years reading my way through the anthropic-reasoning disputes on rationalist blogs and forums, and I have settled on a position I want tested rather than defended. I hold the unrestricted Self-Indication Assumption: when I condition on my own existence, a hypothesis is favored in proportion to the number of observer-moments it contains that could have been mine, rather than sampling uniformly from an independently fixed reference class of actual observers. From that I accept the standard consequences without hedging. In Sleeping Beauty I take the thirder position, because the Tails protocol contains two candidate waking observer-moments and the Heads protocol only one. I also regard the Doomsday-style inference from my birth rank as canceled: the large-population hypotheses pick up an indexical boost of N that exactly offsets the one-in-N likelihood of finding myself at the particular rank I occupy. I bite the bullet on the Presumptuous Philosopher as well. If two otherwise indistinguishable cosmological theories differ only in the number of observers they entail, I think SIA rationally requires favoring the more populous one, and I suspect much of the resistance comes from importing intuitions that are correct only when the competing hypotheses are not themselves claims about how many candidates there are for being me. The one qualification I make is that SIA is cleanly defined only for finite populations; I take the genuinely infinite cases to be problematic for every available view, so I do not regard the measure problem as an objection that lands on SIA in particular.

What I want is a map of what would count against this position. Please distinguish evidence or arguments that should force a large update from those that are merely awkward and can be absorbed by a committed defender with a small patch. In particular: are there versions of the Presumptuous Philosopher that survive the usual bullet-biting — cases where the more populous theory gets its extra observers by degrading the quality of each observer's evidence, or where the surplus observer-moments are stipulated to be subjectively identical to mine — and what would those cases show? What would the world, or my own reasoning, have to look like for me to conclude not that SIA needs a cleverer reference class, but that SIA is simply a false account of how to update on indexical information, or that the SSA/SIA dispute itself is ill-posed? Finally, be blunt about the possibility to which I am perhaps most vulnerable: to what extent has this version of SIA been qualified, by the reference-class choice and by the finite-population carve-out, into having no empirical risk at all? If the position cannot lose, tell me whether that is a defect, given that the claim is best understood as a constraint on rational indexing rather than a claim about what I will observe.
