I spent my scientific career in comparative physiology, much of it on invertebrate nervous systems, and since retiring I have been reading moral philosophy. A dispute I keep returning to is whether sentience—and with it moral considerability—is a matter of degree or a threshold property. The two positions seem to talk past each other. The first, which I will call the Gradualists, treats a honeybee, a robot vacuum that updates a simple predictive model of its environment, and a thermostat regulating toward a set point as points on a single continuum. On this view the bee has more of whatever matters and the thermostat has very little, but the differences are differences in the richness, integration, and valence-like character of the system's control loops, not differences in kind. The second, the Threshold Theorists, denies this. On their view a structural discontinuity separates a system that merely adjusts its state from a system with a felt point of view—roughly, an integrated evaluative model of itself in its world. Below that line, no amount of behavioral sophistication confers direct moral status; a thermostat is owed nothing in itself, and a bee is owed the full regard due to a subject only if it actually crosses the line.

I would like you to draft the joint statement that a good-faith adversarial collaboration between these two camps could both sign. It should first record the claims they jointly accept about what the cases do and do not show: the bee's mushroom-body-dependent learning, its grooming and avoidance after a noxious stimulus, the robot vacuum's homeostatic reappraisal, and the thermostat's set-point regulation. It should then make the residual disagreement precise rather than rhetorical. For each residual disagreement, give a neutral formulation of the point at issue; the wording each side would insist on for its own view; the crux, meaning the sub-question whose settlement would move at least one side; and a concrete, feasible case or manipulation both sides agree bears on the crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I suspect the cruxes will include whether any continuous change in an evaluative process could ever make the difference between no moral patient and a full moral patient, and what to say about an artifact that implements only part of the bee's evaluative circuitry. One recurring possibility both sides should address is that the Gradualists apply the term 'sentience' to any valence-like feedback while the Threshold Theorists reserve it for a felt unity, in which case part of the dispute would be verbal.

Format the deliverable as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, and close with a short note identifying any part of the dispute the collaboration concludes is verbal rather than substantive.
