I'm a software engineer, and last weekend I watched two friends argue for like an hour about whether a dream is something that actually happens while you're asleep, or more like a story your brain slaps together when you wake up. I honestly couldn't tell who was winning, and now I can't stop thinking about it.

It started when Miguel told us about a dream where he was being chased through a warehouse, and Tom said something like, 'You know that probably didn't happen in real time. The whole feeling that it lasted minutes could've been written into youre memory in half a second when you woke up.' Miguel pushed back: 'No way. I felt fear, my legs burned, time passed. If that's not an experience, I don't know what is.' Tom said the only evidence anyone ever actually has is the report afterward, and you can't go back and check the timestamp the way you can with waking events. Miguel then brought up lucid dreamers, how people can decide ahead of time to move their eyes in a pattern inside a dream, and the movements show up in a sleep lab. 'That's a signal coming from inside the dream in real time,' he said, 'so something's unfolding while I'm asleep.' Tom answered that the eye movements are just physical events; they don't prove there's a conscious 'self' in there watching anything, and the memory of the whole lucid scene could still be assembled at the moment of waking and glued to those eye movements in your memory. Miguel said that's nuts: 'If a dream can be backdated like that when you wake, then by the same logic my memory of yesterday's lunch could be fake too, and you'd end up doubting all memory, not just dreams.' Tom said waking memory has ongoing checks—the world is still there, other people can confirm the same lunch—but during sleep there's nothing like that; the only trace of the dream is your own later report. Miguel replied that this just assumes what it's trying to prove, because the lucid signaling case is exactly a check, it just doesn't look like ordinary conversation. Tom said a real check has to be something another person could in principle verify, but the eye movements can't be read off to recover the story; they're only correlated with the same waking report that's in question.

They asked me to referee, and I genuinely didn't know what to say. Can you go through their exchange step by step and say who's right about what, and who is more right overall? I'm sure they're both talking past each other in places, and there are probably things both of them are missing. In particular, does the lucid dreaming point actually settle anything or not?
