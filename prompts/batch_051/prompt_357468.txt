The pitch isn't that the resulting distribution matches some ideal of justice, just that it stops the windfall from frontier AI from concentrating absurdly at the top. I mentioned this to an economist, and he shut the whole thing down by saying this was just Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain point: liberty upsets patterns. You cannot keep a distribution tracking any pattern — a Gini cap, an income floor, a permitted ratio — without continuously overriding voluntary choices, so the whole project of trying to shape how society's holdings end up is doomed. I half-bought it, but I also can't tell how much of that line is a descriptive observation about markets, how much is a moral principle, and how much is rhetorical sleight of hand. I know it comes out of Nozick's entitlement theory, and I've worked through the Wilt Chamberlain example, so you don't need to summarize it for me. What I need before I quote this guy in my piece is someone to disassemble that four-word slogan and tell me exactly what it could be claiming.

Give me the response in these sections, in this order:

Readings (about 350 words): list the distinct precise claims liberty upsets patterns could be expressing, from the nearly trivial to the very strong. Number them, and keep them from collapsing into each other.

Scorecard (about 250 words): for each numbered reading, say whether it is a platitude, a live but contestable view, or false, with a one-sentence justification.
