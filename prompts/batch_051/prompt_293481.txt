I am a functional programmer with a solid working knowledge of type systems — algebraic data types, polymorphism, effect types such as IO, and the requirement that a compound expression be well-typed if its parts are — but I have never studied metaethics. Restate the Frege–Geach problem for expressivism in that vocabulary. By expressivism, I mean the view that moral sentences like 'lying is wrong' do not state facts but express non-cognitive attitudes, more like approvals or commands than descriptions. The problem, as I understand it, is that such sentences embed into compound contexts — negation, disjunction, the antecedent of a conditional — where the attitude is not expressed, yet the compounds still participate in apparently valid inference. For example, 'getting your little brother to lie is wrong' follows from 'lying is wrong' and 'if lying is wrong, then getting your little brother to lie is wrong', even though someone who asserts only the conditional need not be expressing disapproval of lying. The validity of that inference seems to require that the embedded sentence have the same meaning it has when asserted, so the expressivist owes an account of what an attitude-expression means when it is merely embedded. Translate this into my terms: sentences as typed expressions; the contrast between Bool-valued expressions and effect-producing actions; compositionality, on which the type and meaning of a compound is fixed by its parts; operators that are only defined on Bool; and whatever in my world corresponds to mentioning an action rather than performing it. Then give me a residue report: which parts of the Frege–Geach problem map cleanly onto typing and compositionality, which parts you had to distort to make them fit, and which parts have no counterpart in a type system. Conclude by saying whether the residue is a limitation of my vocabulary or a sign that the expressivist's problem is not merely a typing problem — or that some part of it is less clear than it looks. Keep the translation honest: do not quietly assume that moral sentences are truth-valued and then declare the problem dissolved.
