I've been trying to sort out what I actually think about choices that affect future people, and my gut verdicts keep pulling in opposite directions. Here's where I land on a bunch of concrete cases:

- A country can either pay for a clean-energy transition now or keep a cheap fossil-fuel grid. The fossil path will make life significantly harder in a hundred years — worse droughts, more heat deaths, more coastal flooding — but it will also change who ends up existing, since people meet and have children under different conditions. Under either policy the people alive in a hundred years would have lives easily worth living, and no particular future person under the fossil path could say they'd have been better off under the clean path (they wouldn't have existed). I still think the fossil choice is wrong.

- A couple is told that if they conceive this month the child will have a mild, permanent but manageable disability, and if they wait two months for an infection to clear the child would be healthy. They conceive now. I think that's blameworthy, not just a different choice.

- The same couple instead decides never to have children at all so they can travel. I don't think they've wronged anyone; a kid who never exists hasn't been made worse off.

- A country buries nuclear waste that stays dangerous for thousands of years. Later generations keep being born near the site, some people die young from exposure, but each of those people still has a life worth living, and the policy is also part of the causal story of why they exist at all. I read that as wrong too — if anything worse than the couple's case.

- A government tries to maximize births, and the result is a much larger population where most people lead lives that are just barely worth living — low but positive welfare. I'm uneasy, but I don't have the same clear 'this is wrong' feeling I have about the waste case, even though in both cases everyone born has a life worth living and no currently existing person is worse off. These two feel very different and I can't say why.

Is there one principle, or a small set of candidates, that captures this pattern of verdicts? What does it say about the embryo-selection version I keep going back and forth on: a clinic has two embryos, and one carries a small but real risk of a serious genetic illness; if they implant that one and the child is born ill, she'd still have a life worth living, but the other embryo would have made a healthy child. Is choosing the riskier embryo wrong, and is it more like the fossil-fuel case or like the no-kids case? And which of my verdicts above, if any, does your principle force me to give up?
