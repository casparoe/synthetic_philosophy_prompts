I'm the philosophy professor on our county's sanctions review panel (the token non-lawyer). We're hearing an appeal from a woman whose benefit was stopped for six weeks after she missed two mandatory 'work preparation' sessions. Her side: the sessions were moved to 8:30am with three days' notice, which is exactly when she walks her daughter to school; the father has a restraining order against him so he can't cover it, and the bus route that would've worked got cut. The department's side: she signed the standard work-search agreement, re-scheduling was available by phone, and if we accept 'life got complicated' as a defense then every condition becomes a negotiation and the best storytellers collect. I keep telling my colleagues this is the old deserving/undeserving poor question in administrative form. If the condition is a test of willingness to work, her willingness isn't seriously in doubt — she went to the sessions before the time change and found a make-up workshop herself. If it's a test of ability to comply with scheduling, then we're sorting the administratively lucky from the unlucky, and I don't see why that should cost someone six weeks of their only income. Draft the decision memo I should circulate: uphold, overturn, or vary the sanction, with reasons the department's lawyer could actually respond to and an honest treatment of the strongest argument for upholding. I need a bottom line the panel can vote on, and it can't sound to much like a seminar.
