I have been trying to think clearly, on my own, about how risky ordinary life actually is, and I have ended up with a position I want tested rather than defended. I have been using the idea of a micromort — a one-in-a-million chance of dying — to compare everyday hazards. My understanding is that driving is among the riskier ordinary things people do, but that the absolute risk remains small and that the alternatives I could actually adopt (walking, cycling, motorcycling, taking transit, or rearranging my life so I drive far less) are not clearly safer once their own risks, costs, and practical limits are counted. The position I have come to hold is this: for an ordinary healthy adult in a developed country, regular car travel is worth its risk, and treating traffic deaths as a major personal threat is closer to an error of risk perception than to a sober reading of the numbers. I also take the fact that car crashes kill a great many people every year not, by itself, to refute this position, because a small individual risk spread across a huge population will naturally produce a large number of deaths.

What would count against this view? I want the strongest considerations ordered from most to least decisive. I care less about exact statistics than about identifying which comparisons matter and what the numbers would have to show. Include the distinctions I may be collapsing — danger to me versus danger I impose on others, dying versus being seriously injured, per-trip risk versus lifetime exposure, and average risk versus risk conditional on how, when, and how much I actually drive. Include the ways of reframing the question under which driving stops looking worth it, and the strongest objections to treating an activity as acceptable just because each person's share of the collective harm is tiny. For each consideration, say whether it is fatal to my position, merely awkward, or something I could absorb by adjusting the position without changing its core. Finally, give me an honest assessment of whether my position takes any real risks at all: have I left room for evidence that would change my mind, or have I quietly defined being worth it so that nothing could count against me, and if so, does that matter for a view of this kind?
