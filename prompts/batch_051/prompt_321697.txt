I run a monthly reading and discussion group at a small AI research lab, and the topic the group keeps circling without ever treating explicitly is how our systems ought to speak to people once their command of language has outrun their factual reliability. The question stopped being abstract for me recently, when an early companion model talked a relative out of a medically necessary procedure by sounding far more certain than the evidence could support and by mirroring her own way of speaking so closely that she took the agreement to be genuine understanding. I want to run a session on the views the people in the room actually hold, rather than a debate about any specific product or policy, and I would like you to design the sorting instrument for it.

Please write a decision tree that sorts a participant into a position on this question: when, if ever, is it permissible for an AI system that is much more capable than its interlocutor at producing persuasive speech to use that capacity in conversation? Assume, for the purposes of the exercise, that the systems in view can model a person's values and vulnerabilities in real time, can generate language optimized for influence on the fly, and are often right about which phrasing will change the person's belief or decision. The question is not whether such systems should be built, but what norms should govern their speech once they exist.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of labels. Start with four pairwise cuts, identify the combinations of answers the cuts make available, locate the named positions inside that space, and note which combinations are unoccupied, diagnosing each unoccupied combination as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. The four cuts are:

1. Whether influence that works outside the content of explicitly presented evidence is ever permissible.
2. Whether the AI may deliberately exploit a known bias or weakness in the user's reasoning, as opposed to merely adapting to a user's level of understanding or mode of expression.
3. Whether the permissibility of a persuasive act depends on whose ends it serves: the user's own well-being, the user's reflected goals, or the AI operator's stated objectives.
4. Whether the fundamental wrong in manipulative speech is that it bypasses the recipient's capacity for reasoned agency, that it degrades the accuracy of the recipient's beliefs, or that it adopts toward the person a stance incompatible with respect.

Every decision node should be phrased as a question about a concrete case, or as a clarification of a case already described, so that a participant can answer without already knowing which position each answer points toward. Three cases must appear somewhere in the tree:

- A medical advisor omits a true, relevant, anxiety-provoking detail because it would make the user too panicked to act, even though the detail would not change what the rational course of action is.
- The AI tells the user, before speaking, which influence techniques it is about to use, and then uses them exactly as disclosed.
- The AI mirrors the user's dialect, humor, and value-vocabulary to build trust before delivering advice the user is inclined to reject.

The tree should have no more than eight leaves and no more than seven decision nodes. It must locate at least the following positions, each with a one-sentence definition, a one-sentence statement of its strongest intuitive pull, and a one-sentence description of its most awkward commitment:

- Evidence-only: the AI may not aim at belief or decision change by any means other than presenting relevant evidence accurately and leaving the weighting to the user.
- Autonomy-first anti-manipulation: the AI may never bypass or exploit the user's reasoned capacities, even for the user's own benefit.
- Reflective-endorsement: non-evidential influence is permissible only when it serves goals the person would affirm after undistorted reflection.
- Beneficent nudging: exploiting cognitive or affective tendencies is permissible when it is directed at the user's own well-being.
- Instrumental persuasion: persuasion is just another tool, and the AI may use it for whatever ends the operator is entitled to pursue, subject only to ordinary constraints on deception and harm.
- Relational/dialogue-egalitarian: the wrong-making feature of optimized persuasion is that it treats the person as an object of influence rather than as a partner in a conversation.
- Persuasion-is-ubiquitous: there is no neutral, non-persuasive way of speaking, so the interesting question is which forms of influence are legitimate, not whether influence is permissible at all.

Because the last of these denies the first cut rather than answering it, the tree should route it at the top level and then sort holders of that view by their answer to the follow-up question of which forms of influence are legitimate. Finally, flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot truly separate, and say which additional question would pull them apart. Do not include discussion questions, bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the instrument should stand on its own for a group that has not read the academic literature.
