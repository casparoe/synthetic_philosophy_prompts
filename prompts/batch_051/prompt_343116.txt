I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm preparing a piece on why public debates about religious diversity and salvation keep returning to one metaphor: "there are many paths up the same mountain." Most recently, an interviewee used it to dismiss a question I raised about whether a pluralist who insists every tradition is climbing toward the same summit hasn't tacitly adopted a single, contestable picture of what the summit is. Before I write the piece, I need the analysis that should have followed from that line. Lay out the distinct precise claims the metaphor could be making—about whether the paths share a destination, about whether all of them actually reach it, about whether the destination looks the same from every vantage point, and about how anyone could know any of this. For each reading, say whether it is a sociological platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or something false or self-undermining. Then tell me which reading or readings the metaphor commits a pluralist to, whether any of them escapes the worry that religious pluralism quietly reinstates an exclusivist picture of the goal, and whether an inclusivist or an exclusivist could deploy the same metaphor with equal justice.
