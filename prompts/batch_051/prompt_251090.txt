I keep seeing 'if you violate expected utility theory you can be money-pumped' used to settle debates, so I tried to make the argument precise, and now I think somethings off but I cant say exactly where. (1) If you prefer A to B, then for some small fee e you'd rather have A and pay e than keep B, all else equal. (2) Say your preferences are cyclic: A preferred to B, B preferred to C, C preferred to A. (3) Starting with C, a trader offers to swap C for B for a small fee; by (1), you accept. Then B for A for a fee; you accept. Then A for C for a fee; you accept. (4) You end up with C again, minus three fees. (5) Any preferences that let someone, via trades you voluntarily take one by one, leave you with exactly what you started with minus money, are rationally defective. (6) So cyclic preferences are irrational, and therefore the transitivity axiom, and expected utility theory generally, is rationally required. Is this argument valid? If not, which step fails, and does the money pump still work as a coherence argument once the flaw is fixed?
