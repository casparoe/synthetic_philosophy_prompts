I've been arguing with a friend about whether you can honestly form an aesthetic opinion just from what other people tell you, and I decided to write out the cases I actually have stable gut reactions to, because I want somebody to tell me what principle I'm implicitly using, or whether some of my reactions just have to go.

Here's where I land. (1) If my wine-obsessed friend tells me a bottle I've never tried is good, I'm happy to say it's good and even give it as a gift, though I don't pretend to know what it taste like. (2) If a movie I haven't seen gets really high scores from critics I usually like, I'll tell people it's probably good. (3) But if I read a novel and found it shallow, and then a critic I trust tells me it's actually a masterpiece and I missed the point, I don't change my mind that I found it shallow—I might concede I read it wrong, but I can't honestly say I think it's good. (4) On the other hand, if someone tells me the thing to listen for is the bass line in the second movement, and I go re-listen and the whole piece clicks for me, that feels totally legitimate, like their hint plus my own re-listening made the new judgment. (5) If a friend with a much better palate tells me the dish I disliked is excellent and my palate just isn't developed, I still don't enjoy the dish, but I do start believing something like "the dish is excellent for its type." (6) The case I keep flip-flopping on: before I write a review of an album, I deliberately avoid reading anyone else's reviews so my opinion stays mine; after I publish, I'll happily read them and sometimes change how I describe the record. I can't explain why the order matters so much to me.

My rough principle is: other people can give me justified beliefs about whether a thing is good and can point my attention somewhere, but they can't legitimately overturn a first-hand aesthetic response I've already formed—although their say-so can add a separate belief about the thing's quality. But (5) and (6) seem to strain that, and I get the feeling (4) is doing more than just pointing my attention, though I can't say what. Is there one principle behind all of this, or close, and if not, which of these verdicts does it force me to give up? Please also test whatever account you land on against a new case I haven't thought of, and write in American English.
