I sit on the interdisciplinary review panel of a private foundation that funds work on conceptual foundations of physics. We are deciding whether to fund a two-year project called "Kantian Modesty in Cosmology." The applicant, a philosopher of science, proposes to argue that modern Big Bang cosmology has not settled the question whether the universe had a beginning in time, because in Kant's first antinomy both the thesis and the antithesis prove too much. The correct lesson, on the applicant's reading, is that spatiotemporal boundaries can be drawn only within experience, not for the world as a whole. The project would use this to argue that contemporary cosmologists should treat "the universe began" and "the universe is beginningless" as transcendental illusions, and should suspend judgment on the global temporal extent and topology of the universe.

The proposal anchors its reading in this passage from Immanuel Kant's Critique of Pure Reason, Preface to the First Edition (Meiklejohn translation):

"Human reason, in one sphere of its cognition, is called upon to consider questions, which it cannot decline, as they are presented by its own nature, but which it cannot answer, as they transcend every faculty of the mind."

The committee is split. Some think the project confuses an epistemic point about the limits of pure reason with a claim about what physical cosmology may legitimately assert, and would encourage scientists to treat a well-posed empirical question as off-limits. Others think it would fund exactly the sort of conceptual clarification that keeps cosmological models from overclaiming. Draft the decision memo I will circulate before Friday's meeting: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions; state any actionable conditions; and engage the strongest consideration on the losing side honestly. The memo should test whether the proposal's use of Kant is faithful to the antinomy itself rather than merely invoking it as an authority.

As part of the memo, please say how confident you are in the reading of Kant that your recommendation relies on, and single out the point you are least sure of.

Please organize the memo in these sections, by name and in this order:

1. Recommendation  
2. The proposal's core argument  
3. Case for funding  
4. Case against funding  
5. Engagement with the strongest objection to my recommendation  
6. Conditions or alternative  
7. Confidence note

Keep Sections 1 and 7 to one paragraph each; Sections 2 through 6 should be roughly two paragraphs each.
