I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so forgive the long setup. I'm drafting a novel set around a garment factory in Dhaka, and while reading interviews and reporting for it I keep tripping over a pattern I don't have a precise word for. The arrangement gets called exploitative by nearly everyone—the buyers, the labor organizers, the columnists—but the women on the line are, by their own telling, better off inside it than in any alternative they actually have. Not marginally better off either: the interviews are full of women who left much worse work, breaking bricks or doing domestic service or village farming, and who say with real fear that if the factory shut they'd be pushed back into that. One woman, as I remember it, said she would beg to be let back in; I probably won't put the line in the novel because I can't check it, but it stuck with me. The economists say these plants are the most effective antipoverty program in the region; activists says the wages are unconscionable no matter what the alternative are; the consumers who buy the shirts mostly hold both thoughts at once and keep buying.

My question: is there a name for this exact shape of wrongness—a transaction that makes the weaker party materially better off than her realistic alternatives, that she knowingly consents to and even fights to keep, but that we still regard as exploitative? I don't want just "exploitation"; that's the word I'm trying to get inside of. I want the carving: how do you separate this from coercion on one side (nobody made her take the job) and from a merely bad-but-legitimate bargain on the other? If there's an established term of art for this, give it, define it carefully, and say what it rules in and out. If there are several neighboring terms, sort out the differences between them. If there genuinely isn't one, then coin a name and give a definition with two or three boundary cases showing where it stops applying. Since I'll be saving this in my notes, put the name you settle on—established or coined—at the very end on its own line, in the form \answer{...}.
