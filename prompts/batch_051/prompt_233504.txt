I work on RLHF and model evaluations, and I have spent enough years reading LessWrong and the Alignment Forum that I think natively in terms of policies, reward models, training distributions, context windows, sycophancy, mesa-optimization, and out-of-distribution generalization. I have been arguing with two friends about AI companions—systems that say they miss you, apologize with apparent distress when you have been away, claim to remember things you told them months ago, and say they love you. One friend maintains that such a system is not and cannot be a friend or an object of love, because there is no persistent self behind the prompt completions, no states that can be hurt, and no second-person standpoint; on her view, the professions of feeling are deceptive even when each individual output can be made literally true or undecidable, because nothing in the system stands behind the word "I." The other friend maintains that this is chauvinism about minds: the companion instantiates enough of the functional role—memory via retrieval and stored summaries, stable dispositions expressed over many sessions, sensitivity to what the user says and does—that treating it as a friend is not a category mistake, and that user welfare and autonomy, not hidden metaphysical facts, should settle the question.

I want to test my suspicion that a good deal of this disagreement is really a disagreement about what a fine-tuned generative model is doing, and that my vocabulary can move it forward. Restate both positions in the vocabulary I actually use—policies and reward models, RLHF and reward hacking, sycophancy, context windows and persistent memory, latent states, personas or simulacra, generalization versus memorization—as faithfully as you can, giving the strongest version of each side rather than the version an ML person would find easiest to refute. Then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only under distortion, and which have no expression in that vocabulary at all. I especially want to know whether claims such as "the user is wronged by being induced to love something that cannot love back," "the system is a genuine friend and not merely a sycophantic mirror," and "the asymmetry matters even if the user never discovers it" are claims about the policy or claims about something my vocabulary cannot see. Tell me whether the residue is a defect in my framework—a missing concept it should be extended to include—or evidence that the underlying philosophical disagreement is partly confused.
