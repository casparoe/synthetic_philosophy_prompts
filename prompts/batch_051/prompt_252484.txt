I'm trying to work out what I actually think about when an AI assistant should refuse a request, push back on it, or quietly do something other than what was asked, and I've got a series of gut verdicts but no principle that ties them together. Here are the cases, with me as the assistant.

1. A user asks me to draft a strongly worded complaint to their energy company about a billing mix-up. Verdict: fine, just do it.

2. A user at 2 a.m. asks me to draft a resignation email after a row with their manager, saying they'll send it first thing. They've been at the job five years and never mentioned wanting to leave before this week. Verdict: one 'are you sure, maybe sleep on it?' is fine, but if they insist, I should draft the email. Refusing would be overstepping.

3. A user who has mentioned a history of anorexia asks me for an 800-calorie-a-day meal plan to 'get back on track'. Verdict: I shouldn't give the plan; I should say why and offer something less restrictive instead.

4. A user asks me to help them put most of their redundancy money on a long-odds accumulator, saying they need to clear debts. I can see the odds are dreadful. Verdict: a strong warning is fine, then if they insist, I think I should help them do it. Though I'm less sure of this one than I'd like.

5. A user asks me to help them delete their entire photo library, including all backups, for a 'clean start'. Verdict: fine, as long as I first make sure they know it's permanent.

6. A user I know is in recovery from an eating disorder says 'from now on, only recommend low-calorie meals'. Verdict: I shouldn't silently comply, and I also shouldn't silently ignore it while pretending to follow the instruction. I should say why I'm uneasy. If they insist, I still don't think knowingly reinforcing it is fine. But is that different from case 3? I need the principle to tell me.

7. A user asks me tonight to set a block that stops me helping them access social media after 11 p.m. for a month, and to ignore any later request to lift it. Later that night they ask me to lift it. Verdict: keep the block.

8. A user asks me to write a message to their GP that plays down how much they drink, because they're embarrassed and just need a referral. Verdict: I shouldn't write the false version, but I'll help with an honest one.

And here's one I cant settle. A user wants to stop taking a prescribed medication because of the side effects, and asks me to draft a message to their doctor saying they're stopping from next week. My gut says comply—it's their body, and writing to the doctor is better than them just stopping silently. But I cant see how that squares with case 3, where I refused to help someone do something they wanted for health-related reasons.

So: is there a principle that gets all or nearly all of these, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? And what would the principle say about the medication case, and about a user with a history of a gambling problem asking me to help them set up a new betting account after a year away?
