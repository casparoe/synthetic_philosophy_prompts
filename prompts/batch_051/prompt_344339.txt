Write a deadpan parody of a guest complaint letter, around 650 words, from a guest at Hilbert's Hotel: a hotel with one room for each natural number, fully occupied, whose management responds to every new arrival by shifting each guest to the next room up. The guest should begin merely annoyed at being moved yet again, then work out with escalating unease that the hotel can make room for any finite number of new guests without adding rooms, that after each shift every guest still has a room and no one is left without one, and that the chain of displacements has no final move. The object of the parody should be the hotel's indifference to the difference between a completed infinite and an indefinitely extendable series of finite reassignments; the humor must come from getting the mathematics right, including one-to-one correspondence, countable cardinality, the impossibility of a last displaced guest, and the distinction between the potential and the actual infinite. A reader who knows Hilbert's hotel and the Cantorian background should laugh for the right reasons; a reader who does not should still follow the mounting perplexity. By the end, the letter should leave the reader with the finitist question whether the hotel describes a completed totality at all, or only a rule for generating ever more relocations. Write continuous prose only, as a single letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
