I sit on the program committee of a small foundation that funds conceptual work at the intersection of childhood, education, and political philosophy. We are about to commit three years of funding to a working group on whether the state may license or otherwise screen parents, and on the idea most often invoked to justify such proposals: the child's right to an open future, roughly the idea that parents and other authorities may not foreclose a child's future options in ways that leave the child unable to choose later among a sufficiently wide range of lives. The group can pursue only two or three connected questions, not a dozen, so we need to choose carefully, and I would like your help deciding which unresolved conceptual questions in this area deserve priority.

Below are candidate questions we have discussed, but you should not feel bound by the list. (a) Is the right best understood as protecting a range of valuable options, the child's developing capacity for autonomy, or something else, and how much turns on the answer for what parents may do? (b) Does respecting the right require parents and the state to maximize the number or diversity of a child's future options, or only to keep open some adequate threshold of options? (c) How should the view handle choices that are technically reversible but deeply identity-shaping or psychologically sticky, such as intensive early religious formation, elite athletic training, or certain medical and gender-related decisions? (d) Should the idea be extended from individual parents to structural conditions such as poverty, segregated schooling, or algorithmic recommendation systems that also shape children's options, and would that extension make the idea more powerful or less coherent? (e) What does the right actually require of the state — a licensing or screening regime, less intrusive oversight, or something else — and how should the line be drawn between protecting an open future and imposing a particular conception of the good?

For each question you would prioritize, I want to know why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for philosophers and legal theorists rather than empirical researchers, how neglected it is relative to the rest, and what a three-year group could realistically establish. Please defend the ordering rather than merely listing the questions: I am interested in the criteria and trade-offs, what you would deprioritize and why, and what evidence over the next year or two would move a question up or down the list. The deliverable is a defended research agenda, not a survey of the field.
