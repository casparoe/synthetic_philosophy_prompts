In my family group chat my uncle, who works for the interior ministry, ends every argument about the covid lockdowns and border closures with 'the safety of the people is the supreme law.' The way he uses it, once a court says a lockdown order wasn't properly authorized or overstept some legal limit, none of that matters: keeping people alive trumps legality, and legality-talk just misses the point. I'm sure that one-liner is packing several different claims, and I want to know which of them can actually do the work he's making it do. Lay out the precise theses 'the safety of the people is the supreme law' could amount to, from something boring like 'the legal system already contains emergency provisions' to something radical like 'in an emergency the executive just is the law,' and for each reading say whether it's trivially true, substantively true but needing argument, or outright false. Then flag which readings actually support his move from 'this measure was illegal' to 'that doesn't matter,' and which reading he probably means. I'd rather have a menu of readings than one interpretation defended.
