I have seen "geometric rationality" presented as a distinctive alternative to plain expected-value maximization: when a favorable opportunity repeatedly presents itself and winnings are reinvested, the rational stake is said to be the one that maximizes the expected logarithm of wealth. But I am puzzled about what this adds to standard expected utility theory. Is not maximizing expected log wealth simply expected utility maximization with a logarithmic utility function? If so, a Kelly bettor is just an expected-utility maximizer with logarithmic risk preferences, and calling this a separate norm of rationality looks like a relabeling. By the same token, a more risk-averse agent would seem equally rational in choosing a smaller, fractional-Kelly stake. What, if anything, is special about the logarithm here?
