I've been trying to get clear on what people actually mean by qualia, because in the rationalist circles I read the term gets stretched to cover everything from raw sensory feels to the non-functional residue of experience to whatever it is that philosophical zombies are said to lack. I would like a working definition of qualia that can do real classificatory work, and then to see that definition run against the following cases. I have chosen the cases so that some clearly fall under the term, some clearly do not, and several are deliberately awkward.

The cases are:

1. The visual experience of a red apple in normal lighting.
2. The painfulness of a headache.
3. A blindsight patient's forced-choice discrimination of a stimulus they report not seeing, at above-chance accuracy with no conscious awareness.
4. The difference between tasting coffee on your first cup and tasting the same coffee twenty years later after you have become a coffee drinker. I have in mind Dennett's pair of coffee tasters: one says the taste itself changed while the other says the taste is the same but her reaction to it changed, and introspection appears unable to decide between the two.
5. Seeing the duck-rabbit figure as a duck versus seeing it as a rabbit, where the stimulus is identical but the experience seems different.
6. The feeling of familiarity when you encounter someone you know, as distinct from whatever perceptual information allows you to recognise them.
7. Phantom limb pain, where there is no limb at all.
8. The experience of understanding a sentence in your native language versus hearing the same sounds as a stream of phonemes in a language you do not know.
9. What it is like to be a bat, if phenomenality is taken to include the character of echolocation experience that we cannot imagine.
10. The idea that there could be a being functionally identical to you but with no qualia at all, and the question of whether the term should exclude such a being's states.

A few constraints on the definition. It should not simply be 'whatever it is like to have an experience', because that gloss dissolves under the first two cases. It should also not identify qualia with the representation of stimulus properties, because that would classify the blindsight patient's visual processing as qualia even though the patient denies any conscious seeing. And it should not identify qualia with an introspectible difference, because the coffee-taster case is precisely one in which introspection is supposed to fail to distinguish two hypotheses. I want a definition that either gives a principled answer to all ten cases, or says explicitly which of the ten cases is misdescribed by the folk vocabulary and why the definition does not need to answer it.

Then tell me which of the ten cases your verdict is least secure about, and whether the definition you end up with leans towards Dennett's conclusion that qualia are an incoherent posit or towards the realist view that qualia are a legitimate explanatory target.
