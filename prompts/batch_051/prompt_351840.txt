Ok so I keep coming back to a parallel that I can't decide is real or just me pattern-matching. Hilbert's hotel has infinitely many rooms, all occupied, and it makes room for one new guest by moving the occupant of room 1 into room 2, room 2 into room 3, and so on. No new last room gets created; there just isn't one. But a dynamic array does almost the same thing when you insert an element at index 0: each existing element shifts from position i to position i+1, and slot 0 becomes free. The array version is just a boring O(n) operation, while the hotel version is supposed to show something deep about infinity. So my question is whether this is a genuine structural identity or a loose analogy. If it's genuine, what should transfer from one case to the other? Does the array case show that Hilbert's hotel is doing something more innocent than it looks, or does the hotel case show that "full" works differently for infinite collections in a way arrays never capture? Also, would a finitist be entitled to say that the hotel paradox just smuggles in an infinite carrying-out of the shift operation, and that the array comparison makes that objection more natural? I want you to be exact about where the mapping holds and where it comes apart, say how confident you are in each claim, and single out the point you are least sure of.
