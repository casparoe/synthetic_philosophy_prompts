I've been going back and forth with a friend about Peirce and James, and I'd like a decomposition rather than a bare number. The passage I keep returning to is from C. S. Peirce's 1878 paper "How to Make Our Ideas Clear." After he analyzes belief as a rule of action, he writes:

The essence of belief is the establishment of a habit, and different beliefs are distinguished by the different modes of action to which they give rise. If beliefs do not differ in this respect, if they appease the same doubt by producing the same rule of action, then no mere differences in the manner of consciousness of them can make them different beliefs, any more than playing a tune in different keys is playing different tunes.

I read this as a claim about the identity conditions of belief: two beliefs are the same belief if they issue in the same practical upshot. My friend reads it as the engine for a full Jamesian pragmatist theory of truth, on which truth is not a static relation between a proposition and the world but something that happens to an idea as it proves itself useful, so that a belief can become true by working. My credence that the Jamesian view is correct as a theory of truth, not merely as a method for clarifying meaning, is around 0.35; his is around 0.7.

Please give me your own credence in the same proposition: that the pragmatist theory of truth in roughly James's sense—that a proposition's truth consists in the practical consequences of believing it, such that usefulness can constitute truth rather than merely indicate it—is correct when read as a theory of truth. Then decompose your number. I particularly want to see terms for:

- whether Peirce's habit-of-action principle is best read as semantic, epistemic, or methodological;
- whether James's extension is a fair development of Peirce or a distortion;
- whether the practical upshot of a belief can individuate its content without defining truth;
- whether useful false beliefs and useless true beliefs are fatal to the Jamesian view;
- any other factor you think carries significant weight.

For each term, give a rough probability and a line of justification, then show how they combine. Finally, tell me which subquestion your final credence is most sensitive to, and say whether the 0.35/0.7 disagreement is more likely located in our readings of Peirce or in our background theories of truth.
