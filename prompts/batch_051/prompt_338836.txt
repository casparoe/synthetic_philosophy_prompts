I've been turning something over since a seminar last week, and I'd like a second opinion. I've loved Frege's work for years — the sense/reference distinction, the logicist project, the idea that the semantic value of a sentence is its truth-value — and I'd always pictured him as a dry, purely technical thinker. Then someone mentioned that late in life he kept a private political diary, and the excerpts are ugly: antisemitic rants, contempt for parliamentary democracy, hope for an authoritarian strongman. What stopped me short wasn't just the content, it was my own reaction. My first impulse was to quarantine it: the logic is pure, the diary is a sad private artifact, and anyway he was old and unwell.

But I didn't quarantine Heidegger the same way. When I first learned about his Nazism I was quick to say it has to matter, that you have to read the politics into the philosophy. So I've caught myself running an asymmetry: Frege's politics get filed under biography, ignore, while Heidegger's politics get treated as philosophically relevant. Is there anything that actually justifies that? Maybe something about the subject matter (formal semantics versus existential phenomenology), or about how deeply each man's politics enters his actual doctrines? Or is it just favoritism toward the one whose results I like, and suspicion of the one whose style I already found murky? I want a real verdict, not a both-sides shrug. Tell me whether I should keep the asymmetry, give it up, or revise it into something defensible. Keep the reply under 350 words.
