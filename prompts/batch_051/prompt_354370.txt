Okay, here's one I keep going back and forth on. In hinge epistemology the starting point is something like Wittgenstein's On Certainty: there are these bedrock certainties — the world is more than five minutes old, I have hands, other people aren't all automata, that kind of thing — that ordinary doubting and justifying never seems to touch. Not because we've verified them, but because they're the hinges the whole practice of checking turns on.

The narrow thing I want a number on is whether those hinge commitments are beliefs. Like, are they a weird subclass of belief — held unshakeably and without evidence, but still beleifs — or are they a different kind of attitude altogether: non-propositional animal certainty, rules of action, or the scaffolding that makes believing possible in the first place?

Give me your actual credence that hinges are NOT beliefs at all (the non-doxastic view) rather than a special class of belief that just doesn't need justification. Then decompose it: how much of your credence rides on what theory of belief you go with (say representational vs. dispositional/functional), how much on whether hinges are even truth-evaluable, how much on whether a state can be immune to doubt and ungrounded yet still count as an ordinary belief, plus whatever other terms you'd add. Put a rough number on each sub-question, show how they combine, and tell me which one would move the final number most if you changed it — and what would shift that sub-question for you.
