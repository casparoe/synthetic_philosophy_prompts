Hey, I'm a software engineer with basicly no phil background, but I fell down a Nomic rabbit hole last year — the game where changing the rules is a legal move in the game. I got asked to give the opening ten-minute talk at a local "rules and systems" meetup. The audience is mixed: maybe half programmers and board-game people, two or three lawyers who do actual regulatory/governance work, and a bunch of friends who've never heard of Nomic.

I want the talk to be about the vertigo you feel once you notice that in Nomic the rules that say how to change rules are themselves changeable rules. So a proposal can amend the very rule that says how proposals get adopted, and then asking "was that proposal validly adopted?" starts to loop, because the rule you'd use to answer is the one the proposal just changed. A rule you glanced at five minutes ago might be gone, and the rule that took it out is itself safe only until somebody takes it out. I'd like one or two concrete examples from the game (the classic Rule 101 / immutable-rules setup is fine, but feel free to invent a cleaner scenario), and I want it to land as an actual speech, not a lecture or a blog post.

The hard part: the lawyers will notice if I talk sloppy about self-reference or make it sound like "a rule about changing rules" is the same as a contradiction, so the speech has to keep that distinction clean. But at the same time the friends who've never played shouldn't need to know what self-reference even is to follow it. Please write the talk, roughly five minutes / 500-ish words. And don't end by resolving it into a tidy lesson like "all systems need a constitution"; I'd rather leave people a little unsettled.
