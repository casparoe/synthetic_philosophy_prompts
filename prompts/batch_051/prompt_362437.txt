In my reporting I keep encountering the phrase "raw data" used as if it named something that lies beneath all interpretation, and I have begun to suspect that data science is unconsciously refighting an old epistemological battle. Wilfrid Sellars attacked what he called the myth of the given: the idea that there is a layer of immediate, pre-conceptual experience that is simply presented to the mind and can serve as an independent foundation justifying every other belief. His objection was that nothing can play both roles at once. A state that is brute and unstructured enough to be "given" cannot also stand in rational or inferential relations to a belief, while anything capable of justifying a belief is already conceptually shaped and steeped in prior knowledge. The popular notion of raw data appears to rest on the same two-role picture: a stratum of facts received directly from the world, prior to interpretation, that grounds empirical conclusions. In practice, of course, a dataset arrives only after someone has decided what to measure, how to encode it, what to discard, and what counts as an error, so the supposedly neutral foundation is already processed and theory-laden — just as, for Sellars, the supposedly given element of experience is already conceptual.

I want to know whether this is a deep structural parallel or only a loose analogy, and I would like the comparison made precise rather than gestural. Lay out the mapping between the two cases, then say where the structures match and where they come apart. In particular: is the justificatory role of the given in epistemology the same kind of relation as the grounding role that raw data is supposed to play in empirical analysis, or does the analogy break because data are artifacts of instruments and institutional choices while Sellars's target concerns perception, normativity, and the authority of experience? And can you identify something the parallel would predict that turns out false on one side or the other — the kind of result that would show the resemblance is only verbal?
