Ok here's a variant on Plato's cave I've been turning over and I can't tell if its a dumb objection or if it actually does damage to the story. Take the standard setup: prisoners chained facing the wall since birth, only seeing shadows cast by the fire and the puppets. Now change one thing. Suppose the shadows aren't just flickery second-hand images; they form a closed, law-governed system. Every shadow is a deterministic function of the earlier shadows, and the prisoners have worked out the full dynamics. They can predict each new shadow to the minute, and they've built a shadow-clock and a shadow-calendar. One prisoner gets loose and goes up to see the puppets, the fire, and the daylight world. When he comes back, he can't predict the shadows any better than the ones who stayed behind. His 'higher' knowledge gives him causes and an origin story, but no predictive edge. Question: in this variant, does the escaped prisoner know something the others lack in the way the allegory needs him to, or does he just have a causally deeper representation with no epistemic advantage? Work through what Plato's story has to be committed to—about why the Forms beat the shadows of the cave—for the original point to survive, and say whether a predictively complete shadow-world breaks that or leaves it alone. I keep going back and forth on whether the original quietly assumes the shadows are a crummy, lossy image of reality, and this variant feels like it tests exactly that.
