Write a short dialogue between two scholars arguing about a local tradition. The tradition says a Bronze Age mound by the river is the grave of a king who tamed the river, and that he was later worshipped as the river-god. One takes roughly the euhemerist line: the story is a distorted memory of a real local ruler that got inflated into a god over centuries. The other thinks the biography is a later rationalization, and the real core is the personified annual flood; the grave is just an old mound the story got attached to. Have them argue over a few specific pieces of evidence—say, the burial goods (a bronze scepter and a child buried with a river-smoothed stone), the fact that the king's name and the river's name share an old root, and the fact that the king's name doesnt show up in any inscription until centuries after the mound was built. Let each respond to the other's actual points, and neither side should be a strawman.
