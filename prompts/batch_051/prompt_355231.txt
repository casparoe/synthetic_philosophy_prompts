I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy. I have been trying to settle a question that keeps coming up when I read about artificial intelligence: has transformative AI already happened? My trouble is not a lack of opinions about particular cases; it is that I cannot tell whether my case-by-case judgments add up to anything coherent. I would like you to extract the principle behind them, test it, and repair it if necessary.

Please act as an expert in conceptual analysis and the philosophy of technology, someone who is good at turning messy case intuitions into precise criteria. Take your time, and begin by brainstorming several candidate distinctions before committing to one. I care more about an operationalizable principle and an honest account of which of my verdicts it cannot keep than I care about being handed a label for the present moment.

Here are my verdicts on concrete cases.

1. Web search and the web, roughly 1995 to 2005. I think this change was civilization-scale: it altered how nearly everyone acquires information, does some kinds of work, and coordinates with others. But I do not think it counts as transformative AI. The transformation came from networks, browsers, and the digitized corpus; machine learning improved ranking and advertising on top of that, but it did not drive the transition.

2. Smartphones, app stores, and algorithmic feeds, roughly 2007 to 2015. Again enormous in daily life, but I attribute the change to connectivity and form factor, not to AI. Not transformative AI.

3. Current large language models and coding assistants, the 2020s. They have materially changed how I write, debug, and learn, and they look general-purpose. Across the economy I see rapid diffusion, but I do not see a durable, aggregate step-change in growth or in the rate of knowledge production. My verdict: an early phase, maybe, but not yet transformative AI.

4. AlphaFold-style systems in science. A genuine landmark for protein structure prediction and for one style of scientific problem. But one fast discipline does not make a transformed civilization. Not transformative AI.

5. Machine translation at roughly human level. I use it constantly and it removes a real communication barrier. Still, I file it under the same underlying software-and-computation transformation rather than as its own transition. Not transformative AI.

6. Electricity, as a control case. This is my paradigm of a transformative technology: after a lag, it changed where and when work happens, what can be produced, and enabled whole new industries. Not AI, but the benchmark that any definition must accommodate.

7. A hypothetical automated scientist. Imagine an AI system that can propose, run, and interpret experiments across many fields, and in doing so durably doubles the rate of technological progress. My verdict: yes, transformative AI, even if there is no single dramatic consumer product.

8. A hypothetical superhuman narrow optimizer. Imagine an AI superhuman at container routing or supply-chain optimization that cuts global logistics costs by 30 percent. My verdict: a big deal, but not transformative AI, because its scope is one sector and it does not change the rate at which we acquire knowledge.

I am aware that cases 1 and 3 look similar from the outside: both are software systems with machine-learning components that quickly changed how billions of people do something. Yet I treat case 1 as a completed transformation and case 3 as not yet arrived. That is exactly the sort of tension I want you to force me on.

I have been carrying around a tentative definition: transformative AI is an AI system that is general-purpose in the way electricity or computing are general-purpose, and that causes a durable, large acceleration in the rate of progress or knowledge, of the order associated with the Agricultural or Industrial Revolution. This definition fits most of my verdicts: case 3 is general-purpose but arguably not yet a cause of such acceleration; case 7 clearly satisfies it; case 8 clearly fails on scope; cases 1 and 2 fail because the AI was not the driver of the transformation. But I distrust the definition precisely because it fits so easily. It may be a ruler I have secretly cut to my own judgments.

Please do four things. First, say whether there is a coherent principle that captures all or nearly all of my verdicts without being gerrymandered to each case; if there is not, tell me which verdict or verdicts I should give up. Second, state the principle precisely enough to be operational: scope, mechanism, threshold, and how one would in principle tell whether the conditions have been met. Third, test the principle against cases I have not listed, such as recommendation systems, speech recognition, autonomous vehicles, and AI for scientific discovery in general. Fourth, return to my original question: given the resulting principle, what is the most honest answer to whether transformative AI has already happened, and should that answer be yes, no, we are inside the transition, or is the question itself ill-posed?
