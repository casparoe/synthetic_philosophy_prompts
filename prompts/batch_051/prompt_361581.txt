(1) Knowledge is closed under known single-premise entailment: if I know P and know that P entails Q, I can come to know Q by competently deducing it.
(2) Logical omniscience is false for bounded agents like us: nobody actually knows more than a small slice of the logical consequences of what they know.
(3) The standard possible-worlds semantics for knowledge is still the right framework, because its logical-omniscience idealization is a harmless simplification aimed at ideally rational agents.
(4) An account of knowledge that is true only of ideal agents and false of us can't explain why its norms bind actual reasoners.
(5) Multi-premise closure fails: you can know each premise of a long deductive argument individually without being in a position to know their conjunction, because the small chance of error in each premise accumulates.
(6) Multi-premise closure and single-premise closure are really the same requirement; running the single-premise step once for each premise should land you on the conclusion.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look that way, and rank the genuine tensions by how severe they are. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision is, then say which single revision would do the most cleanup.
