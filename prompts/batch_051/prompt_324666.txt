Please argue that Pascals view in the Pensees about justice and force (the idea that justice needs force to be obeyed, force needs justice to not be tyranny, and the solution is to weld the two together) secretly makes justice redundant: once force has done its legitimating work, "justice" explains nothing extra, and nobody can tell whether they're obeying a custom because it's just or simply because it's backed. To force the issue, imagine a border island where two groups have contradictory inheritance customs about shipwrecked cargo (one says you must return it, the other says the sea gifts it to whoever finds it first), both customs feel equally natural to their own group, and one ruler has enough force to impose either. Spell out what this position would let the ruler do, and then say whether calling the outcome just adds anything over calling it successfully enforced. If you think Pascal can dodge this, give his strongest possible reply; if not, say why.
