I'm a PhD student in psychology, and I've been reading Peirce's "The Fixation of Belief" for a seminar. He writes: "The object of reasoning is to find out, from the consideration of what we already know, something else which we do not know." That seems obviously right. But I'm confused about what my own field presupposes. We now have registered reports, where journals accept a study before the results are in, based on the question and method, and commit to publish regardless of the outcome. That looks like it treats reasoning as aimed at finding out something we don't already know. But at the same time, editors at the same journals still desk-reject null results and treat p < .05 as the main criteria for a study being "successful". So the practice seems to presuppose two different views of what reasoning is for. My question: what philosophical commitments about belief and inquiry does this mixed practice presuppose? Is it coherent, or does it quietly hold both Peirce's view and a different one? And what would have to change in editorial practice if we replaced it with the view in the quote?
