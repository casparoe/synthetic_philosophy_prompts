I've got a habit in arguments about whether we should try to radically extend human lifespan, and I want to know if it's defensible. Whenever someone argues that death is good or that life extension would be bad — that mortality gives life meaning, that endless life would be boring, that wanting much more time is greedy or childish — I check whether they themselves are going to die. If they are, I discount the argument to nearly zero. My logic is that they can't have the thing, so they have every incentive to conclude it isn't worth having; it's sour grapes, and what gets called deathism is just a lot of people making the same move at once, which makes it feel like wisdom. My friend says I've got it backwards: the people stuck with death are exactly the ones who've done the hard work of making peace with it, so their arguments deserve more weight, not less, and anyway the truth of an argument doesn't depend on the motives of the person giving it. I cant tell whether my filter is catching motivated reasoning or just letting me dismiss any view I don't like. Under what conditions would a shortcut like this be rational, and is there a version of it that keeps the good part without my friend's objection?
