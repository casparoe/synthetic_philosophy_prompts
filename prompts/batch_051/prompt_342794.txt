i'm a novelist working on a book set in an alpine border town. An avalanche closes the pass and strands two groups above the tree line: a bus of tourists who are citizens of the town's own country, and a slightly larger group of undocumented migrants traveling on foot. The town has one working winch and a few snowmobiles, and a storm coming. I keep rewriting the rescue because I don't actually know what my characters think about whether their compatriots have special claims on them — and they shouldn't all think the same thing, that's kind of the book. So I want to sort them (and maybe myself) properly.

Could you make me a sorting quiz — the kind where someone answers concrete case questions and a scoring key tells them which position they actually hold, not the kind where they pick the label they like — for positions on whether compatriots have special claims over everyone else in need?

A few constraints:

- 8–10 questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options. No question may just ask what label someone prefers or whether they'd call themselves a nationalist or cosmopolitan. Sort people by what they say about cases, not by what they call themselves.
- Build the questions from the logical space first, around the splits I actually care about: (i) whether shared nationality gives any reason at all on it's own; (ii) if it does, whether the reason is grounded in identity/culture/love or in shared institutions/reciprocity/coercion; (iii) whether whatever priority exists survives a big disparity in need; (iv) whether local priority is just an efficiency heuristic or a real division of moral labor.
- Use at least these three cases: the avalanche scene above; a town council vote on the last social-housing unit between a lifelong local family and a newly arrived refugee family; and a doctor deciding whether to bend a clinic's cutoff by one day so a compatriot gets a scarce treatment before a foreigner whose need is slightly more urgent.
- The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these five leaves: strong associative nationalism, liberal/reciprocity patriotism, impartial cosmopolitanism, division-of-labor cosmopolitanism, and a rectification view (where the only specifically political special claim is to repair harm your own state or town has done, so it sometimes points outward rather than inward).
- For each leaf give its one-sentence awkward commitment. If some answer combinations are empty, mark them as empty and say in a sentence whether the combination looks incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag any positions your questions can't cleanly separate rather than pretending they split.
- Some questions should force a choice where the cosmopolitan answer costs the character something real, because that's where people's actual view shows up.
- Keep it usable by someone who's never read this philosophy. No need to name authors or movements; plain-language leaf names are fine.
