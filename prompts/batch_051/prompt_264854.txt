I keep trying to figure out what makes a statement racist, and my gut gives me these verdicts. A politician who says "those neighborhoods just breed crime" is being racist, even though no race is mentioned—it's obviously a dog whistle. A well-meaning supervisor who tells a Black employee "you're so articulate"—I want to call that racist too, or at least racially loaded, despite the good intent. Someone on a date who says "I don't really see color" isn't racist exactly, but it bugs me. A Black comic drops the n-word on stage with a mostly Black audience—not racist in my book, though if a white guy in the crowd said it I wouldnt hesitate to call it racist. And a guy at a party cites a true crime statistic broken down by race with no commentary—I don't think the statement itself is racist, even if it ends up making people more scared of Black men. Is there one principle that gets all of these, or do I have to abandon one of my verdicts? And where does that principle land on the case I keep going back and forth on: a landlord tells a Black applicant "this neighborhood might not be a comfortable fit for you" and genuinely means well. When you answer, try to separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who study this, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
