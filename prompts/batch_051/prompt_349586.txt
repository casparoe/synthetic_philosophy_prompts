I've been arguing with a friend about human enhancement and I keep ending up in the same place. His view, as best I can reconstruct it, is that the therapy/enhancement distinction is just status quo bias with a medical license. He points out that medicine already moves people around on whatever scale it can: vaccines let a child's body do things it couldn't do 'naturally,' glasses get plenty of people to 20/20 or better, SSRI's adjust mood, and if you look at the history of any of these, each was once treated as an unnatural intervention until it became routine. So the boundary between 'restoring health' and 'improving a healthy person' needs some reference point -- normal functioning, species-typical functioning, evolutionary fitness, human nature -- and every candidate either shifts with time and culture or is morally arbitrary. If I'm okay with a cochlear implant for someone who's deaf, he says, I've got no principled objection to giving a hearing person echolocation or infrared vision; the difference is just that I grew up thinking one counted as a cure and the other as a luxury. I have a genuinely hard time finding where his argument goes wrong.

And yet I also can't shake a very strong intuition that there's a difference between giving a kid Ritalin because he has severe ADHD and giving a healthy kid the same pill to grind through the SATs, and that the second is wrong in a way the first is not. Not only because of fairness or consent -- my unease survives even when I imagine a world where everyone has access to the enhancement -- but because it feels like the difference between helping someone be who they are and turning them into someone else. I don't want to know which side wins. I want to know what it would cost to keep the intuition and give up or amend the theory, what it would cost to keep the theory and debunk the intuition as status quo bias or an expression of a value I should not trust, and whether there's a version of the therapy/enhancement distinction that does real moral work without collapsing into 'normal is whatever we happen to be used to.'
