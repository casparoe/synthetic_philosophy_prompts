Here are my verdicts. (1) If two fields planted with the same mixed seed corn are grown in rich and poor soil, and the rich-soil field ends up taller, I'd call that height difference between fields environmental — even though height within each field is partly genetic. (2) If a lab population of mice were all clones of one another and raised in varied environments, I'd expect the heritability of body weight in that population to be zero, even though body weight in ordinary mice is clearly influenced by genes. (3) In the mirror case, if a population shared effectively identical environments, I'd expect heritability to climb toward 100%, which makes me think heritability is as much a fact about the spread of environments as about genes. (4) When a study says IQ is 60% heritable among, say, middle-class American kids, I don't think that licenses any conclusion about why one country's average score is lower than another's. (5) A child born to Spanish-speaking parents and adopted at birth by English speakers will end up speaking English, so I say "language spoken" is about as close to zero heritability as it gets, even though children usually share a language with their biological parents. (6) If someone inherits the Huntington's disease mutation, I want to say their eventually developing it is genetic in an individual-level way that no heritability figure quite captures. (7) I've also read that a trait can be highly heritable and still be eliminated by an environmental intervention nobody in the studied population experienced — like the special diet for PKU — so heritability seems to set no ceiling on what changing the environment could do.

The two verdicts that leave me stuck are these. I accept the textbook-sounding claim that within-group heritability is silent about between-group differences. But I also feel that if a trait is highly heritable in every population studied, it's more reasonable than not to think some part of a between-population difference could be genetic — not that the heritability studies alone prove it, just that they nudge my prior. Those two feel to me like they're in tension, and I can't tell whether that's a real conflict in my views or just a confusion about what "silent" means.
