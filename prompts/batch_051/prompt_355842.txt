Lets do a variation on the tragedy of the commons, Age-of-Em style. The classic setup: a bunch of herders share a pasture, each one gets the full private benefit of grazing one more cow but only bears his own share of the overgrazing cost, so the pasture gets overgrazed even though everyone can see it coming. The standard fixes—private property, quotas with actual enforcement, or bottom-up self-governance—are all just ways of forcing each herder to internalize the congestion cost he pushes onto everyone else.

Now move it into a world of brain emulations. The pasture becomes a fixed pool of compute, and the cows become running ems. Take m original templates, each of which can spawn extra copies of itself basically for free. All copies run at the same time and an extra em slows every other em down a little by consuming shared hardware. To make the private incentive to spawn concrete, suppose each running copy produces average output f(N) = A - cN per day when N ems are running, and all of a copy's output is credited back to the template it came from. So a template running k copies gets total payoff k(A - cN), where N is the total population across all templates.

The thing I want you to pick apart is how the old private-vs-social divergence changes when the extra grazer isn't a separate person but a fork of the same template. My first-pass thought is that the classical analysis almost carries over, with templates as herders and copies as cows: each template internalizes the slowdown to its own copies via the ck term but not the slowdown to other templates' copies. But I don't trust that reading.

Run the setup under at least two rival assumptions and say which survives: (i) each copy is a distinct welfare-bearing agent acting on its own account; (ii) the template-level utility over all its copies is the right unit of analysis, so the copies are less like distinct herders and more like arms of one agent; and (iii) if you think there's a third position, spell it out.

Write the payoff structure for each case and point to the exact term where the classical divergence remains or disappears. Then say whether the commons still gets overgrazed, which of the standard remedies (private property, quotas, self-governance) still work, and whether any new failure mode shows up that the original pasture story didn't have.

Last thing: I keep flip-flopping on the copy-as-separate-person question, so tell me what would change your verdict—which argument or consideration would flip you the other way, and where in your own reasoning its most likely to break.
