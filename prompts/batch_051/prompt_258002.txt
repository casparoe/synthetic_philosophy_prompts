A friend sent me a passage from a popular book on probability and everyday life. The author summarizes the birthday paradox as follows: "In a room of 23 people, the chance is better than even that two of them share a birthday. It's a paradox because 23 is such a small fraction of 365. The math works because each extra person adds many new possible pairings, and each pairing is a fresh chance for a match. The general lesson is that coincidences are far more common than intuition suggests: wherever there are many ways for something surprising to line up, some striking match is almost inevitable. This is why we should distrust stories of uncanny coincidence. When someone tells you one, the rational default — licensed by the birthday paradox — is that the event was probably chance rather than anything meaningful. The very feature that makes the anecdote feel special is what makes it unsurprising."
