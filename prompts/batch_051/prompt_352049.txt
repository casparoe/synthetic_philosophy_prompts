You are a professional philosopher of music, specializing in aesthetics and the ontology of musical works. Think carefully before answering, and where the criteria below pull in different directions, say so explicitly rather than forcing a clean verdict.

Here are the criteria I was taught for when a live sound event is a performance of a musical work W:

(1) Intentional presentation: the event is deliberately staged and offered to an audience as a musical event, not mere background sound, accidental noise, or an overheard rehearsal.

(2) Work-directed intention: the agent or agents producing the event intend to realize W itself, not merely to quote it, imitate it, or treat it as raw material for a different piece.

(3) Adequate conformity: the resulting sounds retain enough of W's identifying features — melody, harmony, rhythm, instrumentation, lyrics, or whatever serves to individuate W — that a reasonably informed listener could recognize W in the event.

(4) Live agential control: the agents exercise real-time, causally significant control over the sounding result, so that the event's particular musical features are attributable to what they are doing during the performance, rather than to the playback of a previously finished recording.

Now consider this case. An electronic musician records a nine-track album called Lattice, a made-up title. Each track exists only as a studio recording assembled from many separately recorded layers: individual vocal takes, drum parts, bass and synth lines, and field recordings. There is no score and no canonical live version. On tour, the musician performs the album alone behind a laptop. For each track, she loads its stored layers and, in real time, triggers and stops them, loops short slices, pitch-shifts and time-stretches them, filters them, and resequences them. In two tracks she sings a new counter-melody over the manipulated layers; in one track she deletes the recorded chords entirely and plays new chords over the retained vocal fragments. The event is billed as a live performance of Lattice, and the audience recognizes each track by its title and central melodic fragment, but no track is heard the way it sounds on the album.

Does each of the nine treatments count as a performance of the corresponding track? Does the evening count as a performance of the album Lattice as a work? Work through the four criteria one by one, note where they underdetermine the verdict, and say whether the case shows that one or more criteria need revision — for example, by weakening the demand for live agential control, by individuating the work through the album recording rather than through a score, or by treating the laptop set as a hybrid practice between performance, remix, and playback.
