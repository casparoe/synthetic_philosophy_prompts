I'm trying to get the landscape straight in the psycopathy-and-moral-knowledge debate, the "do they know that what they do is wrong" question. The positions I've already got are:

(1) The deficit view: psychopaths fail the moral/conventional distinction (they treat "don't hit people" like "don't wear pajamas to school"), so they never really acquire moral concepts in the first place, hence no moral knowledge.

(2) The knows-but-doesn't-care view: the moral knowledge is fully intact; what's broken is the link from judgment to motivation, which is exactly why psychopaths get used as counterexamples to motivational internalism.

(3) Hare's inverted-commas line: they aren't making genuine moral judgments at all; they use "wrong" to mean something like "what people around here call wrong," so there was never any moral knowledge to have.

(4) The shallow-knowledge view: they can state the moral facts the way I can state that a movie was sad when I fell asleep, but they lack the deeper evaluative grasp — what "wrong" is doing, why it matters — that real moral knowledge requires.

I have a feeling there's at least one more genuinely distinct position out there, and I'm not interested in someone relabeling these four or combining two of them. Give me one that answers the "which capacity is intact and which is broken" question differently from all four. Then tell me honestly whether it's any good: does it carve off cases the other four mishandle, or does it collapse back into one of them once you press it?
