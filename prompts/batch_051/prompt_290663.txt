I manage the moderation team for a large UK-based online forum whose community standards require us to remove racist content and, in serious cases, to suspend accounts. We review roughly two hundred flagged posts a day; each moderator has less than ten minutes per post, most have no training in philosophy, and every decision must be documented and be defensible on appeal. We are caught between two intuitive rules — that a post is racist because the speaker intended it, and that it is racist because of its effect on the group concerned — and neither alone captures how we actually treat coded, deniable, or 'ironic' speech. Please write a standing decision procedure my moderators can follow when asked whether a post counts as racist for moderation purposes: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no and clears the post without further inquiry, where it marks a post as actionable even though no racist intent can be shown, and where it must hand the decision to a senior human because the question turns on values or interpretative judgement rather than anything a checklist can settle. The procedure should draw on the debate about intent, effect, and dog whistles, and should say explicitly which distinctions must survive translation into a checklist and which can be collapsed. Then apply it to three posts: one containing a crude racial slur whose author insists the word is being used 'ironically'; one containing a term that functions as a known dog whistle but which also has an innocent literal reading that the author expressly invokes when challenged; and one containing no slur or coded language but which a complainant says creates a hostile environment for members of a particular ethnic group. Finally, say what the procedure cannot do and where it will predictably misfire, without pretending that the hardest judgements can be eliminated.
