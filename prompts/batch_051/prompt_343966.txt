I spent much of last night arguing with a friend about anthropic reasoning, and we could not settle what happens to the presumptuous philosopher argument when the background cosmology stops looking like the tidy finite case in which the argument is usually presented. In the familiar version, two fundamental theories are observationally equivalent except that one predicts a vastly larger total number of observers than the other. The self-indication assumption then treats your own existence as evidence for the more populated theory, so the ratio of the two totals should multiply the more populated theory's probability; the presumptuous philosopher accepts this and ends up massively favoring the larger-population theory, which many people regard as a reductio of the assumption. That move appears to rely on the totals being finite and the ratio being well defined. Now suppose the live cosmological picture is one in which the universe is spatially infinite, or at least the number of observers is unbounded in a way that undercuts finite totals, as eternal-inflation-style models suggest. On the standard idealization, both theories can then imply infinitely many observers, and the plain comparison in terms of a finite multiple of total observers no longer carries literal ratio content. It can be made precise only relative to a cutoff, a measure, or some finite reference class, and different defensible choices can point in different directions. My friend kept insisting the problem simply dissolves once both totals are infinite, because the ratio disappears; I suspected the opposite, that the argument reappears in a worse, measure-relative form. We could not decide.

Please take the role of an expert philosopher working on formal epistemology and anthropic reasoning. First lay out the original argument step by step, making explicit which premise the infinite case removes or damages. Then work through what the self-indication assumption can even say when both total counts are infinite: does it go silent, does it turn into a demand to supply a measure, or does it remain coherent but become hostage to an arbitrary regularization? Is the right repair to restrict the reasoning to the finite observable region or to a carefully defined reference class, and does that repair preserve the presumptuous philosopher's conclusion or dissolve it? Think carefully, brainstorm two or three candidate repairs before committing, and write your answer in American English.
