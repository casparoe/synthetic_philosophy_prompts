I was taught that collusion needs three things: a shared understanding or agreement, an intention to hold down competition, and some reciprocal disciplining mechanism—monitoring and punishing defectors. But through trial and error each learns that high prices are met with high prices, and if one undercuts, the other quickly undercuts back and they both bleed until prices climb again. After a while they settle into nearly identical, stablely high prices. Is that collusion? Go criterion by criterion; where the case fails one, say whether that's the criterion's fault or the case's. And tell me whether "intention" is doing all the real work here.
