I'm a novelist, and I've been trying to nail down the line between manipulation and legitimate persuasion for a pair of characters, because I keep writing scenes where I can't tell wether one of them is doing something wrong. I've more or less settled on a theory I heard on a philosophy podcast a while back: manipulation is influence that gets around a person's ability to decide for themselves—hidden pressure, stacked choices, manufactured urgency, playing on fears or insecurities they can't clearly see—whereas persuasion is influence that puts real considerations in front of them and lets them weigh those considerations. The theory is clean and it gives me a test I can actually run on a scene, which is why I dont want to give it up lightly. But I've hit a case that won't fit. In the novel I'm drafting, a mother is trying to stop her adult son from taking a job overseas. She doesn't lie, she doesn't hide his options, she doesn't threaten him. She sits him down and, completely openly, tells him how much it will hurt her, how badly she'll sleep, how she gave up years of her life for him—and all of it is true. My gut says that's manipulation, full stop. But by the theory, she's just handing him reasons (emotional reasons, but still reasons) and letting him decide, so it ought to come out legitimate. I can't shake the gut. So which one should give? I want a walkthrough of what each move would require—what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the theory to be amended or abandoned—and whether there's a version of the theory that keeps the clean test while still counting the mother's move as manipulation.
