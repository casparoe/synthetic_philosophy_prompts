I've been working through Karl Popper's The Logic of Scientific Discovery on my own, not for any course, and I keep getting stuck on a line of thought that seems to follow from what he says but ends somewhere absurd. Early in the book he draws a distinction between strictly universal statements, like all swans are white, which a single black swan could refute but no finite number of white swans could conclusively verify, and strictly existential statements, like there exists somewhere a black swan, which a single black swan could verify but no finite amount of searching could refute. He says that science must be built on statements that can be refuted by experience, and he treats strictly existential statements as unscientific because they are not falsifiable.

Here is the argument that bothers me:

(1) If Popper is right about demarcation, a statement is scientific only if it is falsifiable.
(2) Popper himself says that strictly existential statements are not falsifiable, because no finite set of observations can show that no instance exists anywhere.
(3) So any strictly existential statement is not falsifiable.
(4) Therefore, by (1), any strictly existential statement is unscientific.
(5) But this conclusion is too strong. Scientists seem to make existential claims all the time: that there is a planet beyond Uranus (Neptune was sought on that basis), that there is an element with atomic number 119 waiting to be synthesized, or that two species share a common ancestor. If these are all unscientific, then a great deal of ordinary scientific practice is being ruled out by a criterion that was supposed to capture it.
(6) So either Popper's criterion is wrong, or there is a way to let these existential claims be scientific after all.

I want to keep (1), or at least keep the idea that falsifiability is doing real work. But I don't see where the argument goes wrong. If I have to choose, I'd rather give up (5) than (1), but giving up (5) seems too costly. Where does this argument fail, if it does? And what exactly would I be paying if I kept the premise you identify as the one to reject?
