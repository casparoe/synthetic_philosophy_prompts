Me and my brother have been argueing for months about what you should do when you learn something bad about your own judgment, and I want the joint statement the two of us could actually both sign. I'm on what you could call the go-with-your-best-judgment side: if I've worked through a question myself and it really looks to me like P, then learning I'm tired, angry, or that a smart person disagrees doesn't by itself make P less likely — I should change my mind only if I find a concrete mistake. He's on what you could call the discount-the-compromised-head side: he says higher-order facts about your own judgment are still evidence, and ignoring them is epistemic akrasia, like a drunk person who insists he's fine to drive because he can't spot anything wrong with his driving. Write the document we'd need: a numbered list of claims we both accept (about the cases, about what counts as evidence, about what would settle it), then a disagreements table with each of our views written in wording each of us would accept for ourselves. Then the cruxes, the sub-questions where settling them would actually move one of us, and for each crux one concrete case we'd both agree bears on it plus what each of us predicts. And flag whatever you think has just been a fight about words.
