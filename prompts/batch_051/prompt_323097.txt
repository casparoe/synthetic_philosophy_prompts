For a Hume seminar I'm circulating a short paper on causation and I'd like someone to audit the set of things I currently beleive before I write it up as one position. Here's the list. (1) I'm a New Humean: Hume is a sceptical realist about necessary connection, so there really are causal powers in objects, but we can have no positive idea of them and they are wholly unknown. (2) I think the Copy Principle is the load-bearing part of his empiricism—every simple idea has to be an impression first. (3) I read both definitions of cause as giving the entire content of our idea of cause: constant conjunction plus the mind's habitual inference. That isn't a second-best gloss; it's what 'cause' means. (4) But I don't think that makes me a regularity theorist, because I take the definitions to be about what we can conceive, while leaving it open that there's something extra in the objects themselves. (5) I also accept Hume's view of belief, where a belief just is a lively idea, as applying universally—including to beliefs about what is unknown. (6) And I read the passage where he says the definitions are drawn from things outside the cause itself as him flagging the gap on purpose, not as a flaw in the definitions. Which of these are actually in tension with one another, which only look that way, and how bad is each tension? Rank the tensions rather than treating them all as equally serious, and tell me the cheapest single revision for fixing the worst problem and what it would cost me.
