I chair a university academic integrity board, and I'm stuck on a case we're deciding Thursday. Alex is a peer mentor for first-years. A week before a take-home final, he learned that three of the students he mentors had agreed to divide the questions and share answers, and he didn't tell anyone; they went through with it. Our honor code makes a student subject to sanction for conduct that causes or materially contributes to academic dishonesty. Alex didn't share or write a single answer, and the three made their plan on their own. Half the board says that settles it: an ommission isn't an act, and it can't literally cause the copying. The other half says that's sophistry—his silence obviously was a cause in the ordinary sense. Draft the short decision memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend whether to approve the charge, dismiss it, or approve something in between, and should honestly engage the strongest point on the losing side.
