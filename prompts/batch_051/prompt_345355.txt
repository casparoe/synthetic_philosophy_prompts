A friend and I recently spent a long evening arguing about Thomson's lamp, and we never reached agreement. I held that the original question is simply ill-posed: the sequence of states off, on, off, on, ... has no limit, so nothing in the setup decides the lamp's state at t = 1. My friend replied that the lamp must be in some state at t = 1, and that the description is therefore incomplete rather than genuinely paradoxical. To break the deadlock she proposed a variant, and we disagreed about what the variant actually shows. I would like an analysis of it.

Take Thomson's original setup: a lamp starts off at t = 0; at t = 1/2 it is switched on; at t = 3/4 it is switched off; at t = 7/8 on; and so on through the times 1 − 2^(−n). Now replace the two-position switch with a dimmer. At each stage n, instead of toggling, you set the lamp's brightness to the exact midpoint between its current brightness and full brightness, where full brightness is 1. The successive brightness values are then 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, ..., which converge to 1. Now ask: at t = 1, is the lamp at full brightness?

How does this variation change the analysis? One view is that the state at t = 1 is now determinately full on, because the partial states converge, whereas Thomson's original was indeterminate because the partial states merely alternated. The rival view is that this simply imports a continuity principle — identifying the state at the limit point with the limit of the earlier states — which was never forced by the setup. Does the contrast show that Thomson's puzzle is an artifact of the binary toggle rather than a deep problem about completing a supertask, or does a residual problem remain in the dimmer version as well? If a continuity principle is doing the work, what licenses it here, given that the operations are instantaneous and the description specifies only times before t = 1? I am not asking only for a survey of positions; give your own view, and say where you think the original dispute between my friend and me was misguided.

Please also say how confident you are in your overall assessment, and single out the point you are least sure of.
