I keep bumping into a distinction in my work and I cant figure out whether it has a name in philosophy, or whether people just gesture at it. Here are two cases. In one, I know an urn was filled by a process I've studied for years, and on the strength of many thousands of previous draws from urns just like it, I'm 70% sure the next draw will be red. In the other, a stranger texts me '70% chance of rain tomorrow' and says only that it came from one quick look at a single satellite image. The number is the same. But the first 70% is backed by something the second one isn't. I don't mean a confidence interval, and I don't just mean being unsure about my own estimate. It's a property of the evidence itself, not of my attitude toward my estimate: the amount, or completeness, or independent corroboration of the evidence that the 70% rests on, holding the 70% fixed. Is there an established term for this? If there is, how exactly does it differ from reliability in epistemology and from the situation where the probabilities themselves are unknown or imprecise? And if there isn't, what should it be called, and where are its edges — for instance, does evidence that lowers the probability still add to this dimension, or does that reset it? I'm not looking for just a name; I want the thing carved out precisely.
