I've been trying to get my head around the Mīmāṃsā view that the Veda is authorless (apauruṣeya) and that the validity of a cognition is intrinsic (svataḥ prāmāṇya) — it keeps coming up in fights about whether testimony can be a basic source of knowledge, and my usual Bayesian toolit keeps choking on it.

Here's what I think I've got straight. On the Mīmāṃsā story, the Veda isn't the word of God or some sage; it's a beginingless sequence of words/sounds, uncomposed by any person. That supposedly matters for testimony because ordinary reports come from a speaker, so you have to worry about the speaker's fallibility and interests, and your trust bottoms out in your estimate of the speaker's reliability. The Veda has no speaker at all, so there's no fallible node left to mistrust. Separately, Mīmāṃsā holds that a cognition arrives as valid by default — svataḥ prāmāṇya — so you don't first infer that your cognition was caused by a reliable process; you're entitled to it until a defeater shows up, and invalidity is what has to be established from outside. Hence Vedic injunctions about dharma, which can't be checked by perception or inference, are the paradigm case.
