i keep noticing my stated position on virtual worlds is out of step with how i actually reason. out loud i say a WoW sword is just a row in a database, but i spent two weeks grinding for one and was genuinely upset when the roll went to someone else. so i tried to make a principle that fits how i actually behave, and ended up with what i call counterfactual realism: an object is real for a person iff its absence would show up in that person's actions or plans. by this standard my WoW gold is real, and when my friend's year-old Minecraft base got wiped by a server reset, his anger was a response to a real harm rather than a category mistake, because the base was load-bearing in his plans and routines and its deletion left a counterfactual hole. i know this sounds like saying real just means has causal powers, except relativized to a person and their plans, and im not sure that move does any real work. the obvious objection is that an imaginary friend becomes real for someone by the same test — is that fatal, or can i patch it by requiring the counterfactual weight to be shareable? and are virtual harms real on this view, or did i just redefine real until it stopped mattering? is there a better name for what im actually pointing at? keep it under 450 words.
