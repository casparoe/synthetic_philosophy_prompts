So I've been trying to get my views on death straight, and I'd like someone to go through them and tell me whether the whole set hangs together. Here's where I've landed, more or less:

(1) Death is a biological fact, not a social convention or a value judgement. There's a fact of the matter about when someone dies, even if its hard to detect in messy cases.

(2) I think the whole-brain criterion is basically right: you're dead when the entire brain, brainstem included, has irreversibly lost function. So a body on a ventilator with a dead brain is a dead person, even though the heart's still beating.

(3) The reason whole-brain death counts as death, for me, is that the brain is what integrates the body into one self-maintaining organism. Without it the body is just a collection of organs, not an organism.

(4) But I also think someone in a persistent vegetative state — cortex gone, brainstem working, breathing on their own — is definitely alive.

(5) I think circulatory death isn't really a second kind of death; it's a proxy. Once the heart irreversibly stops, the brain is destroyed within minutes, so the two criteria end up picking out the same event.

(6) And yet I'm fine with donation after circulatory death: when someone's heart stops and stays stopped for two to five minutes, they're dead and its ok to take their organs, even though their brain hasn't been without oxygen long enough to be destroyed yet.

(7) I also think death has to be univocal. A person can't be dead by one criterion and alive by another, or dead at one time for one purpose and dead at a different time for another.

(8) I accept the data that brain-dead bodies kept on ventilators can regulate their temperature, heal wounds, and in some cases gestate a fetus for months.

(9) And I'm not a personhood person: I don't think you die when you irreversibly lose consciousness or higher brain function, because then the PVS patient in (4) would already count as dead, and that seems clearly wrong to me.

So: does this package hang together? Go through it and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and how badly. For each real tension, say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. Also flag any pairs that only look like tensions but aren't, and say why they dissolve. At the end, tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
