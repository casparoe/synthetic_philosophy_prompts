I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and a pattern I keep noticing in my own work has turned into a position I cannot state precisely enough to test.

I build generative systems, programs that produce music, text, and images by taking corpora of examples and varying a large number of parameters. Early on I noticed that nothing my systems produce is new in an elemental sense: every output is a recombination of inherited material under new settings, a matter of turning dials such as register, palette, rhythm, or vocabulary weight. What unsettled me is that the same seems true of human creativity in forms I had assumed were different in kind. A jazz soloist improvising on a standard is turning dials on a chord progression and a set of licks. An architect I know produces buildings that read as fresh by adjusting a familiar type along dimensions such as massing, setback, and circulation until the setting crosses some threshold. I could not find a counterexample even in the cases I most wanted to count: the invention of linear perspective, or Schoenberg's move to the twelve-tone row, still looked like the discovery that a dimension everyone had held fixed was in fact adjustable. That is, the inventors had found a new dial, not a new category of thing.

The view I have drifted toward is that all creativity is variation on inherited structures: there are no new elements, only new settings and newly discovered dimensions of variation. I am not sure whether this is a deep insight or a triviality, and two things worry me. First, my own formulation seems to smuggle in a distinction between turning an existing dial and discovering that something is a dial at all; I suspect these may be two ends of one process, or that the distinction depends on the frame of description. Second, I cannot tell what the claim denies. If creativity is just recombination, what separates genuine innovation from a procedural generator, and can the person who first introduces a dimension of variation be explained in the same terms as the person who explores it afterward?

I am not looking only for a label. I want to know whether the position I have described is a recognized view of creativity, which named view or views come closest, where my statement departs from them, and whether the distinction between new elements and new dimensions of variation can be made sharp. If no established position fits, a precise statement of the distinction and its boundary cases would be more useful than a coinage.
