I'm a retired developmental biologist who only started reading philosphy seriously a couple of years ago, so maybe I'm pattern-matching too hard. Here's the thing. I keep thinking that Jonathan Haidt's moral foundations theory is just David Hume's program with a modern social-psychology toolkit. Haidt says the moral mind has a few innate, modular "taste receptors"—care/harm, fairness/cheating, loyalty/betrayal, authority/subversion, sanctity/degradation—and that political left and right just tune the same receptors differently. Then I read this in Hume's An Enquiry Concerning the Principles of Morals:

"There has been a controversy started of late, much better worth examination, concerning the general foundation of Morals; whether they be derived from Reason, or from Sentiment; whether we attain the knowledge of them by a chain of argument and induction, or by an immediate feeling and finer internal sense; whether, like all sound judgement of truth and falsehood, they should be the same to every rational intelligent being; or whether, like the perception of beauty and deformity, they be founded entirely on the particular fabric and constitution of the human species."

Now, I can't tell if this is a real structural identity or a loose analogy. On the one hand, Hume says moral distinctions are from sentiment, not reason; they're more like beauty/deformity than true/false; and they're tied to the "particular fabric and constitution of the human species." That sounds exactly like Haidt's claim that moral intuitions are innate, affectively loaded, and vary across cultures while sharing a common architecture. On the other hand, Haidt uses the left/right difference to draw normative-ish conclusions—e.g. that liberals have a narrower moral palate and that this is a problem for liberal morality. I don't see anything in the Hume passage that licenses that move: if morality is like beauty, then the fact that left and right find different things beautiful doesn't tell us who is right. So my question is: is this parallel deep or superficial? Would Hume's text actually predict the left/right foundation profiles Haidt reports, or would it predict something that Haidt's account gets wrong? And can you help me see where the two theories come apart, not just where they match?
