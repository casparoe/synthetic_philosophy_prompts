What's your actual credence that first-order Peano arithmetic is inconsistent — meaning there's a finite derivation of 0 = 1 from the usual axioms (successor, plus, times, the induction scheme), rather than just a nonstandard model satisfying some weird extra sentence. I know the standard line: Gödel blocks a proof of PA's consistency inside PA, but we've got Gentzen's proof via transfinite induction up to epsilon-zero and the easy ZFC proof, and no contradiction has shown up in a century of people hammering on the thing. I don't want the standard line restated; I want you to actually price the failure mode.

Decompose the number. Walk through at least these pieces: (1) how much rests on the consistency proofs we do have, given that they only work in stronger systems that might themselves be inconsistent; (2) how much rests on the no-contradiction-found record, and whether never seeing a short contradiction is any evidence at all against a contradiction so long nobody could ever finish writing the proof; (3) how much rests on there being a determinate standard model of the naturals — something an inconsistent theory simply couldn't be true of — and whether that's doing real work or just re-asserting consistency in fancier clothes; (4) how much rests on arithmetic being a cleaned-up abstraction of finite counting, which intuitively can't be hiding 0 = 1. Give rough probabilities or ranges for the sub-questions, show how they combine into the headline number, and say which sub-question the final number is most sensitive to and what concrete development would move you most.
