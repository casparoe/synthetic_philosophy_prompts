I teach an upper-level seminar called Modality and Metaphysics, and we just spent three weeks on quantified modal logic and the Barcan formulas. I'm writing the final exam now, and I want one long essay question that actually seperates the students who understand the semantics from the ones who have memorized the formulas. My students can all write down BF and CBF and say which one brings things in and which one lets things out, but ask them to build a countermodel and they fall apart.

So here's what I want the question to force. Working in a variable-domain Kripke semantics, they should explain why the converse Barcan formula is valid exactly on frames where domains don't shrink along the accessibility relation, and why that means instantiating CBF with an existence predicate (or x=x if you don't treat existence as a predicate) pushes you straight into necessitism: the view that everything that exists exists necessarily. They should contrast this with the plain Barcan formula, which read contrapositively says that if there could have been an F, then something actually is possibly F, thereby ruling out new possible objects. The crux should be whether a contingentist can coherently keep BF while rejecting CBF, and on what sort of frame; at the least they should be able to produce a countermodel to each formula and say which direction of domain variation each one is sensitive to. I also want them to close by saying what moving to a constant-domain semantics does to both formulas at once, and whether that by itself is a reason to adopt it.

Please write the exam question, a model answer of about 600–700 words, and a marking scheme worth 20 marks that names the specific moves an A answer must contain versus a B answer. For instance, the marking scheme should reward actually distinguishing a countermodel to CBF from a countermodel to BF, not just restating the frame conditions.
