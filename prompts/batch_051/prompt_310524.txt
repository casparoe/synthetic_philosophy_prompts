You are an expert in the philosophy of biology and cultural evolution. Think carefully, and before committing to a number, spend a few sentences brainstorming the strongest considerations on each side.

I want your credence in a claim rather than a survey. The claim — call it the Memetic Selection Thesis, or MST — is this: in at least one substantial domain of human culture, such as religious doctrine, musical form, legal concepts, or technology, the best explanation of long-run change involves genuinely Darwinian selection acting on discrete, particulate cultural variants, or memes. By genuinely Darwinian selection I mean that all three of the following hold: the variants are copied with enough fidelity to form lineages; the variants differ along dimensions such as longevity, fecundity, and copying fidelity, and those differences affect their rates of replication; and the differential replication of the variants themselves does explanatory work over and above the rational choices, explicit intentions, and individual learning of the human beings who carry them.

My own credence in MST is roughly 0.35. A colleague of mine is at roughly 0.75. Give me your number, but more importantly, show me the reasoning that produces it. Please decompose MST into the sub-questions it depends on. I think the load-bearing ones are at least the following, and I would like you to state each as a proposition and assign it a rough probability:

- Do the relevant selection pressures act on the cultural variants themselves, or only on the interests and biases of the human hosts?
- Does a Darwinian process require something like a genotype–phenotype distinction, or some analogous separation of replicator and interactor, and do memes possess it in any cultural domain?
- Are the successes of population-genetic and epidemiological models of culture evidence of real Darwinian dynamics, or only evidence that borrowed mathematics fits some aggregate patterns?
- Where a rational-choice or individual-learning story is available, does variant-level selection add independent explanatory value?
