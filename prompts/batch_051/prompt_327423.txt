I'm in a course on self-reference and consciousness and we've been doing Hofstadter, strange loops, Escher's Drawing Hands, all that. I've become genuinely taken with the strange-loop view of the self and of meaning: the idea that a thing can be a tangled hierarchy, where a lower level feeds back into a higher level and there's no single ground floor — the hands drawing each other, the self perceiving itself, language talking about language. It's elegant because it dissolves the homunculus problem and the need for a first cause; it says stop looking for an inviolate level outside the loop.

But I've got a firm intuition I can't get rid of: there has to be an inviolate level somewhere — some level that isnt itself entangled in the system it explains. In Drawing Hands, the loop only works because there's the page, the lithograph, the medium, the viewer outside the drawing; none of that is drawn by the hands. With the mind, the strange loop seems to run on neurons, and the neurons, at least as physical objects, aren't themselves a strange loop — they just do what neurons do. More generally, if every level is tangled up with every other, I worry you get circularity instead of explanation; you need at least one level you decline to question, even if its just 'the drawing exists' or 'the brain does ordinary physics.' I realise this might sound like I'm smuggling in a homunculus through the back door, but it really does feel different: the hands need the page, that's all I'm saying.

So I don't want a verdict of 'which side wins.' I want to know what it would take for the inviolate-level intuition to be the thing that gets debunked and dropped, what it would take for the strange-loop view to have to admit it needs an inviolate level after all, and whether there's a reading on which both survive — say, one where the inviolate level is real but not metaphysical bedrock, just the pragmatic place you stop asking 'what draws the drawer?' I keep flipping between the two, and I'd rather know which moves are genuinely justified than which one sounds better in the moment.
