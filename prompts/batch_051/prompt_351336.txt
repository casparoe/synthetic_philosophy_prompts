I'm trying to stay broadly Heideggerian about this. (1) in absorbed skilled activity, equipment isn't having explicit representations made of it — it's ready-to-hand, it withdraws, like the mouse when you're mid-flow on a document. (2) the main route by which equipment stops withdrawing and becomes present-at-hand as an object with properties is breakdown — it jams, surprises you, resists. (3) so good interface design should aim at transparency; the tool that's working is one you don't notice. (4) but an expert can also deliberately shift attention to the tool mid-task — notice the weight of the hammer, the lag of the cursor — with no malfunction at all, and then sink back in, which makes me doubt breakdown is the only way things get thematized. (5) I'm also committed to saying the ready-to-hand/present-to-hand difference is a difference in the agent's stance or mode of engagement, not in the physical object, since the same hammer flips between them. (6) and I want the account to cover software too: a well-tuned autopilot or a recommendation feed you don't have to think about can recede into readiness-to-hand just like a hammer.
