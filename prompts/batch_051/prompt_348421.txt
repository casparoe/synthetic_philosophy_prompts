I'm giving the opening toast at a small departmental symposium on Plato's Symposium, before dinner, with the wine already poured. Roughly half the room are Plato specialists who will catch every reference; the other half are advanced ancient-philosophy students who have read the dialogue but do not work on Plato, and no one has come to hear a lecture. I want the toast to do justice to the fact that Plato's treatment of drunkenness is neither a simple prohibition nor a simple admonition to drink in moderation: in the Symposium the guests agree to drink lightly because they are still hung over from the previous night, Socrates then outlasts everyone else over more wine without getting drunk, and Alcibiades arrives very drunk and is nonetheless welcomed in; in the Laws, by contrast, the Athenian argues that a properly ordered drinking party can serve as a controlled test of self-control and modesty rather than a lapse of reason. Write the toast. It should be warm and witty, wear its learning lightly, and be written so that a Plato specialist in the room would not find an obvious misreading of his treatment of drunkenness. Do not include quotations or scholarly citations.
