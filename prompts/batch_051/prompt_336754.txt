John Stuart Mill, in the fifth chapter of On Liberty, argues that the state may compel parents to see that their children receive a certain basic education, but that it should not itself direct that education, because a state monopoly over the minds of the young would be a step toward despotism. The state's proper role, on his view, is to require a common minimum and perhaps test for it, while leaving the content and institutional form of schooling to parents and voluntary associations. Treat that argument as the shared reference point.

Suppose two thoughtful opponents are brought together for an adversarial collaboration about school choice, private schools, and homeschooling. One, call her the pluralist, reads Mill as supporting a system in which the state enforces a floor of knowledge through external examinations but leaves the content and organization of schooling to parents and voluntary associations; she thinks public funding should follow the child to any school, including religious and homeschool collectives, provided the common exam is passed. The other, call him the common-school egalitarian, reads Mill as still permitting the state to set a substantial common civic curriculum, and argues that if the state funds only pluralistic private providers it will fail to guarantee a shared background, so the state should run its own schools as a democratic default while allowing private alternatives at families' own expense.

Write the document they both could sign. It should include: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims about what Mill's principle does and does not settle, including at least one substantive claim rather than a merely verbal one; (2) a precise statement of the residual disagreement, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own position and recognize the other's as fair; (3) two cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side—and for each crux, a concrete institutional case or empirical condition both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. The document should also distinguish the parts of their dispute that are about interpreting Mill from the parts that are about the empirical consequences of different school systems.
