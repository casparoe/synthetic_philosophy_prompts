I spend my day resolving dependency graphs, and when I read about epistemology's regress problem I can't tell whether I'm seeing a real structural parallel or just pattern-matching. The problem is about how a chain of justifying beliefs gets justified: it stops in beliefs that need no further support (foundationalism), loops in mutual support (coherentism), or never stops (infinitism). Dependency resolution seems to face the same three-way choice: a module graph bottoms out in language/runtime primitives that nothing in the project justifies; it contains circular imports that nonetheless load into a working whole; or, in principle, it goes on without ever reaching a base. The first two mappings feel solid to me—primitives as basic beliefs, a coherent cycle as a coherentist web. The third is a stretch, since real dependency graphs are finite; infinitism would have to correspond to a lazily generated infinite chain, if that even makes sense.

I also see friction elsewhere. A circular import is generally treated as a defect even when it resolves, whereas coherentists treat mutual support as a feature rather than a smell; and grounding a build in primitives is just a stipulation about which runtime you chose, which feels too cheap to count as an epistemic foundation. Is this a deep structural identity, a loose analogy, or a confusion? What would have to be true on both sides for results to transfer? And does the analogy make a prediction that fails—for example, should techniques for breaking dependency cycles with interfaces tell us anything about how coherentist justification should be restructured, or is that exactly where the comparison falls apart?
