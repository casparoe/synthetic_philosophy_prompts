Suppose that by 2040 a decisive no-go theorem, backed by replicated experiment, establishes that one mundane biological phenomenon—the synchronized beating of cilia in a sheet of epithelial cells—is strongly emergent. Specifically, even a Laplacean intelligence with a complete microphysical specification of the system and unlimited computational power could not derive the cells' synchronized rhythm from the lower-level laws alone, and the scientific community accepts this as a fact about the world rather than a limitation of current methods. Predict, with your reasoning visible, how the next thirty years of scientific practice change. Which fields gain or lose prestige and funding, and how do researchers reinterpret the explanatory status of molecular biology, neuroscience, and economics? Does reductionist methodology contract, get relabeled, or continue largely unchanged? Mark which of your predictions are robust, which are guesses, and what would have to hold for each to be wrong—for example, if the result is treated as a one-off curiosity rather than a general feature of complex systems.
