I've noticed a pattern in how I judge people, and honestly it embarasses me a little. When someone's decision has a bad side effect — say a CEO cuts costs in a way that means a nearby river will probably get polluted, or a friend throws a party and somebody's car gets scratched — I catch myself treating them like they did the bad thing on purpose. But when the side effect is good — the cost cut accidentally creates jobs somewhere else, or the party helps two lonely people meet — I never think "they meant to do that." So I've made a policy out of it: I treat harmful side effects as intentional and beneficial ones as accidental unless there is direct evidence either way. It saves me alot of mental effort and it seems to line up with how people actually talk about blame. But I worry it's just a bias wearing a rule. Is this a defensible shortcut? When would it actually track what the person foresaw or intended, rather than just tracking my feelings about the outcome? And if it's partly bias, is there a fixed-up version of the rule I could keep, or should I drop the asymmetry entirely and judge every side effect by the same test of what the person knew and expected?
