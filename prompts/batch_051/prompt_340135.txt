Dennett's actual view is that the intentional stance is all there is—if treating a system as a rational agent with mental states predicts its behavior, then it literally has those mental states. So the Chinese Room doesn't refute strong AI; the whole original/derived distinction is just Searle trying to protect a mysterious property that does no work.

Is that about right? Go through it piece by piece and tell me what Dennett would agree with, what he'd object to, and what Searle would say is being strawmanned. I'm especially suspicious of the "literally has those mental states" part but I can't articulate why.
