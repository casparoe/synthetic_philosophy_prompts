I'm a novelist, and I keep worrying that the whole craft of fiction is just manipulation with a nicer name, so I'm trying to draw the line honestly. My working criteria for manipulation: the influence has to bypass or undercut the target's rational evaluation; the influencer has to hide either their intent or the fact that they're trying to influence at all; the move has to exploit some weakness or asymmetry; and the point is to benefit the influencer at the target's expense. Now the borderline case that has me stuck. In my current novel the chapters are told in free indirect style from the perspective of a character who is steadily self-deceiving; the prose never says she's wrong, it just lets the reader occupy her self-justifications, so (I'm told by early readers) you end up half-defending her until the last chapter undercuts it. Is that manipulation or legitimate persuasion? Walk me through my four criteria one by one on this case, say where they leave the verdict underdetermined, and then tell me whether the case shows the criteria need to be changed — especially the concealment one, since I didnt actually hide my aim, I just let the style do the work.
