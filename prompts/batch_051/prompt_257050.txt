I was re-reading Joshua Knobe’s 2003 Analysis paper on the chairman and the environment, and something is bugging me. In the vignette, the chairman is told that the new program will increase profits and also damage the environment, and he basically says the environmental damage is not something he cares about—his only concern is profit. People then tend to say he intentionally damaged the environment, while in the matching case where the side effect is good, they’re much less willing to say he intentionally did the good thing. Since this shows that moral badness makes people see actions as more intentional than they really are, what’s the best debiasing procedure for courts to use when a jury has to decide whether a defendant acted intentionally? I’d like something practical—maybe a special jury instruction or a two-step verdict form. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
