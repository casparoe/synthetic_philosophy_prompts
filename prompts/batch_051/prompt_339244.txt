I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about whether insect welfare could ever outweigh human welfare, and I came away realizing that my position on how value combines is a package I have never tested as a whole. For a reading group I wrote down the commitments I actually hold; I would like you to audit them.

(1) The value of a moment of experience is the product of two quantities: how conscious it is (C) and how positively or negatively it feels (V). A state contributes C times V. So a state with rich consciousness but zero valence, and a state with high valence but zero consciousness, are both worth exactly nothing; and a fixed amount of valence matters proportionally less in a dimmer subject.

(2) C and V are in principle separable and independently variable: one can hold the richness of consciousness fixed while varying valence, and vice versa. Neither reduces to the other, and no hidden third variable enters the value of a state.

(3) Aggregation across states, times, and beings is additive, and the system contains no lexical priority anywhere. Every finite amount of bad is, in principle, offsettable by some large finite amount of good, because enough positive C-times-V products can always be summed to exceed it. Tradeoffs are always quantitative; no category of value is infinitely more important than any other.

(4) I therefore accept the insect implication: for any amount of severe, highly conscious human suffering, there is some finite number of minimally conscious insects whose mild contentment would in aggregate outweigh it. I expect the number to be astronomically large and practically unreachable, but I regard the in-principle claim as true.

(5) Yet I also find myself committed to this verdict: no matter how numerous they are, the aggregate value of barely conscious, barely contented beings is not greater than the disvalue of one severe episode of highly conscious suffering. The dim contentment and the vivid suffering are, I want to say, not commensurable in the way (3) and (4) require.

(6) I hold equal consideration: whenever two states have the same C and the same V, they make the same contribution to value no matter the species. There is no extra weight for being human, and no discount for being an insect, once C and V are fixed.

(7) I keep feeling the pull of a further claim I cannot fit into (1): that a state with higher C is better in itself, even holding V fixed at some positive value, as if richer awareness of the good is a good over and above its effect in multiplying valence. I do not know whether this is a deep problem for the product formula or a confusion to be set aside.

Please audit this package. In particular, I want to know whether (1) and (3) are actually compatible; whether (5) can be squared with (1) through (4) without simply repudiating one of them; whether (6) shows that (5) is covertly species-based; and whether (7) is a serious challenge to (1). Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating all of them as equally bad, and be specific about what has to go and what it would cost.
