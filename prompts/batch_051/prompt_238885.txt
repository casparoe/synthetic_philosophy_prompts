I am a novelist writing a cycle of novels set in a fictional eastern Mediterranean port city. My protagonist is the city's port magistrate. Under a new commercial compact drafted with foreign advice, she has jurisdiction over broad, undefined offenses — abuse of office, bribery, undue influence — and can void contracts, seize cargo, impose fines, and send names to the governor's bench for imprisonment. She hears such cases almost weekly. The stakes are high — livelihoods, family honor, sometimes liberty — and she must decide most cases in an afternoon, without a developed body of precedent, and then justify her ruling aloud before an assembly of merchants and elders who are watching for favoritism. Because I will put this procedure to use across several books, it must be stable enough that readers cannot catch her contradicting herself.

The difficulty she faces is not primarily legal but conceptual. The standard account she was taught runs roughly: corruption is the abuse of entrusted power for private gain. Yet the city lives by two normative idioms at once. In the codified idiom, a good official applies an impartial rule and treats any private benefit as contamination of the office. In the local idiom, gifts, favors, and patron–client bonds are the machinery through which trust is built, the poor get heard, and an official shows she has not become a stranger to her own people; refusing a gift can be an insult, and a patron's standing help to a client is honorable even though the return is never specified in advance. Clientelism is not a one-off bribe: it is a durable relation of mutual obligation, often perfectly public. The question is which of these practices count as corruption under a standard that does not merely impose one idiom on the other — and which distinctions from the theory must survive when the theory is simplified into a procedure, and which can be dropped without making the procedure blind to something essential.

Please give me the procedure. I want it as a sequence of tests she runs in a fixed order, written so that a magistrate with no philosophical training can use it. For each test, state the question she asks about the facts, what a yes or no settles, and whether the case should pass, fail, or be flagged for further judgment. Mark explicitly every step where judgment cannot be eliminated — choosing the applicable norm, deciding whether a relationship is genuine or strategic, deciding what counts as private gain when office and kin overlap. The procedure should handle the hard boundaries: a public festive gift before a case versus a private gift after it; a customary small payment to move paperwork to the front of a queue; standing patronage to a whole neighborhood; an unsolicited favor done for the magistrate's brother. And it should say where it is silently defaulting to the codified idiom, or how it avoids that, because I do not want to write the novel as if one idiom were simply the truth and the other simply corruption.

Run the procedure on these three situations and show the result in each:

(i) A shipbroker gives the magistrate a case of wine at the city's harvest festival, in public, as every established house does; two weeks later he appears before her in a mooring-fee dispute. The wine is customary and small, but he does have the case pending.

(ii) The magistrate's husband's cousin asks her to release a cargo held for inspection, offering nothing, but the family expects it because she is family, and the rule would not permit release. No money or gift changes hands.

(iii) A clerk's widow, respected by everyone, has for years taken small payments from traders to put their paperwork at the top of the queue, using the money for her daughter's medicine. She hides nothing and the community calls it ordinary self-help; the traders who do not pay wait weeks.

At least one of these should come out corrupt even though the magistrate would prefer the opposite, and the procedure should force the matter; say which one and why. Finally, tell me what the procedure cannot do — where it stays silent or would misfire, and where I will have to write the character as relying on judgment rather than on the method.
