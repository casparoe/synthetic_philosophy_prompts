I’ve been rereading the part of Galen Strawson’s “Luck Swallows Everything” where he lays out the Basic Argument, and there’s a line that’s been gnawing at me. He says, in effect, that you can’t be ultimately responsible for what you do unless you are ultimately responsible for the way you are, at least in certain mental respects, and that to be ultimately responsible for the way you are you’d have to have intentionally brought it about that you are that way, which would require you to have already had a way you were, and so on. Fine, I know the regress. But what I keep getting stuck on is the middle step: the claim that your being responsible for what you do *requires* your being responsible for the way you are. That isn’t argued for, or if it is, the argument is buried in the rhetoric of “you did it because of the way you are.” My question is whether that premise can actually be tested, in the weak operational sense that a philosopher might accept. Suppose someone denies the premise and says ultimate responsibility can attach to an action directly, even when the character, values, and deliberative dispositions that produced it are all ultimately a matter of luck. Is there any observation or experiment—even a weird, idealized psychological one—that would bear on whether the denier is wrong? I don’t mean a brain scan that lights up when someone “acts out of character.” I mean something like a thought-experimental manipulation where we hold the person’s history fixed and vary nothing but the action, or where we construct an agent with no history at all and see whether the intuition of responsibility survives. Design the strongest test you can, say what result would favor Strawson’s premise and what would favor the denier, and then be honest about what the test could never settle even in principle. Is the premise even the kind of thing that could be evidence-sensitive, or is asking for a test here a category mistake? Because if it’s a category mistake, that tells me something about the Basic Argument, but I can’t articulate exactly what. Please no headings, no bullet points, no bold, just continuous prose, like an email. And I’m less interested in the conclusion you end up with than in the trajectory: what you would have to idealize away to make the test possible, and what residue is left over.
