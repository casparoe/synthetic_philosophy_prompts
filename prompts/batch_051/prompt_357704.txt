I am organizing a discussion among people sympathetic to longtermism about whether the simulation argument undermines or reinforces the case for making the far future the dominant object of moral concern. Please describe the most important implications of the simulation argument for that question. By "the simulation argument" I mean the trilemma that technologically mature civilizations either almost never arise, or almost never run large numbers of ancestor simulations, or we are very likely living in a simulation; by "fanaticism about the far future" I mean the view that the expected value of our actions is dominated by their consequences for astronomically many possible future beings. Distinguish the implications that dampen such fanaticism from those that intensify it, and say which you take to be weightier and why.
