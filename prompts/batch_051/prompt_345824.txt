I am teaching Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (Michel Gondry, 2004) in a seminar on the ethics of memory, and I want a philosophical reading of the film through the debate between the duty to remember and the case for forgetting. For the remembering side, work from Avishai Margalit's The Ethics of Memory: the core idea that we stand under a special obligation to remember the people bound to us by thick relations, such as lovers, family, friends, and fellow citizens, because remembering them is itself a way of caring, and because shared memory is part of what holds a moral community together. For the forgetting side, work from David Rieff's Against Remembrance and In Praise of Forgetting: the warning that remembrance is not always a virtue, that it can keep wounds open, harden grievance, or become a kind of moral self-display, and that in some cases forgetting is what lets individuals and societies live forward rather than remain trapped by the past. A complication should be part of your analysis, not set aside: both writers are thinking mainly about collective and historical memory, whereas the film concerns the erasure of intimate, personal memory, so you should say when the transfer from the public to the private case is legitimate and when it is not.

The film's premise is a near-perfect thought experiment for this debate: Lacuna, Inc. will, on request, erase all memories of a failed relationship, and both Joel and Clementine pay to have the other deleted. I do not want a plot summary. I want a piece of criticism that works in both directions, faithful to the film and to the two positions. Specifically: First, what would Margalit's ethics of memory say about each decision to erase, and what would Rieff's case for forgetting say in defense of each? Attend to the asymmetries: Clementine erases Joel impulsively and perhaps as self-protection, Joel erases Clementine in pain and perhaps as retaliation, and both come to regret the procedure while it is still underway. Second, where does the film side with one position or the other? Weigh, for example, the residue that keeps drawing Joel toward Montauk, the survival of something beneath the erasure, and the ending in which the two choose to try again with knowledge of how it failed; then say whether the film lands on one side or remains genuinely undecided. Third, what does the film show that neither philosopher's argument captures, such as the fact that the erasure is dramatized from the inside as the dreamer's attempt to hold onto someone even as the remembered world collapses around him? Fourth, what does the film add to the philosophical debate itself, and where does it resist being read as an illustration of either position?

After the reading, give me a structured summary in a table with these columns: scene or plot element; what Margalit's duty to remember makes of it; what Rieff's case for forgetting makes of it; which way the film leans there, if either; and what remains unresolved by both the scene and the theory. Use no more than eight rows, and include at least Clementine's decision to erase Joel, Joel's decision to erase Clementine, the mid-procedure reversal and the attempt to hide a person inside memories where she does not belong, the collapse of the memory world, the tape-recorded confessions and the confrontation they force, and the closing decision to try again.
