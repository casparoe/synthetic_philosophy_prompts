Question (10 points): In one or two sentences each, define retributivism and consequentialism about punishment. Then give one standard objection to each view, and for each objection say whether it targets the justification of the practice of punishment as a whole or the justification of a particular act of punishment.

Student's answer: Retributivism says that punishment is justified because offenders deserve it, and the punishment should be proportional to the offense, which is basically the old eye-for-an-eye idea of lex talionis. The standard objection to retributivism is that it is purely backward-looking. At best it tells you how to treat someone once they have already broken a rule, but it cannot justify having an institution of punishment in the first place, since an institution has to be justified by the good it will produce going forward. So that objection targets the practice of punishment, not particular acts. Consequentialism says punishment is justified when it leads to better overall consequences, mainly through deterrence and incapacitation. The standard objection to consequentialism is that it cannot make guilt a necessary condition of punishment: if framing a known innocent person for a crime would prevent a riot, a consequentialist has no principled way to rule it out. That objection targets particular acts of punishment, which is the opposite of the retributivist problem.
