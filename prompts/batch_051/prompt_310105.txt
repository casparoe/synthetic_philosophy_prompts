So my friend and I got into it last night about whether a heavily edited studio recording is really an instance of a piece of music, or just its own kind of artifact. He is one of those type/token people — the work is a type, performances are tokens of it — and he kept insisting a recording was like a photograph of a play, not the play itself. Eventually he sent me his argument written out:

(1) A performance of a musical work is a single event in which musicians produce the sounds prescribed by the work.
(2) A studio recording is assembled from many takes, edits, pitch correction, and overdubs, so there is no single event in which musicians produce all the sounds.
(3) Therefore a studio recording is not a performance of the work.
(4) The only way to hear the work itself is through a performance of it.
(5) So when you listen to the recording, you are not hearing the work.

I keep feeling this prooves too much, but i cant find the exact place it falls apart. Is the argument valid? If it is, which premise would you reject, and if it isnt, which step fails — and what would the corrected version of that step be?
