I'm in a philosophy of science reading group and last week we got completly stuck on Lakatos. One person kept saying the supersymmetry programme in particle physics is a degenerating research programme—that after decades of null results it just keeps adding epicycles and moving the goalposts—and someone else shot back that degenerating is just a term of abuse with no empirical content. The rough Lakatos picture I remember is: hard core, protective belt, and the idea that a programme is progressive when each new version predicts something the previous version didn't and at least some of those predictions pan out, whereas it degenerates when the changes are mostly post-hoc accommodations. But I have no idea how I would actually go check which one a given programme is.

I'd like you to take on the role of a philosopher of science who has actually worked through Lakatos's methodology, think carefully, and commit to a concrete test that could be applied to a real programme—supersymmetry is the running example, but the test should be stated generally enough that I could use it elsewhere. What data would you gather, what would you operationalize as a new prediction versus a post-hoc adjustment, and what pattern of results would count as evidence that the programme is degenerating, and what pattern would count against? Then be explicit about what the test would and would not settle: can it tell a genuinely degenerating programme apart from one that is just stalled because the math is hard or the experiments are expensive, and where does irreducibly judgment come in? I'd much rather you defend a single best test than give me a list of options, but please start by brainstorming two or three candidate tests, even bad ones, before you settle.
