I've been rereading a lot of mythology recently and I keep tripping over the same thing, and now I'm wondering if it has a name. People talk about "the myth of Medusa" and then they tell the story where Poseidon assaults her in Athena's temple and Athena punishes her by turning her into a monster. But that's Ovid, a Roman poet doing a literary retelling centuries later; in the older Greek sources she's just one of three Gorgon sisters, born monstrous, no transformation story. And yet people state the Ovid version as "the myth," and if you bring up the older one they treat it as a weird variant rather than as a rival telling. Same thing happens with fairy tales: "the Cinderella story" means Perrault's version with the glass slipper and the fairy godmother, even though one of the Grimms' versions has a tree and birds and no godmother, and the older non-European tellings differ in their own ways. Its like one retelling has quietly become the official one, and every other version gets treated as a deviation from it instead of just being another version on the same footing.

Is there a name for this? I mean the assumption that underneath the variants there's one real version, plus the related mistake of treating a later literary retelling as the original. My first thought is that it's somewhere near "canon," but that names the process by which a work gets official status, not the underlying assumption that a myth has to have an official version at all. I want the carving more than the label: what exactly is the phenomenon, what isn't it, and is it always a mistake to talk about "the" version of a myth, or only when the version that got picked is later than the alternatives and people dont notice? Like, when is it just harmless shorthand for "the version we happen to be discussing"?
