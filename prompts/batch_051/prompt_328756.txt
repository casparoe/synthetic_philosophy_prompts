I keep meeting the euhemerist thesis in two forms that need to be pulled apart: as a historical hypothesis — that many of the named gods of ancient Mediterranean religion began as deified human beings, their actual biographies magnified over generations into divine ones — and as a conceptual claim about the kind of thing a god is in such traditions, namely an entity whose identity is carried by a biography rather than by a natural function. I want to know whether the historical half is testable at all, rather than merely a story one can always tell about a god after the fact.

Take a single well-attested figure from Graeco-Roman polytheism — say, a healing god associated with a particular sanctuary — and suppose the question is whether that god began as a remembered human being. Set out the strongest test you can design. Be specific about the observations that would count as traces of a human origin: funerary or hero-cult features surviving in the ritual, an ordinary human name in the onomastic record, a domain that looks like the portfolio of a local benefactor rather than a natural force, or a cult that appears in the written record only after a plausible human candidate's lifetime. Be equally specific about what would count against a human origin: transparently meteorological or agricultural functions, attestations older than any candidate human, or the absence of a tomb or genealogy in a culture that normally supplies both for its deified dead.

Then say exactly what a positive result would and would not establish. In particular, I want to know whether any body of evidence could distinguish 'this god began as a person' from 'this god existed first as something else and only later absorbed a human biography', and whether 'having begun as a person' is even determinate across the relevant centuries — that is, whether the individuation of the god over time is stable enough for the historical claim to be true or false as stated. If the defensible conclusion is that euhemerism is better treated not as an origin hypothesis about particular gods but as a claim that a deification mechanism is available to worshippers in certain societies, say so, and state what observation would have to come out differently for the stronger, origin-level claim to survive.

Please write your answer in British English.
