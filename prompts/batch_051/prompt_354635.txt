I serve on the Municipal Integrity Commission of a mid-sized city and I have been assigned to draft the decision memo in a case the commission will vote on next week. The facts are largely undisputed. During a severe and prolonged heat wave, the city's Director of Public Health learned that the emergency fund for urgent housing interventions had been frozen by a court order and that standard procurement of emergency cooling equipment would take at least three weeks. Reasoning from a defensible but not certain projection that the delay would cause many excess deaths among elderly residents of two public housing towers, she signed a backdated memorandum certifying that a public health emergency had already been declared when it had not, and instructed a subordinate to tell the City Council's finance committee that the equipment was being procured through standard channels, which was false. The equipment was installed within 48 hours, and the city's retrospective review estimates with moderate confidence that the intervention prevented several hundred heat-related deaths. An independent inspector found that she violated the procurement code and knowingly misled a council committee, though she gained no personal benefit and the intervention itself was an efficient use of public funds. The commission has three options: (1) refer the matter for formal censure; (2) close the file without any finding of fault; or (3) close the file without censure but issue a formal finding that the Director's conduct was wrongful and that the good consequences do not erase the wrongfulness. Please draft the decision memo in no more than 800 words. It should open with a one-sentence bottom-line recommendation, give the reasoning for that recommendation, and include a serious, fair treatment of the strongest argument for the option you recommend against.
