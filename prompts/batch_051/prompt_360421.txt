During a study session for the evolution unit in my high school biology class, two of my classmates got stuck on a disagreement I could not referee, and I would like a careful point-by-point verdict. The case we invented was a species of beetle in which males carry a hard spur on the head. In the present population, males with longer spurs win more fights and father more offspring; a phylogenetic reconstruction, however, suggests the spur began as a thickened patch of cuticle that protected the head from abrasion while burrowing, and only later was co-opted for fighting. Maya argued that the function of the spur is protection from abrasion, because that is the effect for which the structure was selected; fighting is an exaptation, a later use, and if any current contribution counts as a function then a pebble lodged on a beetle's head that incidentally deflects a rival's bite would have a function too. James replied that biologists cannot always wait for a historical analysis before speaking of function: field and laboratory work identify what an organ does now, and the spur's present role in winning fights is exactly the sort of reproductive advantage natural selection works on. Maya came back that current usefulness alone cannot distinguish a trait from a lucky accident, since the pebble contributes to reproduction right now but is not part of the organism's inherited design. James answered that he could add the requirement that the thing be a reliable, inherited part of the organism's machinery, but that Maya's view had the mirror problem—a lineage that has spent ten thousand generations maintaining and refining the spur for fighting would still have to call it a burrow-guard, a historical relic. Our teacher later told us that philosophers of biology call these the etiological and the causal-role accounts of function, but nobody could tell us what the two accounts capture or where the argument actually missteps.

Please adjudicate the debate move by move: for each of the four positions (Maya's selected-effects claim, James's current-role reply, Maya's accidental-benefit objection, and James's inherited-machinery counter), say whether it is clearly mistaken, defensible, or clearly right, and briefly justify each rating. Then say what, if anything, each side is missing, and whether there is a fact of the matter about the spur's function or simply two different senses of the word function. Keep your entire answer under 500 words.
