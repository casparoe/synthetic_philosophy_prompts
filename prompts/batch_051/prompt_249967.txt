I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy or neuroscience, and a debate at work about whether our choices are causally determined by our brains has sent me down a rabbit hole on Libet's experiments. A colleague forwarded me a long-form article that everyone in the thread is treating as authoritative. It summarizes the research roughly as follows:

Libet had subjects watch a rapidly rotating clock and flex their wrist at a time of their own choosing, while reporting the clock position at the exact moment they decided to move. An EEG recorded a slow buildup of electrical activity over the motor cortex, the readiness potential, which began about half a second before the movement — roughly 300 milliseconds before the subjects' reported moment of conscious decision. The article concluded that the brain has already made the choice before the conscious mind knows about it, so the felt intention is a side effect or after-the-fact story rather than the cause of the action. It took this to refute the ordinary idea of free will: conscious will is, at most, an emergency brake that can veto an already-initiated movement in the final 100 milliseconds or so, and it dismissed Libet's own appeal to that veto as special pleading. Because the movements were simple and arbitrary, the article treated the findings as applying just as much to difficult, deliberated decisions.

I'm suspicious of the leap from "the brain shows preparatory activity before the person reports an intention" to "conscious choice is causally inert," which feels stronger than the evidence can support. But I don't have the background to judge whether the details — the timing, the role of the readiness potential, the veto — are being reported accurately in the first place. Can you go through this summary and give me an accuracy audit? I want to know which parts a careful neuroscientist or philosopher would accept, which are subtly misdescribed or overstated, and whether the final conclusion survives the corrections. I'm not looking for further reading; I want to understand which steps in the argument are load-bearing and which are just rhetoric.
