I've spent the past couple of years reading about the Kelly criterion in betting and investing forums, and the same argument seems to have been running for decades without anyone actually moving. One side says the Kelly fraction is the correct stake for any repeated favourable bet, because it maximises the expected logarithm of wealth, which is equivalent to maximising the long-run growth rate of a single bankroll. They point out that overbetting draws you toward ruin while underbetting leaves growth on the table, and that ordinary expected-value reasoning is a trap: it averages over parallel histories that no actual bettor lives through, whereas a real bankroll follows one path and should be judged by its time-average growth. The other side replies that this is just expected utility theory wearing different clothes. Maximising expected log wealth, they say, is rational only if your utility for money happens to be logarithmic; a more risk-averse person should bet less than Kelly, a less risk-averse person should bet more, and someone who genuinely valued expected wealth should push much harder than Kelly. On their view, the Kelly camp has smuggled in one particular attitude to risk and presented it as a law of nature.

I am less interested in who is right than in what kind of disagreement this is. My working hypothesis is that the two sides are answering different questions: one is describing which fixed-fraction strategy will come to dominate in the long run, while the other is prescribing how much a particular person should stake given their own preferences. But I am not sure that is fair, because the Kelly side often speaks normatively and not merely descriptively, and the expected utility side does make claims about what a rational agent may legitimately prefer. So I want a diagnosis of the structure of the impasse: is it a verbal dispute about the word 'optimal', a substantive dispute about correct risk preferences, a disagreement about whether the relevant unit is a single bet or a long sequence of bets, or something else entirely? And given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as genuine progress, whether that is a theorem, a distinction, a reframing of the question, or an argument that no progress should be expected.
