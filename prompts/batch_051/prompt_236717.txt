I'm on the board of a small family foundation that gives one grant a year in the language and culture space, and I've been asked to write the decision memo we'll vote on next week. We're down to two finalists. Project A is a request from two academic linguists for three years of funding to document a language — I'll call it Ushket — spoken fluently by about fifteen people, all over seventy. The kids in the community speak the regional majority langauge and aren't learning it; there's no revival movement, and the community council has told us politely that, if forced to choose, they'd rather see the money go to the second proposal. That one, Project B, is from the community's own youth association: the same amount of money for after-school tutoring, exam prep, and a small scholarship fund for maybe two hundred local kids a year. And I should be honest that Ushket isn't some exotic isolate that would rewrite linguistic theory; it's a pretty normal member of a family that's already decently described, so the pitch about science needing good data isn't doing the real work. The real argument for A is what half the board keeps saying: our foundation exists to preserve cultural heritage for its own sake, and a language is worth recording even if nobody ever speaks it again, because a whole way of carving up the world would be lost, and that loss is bad even if the people themselves have moved on. The other half says that's just treating a living community as a museum exhibit, and that culture only has value because actual people value it — so if the actual people tell us they need tutors more than an archive, we should listen. I need a memo of about 800 words with a clear recomendation: fund A, fund B, or split with conditions. It should engage the strongest point on the losing side, and if I recommend a split I should say exactly what conditions I'd attach. I don't need a reading list; I need something the board can actually vote on.
