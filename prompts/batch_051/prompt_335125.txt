In my philosophical logic course we have been working through the Barcan formulas and their frame conditions in quantified modal logic. As I understand it, the Barcan formula, ∀x□Fx → □∀xFx, is valid exactly on frames with anti-monotonic domains, while the converse Barcan formula, □∀xFx → ∀x□Fx, is valid exactly on frames with monotonic domains; in S5, holding both seems to force constant domains, so the same objects show up in the domain of every accessible world. I can follow the model theory, but I am not sure what it shows metaphysically. Does endorsing both formulas really amount to the claim that everything that exists exists necessarily? Or is that conclusion an artifact of reading the quantifier domain as the set of things that exist? Could one accept the formulas while using a fixed domain of possibilia and a separate existence predicate, or in a free-logical setting, and still deny that every ordinary existing thing exists necessarily? I would like an explanation of exactly where the step from the formal validity of the Barcan formulas to that necessitist conclusion does and does not go through, and which semantic or background assumptions are doing the metaphysical work.
