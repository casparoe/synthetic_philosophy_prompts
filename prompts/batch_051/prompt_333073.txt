I am a hobbyist who builds small simulations, and for years I have been turning over a puzzle that I do not have a name for. It also shows up outside my simulations, so I would like you to tell me whether it already exists as a recognized concept and, more importantly, to help me locate its exact edges.

The simulation is a tabletop with a few thousand tiny discs. Each disc follows only two local rules: it is nudged toward the disc just ahead of it, unless the gap becomes too small, in which case it is sharply repelled. Nothing else is in the program. When it runs, the discs sort themselves into large swirls, lanes, and jams. The program also computes a large-scale summary of what the discs are doing -- a swirl, a jam, a clearing -- and displays a simplified sign for that summary at the top of the screen, the way a weather map displays a cyclone. Sometimes the sign is a short word such as CLEARING or BLOCKED.

Here is the puzzle. A fresh disc dropped into a swirl is swept around and flung outward, and it is natural to say the swirl did the flinging. But the swirl is not an extra thing standing behind the discs; it is just the discs arranged in that way. Likewise, when the screen says CLEARING, it looks as if the word has ordered the discs to reorganize, but the screen only ever reports what the discs are already doing. I can turn the screen off and the discs behave identically. And yet the large-scale pattern is exactly what I manipulate when I fix a jam: I set out to change CLEARING into BLOCKED, and I do that by nudging a few discs, and the whole arrangement follows.

I am torn between two thoughts. The first is that only the discs and their local pushes genuinely exist, so the swirl cannot literally do anything; attributing a push to the swirl is a mere shorthand, and the pattern is something I project. The second is that the pattern is doing real work: it predicts what the discs will do, it lets me control them, and I cannot describe my own interventions without it. The same tug appears elsewhere: a queue in a crowd seems to carry a newcomer along, and a market's prevailing mood seems to make individual sellers lower prices, even though the queue and the mood are made of nothing but the people themselves.

My questions are these. Does this exact puzzle have an established name, and is there a defensible resolution of the two thoughts? Please do not stop at a name; I want the boundaries of the concept, and the points where it comes apart from nearby ideas. Is this the same thing as emergence, or does emergence cover more, or less, than what I described? Is one side forced to say the large-scale pattern is unreal, and is the other side forced to say it exerts an extra force on the discs? Or is there a position that keeps both claims -- that the pattern is real and that it does no extra pushing -- and if so, what is real actually doing in that sentence? If no established term fits this setup exactly, say so, propose a term of your own, define it, and give me a boundary case where it stops being clear whether the pattern is doing the work.

When you have finished, make your final line a single sentence beginning with Summary: that states the view you land on.
