I'm on a small product team building a "human progress" timeline feature for our history app, and we've hit a tagging problem. Every entry has to get flagged as either "moral progress" or just "change / tech progress / not really moral," and the content team keeps fighting about edge cases. They've asked me (the only engineer, go figure) to write up a working definition of "moral progress" that we can turn into content guidelines. Not a philosophy paper, just something actionable.

The cases we keep arguing about are these:

- the abolition of chattel slavery (obviously yes)
- women getting the vote (also yes)
- the Civil Rights Act of 1964 in the US (yes)
- the moon landing (obviously no)
- the invention of the smartphone (no)
- South Korea going from authoritarian to democratic (maybe yes?)
- the worldwide drop in extreme poverty since 1990 (some say yes, some say that's just economic growth, not "moral")
- the big drop in violent crime since the 90s (feels like progress, but maybe it's just better policing or lead abatement, not a moral change)
- laws recognizing animal cruelty as a crime (I lean yes, but an intern says that's just "circle expansion" and old-school moralists wouldn't have counted it)
- same-sex marriage being legalized in a bunch of countries (yes for our brand, but the definition shouldn't just flatter the home team)
- the spread of vegetarianism/veganism (totally unclear to me)

I keep seeing the phrase "the expanding circle" tossed around—the idea that moral progress is when the group of beings we think count keeps getting bigger. But that doesn't cover the crime drop or the poverty stuff, unless I'm missing something. I'd like you to either make the expanding circle idea work by extending it, or give me a better definition and say why it beats the expanding circle story.

So: propose a definition of "moral progress" (one or two sentences, something I could paste into a doc), then run it through each of the cases above and flag any wrong or weird verdicts. If your definition gives a counterintuitive answer somewhere, either bite the bullet and defend it or revise. Also tell me whether one definition can do all the work or whether I actually need two seperate ones (like a descriptive and a normative one). Keep the jargon light.
