I work as a backend engineer and have basically no philosophy background, but I got dragged into a forum argument about whether a company that builds its whole product on top of some open-source library and never gives anything back (no code, no money, no bug reports, nothing) is genuinely doing something wrong. From what I could gather, the people saying it's wrong kept reaching for one of two stories. Story A is a fair-play story: there's a cooperative scheme, some maintainers are paying real costs, the company is taking the benefits, so it owes a fair share just because it took, and skipping out on that share is the wrong part. Story B is a harm story: the wrong part isn't the taking itself, it's that the taking makes someone concretely worse off — it uses up something rival, adds an actual cost, or burns someone out until the thing degrades. In the open-source case these two stories blur together, and honestly Story B feels hand-wavy there, because one more download of a library doesn't leave less library for anyone else.

So build me a thought experiment where the two stories come apart, specifically in the direction I find hard to construct: Story B clearly says the person is doing something wrong, but Story A, taken at its word, has to strain to reach them. Constraints: no governments or taxes; the person hasn't joined, signed, or promised anything; and if Story A condemns them it has to go through a stretched notion like accepting benefits or implicit consent rather than just stipulating that they accepted something. Keep the setting ordinary and concrete — a neighborhood, an online group, a shared pond or well, a workplace — not a trolley and not a magic commons. After the scenario, walk through what each story says and why, identify the one feature of the case that does the separating, and say whether removing that feature collapses it back into the open-source-style case where the two stories agree. Then tell me how a defender of Story A would fight back, and whether you think their move is fair or just definitional ret-conning.
