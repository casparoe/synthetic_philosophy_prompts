I have no legal training, but I have been trying to work out a consistent view about when someone should be able to insist that a dispute be settled by private arbitration rather than in court. Here are the cases I keep returning to, and my verdicts on them.

A woman buys a phone. In the box is a card saying that by using the phone she agrees to terms available online, including a clause requiring individual arbitration and giving up class actions. Her battery later overheats and burns her hand, leaving her with about three thousand dollars in medical costs. She wants to join a class action with thousands of other buyers whose batteries failed. I think she should be allowed to join the class action and should not be forced into one-on-one arbitration.

Two companies, each advised by lawyers, negotiate a supply contract and deliberately include an arbitration clause for pricing disputes. I think they should have to arbitrate, and the courts should stay out.

A factory worker is given a stack of onboarding forms and told he must e-sign them before starting. Buried in the forms is a clause requiring arbitration of any employment dispute. After he reports being sexually harassed by his supervisor, he wants a public trial. I think he should get one.

An elderly man is being admitted to a nursing home after a stroke, and his daughter signs the admissions packet at the front desk. A clause in the packet requires arbitration of any neglect or malpractice claim. He later develops serious bedsores from alleged neglect. I think the clause should not bar a lawsuit.

A first-time small-business owner signs a franchise agreement with a large national chain. The agreement is long and full of standard terms, including an arbitration clause. He later alleges the chain misrepresented projected sales figures. My instinct is that he should not be forced into arbitration either, but I am less sure here than in the other cases, and I cannot say what separates this from the two companies that knew exactly what they were doing.

Is there one principle that captures this pattern of verdicts? If not, which of these verdicts should I give up, and why? Please state the principle as precisely as you can, say what it predicts for cases I have not listed — for example, a ride-hailing customer suing after an accident, who never clicked a box but supposedly accepted the arbitration clause through a link in the sign-up terms — and be explicit about which of my verdicts it cannot preserve.
