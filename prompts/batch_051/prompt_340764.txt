Hey — you're a philosopher of art who works on games and interactive art. Please think carefully, start by brainstorming a few candidate concepts before settling on one, and write your answer in American English.

I keep noticing a phenomenon in games that I don't have a name for. Players discover a technique the designers never intended, like cancelling an attack's recovery frames, using the recoil from your own weapon to fly around, or chaining inputs in a way that breaks the intended rhythm. At first it reads as a glitch, but instead of being treated as cheating it becomes the thing high-level play is organized around. Then a patch or a sequel has to decide whether to remove it or keep it; and when the developers keep it, the game feels like it is treating the player's agency as part of the work in a way that a fully polished set of intended mechanics isn't. It's definately near emergent gameplay, but I mean something narrower: the value, specifically aesthetic, that comes from acting through possibilities the designer didn't author. Does that have its own name? If the nearest established term only partly fits, I want to know exactly how the thing I'm describing is narrower or different and where its edges are. A label by itself won't do.
