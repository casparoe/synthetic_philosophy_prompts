Write a short dialogue between two epistemologists who disagree about whether there can be rational epistemic akrasia. One says no: if you have higher-order evidence that you're unreliable about a certain kind of judgment—say you're badly sleep-deprived or you've taken a reason-distorting drug—then you have to discount your first-order reasoning, and its irrational to keep believing the conclusion anyway. The other says yes: when the first-order reasoning is fully transparent and you can check each step yourself, the higher-order evidence is just more evidence, and unless you can actually find a flaw in the reasoning you shouldn't let it move you. Make them genuinely respond to each other's points rather than talking past each other, and don't make either one a strawman.
