Write the joint statement that a Hart-style legal positivist and a Fuller-style natural lawyer could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. By a Hart-style legal positivist I mean someone who holds that law's existence and content are settled by social facts such as enactment and official practice, not by moral merit. By a Fuller-style natural lawyer I mean someone who holds that law is a purposive enterprise with an internal standard of legality, and that a sufficiently lawless exercise of power fails to be law in the fullest sense.

Open with a numbered section of agreed claims. These should be substantive rather than vacuous, and they should include: a shared description of the grudge-informer cases in which post-war German courts faced denunciations made under the Nazi regime; the concessions each side makes to the other's strongest arguments; and what they jointly affirm about law in ordinary, well-functioning legal systems. Where an agreed claim might be accepted in one sense by the positivist and another by the natural lawyer, restate it so the two senses come apart.

Then give a disagreements table. For each disagreement, state the exact proposition the positivist affirms and the natural lawyer denies, or the converse, in wording each side would accept for its own position. Cover at least these four disputes: whether a duly enacted rule that is radically unjust is law; whether a so-called wicked legal system is best described as law, as law-like, or as law in a degenerate or failed sense; whether the positivist's claim that there is no necessary connection between law and morality is best read as a conceptual thesis, a methodological recommendation, or a political stance; and what a court should say when a morally monstrous rule has an impeccable social source.

Finally, identify the cruxes, the sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one side. For each crux, provide a concrete test, case, or body of evidence both sides would agree bears on it, and record in advance each side's prediction of what the test would show. Include at least one test involving a constitution that explicitly makes moral standards a condition of validity, and at least one test involving a regime whose official rules are retrospective, secret, contradictory, or impossible to obey. Throughout, keep three levels of the debate distinct: the concept of law, the standards of statutory interpretation, and the practical duties of officials and citizens under evil law.
