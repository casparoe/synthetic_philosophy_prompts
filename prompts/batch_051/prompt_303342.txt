During my working life I was a natural scientist, and since retiring I have taken up political philosophy. The distinction I keep coming back to is between an act's being legal and its being legitimate. I have been reading John Locke on executive prerogative: as I understand him, he maintains that in an emergency that cannot wait for the legislature, the executive may act outside or even against the letter of the law, provided the action genuinely serves the public good and the people remain entitled to judge afterward whether the trust was abused. Suppose a 21st-century liberal democracy responds to a pandemic by imposing executive-ordered border controls and lockdowns. Reconstruct what Locke would most likely have said about these measures. In doing so, distinguish carefully between their legality under modern emergency statutes and their legitimacy as uses of prerogative. Which features would he have judged legitimate, which a slide toward arbitrary power, and where does his doctrine leave him most strained—especially given that modern emergency powers are often renewed and quietly institutionalized rather than used as one-off acts? Please answer in American English.
