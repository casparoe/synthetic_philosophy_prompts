I'm taking a philosophy of economics class and something's been bugging me. Everyone around me talks about the government printing money as if its obviously dangerous — it'll cause inflation, debase the currency, punish savers, all that. But those same people dont blink at the fact that private banks create most of the money in the economy just by making loans: when a bank writes a mortgage, it credits the borrower with new deposits that didn't exist in any vault beforehand. As far as I can tell, both expand the money supply, and neither one, by itself, creates more real goods or productive capacity. So is there a real principle that makes government money creation worse than private bank money creation, or is the asymmetry just because one feels like printing and the other feels like a private transaction? Give me the strongest justification for the asymmetry you can, and then tell me honestly whether it holds up.
