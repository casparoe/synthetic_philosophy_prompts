Ok so I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind and I've basically ended up defending illusionism about phenomenal consciousness: the view that experiences dont actually have the qualitative properties they seem to have. It seems to us that there's a raw feel — the reddishness of red, the sting of pain — but on the illusionist picture that seeming is generated by access, attention, and our introspective concepts, and there is no extra phenomenal residue that the physical/functional story leaves out.

I've heard all the standard objections, and they mostly feel like variations on the obvious it-feels-like-something move. I'm more interested in the reverse question: what would actually count against the view? Suppose illusionism is false — what does the world have to look like, and what would I have to catch myself doing, for me to be forced to abandon it? I realize I'm not sure anymore which possible findings should move me, as oppose to the ones I'd just absorb into the view.

I want a map of the defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want you to be honest about which advertised defeaters are actually duds. For instance: a clean double dissociation where someone has fully intact access, attention, and discriminative behavior but no phenomenal feels at all (or the reverse, rich feels with no access to them) — does that get any real purchase, or does the illusionist just get to deny the description? What about empirical results showing introspection is more reliable for phenomenal properties than for the sorts of contents illusionists say it confabulates? What about the charge that illusionism is self-undermining, because an illusion is itself a phenomenal experience, so the view presupposes the very thing it denies? Which of these should a committed illusionist concede is genuine bad news, and which are just confused?

I'd also like an honest assessment of whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability. When I re-describe every counterexample as itself an introspective illusion, at what point does that stop being parsimony and start being a degenerating research program? Is that a serious problem for a view like this, or just par for the course in metaphysics? And please be willing to tell me that illusionism is extremely well-insulated, if that's the truth, and whether that's a feature or a bug.
