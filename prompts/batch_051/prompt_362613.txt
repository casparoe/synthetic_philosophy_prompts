I'm writing a campus novel, and in one scene two philosophy professors finally stop arguing and write up their year-long fight over the identity of indiscernibles as a short adversarial-collaboration statement—the statement itself is supposed to appear in the book, so I need the real thing. One of them is a Leibnizian who accepts PII as a necessary truth; the other thinks Max Black's two-sphere universe (two exactly similar iron spheres alone in an otherwise empty space) shows its simply false. Draft that statement in three sections, in this order. "Agreed claims" (about 450 words): numbered propositions both could sign about the setup, about what qualitative vs numerical identity amounts to, and about what would actually count as a counterexample. "Where they disagree" (about 300 words, table format): each side's position stated precisely enough that its own side would accept the wording, with a note on which bits are substantive and which are just word-choice. "Cruxes and predictions" (about 200 words): the sub-questions that would move one of them, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Keep it tight and signable, not a parody.
