Last night a friend and I spent several hours arguing about Skolem's paradox, and I want an audit of the position I found myself defending, because I cannot tell which parts of it are doing the real work. The commitments I accumulated are these:

(1) I accept the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem. Since the language of set theory is first-order and countable, ZFC, if consistent, has a countable model; in such a model there is an object that the model regards as the powerset of the natural numbers and therefore as uncountable, even though the object has only countably many members from an external point of view.

(2) I take this to show that the notion expressed by “uncountable” is interpretation-relative: whether something is uncountable is not settled by an interpretation-independent fact but depends on the model in which the question is posed.

(3) Nevertheless, I hold that “the real numbers are uncountable” is true simpliciter. Cantor’s theorem is a fact about the actual real numbers, and the countable model simply fails to contain most of the actual subsets of the natural numbers.

(4) I think the correct diagnosis is that first-order logic is too weak to characterize “all subsets.” Second-order logic can give categorical axiomatizations of the natural numbers and of the real numbers, and I take such categoricity to restore the determinacy that the Skolem construction seemed to dissolve.

(5) At the same time, I am moved by the thought that if a notion is interpretation-relative, then questions posed in its terms have no determinate answers. This is why I lean toward saying that the continuum hypothesis has no determinate truth value: what counts as the powerset of the natural numbers is exactly the sort of thing that shifts from model to model.

Please treat this as a consistency audit rather than a lecture. Tell me which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension and which merely appear to be, rank the tensions by severity, say what would have to give in each case and at what cost, and identify the single revision that would restore the most coherence at the least cost.
