Write the joint statement that a committed conciliationist and a committed advocate of the steadfast view about peer disagreement could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, for an audience that already knows the rough positions and wants the precise anatomy of their agreement and their disagreement. Begin with the claims both sides accept, stated in terms neither party is silently qualifying: what makes two people epistemic peers in the sense that matters, what the central cases of peer disagreement involve, and which distinctions, above all the distinction between first-order and higher-order evidence, are acceptable shared vocabulary. Then formulate the disagreement exactly, with each view stated in wording its own proponents would accept; the task is not merely to record that one side prescribes belief revision and the other does not, but to locate whether the dispute is best expressed as one about the force of higher-order evidence, about whether there is a unique rational response to a body of evidence, or about something narrower. Make the crux section the core of the document: identify the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side, such as whether the uniqueness thesis is true and in precisely what form; whether conciliationism applies to itself and whether any self-defeat objection goes through once higher-order disagreements iterate; and whether higher-order evidence is best treated as ordinary evidence with a different object or as a distinctive epistemic kind calling for its own principles. For each crux, give the argument or case both sides would agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts that argument or case will show. End by saying which residual disagreements the collaboration concluded were substantive and which were merely verbal, and by specifying what each side accepts would actually move it. I want a committed document, not a survey: choose definite formulations for every contested claim, and defend them as fair, non-vacuous, and acceptable to the side being characterized, rather than offering alternative wordings for me to choose among. Write it as a co-signed memo would be written, in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables; where a disagreements table would normally appear, narrate the disagreement in paragraphs instead.
