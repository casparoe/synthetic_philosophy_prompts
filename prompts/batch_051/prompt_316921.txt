A statute in my country, passed in 1987 and aimed at hoax callers and rumour-mongers, says:

"Any person who communicates, or causes to be communicated, any statement which he knows, or has reasonable grounds to believe, is false, and which is of a kind likely to cause public alarm, commits an offence."

I run a small automated news-summary account on a social network; the bio says the posts are machine-generated. Last month, after a data glitch, the model hallucinated a realistic report that a water treatment plant in our city had a contamination incident and that one suburb should boil its water. I was away and didn't see the post until a day later, by which point it had been reshared thousands of times and two schools had closed for the morning. The water was never unsafe.

I didn't write the post and didn't know it existed until well after it went up. But I did set up the account, picked the model, and know that models sometimes hallucinate. The drafters were thinking of people phoning in bomb threats or spreading malicious gossip, not systems that emit their own falsehoods.

So my question isn't really "am I liable" so much as how the law should even reach this. Take the words, the purpose of the offence, what the 1987 drafters would have said if asked about an account that posts without a human in the loop, and what a reasonable reader of the text expects it to cover: what does each have to say, and which one wins? And run it through the Hart–Fuller debate: would Hart treat this as a penumbral case a judge can settle by ordinary statutory interpretation without taking a moral stand, and would he just say "communicates" and "knows" don't fit because the falsehood was never in my head? Would Fuller say we have to read the statute by its purpose -- protecting the public from false alarms -- and that I caused a false alarm, full stop, so the purpose reaches me even if the words strain? I'm trying to see which side gives the better account of what's happening when the relevant "statement" was produced by a model under my control but not in my mind.
