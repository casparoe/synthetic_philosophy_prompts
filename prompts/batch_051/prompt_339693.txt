I'm a software engineer working on a consumer mobile app, and I've been trying to get clear on something that has been bothering me. Our team's primary success metrics are daily active users and time spent in the app, and the design process is organized around building what we internally call habit loops. We use an infinite feed so there is no natural stopping point, auto-play the next item, delay and re-rank push notifications so they arrive when people are most likely to open the app, and A/B test every change against engagement. At the same time, our public values talk about giving users what they genuinely want and putting them in control, and we do ship things like a screen-time dashboard and a setting to turn autoplay off — though those controls are buried several menus deeper than the features that keep people using, and nothing in the product ever suggests that a person has done enough and should stop.

I'm trying to work out what this whole setup assumes about human choice and what makes a life go well. On the one hand, the design practice seems to assume that if a person keeps using the product, their behavior reveals what they prefer, and that more use is at worst neutral. On the other hand, we deliberately remove stopping cues, lean on well-known failures of self-control, and make it harder to leave or to find the limits we claim to offer, which seems to assume that people's moment-to-moment choices can be steered away from what they would reflectively want. What view of human decision-making does a design practice of this sort presuppose, and is it actually coherent, or does it quietly switch between two incompatible views of the user depending on which is convenient? Is it genuinely committed to one of these views, or is the practice compatible with several rival views that happen to license the same metrics? And what would we have to change in our metrics, in what gets placed where, or in which features are emphasized, if we instead started from the assumption that a person's attention is a limited resource that can be spent against their own interests?
