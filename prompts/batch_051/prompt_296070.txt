Since I was given responsibility for model selection in an AI decision-support system, I have been puzzled by a norm among my colleagues that I am expected to apply. They prefer a model that scores slightly lower on our accuracy benchmarks when its outputs come with a step-by-step rationale that a human reviewer can check, and they are reluctant to deploy a model that cannot be retraced even when it is more reliable. The rationales, however, are usually produced after the fact by a separate module, and the underlying system remains opaque, so the rationale appears to add nothing to our knowledge of how the answer was actually reached. What theory of understanding, epistemic trust, or justification does this practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit epistemology, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it is merely compatible with, and say whether its parts are consistent with the same field's reliance on black-box benchmarking. Then explain what would change in evaluation and deployment if the presupposed view were replaced by a purely predictivist one, and what would change under a demand for mechanistic interpretability instead.
