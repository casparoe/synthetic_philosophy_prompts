I've been poking at the omnipotence paradox again — can God make a stone so heavy even God can't lift it — and I'm struck by how little the dialectic has moved. The logical-impossibility camp says omnipotence ranges only over what's logically possible, so making such a stone isn't a thing to be done in the first place. The incoherence camp says the phrase 'a stone an omnipotent being cannot lift' is like 'square circle,' so the question has no content. The definitional camp says we should just define omnipotence as maximal power, not the ability to do literally anything. Then there are the bullet-biters who say fine, God isn't omnipotent in that sense. Everyone knows all the moves and nobody gets persuaded.

I don't want a survey and I don't want a verdict for one side; I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself. My own hunch is that its mostly verbal — that the camps are using 'can' and 'omnipotence' in systematically different senses, and once you fix the semantics the paradox mostly evaporates. But even if that's right, I'd like you to spell out what each side is actually committed to and whether any residual disagreement is substantive — say, about whether being constrained by logic is a genuine limitation on divine power, or about what a "power" is. If I'm wrong, tell me what the real non-verbal disagreement is. And either way, say what would actually count as progress here: a formal semantics, a theological argument, a modal theorem, or just dropping the question.

When you answer, lead with the diagnosis in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
