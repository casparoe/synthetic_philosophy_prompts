I'm a software engineer and I somehow ended up on my company's metrics council: we review any new metric a team wants to be held to before it goes into OKRs, comp, perf reviews, the works. We've built up a set of precedents over the last year, and now there's a proposal in front of us that I honestly think is undetermined by what we've done before. I want to know what we should decide if we care about being consistent, and what principals we'd actually be committing to.

Our past calls, roughly:
- Rejected lines of code as a dev productivity metric, because it becomes a diff-padding contest.
- Rejected tickets closed per week for support, because people cherry-pick easy tickets and close stuff without actually solving it.
- Rejected number of features shipped per quarter for PMs, because it rewards shipping trivial junk nobody asked for.
- Rejected average first-response time for support as a target, because agents would just send a canned "we're on it" and the number would look great while users got nothing.
- Approved CSAT for support, sampled randomly after tickets close, with a minimum response count.
- Approved 30-day cohort retention for growth, measured in a holdout so they can't cherry-pick who gets counted.
- Approved dollar renewal rate for account management, verified by finance from actual contracts.

The new proposal: the mobile team wants to be evaluated on average session length in the app. Their argument is that it's real downstream user behavior, it's measured automatically from telemetry (so no self reporting), and it correlates with engagement, which is the thing the product team actually cares about. My gut says it's more like the first-response-time disaster, since they can inflate it by autoplaying videos, spamming notifications, or making the app annoying to leave without making it any better. But it's also kind of like the ones we approved, because it's not an internal activity tally. So I'm not sure what rule we've actually been running.

Lay out the candiate rules that fit our precedents, what each rule predicts for average session length, which precedent is the closest analog, and what we'd be signing up for under each choice. Then, if we wanted to settle which rule we're really using, give me the cleanest further case to put in front of the council and what each rule would say about it.
