I sit on the release-oversight committee at a small AI lab, and we're about to ship a reasoning model that works through a long internal scratchpad before it answers. The committee is deadlocked over whether to show users the raw scratchpad, a model-written summary, or nothing at all. The transparency faction says the raw scratchpad is the only honest window into what the model actually did—they say its a matter of not letting the model spin a sanitized story. The product-safety faction says the raw scratchpad is just as generated as the final answer, and there's no guarantee it was actually part of the causal process that produced the output, so showing it would dress up a plausible story as the model's real reasoning and mislead users. I need to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's vote. Draft the opinion: recommend one of the three policies with a rollout timeframe, name one concrete check we should run after launch before reopening the question, and engage the strongest version of the losing position seriously.

Throughout, keep two senses of "faithful" apart—the scratchpad having been part of the causal process that produced the answer, versus the scratchpad being a good explanation for a human reader—because people keep blurring them, e.g. treating "the model says it reasoned this way" as evidence for the first. Also, one member keeps suggesting we settle this by asking the model in a private second pass whether its first scratchpad was faithful; include a short paragraph on why that can't settle the question, or under what conditions it could. Assume readers know chain-of-thought prompting but not the faithfulness literature.
