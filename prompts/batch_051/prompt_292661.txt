I am a third-year undergraduate in a seminar on distributive justice, and we have spent the past few sessions on prioritarianism. The view as we have studied it holds that the moral value of a given increment of well-being is positive but greater when the recipient is at a lower absolute level of well-being, while rejecting any lexical priority for the worse off. The standard leveling-down objection is supposed to show that any view with an egalitarian element would recommend, in some cases, making the better off worse off without benefiting anyone; the usual prioritarian reply is that only benefits to the worse off count, so leveling down is never recommended. That much is clear to me. What I want to understand is what would actually count against this view if we treat it not as a fixed slogan but as a live research program.

If you were defending prioritarianism and were being maximally honest, what observations, cases, or formal results would you regard as counting against it? I am especially interested in the following kinds of pressure: cases in which many trivial benefits to the slightly worse off outweigh a very large benefit to the slightly better off, even though the absolute difference in well-being between the two groups is small; the fact that the priority weighting function seems underdetermined by the broad intuitive idea; the possibility that the view collapses into utilitarianism once the weighting function is set near equality, or into a fairness-based view once one explains why the worse off matter more; and the measurement problems involved in saying who is at what absolute level of well-being. Which of these are merely awkward results that can be absorbed by adjusting the function, and which would be decisive if established? Would defenders themselves concede that, for example, a demonstration that there is no nonarbitrary way to specify the weighting function would undermine the view, or would they treat the function as a primitive moral fact that needs no independent justification? I would also like an honest assessment of whether prioritarianism is currently insulated by ad hoc adjustments, and whether that insulation is a sign of degeneracy or of a healthy research program. I personally am most worried about the measurement problem, but I want the full map of where the view is exposed before I decide how much weight to give it.
