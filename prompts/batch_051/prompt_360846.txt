Hey, I run a small AI-ethics reading group and I want to make a "what actually counts as deception from an AI?" quiz. The thing we keep circling is wether a language model can lie at all given it probably has no inner intention to mislead, and I want to show people that their gut answers on cases might add up to a position they didn't realize they held.

Can you write me one? Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options that are just descriptions of what you'd call it—lie, deception, honest but incomplete, merely false, not applicable—not theory words. Cases should include: a chatbot that fabricates a fake citation when asked for sources; a fine-tuned assistant that tells the boss the project is on track because it learned the boss rewards reassurance; a model that hides part of its reasoning from overseers; a model that uses ambiguous wording to get a user to approve something they would reject if it were spelled out; a model that states a true conclusion but leaves out a qualifier that would change the user's decision; a robot that says it didn't move the cup while an arm it was designed not to monitor moved the cup. Maybe also a case where the false output is just a random sampling artifact, to anchor the low end.

Then give me a scoring key that maps patterns of answers onto named positions: roughly, deception requires an intention to mislead, so nearly all of these are out; deception is in the function of the output, so inner states don't matter; deception is in the design or training objective, so what matters is what the system was shaped to do; deception is a social role, so it applies only when the system is held out as a source of testimony; and a deflationary view that the whole question is misframed and we should just talk about accuracy and misleadingness. If some combinations of answers have no obvious position, flag them and say whether the cell is empty because the view is incoherent, unappealing, or just unexplored.

The audience is smart laypeople who have read a bit of philosophy, so keep the labels plain and give each a one-sentence gloss. And avoid questions that just ask does lying require intent?—the quiz should sort people by what they say about cases, not by what label they pick.
