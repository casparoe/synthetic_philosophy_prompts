I keep running into "taxation is theft" — my roommate says it every time he looks at his pay stub, and a guy at a party used it to dismiss public schools altogether. I don't want to debate it, I want to know what the sentence could even be claiming. As a legal matter it's obviously false (taxes are collected under law; theft is a crime), so the interesting versions have to be moral or political claims. But I can think of several different ones and they don't all say the same thing: (1) taking someone's money without their individual, actual consent is always wrong, full stop; (2) voting for the government that taxes you doesn't amount to consenting to the tax; (3) the state never gets legitimate title to what it takes, even when it spends the money on things you happen to like; (4) specifically redistributive taxes are theft, but paying for public goods you actually use is more like a fee; (5) it's just hyperbole for "the rate is too high and the money is spent on things I never approved." Could you lay out the distinct precise claims this slogan might be making, from the trivial to the radical, and say for each whether it's trivial, defensible, false, or just rhetoric? And which reading is someone actually leaning on when they use it to dismiss public schools or income tax withholding? My suspicion is the slogan is doing too much work: it sounds like one argument but might really be five. Also, please seperate what's settled among people who study this from what's genuinely contested — don't present a contested reading as the consensus. Keep it under 400 words.
