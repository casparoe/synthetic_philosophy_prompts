i've been chewing on something i call the config-file theory of creativity, and i want a straight verdict before it hardens into something i believe. the claim is that all creativity is knob-turning: any domain has a finite-ish set of knobs (in music, tempo, instrumentation, harmonic palette, structure; in programming, constraints, abstractions, error-handling philosophy), and a 'new' work is just an existing work with some knobs set to values nobody had tried. originality is a fresh combination of knob settings, not a new kind of thing, and the big leaps in a field usually come from someone adding a new knob, not from inspiration. it also seems to explain why constraints feel productive, because fewer knobs force you out of the obvious settings, and why a genre starts to feel tapped out once its knob space has been mined. i know it sounds reductive and maybe just a tech metaphor doing too much work. give it to me straight: is there anything here, has someone already worked this out under another name, and what's the clearest example of a real creative jump that just isnt knob-turning on something that already existed?
