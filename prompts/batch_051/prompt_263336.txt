I'm a novelist, and my current project is a near-future novel about a govenment ministry that literally computes welfare scores for policy decisions. The formula takes two numbers for every sentient being: valence, how good or bad its current experience is (say from -10 to +10), and wakefulness, how conscious or "lit up" it is (say from 0 to 1). The book's central fight is over how those two numbers should be combined, and I've realized I actually need to order the positions by how defensible they are before I can write the arguments well.

The three camps I have are: additive, where valence and wakefulness are simply added (maybe with weights); multiplicative, where you multiply them, so a barely conscious good experience counts for almost nothing; and lexical, where wakefulness comes first, so no amount of positive valence in a very dim mind can outweigh the negative valence of a sufficiently awake one—or something close to that. Rank those three from most to least defensible as a way of combining the two factors, and give a few sentences for each on the intuition it captures and the most disturbing concrete case it produces. If you want to give each a score out of ten or put them in tiers as well, that's welcome, as long as there's a clear ranking. Feel free to say the real rule would have to be stranger than all three.
