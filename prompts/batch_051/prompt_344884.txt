I'm TAing an upper-level ethics course and currently grading the short-answer section of a midterm on virtue ethics and moral psychology. The question the students had to answer was: "Explain the situationist critique of virtue ethics. What exactly does the empirical evidence show, and which parts of virtue ethics, if any, does it threaten? Be precise about whose version of the critique you're describing."

One student wrote this:

"Virtue ethics assumes that morally good people have stable character traits like honesty, courage, and compassion, and that these traits explain why they act well across a wide range of situations. The situationist critique, developed by Gilbert Harman and John Doris, uses social psychology to show that this assumption is false. In Darley and Batson's Good Samaritan study, seminary students who were told to hurry were much less likely to stop and help a man slumped in a doorway, and the topic of the talk they'd been assigned made almost no difference. In Milgram's obedience experiments, ordinary people administered what they believed were severe shocks to a learner when an experimenter told them to continue. And Isen and Levin found that people who found a dime in a pay phone were more likely to help someone who had dropped papers. Harman and Doris both conclude from this kind of evidence that there just are no character traits: what we think of as a compassionate or honest person is mostly an illusion produced by the fundamental attribution error. Since virtue ethics is built on the claim that people actually have such traits, the situationist critique shows that virtue ethics is empirically false as a theory of how to live."

I've gone back and forth on how to mark it, and I'd like a second set of eyes before I commit to a number. Could you grade this answer out of 10 and write the specific, point-by-point comments you would leave on the exam to justify that grade? I'd rather not grade it on a general impression, so please make the comments concrete: what does the answer get right, what does it get wrong or gloss over, and how do those specific points add up to the grade you give.
