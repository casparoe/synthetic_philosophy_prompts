Ever since my sister and I took over my dad's exterminating business last spring, I've been the one doing the hiring, and the whole thing has made me wierdly philosophical. Our practice is this: we set a cutoff for new techs -- licensed, passes the basic test, background check comes back clean, interview isn't a disaster -- then we take the first person who clears all of it, even though we both know that if we waited a few more weeks we'd probably find someone stronger. My sister says that's just irrational: if you're paying a wage either way, you should hire the best person you can find, not whoever happens to get over the bar. But I have a feeling the cutoff-and-take-the-first system is doing something else, not just being lazy.

What I want to know is what this practice actually presupposes about rational choice. Does it commit us to thinking that "good enough" is the right target and that maximizing is the wrong ideal for real people with limited time and information? Or is the practice still compatible with maximizing, once you count the cost of searching itself -- and if so, what is the cutoff really doing? I also noticed the two halves might point different ways: setting a cutoff is about keeping someone out, but taking the first person above it is about not caring which acceptable person you get. Are those the same commitment or two different ones? And what would actually have to change in how we hire if we adopted my sister's maximizer view -- would we ever be in a position to stop looking?
