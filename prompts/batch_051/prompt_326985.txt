A friend of mine who does corpus linguistics has gotten pretty insistent that words don't have meanings. His line is that a word like 'draft' is just a distribution of uses: it turns up near talk of writing, near talk of air currents, near talk of beer, near talk of conscription, and these uses hang together by historical accident, not by any shared content. On his view, the 'meaning' of draft is something we retroactively smooth over that distribution; the word itself only has a semantic range. I keep wanting to object that a word can have several determinate meanings that context selects among — 'a draft of the paper' doesn't feel merely distributional — but I can't think of an observation that would actually seperate his view from mine. How would one test whether a word has a meaning (or several) rather than merely a distribution of uses? Give me a concrete experimental design, say what result would support each reading, and be honest about what even a clean result would leave unsettled.
