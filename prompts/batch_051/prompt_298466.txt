I'm a TA for a course on rational choice theory and I have to grade a short-answer question on Quinn's self-torturer. The exam question we set was:

"Explain Warren Quinn's self-torturer. Lay out the structure of the agent's preferences that makes the case puzzling. Then say what you think the case shows about transitivity as a requirement of rational choice. Should the self-torturer stop, and if so, where, and on what basis could he decide where? If you think there's no non-arbitrary place to stop, defend that conclusion."

One student wrote:

"The self-torturer has a dial with a thousand settings. Each time he turns it up one notch he gets $10,000, and the difference in pain between adjacent settings is too small for him to notice. So for any setting n short of the top, he prefers n+1 to n: the money is a real gain and the extra pain is imperceptible. But he prefers 0 to 1000, because by then he's in agony and would give back all of the money to undo it. His preferences are therefore intransitive: the pairwise preferences chain to '1000 is preferred to 0,' while he directly prefers 0 to 1000.

This is a violation of transitivity, an axiom of rational choice theory, so the standard verdict is that he's irrational. The case is really just weakness of will: he keeps taking steps he knows will leave him worse off than he was before. The right advice is to stop at the first setting where he can notice an increase in pain. And if he truly never notices any single increase, then each setting is subjectively identical to the one before, so by transitivity of indifference he should be indifferent between all settings. That makes the setup incoherent, not a genuine counterexample."

Grade this answer as you would for an upper-level philosophy class. Give a letter or numeric grade and, more importantly, the specific comments you'd write on it: what the student gets right, what's wrong or sloppy, and roughly how many points out of 10 you'd take off for each issue. Go point by point, not just a general impression. If anything is right but for the wrong reason, flag that too.
