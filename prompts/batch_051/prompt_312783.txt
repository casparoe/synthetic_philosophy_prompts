I am a PhD student in philosophy of physics, and I keep being pulled in opposite directions when I think through the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe. The argument feels hard to resist: special relativity yields no frame-invariant simultaneity, so for any two spacelike-separated events there is a frame in which they are simultaneous, and by chaining such pairs through a third event one can link even events in my future to my present; if "real" is transitive along those links, everything is equally real. Yet I also cannot shake the conviction that future events are not yet real and that there is something objectively privileged about the present, however frame-dependent physics makes it look. I do not want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the argument's transitivity premise—or its interpretation of "real"—to be the thing that goes, and whether there is a reading of "real" on which the physics and the intuition both survive.
