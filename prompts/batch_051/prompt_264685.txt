I recently had a long argument with a friend about whether Nietzsche's early essay on history can still tell us anything about how we now live with the past. In the second of his Untimely Meditations, the essay usually translated as On the Uses and Disadvantages of History for Life, he distinguishes three ways in which the past can serve life: monumental history, which seeks past greatness as a model for present striving; antiquarian history, which preserves and venerates what has been handed down; and critical history, which hauls the past before a tribunal, breaks it up, and condemns whatever blocks the path of life. He also warns that each of these modes becomes a way of being imprisoned by the past rather than animated by it when it grows out of proportion to the others.

My friend insisted that the essay is fundamentally a diagnosis of historical culture, and that its categories can be applied to developments Nietzsche never saw. I pushed back, arguing that the scale and permanence of digital memory may change the phenomenon rather than merely extend it. The concrete cases we kept circling back to were these: searchable, indefinitely retained digital archives that preserve every minor utterance and image; recent technologies that let people generate moving, speaking images of the dead; and the so-called right to be forgotten, by which a person can demand that parts of their own past be removed from online records and search results. Suppose Nietzsche were shown these developments. Would he regard them as an extreme form of one or another of the three modes of history, or as something that breaks the scheme? Which mode or combination of modes would he think is most needed for life under these conditions, and why? Since we could not settle it, I would like a reasoned prediction that stays close to the essay's own criteria, and that says plainly where the essay would have to be stretched. Please begin with your bottom-line answer in the first sentence, then give the reasoning without preamble or restating the question, and write in British English.
