I was arguing with a friend about whether Kepler’s ellipses are closer to the truth than Ptolemy’s epicycles, and I realized I don’t actually have a principled way to say that—let alone defend it. So I went back to Popper’s Conjectures and Refutations, specifically the part where he defines verisimilitude by subtracting falsity content from truth content: a theory is closer to the truth if it implies more truths and fewer falsehoods, roughly. But then I started reading the later criticisms, and it seems like people mostly agree that particular definition doesn’t work, while still wanting some notion of closeness to truth.

Before I read any more attempts to replace it, I want the desiderata spelled out. What does any adequate theory of verisimilitude actually have to get right? Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. For each requirement, say which cases or results force it, and tell me which one you think is the hardest to satisfy for any view that keeps Popper’s underlying idea.
