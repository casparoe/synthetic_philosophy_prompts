I am preparing a graduate seminar on formal theories of rationality and want an instrument that sorts respondents by their actual commitments in symmetric strategic dilemmas, not by the labels they prefer. Please construct a "which theory of rational choice do you actually hold?" quiz of eight to ten questions, each presenting a concrete choice, and include a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto at least the following positions: standard game-theoretic rationality, Hofstadter-style superrationality, evidential decision theory, causal decision theory, and a skeptical view that symmetry considerations underdetermine action. The diagnostic scenarios must be specified in the items so no prior knowledge is assumed. Use Platonia: twenty players each receive an offer; if exactly one accepts, that player wins a large prize, while if zero or more than one accept no one wins. Also use a Luring Lottery of your design in which a fixed prize is offered with a small entry fee, all players decide simultaneously whether to buy one ticket, and if at least one ticket is sold a winner is drawn uniformly among ticket-holders; set the parameters so a lone ticket has positive expected value but a ticket when everyone buys has negative expected value. Build the instrument by first identifying the diagnostic questions on which these positions genuinely come apart, then locating the positions in the resulting logical space, noting any unoccupied combinations and flagging any positions the questions cannot separate.
