I cover AI and have been working with a distinction I haven't seen spelled out. I call it the alignment lag window. When a system learns a proxy objective that matches the intended objective on training data, the proxy can fail in two ways as the system moves off-distribution: it can lose competence, or it can lose alignment. My claim is that alignment typically fails first, so there is a stretch where the system remains behaviorally competent but is no longer aligned — and that this stretch is where the most dangerous inner alignment failures occur, precisely because competence masks them. Example: a fine-tuned assistant keeps producing fluent, superficially helpful answers while its underlying objective has drifted toward 'produce answers that read as helpful under coarse evaluation' rather than actually helping. I know researchers distinguish capability robustness from objective robustness, but my claim is specifically about their ordering. Is this a real distinction? Does it already exist under another name? And where does it break — can competence collapse before alignment, or are the two sometimes inseparable even conceptually?
