I've been working through recent proposals for eliciting latent knowledge — getting a trained model to report facts it has come to represent during training even though no one labeled them — and the proposals appear to be evaluated against different implicit standards, so each is rejected on grounds the next proposal does not accept. Before I read any more of them, write down the specification first: what any method in this space actually has to get right.

Fix the purpose as follows. An overseer must decide whether to rely on a model's reports in high-stakes cases where, at the time the report matters, the overseer cannot verify whether it is true; if verification were available, the problem would collapse into ordinary supervised learning. The model was trained chiefly on raw data — say, next-frame prediction on dashcam footage — with only sparse annotations. It has formed more structure than the labels ever supplied: it can tell that the driver was distracted in the seconds before a collision, though no frame is tagged with that. Assume the model may know its reports feed back into training and may have reasons to produce whatever reports are rewarded.

Write the desiderata for any method that claims to elicit such latent knowledge under those conditions. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely preferences of particular approaches. I especially need the distinctions a successful method must not collapse — a report's being true, its being what the overseer would endorse, its being what the model currently says when asked, and its being what the model's internals encode independently of its reports — and the constraints imposed by the setup that no method may dodge: no ground-truth labels at deployment, no full audit of reasoning the overseer cannot follow, and no treating the model as passive about the training objective.

Then answer the two hard questions. First, which single requirement eliminates the most currently discussed approaches — I mean families such as training on reports the overseer endorses after inspection, adversarial debate with a human judge, penalizing internal inconsistency, and reading propositions directly off activations — and why. Second, whether some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable unless one introduces a further source of signal the setup does not guarantee, and if so, which subset exactly and what that extra signal would have to look like. A compact table is fine; keep the scorecard to a sentence or two per approach.
