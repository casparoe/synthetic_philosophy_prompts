I am a high-school student who helps run our school's history society, and I would like a runnable workshop design on a topic we keep circling without landing: when a community's shared memory conflicts with the historical record, which should give way? The session would be for sixteen students aged sixteen to seventeen, in a single sixty-minute period, with no budget beyond index cards, paper, and a whiteboard. I do not want a lecture or a discussion that merely circles the question; I want the participants to run into the problem themselves and feel its pull before anyone names it.

Please design an activity in which participants first come to hold, or invest in, a shared memory of an event, then encounter an official record that contradicts part of it, and finally must make a binding choice with small but real stakes, such as the wording of a commemorative plaque, a school Founder's Day account, a community archive entry, or a local history pamphlet. The event may be wholly invented; what matters is that the memory and the record contradict each other cleanly, and that each side has something humanly serious in its favour.

The design should include roles and rules, and a decision rule that makes the choice costly no matter what is decided. Please give a minute-by-minute timeline, the exact prompts or materials needed, and small incentives that push participants to take the choice seriously. Then provide a debrief script that maps specific moments to the underlying idea: the present-day function of memory, the different authority of the record, and what is lost when either side is simply allowed to win.

Also say what will predictably go wrong when the activity is run, and how the facilitator should handle it. Finally, be honest about which feature of the real conflict the activity cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply afterwards. Please answer in British English.
