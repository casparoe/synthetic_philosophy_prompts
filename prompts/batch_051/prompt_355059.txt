I recently joined the allocation subcommittee of our regional health board after a family member's treatment was deferred in favor of interventions for patients who were, by any measure, already substantially better off. In preparation for our first deliberative session, I want the committee to produce an adversarial-collaboration working paper between two members who represent the clearest opposing positions on this issue.

One member is a prioritarian: she holds that benefits matter more the lower the absolute level of well-being of the person who receives them, but that any loss of well-being counts against an outcome; hence she regards the leveling-down objection as decisive against egalitarianism. The other member is a telic egalitarian: he holds that equality itself is non-instrumentally valuable, so a change that makes the better off worse off while benefiting no one has at least one good-making feature, even if it is not on balance better.

Draft the joint statement these two members could both sign. It should contain:

1. A numbered list of claims both accept, specifying the value functions each uses, what each means by 'worse off', how the leveling-down objection is stated, and which outcomes each function is committed to ranking as better, worse, or not ordered.
2. A table of residual disagreements, with each side's own position stated in wording that side would endorse.
3. Three to five cruxes, stated as subquestions such that a settled answer would move at least one side. Include at least one crux about ex ante versus ex post priority under risk and at least one about whether the prioritarian weighting function is a primitive moral commitment or a derivative of equality concerns.
4. For each crux, a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Give particular attention to whether any part of the dispute over leveling down is verbal, and say exactly which terms would need to be disambiguated to dissolve that part. Please write the document in British English.
