I keep noticing a pattern in discussions of moral change, and I do not have a word for it. It first stood out while I was reading about whether certain nonhuman animals should be treated as morally considerable, and then I noticed the same structure in debates about obligations to future people and distant strangers. The pattern is this: a proposal is presented as an expansion of moral concern—a group that had been outside is brought in. But when I look at what actually happened to the boundary, it was not simply pushed outward; the criterion of inclusion changed. A new property is offered as the basis on which the group belongs: a particular kind of conscious experience, say, or a particular kind of agency. Yet the same criterion, taken as stated, leaves some people who had long been treated as clearly inside in a more ambiguous position than before, because they display the property only partially, or only potentially, or only in a different mode. Meanwhile the earlier criterion—whatever used to do the work—is quietly retired. The reform is then narrated, by its advocates and later by others, as the circle getting larger. Perhaps the outcome is an improvement; what I want to know is whether the description “larger” is actually being earned.

Does this configuration have an established name? I am aware of the familiar story of the expanding circle, but this looks like a complication for it rather than an instance of it. I am not interested in a label by itself; I want to understand the underlying structure. Is there a principled test for when a change narrated as an expansion really is an expansion—the old criterion preserved and its scope extended—and when it is a redrawing of the boundary under expansionist language? What existing concepts, if any, mark that difference? If there is no established name, I would want to know what it should be called and what a definition would have to say to avoid simply gerrymandering the boundary.
