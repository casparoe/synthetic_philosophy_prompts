Write a parody of a crisis-communications memo, in the deadpan voice of a high-priced public-relations consultancy, addressed to the board of a fictional multinational that has just caused an environmental disaster and now must apologize to the public. The memo should advise the company on how to perform contrition while, in the same motion, dissolving the conditions under which the corporation could count as a blamable moral agent. It should press, through the advice itself, on the real fault lines in the corporate-moral-agency debate: whether a firm has the unified intentions a blameworthy agent would need; whether a formal decision structure composed of committees, delegated authority, and sign-off chains could itself be the subject of blame, or whether those devices merely subdivide an act until no individual's share reaches the threshold for culpability; and whether corporate personhood can be claimed for rights and standing while being quietly disclaimed for responsibility. The humor should come from the precision of the counsel rather than from broad jokes: sentence-level guidance on grammatical voice and the strategic use of the passive; rules about which executives may apologize and which must let the entity speak; a formula for distributing regret through the organizational chart so that each individual's portion is statistically negligible while the corporation expresses unlimited sorrow; and a closing section on remediation that converts accountability into the creation of new committees, reporting lines, and a standing board-level review of regret allocation. Around 800 words, formatted as a memo with section headings. Keep it legible to a reader who has never encountered the corporate-agency literature, while a specialist should recognize exactly which positions are being skewered and find the skewering fair. Stay deadpan throughout and never announce the target.
