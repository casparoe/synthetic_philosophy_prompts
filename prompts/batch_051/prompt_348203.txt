I am a novelist, and I have spent the past few weeks trying to make my use of the word art coherent. The account I keep returning to is Tolstoy's: what matters is not beauty or technical skill but whether the maker has lived through some feeling and arranged outward signs with the deliberate aim of passing that feeling to an audience, so that the audience is infected by it; sincerity and universal accessibility carry the weight that craft and originality usually receive. I would like a working definition of art, in something like that spirit, that handles the following cases. A gifted actor moves an audience to tears by summoning emotions he does not himself feel; a private diary, written without thought of readers, is published after the author's death and affects strangers; a political speech deliberately stirs a crowd to anger; a child makes a crude drawing and gives it to a parent; a technically flawless sonata leaves a concert hall unmoved; an advertisement is engineered to make viewers feel nostalgia; and an AI-generated image genuinely moves people although no human being felt that emotion before the image was made. My own instincts are that the child's drawing is art and the cold sonata is not, and that I cannot settle the actor, the advertisement, and the AI image. Propose the definition, run it through the seven cases, and tell me where it forces a verdict I should resist or where it surprises me. Please answer in continuous prose only, as though writing a letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
