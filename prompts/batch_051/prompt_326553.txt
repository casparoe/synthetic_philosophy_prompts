In 2022, Google engineer Blake Lemoine publicly claimed that the company's LaMDA language model was sentient, citing conversations in which the system described itself as having feelings, fears, and a sense of self. Google dismissed the claim as unsupported by evidence, and Lemoine was placed on administrative leave, but the episode reignited a public debate about whether a language model could literally understand language or merely simulate understanding. I've seen the usual takes on both sides, but I want an analysis that actually engages the philosophical structure rather than repeating the standard pro- and anti-AI-sentience positions.

Searle's Chinese Room argument holds that a person following a program for manipulating Chinese symbols could pass for a Chinese speaker without understanding any Chinese. From this he concludes that implementing a program, no matter how behaviorally convincing, cannot by itself produce original intentionality; any intentionality the system seems to have is merely derived from the intentionality of its designers and users, and the system itself has none. Dennett's response, as I understand it, is that the person in the room is just a two-bitser—a small, replaceable component of a larger physical system—and that it is a mistake to conclude that because the component lacks understanding, the whole system must lack it. On this view, the system may have its own intentionality, not derived from the component's mental states but realized across the whole arrangement of symbols, rules, and memory.

Using the Lemoine–LaMDA episode as the case, analyze it through this theoretical lens. In particular: Who or what is the analog of the person in the room, and who or what is the analog of the whole system? Does the theory say that LaMDA could have original intentionality, or only derived intentionality, or does the distinction break down in a case where the "component" is a neural network rather than a person? Which features of the episode does the original/derived distinction explain—for instance, the fact that Lemoine's evidence was entirely behavioral while Google's engineers saw only a next-token predictor—and which features does it misdescribe or remain silent about? Finally, what does the episode in turn show about the theory: does it vindicate Dennett's insistence that we look at the whole system rather than a component, or does it suggest that the original/derived distinction was a false comfort even before LLMs arrived?
