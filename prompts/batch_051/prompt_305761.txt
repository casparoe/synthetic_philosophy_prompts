I want you to draft a satirical sidebar for me: an ironic op-ed, around 650 words, in the voice of a Minister of Justice in a small country that has reorganized its whole legal system around Pascal's argument in the Pensées that justice without force is powerless and force without justice is tyranny, and that the only remedy is to weld the two together. Please paraphrase Pascal rather than quote him — I dont want us to get the wording of the fragments wrong.

The minister has to be a consistent Pascalian, not a cartoon villain. Every policy he boasts about should follow from trying to make justice and force one and the same thing. For instance, he can brag that he has merged the courts and the police into a single Office of Rightful Force; that written judgments have been abolished in favor of the custom that the strongest have always enforced; that appealing a verdict is now a crime because it treats an already-enforced judgment as merely one opinion among others; and that the public no longer asks whether a ruling is just, only whether it is carried out — which he regards as progress. The irony must come from the internal logic of the position pushed to its natural end, not from the minister ever noticing the objection.

By the end, a reader should feel the real question: once you insist that justice is worthless unless backed by force, is there any difference left between a just law and a successful exercise of force? The piece should land the standard objections — who gets to define justice, whether the strong end up being called just simply because they are strong, whether the people are being managed rather than governed — without ever stating them. Keep it completely deadpan, no winking narrator. And make the jokes come from getting Pascal right: the role of custom, opinion, and the way people mistake enforced habit for right. No title needed.
