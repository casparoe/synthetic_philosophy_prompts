So I've been chewing on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument, the one that says nobody can ever be truly morally responsible for anything, and I want a fresh reply to it. Quick version, in case it helps level-set: what you do is a result of the way you are. To be truly responsible for what you do, you'd have to be truly responsible for the way you are, at least in the respects that matter. But you can't be responsible for the way you are, because being responsible for it would require having chosen to be that way, and any choosing you did would itself be a product of the way you already were. So you'd have to be causa sui—the cause of yourself—to get the regress started, and that's impossible; heredity and early environment got there first, and every later attempt at self-change is just one more move issued by the self you never got to pick.

These are the standard replies I already know, so don't give me any of these:

1. The reactive-attitudes reply (P.F. Strawson—yes, Galen's dad): moral responsibility lives in our practices of resentment, gratitude, blame, and forgiveness, and those practices neither claim nor need anyone to be self-made, so the Basic Argument is aimed at a target the practices never had.

2. The compatibilist quality-of-will reply: Strawson is working with an inflated notion of "true" responsibility. What ordinary blame and praise care about is the quality of will your action expresses—whether it issues from your own cares, values, and reasoning—not whether you created those cares and values.

3. The regress-can-stop reply: there's no rule that every chain of responsibility has to bottom out in a first link. You can simply stop at the self you find yourself with and hold it responsible from there; the infinite regress is a paper tiger.

4. The overgeneralization reply: if you need to be responsible for the causes of a thing to be responsible for the thing itself, you're equally not responsible for your beliefs, your sanity, or for finding the Basic Argument persuasive. That either undercuts the argument or shows it proves too much.

5. The no-self reply: the argument assumes a substantive self that would need to be self-created before it could be responsible, but if the self is a process, a narrative, or a useful fiction, then the demand to have created it dissolves instead of landing Strawson's conclusion.

6. The revisionist reply: fine, the kind of responsibility Strawson says is impossible really is impossible, but that just means we should revise our notion of responsibility rather than abolish the practice—blame and credit earn their keep as social regulation, and they never needed the self-created self.

7. The libertarian agent-causal reply: deny the setup—an agent can be an uncaused cause of its own subsequent character changes, so responsibility doesn't require prior self-creation.

So here's the task. I want one reply to the Basic Argument that is not on the list and not just a rewording, special case, or combination of anything on it. It also can't be a dodge like "Strawson is only talking about a weird technical sense of 'truly' responsible" unless the dodge actualy does real work in defusing the whole argument. Then tell me honestly whether this new reply is any good and how it sits relative to the seven above.
