I've been going back and forth with a friend about the feeling of being a self, and he gave me a little argument I can't quite shake. The setup is this: a representation is transparent when you don't experience the representation itself, only what it represents — like looking through a clean window, where you see the yard and not the glass, whereas you can also notice the glass itself if you try. His claim is that the sense of self is normally transparent in the same way: in ordinary intropection you don't catch a self-model doing the representing; you just feel the self is directly there.

Here is the argument as he laid it out. (1) If introspection gives you direct awareness of a self, then the felt self is not merely the content of a representation; you are in touch with something that is not just a model. (2) But on the self-model hypothesis, the brain builds a model of the organism as a whole, and this model is normally transparent — you are aware only of the self it depicts, not of the modeling. (3) From the inside, a transparent self-model would feel exactly like direct contact with a real self, even if there were no self beyond the model. (4) So the experience of being directly confronted with a self is predicted just as well by the transparent-model hypothesis as by the direct-contact view. (5) The direct-contact view gets almost all of its support from exactly that experience. (6) If a hypothesis's only support is evidence that is equally consistent with its rival, that evidence cannot justify preferring it. (7) So belief in a substantial self over and above a transparent model is not justified by introspection, and unless there is other evidence the rational stance is to suspend it.

Tell me whether this argument goes through. If it fails, identify the first step that fails and give the closest repair you can. If it goes through, which premise should a defender of the direct-contact view attack, and why? Hard cap: 400 words.
