I've been going back and forth with a friend about the old "could a book be conscious?" style of thought experiment. The setup I keep coming back to is this: imagine a book whose pages encode a complete neural history of a particular person, page after page, so that reading the pages in order at the right speed runs through the same sequence of states their brain actually went through, and the process is supposed to reproduce their stream of thought. The usual line my friend defends is that the pattern is what matters, not the paper, so the mind is "in" the book whenever somebody runs through it correctly. I want to stress-test that line with a variant we can't settle.

Take that same book — two billion or so pages, each page encoding one second of the original person's full neural configuration, birth to death — and tear it apart. Every page goes to a different person in a different timezone. Once a day, at a time chosen by a random number generator, each page-holder recites their single page aloud in exactly one second (grant that this is possible in principle). Nothing is coordinated, so across a day every page gets performed exactly once, but in a random order with plenty of overlap. Each performance is recorded on its own little file, and in principle you could concatenate the files in the correct order and get a complete, correctly sequenced readout of that entire life. In practice nobody ever does; the archive just sits their as two billion unsorted clips.

During a typical day, is the original person's conscious experience happening somewhere, even if it's sliced one second at a time and scattered across the globe? Is there one fragmented mind running through the readings, as many truncated minds as there are clips, or just noise? Does the unsorted archive count as a person, and would that change if once a year somebody played a random thirty-minute block in the correct internal order? I'd like a careful walkthrough of what the "pattern is all that matters" view has to say about each of these, and what a more biological-chauvinist view (only brain-like stuff counts) would say, and where exactly the two views come apart in this version.
