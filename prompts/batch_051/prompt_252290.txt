My kid asked me at breakfast whether 2 is inside 4, like whether 2 is one of the parts 4 is made out of, and I realized I couldn't give a straight answer. I fell back down the structuralism rabbit hole and now I have this little argument, but I half-suspect its too slick. Here it is:

(1) Arithmetic is about successor, addition, and multiplication. Set membership never shows up in arithmetic, so "2 is an element of 4" isn't a claim of arithmetic.

(2) The natural numbers are completely characterized by their place in the successor structure, not by any particular set-theoretic construction of them.

(3) If a claim about numbers gets its truth value only from an arbitrary choice of encoding, then it isn't a real fact about the numbers themselves.

(4) On the von Neumann encoding, 4 = {0,1,2,3}, so 2 is genuinely an element of 4. On the Zermelo encoding, 4 = {3}, so 2 isn't an element of 4.

(5) So "is 2 an element of 4?" has no determinate answer, and treating it as if it did is a category mistake.

Is the argument valid? If not, which step should a defender of the plain set-theoretic reduction (numbers just are sets) attack, and on what reading of that step? Give me exactly five numbered responses, one per step, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion.
