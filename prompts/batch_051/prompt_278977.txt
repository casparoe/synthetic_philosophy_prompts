My friend and I have been going back and forth about when you're allowed to reason that if you do a thing, everyone who thinks like you will do it too. She's got a really worked-out position and I'm trying to pin down what she'd say about a case she hasn't seen. Track record:

- One-shot prisoner's dilemma, against a complete stranger, knows nothing about how the stranger reasons: she defects.
- One-shot prisoner's dilemma, against an exact duplicate of herself — same info up to the start of the game, same decision procedure, and both know it — she cooperates.
- One-shot prisoner's dilemma, against a stranger, but they both know they're running the same publicly posted decision program, and that this is common knowledge: she cooperates.

New case: she's one of 1,000 people in a lottery. Each person either sends in a postcard or doesn't. If exactly one person sends, that person gets $1,000,000 and everyone else gets $0. If nobody sends, everyone gets $100. If two or more send, everyone gets $0. No communicating. Everyone knows the rules, and everyone knows all 1,000 were selected because they gave identical answers on a giant battery of reasoning tasks — so they all know they think alike, but there's no duplicate brain state and no shared posted program. Would she send? I can see at least two rules that fit her past verdicts and give opposite answers here. Which rule is she probably running, why, and what future case would cleanly decide between them?
