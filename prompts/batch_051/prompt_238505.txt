Constitutional monarchies like the UK, Denmark, and Japan keep a hereditary head of state who has no real policy power but still gives formal assent to laws, opens parliament, and in theory could refuse a government's request. The monarch is supposed to be above politics, but the whole arrangement rests on a very specific idea that legitimacy can be inherited and procedural while actual power is democratic. What theory of political authority does this practice presuppose? Is it coherent to say sovereignty lives with the people or parliament while the monarch remains the formal source of all law and authority? And what would have to change legally and conceptually if you tried to keep the same split between ceremonial and effective power but replaced the hereditary monarch with an elected president?
