I sit on my college's disputes panel, which rules on small claims between student societies. During Freshers' Fair, a delivery van struck and wrecked the Philosophy Society's stall in the service yard and drove off; the porter saw the collision but could not make out the livery or registration. The yard is used only by delivery vehicles, and the porter's entry log shows that exactly one van entered during the relevant ten minutes, with no registration noted. The facilities office's records show that nine out of every ten deliveries to the yard that term were made by one firm, Meridian Couriers, with the remainder spread across several other companies. The Philosophy Society claims the cost of the stall from Meridian, and our rules require the claimant to prove the claim on the balance of probabilities. I must circulate a short decision memo before Thursday's meeting: recommend whether the claim should succeed against Meridian, give the reasons, and engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side.
