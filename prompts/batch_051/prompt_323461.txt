I keep hitting a tension between a clean theory of causation and an intuition I cannot abandon. The clean theory says causes must be events, things that actually happen, and that a relation needs two relata; an omission is precisely the absence of an event, so there is nothing there to do the causing. But the intuition is firm: when a deploy fails because I did not run the database migration, my omission caused the outage, and an engineering postmortem that refused to cite omissions would be systematically incomplete. I am not looking for the loose compromise that omissions are causes only in an explanatory or pragmatic sense, unless that sense can be made precise and shown to be more than an evasion. Give me the strongest version of each side. What would it take for the event-only metaphysics to give way, what would it take for the intuition to be debunked or replaced, and is there a precise reading of caused by on which both survive?
