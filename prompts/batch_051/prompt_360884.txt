I run a debate club at a community centre — fourteen regulars, mostly 15 to 19, some of them hyper-competitive and some just tagging along with there mates. We get two hours on a Tuesday night. Lately I've got stuck on a question that keeps me up: we're fine with ranking people at running, chess, even baking, and nobody thinks those rankings corrupt the thing itself. But suggest ranking arguments, scoring a disagreement like a sport, and people act like you've suggested fixing a footrace. I genuinely cant tell if that instinct is right or just snobbery, so I want to test it on the kids without any lecture up front. Could you design an activity or game for one of our sessions, with the rules arranged so the group actually feels the pull of both sides before anyone names it — that competing might sharpen reasoning (they prepare properly, listen for weaknesses, don't let sloppy claims slide) and that it might poison it (they angle for judge points, dodge the hard bits, start treating the person they're arguing with as an obstacle)?

I keep doodling scoring systems: a clean refutation worth the same as a takedown, so the round just ends there and then, or a charity reconstruction rule where you only score after you've restated the other side's point until they say yeah, that's fair. Maybe those are daft, maybe not. I want actual rules, timings for the two-hour slot, a scoring system that doesn't need real money, and a debrief that maps what happened during the game back onto the bigger question about whether making argument a sport improves or corrupts it. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, which part of the idea the game cant reproduce and I'll have to supply myself, and please write the whole answer in British English.
