So one of my cognitive science professors keeps a notebook where she writes down her gut call on weird consciousness cases, and shes freakishly consistent about it. Ive been trying to reverse-engineer what rule shes actually running. Her calls so far:

- A blindsight patient who swears there's a blank spot in her visual field but can point to a light and name its color better than chance: access without experience.
- A guy who drove forty minutes, argued with his passenger about dinner, then realized he had no memory of any of it: he was experiencing the whole time, memory just wasn't recording.
- A current LLM that can describe what it's like to burn your hand more vividly than most people can: no experience, and the eloquence is beside the point.
- An octopus that watched another octopus open a latched jar and then did it with a different technique: yes, something it's like to be that octopus.
- Someone under propofol with a deep-sleep EEG and zero response to commands: not there at all.
- A split-brain patient whose right hemisphere can point to a picture of a hammer that the speaking left hemisphere insists it never saw: the right hemisphere saw it and the left hemisphere honestly didn't.

The new case shes reading about: a patient who is cortically blind but, unlike the usual blindsight patient, doesn't need to be nudged into guessing. She spontaneously says there's a red mug on the table, reaches for it, writes it in her diary, plans her route around furniture, and insists the whole time, quite sincerely, that she has no visual experience at all. The information is fully available for speech, memory, reasoning, planning; the light just never comes on for her.

My question isnt whether the patient is conscious but whether my professor's track record already commits her to a particular verdict here, and whether that verdict comes out the same or different on the access/phenomenal distinction, global workspace theory, and a plain report-based view. What rule is she actually running, which other rules fit her notebook just as well, and what further case would finally force them apart?
