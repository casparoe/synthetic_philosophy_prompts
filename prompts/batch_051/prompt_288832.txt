Take Nozick's experience machine as the base case, but change one assumption. In the standard setup the machine can give you a life with more pleasure than real life, and the hedonist is stuck saying you should plug in because that's what you have most reason to maximize. Now suppose the people building the machine discover that there isn't actually any single hedonic quantity out there to crank up. The things we call pleasures are a messy family of states—relief, absorption, satiation, affection, the click of a solved proof, the burn of exercise, even schadenfreude—and they just don't share any common ingredient. The machine can still fill your days with experiences most people would call pleasant, but there's no fact about how much total pleasure a given simulated life contains, because the pleasures don't aggregate into one number.

How does this change the dialectic? Does the hedonist's case for plugging in collapse, or does it just get pushed back to a more careful formulation? Does Nozick's anti-hedonist argument still need its famous appeal to reality and autonomy, or does the heterogeneity of pleasure do some of that work on its own?

And here's the sharper part: if the hedonist replies that what all pleasures share is just that you want the experience to continue, or that they all light up a common reward pathway, is that enough to answer the heterogeneity problem, or is that just redefining pleasure into something thinner so the maximization talk can survive?
