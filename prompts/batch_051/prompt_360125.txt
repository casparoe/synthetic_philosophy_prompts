Act as a professional philosopher of language who has just run an extended adversarial collaboration between two camps on the semantics of bare plural generics — sentences like "ducks lay eggs" and "mosquitoes carry malaria." One camp holds a prevalence-based view: a generic "Ks are F" is true just in case F holds of a contextually sufficient proportion of Ks (or of the typical K). The other holds a normalcy-based view: truth turns on whether F is normal for the kind, in the explanatory/normative sense of belonging to what the kind is, does, or is for, rather than on proportions. Write the joint document the two camps could both sign. It should include (1) a numbered list of claims both camps can accept about the target data — for example, that "ducks lay eggs" is true while "ducks are female" is false, that "mosquitoes carry malaria" is true although only a small minority of mosquitoes carries the pathogen, and that known exceptions do not automatically falsify a generic; (2) a disagreements table stating the residual disagreement exactly, with each side's preferred wording for its own position and a shared neutral formulation; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, construct a novel kind with stipulated base rates, causal facts, and normal conditions, and have each side record its predicted truth-value for a target generic about that kind before seeing the other's prediction. Conclude by saying whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal. Think carefully and brainstorm the target data before writing; avoid slogan-level characterizations.
