I keep going back and forth on a view that feels right when I argue for it and wrong when I actually play certain games. The view is: games are just rule-systems plus make-believe, so subject matter is ethically inert — calling a counter a 'soldier' rather than a 'knight' or a 'spice trader' does no normative work, and once you've got consent and fair rules a war game is on all fours with chess. But then I play something that is genuinely good at making storming a position feel tense, desperate, and fun, and I can't shake the sense that it's doing something chess is not — something about turning the real horror of war into a source of entertainment. I don't want a ruling on which side wins. Walk me through what it would cost to give up the intuition, what it would cost to give up the theory, and whether there's a reading of 'doing something' on which both survive.
