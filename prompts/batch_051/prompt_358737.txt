I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about how the value of conscious welfare combines across intensity, duration, and number of lives. 1. Within a single mind, one hour at intensity 40 is just as good as four hours at intensity 10.
2. Across two different lives, one person at intensity 75 for one year seems to me better than two people at intensity 40 for one year each, even though the former has fewer total intensity-years (75 against 80).
3. A thousand lives at intensity 60 each is better than a hundred thousand lives at intensity 1 each.
4. Adding one extra life at intensity 3 to a world of ten lives at intensity 60 each makes the world no better; I waver between "no better" and "slightly worse."
5. A world containing one life at −30 and one at +90 is worse than a world containing two lives at +25 each, even though the first has the higher net total (+60 against +50).
6. A world with two lives at 80 and 20 is worse than one with two lives at 45 and 45, even though the first has more total welfare (100 against 90).

Is there a single principle that captures these verdicts, or close? If not, what is the smallest set of candidate principles that jointly covers the pattern, and which of my verdicts does each have to overturn? Also tell me what the favored principle predicts for a case I haven't settled: one life at intensity 90 for one year, against five lives at intensity 20 for two years each.
