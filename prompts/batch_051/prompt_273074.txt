I'm doing a history of math project on when counting stops being just counting and starts actually being mathematics. The candidate thresholds I've collected so far are: tally sticks (like the Ishango bone), where a mark just corresponds to one object; clay tokens and the bullae (sealed clay envelopes) used to keep accounts; the first abstract number words, where a number can be named seperately from any particular bunch of things; and Plimpton 322, the Babylonian tablet that shows someone generating Pythagorean triples rather than merely recording quantities. I need one more candidate threshold that is not basically a version of these — so not another counting device, not another record-keeping/accounting thing, and not just a fancier numeral system. What would you add, and what does it capture that my four miss, and where does it break down as a marker of 'real' mathematics?
