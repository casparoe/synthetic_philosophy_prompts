I'm writing a flowchart post about when a judge gets to ignore what a statue literally says because the result is too stupid — the absurdity doctrine. I want the tree to sort people by how they actually answer cases, not by whether they call themselves textualists. Start from a fixed made-up case: a state makes it a felony to 'use an animal in a race on a public highway,' and the DA charges a guy who rode a horse in a road race; on the literal reading horses are clearly animals, and the drafters probably just forgot to write the exception, or assumed everyone would read it sensibly. Branch on the real forks: whether plain meaning is even fixed before you look at the absurd result; whether the judge is recovering what the legislature actually resolved or what it would have resolved; whether 'absurd' means internally incoherent or bad in the wider world; whether the court may add a fix or only narrow the text, and how deep the edit can go. Leaves should be the actual camps — strong textualism with only scrivener's-error, golden rule, purposivism, old Holy Trinity style result-fixing, the realist 'it's all window dressing' — each with a one-line gloss, and a note wherever your tree can't separate two of them. Also force apart 'it would have been absurd when enacted' and 'it looks absurd now'; I think a lot of people conflate them. Seven to nine nodes max, and every node should be answerable without already knowing the labels.
