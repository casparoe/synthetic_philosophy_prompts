My AP European History textbook summarizes Marx's view of technology roughly like this: Marx welcomed the introduction of labor-saving machinery because it reduces the amount of human labor needed to produce goods, and he regarded automation as a force of progress that would gradually free workers from exhausting work and push society toward something more humane. According to the textbook, that is why Marx was broadly pro-technology.

This seems too tidy to me, because I have also read that Marx described factory machinery as an instrument used against workers, one that caused unemployment, deskilling, and misery under capitalism. I do not know how to put those two pictures together, and I am not sure whether the textbook is being fair to him or just repeating a simplified version that drops the parts that do not fit.

Could you give me an accuracy audit of the textbook summary? Specifically: which parts of it are faithful to Marx, which parts are oversimplified or wrong, and how would a careful account hold together his critique of machinery under capitalism with whatever positive things he said about automation? I would also like to know whether the summary's implied conclusion—that Marx was simply pro-technology—actually follows once the summary is corrected.
