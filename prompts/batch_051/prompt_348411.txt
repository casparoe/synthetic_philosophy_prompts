Consider a legislative proposal that would require employers to obtain specific, informed consent before using brain-computer interfaces to track employees' attention, fatigue, or fragmentary inner speech, and that would classify coerced neural monitoring as a distinctive rights violation rather than as a form of ordinary workplace surveillance. Mill's view, roughly, was that each person's body and mind are that person's own, and that society may rightfully coerce someone only in order to avert harm to other people. What view would Mill likely take of this proposal? In particular, where would his harm principle come under strain when what is monitored is not outward conduct but inferred mental content, and what clarifications or revisions of his position would he plausibly need to offer?
