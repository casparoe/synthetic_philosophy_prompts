I am beginning the literature review for a dissertation chapter on presupposition, accommodation, and the conversational scoreboard, and I want to write the specification down before I read any more theory. The purpose I care about is a theory that explains conversational update: when a move in a language game is felicitous or defective, what it adds to and requires of the shared context, and how the scoreboard tracks those changes across an exchange. I keep seeing debates that turn on one data point at a time — the present king of France, factive verbs like "regret," aspectual verbs like "stop," particles like "too," the contrast between "If John has children, his children are late to pick him up" and "If John is a scuba diver, he will bring his wetsuit," or a speaker who says "I'm sorry I'm late; I had to take my daughter to the doctor" to someone who did not know she had a daughter, without the audience objecting — and I would like the full list before I adjudicate any of them.

Write the desiderata: what an adequate theory of presupposition and accommodation must actually get right, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. I want in particular the distinctions such a theory cannot collapse (presupposition vs. entailment vs. implicature; presupposition vs. what is already common ground; the common ground vs. the conversational scoreboard); the projection behavior it must predict or at least respect under negation, conditionals, modals, questions, and quantifiers; the accommodation phenomena it must allow and constrain, including informative presuppositions, local versus global accommodation, and why a hearer cannot simply accommodate anything a speaker presupposes; and an explicit verdict on which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely one camp's picture — for example, whether presupposition failure makes a sentence false, truth-valueless, or merely infelicitous. Then flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most to rule out the standard candidates: a purely semantic truth-value-gap account, a purely Stalnakerian common-ground account, and a Lewis-style scorekeeping account. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring the theories in detail; a table for the requirements is fine.
