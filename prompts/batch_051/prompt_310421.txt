I've been chewing on Spinoza's account of the intellectual love of God and I keep getting stuck on the word love. The theory has a lot going for it: everything follows necessarily from God's nature, so there's no point resenting how things turned out or bargaining with reality; the best state a person can reach is to understand themselves and everything else under the aspect of eternity, and from that understanding there arises a joy accompanied by the idea of God as its cause. That's supposed to be the highest blessedness, and I find the picture genuinely attractive — it makes peace with the world, dissolves blame and resentment, and turns understanding itself into a kind of salvation. But my gut won't let me call that state love. Love, as I actually experience it, seems to need an object that could have been otherwise or at least a person-like presence that can respond; loving a necessitated, impersonal totality feels like giving a deep clear-eyed acceptance a name it doesnt deserve. It also doesn't help that in his account of the emotions Spinoza defines love as a joy that involves the idea of an external cause, while the intellectual love of God has God as its cause — and God is supposed to be the immanent cause of everything, not something outside me. So I'm not even sure the intellectual love of God counts as love on his own definition.

I'm not interested in the standard free will versus determinism debate here. I want a negotiated verdict on this specific clash. What would it take for my intuition to be the thing that should go — is my concept of love just too anthropomorphic, too tied to scarcity, need, and the fear of loss, so that Spinoza's calm, eternal, non-mutual love is actually the more mature form of the thing? What would it take for the theory to be the thing that goes or gets amended — maybe Spinoza should admit that love is being used in a technical or honorific sense here, discontinuous with ordinary love, or that his picture of the intellectual love of God is in tension with his own definition of the emotions? And is there a reading on which both survive, for example by distinguishing kinds of love, or by saying my intuition tracks something real about interpersonal love but not about the highest good? Give me the strongest version of each move and then say which one you would actually take, and on what grounds.
