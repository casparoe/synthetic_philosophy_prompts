I'm a PhD student in metaphysics and I keep hitting the the same wall in the counterpart theory vs. transworld identity debate, and I'd like a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than another run-through of the positions. Roughly: the transworld identity view says "Nixon could have won in 1968" is true because there's a possible world in which Nixon himself wins — same guy, different world-indexed property. The counterpart theorist says no individual exists in more than one world, so the sentence is made true by a world-bound counterpart of Nixon that resembles him closely enough; modal predication is really a similarity relation. Both sides agree on a huge range of ordinary de re modal truths like "Nixon could have won"; they just disagree about what those sentences are about. After fifty years, no one moves, and my working hypothesis is that the disagreement is substantive and is about what de re modal claims are about in the first place — the very same object across worlds, or a structured pattern of similarity among world-bound individuals. Is that diagnosis right? Or is the dispute actually verbal, or is one side asking a semantic question while the other asks a metaphysical one, or is it just a clash of starting intuitions neither side can argue for? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a formal result, a distinction, a better account of why we care about de re modality, or should we expect no movement at all? I'm not asking who wins; I want the structure of the impasse.
