In a reading group on metaethics, I went back to the preface of G. E. Moore’s *Principia Ethica* and got stuck on this passage:

> I have tried in this book to distinguish clearly two kinds of question, which moral philosophers have always professed to answer, but which, as I have tried to shew, they have almost always confused both with one another and with other questions. These two questions may be expressed, the first in the form: What kind of things ought to exist for their own sakes? the second in the form: What kind of actions ought we to perform?

Moore sharpens the division a little later when he says that, for answers to the first question, “no relevant evidence whatever can be adduced: from no other truth, except themselves alone, can it be inferred that they are either true or false,” whereas answers to the second question depend on causal truths combined with those first-question ethical truths.

I keep noticing a parallel with reinforcement learning. A reward function specifies which states or outcomes are good, while a policy specifies what to do; the policy is justified only by combining the reward function with a model of the environment’s dynamics. That looks structurally like Moore’s picture: the first question fixes the ethical data, and the second is answerable only by combining those self-evident ethical truths with causal truths about consequences. Even Moore’s claim that first-question answers admit no evidence sounds like the way a reward function is stipulated rather than inferred from the environment. I want to know whether this is a genuine structural identity, a loose analogy, or a confusion. Specifically, do Moore’s constraints on intrinsic value—non-naturalness, organic unities, self-evidence—rule out identifying it with an arbitrary reward function, or are those constraints the metaethics analogue of constraints you would need to place on reward design? And to run the transfer test in the other direction: do phenomena such as reward hacking, sparse rewards, or the need for reward shaping map back onto Moore’s account of deliberation and right action in a way that reveals something true about it, or do they start producing false predictions?
