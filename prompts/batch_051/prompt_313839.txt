I retired a few years ago from a career in experimental plant physiology, and Ive been using the free time to work through things I never read properly—lately the early Greek poets. I'm stuck on a passage in Hesiod's Theogony where he describes the Styx oath, the one the gods themselves swear by:

"For whoever of the deathless gods that hold the peaks of snowy Olympus pours a libation of her water and is forsworn, must lie breathless until a full year is completed, and never come near to taste ambrosia and nectar, but lie spiritless and voiceless on a strewn bed: and a heavy trance overshadows him. But when he has spent a long year in his sickness, another penance more hard follows after the first. For nine years he is cut off from the eternal gods and never joins their councils or their feasts, nine full years."

What strikes me is that this reads almost like a piece of lab equipment: a fixed protocol (fetch the water, pour the libation), a detection step (if you are forsworn, the water somehow knows), and a metered punishment (one year of coma, then nine years of exclusion). A friend who teaches classics insists that an oath "binds" in a way an ordinary promise doesnt, and I honestly cant tell whether that is a metaphor or a hypothesis. So I want to think through whether it could be tested.

Please act as a professional philosopher who works on social ontology and the methods of experimental psychology, and think carefully before answering. Begin by brainstorming what "binding" could concretely mean—fear of detection, reputational cost, a change in how the swearer sees their own later choice, something else. Then take the claim "swearing an oath changes a person's behavior beyond what an otherwise identical plain promise does" and propose one concrete experiment or observational study that would test it. Be specific about the population, the manipulation, the control, and the measured outcome. Say what result would count as evidence for a real binding effect, what result would count against it, and—this is the part I most want—what the test still would not settle, especially about whether the Hesiodic machinery (a god as enforcer, a self-curse) is doing any of the causal work. Please write your answer in British English.
