I'm on my university's equal opportunity committee and we've got a grievance that's splitting us down the middle. A grad student who teaches intro courses has a thick Appalachian accent. She was passed over for a departmental teaching award, her evals run about a half-point below the department average, and a bunch of the written comments are just making fun of the way she talks. She's filed a grievance saying she was discriminated against on the basis of her accent. The trouble is our nondiscrimination policy lists race, color, national origin, sex, religion, disability, age, sexual orientation, and gender identity, but it says nothing about accent or regional background. Some members want to read "national origin" to cover it; others say accent isn't a protected characteristic and if we stretch it we'll be on the hook for height, weight, attractiveness, you name it.

I need to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's meeting. I want it to actually decide this grievance rather than punt, and I want it to set out a general test for when an unlisted characteristic should be treated as suspect, with critiera the committee can actually apply in future cases. Underneath that is the philosophical question of what makes a ground of discrimination suspect in the first place: immutability, a history of group subordination, irrelevance to the thing being distributed, the fact that you can hide or change the trait only at a real cost to yourself? One junior member keeps arguing that immutability is the wrong test, becuase accents are changeable but the demand that she sand off her accent is itself part of the injury; another says the real test is whether the trait is a legitimate qualification for the thing being distributed. I want the opinion to take those positions seriously, not just gesture at them, and to give an honest treatment of the strongest argument against whatever you recommend. Start with the memo itself, no preface or "here is a draft," and make the first sentence the actual recommendation.
