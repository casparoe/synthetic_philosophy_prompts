I've been thinking about a way to solve one-off trust problems, like a stranger buying an expensive item from you and you have to ship before they pay, with no reputation or repeat business to fall back on. What if there was a service where both sides hand in a small computer program instead of a normal contract. Each program gets the other program's source code, then outputs ship or don't-ship, pay or don't-pay. The service runs them against each other and the result is legally binding. Since the programs can read each other, you could write one that pays exactly if the other program ships to a program that pays exactly if, and so they sort of mirror each other. I'm trying to figure out how this would actually play out: what programs people submit, does this actually produces the ship-and-pay outcome where a plain contract wouldn't, and where does it get gamed first? I keep going back and forth about wether the whole thing just moves the trust problem to whoever runs the service, and whether the mirroring programs can just hang in an infinite loop. Walk me through it like a mechanism: incentives, failure modes, second-order effects, and whether the bugs can be fixed without killing whatever makes it work.
