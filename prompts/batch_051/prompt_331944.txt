okay so ive been fussing over a distinction and i want someone to actually tell me if its a real thing or if ive just welded together chaos theory and old verificationism and called it a day. i call it shadow randomness. pick any deterministic chaotic system, double pendulum, lorenz attractor, the weather, whatever. for any fixed precision of measurement, theres some finite time horizon past which the observations you get are indistinguishable, by any test you could actually run, from a genuinely stochastic process with the same underlying statistics. i dont just mean prediction is hard or that the error bars explode. i mean no empirical procedure, not even in principle with finite resources, could tell apart "deterministic chaos" from "true randomness" after that point. so far i know this is basically just sensitivity to initial conditions plus finite precision, not my invention.

but heres the part i actually want checked. i think this shows determinism and randomness are not opposites, because theyre properties of different things. determinism is a property of the equations or the laws, randomness is a relation between a sequence of observations and the measuring capacities of some class of observers. so the same process is deterministic at the level of the equations and random at the level of observation, and there is no further fact about which one it "really" is, because "really is X" just means "X is the best description available at the level you are operating at." i have a corollary i keep throwing at people: the question "is the world deterministic or random?" is ill-posed unless you specify a target precision and a time horizon. without that, its like asking whether water is really H2O or wet.
