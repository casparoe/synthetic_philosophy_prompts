ok so for a reading group on promising i have to write down where i actually stand before the others pick at it, and i'd like an audit first. here's where i am:

(1) i hold something like an assurance view of promises: the wrong in breaking one isn't the bad consequence, it's that i let someone rely on me and then left their expectations running ahead of reality.
(2) that makes small promises real. if i say i'll send a reading by tuesday and don't, "it's just a little thing" doesn't get me off the hook — the smallness of the favor doesn't decide whether a promise existed.
(3) but i don't think being fifteen minutes late to a casual "see you at 7" plan is a broken promise. it's rude, sure, but more like poor planning or bad manners than a moral wrong — unless the person clearly arranged their evening around the exact time.
(4) i also think convention does real work here: in a friendship where 7 has always meant 7-to-7:30, you aren't late at 7:15, because what got assured was the window, not the minute.
(5) and i think that if the person i kept waiting says afterward that they genuinely didn't mind, any wrong there might have been is gone — not just forgiven, gone, like it never occured.

which commitments are actually in tension, which ones only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix? rank the tensions by severity rather than telling me they're all equally bad.
