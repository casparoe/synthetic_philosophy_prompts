I've been trying to draw a distinction I keep reaching for when reading about path dependence, and I want to know whether it is real or whether I've just relabeled something standard. I call it ornamental path dependence.

A claim of path dependence usually does two jobs at once. It says (a) that the system was sensitive to an early accident — a small difference in initial conditions would have sent it down a different track — and (b) that the track, once chosen, persisted even when later forces pulled against it. I want to pull out a third question that usually gets smuggled in: whether the early accident did any work in fixing the eventual outcome, as opposed to merely fixing the route by which an overdetermined outcome was reached.

Let the destination be the coarse-grained outcome the path-dependence claim is ultimately trying to explain: which technology became the standard, whether a region industrialized, which side of the road people drive on. Let the route be the specific sequence of intermediate states. My claim is that a great deal of what gets called path dependence is route contingency combined with destination overdetermination. The early event changed how the system got to the destination, but the destination itself was being held in place by selection pressures that were active throughout and would have produced a functionally equivalent outcome from a wide range of nearby starting points. The history is real, but it is counterfactually idle with respect to the destination. That is what I am calling ornamental path dependence: the early accident is doing narrative work, not causal work.

The clean example of the non-ornamental kind is driving conventions. Once a country settles on left or right, the choice persists because of coordination, and either side would have been a stable equilibrium; the early accident selects among symmetric basins and thereby fixes the destination. That is genuine basin selection. What I suspect is ornamental would be something like a company whose early product decision is later narrated as the reason it came to dominate its market, when the market pressures that favored the winning design were present before and after the decision, and a competitor that made the opposite early choice would have been pushed onto the same broad design anyway. Treat that example as a placeholder; I know it is under-described.

So I have four questions. First, is this distinction already in the literature under a better name, or does it collapse into existing distinctions among increasing returns, reactive sequences, and critical junctures? Second, is ornamental path dependence coherent, or does it evaporate once you notice that destinations can be individuated at any level of grain — coarse enough to make everything overdetermined, fine enough to make everything contingent? If the distinction only works relative to an arbitrary choice of grain, is that fatal or just unavoidable? Third, what is the strongest real-world case of genuinely non-ornamental path dependence — real basin selection — and what feature of it earns the label? Fourth, am I wrong that most popular path-dependence stories are ornamental? Give me the case that most strongly resists my framing.
