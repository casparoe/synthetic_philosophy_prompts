For an undergraduate seminar on the ethics of emotion, I would like a decision tree that sorts a respondent into a position on whether emotions belong to the good life or are at best noise to be managed. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of labels, using these four splits: whether emotions have cognitive or evaluative content (can an emotion be true or false about what is good or bad); whether any emotions can be appropriate to their object rather than all being disturbances to be eliminated; whether an emotion's value, where it has one, is intrinsic or only instrumental for living well; and whether moral value is itself partly constituted by our emotional responses rather than merely tracked or expressed by them. The leaves should include a strict Stoic apatheia position, an Aristotelian metriopatheia position, a Humean sentimentalist position, a Romantic position on which the emotions are the most honest or revelatory part of a life, and an Epicurean-style management position (label it unnamed if you think the historical label misleads). Use these cases where they help split positions: grief at a friend's death, anger at a public injustice, fear before a dangerous but necessary action, and delight in music or landscape. Phrase each branch node as a question someone could answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to; note which combinations of answers are logically possible but unoccupied, and say briefly whether each is unoccupied because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; and flag any two leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate. Where a leaf idealizes a historical view, say so in one sentence, and throughout separate what is settled in the interpretation of these positions from what is genuinely contested among scholars, without presenting a contested reading as the consensus.
