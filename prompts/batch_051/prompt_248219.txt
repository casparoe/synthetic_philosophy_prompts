Consider the standard pipeline by which a large language model is made helpful and harmless through reinforcement learning from human feedback. Crowdworkers first compare two completions and a reward model is trained to predict which one they preferred; that reward model is then frozen while the policy is trained to maximise its scalar output, with a penalty for straying from the base model. The resulting system is then deployed to users whose instructions, contexts, and values differ greatly from those of the raters and from the comparison task. What theory of value or preference does this design presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory: what must human preferences be like, along which dimensions, and with what stability, commensurability, and interpersonal convergence, for this to be a reasonable way to encode them? Identify what the pipeline strictly presupposes, what it merely fits well with, and any tensions between presuppositions at different stages, for example between pairwise judgement and scalar optimisation, or between rater values and end-user values. Conclude by saying what would have to change, in the architecture, the data, or the objective, if the presupposed view were replaced by the view that preferences are context-dependent, partly constructed by the elicitation method, and frequently incommensurable.

Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who study these systems, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.

After the analysis, give a brief structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields: 'implicit_theory', a one-sentence statement of the theory the pipeline presupposes; 'strict_presuppositions', a list of claims the design appears to require; 'mere_fits', a list of claims it accommodates but does not require; 'internal_tensions', a list of conflicts between presuppositions at different stages; 'changes_under_alternative', a list of concrete changes if preferences are instead taken to be context-dependent, constructed, and incommensurable; 'settled', a list of points on which informed opinion converges; and 'contested', a list of points on which informed opinion genuinely diverges.
