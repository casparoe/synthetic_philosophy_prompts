My partner and I have been trying to decide whether to have a child, and a long argument with an old friend about it has left me unsure whether I hold a defensible view or just a confused double standard. My instinct was that, when we are deciding whether to bring a new person into existence, we ought to weigh the suffering the child might experience more heavily than the happiness the child might experience. The reasoning I offered was this: if the child exists and suffers, that is bad for the child, but if the child never exists, there is no one to be deprived of the happiness, so I could not treat the absence of that happiness as a loss. My friend replied that I cannot have it both ways. If I am willing to count the child's prospective happiness as a genuine reason in favor of having the child, then consistency requires me to count the loss of that happiness as a reason against not having the child; otherwise the very same happiness counts on one side of the ledger and vanishes from the other. He argued that the only coherent positions are either to treat the possible child's future happiness and suffering symmetrically, as in an ordinary cost-benefit calculation, or to say that a possible person has no interests at all, so neither happiness nor suffering should count except insofar as it affects the people who already exist. He also pressed me on an implication: if any anticipated suffering really has a special weight that anticipated happiness lacks, then almost any life that contains some real suffering would seem to be a life one should not start, and I am not sure I want to accept that conclusion.

I still feel there is something right about my original asymmetry. Coming into existence does not seem to be the mere mirror image of never existing, and there seems to be a difference between failing to create a happy person and causing an existing person to suffer. But I could not articulate the difference without sounding as though I believed pain has some special metaphysical status that pleasure lacks. Can you help me think this through? In particular, I would like to know whether my friend's consistency objection is sound, and if it is not, what exactly breaks the symmetry. I would also like to know whether my initial asymmetry really commits me to the strong conclusion that nearly any life containing some suffering should not be started, or whether one can hold the asymmetry while still finding the decision to have a child permissible.
