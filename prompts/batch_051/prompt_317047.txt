You are preparing a recorded colloquium Q&A with a visiting philosopher who defends a Leibnizian answer to the question why there is something rather than nothing. Her core position is that the question is meaningful, that it demands a sufficient reason for the existence of the actual world, and that no contingent fact or collection of contingent facts can provide such a reason. In her opening statement she cites Leibniz’s Principles of Nature and Grace, section 7, where Leibniz argues, in effect, that because nothing is simpler and easier than something, the fact that something exists rather than nothing calls for a sufficient reason, and that this reason must lie in a necessary substance that carries the reason for its own existence within itself; otherwise the series of contingent things would remain ultimately unexplained.

Write a sequence of interview questions that tests this position without falling back on generic questions about how she got interested in philosophy. The questions should press the argument where critics have actually pressed it, and each question should include the follow-up a well-prepared Leibnizian would have to face after giving her first reply. In particular, structure the sequence so it forces her to clarify whether the principle of sufficient reason is supposed to apply to the whole contingent series and not just to each member of it; whether a self-explaining necessary being is genuinely more intelligible than a brute contingent fact; whether the disjunction between nothing and something is a real alternative or a projection of our ordinary causal habits; and whether the proposed answer names the desired stopping point rather than explaining it. End with a question about what empirical or modal difference it would make if the view were false—that is, what possible world or observational outcome would count against it. The questions should be of the kind that could not be answered simply by restating the position, and they should become progressively harder for the visitor to deflect.
