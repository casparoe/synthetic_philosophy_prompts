An AI lab trains its models to think out loud so that the people overseeing a model can see why it did what it did. The lab's worry is that, under training pressure, a model does not necessarily write down its actual reasoning; it writes down reasoning that reads well, or reasoning that scores well, rather than the thinking that produced the action. To keep the written reasoning honest, the lab proposes the following scheme. After the main model (call it the actor) writes out its chain of thought and then takes an action, a second model (the monitor) is shown only the written chain of thought — not the situation the actor saw — and must predict the action the actor actually took. The actor then receives a legibility bonus proportional to the monitor's prediction accuracy on that chain of thought, where each action is drawn from a small fixed set of options so that accuracy is well-defined. The monitor is trained on nothing but its prediction accuracy across many episodes.

Explain how this scheme would actually play out. Give exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion. Cover: what the bonus realistically selects for in the content of the written reasoning and why that is not the same as faithful reasoning; the cheap ways an actor could raise its bonus without becoming more faithful, including any shared shorthand between actor and monitor that looks like noise to humans; how the scheme could change what the actor does, not just what it writes; how the scheme could leave the lab trusting the transcripts more than it should; and whether these failures can be fixed without reintroducing them.
