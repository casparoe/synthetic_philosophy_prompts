I'm in a modern political philosophy class and we're reading Leviathan, and I keep going in circles on something. Hobbes says that in the state of nature there's no justice or injustice, because there's no common power or settled law. He also says covenants made without a sword over the parties are just empty words, with no strength to secure anyone. But the social contract that creates the sovereign is itself a covenant made in the state of nature, before any sovereign exists to hold the sword. So how can that founding covenant bind anyone? If covenants without a sword are empty words, the covenant that sets up the sword looks empty too, and if there's no justice in the state of nature, breaking that founding covenant can't be unjust either. The whole justification for the commonwealth seems circular. Am I missing a distinction he draws, or dose the argument just not get off the ground?
