I'm prepping an episode of my small philosophy-of-mind podcast, and next week's guest is a philosopher who defends illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. Her view, as best I can pin it down, is that introspection systematically represents us as having qualitative, ineffable, nonphysical-seeming properties, and that the right move is to reject the represented properties themselves while explaining why the representation is so compelling. The thing I find hard to corner her on is that she always defuses the self-defeat objection by saying an illusion doesn't need to feel like anything, it just is a representation we take at face value, and that the real target is the meta-problem of consciousness: explaining why we are so convinced there is a hard problem at all.

Draft 8–10 interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view. I want at least one question each on: (a) the self-defeat worry, that being mistaken about something it is like seems to itself require something it is like; (b) how introspection could be that systematically wrong about how things seem to us without us noticing; (c) what she actually takes the difference between illusionism and eliminativism to be, since she insists they're not the same position; and (d) what empirical or conceptual results would actually lower her confidence in illusionism. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and make sure at least one follow-up forces her to say whether the meta-problem carries any argumentative weight for the view or is just a piece of data the view wants to explain.
