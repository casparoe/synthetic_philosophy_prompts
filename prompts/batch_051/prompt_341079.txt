I've been reading Schopenhauer's The World as Will and Representation after a friend insisted he was the single best philosopher of music, and the core idea I keep circling is this: he says the other arts give us the Ideas by way of particular objects, but music skips the Ideas and gives us the will itself directly. Music reproduces joy, sorrow, longing, and so on in the abstract, without any particular person or situation being depicted. He even maps it out: the bass corresponds to inorganic nature, the middle voices to plants and animals, and the melody to the human level of the will.

Objections to his view that I already know and don't need repeated: (1) the cultural-convention objection, that musical expression is learned and local, so it can't be the universal language he says it is; (2) the objection that he was really generalizing from the Austro-German instrumental music of his lifetime and ignoring other traditions; (3) the objection that his bass-to-melody hierarchy of the will's objectifications is arbitrary and just physiology by other means; (4) the objection that if music directly copies a blind striving will, it should sound chaotic or painful rather than ordered and beautiful, like why would the thing-in-itself sound like a Hayden quartet; (5) the objection that pure instrumental music is emotionally underdetermined and needs titles, lyrics, or programs to carry definite content, so the supposed direct expression is overstated; (6) the objection from song and opera, that he treats words as a foreign addition to music when a lot of musical power actually comes from text.

I want an objection to his view that is not on that list and not a variant or combination of anything on it—not a rewording, not a special case, not just pointing out he used "will" loosely. Then tell me honestly whether the new objection is any good or whether it only works by leaning on a different sense of will.
