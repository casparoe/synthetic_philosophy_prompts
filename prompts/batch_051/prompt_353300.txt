I'm a city councillor and I need a real argument about whether the old carbon-tax bylaw catches a diesel-powered EV charging plaza, because I have to cast a vote. The bylaw, passed in 2006, reads: "Every person who sells gasoline or diesel fuel at retail within the City for use in a motor vehicle shall collect and remit a carbon tax of five cents per litre of fuel so sold." The preamble says its purpose is to reduce greenhouse-gas emissions from transportation fuel purchased in the City. At the time, everyone was picturing ordinary gas stations. Now a company has applied to build a truck-charging plaza by the highway: electric trucks plug into fast chargers, but because the local grid can't handle the peak draw, the chargers are fed by a row of diesel generators running behind the lot. The company buys diesel, burns it on-site in stationary generators, and sells the resulting electricity to the trucks. The company says the bylaw plainly doesn't apply—it doesn't sell gasoline or diesel, it sells electricity, and the diesel is burned in a generator, not in a motor vehicle. Our climate staff say the bylaw's whole point was to put a carbon price on transport energy sold inside city limits, and the 2006 council would have thought this was an obvious dodge if someone had described it to them. Walk me through what each approach to interpretation gives here—the plain words, the purpose, what the drafters would most likely have said if asked, and what a reasonable operator would have understood the bylaw to cover—and tell me which approach should control. I don't want to hide behind "it depends."
