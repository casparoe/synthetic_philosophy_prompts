An essay I read summed up the whole "lessons of history" debate like this:

"The Munich analogy is the most expensive piece of reasoning in modern foreign policy. Leaders keep pattern-matching every adversary to Hitler and every concession to 1938 — that's how the US talked itself into Vietnam, and it's how the Thucydides Trap is now making a US–China war feel inevitable before it's actually happened. Both moves have the same flaw: they cherry-pick a few historical cases and ignore everything that made those cases what they were. The real lesson of history is that history has no lessons for policy. Policymakers should stop appealing to the past entirely and decide each case on its own current facts."

I suspect the last step is too strong, and something about equating the Munich analogy with the Thucydides Trap seems off to me, but I dont know enough to sort out which parts a careful historian would actually endorse.
