The target is the epistemological debate around what gets called the bootstrapping problem and easy knowledge, as it arises for process reliabilism. As I understand it, reliabilism says, roughly, that a belief is justified just in case it is produced by a process that is in fact generally reliable. The bootstrapping worry is then this: a subject with reliable color vision can look at a wall, believe it is red, and reason that since the wall is red and it looked red, her color vision got this case right; after enough such cases, she concludes that her color vision is reliable. Reliabilism seems forced to accept this inference, since each step is produced by a reliable process, and yet the conclusion looks as if it was obtained for free, because every premise depends on the very faculty whose reliability is at issue. The standard dramatic version, the Roxanne gas-gauge case, has a person reading a gauge, forming beliefs about the tank on the gauge's say-so, then combining the gauge's reading with the belief it produced to conclude that the gauge read correctly on that occasion, and finally concluding after repetition that the gauge is reliable, without ever checking it against anything outside itself.

Make the mapping explicit rather than gestural: say which objects and claims in the epistemological setup correspond to which objects and claims in the statistical setup, and resist the impulse to make everything fit.
