I keep hearing people defend inheritance taxes by saying the point is levelling the playing field, not punishing the rich. But I cant tell whether that's a claim about outcomes — rich kids end up less rich — or about opportunities — how far a kid gets genuinely depends less on who their parents are. They're different, and the first could hold while the second doesn't. Is the opportunity claim actually testable? Give me the strongest test you can, on data that exists or could plausibly be assembled, of the proposition that heavier inheritance taxes make a child's life prospects depend less on their parents. A friend suggested the wedge is the timing of a parent's death relative to the child's education: an inheritance arriving before a kid finishes school should affect opportunity, a windfall after the kid is grown should mostly affect consumption. Is that the right wedge, or is there something better? Spell out the comparison, what result would favour each reading, and what even a clean result would leave unsettled.
