I've been reading about the tight-loose distinction in cross-cultural psychology (tight cultures pile up strong norms and punish deviance hard; loose cultures have fewer norms and shrug off a lot of deviance), and it suddenly seemed like exactly the static vs dynamic typing distinction in programming. Static typing is a tight culture: the compiler is a strict enforcer that rejects violations before the program ever runs. Dynamic typing is loose: the rules are thinner and whatever violations exist get caught late, at runtime, if they get caught at all. My question is whether this is a genuine structural identity or just me pattern-matching. If it's genuine, do the standard findings transfer — that tight systems coordinate better under threat but loose systems explore and innovate better? And does the analogy have any payoff for the question of how rule-bound an online community should be, or is that where the whole thing falls apart?
