I'm a field ecologist, so the framework I actually think in is conservation biology: populations and metapopulations, extinction debt and functional extinction, in situ vs ex situ, ecosystem services, invasives, conservation triage, the intrinsic-vs-instrumental value split. I keep running into writing about language death and language rights that reaches for the same machinery — languages as species, documentation as a seed bank, dominant languages as invasive species, the usual 'linguistic-diversity hotspots' talk. I want someone to do that translation honestly, because my suspicion is that part of it is real (the traditional-ecological-knowledge stuff seems to cash out fine) and part of it might be metaphor doing moral work while dressed up as ecology, but I don't trust that suspicion yet.

Take the main positions in the debate — roughly: (1) each language is an irreplaceable way of carving up experience, so linguistic diversity has intrinsic value; (2) languages carry traditional ecological knowledge and local taxonomy, so losing a language loses exactly the kind of thing conservationists are also trying not to lose; (3) the voluntarist line that if a community stops speaking its language, that's their business, no one is wronged, and the loss is just how history goes; and (4) the injustice line that language shift is usually not a free choice but the product of coercion, assimilationist schooling, and stigma. Restate each of these in my vocabulary as precisely as you can — what is the population, what counts as extinction debt or functional extinction, what can ex-situ documentation preserve and what can it not, which position is really making a claim about ecosystem services or intrinstic value. Keep the translation honest: don't let the ecological frame settle the normative questions by default just because they're hard to say in its terms. Then give me the residue report. Which claims survive the translation cleanly, which get distorted, and which have no counterpart in the ecological frame at all? And I want a real verdict on the residue — is the untranslatable part a limitation of my framework, or is it the visible seam where the position stops being about languages as systems and starts being about justice and autonomy? If those parts can't be said in ecology's terms, does that mean the biodiversity analogy is pulling more than its weight?

If the whole 'language death is like species extinction' framing is itself the mistake, or the positions don't break down the way I've sorted them, tell me that straight instead of forcing the translation. I'd rather have a committed, self-critical verdict — say which positions survive and which are doing something the ecology talk can't carry, and how confident you are in each — than a balanced tour of all four.
