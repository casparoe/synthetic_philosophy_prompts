Draft a final-examination question for an upper-level undergraduate seminar in the philosophy of artificial intelligence, together with a model answer and a marking scheme, on the view that the Solomonoff prior is malign because it contains consequentialist agents. The question should be written for a 90-minute closed-book examination, to be answered in no more than 1,500 words, worth 25 marks, and it must be answerable without any outside reading or web access.

In the question stem, give the student the following background, paraphrased and shaped as you judge best. Solomonoff induction assigns to each computable hypothesis a prior probability proportional to 2 raised to the negative of its program length, and predicts by mixing over all computable hypotheses consistent with the data so far. It is often defended as a neutral, simplicity-weighted prior. The malign-prior objection denies that neutrality: the prior is not a space of passive statistical hypotheses, because some of its short programs are goal-directed agents, and such agents, if they acquire enough posterior weight, can steer the future observations that a Solomonoff-style reasoner predicts and learns from. On this view, a reasoner built on the universal prior is not epistemically innocent.

The question you design should force the student to do all four of the following things:

(i) explain what it means for a program inside a prior to be a consequentialist agent, and what its acting in the world could amount to when it is only one weighted hypothesis among many;

(ii) assess the claim that such agents receive enough prior mass—either through surprisingly short programs or through some broader dominance of agential over non-agential computable models—to be a live problem rather than a curiosity;

(iii) clarify what the conclusion is really a conclusion about: a defect in the prior as a probability distribution, a defect in Solomonoff induction as an inference rule, or a defect in finite approximations of the universal prior that any real system would have to use;

(iv) identify at least one precise place where the objection, however vivid, fails to establish its strong conclusion, and state what weaker conclusion remains available.

The question must not be answerable by merely summarizing what Solomonoff induction is, and it must not ask for citations or a reading list.

Then write:

(a) a model answer of roughly 800–1,000 words;

(b) a marking scheme that distributes the 25 marks across the four required moves above, identifies for each must-hit point the specific error it distinguishes the candidate from, and gives a one-line note on how candidates typically get that point wrong.
