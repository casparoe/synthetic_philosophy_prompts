I've been going down a rabbit hole on typography for a piece, and I keep running into the same move: designers say a typeface is a "family," and that the letters share a "spirit" rather than any set of measurable features. The conclusion they draw is that theres no specifiable essence of a lowercase "a" — family resemblance all the way down, not a checklist of strokes, apertures, and terminals. A designer I interviewed even argued that an old AI project called Letter Spirit (which, as I understand it, tried to get a machine to recognize and invent "a"s in a given style) was bound to stall if it went looking for explicit rules.

Before I repeat this argument, I want to know how far the family analogy actually holds. Lay out the correspondence table — what maps to the parents, the genes, the shared environment, the family resemblance, the black sheep — then the places it breaks. And tell me whether the conclusion (no crisp essence of a letterform, only graded family resemblance) survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the argument the whole time. If you'd swap in a better analogy, tell me what and why.
