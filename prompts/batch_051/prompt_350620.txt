I work as a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm preparing a longform piece on whether remix culture and generative tools force us to revise our concept of creativity. I keep circling back to Douglas Hofstadter's suggestion that variation on a theme is the crux of creativity: that making something new is largely a matter of exploring the space of small changes around an existing structure, turning the knobs of a pattern to see which settings make it come alive. I'd like help working out what principle, if any, makes sense of my verdicts on particular cases.

Here are the verdicts I've collected. (1) A jazz pianist improvises on a Gershwin standard, reharmonizing a few bars and displacing accents; I judge this genuinely creative, though less so than composing the tune in the first place. (2) A poet's translation of a Rilke poem departs repeatedly from the literal meaning in order to reproduce its sound and emotional arc in English; I consider this fully creative work, not a diminished copy. (3) A nightcore producer takes a finished recording and accelerates it by twenty percent with a corresponding pitch shift; I consider this barely creative at all. (4) A novelist retells the Odyssey from the standpoint of the suitors' servants; I consider this highly creative. (5) A painter reproduces a Vermeer composition but replaces the entire palette with a complementary-color scheme, as if running the image through a filter; this feels clever but thin, much less creative than (4). (6) A choreographer reuses a classical ballet's movement vocabulary but changes only the sequence and grouping; I find this surprisingly creative.

I want you to act as a philosopher of creativity and work out whether one precise principle can capture all six verdicts. I'm especially aware that (2), (5), and (6) can all be described as turning knobs on an existing work, yet I rank them very differently; and (4) looks like a larger departure than (3), but both can be described as small changes around something already there. Please think carefully, brainstorm several candidate principles, test each against the cases, and then give me your best account: what makes a variation creative rather than merely derivative, and which of my verdicts, if any, does the principle force me to abandon? Where your account has surprising predictions, test it on further cases I haven't listed, such as a cover song that keeps the melody but changes the lyrics, or a curated supercut of existing footage.
