I'm trying to work out a single coherent position on how different components of value combine, and I'd like the following set of commitments stress-tested before I present it. I want to know whether the package is jointly tenable, and if not, exactly where it gives way.

Here is what I currently believe.

(1) Within an individual life, well-being is multiplicative rather than additive. The value of a stretch of experience is the product of two factors: its valence, meaning how good or bad it feels on balance, and its degree of consciousness, meaning how awake, aware, and phenomenally detailed the experience is. A mild pleasure in a barely conscious creature contributes almost nothing to welfare; a fully alert state with valenced-neutral experience also contributes almost nothing; the factors do not merely add.

(2) Severe suffering has lexical priority within a life. Once an individual's pain crosses a threshold of severity, no amount of that same individual's happiness, insight, beauty, or other positive experience can compensate for it. Below that threshold, ordinary tradeoffs apply: enough mild goods can outweigh a moderate bad.

(3) Across lives, value is additive. The value of a state of affairs is the sum of each individual's welfare, with each individual's welfare computed as in (1) and constrained by (2). I see no need for a separate distributive principle; equality matters only through its effects on individual welfare.

(4) Only experience itself has non-instrumental value. Contact with reality, in the sense relevant to whether a life spent in an illusory or simulated world is worse, adds nothing beyond what is already captured by the experience's valence and consciousness.

(5) For decisions under risk, the right norm is to maximize expected value, with utilities supplied by the commitments above. Small probabilities count for their probability-weighted value: a small chance of a large good can justify a moderate sure cost, and a small chance of catastrophe counts exactly in proportion to its probability and disvalue.

Do these hang together? Please give me a point-by-point audit rather than a general discussion: identify which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension and which only look as though they are, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each tension say what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost. I do not yet know which element is doing the most damage, so please don't just tell me the package is fine, and don't flag everything as equally problematic.
