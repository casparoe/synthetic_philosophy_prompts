I sit on my companys internal model review board. We have a standing compute governance policy: any training run over 10^25 FLOP triggers a pause review, and anything over 10^26 needs the board to actually vote before work can start. The team is asking to begin a run at about 2.4 × 10^26 — a 3x jump over our last model and the first one that really trips the vote under the new rule. Our safety evals all came back clean: no meaningful jump on bio or cyber tasks, no new self-exfiltration behavior, mostly incremental gains over the last release. But our red-team lead keeps saying that's the whole problem — evals only catch the failure modes we already thought to test, and a 3x compute jump is exactly where that stops being reassuring. Product argues that if we pause, a less careful shop ships something comparable within a year and we just lose whatever leverage we have; safety argues the policy was written precisely so we don't get talked into "this one is fine" every single cycle. The board's split three ways: product wants to proceed, safety wants to block, and a third group wants to approve only if we hold back the public API and do a second external red-team before any release, though they're still fuzzy on what that audit would actually look for. The chair wants each of us to write a short memo with a real vote — proceed, delay with conditions, or block — by Thursday. Draft mine. It has to give one recommendation and deal honestly with the strongest version of the losing side's argument, not a strawman I can knock over at standup.
