"In evaluating whether an AI system is aligned—for instance, whether a language model is non-sycophantic or robust to jailbreaks—what is the difference between a worst-case guarantee and an average-case guarantee? Is it ever reasonable to accept only average-case evidence when the stakes are catastrophic? Use at least one concrete example."

Here's the student's answer:

"A worst-case guarantee says the system has the desired property on every possible input or in every environment it could face, while an average-case guarantee says it has the property on most inputs drawn from some distribution, or with high probability over a test set. In practice we can only ever measure average-case behavior, because the input space is effectively infinite and any benchmark is finite. So worst-case guarantees are out of reach for any realistic system, and since safety engineering is ultimately about reducing expected risk, average-case evidence is the right standard even when the stakes are catastrophic. For example, if a model refuses harmful requests on 99.9% of a red-team set, that gives us a good estimate of how often it will refuse harmful requests in deployment, which is what matters for expected harm. Requiring a guarantee that no input can jailbreak it would be asking for certainty, and certainty is not available anywhere in engineering."
