I run a mid-sized logistics company, about 80 employees, and I keep getting burned when I rely on people's good character — a manager I trusted coverd up safety problems, another quietly fudged numbers to look good. I've been reading summaries of Han Feizi and the basic stance resonates: govern through clear, impersonal rules; use fixed rewards and punishments tied strictly to measured results; and constantly check whether the actual work matches the stated duty, instead of trying to cultivate virtue or doing trust-based delegation. I want that turned into a procedure I can actually run every month for my managers, and that a front-line supervisor with no background in Chinese philosophy could run too. Give me the steps in order: how to write a performance standard for a job, how to audit whether the actual work matches the stated duty, how to decide whether to trust someone's word or intentions, and how to respond when results are bad but motives were good, or results are good but the person broke the rules. For each step, say what a sufficient answer looks like. The procedure should say where it stops with a no, where it has to hand the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than facts, and which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure. Then run it on two of my people: a dispatcher who hits every KPI by offloading work onto junior staff, and a warehouse supervisor who missed her targets for two quarters because she slowed down to catch a large fraud. Flag the result I won't like, and say what the procedure can't settle.
