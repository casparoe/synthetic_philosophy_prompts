I'm working on a piece about why experts so rarely change their minds when new evidence comes in, and an interviewee pointed me to the dogmatism paradox. As I understand it, the argument runs: if you genuinely know P, then you already know that any alternative Q that would undermine P is false. So if a new study supports Q, it's supporting a falsehood, which makes the study misleading — and you're rationally entitled to dismiss it without reading past the abstract. That seems to license never changing you mind about anything you actually know. Am I missing a step, or is the upshot of the paradox really that closed-mindedness is the rational policy for anyone who knows much at all?
