I am a retired physical chemist who has recently begun reading, somewhat unsystematically, in the philosophy of artificially intelligent systems, and I keep encountering the same claim: that an agent can be built to have low impact, or to avoid side effects, without anyone first specifying in full what matters. I want to understand this claim before I read any particular proposal for an impact measure, because in the laboratory the notion of a side effect only ever functioned against a background of things I already cared about.

Please write me the desiderata for an adequate account of side effects, or of low impact, for an artificial agent acting on a narrow goal in a rich human environment. Fix the purpose this way: the designers have not enumerated the ways in which the world matters, cannot simply be given a complete specification of human value, yet wish to prevent the agent from making the world worse in ways that count while it pursues its task. What must such a theory get right?

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. The list should include at least the following. First, the cases it must handle and get the right verdict on: an effect that is easily reversed versus one that is not; an effect on inanimate objects versus one on the expectations, attention, or incentives of other agents; an effect the user would have welcomed versus one the user would have paid to prevent; an agent that changes too much versus one that refuses to act at all. Second, the distinctions it must respect rather than collapse, such as the distinction between an effect's falling outside the stated goal and an effect's being undesirable, and the distinction between a change's being measurable and a change's being harmful. Third, the formal or conceptual constraints the measure cannot quietly violate, such as remaining well-defined when the agent's own intervention alters the baseline against which change is assessed, or when the agent is uncertain about the consequences of its actions.

Mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp. Then tell me whether some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, a demand that the agent not alter the world beyond its narrow goal together with a demand that it still be useful at all — and, if so, which requirement should give way first. A final paragraph on which single requirement does the most to rule out naive proposals will be enough; I do not need an annotated survey of existing measures.
