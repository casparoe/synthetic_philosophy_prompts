I'm drafting a position statement for a metaethics workshop and would like a consistency check before I circulate it. Here are the commitments I've been treating as a package.

(1) Moral realism: there are objective moral facts, and whether an act is wrong does not depend on any individual's or culture's attitudes toward it.

(2) I accept the broad cross-cultural moral psychology picture: what people treat as morally weighty — and the relative importance they give to harm, fairness, purity, authority, and loyalty — varies substantially across societies, and WEIRD (Western, educated, industrialized, rich, democratic) populations are statistical outliers on several of these dimensions.

(3) I take this variation to support a debunking conclusion: a large share of any person's moral intuitions are inherited from their culture and are not reliably tracking the objective moral facts.

(4) Nevertheless, I hold that reflective equilibrium is the right method in ethics, and I think a sufficiently wide equilibrium — one that incorporates cross-cultural evidence — can filter out culturally inherited distortion rather than simply laundering it into coherence.

(5) I am committed to the reality of moral progress: the abolition of chattel slavery and the extension of equal standing to women are improvements, and their gradual near-convergence across societies gives partial, defeasible evidence that we are moving toward objective moral truth.

(6) I lean toward explaining the WEIRD/non-WEIRD differences mostly through differing non-moral background beliefs about the sacred, the afterlife, and threats to group survival, operating on a broadly shared core of harm- and fairness-based concerns; on this picture, the measured diversity overstates the underlying evaluative diversity.

Please audit the set. Identify which pairs of commitments are actually in tension and which only look as if they are, and explain the difference. Then rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one say what the least costly revision would be and what it would cost. I have not settled which commitment I would give up, so don't write the audit as if one fix is obviously correct; but do finish by saying which single revision would clean up the most at the least cost.
