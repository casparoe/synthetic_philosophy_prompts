After thirty years in chemical physics, I retired and started reading philosophy of physics. The puzzle I keep returning to is Maxwell's demon. In his letter to Peter Guthrie Tait, Maxwell described a being who watches individual molecules and works a trapdoor, letting faster molecules accumulate on one side and slower on the other, so a temperature difference appears with no work done. Modern discussions turn on information acquisition and erasure, but I find people talk past each other because they never state what a theory of the relation between information and thermodynamic entropy has to actually deliver. So here's my request. Write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of that relation must get right. Include the cases it has to handle, the distinctions it cannot collapse (e.g., knowing a microstate vs. having a bit reside in a memory), the established physics it cannot contradict, and the conditions under which the demon is ruled out or allowed. For each requirement, say why it's a requirement. Then mark which are non-negotiable and which are camp-dependent. And tell me whether some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, because I suspect the real lesson is in the tension, not in any single resolution. Thats the part I most want. Skip the scorecard; I'll do that myself.
