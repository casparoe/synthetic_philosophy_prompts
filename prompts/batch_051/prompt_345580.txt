I've been going back and forth with a friend about a thought experiment I later learned is usually called Molyneux's question. The setup is simple: someone born blind learns to identify a cube and a sphere by touch — two solid objects, the same size and material. If the person's sight were restored and the two objects were set down in front of them, before any touching, would they be able to point to the cube and the sphere correctly? People tend to answer this as if it were a clean yes/no experiment about whether shape is represented in some sense-neutral way that both touch and vision share. But the real cases I've read about seem confounded: a congenitally blind person's visual system has spent years developing without normal input, surgery rarely yields clear vision immediately, and by adulthood the person has heard endless verbal descriptions of cubes and spheres, so they might succeed by an explicit rule — sharp corners, flat faces — without anything like normal visual shape recognition. I've also wondered whether the question has to wait for a rare and messy surgery at all: one could go the other direction, having a sighted person blindfolded and taught to recognize shapes through a sensory-substitution device that turns shape into sound or vibration, then test them visually — but that may be a different question about translation between an artificial sense and vision rather than about touch and vision themselves.

I want to know how a careful test of the underlying claim should actually be designed. Suppose the claim is that the ability to recognize a previously touched shape on first sight shows that touch and vision access a common representation of shape, rather than two separate representations patched together by learned association. What subject population, task, and controls would you use to test something close to that claim? What pattern of results would count for and against the common-representation view? And after a clean result, either way, what would it leave open — for example, whether the test is really about a shared representation or instead about the visual skill of parsing the scene in the first place?
