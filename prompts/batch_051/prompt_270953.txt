I keep running into a dispute about whether practical stakes can change whether someone knows something, and my sense is that the two sides are built to fit exactly the same observations, which makes me think the argument is stuck for a reason neither side wants to name. Imagine someone looking at a radar map who says they know it will rain during their picnic, and someone else with the same forecast, the same evidence, and the same confidence who says they know it will rain during an outdoor ceremony they stand to lose a great deal on. People are much less willing to credit the second person with knowledge, even though nothing differs except what is at stake. One side explains this by saying the word 'know' shifts its standards with context, so the second person is being held to a stricter use of the word. The other side says the word means the same thing in both cases, and instead claims that knowledge itself is partly a matter of what one has at stake, so the second belief really fails to be knowledge. My working hypothesis is that this is largely a verbal dispute about how to use the word 'know', and that the two camps have built rival ways of talking around the same facts. Is that diagnosis right? If not, where is the genuine disagreement, and what would actually count as progress?
