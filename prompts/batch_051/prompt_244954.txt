I'm teaching Brave New World next term and I keep thinking about it alongside the World Happiness Report stuff — countries now chase their ranking and treat it as a measure of how well they're doing. Read the novel through that lens. The World State looks like it would top any crude happiness ranking: everyone's content, no one's hungry or lonely, and unhappiness is treated as a technical fault to be fixed with soma or conditioning. So what is Huxley actually attacking — the idea of making happiness a policy target at all, or just a bad operationalisation of it, self-reported satisfaction with a life emptied of everything that might make satisfaction worth having? I want a reading that's fair in both directions: where the novel anticipates real problems with happiness metrics (Goodharting, adaptation, the difference between feeling good and flourishing), and where it cheats by making the only alternative to the World State a set of characters who are clearly more noble but not obviously happier on anyone's measure. And the practical question I want to end on: if a country did decide to top the rankings, would it be pushed toward anything like the World State, or is that just a slippery slope Huxley rigged? Answer in British English, please.
