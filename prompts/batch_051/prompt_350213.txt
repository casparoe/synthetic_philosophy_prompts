For a graduate epistemology seminar, I should like a careful comparison of the KK principle and the luminosity of mental states. By the KK principle I mean the thesis that if a subject knows that p, then the subject knows that they know that p; by luminosity I mean the thesis, applied to mental conditions, that whenever such a condition obtains the subject is in a position to know that it obtains. Please make both formulations precise, and then assess the relation between them. In particular, is the KK principle simply luminosity restricted to the condition of knowing that p, and do the two theses stand or fall together? If they come apart, say which is the stronger and locate the divergence exactly — for instance, whether it lies in the step from being in a position to know to actually knowing, or elsewhere. Throughout, separate what is settled about this relation from what is genuinely contested amongst epistemologists, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
