Longtime lurker here. I've been going in circles for years on epiphenomenalism about phenomenal consciousness: the claim that qualia are real but do no causal work. Not the eliminativist view that they don't exist, and not interactionism — specifically the view that there's a what-it's-like but it's along for the ride. The thing that keeps tripping me up is the paradox of phenomenal judgment: if my pain-quale causes nothing, then my saying 'I'm in pain' and my judging that I'm in pain have to be fully explained by physical goings-on that would run the same way if the quale were different or gone altogether — so how could I ever know about the quale? And yet that knowledge feels about as certain as anything I've got.

What's your actual credence that phenomenal consciousness is epiphenomenal? Don't give me 'it depends what counts as causation' — I want a number and the decomposition. How much of the total rides on whether the paradox of phenomenal judgment is a real self-undermining argument versus just a intuition pump, how much on whether introspection gives me non-causal evidence of my own qualia, how much on whether mental causation requires denying physical closure or merely requires a looser supervenience, and which of those sub-questions would move your number most if it got settled. Bonus: is there any conceivable observation or experiment that would actually update you here, or is the whole dispute fixed by background commitments?
