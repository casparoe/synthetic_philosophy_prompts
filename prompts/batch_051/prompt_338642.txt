I work as a machine learning engineer on a clinical decision-support tool that uses patient data to flag individuals for further psychiatric evaluation, and our team has begun to notice something uncomfortable: the labels we deploy do not simply describe an existing population. Once a label such as high risk enters a patient's record, clinicians change how they treat the patient, the patient is sometimes told the label or infers it from the way they are handled, and the behaviors we are trying to predict — missed appointments, crisis visits, self-harm incidents — start to shift in ways our validation data did not anticipate. A psychiatrist colleague put this in philosophical terms: she said we are seeing something like Ian Hacking's looping effect of human kinds, the idea that classifying people can change the people classified, and that those changes can loop back and alter the classification itself. She contrasted interactive kinds, which are changed by being classified, with indifferent kinds, which are not.
