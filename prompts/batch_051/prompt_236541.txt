I spent a career in a lab and never thought I'd end up caring about what makes an intention 'shared', but now I can't stop seeing it in heist movies. I just rewatched Ocean's Eleven and it looks like a test case for the joint-action literature: eleven people with different motives, each holding one piece of the plan, some of them in the dark about the whole thing — Linus doesnt know the full scheme, and Danny and Rusty are running a private game inside the bigger one. Read the film through Bratman's meshing subplans, Gilbert's joint commitments, and Searle's irreducible we-intentions. Where does the crew look like a Gilbert-style plural subject, where like a Bratman-style web of individual plans, and where does neither framework get it right? I'm especialy curious about two moments: the scene where Linus is kept deliberately in the dark, and the ending, where they melt away one by one from the Bellagio fountain. Does the film show that you can act together without everyone sharing the whole goal, or is that just several joint actions stacked inside each other? Give me a reading that's fair to the movie and to the philosophy — if the film resists the theories, say so.
