I'm a retired physicist who got into philosophy of action and decision theory a couple years back, and I've collected a set of commitments that each feel right in isolation but that I can't tell whether they form a consistent package. I'd like you to audit them.

Here's where I've landed:

1. I'm a thorough Bayesian. Any meaningful proposition gets a probability, and that includes propositions about my own future actions. "I will give to the pledge drive tonight" is a proposition like any other, and I should have some degree of belief in it.

2. But I'm equally convinced that while I am actually deliberating about what to do, I can't treat my own choice as an event I'm forecasting. If I catch myself thinking "I'll probably give, knowing me," I've stopped deciding and started spectating. Deliberation crowds out prediciton, or something like that.

3. Predictions about me from the outside feel completely different. My wife can know me well enough to be 80% sure I'll give, and I can know that her prediction is well-calibrated, without that seeming to threaten my ability to deliberate one bit.

4. Here's the case that ties me in knots. Suppose a psychologist with a good model of my behavior offers me a bet at even odds on wether I'll donate tonight, before I sit down to decide, and I know my own track record says I'm much more likely to donate than not. I think it would be irrational to turn down a bet that's clearly favorable by my own lights just because the subject of the bet happens to be me. So I'd bet on "I'll donate" — and then, an hour later, I'd still expect myself to deliberate about donating rather than just doing whatever I bet on.

5. I'm also a determinist, in the boring sense that an outside observer with enough information about my brain and environment could in principle predict my choices with high accuracy. I don't think that commits me to fatalism or makes deciding pointless.

Are these five jointly tenable? I'd like an audit, not a lecture: which pairs are actually in tension, how severe is each, and what's the cheapest way to fix the package? I'd also like to know which conflicts only look like conflicts and dissolve once you're careful.

Format: exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion. The five items should be the five most important tensions or apparent tensions, ordered by severity. For each item, include a quick confidence tag (high/medium/low), and make sure one of the items explicitly flags the point you're least sure about.
