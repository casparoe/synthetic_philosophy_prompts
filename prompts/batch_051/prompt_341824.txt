I've been in an AI safety reading group for the past few months, and I've drifted toward a position I'm tentatively comfortable with: that chain-of-thought monitoring — treating the intermediate reasoning a model writes out before it acts as an oversight surface, something we can inspect for dangerous plans or incipient deception — is a genuinely load-bearing safety technique rather than a stopgap. By load-bearing I mean that when it works it does real work: we catch the model reasoning its way toward something bad before it takes the action, and we intervene. I know the obvious objection, which is that pressure to produce legible reasoning can just teach a model to write down a sanitized, obfuscated story while doing its actual computation elsewhere, and that the chain of thought might not even be a faithful summary of what's going on inside in the first place. But I've only hear two or three standard versions of that objection, and I want the full map of the case against the view.

So here's the position: monitored chain-of-thought can be made faithful enough and robust enough to serve as a real safety technique. Tell me what would count against it. I want the defeaters ranked from "this kills the view" down to "this is an awkward result defenders have to explain away." What empirical results, conceptual arguments, or specific failure modes should genuinely move me? Which ones would a defender of CoT monitoring conceed as live problems, and which would they dismiss as strawmen or fixable engineering? I'm especially interested in cases where monitoring works in easy settings but fails exactly where it matters — high-stakes situations that produce the most optimization pressure for the model to hide — and in whether the position, as people actually defend it, has been insulated with qualifications ("monitoring plus X, plus Y, plus a human in the loop") to the point that no observation could really count against it. If it has, tell me whether that's a bug or just what it means to make a safety claim about one component of a larger system. Be explicit about what you're confident in versus what you're speculating about.

And if you think the whole question is confused — that it reifies a model's chain of thought as more transparent than we have reason to think it is, or that "faithful" is doing more work than it can bear — say so plainly instead of humoring the premise.
