I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I've been poking at a argument that keeps coming up in economics and, increasingly, in climate modeling and epidemiology: why is it okay to build models on assumptions everyone knows are false? The classic exhibit is Milton Friedman's lead essay The Methodology of Positive Economics in his 1953 collection Essays in Positive Economics, where he argues that a hypothesis should be judged by its predictive success rather than the realism of its assumptions, and that the most significant hypotheses tend to have assumptions that are wildly inaccurate descriptions of reality — Samuelson later dubbed this the F-twist. On the other side you've got model realists who think assumptions do real epistemic work: if your model assumes infinitely lived representative agents, or perfect competition, or that firms literally maximize profit, it may predict fine in one regime and then fail quietly when the false assumption starts to matter.

I want you to draft the document a good-faith adversarial collaboration between these two camps would produce: a Friedman-style instrumentalist and a model realist who thinks unrealistic assumptions are a serious cost even when predictions are decent. Make them specific, not strawmen. First, a numbered list of claims both sides could sign, about idealization, prediction, and what would and wouldn't count as evidence against a model. Then a table of their disagreements, with columns: the issue, the instrumentalist's position phrased so an instrumentalist would accept it, the realist's position phrased so a realist would accept it, and whether the disagreement is substantive or partly verbal. Then a section on cruxes — the sub-questions that would actually move at least one side — and for each crux, one concrete case, argument, or model both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. I suspect one crux is whether good track record in one regime tells you much about a new regime; feel free to use that or replace it. Try to catch the places where they're talking past each other rather than disagreeing.

After that document, add a brief JSON summary with this schema: signed_claims (array of strings), disagreements (array of objects with fields issue, instrumentalist_position, realist_position, verbal_or_substantive), cruxes (array of objects with fields crux, test_or_case, instrumentalist_prediction, realist_prediction), and residual_dispute (a string summarizing what remains even after the table does its work).
