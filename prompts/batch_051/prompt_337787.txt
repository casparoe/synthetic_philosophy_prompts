An essay in a technology magazine summarized Langdon Winner's well-known argument that artifacts can have politics roughly as follows. Winner's view is that technologies are never politically neutral. Some artifacts settle political questions by design: the low-hanging overpasses Robert Moses built on Long Island were deliberately made to keep buses, and therefore poor and Black riders, away from public beaches. Other artifacts are inherently political because they can function only under a particular kind of political system: nuclear power demands centralized, even authoritarian, control, whereas solar power is naturally democratic. According to the article, Winner's conclusion is that technology determines politics—choose the artifact and you have already chosen the political outcome. I found the conclusion suspiciously strong, but I don't know whether the earlier claims are even accurate. Which parts of this summary are fair to Winner's actual argument, which are subtle overstatements or conflations, and does the conclusion about technological determinism actually follow from the accurate parts?
