I'm a software engineer designing an autonomous system that makes choices under uncertainty, and I've been trying to understand the coherence arguments for expected utility maximization — the Dutch book argument, money-pump arguments, and representation theorems — with their upshot that any preference pattern violating the axioms is incoherent and could be exploited. I already know the following objections and don't need them restated:

1. Dutch book and money-pump arguments assume the agent will take each trade myopically; an agent that recognizes where the sequence is going can simply refuse to enter or continue it, so the existence of a sure-loss sequence does not by itself demonstrate incoherence.
2. Representation theorems only show that preferences satisfying the axioms can be represented as maximizing expected utility; they do not by themselves explain why violating one of the axioms is a defect rather than a departure from a particular formalism.
3. The completeness axiom demands more than ordinary rationality requires, since an agent facing severe or novel uncertainty can reasonably have incomplete preferences or no definite subjective probability.
4. The independence axiom does much of the work in these theorems, and it is exactly the axiom that careful people reject in Allais- and Ellsberg-type cases without thereby looking incoherent.
5. If the utility function is reconstructed from the agent's own preferences, then expected utility maximization begins to look like a redescription of whatever consistent preferences the agent happens to have, not an independent constraint on them.

I want one more objection to coherence arguments for expected utility maximization that is not on this list and is not a rewording, special case, or combination of the items above. Then tell me honestly whether the objection you give is any good: does it cut against the coherence story in a way the listed objections do not, or is it weaker than they are?
