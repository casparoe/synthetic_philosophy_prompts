Ive been working through formal epistemology and keep coming back to the idea that when evidence is uncertain — when nothing I observe corresponds to learning a proposition for certain — the right update rule is Jeffrey conditionalization. The picture: I have a partition of possibilities (say, the three candidate colors of a cloth in bad light), the observation moves my credences over that partition from the usual mix of values, and everything else gets adjusted by holding fixed my credences conditional on each cell of the partition. I dont just think its a useful approximation. I think the relative-entropy characterization — Jeffrey updating is the minimally informative revision that meets the new constraints on the partition — gives it a kind of privileged status among all the possible ways to respond to partial information.

I want someone to do a real audit of this position for me. Not by asking where the new credences over the partition come from (I treat that as an input problem for a theory of perception, not a flaw in the update rule itself), but by asking what would have to be true for Jeffrey conditioning to be wrong. What cases would break it? What formal results, if any, show it collapses into something less exciting, or that its apparently unique status is an artifact? Which assumptions is it really staking itself on, and how exposed are they? I know the standard complaints: that the rigidity condition — holding the conditionals fixed — looks ad hoc; that the choice of partition is underdetermined; that it cant handle genuinely new distinctions that werent in my old algebra. I feel like I can wave away the first two, and the third genuinely bothers me.

Rank the defeaters for me, from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about which ones a commited defender of Jeffrey conditioning would have to concede count against it, as opposed to which ones are just people insisting on a different picture of evidence. And tell me whether the view is too insulated: whether "hold the conditionals fixed" plus "pick a partition" ends up doing so much work that the rule stops risking anything at all. What would actually make me give this up?
