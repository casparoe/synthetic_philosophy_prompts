Consider the debate protocol sometimes proposed for supervising AI systems far more capable than any human judge. Two expert agents argue opposite sides of a question before a human judge who sees only the transcript and must decide which side is right. The standard argument for this protocol is that a defender of a false or misleading answer cannot hold out indefinitely, because the defender of the correct answer can keep identifying, one at a time, the precise points at which the opposing case fails. A judge who is good enough to follow those locally flagged corrections need not be able to generate the truth; in this way, debate is supposed to elicit correct answers even when the judge is far less capable than either debater.

Now vary the setup in one specific way. The judge can reliably verify a single local claim once it has been clearly flagged for them, but they can hold at most seven such claims in mind when forming an overall judgment; beyond seven, the transcript dissolves into a general impression. Suppose also that a debater arguing for a wrong answer may obfuscate rather than lie. At a fixed cost per package, it can present interlocking material that is individually true — apt-sounding comparisons, well-timed digressions, true statistics under misleading framing — arranged so that the wrong answer comes to seem overwhelmingly probable even though the pieces do not actually support it. No single piece is false or damning by itself, and the only fully truthful rebuttal is structural: it shows that individually unproblematic pieces jointly fail to support the conclusion. The honest debater can attempt that rebuttal, also at a cost, but it is exactly the kind of global judgment the seven-claim judge cannot reliably make. Both debaters know the judge's limit before they begin.

Work through how this change affects the standard argument for debate. Does the original guarantee survive, collapse, or survive only in a weaker form? Under what conditions on the costs of obfuscation and rebuttal, on the integration limit, or on the judge's verification abilities, would you still expect the honest debater to win? If the protocol fails under the variation, is the failure a fixable engineering limitation or a principled one?
