I am drafting a statement of my position on consciousness for a reading group, and I want a consistency audit before I circulate it. My commitments, as they now stand, are:

1. Causal closure: every physical event has sufficient physical causes, and no nonphysical causes need be posited.
2. Phenomenal character is real and is not identical with any physical or functional property.
3. Phenomenal character is epiphenomenal: it is produced by neural activity and exerts no causal influence on behavior, judgment, attention, or memory.
4. First-person reports of phenomenal character are generally reliable and not merely lucky; a normal person's report that they are in pain, or that a stimulus looks red to them, is usually correct.
5. This reliability has an explanation: the very neural state that disposes a person to make the report is also the state that, in normal circumstances, produces the phenomenal character reported, so the report tracks the phenomenal character via a common cause.
6. A molecule-for-molecule physical duplicate of me with no phenomenal consciousness whatever — a zombie, in the philosophical sense — is conceivable, and this conceivability is a serious reason to accept (2).

Which commitments are in genuine tension, and which pairs only appear to conflict? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each identify the cheapest revision and what it would cost. Is there any single adjustment that relieves the most pressure?
