I keep a rough checklist for deciding when putting someone at risk without their consent crosses into being wrong, and I just hit a case where different boxes give me different answers. Here are the criteria I normally use:

1. The harm, if it happens, is serious — death, serious injury, major financial loss, something like that.
2. The risk isn't a merely hypothetical one; it's large enough to count as a real chance.
3. The person exposed gets no real benefit from the risk-producing activity.
4. It was feasible to ask them or at least give them a meaningful chance to opt out, but nobody did.
5. They can't easily avoid the exposure by ordinary precautions.

Now the case. A city approves a summer drone light show over the river: 30 nights, about 200 drones per night. The operator's own filing estimates that over the whole season there is roughly a 2 percent chance that one drone will suffer a loss-of-control failure that sends it into the riverwalk or a facing apartment, which could seriously injure someone. The city publishes the schedule, so in principle people could keep their windows shut and stay off the riverwalk on those nights, but the show has become a big civic event, and many people in those apartments moved in before it existed. Residents do get something out of it — they can watch the shows, and the city says the tourism money keeps taxes down — but the real beneficiaries are the operator and the riverside businesses. The operator carries insurance, so an injured person would likely be compensated, but that's after the fact.

Under my criteria, is this a wrongful risk imposition without consent? I'd like a definite verdict, not a menu of considerations. Work through each criterion, tell me where it cuts and where it's indeterminate, and say whether the case shows that my checklist needs changing — for instance, whether serious harm and tiny probability should get combined into expected harm rather than kept as seperate boxes, or whether compensation after the fact should count at all. And please write in American English.
