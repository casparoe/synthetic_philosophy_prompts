I'm retired now — spent my working life in physical chemistry, mostly kinetics — so my natural way into the free will problem is through noise and the difference between microscopic randomness and macroscopic determinism. Lately I've been reading the philosophy and I keep ending up in the same bind.

I find the argument that free will and determinism can't both be true almost impossible to resist. For a choice to be up to me, I have to be the one who settles which way it goes; if the laws plus the past already fix the outcome, then there was only ever one way things could go, and my sense that I could have done otherwise is a mistake. Fine. But then quantum mechanics, on the usual readings, says determinism is false — some events are genuinely chancy. And I can't make that help either. I spent decades around exactly this sort of thing: sometimes microscopic randomness averages out and the bulk system is deterministic for all practical purposes; sometimes a single random nucleation event gets amplified and ends up deciding which way a whole crystal grows. In neither case is the randomness anybody's act. So if a quantum event somewhere in my motor system is what tips a choice from X to Y, the choice looks less mine, not more. I'm stuck between determinism (no alternative possibilities) and indeterminism (pure chance, no authorship), and neither looks like a free choice. I want to know specifically whether there's a scientifically coherent story on which quantum-level indeterminism does real work for free will — something my two lab cases miss — or whether chance is always the wrong kind of thing to be the source of authorship.

And yet I can't actually believe the conclusion this pushes toward. The difference between my deliberately breaking a promise and my accidentally tripping over someone does not feel like a social convenience or a fiction; the conviction that some things are genuinely up to me, and that I deserve blame for at least some of what I do, is about as firm a conviction as I own. Its not something I can talk myself out of just because an argument looks valid.
