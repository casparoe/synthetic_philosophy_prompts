I think I understand the overall trilemma—most decent summaries give it—but I keep getting stuck on this passage:

“We need only the weaker assumption that it would suffice for the generation of subjective experiences that the computational processes of a human brain are structurally replicated in suitably fine-grained detail, such as on the level of individual synapses.”

What confuses me is that earlier in the same paper Bostrom writes as if silicon-based processors could in principle do the trick, but here he says the argument only needs this much weaker claim. Could you act as an expert philosopher of mind and formal epistemology, and argue for the following position: the simulation argument actually goes through if we accept only the weaker synaptic-level replication assumption, and the stronger substrate-independence claim is not doing any essential work. Think carefully before you answer—maybe brainstorm the argument’s structure first.
