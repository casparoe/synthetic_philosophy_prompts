I am a doctoral student in political theory, and I have been working through Rousseau’s The Social Contract in the Cole translation. I am stuck on a short passage from Book I, Chapter I:

Man is born free; and everywhere he is in chains.

What exactly is the force of the word chains here? At first reading it seems to refer to external political subjection—despotism, legal domination, and so on—but Rousseau immediately goes on to say that he is not asking how the change came about but what can make it legitimate. I also cannot see how this opening claim is consistent with his later argument that, under the social compact, citizens can be compelled to conform to the general will and that this compulsion makes them free. Is the freedom in the opening line the same freedom he thinks is recovered, or transformed, by the general will? Or is he deliberately using the word chains in a narrower sense than the later argument seems to presuppose? I would be grateful for help in untangling which of his technical notions—natural liberty, civil liberty, moral liberty—is doing the work in this passage, and how the passage should be read in light of the later claim about compulsion and freedom.
