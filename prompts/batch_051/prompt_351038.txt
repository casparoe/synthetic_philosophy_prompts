I'm a philosophy professor, and I had a dumb argument at a dinner party that's still bugging me. A friend in finance offered me this game: he flips a fair coin until it comes up heads, and if the first heads is on flip n he pays me 2^n dollars — so $2 if heads on the first flip, $4 if the first heads is on the second flip, $8 on the third, and so on, doubling each time. He said the expected value is infinite, since you're adding up (1/2^n)(2^n) = 1 forever, so any finite entry fee is a rational bargain. He asked me for $100 to play, I said no, and he told me I was being irrational.

Here's my worry. His math looks right in the abstract, but he can't actually pay an infinite amount. Say the most I could ever realistically collect from him is about a million dollars (after that he's broke, or he just doesn't pay). How much should I have been willing to pay to play? I don't want a generic "consider your risk tolerance" answer — I want the precise flaw in the infinite-expectation reasoning once the payout is capped by what he can actually pay. And was I right to decline at $100?
