My partner is a Frankfurt-style compatibilist who thinks the cases show the opposite — that responsibility lives in the actual sequence that produced the action, not in the unused backup possibilities. We both accept the standard Jones-and-Black setup, with this detail: Black has planted a device that reads a very early, involuntary neural tell that reliably precedes any decision to shoot Smith; if the tell appears, the device will force Jones to decide to shoot; but Jones decides on his own, the tell never appears, and Black does nothing. We both agree Jones is morally responsible for shooting Smith.

Draft the joint statement we could both sign. Please write the whole thing in American English. It should open with a paragraph of neutral framing, then a numbered list of claims both sides accept (don't let these be vacuous — they should be substantive enough that a third party would learn something from them), then a section stating our exact disagreements in wording each side would accept as a fair statement of its own view, with each disagreement flagged as substantive or merely verbal. Then give the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of us. We keep coming back to two: (a) whether the involuntary neural tell is a "flicker of freedom" that counts as an alternative possibility in the sense PAP needs, and (b) whether a Frankfurt-style case can even be built without any such tell, and whether stripping the tell out would change the verdict. For each crux, propose a concrete test case or tweak to the main case that we would both agree bears on it, and say in advance what each of us predicts it will show.
