Last term my eleven-year-old daughter asked me whether the number 2 is “inside” the number 4, and I was unsettled to discover that the honest answer depends on a conventional choice about how natural numbers are coded as sets. That small question has since pulled me back into thinking about mathematical structuralism, and I would like to turn it into something useful for my upper-level philosophy of mathematics seminar.

I am setting this year’s final assessment. Please draft one essay question on the identity problem for mathematical structuralism, focused on whether claims such as “2 is an element of 4” have a determinate truth value and on whether structuralism can say what makes 2 the thing it is. The question should be answerable in 90 minutes by an upper-level undergraduate who has studied the philosophy of mathematics, and it should be self-contained: it must not require the student to cite particular books or papers. The difficulty should come from the crux rather than from recall. A student should be unable to answer adequately by reciting summaries of set-theoretic reduction and structuralism. In particular, a good candidate will have to compute the two standard set-theoretic codings of the natural numbers and show that they disagree about membership, extract the argument this is meant to motivate structuralism, state structuralism precisely enough to explain why the membership question is meant to be ill-posed rather than simply false, and then confront the objection that structuralism has merely relocated the identification problem to the level of places within a structure.

Please provide three things. (i) The question itself. (ii) A model answer of 600–800 words in British English, taking a clear position on whether structuralism ultimately has a satisfactory answer to the identity problem. (iii) A marking scheme with five must-hit points, each worth 5 marks out of a total of 25, and, for each point, a one-line note on how candidates typically get it wrong.

Finally, because this will be parsed automatically, the very last line of your response should take the form \answer{...} and should contain one sentence summarising the crux the question is designed to test.
