I serve on the conduct committee of a residential co-operative. Our standing resolution requires us to test every proposed co-operative rule by asking whether it conforms to the golden rule, and we treat our earlier decisions as precedents to be extended consistently rather than as one-off judgements. Our written records summarise the committee's reasoning, but the committee has never adopted a formal wording of the test. I have to draft the committee's recommendation on a new proposal, and I would like a careful analysis that works from the record alone; no references or outside sources are needed.

Here are the relevant precedents.

1. Washing machine hours. The committee approved a rule forbidding use of the washing machine between 10 p.m. and 8 a.m., since the machine stands beside two bedrooms. A resident, Dana, whose work hours make late washing convenient, objected that she personally would not mind hearing the machine at night and would prefer to be able to wash late. The committee's recorded reasoning was that the rule requires doing as one would be done by, and that anyone trying to sleep beside a running machine would want it switched off; the test is not settled by one member's unusual tolerance.

2. Compulsory meeting attendance. The committee rejected a proposed rule requiring every member to attend the monthly house meeting. Its supporters argued that the meetings leave everyone better informed and better off. The committee's recorded reasoning was that nobody would wish to be compelled, even into something beneficial, and that a rule one would resent having applied to oneself cannot be applied to others.

3. Emergency torches. The committee approved a rule requiring each household to keep a working torch in a bracket outside its bedroom door. A member, Jules, said she saw no need for it and did not want anything fixed near her door. The committee's recorded reasoning was that a person's actual preference in comfortable circumstances is not decisive; in an emergency, any of us would want a working torch close at hand, and a rule that anyone would want applied to them in the situation that counts does not violate the golden rule.

4. Shower-time monitoring. The committee rejected a proposal to require every member to log their shower times, with the logs reviewed to reduce water use. Even its proponents admitted they would dislike having their own showering timed in this way. The committee's recorded reasoning was that a rule one would dislike having applied to oneself, and whose burdens no affected person could reasonably be expected to accept for a modest saving, fails the golden rule.

5. Courtyard loudspeakers. The committee approved a rule forbidding loudspeakers in the shared courtyard after 9 p.m. A member, Priya, wrote that she actually enjoyed falling asleep to faint music from the courtyard and would not have objected to the noise. The committee's recorded reasoning was that the relevant question is whether a person kept awake by amplified music would want relief, not whether an unusual contrary preference would make the rule inconvenient.

The new case is as follows. A proposal asks that, after 8 p.m., the main lounge be lit only by dim amber lamps, with the ceiling lights switched off until 7 a.m. The stated purposes are to reduce energy use and to make the lounge calmer in the evening. The proposing members, and most of the conduct committee, say they themselves would be content, or even pleased, to have the lounge dimmed in this way; several say they actively dislike bright light at night. One member, Iris, has objected. She earns part of her income painting in the evenings, and the main lounge is the only room in the co-operative with enough space and light for her easel and lighting rig; her own room is a small north-facing bedsit. She has asked that the ceiling lights remain usable in the lounge for two hours each evening, or that a comparable properly lit room be made available to her. Her supporters on the committee say her objection is one that a reasonable person in her position would make. Her opponents reply that the lounge is a shared sitting room, not a studio, and that she cannot expect the co-operative to keep it brightly lit for one member's convenience.

The committee has to choose between approving the rule as written, rejecting it, or approving it only with an accommodation that guarantees Iris access to a properly lit workspace in the evening. Please give me:
(i) the decision you recommend: approve as written, reject, or approve only with an accommodation;
(ii) the precedent you judge to be the closest analogue to this case, and why;
(iii) a statement of the distinct principles that are each consistent with our precedents, and what each would yield on the new case;
(iv) if the precedents underdetermine the decision, a clear statement of which principle we would be adopting with each possible choice, and your best judgement about which choice coheres best with the record as a whole, with reasons; and
(v) the further case, not among these precedents, whose outcome would most clearly reveal which principle the committee is in fact following.

Please write your answer in British English.
