I work as the researcher for a small documentary series, and I've started to feel like a referee without a rulebook. Our episodes keep landing on stories like the Trojan War, King Arthur, and the Exodus, and each time we have to decide whether to present them as legend, myth, "a legend that may preserve a historical core," or history at least in outline. These labels go to a large audience, with a fact-checker crawling through the script afterwards, and my writers keep using the phrase "there's a historical core here" as if it's obvious. The trouble is the evidence behind that phrase seems to vary wildy between cases, and we don't have a specialist historian available for every story.

I'd like a decision procedure we can actually run in a one-hour research meeting, before the executive producer signs off. It can be a checklist or a flow of questions in order, but it needs to say: what we're asking, what counts as a good enough answer, where the procedure stops with a "we can't say this," and where it has to hand the decision to an actual person because the question has become a judgement call. Build it from whatever historical method genuinely has to say about how a legend becomes history, but keep it usable by smart non-scholars. Then run it on three cases so I can see it work: the destruction levels at Hisarlik versus Homer's war with heroes and a ten-year siege; King Arthur, where the earliest documents naming him are centuries after the period he would belong to; and the Exodus, where the biblical account is our main narrative and there is no direct Egyptian evidence of a mass departure. At least one of these should come out in a way I won't like, and you should say so. Please also flag the cases the procedure can't settle and what it will systematically miss.
