I'm grading an intro aesthetics midterm and could use a second set of eyes on one answer. The exam question I set was:

“Below is a passage from W. E. B. Du Bois’s 1926 essay ‘Criteria of Negro Art’:

‘The thing we are talking about tonight is part of the great fight we are carrying on and it represents a forward and an upward look -- a pushing onward.’

In your own words, explain what Du Bois means when he insists elsewhere in the essay that all art is propaganda, like it or not. Does this passage show that Du Bois thinks art is valuable only insofar as it serves a political struggle? If not, what else does he think art must do?”

Here is the student’s answer:

“Du Bois is rejecting the view that art can be separated from politics. The passage calls art ‘part of the great fight,’ so he clearly thinks even the discussion of beauty is continuous with the struggle for Black rights. His claim that all art is propaganda means every artwork has a viewpoint and tries to persuade. Since all art does this, Black artists shouldn’t be ashamed of making art that advocates for racial equality. But Du Bois does not think art is only a political tool. He says beauty matters too, and that Black artists should be free to depict Black life with honesty and dignity. In fact, the deeper point of the passage is that the fight for freedom is itself an aesthetic project, because what Black people are fighting for is the right to create and enjoy beauty. So the value of art for Du Bois is not reducible to serving a political struggle; propaganda and beauty are two sides of the same coin.”
