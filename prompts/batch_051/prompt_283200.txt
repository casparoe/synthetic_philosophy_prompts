In the past year I have had the same sentence close off three different arguments, and it seems to me to be doing different work in each. A colleague said my flying to a conference was wrong because "what if everyone did that?" — if everyone flew at that rate, emissions would be catastrophic, so the individual flight is supposed to inherit the wrongness. A friend used the same move to argue I had a duty to vote: if everyone stayed home, democracy would collapse. And I have caught myself wanting to say it to someone who buys pointlessly wasteful gadgets: if everyone bought one, the aggregate harm would be appalling. I suspect these are sharply different principles flying under one verbal flag, and I want to be able to tell them apart.

Please dissect the one-liner "what if everyone did that?" as it actually functions in moral argument, and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making. Candidate readings I can see already: a Kantian test of whether my maxim could be willed as universal law; a rule-consequentialist or generalization argument about the consequences of everyone following the rule; a claim about my marginal contribution to a collective harm or to crossing a harmful threshold; a consistency-of-prescriptions requirement that I not make a special exception of myself unless I could still endorse the rule from every position; and a fairness or free-riding charge. Are these genuinely distinct, and are there others worth separating? For each reading, give a one-sentence statement of the principle, a one-sentence verdict on whether it is a platitude, a defensible substantive principle, or a false inference, and say which of the three cases — the flight, the vote, the gadget — it would actually support. I would also like one sentence noting whether the reading's force depends on who "everyone" ranges over (literally all humans, everyone in my circumstances, or everyone inclined to act that way if permitted). Please format the answer as a numbered list of readings with those components, and keep the entire answer under 1,000 words.
