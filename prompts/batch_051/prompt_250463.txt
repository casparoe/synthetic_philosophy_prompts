I am a retired natural scientist now working through the philosophy of economics, and I find myself caught between a theory that displays every virtue my old discipline taught me to respect and an intuition I cannot persuade myself to set aside.

The theory is the ordinalist, revealed-preference treatment of utility. On this view an individual's utility is only a numerical representation of their choices, defined up to a monotone transformation, and the only well-defined comparisons are intrapersonal: one outcome is preferred to another, or one gain is larger than another, only relative to a single person's behavioral rankings. To assert that A's distress outweighs B's begins to look like a claim with no measurement procedure behind it, more like the speaker's own valuation dressed up as a fact. I keep reaching for a calibration analogy: two instruments each capable only of ranking temperatures within their own rooms, with no common scale between them, would leave the question of which room is warmer unanswerable.

Yet the intuition will not go away. Some interpersonal comparisons seem to me to be among the best-supported judgments we make, not the worst. That a patient with severe burns benefits more from an analgesic than a patient with a mild headache, or that a famine-relief program does more for its recipients than a yacht subsidy does for the wealthy, seems corrigible, evidence-responsive, and anything but arbitrary projection. If the theory says such statements are meaningless, I suspect the theory has lost touch with what benefit means; if the intuition is an illusion, I want to know precisely which cognitive bias or sympathetic error produces it.

Act as a professional philosopher of social science and welfare economics. Think carefully, begin by reconstructing each side in its strongest form before you argue for any resolution, and then give me a negotiated verdict rather than a verdict for one side: what it would take for the intuition to be debunked and dropped, what it would take for the theory to be amended or abandoned, and whether there is a reading of "benefit" and "comparison" on which both survive.
