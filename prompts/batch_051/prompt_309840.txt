The Constitution of the Republic of Meridia contains the following provision, drafted in 1962 and never amended: "All state power belongs to the people, and shall be exercised in accordance with the will of the people. The will of the people shall be expressed by universal suffrage in elections, and in referendums held in the manner prescribed by law."

A civic-technology firm now operates a secure national platform on which any registered voter may at any time record a standing yes or no on any bill pending before the Assembly. Last week, in the seventy-two hours before the Assembly voted on a treaty establishing a permanent customs union, the platform recorded positions from more than half the registered electorate, with fifty-eight percent of those recording a view opposed. No law authorizes the platform or gives its results any legal status. The President, who has authority to refuse to sign the treaty, says the platform vote is a clearer expression of the will of the people than anything the Assembly has received, and that the first sentence of the provision obliges her to follow it. The Speaker of the Assembly says the second sentence is exhaustive, and the platform result is at most a petition.

Analyze whether the provision applies to the platform vote and whether the President is bound by it. Give the answer that each of these approaches yields: the ordinary meaning of the words, the purpose of the provision, what the drafters would have said if asked in 1962 whether a future mass medium for registering voter views could count as an expression of the will of the people, and what a reasonable reader today would understand. State which approach should control and why. In doing so, address what the case reveals about the populist idea that there is a 'will of the people' waiting to be found and obeyed, as against the idea that such a will is an artifact of legally constituted procedures.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
