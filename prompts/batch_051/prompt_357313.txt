I am a novelist, and I am trying to think through a dispute over a co-writing agreement I signed. The agreement contains this clause:

“A manuscript shall be deemed a joint work of the parties when each party has contributed written material to the manuscript, and when each party prepared that material with the settled intention that it would be combined with the other party’s material into a single continuous narrative.”

My co-author and I outlined a novel together, divided the chapters, and agreed to revise one another. Shortly afterwards she lost interest. She sent me roughly eight thousand words of notes and scene fragments drawn from our outline, then left the country and became unreachable apart from a single email: “Use whatever helps; the book is yours to finish.” I wrote all of the final prose myself, weaving her fragments in and rewriting most of them beyond recognition. She now claims co-authorship under the clause.

The drafters plainly imagined two writers working continuously side by side; they did not imagine one party contributing fragments and then withdrawing while the other finishes alone. I want to know what the clause requires on the three standard approaches to interpretation — the words themselves, the purpose of the provision, and what the drafters would have said if asked — and where those approaches conflict.

But I also want the philosophical question beneath it. I gather that philosophers of joint action divide over whether a joint act requires, first, that each participant hold a distinctively collective “we-intention”; second, that each participant’s individual intentions and plans simply mesh together under common knowledge; or third, that the parties enter a joint commitment that neither can rescind by unilateral say-so. Do the interpretive approaches and these philosophical accounts run together, or do they come apart? On each account, did she and I write this book together — and if the best contractual reading and the best philosophical account disagree, which should govern, and what was the clause really tracking?
