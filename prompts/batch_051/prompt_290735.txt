I'm on the academic integrity committee at a mid-size university. A vendor wants to pilot its proctoring platform for online exams. The platform records eye gaze, keystroke rhythm, mouse hesitations, and background audio, then uses a model to assign each student a 'suspicion score' from 0 to 100 and flags cases above a threshold for a human reviewer. The vendor stresses that no one is ever sanctioned on the basis of the score alone, because a person always makes the final call. Some members think this is just an efficient version of a proctor walking the room. Others argue an exam is never a neutral measurement, that it's a mechanism of sorting, normalization, and knowledge-production, and this platform intensifies that by treating every student as a potential cheater and making a statistical profile the baseline for innocence. I need to circulate a short memo with a clear recommendation: approve, reject, or approve with conditions. The memo should actually engage the strongest objection on whichever side loses, rather than caricaturing it, and it should give the pilot organizers something concrete they could respond to.
