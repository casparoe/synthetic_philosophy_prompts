I'm on the doctrine committee of our denomination and we've been asked to draft a study document on Scripture and translation, which means I've now sat through three meetings of the same two people. One holds that inspiration attaches only to the original-language texts — the "autographs" — so a translation is inspired only in a derivative, second-hand way, like a good photograph of a Vermeer isn't a Vermeer. The other keeps pointing out that the New Testament writers quote the Septuagint as Scripture, that the church has never corporately had the autographs in its hands, and that the King James translators themselves, in their own preface, were a lot more comfortable calling a translation God's word than the KJV-only crowd is. I dont want another position paper; I want to see these two actually talk.

Write me a dialogue between two equally competent characters — call them what you like, maybe a systematic theologian and a Bible translator — arguing about whether a translated text can be inspired in its own right. Neither should be a strawman, and they should respond to each other's actual moves rather than delivering alternating speeches. Push them toward the hard pressure points: whether inspiration belongs to the text or to the act of speaking/writing, what it means when an apostle treats a Greek translation as carrying divine authority, and whether an autographs-only view leaves the church today with no inspired text at all.

Format it as: Dialogue, about 600 words; then a titled section called "The real hinge", about 200 words, saying what the genuinely unresolved crux is and what turns on it for wording our denominational statement.
