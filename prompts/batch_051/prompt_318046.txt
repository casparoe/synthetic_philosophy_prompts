Over the past two years I have been writing a novel about a mathematician, and through her I have picked up an intellectual habit that now governs more of my thinking than I intended. The habit is this: whenever an argument turns on an arbitrary labeling, I discount it. My protagonist puts the principle in mathematical form. Name the roots of a polynomial x1 through xn, and she refuses to conclude anything whose content changes when two indices are exchanged; choose a basis for a vector space or a coordinate system, and she treats any conclusion that changes under that choice as being about the notation rather than about the object. She asserts only what is invariant under the relevant symmetry group, and she calls everything else non-canonical. I have started applying the same test well outside mathematics. Last month I dismissed a critic's claim that my third novel was the turning point of my career, because the claim depended on dividing a career into periods and the periodization depended on which works one counted as early; I also dismissed a reviewer's explanation of a character's motives because the explanation reversed when the character's two central relationships were listed in the opposite order. The policy feels exacting, and it has kept me from mistaking my own categories for features of the world. But I am no longer sure it is a virtue. A convention, once fixed, can have downstream consequences that are real even if the convention itself was arbitrary, and some objects carry enough extra structure that a choice which looks arbitrary is not arbitrary at all. Is this habit defensible as a general way of forming beliefs, or have I turned a mathematician's requirement of well-definedness into a way of refusing any judgment that could have been made differently? Under what conditions does the demand for invariance under relabeling track something genuine, and when does it simply discard information? And how would I tell, from the inside, whether I am enforcing a canon of well-definedness or protecting myself from particular, defeasible commitments?
