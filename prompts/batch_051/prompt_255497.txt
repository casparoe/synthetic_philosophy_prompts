In ordinary moral thinking I keep noticing an asymmetry around a karmic idea that good and bad fortune track moral desert. When someone who has been cruel or corrupt is prospering, people often take comfort in the thought that the person's moral debts will eventually be settled, that wrongdoing gets paid back in kind, whether in this life or a later one. But if the same principle is turned around, and someone's present suffering is treated as the settling of a debt that person incurred, most of the same people find the suggestion repellent. Both claims look like applications of one rule, just pointed in different temporal directions. Is there a defensible reason to accept the forward-looking application while rejecting the backward-looking one, or is the first only a comforting story whose logical counterpart we refuse to accept? I want the strongest justification you can build for the asymmetry, tested against hard cases such as a child born with a serious illness or a person who prospered through deliberate cruelty, and an honest verdict on whether the asymmetry should be kept, dropped, or reversed.
