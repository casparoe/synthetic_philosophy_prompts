I'm a philosopher (mostly language), and a thing keeps happening in my department that I don't have a clean name for. We have a seminar room with no assigned seats, but everyone sits in the same places every week. It started years ago, presumably because the first few meetings happened to go that way and nobody had any reason to sit elsewhere. Now, when a visitor or a new grad student sits in the wrong seat, people are actually put out — not just suprised, but mildly annoyed, as though a rule had been broken. The same thing happened with the order of questions after talks: some arbitrary pattern from the early meetings has hardened into something people now treat as obligatory, and violators get tutted at. I can see how a convention — an arbitrary solution to a coordination problem that everyone follows because everyone else follows it — might arise. What I don't have a name for is the second step: the point where the regularity stops being just a mutually convenient pattern and starts carrying normative force, where people expect compliance and react to non-compliance with something like blame. Is there an established term for that transition, and how do the standard accounts of convention and social norm draw the line? I'm not asking for a bare label; I want to know exactly where the boundary is supposed to fall between 'we all happen to do this because it solves a coordination problem' and 'we ought to do this,' and whether the best accounts treat it as a difference of degree or of kind. Also, please flag what's settled and what's still genuinely contested here rather than presenting one side as the consensus.
