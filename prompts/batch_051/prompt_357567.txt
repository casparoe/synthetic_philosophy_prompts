For a module on political obligation, I want a sorting instrument on the ethics of voting: a quiz of six to eight questions with a scoring key that sorts readers by what they say about concrete cases rather than by which label they prefer. Each question should present a voting situation with two or three answer options, and no question should name a position or ask the student to self-identify with one. The positions that should come out at the end include at least the civic duty to vote, lesser-evil consequentialism, expressive purism or third-party expression, rational abstention, and radical abstention from an unjust system. Before writing the questions, work out the logical space. I take the main dividing questions to be: whether an individual vote has enough expected influence to ground an outcome-based duty; whether a vote expresses endorsement of or complicity in a candidate's conduct; whether there is a freestanding duty to participate in self-government; and whether the justice of the political system conditions what voting means. Pin down each leaf as a combination of answers to those questions, and use scenarios such as voting for a lesser-evil candidate who shares none of the voter's values, casting a third-party vote in a safe district, voting while seriously uninformed, and voting in an election the voter regards as part of an unjust system. Then identify which combinations of the dividing questions remain unoccupied, and say whether each is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Finally, flag any positions the quiz cannot separate and say why, for instance whether fairness-based and civic-republican versions of the duty to vote would answer every question the same way.
