ok so here's roughly where I've landed on the gender-equality paradox and I'm trying to figure out if it hangs together: (1) the cross-national pattern is real — countries that score higher on the usual gender-equality indices have bigger sex differences in fields like STEM, and it's not just a measurement artifact or an artifact of how you lump fields or countries together. (2) gendered career preferences are mostly made, not born: stereotypes, norms, what kids get encouraged into, role models, all of that. (3) part of the explanation is that in richer, more equal countries people are freer to act on the preferences they already have, wherever those came from, so underlying preferences show up more directly in actual choices. (4) a society in which women and men are free to choose different tracks, and do end up in different tracks, is not for that reason less equal or less just, as long as the tracks are genuinly open and the choices aren't coerced. (5) and I still think we should push hard on the pay gap, the leadership gap, and the domestic work gap. Is this a consistent package? Which parts pull against which, which apparent conflict is actually fine, and if it doesn't cohere what's the cheapest way to make it cohere?
