In my doctoral work on the moral limits of markets, I have become convinced that 'commodification' does at least two distinct jobs in the literature: it sometimes tracks a descriptive change in how a good is valued and treated, and sometimes tracks a thicker moral complaint about what a transaction expresses or does to the participants and to bystanders. I want a single working definition of commodification that can be tested, not merely stated. Here are the cases I care about: (1) selling a kidney to a stranger for money; (2) a shop raising the price of bottled water after a hurricane; (3) paying citizens to vote; (4) commercial surrogacy; (5) paying donors for blood plasma; (6) a firm buying and retiring personal data; (7) a hospital selling queue-jumps for elective surgery. I am confident that (1) and (3) are commodification and that (5) is not, and I have no settled view on the rest. Propose the definition, run it through all seven cases, say where it forces a verdict you find surprising, and then defend or revise it accordingly. I would rather have a definite definition with one controversial consequence than a hedge.
