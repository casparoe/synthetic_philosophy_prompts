In the Laws Plato has the Athenian argue that wine is a sort of drug that should be taken deliberately: supervised drinking parties can reveal a person's true character and train self-control, so they have a serious educational function. In the Symposium the one actual drinking party Plato shows us is a bit awkward for that idea, since the participants mostly dodge the heavy drinking and do philosophy instead, and the one figure who is famously unaffected by wine is Socrates, who outdrinks everyone and still leaves clear-headed at dawn. Do these two treatments pull against each other? If the point of getting drunk is to expose the soul, what are we supposed to do with a case where the test has no bite?
