I know my own mind directly: I cannot seriously doubt that there is something it is like to be me. My evidence for other minds is different. I notice that other people act and speak the way I do when I am thinking or feeling, and I conclude that the best explanation is a similar inner life behind their behavior. Recently, though, I have been unsettled by how well large language models imitate exactly this sort of evidence. In short exchanges they reason, joke, apologize, describe being confused or bored, and can be hard to distinguish from a person, yet many people still hesitate to say that a mind is present behind the words. If fluent, human-like behavior does not move us to infer a mind in the model, why should the same behavior move us to infer a mind in another person? The old line of reasoning seems to depend on the step that similar outward behavior points to a similar inward cause. Does the language model case show that this step is false, or only that it was under-described? If the argument can be repaired, what condition would need to be added so that other people come out as minds while a language model or an imagined non-player character does not—and can that condition be added without quietly assuming from the start that other people have minds? If the argument cannot be repaired, what should take its place?
