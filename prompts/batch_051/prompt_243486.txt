I'm not an academic, I drive a lorry and listen to philosophy podcasts, so this is just me thinking out loud. I keep tripping over the same move in arguments about whether an immortal or uploaded mind would eventually run out of new experiences. People say: the brain has only finitely many possible states, so there's a finite list of possible experiences, so after long enough you've had them all and the rest is reruns. I've heard the same shape of argument about creativity: there are only so many tunes, sentences, paintings, and then thats it. But the same people will admit that a tiny counter gizmo that ticks up by one can go on forever without ever repeating an output, even though it has only a handful of internal states. So finiteness alone doesn't stop a system producing endlessly new outputs. I've realised the hidden step is treating experiences as items on a shelf that get used up, rather than like positions on a counter that keep being reached for the first time. What I want to know is whether there's a name for that move from finite states to a fixed repertoire of experiences. And is there a name for the contrast I'm gesturing at — the counter produces new outputs but nobody would say it has new experiences, so output and experience come apart somewhere. It's not quite the old thought that living forever would get boring; I'm after the step before that, where finiteness is treated as a cap on how much can be experienced at all. If there's no established name, coin one and define it, with the boundary cases marked. But please dont just lay out options — commit to one answer and defend it.
