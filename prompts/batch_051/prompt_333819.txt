Here's a thought I keep coming back to. Otto Neurath spent a big part of his career pushing Isotype, a system of standardized pictograms meant to make social and economic facts legible across language barriers — he thought of it as a kind of international picture language, but a very disciplined one: symbols had to be simple, repeatable, and combined by rules so you could compare quantities at a glance. He was gone long before emojis showed up. Emojis are now the closest thing most people have to a shared picture language, but they're almost the opposite of Isotype: semantically fuzzy, controlled by tech platforms and the Unicode Consortium, used more for tone and vibes than for visual argument. So here's what I want: predict, as specifically as you can, what Neurath would have thought of emoji. Would he treat them as a partial vindication of the idea that pictures can do real communicative work without prose, as a textbook case of why picture languages need standardization, or as something else — a genuinely mixed verdict? Don't just say he'd have complicated feelings. Ground it in the actual commitments of the Isotype project as you understand them (standardization, repeatability, visual neutrality, universal legibility, whatever you take to be central), and then tell me whether, if he were unhappy with emoji, there's a plausible redesign or usage convention he'd have liked better.
