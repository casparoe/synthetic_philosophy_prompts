Hey, I run a small democracy-reform reading group and I need a "which position on sortition / lottocracy do you actually hold?" quiz for an upcoming session. I want people to be classified by how they answer concrete scenarios, not by which label they like, so please write me 9 questions. Each should be a little case with 2-4 answer options: e.g., a mid-sized city gives a randomly selected citizens' council binding power over the zoning code; a national climate assembly chosen by lot can pass laws the elected parliment can't touch for five years; a randomly selected oversight board sets the ballot-initiative agenda and audits agencies but cannot legislate; a constitutional amendment would abolish recall elections so allotted assemblies don't fear backlash. That kind of thing. Include a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto: full/pure lottocracy (replace the elected legislature), mixed or bicameral sortition (one house by lot, one elected), deliberative mini-public / citizens' assembly (issue-specific and usually advisory or agenda-setting), sortition-as-oversight / anti-corruption, and anti-sortition electoral-accountability views. Build the scoring around two independent axes rather than the labels themselves: (1) is the lottery mainly for descriptive representation (mirroring the population) or mainly for breaking career politicians' and donors' grip; and (2) does legitimacy require that voters can throw someone out later, or can it come from the deliberative process itself. Make sure no question just asks "do you support replacing elections with lotteries?" Also flag any answer patterns that land between leaves and briefly say whether the gap is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored. And if two leaves turn out indistinguishable given the questions, say so rather than pretending the scoring key can handle it.
