Produce the joint statement that an endurantist and a perdurantist could issue at the end of an adversarial collaboration on how ordinary objects persist through time: a document both parties can sign as an accurate record of where they agree and where they still part ways. Open with a numbered list of shared claims, worded so that neither side reads them as already committing to the other's view — include what each side is not claiming, and what change and persistence minimally require. Then state each residual disagreement twice, once in wording the endurantist owns and once in wording the perdurantist owns, and flag any disagreement that looks merely verbal. Next identify the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party — and for each crux give a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it (for example, the problem of temporary intrinsics, a coincidence/constitution case, or a fission/fusion case). For each case, record in advance what each side predicts it will show, and state what evidence or argument each side would accept as counting against its own view. Do not adjudicate; the document should preserve the residual disagreement exactly. Conclude with a short note saying how confident you are in these formulations and singling out the formulation you are least sure about.
