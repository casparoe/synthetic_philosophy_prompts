For a piece I am writing on the value of inner quiet, I would like a consistency audit of the following package before I commit to it.

(a) Ataraxia—the felt state of freedom from disturbance—is a final good, not merely an instrument to other goods.
(b) The Stoic diagnosis is broadly right: most everyday agitation is manufactured by our judgments about things not in our control, and it can be dissolved without real loss.
(c) Yet full apatheia, understood as the extirpation of the passions, goes too far: grief at a loved one's death, and anger at an injustice done to someone close to you, are appropriate responses, and a person who never felt them would be missing something rather than better off.
(d) A wise person practices selective attention with respect to distant evils she cannot affect; not everything true deserves a place before the mind.
(e) Still, I am suspicious of peace bought through ignorance: if a true piece of information would predictably make you less tranquil, that is not by itself a sufficient reason to avoid it.
(f) Anger is often a net cost to the person who feels it, but it is sometimes the only thing that moves a person to intervene when calm deliberation has already failed.

Please work through these as an auditor: identify where the real strains are, where the tensions are merely apparent, and what the cheapest repair would be in each case. Return exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, ordered from most to least severe.
