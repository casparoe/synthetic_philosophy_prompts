I recently spent a long evening arguing with a close friend about corruption. She had just returned from several years working abroad, and she insisted that Western anti-corruption standards are being imposed on societies where gift-giving and personal networks do different moral and social work; I kept replying that some transactions are corrupt regardless of local custom, even though I could not state the underlying principle cleanly. We never reached agreement. Since then, I have been turning over what seems like a deeper puzzle within my own political system.

Consider the following pattern. A mid-level public official can be disciplined or prosecuted for accepting a modest gift from a company whose license application is pending before her office — no explicit bargain needs to be proven; the appearance of an improper relationship can be sufficient on its own. Yet the same company may legally contribute large sums to the campaign of the politician who oversees that office, and after leaving office the politician may take a well-paid position with the company. Likewise, a local party boss who delivers a publicly funded infrastructure project to a neighborhood in exchange for its electoral support is widely condemned as corrupt, while a legislator who votes for a spending package partly because it will benefit her district and therefore her re-election prospects is said to be doing her job.

I want to understand what this selectivity commits us to. What theory of corruption — of what makes a transaction corrupt in the first place — would make the first practice in each pair corrupt while leaving the second as legitimate ordinary politics? What does the pattern presuppose about the wrong-making feature of corruption: is it the presence of a quid pro quo, the secrecy, the breach of a role-specific duty, the conversion of public office into private advantage, the intent to influence, or something else? Do these practices presuppose one consistent account, or are they merely compatible with several rival accounts? And where do they fail to settle which account is correct? In answering, please distinguish between what the practices strictly presuppose and what they merely fit well with.
