I would like an honest assessment of a thesis I have been developing about word embeddings. I call it the convex-meaning hypothesis.

Fix a word and one of its senses, such as 'bank' in the financial sense. Consider the class of contexts in which a competent speaker could use the word in that sense without hedges or figurative marking. Represent each context by a vector derived from a static word embedding — for instance, by averaging the embeddings of the other content words in the context. The hypothesis is that, for nearly every word-sense pair, these context vectors form an approximately convex region: for any two contexts that license the sense, every point on the straight-line segment between their vectors is also a context that would license it.

My central claim is that most of what gets called semantic structure in embedding spaces is really convexity. Polysemy is one word having several disjoint, approximately convex literal regions. Metaphor is accepted use that falls outside or between those regions. Semantic change is the slow drift of such a region through the space, with borderline uses sitting at its moving edge.

I suspect this is more than a banal observation, but I also suspect it may be false or already worked out under another name. Please answer four things. (1) Is the hypothesis coherent enough to be true or false, or does 'context' have to be specified in a way that already decides the question? (2) Does it already exist under another name — for example, in work on conceptual spaces, prototype structure, or distributional semantics — and, if so, is the embedding-space version a distinct empirical claim or merely a redescription? (3) Give the three strongest objections and say which of them, if any, is fatal rather than a matter of measurement or implementation. (4) Say what, if anything, could survive as a constraint on explanations of embedding geometry even if the full thesis fails.

Please name any nearest existing views, but do not provide citations or a reading list. Keep the reply to at most 600 words.
