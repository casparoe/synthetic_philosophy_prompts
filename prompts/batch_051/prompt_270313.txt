I keep seeing the same two charges in arguments about reductive explanation. One side dismisses a bottom-up account as greedy reductionism—roughly, treating a higher-level phenomenon as exhausted by its parts in a way that loses the very thing that needed explaining. The other side dismisses any appeal to emergent or higher-level structure as a skyhook—a cause lowered in from above without being anchored in any mechanism—while insisting that the only acceptable explanatory devices are cranes, built up gradually from existing machinery. But the standards behind these charges are almost never stated, and I want the specification before I trust either charge.

Please write the desiderata that a theory of good versus greedy reduction—and of legitimate higher-level explanation versus skyhooks—actually has to meet. By a theory here I mean an account of when an explanation that moves downward to mechanisms is genuinely explanatory rather than misleadingly deflationary, and when an explanation that stays at a higher level or appeals to organization from above is doing work rather than merely naming a gap. Give me the requirements: the cases it must get right (such as temperature versus molecular motion, life versus chemistry, an institution versus the actions that sustain it), the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the empirical or conceptual results it cannot contradict, with the reason each is a requirement. For each requirement, say whether it is a genuine constraint on any adequate account or a preference of one camp in the debate. Then tell me which single requirement does the most work to exclude bad candidates, and whether any plausible subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, so that the list itself is a result.

The purpose I have in mind: I need a standard good enough to decide, in a live argument, whether a reduction of some human or social phenomenon to lower-level mechanisms is being resisted for legitimate reasons or merely because it is deflationary, and whether an appeal to something emergent or intentional is carrying explanatory weight or merely relabeling the very thing it was supposed to explain.

Finally, for the status you assign to each desideratum, tell me what would change your mind—what kind of case, argument, or finding would move a requirement from negotiable to non-negotiable or the reverse—and say where your reasoning about which requirements matter most is most likely to fail.
