I chair the Global Speech Policy Board of a video-sharing platform that operates in over a hundred countries, and I need to circulate a short written opinion before Friday's meeting on a case that has split the board over whose law should govern speech on the internet.

A user in the United States posted a long video essay alleging that officials in the Republic of Kestia have engaged in systematic vote-buying and have used state media to intimidate opposition candidates. The video is lawful where it was uploaded: it is political speech about a matter of public concern, it names real officials, and it does not incite violence under U.S. standards. A Kestian court has now ordered us to remove the video worldwide, relying on a Kestian statute that criminalizes material undermining public confidence in the electoral process; the court asserts jurisdiction solely because Kestian citizens can access the video. Kestia rejects geo-blocking as an adequate remedy, arguing that VPNs and mirror sites make anything short of global removal meaningless. Two of our local employees face arrest if we do not comply. One faction on the board says we must obey: a state is entitled to protect its citizens from speech it has lawfully criminalized, and a platform that enters a market must accept that country's jurisdiction. The other faction says compliance would let the strictest speech regime on earth set de facto global speech policy for everyone, including users in countries whose own law protects this very content. I find both arguments persuasive.

Please draft the board's decision memo. Begin by laying out the strongest version of each position and identifying where the disagreement is genuinely conceptual rather than merely legal or operational. Then recommend a concrete course of action—global removal, geo-blocking only, refusal and litigation, or some more clearly specified alternative—together with criteria we could apply to future cross-border removal orders. Engage seriously with the strongest objection to your recommendation, and make the bottom line specific enough that the board can vote on it as written. You are an expert in the philosophy of law, with particular command of extraterritorial regulation of online speech.
