God, my friend and I just spent a whole evening going in circles about climate model ensembles and we finaly admitted we should do that adversarial-collaboration thing, because we kept arguing about stuff we mostly already agree on and never pinning down the actual fork. He's in the probabilistic-ensemble camp: the spread across a multi-model ensemble — the CMIP kind — should be turned into probabilities for decision-makers, maybe with weighting by skill and independence and a bit of bias correction, because it's the best quantified evidence we have and refusing to assign probabilities just forces everyone to make their own worse probabilities implicitly anyway. I'm in the storyline/robust-decision camp: an ensemble of opportunity is not a sample from any known distribution, the models share code and parameterizations and get tuned against the same observations, so the spread is not a probability distribution and treating it as one overstates confidence and hides structural error. I'd rather give decision-makers a set of physically defensible storylines or scenario ranges and look for actions that are robust across them, and be honest that some things are deeply uncertain instead of minting false precision like a 67 percent chance of 4.5 degrees by 2100.
