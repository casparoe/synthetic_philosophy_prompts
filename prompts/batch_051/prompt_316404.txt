For my hermeneutics seminar I volunteered to put together a page on how to tell which commands in scripture are transcultural, binding on all believers in all times, and which are bound to the ancient Near East or the first-century Mediterranean world, but I keep seeing people gesture at criteria and then drop them the moment they don't like the result. I don't want a list of which commands fall where, and no bibliographies or citations since I can't look anything up anyway. What I want is a list of desiderata tight enough to actually use: before any theory that sorts cultural from transcultural commands gets to count as adequate, what does it have to be able to do? Not just cover the data in general, but the specific cases it has to get right, the distinctions it has to respect, and the results or limits it has to stay consistent with, plus the reason behind each item on the list.

The cases I have in mind: head coverings in 1 Cor 11 where Paul appeals to creation and nature and almost nobody treats the actual covering as binding today; slavery and the household codes where direct commands to obey get treated as culturally bound even by people who call themselves literalists; dietary laws and the way the NT revises them internally (Mark 7, Peter's vision in Acts 10, the Jerusalem council in Acts 15 where the apostles load a couple of OT-type restrictions onto gentile converts and not the whole law); the holy kiss and foot-washing, direct-looking commands that got reframed as local custom; usury and the sabbath where Christians in different eras come to opposite conclusions; and the passages about women keeping silent or not teaching. The thing I'm most worried about is reverse-engineering: a criterion that lands on those verdicts only because it was built to land on them isn't doing real work.

For each desideratum tell me whether it's non-negotiable or just a preference of one camp (the moral/ceremonial/civil split, the urge for the theory to output only modern-friendly moral conclusions, that sort of thing). And I'd really like an honest take on whether the whole set is jointly satisfiable. It seems like you can't have all three of: not reverse-engineered from current moral instincts, matching the consensus verdicts on slavery and head coverings while holding the line on sexual ethics and care for the poor, and one single criterion applied the same way in every case. If some subset is unsatisfiable, name it and say why, because thats the section I actually care about. Also say which one desideratum does the most excluding, the one that kills the worst candidates fastest. Don't waste space scoring existing theories against the list, I'll do that part myself.
