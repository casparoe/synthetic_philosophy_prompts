I work at a state utility comission and we keep running into the same puzzle. When we do cost-benefit analysis for, say, a new powerplant, we discount future costs and benefits at 3% a year, so a climate-related death avoided in 2100 counts for a small fraction of a death avoided next year. But when we set rules for nuclear waste or groundwater contamination, we talk as if harms to people centuries from now are every bit as serious as harms today, and we write standards that sure look like they don't discount those future lives at all. What view of our obligations to future people does each of these practices presuppose, and are the two practices even consistent with each other? I want the answer in prose, but then give me a table with columns: practice, implicit view of intergenerational justice it presupposes, rival view it rules out, whether the practice strictly requires that view or merely fits well with it, and what would have to change if the view were replaced. After the table, give a JSON summary with the same fields, as an array of objects under a "practices" key, one object each for the three or four practices you think matter most.
