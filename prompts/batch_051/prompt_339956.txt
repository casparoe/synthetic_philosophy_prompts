I sit on a regional end-of-life review board, and a friend on the board and I spent the evening arguing about whether our past decisions express a single underlying principle or just a collection of case-by-case judgments. Next month we must rule on a new application, and we disagree about what our own precedents require. Our four major decisions, as I remember them:

1. We approved a lethal prescription for a 71-year-old with metastatic pancreatic cancer, a prognosis of weeks, severe pain, full capacity, and a settled, repeated request.
2. We approved the withdrawal of ventilation from a 29-year-old with a complete high-cervical spinal cord injury — cognitively intact, not terminally ill — at his settled request, knowing he would die as a result.
3. We rejected a lethal prescription for a 47-year-old with treatment-resistant major depression and no terminal illness, despite two psychiatrists finding capacity, on the ground that the suffering was psychiatric and possibly still treatable.
4. We rejected a lethal prescription for a 53-year-old with advanced multiple sclerosis, bedbound and in severe neuropathic pain, not terminal, with capacity but a single request made shortly after moving to a care home; we asked for a trial of improved pain management first.

The new applicant is 61, with locked-in syndrome after a brainstem stroke: fully conscious, cognitively intact, communicating reliably by eye movement, and not terminal — he could live for years. He has chronic neuropathic pain and severe spasticity that have not responded to available treatment, and he describes his situation as unendurable. He has requested a lethal prescription steadily for seven months; two psychiatrists find full capacity and no treatable depression, and they attribute his distress to his neurological condition rather than to a psychiatric disorder.

What should we decide if we are to be consistent with our four prior rulings? Which precedent is the closest analog, and if the four genuinely underdetermine the answer, what principle would we be adopting with each choice, and what further case would cleanly separate the candidate rules?
