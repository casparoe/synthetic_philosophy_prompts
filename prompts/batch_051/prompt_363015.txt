I am a retired physicist. My career ran from spectroscopy to solid-state physics to computational materials science, and for most of it I built and tested models: Hamiltonians containing more adjustable parameters than the data could constrain, simulations that reproduced one set of measurements and then failed on the next, and effective descriptions valid at one length scale but unreliable at another. I am fluent in the vocabulary of model selection, regularization, overfitting, identifiability, out-of-sample prediction, and effective theories that hold only below a chosen cutoff.

I have recently been reading about scientific realism and the pessimistic meta-induction, and I keep meeting the debate in terms I do not naturally think in: a successful theory is approximately true, its central terms refer, and the history of science is full of theories that were successful and yet came to be regarded as false. I want to know how much of this debate survives when it is restated in the vocabulary I used for forty years.

Restate the three main positions as faithfully as you can: the realist who takes the predictive success of mature theories as evidence that they are approximately true; the pessimist who runs an induction over the history of successful but superseded theories; and the structuralist who tries to salvage only what persists across theory change. Give me the best-available translation of each into the language of model fitting and statistical inference, and lay out the mapping explicitly: where underdetermination of theory by data becomes non-identifiability of parameters, and where the no-miracles intuition becomes a claim about how improbable our long-run predictive record would be under an appropriate null hypothesis. I want a disciplined translation, not an analogy, and where a term has only an awkward counterpart, that should be made explicit.

Then give me the residue report. Which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all? In particular, does the phrase approximately true have a precise counterpart, such as a claim about parameter values or about convergence of a sequence of effective models, or is it doing work the modeling vocabulary refuses to do? And does any of the philosophical talk about reference have a counterpart in how I decided which degrees of freedom to treat as real?

My operating suspicion is that approximate truth is best expressed as a claim about the stability of certain structural features under successive refinements of an effective model, and that much of the disagreement concerns how far that stability licenses inference. Test that suspicion rather than merely confirming it. Then tell me whether the residue points to a defect in my vocabulary, to a philosophical debate that is less substantive than it sounds once restated, or to something my vocabulary is genuinely missing.
