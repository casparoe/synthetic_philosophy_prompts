I have been developing a revision of Abelard's claim that sin lies in consent rather than in the deed; I call it the Principle of Deferred Consent. The principle says that what makes an episode a sin is neither the outward act nor any consent that precedes or accompanies it, but the existence of some moment at which the agent ratifies the act as her own under the description that makes it wrong; if no such ratification ever occurs, there is no sin. Thus, if someone strikes another in a reflexive panic and later feels only satisfaction, the sin occurs at the later moment of ratification rather than at the moment of striking; but if someone consents in advance to a theft and then genuinely withdraws that consent before acting, she has not sinned at all. I recognize that this lets later choices settle whether an earlier episode was a sin. Present the three strongest objections to this principle and assess whether any of them is fatal rather than merely revisionary.
