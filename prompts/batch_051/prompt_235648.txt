After a long argument with a friend about a group of asexually reproducing plants, I found myself returning to a puzzle I could not quite name. My friend insisted that there must be a fact of the matter about how many species the group contains, even if we currently disagree about the evidence. I kept pointing out that the answer seems to depend on which criterion one adopts, and that no criterion is obviously privileged: an interbreeding criterion has no clear application to asexuals, a shared-history criterion divides the group one way, and morphology or ecological role cuts another way. The very same specimens fall under different groupings depending on the criterion, and this does not look like ordinary scientific uncertainty that more data would resolve. Is there an established name for this kind of situation, and for the view that there may be more than one legitimate way to sort the same organisms rather than a single underlying division waiting to be discovered? I am not satisfied with a bare label; I want to know exactly how the phenomenon should be characterized, how it differs from mere scientific disagreement or ignorance, and what it does and does not imply about the reality of species. If no single established term covers the whole thing, tell me the nearest candidates and how the phenomenon differs from each. Please answer in exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
