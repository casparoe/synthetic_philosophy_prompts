ok so i'm writing a short paper about when a theory with a lot going for it should override a firm intuition, and vice versa, and before i pre-circulate it i want someone to check whether my own view actually hangs together. i've got it down to six commitments:

1. the right method is reflective equilibrium. there's no fixed tribunal: theories and intuitions both get revised, and nothing is immune just because it feels obvious.

2. in physics, theory clearly beats intuition sometimes. general relativity and quantum mechanics violate extremely firm intuitions about simultaneity, locality and determinism, and the right response is that those intuitions give. but the reason isnt that the theories are beautiful: its that they have independent track records of novel prediction and unification. for me that's the whole ballgame—a theory can override an intuition when it has independent confirmation, not merely when its elegant.

3. in philosophy, by contrast, i still treat case intuitions as primary evidence. in ethics and decision theory, the felt verdicts about standard cases (trolley variants, Newcomb's problem, and so on) are the best data we have, and a theory that contradicts a broad stable pattern of firm intuitions is probably false, not just surprising.

4. but i don't think all intuitions are equal. i heavily discount intuitions that look driven by disgust, cultural training, or snap emotional reaction, and i trust intuitions that are stable under reflection, widely shared, and emotionally flat.

5. i'm also suspicious of appeals to expert intuition as a way of settling the hard cases. when a philosopher claims to have a direct intuition that such-and-such about a contested case, i usually think they're smuggling their preferred theory back in as data. expert consensus on cases feels like laundering conclusions into premises.

6. still, i'm not an intuition skeptic, and i dont think 2 and 3 are special pleading. the difference is just the independent-confirmation fact from 2: in philosophy, no theory has earned the right to bully intuitions because none has the predictive record physics does, so case intuitions remain the default source of evidence.

so: which of these are actually in tension with which, and which pairs only look like tension but dissolve once you spell them out? rank the real tensions by severity (not all equally bad), say for each what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most while keeping as much of the rest as possible. please keep the audit under 800 words.
