I have been working through the absent-minded driver problem and would like a document that could help two camps see exactly where they split. In the standard case, a driver knows he will pass two indistinguishable exits. Exiting at the first yields 0; exiting at the second yields 4; driving past both yields 1. From the planning stage, the best plan is to continue at the first exit and take the second, yielding 4. But when the driver is at an exit, he cannot tell whether it is the first or the second, so any decision rule available to him at that moment must treat both exits alike. If he continues with probability p at each indistinguishable exit, his expected payoff is p^2(1) + p(1-p)(4), maximized at p = 2/3, with a value of 4/3. The planning-optimal camp takes this to show that the driver should simply carry out the plan and get 4; the action-optimal camp takes it to show that the plan is not available at the moment of choice, and that self-locating ignorance forces the mixed strategy.

Write the joint statement a planning-optimalist and an action-optimalist could both sign at the end of a long adversarial collaboration, leaving only residual disagreements. Please use the following sections in this order:

1. **Agreed claims** (roughly 250 words): a numbered list of claims both sides can sign about the payoff structure, the information available at each stage, the strategy spaces, and the mathematical results. These should be neither vacuous nor slanted.

2. **Disagreements** (roughly 350 words): a table or numbered set of exact remaining disagreements, framed in neutral language, with a separate one-sentence statement each side accepts as a fair statement of its own view.

3. **Cruxes** (roughly 400 words): the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, give a concrete variant or case both sides would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. I suspect some cruxes will concern whether the driver can bind himself ex ante, whether “exit at the second exit” is an available action when the exits are indistinguishable, and whether rationality should be assessed by the quality of the plan or by the quality of the act at the decision node.

4. **What is merely verbal** (roughly 200 words): say which parts of the dispute, if any, turn on different uses of “available action,” “rational choice,” or related terms, and which parts are substantive.

Make the agreed section constructively specific rather than a list of truisms, and make the residual disagreement exact. Do not write a verdict siding with one camp.
