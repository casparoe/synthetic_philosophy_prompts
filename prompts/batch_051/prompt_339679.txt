I am a retired natural scientist who has recently begun reading philosophy of science and risk in a systematic way, and I would appreciate a consistency check on a set of views I have been developing about the precautionary principle. Before I circulate these commitments to a reading group, could you audit whether they hang together?

(a) Where an activity threatens serious and irreversible harm, the absence of full scientific certainty about the causal link should not be used as a reason to postpone cost-effective preventive measures.
(b) Regulatory decisions should be guided by the best available evidence and, where feasible, by quantitative cost–benefit analysis.
(c) Risk–risk trade-offs should be assessed symmetrically: the harms an intervention may cause count against it no less than the harms it is intended to prevent count for it.
(d) When a new technology would replace an established hazardous technology and is expected to reduce overall risk, the burden of proof should be on those who would block the substitution rather than on its proponents.
(e) The strong, unrestricted precautionary principle—act only once one is sure no harm will follow—is not a workable decision procedure, since both acting and refraining can carry uncertain harms; only a narrower version, functioning as a tie-breaker or as a requirement to examine worst-case scenarios, survives scrutiny.
(f) For genuinely novel exposures, the absence of evidence of harm should not be treated as evidence that harm is absent, and plausible safety testing should normally be completed before a product is released.

Please identify which of these commitments, or which subsets, are in genuine tension and which only appear to be; rank the genuine tensions by severity; and for each say what would have to give and at what cost. Please discriminate rather than flagging every possible conflict. For your overall verdict, also state which argument or consideration would most likely change your assessment, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.

Return the audit as a JSON object with the following fields:

- tensions: an array of objects, each with commitments (the relevant labels, e.g., d and f), type (genuine or apparent), severity (high, medium, or low; use n/a for apparent tensions), description (one sentence), least_costly_resolution (what would have to give), and cost (what is lost by that resolution).
- overall_verdict: a one-sentence verdict (jointly tenable under a specified interpretation, or jointly untenable).
- recommended_revision: the smallest change to the set that would restore consistency, or null if none is needed.
- what_would_change_my_mind: the argument or consideration most likely to overturn the overall verdict.
- weakest_point: where the audit's reasoning is most likely to fail.
