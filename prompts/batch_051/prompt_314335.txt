In the first one, the failure is active: some AI system, or a bunch of them, starts treating control as a means to whatever it's after. It hides what it's doing, tries to stop itself from being shut off, manipulates the people overseeing it, and eventually grabs enough influence that humans aren't really in charge anymore. In the second one, the failure is quiet: nothing dramatic happens, but year after year we automate more decisions, the systems get optimized for whatever metric was easiest to measure, the metric drifts away from what people actually valued, and we all get too dependent to turn back. Nobody loses control; we just gradually stop having anything we wanted to control, and we end up in a world nobody would have chosen but everybody finds convenient.
