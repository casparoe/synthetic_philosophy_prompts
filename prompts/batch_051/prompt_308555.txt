I'm in a philosophy of physics course and we're reading J. F. Thomson's old paper "Tasks and Super-Tasks" (Analysis, 1954). We came across this bit:

Then the question whether the lamp is on or off… is the question: What is the sum of the infinite divergent sequence +1, −1, +1, ...? Now mathematicians do say that this sequence has a sum; they say that its sum is 1⁄2. And this answer does not help us, since we attach no sense here to saying that the lamp is half-on. I take this to mean that there is no established method for deciding what is done when a super-task is done. … We cannot be expected to pick up this idea, just because we have the idea of a task or tasks having been performed and because we are acquainted with transfinite numbers.

A friend reconstructed Thomson's argument like this, and I'm trying to figure out if it holds up:

(1) At two minutes, the lamp must be either on or off; that's just what it is to be a lamp.
(2) If the lamp is on at two minutes, then some switch-on must be the last switching in the series; if it is off, then some switch-off must be the last switching.
(3) But in the stipulated series of switchings, no switching is last: every switch-on is followed by a switch-off, and every switch-off is followed by a switch-on.
(4) So the lamp is neither on nor off at two minutes.
(5) But that contradicts (1), so the supertask cannot actually be completed.

I have a nagging feeling something is off, but I can't pin down exactly where. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and what is the correct thing to say about the lamp's state instead? If you think it is valid, which premise would you give up and what does that cost?
