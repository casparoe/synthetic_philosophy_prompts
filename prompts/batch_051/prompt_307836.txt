Been having a bit of a crisis about blame lately so sorry if this is rambly. My brother did something genuinely awful a few weeks ago and I keep swinging between being furious at him and feeling like maybe nobody is ever really at fault for anything, which is messing with my head more than I expected. I read about Galen Strawson's Basic Argument and as far as I can tell it goes: to be truly responsible for what you do, you'd have to be truly responsible for the kind of person you are. To be truly responsible for that, you'd have to be responsible for whatever made you that kind of person, and so on back and back until you hit stuff you never chose—genes, upbringing, whatever. So nobody is ultimately responsible for anything. That part I follow.

Here's what bugs me. Doesn't that whole infinite regress only get going because each earlier step determined the later one? Like it's really just a determinism argument wearing different clothes. If the world is genuinely indeterministic, and some of my choices aren't determined by anything prior, then those choices are breaks in the chain—little fresh starts where I'm not just a product of what came before. So over a lifetime of enough undetermined self-shaping choices, couldn't I gradually become responsible for the person I've made myself into, in exactly the way Strawson says is impossible? Me and my brother both made dozens of those choices, so why would the argument still apply to us? It seems like the Basic Argument only works if you already assume determinism is true, which would mean it's not really the knockdown argument it's suppossed to be.
