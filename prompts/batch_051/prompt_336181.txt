im in a metaphilosophy course right now and we're doing the unit on whether philosophy makes progress, and I keep running into the same line everywhere that philosophy never solves anything and just recycles the same old debates, so I want my term paper to defend the other side. my plan is to collect the strongest arguments that philosophy does make progress, even if it's not experiment-and-answer progress. so far I have five. (1) logic: the move from aristotle's syllogistic through frege to model theory and modal logic is as cumulative as anything in mathematics. (2) philosophy builds up conceptual machinery that sticks: distinctions like a priori/a posteriori, use/mention, type/token, de re/de dicto, supervenience, and so on just become part of the toolkit and nobody has to reinvent them. (3) the spinoff argument: philosophy keeps giving rise to fields that then progress on their own (physics, psychology, and more recently parts of cognitive science), so the philosophical activity isn't stalled so much as generative. (4) there's a graveyard: a bunch of once-live positions are now only really studied historically because they collapsed under objections, so at least some views do get eliminated. (5) even where we haven't answered a question, we've made the question better — old formulations now look flat-footed, and you can't even enter debates like free will or personal identity without engaging a huge preexisting map of positions and distinctions.

I want a sixth argument that is genuinely different from those five, not a restatement, special case, or combination of them. if you can't find one, say so and give me the best attempt anyway, and then tell me honestly whether it's actually any good or whether a sharp grader would see through it immediately.
