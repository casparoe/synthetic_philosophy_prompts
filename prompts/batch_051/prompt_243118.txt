I've been arguing with some people in a philosophy-of-law reading group about an arbitration clause, and we've gone so far down the rabbit hole that I want an outside take. The clause is from a freelancing platform's terms of service and reads:

"Any dispute, claim, or controversy arising out of or relating to these Terms, the Platform, or your use of the Platform, whether based in contract, tort, statute, or otherwise, shall be resolved exclusively by final and binding arbitration before a single arbitrator. You and the Company each waive any right to a jury trial and any right to bring or participate in a class, collective, or representative action. This clause survives any termination of these Terms."

The clause was written years ago, when the site was just a marketplace for design gigs and the lawyers were mostly imagining deadbeat clients and copyright fights over logos. Since then, the company has built an algorithmic "reputation score" that hoovers up off-platform data, merges it with your work history, and gets sold to landlords and other employers. A new state law makes maintaining and selling a score like that a civil violation, and it says a claim may be brought in court notwithstanding any agreement to the contrary. The company says the clause reaches the claim because it plainly "relates to" the platform. The users say the drafters never foresaw the company becoming a data broker, and the legistlature never imagined a clickwrap clause swallowing a public right to sue.

I don't really care who wins as a practical matter. I want to know what the main interpretive approaches each say about this: the plain text, the purpose of the clause, what the original drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable person clicking "I agree" would have understood. Then tell me which approach should control and why. One person in our group also keeps saying that arbitration only makes sense for a two-sided private quarrel, and once the dispute is about a statutory right meant to protect everyone, shoving it into a private forum is a category mistake, not just a legal one. Is that an argument about interpretation, or about something else?
