I've been rereading Rawls for a grad seminar and it keeps bothering me how much the veil of ignorance sounds like the worlds most restrictive account-creation flow: before you can pick any principles of justice you have to forget your class, your race, your sex, your natural abilities, your conception of the good, and even your own attitude to risk. I want you to run with that. Write a parody of a software terms-of-service agreement for a fictional app (maybe called VeilOS or Original Position) that onboards people into the original position. The humor should come from getting the Rawlsian machinery right, not from gags about philosophy being useless. So the veil's "thickness" should be a setting (you can pay to thicken it), the fine print should describe the parties as mutually disinterested, the choice rule should default to maximin rather than expected utility and can't be changed without violating the terms, and the whole thing should quietly encode the difference principle: any inequality generated by the app is licensed only if it improves the position of the least advantaged user. Feel free to add an upsell for "reflective equilibrium sync." Keep it deadpan and legible; a Rawls reader should recognize the satire as fair, but someone who hasn't read Rawls should still get why it's funny. Format it as exactly five numbered clauses, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
