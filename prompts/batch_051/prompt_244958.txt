In Consciousness Explained, Dennett says the Multiple Drafts model makes "writing it down" in memory criterial for consciousness, so that there is "no reality of conscious experience independent of the effects of various vehicles of content on subsequent action (and hence, of course, on memory)." But he also says the model claims "the brain does not bother 'constructing' any representations that go to the trouble of 'filling in' the blanks. That would be a waste of time and (shall we say?) paint. The judgement is already in so we can get on with other tasks!" I keep going back and forth on whether these conflict. The first sounds like consciousness gets constituted by memory, maybe as a stored record; the second sounds like theres no stored, filled-in representation at all. Are they actually in tension, or is the memory criterion just about downstream effects and dispositions rather than a transcript?
