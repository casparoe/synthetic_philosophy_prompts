I'm on the board of a small township library in a state whose constitution has this clause: "No moneys of the township shall ever be granted to any church or religious association for the promotion of religious worship." Our library pays for a yearly subscription to a meditation app, Meditare, that any cardholder can use free. The app is owned by a Zen monastery, and the monastery openly says it uses the subscription revenue to maintain it's meditation hall and pay its teachers. The app has both secular mindfulness courses and explicitly religious Zen liturgies; the subscription gives access to both. The clause was adopted in 1875, and the drafters were clearly thinking of cash grants to a congregation's building fund, not a public body paying an app-store subscription that indirectly funds religious worship. So does our annual payment violate the clause? I'd like you to work through what the words say, what the purpose of the clause says, what the drafters would have said if they'd been asked, and what a reasonable reader today takes the clause to mean. Then say which of those approaches should win here and why. Please write it in continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables.
