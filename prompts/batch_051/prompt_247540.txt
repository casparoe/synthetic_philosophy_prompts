I am the head of trust and safety policy at a social media platform that removes on the order of two thousand accounts a week, and I need a reusable decision procedure for one recurring decision: when is permanent deplatforming of an individual account justified, as opposed to a warning, a time-limited suspension, demonetization, reach reduction, or no action at all. The reviewers are not philosophers and have about thirty minutes per case. Every decision must produce a written justification we can publish if challenged, and a quarterly audit compares outcomes across cases, so the procedure must force stated reasons rather than gut reactions and must make relevantly similar cases come out similarly even when different reviewers handle them.

Write the procedure from these materials, which I want you to take seriously and make operational. First, there is a difference between backward-looking and forward-looking justifications for a sanction: responding to what the person did and how culpable they were, versus acting in order to deter others, incapacitate the person, or communicate a norm. Second, on the expressive view, punishment is not merely a technical device for harm prevention; it publicly condemns the person as a wrongdoer, and that differs from quarantining a dangerous object. Third, proportionality must be anchored to the offense and the culpability, not to the account's follower count, the size of the news cycle, or the intensity of a boycott. Fourth, because the platform is rule-maker, enforcer, judge, and court of appeal in one, the procedure has to supply the discipline that a separation of powers would otherwise provide — above all, no ex post facto rules, and no status-based banishment without defined reentry conditions.

Give me the procedure as a numbered sequence of questions to work through in order. For each question, state what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure stops and directs a lesser sanction or no action, and whether that step is fact-finding or an irreducibly evaluative judgment. Then apply the procedure to three cases, showing the reasoning at each step: (1) a public health agency account posting false statements about a vaccine during an outbreak; (2) an influencer with a long record of near-miss content — dog whistles, proxy harassment, deniable threats — against whom a large campaign is demanding a permanent ban even though no single post clearly violates an existing rule; and (3) a journalist who posts a private citizen's home address, draws harassment to it, then removes the post and apologizes. I expect the procedure, if it is honest, to give me an answer I will not like on at least one of these cases; do not soften it. Finish by stating plainly what the procedure cannot do — which decisions cannot be reduced to a checklist and must remain an institutional or value choice.
