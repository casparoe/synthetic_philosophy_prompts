You are a professional philosopher specializing in the ontology of art, and I am trying to sort out the type/token picture of music: the idea that a musical work is an abstract type whose tokens are performances. For that picture to be more than a slogan, I need a working definition of "a performance of a musical work." Please start by saying what job the definition is meant to do, then propose it and run it against the following cases. I take these to be clear positives: an orchestra playing a Beethoven symphony in a concert hall; a singer performing a song from memory at a wedding. This is a clear negative: someone silently reading a score on the subway and hearing the piece in their head. I am genuinely unsure about these five: a DJ playing a finished track from a laptop in a club; a player piano rendering a roll cut from a recording of a now-dead pianist's interpretation; a studio recording assembled from two hundred takes that no one has ever played end to end; a computer synthesizing a fully notated electronic work directly from a score file; a jazz trio improvising on the chord changes of a standard. State the definition, run it through all eight cases, flag where it gives an unexpected verdict or no verdict, and tell me whether "performance of a work" is binary or comes in degrees. Finally, say what your definition implies about whether a recording can itself count as a token of the work.
