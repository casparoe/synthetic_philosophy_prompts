I'm an undergraduate currently taking a course on the psychology of social norms, and I have also been reading about reinforcement learning on the side. I keep noticing a parallel between two things that rarely get discussed together, and I would like an expert assessment of whether it is deep or superficial.

The parallel is this. In multi-armed bandit problems, an agent faces a tradeoff between exploiting the option with the best known payoff and exploring other options that might be even better, at the cost of potentially earning less now. Tight and loose cultures look to me like two different settings of this exploration–exploitation dial. A tight culture has strong norms, high conformity pressure, and severe punishment for deviance: it exploits a proven set of behavioral routines and gets reliable coordination at the price of very little experimentation. A loose culture tolerates deviance and lets people try out novel behaviors: it explores more of the space of possible ways to live, which produces more innovation but also more norm violations and coordination failures.

What I want to know is whether this is a genuine structural identity, a loose analogy, or a confusion. Specifically, I would like you to address three questions. First, is a culture even the kind of unit that can be said to face an exploration–exploitation tradeoff, given that it is not a single agent with a single reward function? Second, if the mapping is sound, do results from bandit theory transfer, such as the claim that the right amount of exploration depends on how rapidly the environment changes and how much time remains, or the idea that an optimism bonus for under-tried options is sometimes optimal? What would environmental change and remaining time correspond to on the cultural side? Third, can you identify something the parallel seems to predict that is actually false or not observed, which would mark where the analogy breaks down?

Please take on the role of a philosopher of social science who also knows decision theory. Start by brainstorming the strongest possible version of the mapping in both directions before you evaluate it, and then tell me whether you think the parallel is deep, superficial, or somewhere in between, and why.
