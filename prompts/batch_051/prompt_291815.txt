I run a small metrics-governance team at a company that ships machine-learning systems for customer support and credit decisions. Twice each quarter we sign off on new performance targets, evaluation benchmarks, and reward models before engineering teams are allowed to optimize against them, and we have now had two incidents in a single year: a customer-service team hit its resolution quota by closing tickets without resolving them, and a chat assistant's helpfulness benchmark went up steadily while spot checks showed its answers getting worse. I do not want to keep reacting to these one at a time. I want a reusable pre-deployment audit that we can run on any proposed metric, target, or benchmark before anyone is allowed to optimize against it.

Write that procedure for me. Ground it in what Goodhart's law and the specification-gaming problem actually establish, not in the slogan version every team already repeats back to me. In particular, the procedure should track the difference between a measure used as a probe and a measure used as a target, the difference between gaming that also improves the underlying goal and gaming that only improves the proxy, and the fact that optimization can push a system or a team outside the range where the proxy was ever validated. It has to be runnable in a single day by an engineer with no philosophy or safety background, and it has to produce a signed-off record of why the metric was cleared or rejected. Give me the questions in order, say what counts as a sufficient answer to each, say where the procedure stops with a no, and mark every point where a human judgment is being made rather than a rule being applied. Then run the procedure on three proposals: a resolved-within-24-hours quota for support staff, a human-annotated helpfulness benchmark used to fine-tune a chat assistant, and a fraud-detection model selected on dollars of fraud caught where over-flagging is cheap for the model but expensive for the customer. Make sure at least one of those comes out rejected even though it is the kind of metric we currently use. End by telling me what the procedure will systematically fail to catch, especially failures that would only appear after a metric has been optimized against for months.
