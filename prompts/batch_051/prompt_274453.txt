A province rolls out a monetary experiment, and I want you to run the mechanism rather than give me a referendum on it. Every month the central bank creates new base money equal to about 0.3% of GDP and credits it, in equal chunks, to every adult's account at the central bank. Heres the catch: the credits are spend-it-or-lose-it. Unspent money is deleted after 60 days, and the credits can only be spent on current goods and services—no saving, no assets (houses, stocks, bonds, crypto, jewelry above a price cap), no paying down existing debt. Walk me through how this actually plays out. What does it do to grocery prices versus house prices? Who gets better off and who gets worse off? How do people, landlords, employers, and local governments game the restrictions—which loophole gets exploited first? Holding one of these credits, is a resident holding wealth or just liquidity? And at the aggregate level, does this kind of printing create any real wealth, or does it mostly churn and redistribute claims on existing wealth? Keep the answer under 500 words and in American English.
