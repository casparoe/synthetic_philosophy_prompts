Last night I had a long argument with a friend about whether Bertrand’s paradox is a narrow problem about defining “random chord” or a deeper problem for the principle of indifference. She held that once you specify a real physical procedure for selecting the chord, the paradox dissolves; I countered that the principle of indifference is supposed to earn its keep exactly when no sampling procedure has been specified, so treating it as an instruction to always specify a procedure is close to giving it up.

In Chapter IV of A Treatise on Probability, Keynes argues that the principle of indifference should be applied only to alternatives that are, relative to one’s evidence, indivisible. If the alternatives can be further divided, he says, applying the principle to different partitions can produce incompatible equal probabilities; his illustration is the color of an unknown book, where red/not-red, blue/not-blue, and so on would each seem to license a probability of one-half for the first alternative. The text concedes that the principle is not self-applicable, but it also does not fully solve the problem.

Here is the list I want you to rank:

1. The naive principle: when no evidence distinguishes them, assign equal probability to every alternative.
2. Keynes’s indivisibility restriction: apply the principle only to alternatives that are ultimate or indivisible relative to the evidence.
3. A transformation-invariance restriction: require the resulting probabilities to be invariant, or to transform consistently, under relevant reparameterizations.
4. A maximum-entropy rule: among all distributions that encode exactly one’s stated constraints, choose the one with maximal entropy.
5. The deflationary view: treat indifference only as a convention for fixing a privileged partition, with no claim to track objective ignorance.

Please sort these into tiers by how well they cope with the Bertrand cases and with the book-color type of contradiction, while still licensing priors where ignorance appears to demand them. For each tier, give a sentence or two of reasoning. Then say what would change your mind about at least the top and bottom tiers: which argument or example would most plausibly move the top item down, and which would move the bottom item up.
