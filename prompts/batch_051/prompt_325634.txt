I've been trying to get my views straight about advance directives because my dad had a stroke and we had to use a document he wrote years ago. I wrote down where I think I stand, but I suspect some of it doesn't hang together. Here's the list:

1. A competent adult's advance directive should be honored even when the person later lacks capacity and now says something different.
2. The authority of the directive comes from the fact that the earlier person and the later person are the same person.
3. What makes someone the same person over time is psychological continuity—overlapping memories, beliefs, plans, and so on.
4. A person can make binding decisions about their future body, so refusing a feeding tube in advance is like making a contract or a will.
5. If the later person seems content and wants to eat, that by itself doesn't make the earlier refusal wrong; current preferences aren't automatically more authoritative just because they're current.
6. But I don't think the earlier self should be a dictator either. If the later self has a genuinely different set of values or worldview, then it might be better to think of them as two persons, and the earlier one's preferences shouldn't automatically win.

Could you go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly? For each pair in tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest fix would be, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
