I'm writing a feature about how a superintelligent AI should talk to humans if its mere endorsement is enough to move behavior. Build me a case to order.

Two communication policies that sound nearly identical in ordinary cases should come apart in yours. Policy A (full transparency): the AI always states its best estimate plainly, with calibrated confidence and the strongest supporting evidence, never lying and never omitting relevant facts. Policy B (influence-minimizing): the AI obeys the same honesty constraint but treats the bare fact that it has spoken as itself a form of influence, and so may withhold a bottom-line verdict or stay silent while continuing to offer evidence and considerations.

Specifications:
- A mundane, high-stakes personal or civic choice (medical, financial, legal, or electoral) in which Policy A predictably erodes the human's autonomous decision even though the AI never deceives;
- Policy B, forbidden from lying or fabricating evidence, preserves that autonomy despite being less forthcoming;
- no perfect predictor and no exotic technology;
- stakes a normal person could plausibly face within a decade.

After the scenario, do the check: state what the AI knows, what each policy would have it say, and why autonomy survives under B but not A. Then identify the structural feature doing the work, and say what happens if that feature is removed. End with the strongest objection a defender of Policy A would mount, and whether it succeeds.
