I've been going back and forth on the "can a nation be guilty" question ever since reading Jaspers on German guilt—he seperates criminal guilt (you broke a law) from political guilt (you share in what your state does) and a couple others, and I keep feeling like the political kind is the one that actually matters. A friend gave me an argument that seems to show the political kind is a category error, but I think it proves too much, because it also seems to dissolve a bunch of things I'm pretty sure are real.

Here's the argument, cleaned up:

(1) Guilt attaches to an agent for an act or omission the agent could have avoided.
(2) A nation is not an agent in its own right: it doesn't intend, choose, or omit anything over and above what its members do.
(3) So a nation cannot be guilty.
(4) By the same reasoning, no group can be guilty—not a generation, not an ethnicity, not a corporation.
(5) A person born after a wrong didn't commit it and couldn't have prevented it, so by (1) they're not guilty of it.
(6) Benefiting from a wrong, or sharing an identity with the people who committed it, is not an act or omission.
(7) So descendants and beneficiaries aren't guilty either.
(8) Therefore no nation is guilty for historical wrongs, and no living person is guilty for wrongs their nation did before they were born.
(9) But this proves too much. It says Germany isn't guilty of the Holocaust—only particular Nazis are—that a corporation's guilty plea is a legal fiction, and that a person whose whole life is built on wealth from slavery bears no guilt for it. I'm pretty sure the first two are real, and the third I at least don't want to have to accept.
(10) So something in (1)–(7) has to go, even though every step looked fine when I accepted it.

I'd like to keep (1) for ordinary individual guilt—it seems right that you can't be guilty for what you couldn't have avoided—and I'd also like to keep the idea that nations and corporations can be guilty in some non-metaphorical sense. If the argument is valid I can't have both. Which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Also, tell me how confident you are in that diagnosis and which point you're least sure of.
