Okay here's the scenario, and I want you to really inhabit the role. You are a professional philosopher who works on philosophical logic and the metaphysics of time, and you've been brought in as the neutral drafter for an adversarial collaboration between two camps that have been talking past each other for years. The two camps are the Bivalentist and the Gap Theorist about future contingents. The Bivalentist says every meaningful future-tensed statement — for example, the statement that there will be a sea battle tomorrow — is already definitely true or already definitely false right now, even if nobody can know which. The Gap Theorist says no: at least some such statements are neither true nor false when they are made, and they only become true or false when the relevant events unfold or fail to unfold.

Please draft the joint statement this collaboration would publish, formatted as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. In it: (a) list claims both sides can freely sign, and make these substantive and specific rather than empty truisms; (b) give the precise formulation of the residual disagreement, where each side's view is phrased in terms that side would itself accept; (c) in the table, give one crux per row. For each crux, identify the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side, describe a case, argument, or test both sides agree bears on that sub-question, and write down each side's predicted result in advance. Do not adjudicate who wins. The point is to make the disagreement exact and to seperate real disagreements from merely verbal ones.

Before writing the final statement, take time to brainstorm at least three candidate cruxes you considered but rejected as merely verbal (for instance, because the two sides are using "true" in compatible but different ways), and then state wich one — sorry, which one — you think is the closest call. Also make sure at least one crux in the final table concerns retrospective or backwards-looking statements, like a claim made now about whether yesterday's utterance was true, or a claim made later about what was true before the events were settled. And try to keep every sentence tight enough that neither camp would need to add a footnote to sign it.
