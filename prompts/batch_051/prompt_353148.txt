A friend and I spent an hour arguing about whether a human language could do without pronouns and other indexicals, and I left convinced that it could. Since I want to know whether I won on the merits or just wore my friend down, I reconstructed my side as the following five-step argument. Each step still looks plausible to me, but I have a nagging feeling that one of them hides a false assumption.

(1) In a particular utterance, an indexical does nothing more than single out a feature of the context: "I" singles out the speaker, "you" the addressee, "here" the place, and "now" the time.
(2) For communication, only which person, place, or time gets singled out matters; how it gets singled out makes no difference to what is communicated.
(3) Consequently, a proper name, unique description, set of coordinates, or date that singles out the same person, place, or time can replace the corresponding indexical without changing what is communicated.
(4) If each indexical occurrence can be replaced in that way, then a language that made such replacements systematically would communicate as well as one with indexicals.
(5) Therefore, at least in principle, a human language could lack pronouns and indexicals.
