I sit on my jurisdiction's Em Labour Standards Board. An employer holds a brain emulation (an em) of a software engineer. The original signed an employment contract fixing a 40-hour subjective working week with overtime pay; the biological original has since died. The employer now proposes to run a copy of the em at ten times normal speed for three wall-clock days—roughly one month of subjective life and work—then deactivate that copy and restore the original em from the backup taken before the accelerated run. The restored em will have no memory of the month. The employer argues that no one has been made to work unpaid overtime: the only subject who experienced the accelerated work was a temporary copy created for the purpose and then deactivated, and the restored em is the very party who agreed to the contract. Half the Board reads the proposal as unpaid overtime and the wrongful termination of a conscious worker; the other half regards it as a routine use of backup and restoration. Draft the Board's decision memo: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with reasons the Board could act on, and engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side.
