A friend and I spent most of last evening arguing about whether the word “cup” in a model trained on nothing but text means anything by the model’s own lights, and he eventually pushed Harnad’s paper at me. The passage that stuck is this one, from Stevan Harnad’s 1990 paper “The Symbol Grounding Problem”:

Symbolic representations must be grounded bottom-up in nonsymbolic representations of two kinds: (1) "iconic representations" , which are analogs of the proximal sensory projections of distal objects and events, and (2) "categorical representations" , which are learned and innate feature-detectors that pick out the invariant features of object and event categories from their sensory projections. Elementary symbols are the names of these object and event categories, assigned on the basis of their (nonsymbolic) categorical representations.

From that passage I extracted three criteria for calling a token a genuinely grounded symbol: (i) beneath it there must be nonsymbolic representations that are analogs of sensory projections; (ii) a learned or innate feature-detector level must pick out the invariant features of the object or event category from those projections; and (iii) the symbol must be attached to the category because of that projection-to-category link, not merely because it sits in a network of other symbols or human glosses.

Now consider the case I cannot decide. Imagine a stationary robot with a single camera and no actuators at all. During training it receives raw video frames paired with English captions written by people who were looking at the same scenes the camera recorded. The system learns its own visual categories from the frame stream and also learns, purely from the captions, which words go with which categories. After training it can label novel objects and compose new sentences that track what is in front of the camera, but it cannot act, point, correct a mistaken label, or otherwise bring its words into contact with the world on its own initiative. Does the token “cup” in this system qualify as grounded according to my three criteria? Go criterion by criterion, say exactly where they underdetermine the verdict, and then tell me whether the hard case shows that one of the criteria — perhaps the “attached to the category because of the projection-to-category link” condition — needs to be restated.
