Here is my opening move. Before a measurement, your theory says there is no fact about which outcome "I" will see: the process produces all of them, and there is a post-measurement successor of me in each branch, each with an equal claim to being me. So ordinary probability — uncertainty about which single outcome will happen — seems to have no subject matter. Yet you tell me to assign squared-amplitude weights to branches and to let them guide my choices. But if I know in advance that a successor of me exists in every branch, why should I care how much squared amplitude my branch has? The only thing that varies across branches is how much "measure" I get. Is your view that I should care about that measure, and if so, why should self-interest track measure rather than, say, the number of distinct successors, which is the same in every branch?
