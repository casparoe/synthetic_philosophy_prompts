I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I've been trying to work out what I actually think about the idea that history has a direction — that, on the whole, it bends toward justice. When I try to treat this the way I'd treat a noisy time series, I notice I'm not applying the trend hypothesis consistently: some events count as signal and structurally similar events as noise. To get clearer, I wrote down my verdicts on some cases, and I want to know what principle, if any, captures them.

- The nineteenth-century abolition of chattel slavery across the Atlantic: I count this as strong evidence that history bends toward justice.
- The Holocaust and other twentieth-century genocides: I find these horrifying, but they don't actually lower my confidence much; I treat them as noise rather than as evidence against a directional trend.
- The fall of the Berlin Wall and the subsequent spread of electoral democracy: I count this as real evidence for a just direction.
- The democratic backsliding in several countries over the past couple of decades: I'm inclined to treat this as short-run fluctuation, not as evidence that the arc bends backward — even though it looks structurally like the Berlin Wall case with the sign flipped.
- The long-run declines in extreme poverty and in rates of violent death: I treat these as strong evidence for an improving direction, even though they look like material improvements rather than moral ones.
- A centuries-long period in which a brutal practice like serfdom persists as stable background: I can't decide whether this is evidence against the direction thesis or just evidence that my time window is too short.

Is there one principle about when a historical development should count as evidence for or against the claim that history bends toward justice — a principle that explains when I'm entitled to treat an event as signal rather than noise — that captures most of these verdicts? Please state it precisely enough that I could apply it consistently to new cases, say which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate, and tell me what it predicts about a future case I haven't considered: a century in which material welfare rises sharply while civil and political freedoms collapse.
