ok so ive been mulling over idolatry for a while (we did it in religious studies but it keeps showing up outside religion too, like the way people talk about 'idolising' a celebrity or a relationship). i have a homemade distinction i want someone to actually attack rather than be encouraging about.

I call it 'load-bearing value'. The idea is that the things you care about fall into two roles. Some things are load-bearing: you rest your sense that your life is heading somewhere on them, you let them set the test for whether a day or a year was wasted, you let them tell you who you are. Other things are carried: you genuinely care about them, maybe a lot, but they sit inside a life that is already being held up by something else — if they fail, it hurts but your sense of self doesn't cave in. My theory is that idolatry (broadly, not statues and altars) is exactly this: taking something that can only be carried and making it load-bearing. So the wrong in 'valuing something the wrong way' is not about the object being bad, and not about caring too much exactly, but about a kind of category mistake about what you're leaning on. A partner, a grade, a football team, a political cause, even a future version of yourself can be fine to love; the question is whether it's holding you up or you're holding it.

Then it got uncomfortable, because the same sentence can be said by two people — 'if I lost this band, I'd be destroyed' — and one seems like a genuine vocation and the other like an idol. So maybe my test is just a disguised way of saying 'I don't approve of how much you care'. Also maybe some things *should* be load-bearing (a child? justice?) and then the theory collapses into a boring list of approved objects and stops doing any work. And part of me suspects I've just reinvented something theologians have said more carefully.

So: is this a real distinction or just a metaphor with a nice name? Where does it break — can you give me a clear case where making something load-bearing is fine, or where caring about something in the carried way is still idolatrous? And honestly, what is the strongest version of my idea, and what is the exact point where even that version fails? Please keep seperate which parts of this are settled in the philosophy and theology of idolatry and which are genuinely contested, rather than making a contested view sound like the obvious consensus. And write the answer in British English.
