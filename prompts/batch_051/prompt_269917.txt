Take the sorites paradox, but make the vague predicate 'is a lowercase a' instead of 'is a heap'. You start from an unambiguously legible double-storey 'a' and morph it one Bézier point at a time toward a lowercase 'o', keeping every intermediate form readable until, somewhere along the line, people stop calling it an 'a'. Then run the same kind of interpolation from the original 'a' toward a Greek alpha that is visually indistinguishable from a single-storey 'a' but is a different letter. Do the standard treatments—epistemicism's sharp cutoff, supervaluationism's truth-value gaps, contextualism's shifting threshold—carry over unchanged when the category is a learned, functional one whose whole point is being read as 'a', rather than a merely observational one like 'heap'? Does any of them stop applying, or just relocate the boundary? And does the alpha endpoint show that the boundary can't live purely in the shapes, and if so where does each view put it instead?
