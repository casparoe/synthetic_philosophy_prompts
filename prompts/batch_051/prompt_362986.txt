I'm a retired microbiologist, and after a couple years in a philosophy reading group I've finally written down what I actually think about the ethics of bringing new people into existence. Next meeting we're each reading our position statement out, and I'd like a consistency check before I read mine allowed. Here's the package:

(1) I'm convinced by something like Benatar's asymmetry between pain and pleasure: the absence of pain is good even when there is no person for whom it is good, but the absence of pleasure is not bad unless there is some existing person who is deprived of it.

(2) From that I've come to think coming into existence is always a harm to the person who comes into existence, since it exposes them to possible pain but never gives them a benefit they would otherwise have missed out on.

(3) I also think death is bad for a person, normally because it deprives them of future goods.

(4) I hold a person-affecting constraint on outcomes: an outcome can be better or worse than another only if it is better or worse for some particular person.

(5) Case verdict: if a couple can either not conceive or conceive a child who will have a happy life, neither outcome is better; but if the child would live in severe pain, not conceiving is better than conceiving.

(6) Once someone exists, their life can still be genuinely worth living overall. I'm not saying everyone alive would be better off dead.

Do these hang together? I keep going back and forth about which moves are doing the real work, so I want an audit of the set rather than a lecture on each position. For your reply use these sections, in this order: The real tensions, ranked (about 350 words; for each say which commitment has to give and what we lose by dropping it), Apparent tensions that dissolve (about 200 words), and The cheapest single revision (about 150 words).
