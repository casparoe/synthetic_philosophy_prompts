Me and a friend went in circles last night over whether evolution can be reconciled with the idea that creation was originally good and pain-free for animals. He kept pressing two texts. Genesis 1:30 (KJV) says, "And to every beast of the earth, and to every fowl of the air, and to every thing that creepeth upon the earth, wherein there is life, I have given every green herb for meat: and it was so." Whereas Psalm 104:21 says, "The young lions roar after their prey, and seek their meat from God." I cant shake the thought that the first verse commits the text to animals being plant-eaters before the Fall, while the second is perfectly at ease with predation as something God supplies. If the fossil record has predation stretching back before any human sin existed, the usual "the Fall changed the rules" move seems to leave no fall there to change them. Are these two passages actually in tension, and can they be reconciled without special pleading (and without just hiding behind "meat" being old English for food)? Lay out the candidate readings, what each one costs, and which is the honest reading rather than special pleading.
