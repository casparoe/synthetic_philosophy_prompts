In a seminar on comparative moral philosophy, we have been reconstructing karma as a theory of moral desert, and I find myself both attracted to the theory and unable to accept one of its implications. The theory, as I have come to understand it, has genuine systematic virtues. It offers a single principle that makes the distribution of happiness and suffering morally intelligible: no pleasure or pain is brute luck, because each is the ripening of some earlier deed performed by the same agent. It thereby dissolves the problem of moral luck, supplies a uniform standard for assessing actions by the kind of fruit they tend to produce, and promises that over the long run the moral ledger balances. These are real theoretical advantages, and I do not want to surrender them casually.

Yet I also have a firm intuition that I cannot set aside. When I imagine a person suffering now because of a wrong committed in a past life—a life of which they have no memory and with which they feel no psychological continuity—I cannot bring myself to say that this person deserves the suffering in the sense of desert that matters to me. It seems to me that the theory may be quietly trading on an equivocation between desert and mere causation, or treating the bare persistence of some underlying continuant as sufficient for the later person to be the accountable party. I am not asking for a simple verdict for one side against the other. I want an assessment of what each possible resolution would cost: what would have to be true for the intuition to be debunked as a parochial fixation on memory and present selfhood, what would have to be true for the theory to be amended or abandoned, and whether there is a defensible compatibilist reading—for example, one that locates the subject of desert in the whole series rather than the present stage, or one that retains a karmic framework while giving up its desert-based character. Please present the strongest version of each move, explain what would justify adopting it, and then offer your own considered judgement about which move is best justified. Your response should be in British English.
