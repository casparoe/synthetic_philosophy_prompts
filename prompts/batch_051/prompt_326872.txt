I have been asked to write down my current position set on how effective altruism should allocate resources and why, for a working group that will then audit it. I want to keep all seven because each of them strikes me as right, but I suspect there may be tensions I am not seeing. Here they are.

First, I think the movement's reliance on measurable, scalable outcomes has created a systematic measurability bias: political and institutional change, including litigation, movement-building, and regulatory reform, is often the highest-leverage work available, yet it is chronically underfunded because its feedback loops are slow and noisy. Second, I still think earning to give is a reasonable fallback for many people, because direct work on institutional change has high variance in personal fit and is hard to evaluate ex ante, so a person without a clear comparative advantage should not default to it. Third, I accept the use of expected value estimates that assign small probabilities to very large future impacts, but only when the model structure is stable enough that changing the parameters by a factor of ten does not flip the decision. Fourth, global health and development interventions remain the most epistemically secure giving opportunities, and I think any other cause should be judged against that baseline rather than merely replacing it. Fifth, the recent shift toward AI existential risk within effective altruism is broadly justified, even though it relies on exactly the kind of fragile expected-value reasoning that I elsewhere criticize. Sixth, the largest failure mode in the movement is not its measurement fetish but the concentration of funding power in a few large foundations, which produces groupthink and suppresses unfashionable priorities. Seventh, we should be more pluralistic about which decision procedures count as legitimate for allocating resources, but we should not give up the demand that people make their reasoning explicit and open to correction.

I would like you to go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which pairs only appear to be in tension. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, what the cheapest revision would be, and which single revision would do the most to make the whole set hang together.
