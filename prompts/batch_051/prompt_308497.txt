so I recently got into illusionism about phenomenal consciousness — the view that things like the felt redness of a ripe tomato or the awfulness of pain are a kind of introspective illusion. It seems to me that this view refutes itself, but I also know philosophy arguments that feel self-evident are usually hiding something. I cant figure out where, so here goes:

(1) Illusionism says our experiences seem to have qualitative, felt properties (qualia), but they actually don't have those properties.

(2) An illusion is when something seems to have a property that it doesn't really have.

(3) But if an experience seems to have a property, then there has to be something it's like to have that seeming. A bare physical process with no feel can't seem anything to anyone — at most it could cause other things.

(4) So the very seeming the illusionist needs is already a bit of qualia: it's a felt, qualitative state.

(5) Therefore illusionism is incoherent, because it has to presuppose the existence of qualia in order to state that qualia are an illusion.

Am I right? If not, say exactly which step fails, and what the strongest, non-question-begging version of that step would be. If the problem is that a word shifts meaning between steps, tell me which word and the two readings. If no step fails, why would anyone still take illusionism seriously?
