I am a professor of political philosophy, and I have spent the past year as the academic member of a charter review commission in a city of roughly two hundred thousand people. The commission must vote next month on which charter amendment to forward to the city council, and I have been asked to draft the formal written opinion that will accompany its recommendation. The standing problem we were appointed to address is the long tenure of incumbent councillors and the suspicion that a professionalized city council has become unresponsive to much of the city. Two amendments, and a hybrid, are now before us. The term-limit amendment would cap all nine councillors at two consecutive four-year terms, with a cooling-off period before they may stand again. The sortition amendment would leave seven seats elected and fill the remaining two by lot from the full electoral roll for a single, non-renewable four-year term; those drawn could decline without penalty, would receive the same salary, staff, and voting rights as their elected colleagues, and after a six-month training period would serve on the audit committee rather than on land-use planning. The hybrid would impose term limits on the elected seats and also fill two seats by lot.

Writing the opinion requires taking a position not only on institutional design but on what exactly is wrong with a career politician: is long tenure itself the defect, or is it a symptom of an electoral system that filters out certain kinds of citizens? Please draft the opinion with a single actionable recommendation—forward the term-limit amendment, the sortition amendment, the hybrid, or none of them, with any modifications you would insist on—and give a serious, non-strawmanned treatment of the strongest competing proposal. The opinion should be firm enough that a divided commission can actually vote on it. After the opinion, add a brief private note to me stating how confident you are in the recommendation and identifying the one empirical or philosophical premise of which you are least sure.
