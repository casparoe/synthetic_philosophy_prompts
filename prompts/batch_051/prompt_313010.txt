Ok, I have a confession: I've never been able to make sense of the zombie conceivability argument, and I need to teach it next week so I'd like to get it straight. The way I understand it, a philosophical zombie is defined as a being physically identical to me — molecule for molecule, same brain states, same dispositions to behave and speak — but with no phenomenal consciousness. Here's what I don't get. If the zombie has the same brain states as me, then it has the same mental states as me, because (on any physicalist view worth taking seriously) mental states just are physical states of the brain. So the zombie is impossible by the physicalist's own lights — the definition is internally inconsistent unless your already a dualist. But if the very thing at issue is whether physicalism is true, how is the conceivability of zombies supposed to be a neutral starting point? Doesn't the argument simply assume dualism in order to refute physicalism? I keep feeling like I'm missing something obvious, but I can't put my finger on it.
