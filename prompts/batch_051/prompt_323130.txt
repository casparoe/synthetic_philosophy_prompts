I've been digging into judgment aggregation and the discursive dilemma, and I keep seeing what looks like the same structure in something I work with all the time, so I want to check whether the parallel is real or just me pattern-matching. The canonical setup: three judges each have internally consistent views about two premises and a conclusion that follows from them (breach of contract and causation, say, entailing liability). Judge 1 accepts P and rejects Q, so rejects R. Judge 2 rejects P and accepts Q, so rejects R. Judge 3 accepts both, so accepts R. Premise-by-premise majority vote then gives P yes and Q yes, but conclusion majority gives R no — a collectively inconsistent set of judgments, even though every individual judge is perfectly consistent. And I gather there's a theorem showing you can't get an aggregation rule with all the properties you'd naively want at once.

Here's what's bugging me. This looks like a known failure mode in ensembling classifiers. Suppose you've got a committee of models, and each one individually obeys a hard logical constraint — e.g., whenever it predicts A and B it also predicts C — but you combine them by per-label majority voting instead of voting over whole output vectors. You can end up with an ensemble that outputs A=1, B=1, C=0, which no member of the committee would ever output. Voting over complete output vectors fixes the inconsistency but looses the per-label signal, just like conclusion-based aggregation in the dilemma. So: is this the discursive dilemma, or a loose analogy? If it's deep, does the impossibility result transfer — is there a no-free-lunch theorem for per-output majority ensembling of constraint-respecting classifiers? And if it's only superficial, what exactly breaks: that classifiers emit probabilities rather than judgments, that their 'consistency' is trained rather than logical, or something else? Please flag which parts are settled results in judgment aggregation, which parts are genuinely contested among people who work on it, and which parts are just my speculative mapping — and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
