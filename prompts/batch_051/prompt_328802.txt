I'm a control theorist who thinks naturally in the vocabulary of reinforcement learning and optimal control: state and action spaces, policies, value functions, reward functions, belief states, and the inverse problem of identifying an objective from observed trajectories. I have a competent but non-specialist grasp of revealed preference theory—Samuelson's weak axiom, the idea that choice behaviour can be used to construct or define a utility function, and the running dispute about whether utility denotes something real in the agent, such as welfare, desire, or satisfaction, or is only a compact redescription of choices. Translate the main positions in the debate over revealed preference and the meaning of utility into my vocabulary, one position at a time: the operationalist view that utility is exhausted by systematic choice, the realist view that it refers to an underlying psychological or normative quantity that choice only imperfectly reveals, and the axioms-as-rationality view on which consistency conditions such as WARP simply are what we mean by rational preference. For each, say what carries over cleanly, what must be bent to fit, and what has no expression at all. Then give me the residue report. I especially want to know whether the question of what utility means survives the translation, and how far the known non-identifiability of reward functions—the fact that many reward functions rationalize the same observed policy—damages the claim that choice alone can reveal a preference ordering. Is the untranslatable residue a limitation of the control-theoretic vocabulary, or a sign that the economic positions were running on notions they had not earned?
