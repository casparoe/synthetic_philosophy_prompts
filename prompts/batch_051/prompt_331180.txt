Draft the joint statement that a defender of expected utility theory's independence axiom and a behavioral non-expected-utility theorist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration about the Allais paradox. First, give a numbered section of claims both sides accept: about the observed common-consequence and common-ratio choice patterns, about the formal content of the independence and sure-thing principles (and reduction of compound lotteries, where relevant), and about any part of their dispute both now regard as merely verbal. Then give a disagreements table with the residual disagreement stated exactly, each side's view worded as its own proponents would accept: in particular, whether Allais-type choices are rational evaluations that expected utility theory fails to represent, or systematic errors that reflection should correct, and whether the certainty effect, regret, or outcome-weighting belongs inside preference itself or only in the explanation of departures from it. Then list the cruxes as sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one side, and for each crux describe at least one concrete test, case, or argument both sides agree bears on it—with each side's prediction written down in advance. Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on the topic, and do not present a genuinely contested view as the consensus.
