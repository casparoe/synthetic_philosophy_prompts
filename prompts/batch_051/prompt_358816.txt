Got into a long, circular argument with a friend last night about whether you can compare historical atrocities, and I keep coming back to a pattern in the back-and-forth that I don't have a word for. I'd made an offhand remark that the Armenian genocide and the Holocaust weren't the same in scale, and she got angry—not at the factual claim, she said, but at the entire act of putting them on a scale. Her line was that ranking mass death is already obscene because it converts suffering into a ledger and always ends with someone's dead mattering less. Fair enough, but then I noticed she was doing the same thing with the sign reversed: she said the Soviet famines were "uniquely" cynical because the state could have fed people and chose not to, which is a comparison to every other way people have died in famines. And I was no better: when I objected that intentional extermination is different in kind from callous neglect, I was drawing a line that put one class of atrocity above another, even though I'd spent the last hour arguing you can't rank them.

Since then I've spotted the same thing in how people talk about all of this. Saying something was "unprecedented" or "the worst thing a government can do" is already a comparative claim, but it usually passes as non-ranking because it isn't presented as a list. People who insist that comparisons are obscene will reach for comparative numbers or uniqueness claims the moment they need them, and the prohibition mostly seems to bite when the other side's comparison is going somewhere we don't like. I don't think the lesson is just that everybody is a hypocrite, though maybe it is.

Is there an established term for this—the taboo on explicit ranking coexising with implicit comparative claims, and the way "these things can't be compared" functions as a move inside an argument rather than as a genuine refusal to compare? I know there's a whole debate about comparing atrocities, but I'm not after a reading list. I want to know whether the literature distinguishes between ranking-as-accounting and whatever it was we were doing instead, and whether the taboo itself can be stated in a way that isn't self-undermining. If there's no exact term, what would you call it and where are its edges—when is a comparison the genuinely obscene thing and when is it just a disagreement about which comparisons count as legitimate?
