Write the joint statement that a committed total utilitarian and a committed person-affecting population ethicist — one who treats merely adding lives worth living, without affecting anyone else, as a neutral change rather than an improvement — could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the repugnant conclusion. For the purposes of the document, take the repugnant conclusion to be the claim that for any population all of whose members have lives at a very high positive level of welfare, there is some much larger population all of whose members have lives that are only barely worth living such that, other things being equal, the larger population would be better. The total utilitarian accepts this as a consequence of taking betterness to be just total welfare, so that many lives barely worth living can in principle be better than fewer lives at far higher welfare; the person-affecting theorist rejects it, holding that one outcome can be better than another only by being better for, or worse for, particular people.

Do not spend the document restating these positions; use them. The document should contain the following sections.

(1) A numbered list of agreed claims about the repugnant conclusion, the mere addition paradox (the chain from a high-average population through adding extra worthwhile lives and then equalizing), and the choice among total, average, and person-affecting principles. Each claim must be precise enough that neither side is signing something vacuous or slanted, and the list should include at least one claim each side finds genuinely costly but can accept.

(2) A disagreements table. For each row: the issue; the total utilitarian's position in wording a total utilitarian would sign; the person-affecting position in wording a person-affecting theorist would sign; the crux of the disagreement; and a concrete case or argument both agree bears on that crux, with each side's prediction about it recorded in advance. Cover at least: (a) whether adding lives worth living to a world, without lowering anyone's welfare, makes that world better; (b) the procreation asymmetry between bringing into existence lives worth living and lives not worth living; and (c) whether the repugnant conclusion should ultimately be resisted or accepted on reflection.

(3) A short closing section on which parts of their disagreement, if any, are merely verbal, and which popular formulations of the debate each side agrees are misleading.

Do not manufacture agreement they do not have; the value of the document lies in making the residual disagreement exact.
