I'm putting together an AI-alignment reading group and two members have been going back and forth for months about how a agent should be built. One is an approval-directed advocate: roughly, train agents to do what human overseers would approve of, keep corrigibility and ongoing human judgment at the center, and don't try to lock in a goal. The other is a goal-directed advocate: roughly, build agents around an explicit objective or a latent utility function they're actually maximizing, because approval is noisy, gameable, and falls apart under distribution shift.

They've agreed to an adversarial collaboration, and I want you to draft the joint statement they'd both sign. Structure it as: (1) a numbered list of claims both can accept without flinching — about what current systems actually do, about the failure modes of each approach taken pure (reward gaming and sycophancy on one side, goal misspecification and lock-in on the other), and about the minimal conditions for either approach to count as alignment; (2) a disagreements table where each row gives the dispute twice, once in wording the approval-directed person would own and once in wording the goal-directed person would own; (3) a short list of cruxes, sub-questions such that settling them would genuinely move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a concrete hypothetical or experiment both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Make the best version of each position sharp rather than making both sound like the same boring moderate; I want to see where each would wince. And if part of the whole fight is verbal — say, both want corrigibility but operationalize it differently — say which part is verbal and what substantive residue remains.
