I've been going in circles on whether many-worlds can actually recover the Born rule, and I've got my worry down to six steps. Tell me where it goes wrong, if it does.

(1) Suppose a measurement has two possible outcomes, up and down, with squared amplitudes 0.9 and 0.1. On many-worlds, both outcomes happen, each on its own branch.

(2) Before the measurement, the observer hasn't split yet, and there's no fact about which future branch is "theirs"; both descendants are equally continuous with the original person.

(3) If two future branches are on a par in that way—neither singled out as the observer's future—the observer should assign them equal probability.

(4) So the observer should be 50/50 about seeing up versus down, no matter what the amplitudes are.

(5) But quantum mechanics, via the Born rule, says they should expect up with prob 0.9 and down with 0.1, and that's the rule confirmed by repeated experiments.

(6) So many-worlds either predicts the wrong probabilities or has to add the Born rule as an extra postulate, which defeats the point of the interpretation.

I have a feeling step 2 or 3 is doing something shifty, but when I try to pin it down I end up making the Born rule sound even more mysterious. Is the argument valid, and if not, which step fails? If the culprit is step 2 or 3, give the strongest reading of that step you can and say whether the argument still fails on that reading. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble, and don't restate my steps back to me.
