Question: State the no-free-lunch theorem in your own words and explain how it has been used to support a skeptical conclusion about induction. Is that use legitimate?

Student answer: The no-free-lunch theorem says that in supervised learning, if you average over all possible target functions, every algorithm has the same expected error on new examples, so no learner is better than any other and they're all as good as random guessing. This is often taken to show that induction is unjustified. Since ordinary inductive inference is just one way of learning a function from data, and the theorem says no way is better than any other, induction can't be rationally preferred to anything else. In fact the theorem is a precise modern version of Hume: it proves mathematically that the future needn't resemble the past. The only way out is to assume the world is simple or uniform, but that assumption is itself something we only accept by induction, so the problem remains.
