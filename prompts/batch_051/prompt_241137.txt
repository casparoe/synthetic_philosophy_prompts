I write for a magazine that covers science and ideas, and I keep seeing whole-brain emulation framed as a debate about whether the emulation would be conscious. That feels like it's skipping a step. I'd like you to argue the following position: even if we grant that a scan-and-simulate emulation of Sam's brain produces a conscious mind with all his memories and dispositions, it is still not Sam's mind — not for any spooky reason, but because psychological continuity is not the right criterion for numerical identity. Part of what individuates a mind is that it is the causal upshot of one particular living body's ongoing history, and once you separate the mind-stuff from that history, you've created a mental duplicate or a descendant, not a continuation. Make the strongest case for that view, then explain whether the argument changes if the upload is destructive — that is, if the original brain is scanned at high resolution but destroyed in the process. Also, am I right that this kind of view gets misdescribed as dualism? I dont think it is.
