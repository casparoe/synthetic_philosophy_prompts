I have been thinking on my own about the purpose of biographies, and I have drafted a short essay that I would like some feedback on. I am not a classicist; I came to this just by reading Plutarch's Lives, especially the remark he makes in the opening of one of the lives, where he says he uses his work as a mirror, arranging his own life toward the virtues of the men he describes. My essay takes that image seriously and asks what has to be true for a written life to work as a mirror. My tentative argument is that the mirror effect depends on judgment and comparison rather than on getting inside a single person's head. Plutarch pairs a Greek and a Roman life, then ends with a formal comparison; the reader is placed between two differently inflected lives and has to weigh them. The moral lesson is not in either life alone but in the act of comparing. And comparison is possible because Plutarch treats character as fairly stable and public, shown in concrete deeds, not because he reconstructs someone's private development. I then claim that modern biographies usually do the reverse: they present one person as a unique, developing interiority, and they explain public actions as symptoms of that interiority. That may produce good explanation, but it prevents the life from being a mirror, because you cannot compare yourself with an interior process that is reconstructed as entirely individual. The essay ends by suggesting that the modern descendant of Plutarch is not biography but something closer to a comparative case study or a set of deliberately contrasted portraits, and that the hagiographic impulse survives in weakened form when people argue about positive role models.

I would like comments on the essay, especially on three things. First, is my reading of Plutarch's mirror image fair, or am I loading it with a theory of comparison he did not intend? Second, is the contrast with modern biography too strong or too vague, and are there obvious counterexamples I should address? Third, which part of the argument needs the most development before it could convince a skeptical reader? I want to know where I have simply asserted something that needs more support, and whether the core idea is genuinely interesting or only restates something familiar.
