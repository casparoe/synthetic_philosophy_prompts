I keep seeing scales of civilisational advancement introduced by their advocates rather than tested against a shared specification, so I want the specification first. Take the familiar options as data points rather than as the object: Kardashev-style scales that rank civilisations by the energy they can command, Barrow-style scales that rank them by the smallest spatial scale at which they can engineer matter, and the more recent scales built around information processing. Write the desiderata that any adequate scale of civilisational advancement must satisfy. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it: the phenomena the scale must capture, the distinctions it cannot collapse (energy throughput versus efficiency in its use, theoretical knowledge versus engineering capacity, leaving a detectable signature versus being able to survive, and scale versus stage), and any physical results it must respect, such as thermodynamic limits or light-speed bounds on coordination. Make the edge cases do some work: a civilisation that deliberately minimises its energy footprint, one with sophisticated theory but negligible artefact production, and one that engineers at the atomic scale but not at the planetary scale. Indicate which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of one research programme, and flag any pair that looks hard to satisfy jointly. Then briefly note which single requirement would exclude the most familiar proposals. Please write the answer in American English.
