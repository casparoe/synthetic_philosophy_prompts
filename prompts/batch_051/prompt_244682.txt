I've been trying to get clear about a pattern I keep noticing in disputes over shared projects, and I would like to know whether it has an established name. The pattern is this. A number of people jointly maintain something that anyone in the group can use. One person uses it regularly but declines to contribute to the work or the cost, and their stated reason is not that they dislike the thing or can't afford to help. It is that their contribution would make no difference: the thing would be maintained whether or not they helped, and their use of it doesn't noticeably reduce what is available to others. For instance, a person listens every day to a station supported by listener donations but never gives, saying the station would survive without their money and a pledge from them wouldn't change the programming. Or a neighbor spends hours sitting in a community garden that others weed and water but never helps with the upkeep, on the grounds that the garden is already beautifully kept and one more pair of hands would be superfluous. The people who do the work tend to resent this, but I have trouble saying exactly what the non-contributor is doing wrong, if anything, when their abstention truly leaves everyone else no worse off. Is there a recognized term for this precise situation — drawing on a shared practice while opting out because one's contribution would be redundant — and how does it differ from the more familiar case where someone takes the benefit and their failure to contribute does impose a real cost on others? If no single term fits, what are the nearest concepts and where exactly are the boundaries? I am less interested in the label than in getting the shape of the phenomenon right.
