Last night a friend and I spent a long evening arguing about whether philosophy makes progress. The argument went in circles, and it eventually became clear that we were not really disputing the facts: we were using the word 'progress' in different ways, each of us reaching for examples that suited our side. I would like a working definition of progress — progress for a field of inquiry or a body of intellectual work, not progress toward a personal goal — that would let us argue on shared ground.

Please test your definition against the following cases. I have noted my own leanings in parentheses, but I want to know where the definition itself leads.

1. A mathematician proves a conjecture that had resisted proof for a century. (Clearly progress.)
2. Over two decades, a medical research program develops, tests, and widely deploys a vaccine that effectively eliminates a disease. (Clearly progress.)
3. An academic subfield changes its dominant vocabulary, examples, and central questions every few years largely in response to prestige and funding incentives, dropping old problems not because they were solved but because they went out of fashion, and without accumulating new results or understanding. (Clearly not progress.)
4. A discipline repeatedly rediscovers arguments and positions that had already been worked out in an earlier period, and presents them as new. (Clearly not progress.)
5. After two millennia of debate about free will, there is no convergence of opinion, but the debate has produced much sharper distinctions and better-formulated positions than the ancients had. (I suspect this is progress; my friend denies it.)
6. A field eventually concludes that one of its central questions was ill-posed, and dissolves the question rather than answering it or replacing it with a more precise one. (I genuinely do not know.)
7. A successful theory is replaced by a successor that makes slightly more accurate predictions but, by the standards of the earlier theory's practitioners, seems to explain less. (Borderline.)
8. A research tradition multiplies increasingly subtle interpretations of a fixed set of texts, with no agreed method for showing one interpretation to be more adequate than another. (I lean toward 'not progress,' but I am not certain.)

Propose a definition, run it through all eight cases, and say honestly where it gives the wrong answer or no answer. Then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts it forces. Finally, return to my original dispute: on your best definition, does philosophy make progress, and how does its kind of progress compare with that of the natural sciences?
