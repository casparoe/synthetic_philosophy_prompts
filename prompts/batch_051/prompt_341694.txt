Propose a working definition of sentience — the capacity to have experiences, such as pain — that classifies the following cases correctly: a healthy adult human; a digital thermostat; a honeybee; a lobster; a large language model executing a single forward pass; a single human cortical neuron kept alive in a dish; a human fetus at twenty weeks' gestation; a whole-brain emulation of a mouse running at biological speed; and a reinforcement-learning agent that minimizes a negative reward signal. I take the human to be clearly sentient and the thermostat clearly not, and I have no settled view about the honeybee, the forward-pass model, the isolated neuron, and the reward-driven agent. Run your definition through all nine cases, flag any that it leaves indeterminate or classifies in a way you would be unwilling to defend, and then either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. Lead with your final definition in the first sentence, then give the reasoning and the case-by-case tests; do not restate the question. Write in American English.
