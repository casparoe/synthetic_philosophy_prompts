I chair my department's curriculum committee, and we are redesigning the required junior seminar on philosophical method. A faction proposes building the course around reflective equilibrium: students would formulate considered judgments about cases, organize them under general principles, and revise judgments and principles in alternation until they cohere; in the ambitious form now on the table, students would also loop in background theories about persons, society, and language and revise those as needed. An opposing faction insists that reflective equilibrium taught this way is not a way of testing whether judgments are warranted but a procedure for making one's starting intuitions look coherent, and that making it our signature method would credential rationalization. Draft the short memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting: recommend adopting the proposal, rejecting it, or adopting a modified version; include a concrete course plan or alternative; engage the strongest version of the objection rather than rehearsing the standard defense; and close with a motion the committee could vote on.
