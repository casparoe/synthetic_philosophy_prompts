I've been chewing on the difference between killing someone and just letting them die, and I keep coming back to Rwanda in 1994. The UN already had peacekeepers there, but after ten Belgian soldiers got killed, the Security Council pulled most of the force out—down to a few hundred men, right as the killing was about to get way worse—and no outside power sent in a real intervention force. So the world mostly stood by while something like 800,000 people were murdered in about a hundred days. I want you to analyze that episode through the doing/allowing distinction. Specifically: were the outside actors "letting die" rather than killing, and does that line actually do the moral work people claim it does? Was withdrawing the peacekeepers a mere omission, or something more like an action with consequences everyone could see coming? Where does the episode break the distinction, or show that it can't handle situations where people could step in but choose not to? And what does that say about the distinction itself—is it doing real moral work, or is it mostly a way for powerful actors to feel clean? Don't get too academic about it, just follow the question where it goes. End with a one-sentence summary of your overall verdict as the last line.
