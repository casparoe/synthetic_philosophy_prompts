Act as an expert on decision theory and the philosophy of sunk costs, with a side interest in legal and fiduciary interpretation. Think carefully before answering.

I'm a trustee of a small private foundation. Our trust deed, written in 1986, has a clause that reads exactly as follows:

"Section 5.3: In deciding whether to renew, suspend, or terminate a funded project, the Trustees shall take into account sums already advanced by the Foundation for that project. A project shall not be terminated solely on the ground that its remaining expected benefits are exceeded by its expected future costs, unless continued funding would materially impair the Foundation's ability to carry out its other charitable purposes."

We are now in a situation the original draughtsman obviously did not have in mind. We funded a three-year youth mentoring program with $600,000. Eighteen months in, we have spent about $420,000. A well-run randomized evaluation in a neighboring city found no measurable effect on school attendance or arrests, and our own interim monitoring looks discouraging. The remaining two years would cost another $180,000. Most trustees accept that the expected future benefits are now lower than the expected future costs. But the founder's daughter, who is also a trustee, argues that pulling the plug would turn the $420,000 already spent into a total waste, and that Clause 5.3 was meant to prevent exactly that kind of cold cost-benefit thinking.

Draft the memo I'll circulate to the board. It should recommend a concrete decision (continue, suspend, or terminate) and explain how Clause 5.3 should be applied here. Address the strongest competing reading: some trustees think the clause is just an irrational codification of the sunk cost fallacy and should be read as narrowly as possible, while others think the founders deliberately chosen to honor sunk costs and we should respect that. Also tell me whether there is any defensible rationale for weighting sunk costs at all, or whether they are always irrelevant from a decision-theoretic point of view.
