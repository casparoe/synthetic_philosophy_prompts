I've been trying to reconstruct an argument from Gödel's musings on rotating universes, and I can't tell whether it's clever or just sneaks something in. Here's my reconstruction:

(1) In his rotating solution to general relativity, there are closed timelike curves; a future-directed timelike path can loop back and arrive at the same event it started from.
(2) So within that universe, the relation "can be reached by a future-directed timelike curve" is not a global strict partial order; it has cycles.
(3) If objective passage of time is real, then for any two events, it must be a determinate matter which is earlier, later, or simultaneous, and that ordering cannot loop.
(4) But in the Gödel universe, the ordering loops.
(5) The Gödel universe is physically possible in the sense of being an exact solution to the field equations with physically motivated matter (rotating dust).
(6) Therefore there is a physically possible world where objective passage of time is not real.
(7) So objective passage of time, even if it exists in our universe, is not an essential feature of time itself.

I want to know whether this is valid, and if not, exactly which step is the weak one. I especially wonder if step (3) is too strong, since some people might say objective passage doesn't need a global ordering, or step (5) might be doing too much with "physically possible." Give me the exact step and the reading that makes it fail, or tell me the argument is sound.
