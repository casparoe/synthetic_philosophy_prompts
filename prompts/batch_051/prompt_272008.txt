I am a high school student, and I have been trying to work through something about determinism and chaos theory that keeps bothering me. I have written out the argument below, which seems to show that determinism does not really secure the predictability of the future. I half-suspect that the argument is too smooth, but I cannot locate the bad step.

(1) Determinism is the view that the complete state of the universe at any one time, together with the laws of nature, uniquely fixes every later state of the universe.
(2) If a later state is uniquely fixed by an earlier complete state plus the laws, then a reasoner who possessed that complete state and the laws could in principle derive the later state with certainty.
(3) Therefore, if determinism is true, the future is in principle predictable from the present plus the laws.
(4) But chaos theory describes deterministic systems in which two initial states that differ only at some extremely fine scale can evolve into future states that are completely different at an observable scale.
(5) Any real measurement has finite precision, so a real observer can at best determine that the actual initial state lies inside some nonzero interval; in a chaotic system, many different states inside that interval lead to different futures.
(6) Therefore, a real observer in a deterministic but chaotic universe could not determine which of those futures is the one that will actually occur.
(7) So determinism does not imply that the future is predictable.

Is this argument valid? If not, which numbered step fails? Please be specific about the step and about the reading of any terms on which the argument's success depends.
