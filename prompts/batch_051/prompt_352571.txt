In particle physics we have a technical notion of fine-tuning: your theory is fine-tuned when a low-energy prediction depends on a cancellation among large parameters to an absurd number of decimal places, like the Higgs mass or the cosmological constant. We normally treat that as a symptom that we're missing some mechanism — a symmetry, new particles, whatever — not as evidence that the parameters were chosen. The philosophical fine-tuning argument seems to be doing something different: it says the life-permitting values of the constants form a tiny sliver of all possible values, and that calls out for explanation in a way a designer or a multiverse would supply. I suspect these are two quite different concepts that happen to share the name, and I'm not sure the design argument ever licenses the step from "physically unnatural" to "improbably small slice of parameter space." But I'm also not certain naturalness is clean of the same move — maybe the physicist's naturalness principle is itself smuggling in a probability measure, or an anthropic self-selection step, when we're not looking.
