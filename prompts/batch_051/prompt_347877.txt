I am a PhD student in the history and philosophy of sport, and I am drafting a seminar paper on whether competitive institutions can ever preserve the intrinsic character of play. My point of departure is Huizinga's analysis in *Homo Ludens*, where he distinguishes genuine play by several features: it is voluntary rather than compelled, it is experienced as standing outside ordinary life, it is bounded in time and space, and it is pursued for its own sake rather than for material gain. Pieper's *Leisure, the Basis of Culture* presses a related point, treating leisure not as recuperation from work but as a receptive, non-instrumental mode of activity ordered toward goods beyond utility. I want to test these frameworks against a concrete historical episode: the long struggle over Olympic amateurism, from Avery Brundage's rigid enforcement of amateur eligibility in the 1950s and 1960s to the formal abandonment of the amateur requirement in the 1980s and the admission of professional athletes, most visibly in the 1992 basketball tournament. The standard story is that the old amateur code was either a principled defense of sport as pure play or a hypocritical system of class exclusion, and that Soviet-bloc full-time amateurs, nominally employed as soldiers or students, exposed its bankruptcy.

I would like you to analyze this episode through Huizinga and Pieper. Specifically: who counts as the relevant player in the amateurist framework, and did that framework treat athletic competition as separate from ordinary economic life in anything like Huizinga's sense? Did it preserve play as an end in itself, or did it force athletes to perform non-play labor under the appearance of play? What would each theorist have predicted about the trajectory of the amateur ideal, and which features of the episode does their account explain, obscure, or misdescribe? Finally, does the collapse of amateurism count for or against the claim that play loses its character when tied to material compensation? I would appreciate a disciplined mapping that is explicit about where each theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak.
