Got into an argument at a family dinner about whether I should put a little money on a prediction market for the next election, and my brother-in-law kept saying "it's all priced in" every time I brought up some analysis I'd read. At first I thought he just meant the boring thing: the current price already reflects what's public. But by the end of the night the same phrase was being used to mean "you can't make money off information, period" and "there's no point even forming your own opinion." Then I went and read about the no-trade theorem (roughly: if everyone's rational and starts with the same prior, they shouldn't trade on differences in information alone, because one person's willingness to trade itself reveals information) and now I'm less sure what the slogan is even doing. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims "it's all priced in" could be making, say which are trivially true, which are substantive and defensible, and which are just false, and then tell me which of those readings the no-trade theorem actually supports or undercuts — and what a market would have to look like for the saying to be a deep truth rather than a conversation-stopper?
