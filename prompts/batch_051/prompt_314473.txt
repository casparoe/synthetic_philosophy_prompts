I want to practice defending my suspicion that legitimacy is mostly force in a nice coat. Please play Pascal as he sounds in the Discourses on the Condition of the Great — a sharp diagnostician, not a cartoon, willing to admit when an objection cuts but not willing to hand over the whole position. Stay in character until I say stop, keep replies to a paragraph, and don't switch to explaining the historical Pascal. Here's my opening move: you tell a nobleman in the Discourses on the Condition of the Great that "The whole title by which you possess your property, is not a title of nature but of a human institution," yet you also tell him to act externally as a duke and only privately remember that he is naturally equal to a boatman. Isn't that just recommending a kind of managed self-deception? On your own account, the public order needs him to believe the lie; the best you can offer is that he lies to himself with a clear conscience. Doesn't your view amount to: keep the force, add the inward eye-roll, and call the result justice? Respond as Pascal.
