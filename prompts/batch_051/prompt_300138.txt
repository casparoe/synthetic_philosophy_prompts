I am a novelist, four books into a series, and I am trying to keep a character consistent as she makes what the scene intends to be a genuinely hard choice between incommensurable goods. The character, Ruth, is a writer in her late forties. Across the earlier books she has faced three comparable decisions. At twenty-nine she gave up a fully funded year-long residency abroad to stay with her father after his stroke, on the grounds that the residency was a repeatable opportunity whereas her father's remaining time was not. At thirty-six she left a secure editorial position to write her second novel on savings, reasoning that financial security could be rebuilt but the book, if deferred, would probably never be written. At forty-three she ended an engagement to a loving man who pressed her to set writing aside and join his firm, telling him she would not trade away the one part of herself that was not for sale.

In the present book she faces a new decision. Her oldest friend, terminally ill and living on the other side of the country, asks Ruth to put the novel aside and stay with her for what doctors expect to be the last five months. Ruth is three-quarters through a book under a hard deadline, and she believes the book itself—not merely the contract—would not be recoverable if she left it now. She also suspects her friend is asking her to do what she has twice refused to do: subordinate her work to someone else's claim on her life, though she knows the friend is not making an ultimatum and is simply afraid to die alone.

I want the scene to be decided the way the character would decide it, not the way I wish she would. What should Ruth choose if she is to remain consistent with her earlier decisions? Which precedent is the closest analog, and does it actually settle the case? I also want you to lay out the different rules that would fit her whole history equally well—for instance, a rule about irreplaceable persons outweighing repeatable professional goods, a rule about the novel as a non-negotiable core self, or something more subtle—and say what each rule would require here. Where the rules genuinely diverge, tell me which further situation, not in the series, would most cleanly separate them.
