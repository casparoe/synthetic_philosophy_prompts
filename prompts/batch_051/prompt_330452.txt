I'm drafting a proposal for a 6,000-word philosophy-of-religion term paper (one semester) and want to turn a half-formed idea into a tight research proposal. The topic is the proportionality objection to the doctrine of eternal conscious torment. The standard retributivist defence is that a sin against an infinitely good God has infinite gravity, so an infinite penalty can still be proportionate. My worry is that this defence quietly swaps the principle it was meant to preserve: proportionality to what the offender deserves, given the offender's culpability and the wrongness of the act, gets replaced by proportionality to the status of the offended party. If the victim's infinite dignity is doing all the work, it is unclear that the punishment still tracks the offender's desert; if it is not doing the work, it is unclear how a finite creature commits an infinite offense. Please turn this into a proposal that: (1) states a determinate research question; (2) explains why the main positions — eternal torment, annihilationism, universal salvation, and the reading of hell as final exclusion rather than inflicted pain — each leave the underlying question open; (3) sets out the approach and why a systematic dilemma/diagnostic argument, rather than historical or exegetical study, is the right method; (4) identifies the possible conclusions and the considerations that would decide between them; and (5) gives an honest account of what would count as failure and what is realistic to complete in one semester. Do not include citations or a bibliography; I will add those separately.
