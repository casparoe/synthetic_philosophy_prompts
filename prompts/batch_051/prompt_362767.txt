For an ideas piece I'm doing on whether karma can be a real theory of moral desert, I got into it with a Buddhist friend who wants to hold both that there's no persisting self and that your actions' fruits come back to you in a future life. That feels incoherent to me, and I finally wrote my argument out:

(1) Desert requires the reward or punishment go to the same individual who did the act; if someone else gets it, it's not desert, just luck or spillover.
(2) Karma, as a theory of moral desert, says the fruit of a deed ripens on the doer in a later life.
(3) On the no-self view, there's no abiding subject, so the being in the next life is not strictly identical to the earlier doer.
(4) So the recipient of the fruit is a different individual from the doer.
(5) A different individual can't deserve the doer's fruit.
(6) So karma-as-desert and no-self can't both be true.

Is this valid, and if not, which step is the bad one? I half-suspect the wiggle is in step 4, sliding from "not strictly identical" to "different individual," but maybe I'm the one stacking the deck to hard.
