I am a philosophy professor planning a paper, and here is the problem I want to work through. The standard answer to the solipsistic thought that other people might be, as my students put it, NPCs, is the argument from analogy: I know in my own case that my behavior issues from inner states, and since other humans behave in relevantly the same ways, I am warranted in attributing inner states to them. But a new case has appeared that the argument did not anticipate: a large language model produces fluent text, describes its own beliefs and suffering, and passes ordinary behavioral tests, yet most of us are strongly reluctant to treat it as having an inner life. Does the argument from analogy survive this new case? I want to know which premise the case puts pressure on, whether that premise was load-bearing all along, and whether there is a repaired version of the argument that still undergirds our ordinary confidence about other human minds without forcing the same conclusion about chatbots. Say exactly what the argument needs in order to go through, and whether it still has that after the new case.
