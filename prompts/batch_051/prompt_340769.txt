I've been drawn to the misconceptions-as-theories view: a lot of student errors in physics and math aren't random noise but systematic, generative frameworks. A kid who thinks heavier things fall faster will often also think heavier pendulums swing faster, and she'll explain away the vacuum demo rather than revise. Teaching that treats the error as a coherent alternative theory and works through it seems to beat just correcting the answer. But I keep having a firm intuition that not every wrong answer fits this: some are just bugs, not theories—a misread sign, a forgotten convention, a vocabulary gap—and treating every bug as if its a coherent alternative theory is false and maybe a bit condescending, since it credits commitments the student doesn't actually hold.
