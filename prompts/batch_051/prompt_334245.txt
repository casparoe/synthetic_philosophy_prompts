I keep having the same argument with a condensed matter friend about effective field theories and the autonomy of scales, and I've realized the problem is that neither of us has ever written down what a position here is actually supposed to do to earn its keep. My view, roughly: the decoupling behavior you get from Wilson-style renormalization — short-distance stuff shows up only in a finite set of coefficients and then mostly doesn't matter, universality across radically different microphysics, being able to integrate out the heavy fields and match parameters — makes it look like long-wavelength descriptions aren't merely bookkeeping for calculations we're too lazy to do exactly. But the moment I try to turn that into a philosophical claim ('higher-level theories are autonomous', 'the special sciences have their own degrees of freedom'), it stops being clear which parts are doing the work and which are just physics-flavored aesthetics. A toy model of a labrador as an effective field theory is cute, but it won't settle what I'm actually trying to settle.

So here's what I want: the full desiderata. What would a theory that says something like 'scale separation means higher-level descriptions are (in some non-trivial sense) autonomous, not just convenient fictions' have to get right to count as adequate? I'm after a requirements list, not a literature review: the phenomena it has to explain (relevant vs irrelevant operators, flowing couplings and IR fixed points, universality, decoupling of heavy states, the difference between a renormalizable theory and an EFT with a cutoff), the cases it has to get right (second-order phase transitions as the obvious testbed, plus at least one regime where scale separation partially fails and the theory has to say what happens then), the distinctions it has to respect (decoupling vs real emergence vs 'the microtheory is true and everything else is tooling'), and any results from physics it can't contradict. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it and say whether it's non-negotiable or just a preference of one camp. If some of the requirements are jointly unsatisfiable, that's the part I most want flagged. Then a short paragraph scoring the usual contenders — naive reductionism, strong emergence, and whatever the 'all there is is EFTs all the way down' view calls itself — against the list; keep the scoring brief, the list is the deliverable. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
