I've been writing up the hole argument for a course on the philosophy of space and time, and I'm struck by how cleanly the standard story runs. It is usually presented as having settled something: that manifold substantivalism — the view that spacetime is a container of points whose identities are fixed independently of the matter and metric fields on them — is untenable, because the hole construction saddles it with radical indeterminism. The reasoning is that one takes a model of general relativity (a manifold together with metric and matter fields), then applies a diffeomorphism that is the identity outside some matter-free region (the "hole") and nontrivial inside it. If the points of the manifold are real, the original and the transformed model describe different possible worlds — a given point winds up with different field values in the two models — yet no observation can tell them apart, since all measurable content is diffeomorphism-invariant. The usual moral is that this forces us either to abandon substantivalism or to adopt a "sophisticated" version that identifies the two models, at the price of some reinterpretation of determinism.

I want a straight accounting of what would have to be true for this standard conclusion to be wrong or overreaching. Which specific assumptions of the argument could fail — concerning determinism, the identity of spacetime points, what counts as observable content, or the relationship between mathematical models and physical possibilities — and what would the theory, the world, or our best account of representation have to look like for each of those escape routes to be live rather than merely logically possible? Distinguish the responses that accept the argument but renegotiate its price (sophisticated substantivalism, determinism only "up to diffeomorphism," giving up primitive identity for points) from responses that would actually overturn it. I would also like to know which concessions a defender of manifold substantivalism would themselves grant as counting against the view, and finally an honest verdict on whether the position has been insulated from refutation — and whether that sort of insulation is a problem for a metaphysical view of this kind.
