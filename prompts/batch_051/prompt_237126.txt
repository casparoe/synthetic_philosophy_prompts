I run the question review team for a trivia app, and we take user-submitted true/false questions about fictional characters. The same fight keeps breaking out: someone submits "True or false: Sherlock Holmes lived at 221B Baker Street," and half my reviewers want to fact-check it against the stories while the other half says it's not true or false at all because Holmes doesn't exist. I need one reusable decision proceedure my reviewers can run on any question mentioning a fictional character, in about a minute, with no philosophy background. I want the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, and the label the app should attach: true, false, true-in-the-story, neither, or reject the question as ill-formed. The procedure has to handle the hard cases we actually get: questions that mix fiction and reality, questions the stories leave unsettled, and reviewers disagreeing about whether the name refers at all. Reviewers can close out "true" and "false" themselves, but "neither" and "reject" get escalated to me, so the procedure should say when to escalate. Then run it on three submissions: (1) "True or false: Sherlock Holmes lives at 221B Baker Street"; (2) "True or false: Sherlock Holmes is an only child"; (3) "True or false: Sherlock Holmes never actually solved a crime." I care less about the verdicts than about getting a procedure that makes the same call no matter which character is substituted, that will sometimes give me a verdict I don't like, and that says honestly where the decision is a judgment call the method can't settle.
