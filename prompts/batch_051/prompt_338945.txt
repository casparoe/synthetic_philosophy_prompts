I've been going down a rabbit hole about disgust and sacredness the last few weeks, and I've ended up with a distinction I keep using but haven't seen anyone name. I call it contact-purity versus concept-purity. Contact-purity is the kind of violation where the wrong seems to come from actual or imagined physical contact: touching a corpse, eating something taboo, sleeping with someone the community says is off-limits. The response is washing, avoidance, quarantine, maybe a cleansing ritual. Concept-purity is different: nothing touches anything, but a meaning gets handled in the wrong way — a sacred text used as a doorstop, a flag burned as a joke, a prayer said in a mocking tone, a dead person's name used as a curse. People still reach for the language of defilement and desecration, and they still want the offender punished or excluded, but there is no contact to wash off.

I've been thinking the second kind is the real test for whether purity is a distinct moral value rather then just harm-avoidance about germs and bodies. Contact-purity could be explained away as overgeneralized contamination avoidance — disgust doing risk-detection for disease — but concept-purity seems to require that meanings or symbols themselves can be stained, and that the stain transfers to whoever uses them wrongly. Is this distinction already in the literature under a better name? Does it hold up — for example, is concept-purity just contact-purity at one remove, where the "contact" is with the word or image rather than the thing? And where does it break? Give me the strongest case of a purity violation that fits neither category, or a case where the two come apart in a way my description can't handle.
