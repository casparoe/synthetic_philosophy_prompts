Before I read yet another proposed resolution of the Fermi paradox, I want to settle what a resolution is actually being asked to do, because I keep seeing people defend a favourite story by leaning on one constraint at a time: someone says the galaxy is old, so life must be rare; someone else says rare Earth is special pleading; a third says the zoo hypothesis is unfalsifiable; a fourth says the real problem is not radio silence but the absence of self-replicating probes. I would rather have the full specification first, and only then read the candidates against it.

Please write me the desiderata for an adequate resolution of the Fermi paradox. For each requirement, give the consideration behind it: the precise feature of the observed silence it is meant to capture, such as the absence of technosignatures, megastructures, colonization, or artifacts; the fact that life arose on Earth fairly early in the planet's history; and the broader tension between an old galaxy and an empty one, together with the methodological or probabilistic results it must not contradict. I want a clear verdict on which requirements are non-negotiable and which are only the preferences of a particular camp, for example whether a resolution must preserve some version of the Copernican assumption or may deny it only by supplying a mechanism for Earth's specialness; whether it must be testable by present or near-future observations or only in principle; whether it must explain the absence of self-replicating probes specifically or only the absence of radio; and whether it must state what would have disconfirmed it. Most of all, I want to know whether the full set of requirements is jointly satisfiable: if some pair cannot be met by any single resolution, say which pair, and whether the right response is to relativize one of the two to a camp or to accept that no resolution currently on offer is fully satisfying. Then, briefly, run the main families of resolution, rare Earth, a Great Filter behind or ahead of us, the zoo hypothesis, the simulation idea, the claim that we are first, and the claim that technological civilizations simply do not expand, past the list, and tell me which one requirement does the most to exclude the currently popular candidates. Keep the scoring short; the requirements and their motivations are what I need, not the report card. Please write everything as continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way you would write an email or a letter.
