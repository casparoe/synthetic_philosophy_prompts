Spent the evening arguing with my friend about the Meno problem: if a true belief about the road to Larissa gets you there just as well as knowledge does, why is knowledge worth more? Between us we listed five answers. (1) The stability answer: knowledge is true belief tied down by an account of the reason why, so it stays put, while a bare true belief is flighty. (2) The reliability answer: knowledge is produced by a reliable route, so it would still be right in nearby cases, while a lucky true belief only happens to be right this once. (3) The understanding answer: knowledge gives you the why, not just the that, and understanding why is more valuable than merely having the right answer. (4) The credit answer: knowledge is a success for which you deserve credit, an achievement in a way a lucky true belief isn't. (5) The resilience answer: knowledge can be defended and survives questioning, while a bare true belief collapses as soon as someone challenges it. My friend is convinced that's everything and that any other answer will collapse into one of these five. I don't believe him — I think there's at least one more move. What is it? It has to be genuinely different, not a rewording, special case, or combination of the five. Then tell me honestly wether it's any good.

Give me your answer as a JSON object with fields: name (a short label for the answer), core_idea (one or two sentences), how_it_differs_from_the_five (why it is not a variant of any of them), and verdict (one sentence on whether it is actually any good).
