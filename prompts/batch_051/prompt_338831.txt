Over the last few weeks I've been keeping a running list of my friend's verdicts about having children, because she's weirdly systematic about it and I want to figure out what principle shes actually running. So far she's said all of the following. (1) If a couple knows that a child conceived now would have a painful illness and a life that is on balance not worth living — call it a -20 on the rough lifetime welfare scale she likes, where 0 is the worth-living line — she says it would be wrong to conceive now, and the wrongness doesn't depend on how much the parents want a child. (2) If a healthy, well-off couple is deciding whether to have any children at all, and any child they had would have a very good life (say +60, with only the ordinary frustrations of a normal life), she says having one is permitted and not having one is also permitted; there's no obligation either way. (3) If a couple doing IVF has to choose which of two embryos to implant, and both would have lives worth living, but one is predicted to have a +80 life while the other would have a +30 life because of a manageable but painful chronic condition, she says it would be wrong to choose the +30 one.

The case I actually care about (not idle curiosity, my partner and I are making a decision sort of like this) is: a couple is deciding whether to conceive a child who would have a life that is clearly worth living overall — say +40 — but who would go through several years of significant chronic pain in adolescence. The pain would be treatable but not fully, and the person would, by their own lights, be glad to have been born. What would my friend say about conceiving this child? I want a committed answer: lay out the two or three rules that fit her track record, say which one you think she's actually running and what verdict it gives on the new case, defend that choice, and tell me what further case would settle which rule she holds.
