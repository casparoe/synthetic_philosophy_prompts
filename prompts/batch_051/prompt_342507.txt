Here's one I've been turning over. Dennett's heterophenomenology tells the science of consciousness to treat subjects' reports as its data, but to interpret them as fictions via the intentional stance — staying neutral about whether the reports correspond to anything real inside. Then along come no-report paradigms: instead of asking people what they saw, you decode the content from fMRI or EEG, or let their own eye movements do the reporting. The selling point is that you've gotten rid of the very thing heterophenomenology built the method around. So my question is whether heterophenomenology survives this development. Does a no-report paradigm actually eliminate reports, or does it just relocate them to the instructions, the calibration trials, and the experimenters decision about what the neural signal is a signal of? And if it really does eliminate them, is that a refutation of heterophenomenology, or a change of subject to something else? I'd want the answer to name the specific commitment of heterophenomenology that no-report paradigms put pressure on, and say whether the method can absorb them or has to be modified.
