For a seminar on the logic of identity, I want a specification sheet for any adequate formal account of the Trinity, before I read the standard options. Take this passage from the Athanasian Creed, old translation revised, as a fixed constraint:

The Father is made of none: neither created, nor begotten.  
The Son is of the Father alone: not made, nor created: but begotten.  
The Holy Ghost is of the Father and of the Son: neither made, nor created, nor begotten: but proceeding.  
So there is one Father, not three Fathers: one Son, not three Sons: one Holy Ghost, not three Holy Ghosts.  
And in this Trinity none is afore, or after another: none is greater, or less than another.

The account must let these sentences say what they seem to say under a single coherent reading of “is,” not just paraphrase them into something weaker. I want the requirements: what any theory of how the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost can be distinct persons but one God has to deliver to count as adequate. For each requirement, give the exact creedal sentence or inference that motivates it; mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps; and say which single requirement does the most to exclude familiar approaches such as strict identity with three roles, relative identity, or the difference between Latin and Greek accounts of origin. If some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable in ordinary first-order logic with identity, say so explicitly — that is the part I most want to see. Keep the response to at most 800 words; the passage does not count.
