I keep going back and forth on a proposal for settling contract fights between a company and its union. Instead of an arbitrator picking a number, you run the Kalai–Smorodinsky bargaining solution mechanically. The disagreement point is the no-deal outcome: for the union, strike pay minus lost wages; for the company, profit from whatever it can still run during a strike. The feasible set is every wage-and-benefit package an independent auditor certifies the company can afford without going under. You compute each side's utopia point — the best the union could conceivably get while the company still gets at least its disagreement payoff, and the best the company could get while the union gets at least its disagreement payoff — then draw the ray from the disagreement point through that utopia point and pick the best feasible deal on the ray. Both sides end up getting the same fraction of their best-case gain over no-deal.

I dont want to know if its fair; treat it as a mechanism. If both sides understand the rule and care only about their own payoff, what do they start doing? Would a side ever want its own no-deal outcome to be worse (say, by skipping a strike fund), or is a better outside option always good for you here? Would the company try to get the auditor to certify fewer generous packages as feasible, so the union's utopia point comes down, and what would that do to the quality of the auditor's information? Where does it get gamed first, and can the holes be patched without just turning it back into an ordinary arbitrator?
