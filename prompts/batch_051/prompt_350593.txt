I'm a retired physical chemist who got into philosophy late, and I keep worrying at a practice that seems philosophically loaded: fan wikis and their canon rules. A well-run fan wiki states as flat fact that Sherlock Holmes lives at 221B Baker Street, and it will quietly correct you that something from a tie-in novel or an adaptation "isn't canon," as if there's an objective fact of the matter about a man who never existed and events that never happened. What does this practice presuppose? Does it commit the wiki to there being fictional objects — to Holmes existing in some sense, perhaps as an abstract artifact — so that sentences about him can be true or false? Or can the whole thing be paraphrased as "according to the Doyle stories," with no commitment to the man himself? I'd like you to reconstruct the practice's implicit theory of truth in fiction, but to be careful about where the practice strictly forces a commitment and where it's compatible with several rival views. Also, am I conflatting two questions — what makes "Sherlock Holmes" refer, and what makes something true in the fiction? And could a thoroughgoing antirealist about fictional characters still run the wiki's canon distinction in good conscience, or does the practice push them into claiming more than they'd like?
