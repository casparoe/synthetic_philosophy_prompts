ok so my philosophy club is doing this thing where we each write out our whole "view profile" on a topic before the meeting, no sources, and then everyone else gets to attack it. i got stuck with infinite ethics / population ethics, which is annoying because now i have way too many opinions about it. here's where ive landed:

- im a total utilitarian: the right action is whichever one produces the biggest total net well-being, and possible people count exactly as much as people who already exist.
- so i bite the bullet on the repugnant conclusion. a huge enough population of lives barely worth living beats a much smaller population of amazing lives, as long as the total sum is bigger.
- i also think there's a procreation asymmetry: bringing a miserable life into existence is wrong in a way that failing to bring a happy life into existence isn't. creating a happy life is nice, but i don't think we're ever obligated to do it.
- in infinite cases i think the total-sum approach breaks, since infinity plus a finite number is still infinity. so adding one happy life, or a billion, to an already infinite population doesn't actually make the world better.
- but i also think its insane to let that make us indifferent to finite suffering. if i could cure five actual people's chronic pain right now, the fact that the total future population might be infinite shouldn't make that not matter.
- and my friend keeps pushing this toy case: an infinite line of people all at welfare 10, versus the same line all at welfare 11. i want to say the second world is clearly better even though both totals are just "∞."

what i need is an audit of whether this package actually hangs together or whether im just switching which underlying principle i use depending on how the case feels. tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, rank the genuine ones by how bad they are, and say what i'd have to give up for each. also tell me which single revision would clean up the most. and please don't just give me a general overview of the standard positions; go through my actual bullet points.
