For an independent study project on vector space models of meaning, I want to settle on the criteria such a model should be judged by before I read much further, rather than absorbing the standards of whichever approach I happened to start with. The candidate requirements I have collected are these: (1) semantically similar words should lie close together under cosine similarity; (2) analogies should be recoverable as vector offsets, as when king − man + woman yields a vector near queen; (3) antonyms such as hot and cold should not count as highly similar merely because they occur in the same contexts; (4) a polysemous word such as bank should not be forced into a single point; (5) the meaning of a phrase should be obtainable from the meanings of its words by a regular compositional operation; (6) hypernymy—a dog being a kind of animal—should correspond to some geometric relation, perhaps a direction or a containment relation; and (7) the space should be isotropic, so that distances are not dominated by a few directions. Please write the desiderata: which of these are genuine requirements on an adequate theory of semantic structure, which are negotiable or merely the preferences of a particular approach, and which pairs, if any, cannot be jointly satisfied—each backed by the case or consideration behind it. Wherever you make a contested call, especially about what is negotiable or about an unsatisfiable pair, please say what argument or finding would change your mind or where your reasoning is most likely to fail. Write in American English.
