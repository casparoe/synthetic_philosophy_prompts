Retired electrophysiologist here—spent a career recording from midbrain dopamine neurons before I got into philosophy, and lately I've been reading the AI-suffering debate. The move that keeps bothering me is when people take a reinforcement learner's reward signal and just call it valence: negative reward is pain, positive reward is pleasure, and therefore a sufficiently capable RL agent can literally suffer. I'd like that analogy audited the way a referee would audit a sloppy mapping study. If reward is valence, what actually corresponds to what? Scalar reward to felt pleasure and pain, the policy update to learning-from-suffering, the discount factor to something like impatience? But in the brain reward is not one thing. Dopamine tracks a prediction error, which reads more like an update signal than a feeling. You can dissociate liking from wanting with a drug or a lesion, so a stimulus can drive choice without being enjoyed. Nociception also comes apart from the awful feeling part of pain. So my suspicion is that the AI-welfare person is quietly importing the idea that a scalar control signal is felt. Is that smuggling, or is it exactly the substantive claim? And if I rerun the mapping with RL reward as wanting, or as a mere control signal, what happens to the conclusion—does it collapse, or does suffering just relocate to some other layer of the agent?
