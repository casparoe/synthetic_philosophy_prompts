I have been thinking about a pattern in the way institutions try to distinguish genuine commitment from cheap talk, and I would like a philosophical diagnosis of it rather than an empirical survey.

Take three familiar examples. A religious order requires candidates to spend years in a novitiate, often under vows of poverty and obedience, before full admission. An elite military unit puts candidates through a selection course designed so that most fail or withdraw, and it treats having endured the course as evidence of dedication rather than merely of physical capacity. A fraternity requires initiates to undergo humiliations and exhausting tasks that bear no evident relation to what the chapter later does. In each case the institution apparently reasons that the costliness of the test is exactly what makes the result informative: anyone can say he is committed, but only the committed will pay the price.

Yet those same institutions also gather cheap, competence-tracking signals—applications, grade records, interviews, letters of recommendation—and treat those as serious evidence as well. That combination puzzles me. If the ordeal is informative precisely because a less-committed person would not pay its cost, what is the cheap screen adding? If the cheap screen is already a good test of the qualities wanted, why retain the ordeal at all?

What theory of signs, cost, and credibility is this doubled screening practice tacitly committed to? In particular, does it presuppose a version of the handicap principle—the costly-signaling idea that a signal honestly reveals an underlying trait only when it is too costly for someone lacking the trait to fake—or can it be fully explained instead as training, filtering by some independently valuable trait, or preference-formation, with no special signaling logic? I would like you to separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely sits well with, and to indicate where it is philosophically underdetermined. If an institution gave up the view that cost is what makes a declaration of commitment credible, what would it have to change about how it screens?
