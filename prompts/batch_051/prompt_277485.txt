I'm the curator of a county historical society and we're rewriting every label in our permanent exhibit. The board is split right down the middle. Half are Ranke people: they think the point of a history museum is to say what actually happened, full stop. The other half keep throwing Carr and Hayden White at me — the facts are always selected and arranged into a story, and every story is told from somebody's present point of view, so the what-actually-happened part hides a lot of choices. I need a procedure, not a referee.

Write me a reusable procedure my label writer and I can run in about an hour per label before anything goes to the board. I want it as a decision procedure: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a no, and where it hands the call to a person because it's a value judgment. It should separate the different kinds of claims a label makes — a date, a person's motives, what an event meant — because they probably shouldn't be hedged the same way. And tell me honestly where the procedure can't settle a question and a human has to.

Then run it on three labels we're actually stuck on: (a) an 1868 massacre where local oral history and the surviving court record disagree about who started it; (b) a founder's motives for donating the land, where the only source is his own diary; (c) a label about the daily life of an enslaved family reconstructed from purchase receipts and a single runaway ad. I want you to include the case where the procedure forces us to print something the board won't like, and say plainly what the procedure cannot do.
