I've been thinking about whether special relativity really forces the block-universe picture, and a colleague presented me with a version of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument that strikes me as too strong to leave alone. It goes like this:

(1) In special relativity, simultaneity is not frame-independent: for two spacelike-separated events, some inertial frames place one earlier, others place the other earlier, and still others place them as simultaneous, with no frame privileged.
(2) Take my present decision here-and-now and a supernova in Andromeda that my own frame regards as happening today. The two events are spacelike-separated, so there are frames in which I am deciding while the supernova is already burning, frames in which it has not yet burned, and frames in which the two are simultaneous.
(3) For an event to be "real now" just is for it to be simultaneous with my here-and-now.
(4) Whether an event is real cannot depend on a choice of inertial frame.
(5) So the supernova is real regardless of whether any given frame places it in my past, present, or future; its being "still to come" or "already over" is not an objective feature of the world.
(6) Therefore there is no objective now, and the block universe is the only coherent picture.

My colleague regards this as decisive. I cannot locate exactly where it goes wrong, and I hold two unresolved suspicions: that the trouble is somewhere in (3), and that the argument overreaches because it runs only for spacelike-separated events and says nothing about events inside my forward light cone. Please adjudicate: is the argument valid, and if it is not, which step fails?
