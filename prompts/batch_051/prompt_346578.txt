I have been reading W. E. B. Du Bois's writing on art, and his central claim will not leave me alone: art is never politically innocent, because even the work that calls itself pure is propaganda in a broader sense — it shapes what audiences find beautiful, valuable, or true, and the only honest question is what a given artwork is propaganda for. I find this line genuinely powerful. It is unified where my own views are patchy, and it turns the accusation of propaganda back on the person who demands purity, since that demand itself looks like a way of policing whose subjects and whose anger may count as beautiful. But I have a firm intuition that pushes the other way: when I sit with an instrumental piece I love, or an abstract painting whose whole subject is its own colour and surface, what matters seems to be exactly that the work is not for anything — it does not recruit me, persuade me, or serve a cause. I do not want to be told which side wins. Walk me through what it would take for the intuition to be the thing I should give up, what it would take for Du Bois's claim to be amended or abandoned, and whether there is a reading of 'propaganda' on which both survive. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Use British English.
