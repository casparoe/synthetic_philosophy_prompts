Alright, imagine two people who have been arguing for a year about whether ordinary insect pest control is morally fine or morally much more serious than people treat it. One is a precautionary insect-welfarist: she thinks that because lots of insects plausibly have a felt life of some sort, and we kill them in ridiculous numbers, routine things like spraying aphids in the garden, setting ant bait in the kitchen, or fogging for mosquitoes deserve real moral weight, and we should accept genuine inconvenience or cost to avoid insect deaths. The other is an ordinary-practice defender: he thinks that, given the deep uncertainty about insect sentience and how thin an insect's psychological life could possibly be, killing pests is morally negligible in nearly all cases, and current control methods are basically permissable. They decide to write up a joint statement of their adversarial collaboration. Write the document they should have produced.

The document needs: a numbered agreed-claims section, where the claims are neither vacuous nor slanted and both sides would actually sign; then a disagreements table with rows for the disagreement, the wording each side would accept for its own view, and a note on whether each disagreement is substantive or partly verbal. Then list the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, give one concrete test, experiment, intervention, or comparison case that both sides would agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts about it. Keep the tests realistic and something both sides could agree to without specialist literature or citations; the document shouldn't cite or reference any sources, it should stand on its own reasoning.

At the very end, add a short note saying how confident you are in the claims and wordings in your draft, and pick out the single point in the whole document that you are least sure about.
