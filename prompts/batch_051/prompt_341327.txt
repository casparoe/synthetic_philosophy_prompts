I'm putting together a handout on the tool-analysis in Being and Time and I want to push past the usual glosses. Here's my running list of what the broken hammer is supposed to reveal:
(1) that readiness-to-hand normally withdraws, and only becomes thematic when it fails;
(2) that a failure lights up the equipmental whole — the workshop, the project, the network of in-order-to assignments — rather than just the single tool;
(3) that the un-ready-to-hand shows up in the three modes of conspicuousness, obtrusiveness, and obstinacy;
(4) that the present-at-hand is a derivative mode that emerges only when the tool is peeled out of its referential context.

I keep feeling there's a fifth point the breakdown is doing that none of these four captures. Give me one more thing the hammer-breaking analysis reveals or accomplishes, that is genuinely different from (1)-(4) — not a rewording, combination, or special case — and say briefly what it adds to a reading of that part of the book, plus wether you think it's actually in the text or just something people say about it.
