so the smartest person i know who is still a practicing christian keeps saying petitionary prayer is just like asking a friend for help. you tell them what you need, they might not have realized, or they might need to hear it from you, and the asking itself is good for the relationship even beyond whether you get the thing. she uses this to defuse the old puzzle that praying to a god who already knows what you need and never changes his mind is pointless: the asking still does work, just like with friends, so stop treating God like a vending machine.

i don't buy it but i can't yet say exactly why, and i want to know how far this analogy actually holds. give me the correspondence table first — what's supposed to map onto God, onto the favor, onto the friend not knowing, onto the relationship, and so on. then go through where it breaks when you swap in an omniscient and immutable God, and tell me which breaks actually matter for the conclusion and which are just superficial. in particular i want to know whether the analogy is quietly importing the very features that make asking a friend useful — ignorance, being moved, maybe changing your mind a bit — and whether the line that "the asking is good for the relationship" can survive once those are gone. if the analogy can't carry it, is there a better one people should be reaching for instead, or does the conclusion just collapse?
