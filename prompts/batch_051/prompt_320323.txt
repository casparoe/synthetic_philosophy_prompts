Here is a parallel I have been unable to decide about, between self-amending rules and self-reference in logic and computation. Consider a Nomic-style system: a finite list of rules, among them a rule specifying the procedure by which the rules, including that very rule, may be changed. Call the amendment rule R. Suppose the players use the procedure prescribed by R to replace R with a new rule. At the moment the replacement takes effect, the authority for the change appears to come from the very rule that the change destroys; after the change, the act no longer has an operative rule behind it, yet if the act was never authorized, R was never replaced. This looks to me like the familiar shape of a sentence that says of itself that it is false, or a program that takes its own source code as input. The tempting translation is: ruleset as formal system, amendment as derivation, R as a fixed point, validity as something like truth.

But I can also see reasons to think the resemblance is only verbal. A rule is a directive, not a truth-bearer; an amendment occurs at a time and through a procedure, whereas a self-referential sentence is paradoxical all at once; and an amendment clause can amend itself and the system simply moves on, which the Liar sentence cannot do.

I would like to know whether this is a genuine identity of structure or a loose analogy. Specifically: if I attempted to make the mapping precise, what would have to be true of rules and amendments — and what would have to correspond to truth, to provability, and to the moment of change — before the fixed-point and diagonal results from logic and computation could be transferred? And if the analogy were taken at its word, what would it predict about a self-amending ruleset that in fact fails — for example, that it should be able to encode a contradiction, or that some amendment's validity should be undecidable from inside the system? I am less interested in the answer to those toy predictions than in what their failure or success would show about why legal self-reference tends to be harmless even though logical self-reference is not.
