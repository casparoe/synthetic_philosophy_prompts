Okay here's a thing I keep coming back to. Suppose you could sit Herodotus down in front of a modern internet and just let him go for a while. The bit I'd most want to watch him react to is the whole pseudohistory ecosystem: Atlantis hunters, ancient aliens people, Fomenko's New Chronology, all of it. I know there's no right answer, but I want a serious guess, argued from what we actually know about how he worked in the Histories. He's always reporting conflicting accounts and saying who claims what, he ranks things by whether he saw them himself or only heard them, and he keeps the wild stories in while sort of shrugging about whether he buys them. So would he read the ancient aliens and lost-civilization crowd as basically doing his own thing, collecting travellers' tales and weighing them against each other, or would he think they've dropped the actual method, the part where you tell the reader your source and how much credit you give it? And do you think his reaction to Fomenko, where the whole timeline of antiquity is supposedly a giant fake, would be different from his reaction to the claim that Atlantis was real and we all just forgot? I'm after how he'd reason, not just what he'd conclude — dont just tell me he'd be horrified or intrigued.
