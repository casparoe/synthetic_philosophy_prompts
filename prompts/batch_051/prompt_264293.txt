In my high school philosophy seminar, we have been discussing whether AI alignment is solvable in principle, and I find myself torn between a theory and an intuition. The theory is what I have been calling specification realism: if a person or group can state what they value as a function from possible outcomes to scores, then aligning an AI is in principle just the problem of making the AI optimize that function, and any remaining failures are engineering problems rather than conceptual ones. The intuition is that no fixed specification can anticipate everything that matters; in a sufficiently novel situation, the AI will follow the letter of the specification and produce an outcome that the people who wrote the specification would reject. I do not want a verdict. I want to know what would justify giving up the intuition as a confusion about what solvable-in-principle is supposed to cover, what would justify weakening the theory so that it no longer claims in-principle solvability, and whether there is a reading of in principle on which both can be maintained.
