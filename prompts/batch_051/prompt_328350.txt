Lately I have been reading about heterophenomenology, the approach to studying consciousness that takes subjects' verbal reports about their experience as its data while claiming to remain officially agnostic about whether those reports track a real private stream that a subject might describe accurately or inaccurately. Its defenders say this is the virtue of the method: the investigator assembles what is often called the heterophenomenological world, an inventory of the objects and properties the subject seems to be conscious of, and then treats that world as something to be explained rather than as an authority to be trusted or a fiction to be debunked. A friend in my seminar insists that this neutrality is real and marks the difference between heterophenomenology, naive introspectionism, and unsophisticated eliminativism. I am less convinced. It seems to me that the moment one reserves all real ontology for the third-person explanatory account and treats the subject's reported world as the investigator's construction, one has already stacked the deck toward the conclusion that there is no inner presentation to report, or that if there is one it does no explanatory work.

I would like to know whether that suspicion can be made into a testable question rather than left as a standoff over metaphors. Concretely: design the strongest test you can of the claim that heterophenomenology is methodologically neutral between first-person realism and its eliminativist or dispositionalist rivals, a test that would discriminate between a method that merely postpones the ontological question and one that surreptitiously answers it. Your test may be an experiment, a comparison of research programs, or a historical argument about what the method has actually delivered, but it must be specific enough that we could say in advance which result would count for and against the neutrality claim. Then be honest about the residue: after your best possible result, what part of the quarrel would remain purely conceptual, so that no experimental outcome could settle it, and what a clean positive result for neutrality would and would not show?
