Analyze the 2013 Rana Plaza garment factory collapse in Savar, Bangladesh, through the lens of mutually beneficial exploitation, and then give an honest account of where that lens breaks down. I understand mutually beneficial exploitation roughly as follows: a transaction can leave both parties better off than they would have been under their next-best alternative, yet still be exploitative because of the background conditions under which it takes place, the terms of the deal, or the distribution of its gains. The workers who made clothing in that building had chosen factory work over worse rural alternatives, and the factories and international brands profited from their labor, so there is a real sense in which the arrangement was mutually beneficial. The facts I have in mind are these: the day before the collapse, serious cracks appeared in the building; a bank and shops on the lower floors closed; garment workers were nonetheless directed to report for work the next morning; the eight-story structure collapsed during the workday, killing more than a thousand people. I want a careful mapping rather than a denunciation. Who exactly are the parties to the putatively mutually beneficial exchange, and what did each gain and give up? On the theory's own terms, what is the wrong, if there is one, in the employment relationship, in the decision to send workers into a visibly damaged building, and in the position of the international brands that did not directly employ the victims? Which features of the episode does the theory explain well, and which does it misdescribe or pass over in silence? Finally, tell me what the episode shows about the theory itself: a confirmation, an anomaly, a limit, or a case it was never designed to handle.
