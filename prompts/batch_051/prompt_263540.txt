The standard argument for counting brain death as death was that the brain is the body's master integrator—lose it and the organism stops functioning as an integrated whole and falls apart into a collection of organs. But there are now well-documented cases of brain-dead bodies on ventilators doing surprisingly organism-like things for months: healing wounds, mounting immune responses, regulating temperature, growing, even sustaining a pregnancy to term. Does the master-integrator argument for brain death survive that, or does it fall apart? Which premise exactly takes the hit, and is there a repaired version that still draws the line at brain death, or does this push you toward a higher-brain/personhood criterion (or back to the heart)?
