I'm putting together a little anthology of political-economy-flavored satire and I need a piece for it. Could you write a mock job advertisment—like one of those aggressively cheerful startup careers pages or a old-timey skilled-labor want ad, your pick—set in a near-future world of whole brain emulations in roughly Robin Hanson's Age of Em sense: minds get scanned, copied, and run on hardware at whatever speed somebody is willing to pay for. The company is hiring one extremely competent professional—or rather ten thousand copies of that same person to do the work of a whole department.

The ad should stay deadpan all the way through, and the jokes should come from the actual logic of em labor markets, not just generic AI-worker gags: copies underbid each other until wages fall to the cost of running the hardware, subjective and objective time drift apart when your speed gets throttled, death becomes kind of an HR issue because you can be restored from a backup (except when you can't), and nobody can quite say whether a copy is you or just an employee that happens to share your memories. Maybe include a benefits section or some fine print that quietly takes a stand on whether a spun-down copy was ever a person. Aim for about 450 words, hard cap 500. It should be funny to someone who has thought about Hanson's economics and still legible to someone who hasn't.
