I'm putting together a little sorting quiz for the first meeting of a reading group on the ethics of belief, and I want it to work on people who haven't read the Clifford–James material yet. What I'd like is a decision tree, or a short scoring quiz with a key if the tree gets to unwieldy, that starts from very concrete questions and ends by assigning a person to a named position in the debate. Please don't build it by working back from the famous labels; work out the logical space first, then locate the standard positions in it, and make the thing sort people by what they actually think rather than what they'd call themselves. The branch questions should be cases or claims someone can answer without knowing any of the jargon. Use at least these two as anchor cases: the shipowner who talks himself out of his doubts until he sincerely believes his unsound ship is safe (Clifford's sort of case), and the sort of case James liked where believing a thing can help make it true, like a climber's confidence in a forced leap. The tree should turn on these dividing questions: whether evidence is the only thing that can properly justify an attitude or whether practical considerations can do so too; whether we can form beliefs simply by deciding to; whether raising the practical stakes can lower how much evidence is needed for full belief or only affect how confidently one is willing to act on it; and whether 'faith' is a distinct kind of commitment rather than ordinary belief. Make sure these leaves show up: a strict Cliffordian evidentialism, a Jamesian permissivism, a two-attitude pragmatist view (pragmatic reasons can justify acceptance but not belief), a stronger pragmatist view (pragmatic reasons can justify belief itself), and a fideist position. If some combination of answers is unoccupied by any live position, say so and say why nobody is there: incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag any leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate and say why. The audience is bright undergraduates, so keep it to about eight to twelve leaves and define any technical terms as you go. Please write the response in British English, and as continuous prose only: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. Write it like an email or letter.
