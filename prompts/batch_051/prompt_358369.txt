Every few months the same fight breaks out in my rationalist-adjacent group chat and it never converges. One side says AI safety people have an obvious blind spot: if we're going to build systems that might be sentient, or that might contain sentient subprocesses, then we have a live obligation to account for their welfare now, and it's negligent to treat "mindcrime" or suffering subsystems as a joke until we have proof. The other side says this is exactly the kind of motte-and-bailey that gets alignment defunded: nobody can even define sentience, the probabilities people throw around are wild-ass guesses, and adding an unmeasurable welfare criterion to an already impossible safety problem mostly launders anthropomorphism into policy. Both sides have read the same posts about substrate-independence and moral uncertainty, and if anything that's made it worse: each side now uses "we have to act under uncertainty" as a cudgel against the other.

I'm trying to figure out what kind of disagreement this actually is, because after two years it doesn't look like either side is updating. Is it a hidden empirical bet about timelines and the base rate of valence in ML systems? Is it a disagreement about the burden of proof for consciousness claims—one side treating false negatives as the expensive error, the other treating false positives as the expensive error? Is it a clash between roughly whole-brain/global-workspace-flavored criteria and more computational-functionalist criteria where each side has picked a different reference class without saying so? Or is it, at bottom, a values/priorities dispute about how to allocate scarce effort under deep uncertainty, dressed up as a factual argument?

My hunch is that it's mostly the burden-of-proof thing plus a disagreement about which question is even being asked—one side asking "should we plan for probable digital minds?" and the other asking "should this change what alignment labs do this quarter?" But I want a real diagnosis, not just "it's complicated." Given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress here: a specific empirical result, a sharper concept, a formal result about acting under consciousness uncertainty, a cleaner separation of the questions—or should we expect this dispute to be permanently stalled because the two sides have different priors that no feasible evidence will move?
