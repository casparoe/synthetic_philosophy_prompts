I'm a journalist who covers philosophy of mind and cognitive science, and I've been asked to lead a one-off seminar for upper-level undergraduates in a science-writing program. I want to give them a take-home essay question built around a specific passage. The passage is from Thomas Henry Huxley's 1874 essay *On the Hypothesis that Animals are Automata, and its History*. In it, Huxley claims that the feeling we have of initiating a voluntary action does not play the causal role it seems to; instead, that feeling is a kind of collateral product or indication of the neural events that actually produce the movement. He does not deny that the feeling exists, only that it is the mover. Write an essay question that asks students to interpret this passage, then test its central claim against at least one contemporary idea about consciousness as a user illusion, and apply it to a concrete case—say, deciding to reach for a cup. Then provide a model answer of around 700 words and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B by naming the specific moves each must contain, including at least two common misunderstandings that should cost marks.
