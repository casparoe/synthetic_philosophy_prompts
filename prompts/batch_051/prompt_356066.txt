I've got a half-baked theory I've been calling "slice discounting" and I want an honest take on whether it's actually a theory or just me mapping my job onto philosophy. I'm a software engineer, so I think in terms of time slices and discount curves. Standard hyperbolic discounting says I weigh the near future too much. Fine. But that still frames weakness as a values problem: I want X at t1, Y at t2, and the weights are off. I think akrasia is more like a permissions bug. When the present slice runs the decision, it treats the future slice's valuations as real but not authoritative, like a child account on a server that can read files but can't push changes. The moment it wins, the later slice inherits the commit and has to reconcile. So the weak-willed person isn't choosing against their better judgment exactly — they're choosing under a temporarily different judgment that has root access. Aristotle seems to me to describe this in Nicomachean Ethics, Book VII (Ross translation) when he says: "the incontinent man, knowing that what he does is bad, does it as a result of passion, while the continent man, knowing that his appetites are bad, refuses on account of his rational principle to follow them." That reads like passion is not overriding knowledge but changing which process is executing. Is this just hyperbolic discounting with extra steps, is there already a name for it, and what's the most obvious case where my theory breaks? Also: would Aristotle's account actually cash out as a permissions bug, or am I reading my job into him?
