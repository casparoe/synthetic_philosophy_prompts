I'm a philosophy of biology PhD student and I keep tripping over what looks like two different uses of "function" in the literature, and I can't tell if I'm inventing a distinction or if it's a recognized thing. In some papers the function of a trait seems to be whatever effect it was selected for in the past: the heart's function is pumping blood, and the sound it makes is not a function even though hearts reliably make it, because that sound never got anything selected. In other places people seem happy to call any contribution a part makes to the overall working of a system a function: so if I'm explaining how the circulatory system produces the lub-dub that doctors listen to, the heart's making that sound does count as a function relative to that capacity. Or take a protein that originally spread because it detoxified one compound and now also degrades something else: some people say the new activity is just a side effect, others call it a function too because the cell's chemistry depends on it. I know this is somewhere near the exaptation stuff, but that's about co-opted traits, and here both sides can agree something got co-opted; the arguement is about whether the co-opted use deserves the word "function." Does this split have a name of its own, and how exactly do the two camps draw the line? I'm not just after a label — I want to know whether these are really two rival analyses of one concept or two different concepts that happen to share a word, and where the hard cases (vestiges, novel uses, broken parts) land on each.
