Consider two people who stop working in their forties and live on the same unearned income. One draws the income from an inherited trust fund; the other receives it as a universal basic income. In ordinary judgment, the first is usually treated as fortunate and free, while the second is treated as idle and a burden on others. This is curious, because the first person's income depends on a particular benefactor's past choices while the second person's is unconditional and guaranteed by a general rule, so the first looks, if anything, more dependent. What, if anything, tracks and justifies this asymmetry: the voluntariness of the source, the recipient's relation to it, the fact that public money is raised by taxation, or something else? Give me the strongest principle you can build, test it against a nearby case in which a living parent funds the same idleness on the explicit condition that the recipient never work, and say whether the asymmetry should be kept, reversed, or abandoned.
