I am a third-year undergraduate currently taking an animal ethics seminar, and a case that came up in discussion has split the room. A student in the class volunteers at a sanctuary for farmed animals, where a small flock of hens was rescued from an egg operation. The hens are not bred, sold, or confined in the way farmed hens are, and the sanctuary provides them veterinary care; but they still lay eggs, and unless a volunteer takes the eggs home the sanctuary discards them. I hold a fairly strict vegan position and have generally treated the consumption of animal products as off-limits, yet I have difficulty saying what, exactly, would be wrong about eating one of those eggs. The hen is not brought into existence for it, is not deprived of anything she seems to want, and the egg will be thrown out if nobody takes it. Another student disagrees. In her view, the egg is still a product of the animal's body, so consuming it treats the hen as an instrument from which something is harvested; she also thinks that visibly eating the eggs in front of staff, volunteers, or visitors normalizes the idea that animal products are food, and that this normalization feeds back into the broader demand for commodity eggs.

I would put my own credence at roughly 0.3 that eating such eggs is permissible all things considered, while my classmate is at about 0.8, and we cannot locate where our disagreement actually lives. Please give me your own actual credence in the following proposition: it is morally permissible, all things considered, for a person who otherwise rejects animal exploitation to eat the eggs of healthy, well-treated rescued hens at a sanctuary, when the eggs would otherwise be discarded and no one else has a competing need for them. I do not want a bare answer of the form that it depends; I want a number together with the decomposition that produces it. Break the question into the sub-claims your credence depends on, revising or expanding my list as you see fit: whether the wrongness of consuming animal products is located entirely in the causal chain of production such as breeding, confinement, and killing, or also in the bare act of consuming something an animal produced; whether eating the egg expresses a problematic stance toward animals or shifts social norms in a way that makes demand for commodified animal products more likely; whether the hen has any morally relevant interest in these eggs or their disposal; and whether the fact that the eggs would otherwise be wasted has independent moral weight. Assign a rough probability to each sub-claim, explain how they combine into your final number including any conditional dependencies, and then tell me which single sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would do the most to move you on it.
