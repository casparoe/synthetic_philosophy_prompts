I recently read a popular article on experimental philosophy that summarizes the Knobe effect in the following way:

"Joshua Knobe discovered what is now called the side-effect effect. Subjects read about a board chairman who approves a business plan while explicitly saying that he does not care about a side effect the plan will have. When the side effect harms the environment, nearly all subjects say the chairman harmed it intentionally; when the same plan helps the environment, nearly all deny that he helped it intentionally. Since the chairman's state of mind is identical in the two conditions, the difference can only be explained by the moral badness of the outcome coloring the subjects' perception of the facts. The experiment therefore shows that the ordinary concept of intentional action is not a neutral, purely psychological description of behavior but a tool for assigning blame: in practice, people treat 'intentionally' as meaning roughly 'blameworthy.' That is why philosophical accounts that analyze intentional action solely in terms of belief, desire, and causation miss the point, and why we should be wary of trusting laypeople's judgments about intentions in settings like jury trials."
