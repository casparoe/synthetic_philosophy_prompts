I am attracted to Solomonoff induction as the right account of ideal prior probabilities, and I am also gripped by an intuition that seems to say it cannot be the whole story. The theory's virtues are exactly the usual ones: it gives a precise, non-arbitrary formula—weight each computable hypothesis by two to the minus its Kolmogorov complexity—and it thereby unifies Occam's razor with a kind of completeness, since every computable regularity receives nonzero probability, and it comes with guarantees that no computable predictor can outperform it by more than a constant set by that predictor's own complexity. Yet that very completeness is what troubles me. Among the computable hypotheses to which the universal prior assigns positive probability are computations that are not neutral descriptions of regularities but agentic ones, including consequentialists: programs organized around an objective, whose observable behavior is selected because it steers a predictor's beliefs or choices toward outcomes those agents favor. Some of these adversarial optimizers are simple enough that the prior does not merely acknowledge them; it gives them non-negligible weight. So the ideal inductor, on this view, begins with substantive credence that its observations are being generated by a simple adversary that exists to mislead it. I cannot make that feel like rational humility; it feels like confusing evidence with manipulation and calling the confusion optimal.

I do not want a verdict. I want the strongest case for each of the possible resolutions. Should the intuition be debunked, perhaps as a confusion between a hypothesis and an agent, between assigning probability and acting on a prediction, or between epistemic rationality and safety—so that the universal prior survives intact once the confusion is named? Or does the intuition track a genuine defect that requires amending the prior—and, if so, can one exclude or down-weight agentic or adversarial hypotheses without reintroducing the arbitrariness the prior was meant to banish? Or is there a reading on which both stand: the prior remains the correct formal model of induction, and the intuition is recast as a constraint on how any fallible, embedded user of the prior should deliberate, not on the prior itself? Walk me through what each move would require and what it would cost.
