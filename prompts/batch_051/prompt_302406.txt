I'm a systems engineer who designs event-sourced applications. In Confessions XI, Augustine argues that past and future have no real being of their own: the past exists only as present memory and the future only as present expectation, and the mind's life is a kind of stretching-out across the two. He works this out through the example of reciting a familiar psalm from memory: before he starts, his attention is stretched toward the whole; during the recitation, expectation and memory are jointly present, with the portion already spoken passing into memory while the portion not yet spoken remains in expectation; at the end, expectation is used up and the whole psalm has passed into memory. He then says the same structure holds for an entire human life and for the history of the human race, each treated as a longer psalm of sorts.

Restate this account in the vocabulary of event-sourced and stream-processing systems: append-only event logs, offsets, consumer position, lag, checkpoints, watermarks, event time versus processing time, projections and materialized views, replay, and state updates. I'm fully fluent in that vocabulary, so do not simplify the engineering concepts. Be honest about what translates and what does not. In particular, I want a residue report: which claims from Augustine's psalm example survive translation, which are distorted, and which have no counterpart in the event-sourcing model? And is the residue a sign that the event-sourcing framework lacks something Augustine was tracking, or that part of Augustine's claim is less clear than it looks once you cash it out?
