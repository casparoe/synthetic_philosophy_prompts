The topic is whether there's actually a moral difference between lying and misleading someone, or whether that distinction is just something lawyers and PR people hide behind. Don't explain the topic to me — give me a plan for how to teach it to people whose ethics training so far is basically 'get the facts right and don't fabricate.' I'm fairly sure the first thing to establish is the gap between what a sentence literally asserts and what a listener is entitled to take away from it; is that really where I have to start, and what depends on getting that in place before everything else? Tell me what that example has to have and what it has to lack, and whether I should ditch the old 'used car's floor panels are rust-free' move — if so, why. Where in the sequence should I head each of those off, and how? Finally, give me one closing question I can put to them to see whether the idea actually landed, and tell me what I should deliberately leave out at this level — and say so to the room.
