Since I retired from a career in chemistry, I have been attending a philosophy discussion group, and I would like a second opinion about a dispute I keep losing. Our example was the coin mechanism in a vending machine. It settles into one internal state just in case an actual quarter, rather than a slug, has passed through, and the rest of the machine is wired to treat that state as authorization to dispense. A younger member of the group argued that this state means, quite literally, that a quarter has been inserted: it reliably tracks the relevant condition, and it is used downstream exactly as if it carried that content. I resisted. I wanted to say that the mechanism's state means something only because we assigned it that meaning, whereas my own thought, when I watch a quarter drop, is about the quarter in a manner not merely borrowed from a designer's purposes. Pressed to say what this distinction amounts to beyond a difference in origin, I could not. What I chiefly want to know is whether the distinction I was drawing is real, and whether it should be allowed to do any work in how I speak about devices and minds. Can you help me think it through?
