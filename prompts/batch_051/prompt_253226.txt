For a metaphysics reading group, write a decision tree that sorts a reader into a position on what the grandfather paradox shows about backward time travel. Begin with a single yes/no question: whether a time traveler can reach the past at all. Every later branch node should be a concrete claim or miniature case that someone can answer without already knowing the positions — for instance, whether 'changing the past' is coherent, whether a traveler who kills her grandfather before her parent is conceived disappears or persists, whether such a killing is logically impossible or merely something that will not happen, and whether the original timeline survives alongside an altered one. The leaves should include at least five positions: the impossibility view; the fixed-past or consistency view, on which the traveler simply does not kill him and his survival was already part of the fixed past; the branching-timelines view; the Novikov-style self-consistency view, on which contradictory histories have probability zero; and, if you can separate it, a changeable-past or hypertime view. Build the tree from the space of answers rather than from a list of labels: work out which combinations of answers are coherent, locate each position in that space, note any unoccupied combinations and diagnose whether they are empty because they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and give each leaf a one-sentence statement of its most awkward commitment. Flag any leaves the tree cannot separate with yes/no questions and say what additional question would separate them. If a clean tree would distort the positions, or some leaf cannot be isolated without smuggling in assumptions, say so plainly rather than forcing the structure.
