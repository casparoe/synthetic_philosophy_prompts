I'm a novelist, currently writing a book where a married couple has a long-running fight about whether to sign up for cryonics — one of them is a Bay Area rationalist type who thinks its an obvious positive expected value bet, the other is a biologist who thinks the whole thing is a Pascal's mugging run through a midlife crisis. I've read enough forum threads to know the two positions mostly talk past each other, and I want to give both a fair treatment on the page. So here's what I want: pretend a committed cryonics advocate and a committed cryonics skeptic actually went through a good-faith adversarial collaboration, and write the document they'd produce. I don't need the dramatized argument itself; I need the thing they could both sign.

Please give me three sections. First, a numbered list of agreed claims about what cryonics actually involves, what is and isn't known, what current preservation can and can't do, what the costs are, and which uncertainties are real — written so that neither side would object to any word. Second, a disagreements table. Each row should state a question in neutral terms, then give the advocate's answer and the skeptic's answer, each phrased so a real holder of that view would say "yes, that's what I mean." Cover at least these disputes: whether expected-value reasoning with wildly unknown revival probabilities is decision-relevant; whether the main uncertainty is technical revival or institutional survival over decades or centuries; what the word "revival" has to mean for the bet to pay off — the original person, a close-enough copy, some continuer of memory or values; and wether the alternative uses of the life-insurance premiums change the decision. Third, the cruxes. For each crux, give me the concrete observation, experiment, or historical event both sides would agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts. I'm especially interested in these: successful vitrification and revival of a whole mammal in a lab; whether memory-relevant structure can survive the ischemic interval before perfusion; the long-term survival record of cryonics organizations under financial stress; and a worked small-probability large-payoff wager both sides agree is structurally analogous to cryonics, with each side's prediction about how a rational agent should handle it.

Finally, if part of their disagreement is verbal, say exactly which part and why — I suspect they sometimes mean different things by "a rational bet" and maybe even by "revival," and if a stipulative definition would dissolve one of those fights, write the definition they'd both accept.
