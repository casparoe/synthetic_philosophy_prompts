So I stayed up way to late last night arguing with a friend about Szasz. His version wasn't the dumb caricature: he wasn't saying people don't suffer, just that calling it an illness is the mistake. Real medicine, he kept saying, deals with lesions, infections, malfunctions you can point to or at least assay. But depression and schizophrenia get diagnosed from a checklist of symptoms, and then the label gets used to explain the exact same symptoms it was built from. I said that doesn't make the category fake — a disease doesn't stop being real just because you haven't found its mechanism yet; epilepsy was diagnosed purely by symptoms long before the EEG. He said that's different because nobody ever doubted epilepsy was a brain event, whereas the whole fight in mental illness is whether the category picks out a natural kind or just a label for problems in living. Round and round.

So here's my question. If you had to design the best empirical test of his claim — not that the suffering is fake, but that "mental illness" is a misleading or bad medical category — what would you actually measure or manipulate? What result would make you more confident Szasz is basically right, what result would make you more confident he's basically wrong, and what part of the dispute would still be left standing no matter how the data came out because it's really about what counts as an illness at all?
