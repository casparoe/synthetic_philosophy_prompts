I write about science and ideas, and I’m working on a piece about the “existential void” people describe in depression and after burnout. Here’s an argument I keep running into, roughly in this form, and I want to stress-test it.

(1) The void in existential emptiness isn’t just a low mood; it presents itself as an awareness of something missing.
(2) If a state is an awareness of something missing, then there has to be a determinate thing it is an awareness of — the way hunger is an awareness of food and grief is an awareness of a particular person being gone.
(3) But in existential emptiness there is no determinate thing missing: the person usually can’t say what it is, and it isn’t a job, a partner, a god, or a goal.
(4) So the state isn’t, after all, an awareness of anything missing.
(5) Therefore the void should be read deflationarily — as an objectless mood, with about as much “revelatory” force as a headache — and talk of it disclosing something about one’s life is a mangling of the facts.

The conclusion feels too quick, but I can’t locate the slide. Is the argument valid, and if not, which step fails, under what reading? Wherever you land, tell me what would change your mind about it — which argument would flip you, or where your own reasoning is most likely to give out.

Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no intro, no wrap-up.
