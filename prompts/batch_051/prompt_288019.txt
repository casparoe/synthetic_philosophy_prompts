I run a discussion group attached to a rationalist meetup: the regulars have read the Sequences, talk fluently about map and territory, compression, and levels of abstraction, but have little formal philosophy. Next month I have ninety minutes and a whiteboard to get them from the idea that the map is not the territory to Dennett's three stances — the physical stance, the design stance, and the intentional stance — together with the real-patterns claim underneath them: a pattern deserves to be called real when adopting it buys you predictive power, not when it matches some privileged bottom-level description.

Plan the session rather than giving me the explanation itself. I want the order in which the pieces must be introduced and why each depends on the previous one. I want the single running example that should carry the whole thing, with the properties it must have and the properties it must not have; in particular, tell me whether the thermostat or the chess-playing machine is the better carrying example and why the loser would actively mislead this audience. Then give me the misconception map for these specific people. My guesses are that they will identify the intentional stance with the map and the physical stance with the territory, that they will treat a pattern as real only when they can point to a mechanism, and that they will arrange the three stances as a ladder on which the intentional stance is the useful fiction to be outgrown. State where in the sequence each should be headed off before it hardens. End with one question I can put to the room that would show me whether they have understood the predictive criterion of pattern-hood rather than merely learned to sort explanations into three boxes, and tell me what I should explicitly refuse to settle at this level.
