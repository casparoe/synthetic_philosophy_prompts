I've been going back and forth between enactivism and representationalism for a course I'm TAing, and after a long argument with me and my officemate I think I've landed on a position I want pressure-tested. I call it "frozen affordances." The basic idea is that a representation isn't a picture or a sentence in the head; it's an affordance that has been detached from the situation that originally gave rise to it and kept around in an action-ready state. A squirrel that returns to where it buried a nut isn't consulting an internal map, it's re-activating a readiness to dig that outlasted the burying context. A person planning a hike the night before is running through turn-left, climb-over, stop-at-the-creek readinesses with the world switched off. So far I realize this sounds like enactivism with extra steps.

The part I actually want checked is the next move: I want to say that this freezing is what makes something count as a representation. So representationalism isn't false, it's just a special case — representations are affordances that outlived their niche. Enactivists are right that online, situated cognition is the basic kind, and representationalists are right that once an affordance gets frozen it can be combined, manipulated, and reused in ways that look exactly like classical representation. My question: is this a genuine middle position or have I just relabeled the online/offline distinction? Give me the three or four strongest objections, tell me which if any are fatal, and give me the clearest example of a cognitive phenomenon my view handles worse than both of the standard views.
