You are a journalist who covers philosophy and the human sciences, writing an in-depth essay for an educated but non-specialist readership. Start by brainstorming the strongest arguments on both sides of the following controversy, then compose the essay itself.

Write an essay on whether universalizability reasoning in ethics—the move of asking “What if everyone did that?”—is a substantive moral test or merely a consistency check that formalizes judgments we already hold. Examine the idea as it appears in Kant’s categorical imperative, in R. M. Hare’s claim that moral judgments are universalizable prescriptions, and in Peter Singer’s use of the test in practical contexts such as distance and need. Address the main objections: the choice of how to describe an action can rig the test; almost any maxim can pass if stated specifically enough; universalizing a harmless act can produce absurd consequences; and particularists may reject the demand for universal rules altogether. In the final section, take a definite position: does the “what if everyone did that?” question provide real moral leverage, or does it mostly add rhetorical pressure? Defend your verdict rather than ending on a non-committal note.
