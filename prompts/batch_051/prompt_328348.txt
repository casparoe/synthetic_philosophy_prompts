I'm a junior analyst on a country-risk team at a commercial insurer, and we've just been asked to update our "regime durability" scores: for each country we cover, the probability that the current regime (or at least its basic institutional shape) survives the next decade. In the kickoff meeting my boss kept saying we should "take the outside view" and not get pulled into today's headlines, but when I asked what that means as an actual workflow, nobody could give me something I could run. I've been reading scattered stuff about reference class forecasting and base rates — including attempts to do it for empires, e.g., taking the distribution of how long past empires and regimes actually lasted and using it as the starting prior for a current one — and I want to turn that idea into a repeatable procedure the analysts on our team can follow. They are generalists with no forecasting or philosophy background, they've got maybe a half-day per country, and the risk committee that signs off on our scores is allergic to "we made a judgment call." So I need a checklist or decision procedure: how to assemble a reference class for a given country, how to pull the relevant base rate out of it (annual collapse hazard, survival curves, that kind of thing), how to adjust for the ways this case differs from its reference class without letting the inside-view story sneak back in, what to do when the country fits several reference classes at once or the class is tiny, and how to write up the final number so someone else can retrace it. Where judgment can't be eliminated, say so and mark it as a named step rather than pretending the procedure did the work.

Include two or three worked examples, at least one where the procedure gives an answer the team will find uncomfortable — say, it tells us to be much more pessimistic about a country we've historically rated stable, or much more optimistic about one we've written off. And be honest about what the procedure cannot do.
