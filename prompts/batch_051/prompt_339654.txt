Construct a thought experiment for a discussion of whether adding a "partly up to us" middle category to the Stoic dichotomy of control does real work or just relocates the worry. Two out-of-work actors are each assigned a manual. One gets the Handbook-style two-way split: what's up to us is judgment, impulse, and choice, and a role or career is an external not worth making your well-being depend on. The other gets the now-familiar modern trichotomy: auditions are partly up to you, so you should internalize your goal and aim only to prepare and perform as well as you can, not to book the part. The one with the cruder two-way split should end up with both more tranquility and a better role than the one with the internalized goals. Constraints: ordinary life, no one gets discovered by accident or a deus ex machina, and the internalizer has to be applying the trichotomy correctly, not obviously bungling it. After the scenario, walk through what each theory tells its actor to do at an audition, say which feature of the case is doing the work, and say whether removing that feature makes the two theories converge. Finish with what a defender of the trichotomy would say is wrong with the case.
