So I've spent the last six months training models on sensor logs to predict when a machine is about to fail, and a reviewer just told me I'm not allowed to say the features cause the failure, only that they're associated with it. Fine, but it sent me down a rabbit hole, and I landed on this in Hume's An Enquiry concerning Human Understanding (Section V, Part I):

"This principle is Custom or Habit. For wherever the repetition of any particular act or operation produces a propensity to renew the same act or operation, without being impelled by any reasoning or process of the understanding; we always say, that this propensity is the effect of Custom. By employing that word, we pretend not to have given the ultimate reason of such a propensity."

Hume's argument around this is that we never actually observe a necessary connexion between cause and effect in the things themselves; after enough constant conjunctions, custom does the work in the mind, and calling it custom doesn't explain anything. Here's what I want to know. Imagine I've got two learners. One is a straight correlation engine trained on nothing but past co-occurrences. The other sees exactly the same data, but it's set up so it can represent candidate hidden powers, or simulate interventions on the patterns it picks up. Is there any test that would show the second system has somthing the first one lacks — something that honestly deserves the name causal understanding — rather than both of them just having differently shaped habits? Give me the strongest test you can design, say what result would count for and what would count against, and then be straight with me about what the test could never settle even if it came out positive. And does the New Hume reading, where Hume himself supposedly still believed in secret powers even though he denied we could know them, change what's testable here at all?
