I’ve been reading Newton’s Principia for a philosophy of physics class, and I keep tripping over the line in Definition III where he says: "but motion and rest, as commonly conceived, are only relatively distinguished; nor are those bodies always truly at rest, which commonly are taken to be so." That sounds relationist, but then the bucket argument later pushes him to absolute space. I’ve been trying to get the bucket to work with only relations, and I came up with something I call “inertial debt.” Every chunk of matter that ever existed leaves a kind of debt in the local inertial structure of spacetime, and what the bucket detects is the accumulated debt from the whole past, not from the present stars. So if you could remove all current matter while leaving this debt behind, the water would still climb the sides, and you could still say it’s all grounded in matter, just matter in the past. I know this sounds like I’m making relationism unfalsifiable. Is there a version of “inertial debt” that is genuinely distinct from spacetime substantivalism, or is it just absolute space with a history attached? Give me the strongest objections and say whether any is fatal rather than just making the view ugly.
