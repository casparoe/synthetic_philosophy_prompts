I am a PhD student in computer science and the student member of the review committee for my university's seed-grant program in AI alignment. We are down to two finalists for a single award, though we may split the funds under revised scopes. Proposal A would formalize a scheme for human oversight of a learned system and attempt to prove worst-case guarantees: bounds on failure that hold even under adversarial conditions such as distribution shift or a misleading sub-model. Proposal B would instead run a large empirical study of the same style of oversight across many models and task distributions, estimating average-case reliability and cataloguing typical failure modes. The committee is split. The theory-minded members argue that only worst-case guarantees matter for high-stakes deployment, and that average-case results are easy to overfit. The empirically minded members argue that worst-case bounds will be so conservative or idealized that they guarantee nothing about any actual system. Please draft the decision memo I will circulate before Friday's meeting. It should recommend a definite course of action — fund Proposal A, fund Proposal B, or split the award with conditions attached — defend that recommendation with reasons the committee can deliberate on, and take seriously the strongest argument for the losing side rather than surveying the options and leaving the choice to us.
