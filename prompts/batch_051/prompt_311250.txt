I lead a small causal-inference team at a streaming company. Several times a month we are asked to sign off on whether a proposed experiment or analysis really supports a causal claim — for example, that a new ranking model caused a retention increase, or that a billing change caused a churn spike. I would like to turn Woodward's interventionist account of causation into a written procedure that my team can run before approving such claims. I take interventionism to be roughly the view that X causes Y just in case there is a possible surgical intervention on X — a manipulation that changes X while not affecting Y through any route that does not pass through X — such that Y would change (or its probability distribution would change) under that intervention, and the relationship is invariant across a suitable range of background conditions. I am aware this raises a circularity worry: the notion of an intervention is itself specified in causal terms (it must not influence Y through a route that bypasses X), so interventionism cannot serve as a reductive definition of causation. Please write a decision procedure — a checklist or decision tree in words — that operationalizes this for team members who work with data and experimental designs but are not philosophers. The procedure should include: the questions to ask in order; what counts as a sufficient answer to each; where the procedure stops with "not justified"; where it must hand the decision to a senior human because the question is one of substantive causal judgment rather than mechanical fact; and how the team should handle the circularity worry without pretending it disappears. Then run the procedure on three proposals: (a) a randomized experiment that varies the order of the first five rows of recommendations in order to estimate the effect of content diversity on next-week retention; (b) a natural experiment in which a bug made the app slow only for users on one server version, used to estimate the effect of load time on daily active use; and (c) a proposal to vary the subscription price for a random subset of users and attribute any downstream churn exclusively to the price change. Include at least one worked example in which the procedure returns an answer the requester will not like, and end with a clear statement of what the procedure cannot decide.
