I'm a science and ideas writer, and I keep noticing an asymmetry in how we talk about the Enlightenment that I want to press someone on. We credit Enlightenment universalism with scientific medicine and human rights; we teach it as the moment reason started winning. But when critics put the Enlightenment on trial, they blame that same universalist drive—the impulse to classify, measure, and administer—for scientific racism, colonial government, and reducing people to data points. The urge to produce a taxonomy of orchids and a taxonomy of human races is one urge. So what I want to know: is there a principle that lets us keep the progress and condemn the domination, or are we just cherry-picking from a single package? Give me the strongest principle you can find, stress-test it against a similar case that could sink it, and tell me whether we should keep the asymmetry, reverse it, or give it up.
