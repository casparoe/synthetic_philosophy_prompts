I am a general practitioner who has recently been appointed to the panel that reviews applications to detain people under mental health law. The appointment has forced me to confront something I have skirted for years: a patient I know well recently asked me, flatly, whether she was ill or simply miserable in circumstances that would make almost anyone miserable, and in the same week the panel renewed the detention of a man with a schizophrenia diagnosis who had stopped eating because he believed his food was poisoned. I have since been reading Thomas Szasz's argument that mental illness is a myth and that what we call mental illnesses are mostly problems in living. I am not convinced, but I also cannot see how to practise as though it were simply false.

I want a reusable decision procedure I can run in a ten-minute consultation or a forty-minute panel discussion to decide whether a person's psychological distress should be handled as an illness or as a problem in living, and therefore whether what follows is medical treatment, legal coercion, or social and practical support. Build it from the strongest version of Szasz's distinction, without assuming I have read him and without giving me a reading list or requiring sources. It should be a checklist or flowchart in words, with the tests applied in order, short enough to run under the time constraints, transparent enough for two other panel members to sign off on, and defensible in the written record. Tell me which of Szasz's distinctions must survive the simplification and which can be dropped. Flag every step where judgement cannot be eliminated, and say plainly where the procedure is likely to misfire. Then apply it to three worked cases: a man whose low mood and poor concentration began after redundancy and lift when he is with his grandchildren; a young woman with commanding auditory hallucinations and no insight into them; and a solicitor who drinks heavily and says he cannot stop, after an accident that killed his passenger. At least one of these should produce an answer I will not like. Say what the procedure, even when followed correctly, cannot settle.

Lead with the bottom line of your procedure in the first sentence, and then give the reasoning behind each step, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Please write the answer in American English.
