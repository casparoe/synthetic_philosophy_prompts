I'm a software engineer working on an ML platform team, with no formal background in philosophy, and I need a one-page research proposal for my company's small interdisciplinary program that funds conceptual work on machine learning — work that won't ship a product but is supposed to clarify something the field is confused about. I have a half-formed idea and need it turned into a tight proposal.

The idea starts from Douglas Hofstadter's discussion in Gödel, Escher, Bach of three layers of a message: the frame message (the implicit signal that something is a message to be decoded), the outer message (the structural or physical setup by which it gets decoded), and the inner message (the content that is conveyed). His examples involve record players and DNA, and his conclusion, as I understand it, is that meaning does not sit in any single layer but emerges from the interaction between whatever carries the information and whatever does the decoding.

I keep seeing a deadlock among engineers and researchers about whether large language models produce meaning or merely manipulate uninterpreted symbols — roughly the stochastic-parrots position versus the grounded-understanding position. The debate seems to persist partly because each side locates meaning in a different place: the training corpus, the learned weights, the computation at inference time, the prompt or system instructions, or the human who interprets the output. I suspect Hofstadter's distinction can be mapped onto a language model in a way that explains the deadlock, but also that the mapping exposes an oddity his original examples don't have: in a record player the three layers are physically separated, whereas in a language model the same trained weights seem to be doing the work of all three layers at once.

Please turn this into a one-page research proposal for the program. It should contain: a precise statement of the research question; why the existing positions in the understanding-versus-pattern-matching debate leave the question open and why Hofstadter's distinction hasn't simply settled it already; the method, including what I would actually do month by month and why a conceptual mapping and argument is the right tool rather than a new benchmark, a probing study, or an interpretability experiment; what a successful deliverable would look like for an audience of engineers who are skeptical that philosophy does anything; and what would count as the project failing. Keep the scope realistic for six months, and don't include a bibliography or literature review — I'll supply the references myself.
