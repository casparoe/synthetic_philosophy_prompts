People often defend a simple way of comparing outcomes: an outcome is better when it contains more total well-being, and making the world better means adding to that total. This thought seems to take for granted that the ledger is finite, so that particular lives saved or lost actually move the sum. But cosmologists now take seriously the possibility that the universe is spatially infinite and contains infinitely many sentient beings. If that is so, then saving one life or preventing one catastrophe changes the total only by a finite amount, and an infinitely large total with any finite amount added or taken away is still infinitely large; the comparison no longer moves. Does the original "more total well-being is better" argument survive this change in the background assumption? If it needs repair, which premise exactly is giving way, and is there a weaker principle that keeps the original motivation without implying that nothing finite matters?
