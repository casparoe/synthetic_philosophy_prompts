I spent most of my career in physical chemistry, largely in spectroscopy, where a researcher lives unapologetically among material constants—refractive indices, oscillator strengths, rate constants—and I retired recently and found my way into philosophy of science. Reading about structural realism left me uneasy in a way I could not at first articulate, and after two years of notes I have arrived at a position I would like checked before I try to write it up.

I call the position the calibration cut. The idea is that when a theory is replaced by a deeper one, its contents fall into three tiers. First, the bare dynamical form of the equations and their symmetry groups, which survives almost intact. Second, the interpretive furniture attached to that form—ether, corpuscles, flux lines, virtual exchange—which is routinely discarded. Third, the dimensionful constants and material parameters that practitioners actually measure and tabulate. My claim is that structural realists run the first and third tiers together under the word "structure," and that this is a mistake. A refractive index or a conductivity is not structural in the way a field equation is; it is a locally calibrated coefficient that gets re-derived, renamed, or demoted when the deeper theory arrives. What deserves realist commitment, I propose, is only the dimensionless invariant core: those relations that can be stated without appointing a unit, together with the constraints that keep their form stable across theory change. The Fresnel–Maxwell transition is my best witness: what survives is the form of the wave relation, not the optical constants of any particular medium. I recognize this is close to known views—perhaps epistemic structural realism, perhaps the old debate over whether dimensionful quantities are conventional—but I do not think it is identical to either, because I am not an antirealist about all constants, only about the dimensionful, tabulated, regime-bound ones.

Before I commit further, I would like an honest assessment. Reconstruct the strongest version of the calibration cut you can; tell me what it correctly notices, which existing positions it is nearest to, and where it breaks or stops being distinctive. I am especially worried about the fine-structure constant and other dimensionless constants that look brute and world-making, and about the suspicion that a formalism with all units stripped away simply does not have enough content left to be the object of anyone's realism. Please be candid: I would rather hear the fatal objections now than discover them after I have embarrassed myself in print.
