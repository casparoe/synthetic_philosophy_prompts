I've had a rule for the past few years that when I'm not sure something is okay—usually small stuff like padding an expense report, taking credit for work that was mostly someone else's, or telling a friend I'll help them move and then staying home—I run it through a rough Kant filter. I ask myself whether I could rationally will that everyone act on the same maxim I'm about to act on. If the answer is no, I treat the action as off limits, full stop, even if the stakes are tiny. If the answer is yes, I mostly let myself proceed. Honestly its kept me from doing a fair number of things I would have felt gross about afterward, and it's fast enough that I actually use it in the moment instead of rationalizing for an hour.

My friend who has actually read Kant says I'm not doing Kant at all. He says Kant separates the test into two distinct failures: a contradiction in the conception of the universalized maxim, where the action becomes impossible or inconceivable once everyone does it, and a contradiction in the will, where you can picture the world but couldn't coherently will it as a rational agent. My filter, he says, collapses both into the question of whether I'd dislike living in the resulting world, which is the kind of gut-level ick Kant wasn't asking about. He also says maxims are slippery: I can always redescribe what I'm doing—say, from borrowing money I don't intend to repay to getting myself out of a short-term bind—until the test spits out whatever I was already leaning toward. So his view is that my shortcut isn't really Kant, it's just my prior leanings wearing a Kant costume.

I can't tell whether he's right, and more importantly I can't tell whether it matters. I want the filter to check my biases, not launder them. Is it defensible to keep using the universalization test as a rough heuristic even if it mangles the real thing? Is there a version of the habit that keeps the speed and the veto power but doesn't let me redescribe my way to the conclusion I already wanted? And from the inside, how would I notice the difference between actually letting the test constrain me and reaching for it after I've already decided? Please answer in American English.
