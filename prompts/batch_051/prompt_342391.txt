Write a dialogue between two historians of the French Revolution who genuinely disagree about what the Revolution was. One takes a broadly Burkean line: the revolution was a catastrophic rupture that destroyed slow-grown institutions, customs, and obligations, and the Terror was not an accident but the natural result of trying to redesign society from scratch on abstract principles. The other takes a structural/class line: the old regime was already failing to manage the material contradictions of it's time, the revolution was the collision of archaic political forms with underlying economic shifts, and the Terror has to be explained through war, counter-revolution and class conflict rather than ideology. They should push each other on whether the Terror was a contingent outcome or a structural inevitability, and whether the claim that revolutions devour their children is an explanation or just a metaphor. Make it a real back-and-forth in which each character responds to the other's actual points, and neither is a strawman. Write in British English, around 900 words.
