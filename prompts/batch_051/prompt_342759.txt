I'm an autodidact reading early analytic philosophy on the side, and there's a pattern I keep generating without knowing whether it has a recognized name. Take the sentence "a is F." If I think of it as built from objects, it can't just be a heap of the two things a and F, because a heap doesn't say anything. The obvious fix is to add a third item — a relation like instantiation or exemplification — that ties a to F. But now "a stands in that relation to F" looks like yet another state of affairs, and I need a further relation to tie a, the first relation, and F together. Then a third relation, and so on. The same spiral appears with ordinary relations: if one ball is to the left of another, the relation "is to the left of" seems to need gluing to each ball, which requires another relation, and so on. This feels less like discovering a real infinite structure and more like a symptom that I'm treating the glue as one more object that itself needs gluing.

Does this exact pattern have a name of its own? Is the usual verdict that the regress is vicious or benign, and what are the main ways out — and which ones genuinely escape the spiral rather than just relocating it one step further or changing the subject?
