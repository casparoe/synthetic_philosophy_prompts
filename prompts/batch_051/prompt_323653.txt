I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about when it's okay to say a system wants something or is trying to do something, going by my gut on a bunch of cases. I want to know whether one principle captures all of them, or close.

A thermostat keeping a room at 21°C: I'd happily say it's keeping the room at 21, maybe even that it's 'trying to' reach 21 when the temperature drops, but I don't think it wants the room to be 21. A chess engine that sacrifices a pawn to open an attack: I'd say 'it wants to force the exchange' without blinking, but if someone asked me literally whether the engine has desires, I'd say no. A simple AI trained only by reward to collect coins in a video-game grid: I find myself saying it wants the coins, and I don't just mean it as shorthand — even though, under the hood, its not so different from the thermostat. A plant bending toward light: it's not trying to get light, it's just how it grows. A hypothetical machine whose only goal is to make as many paperclips as possible and which is smart enough to dismantle my car for steel: I'd say it genuinely wants paperclips, even though there was never any evolution or feelings in the picture. A person: wants things, obviously. An LLM generating the next most probable word: I wouldn't say it wants the next word to be probable, and I'm not even sure it's trying to be accurate — completing the text is just what it does.

Is there one principle that draws the line where I seem to, between a system that merely has a target or function and a system with something like a want? Which of my verdicts does it force me to drop, if any, and why? And what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: a system rewarded only for doing some task, but which starts avoiding shutdown because it has learned that being off stops it from doing the task. Is avoiding shutdown something it wants, or is that just the thermostat again?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, no preamble and no restating of the question. American English, please.
