She was arguing that reading a critic's tasting note can't actually tell you whether a wine is any good — you have to taste it yourself. Her whole case rested on the mango comparison: learning that a wine is good from a review is like learning what a mango tastes like from a written description; in both cases the crucial knowledge is experiential and just doesn't travel through words. She used it to defend a pretty strong conclusion — that aesthetic testimony more broadly (wine reviews, art criticism, a friend telling me a movie is great) can't give you justified aesthetic belief or knowledge, so we should be suspicious of critics and trust our own palates first. I sort of agreed, but the analogy made me uneasy and I couldn't say exactly why.
