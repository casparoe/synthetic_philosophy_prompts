There is a debate I keep seeing in longtermist circles that I have never been able to get a clean grip on. The other side argues that this is the very shape of a totalitarian program — a small group claiming that the stakes are so large that present people's preferences may be overridden, and that unaccountable control is justified by a future nobody can predict or verify.

Restate both sides of this debate in the vocabulary I actually work in: plants and controllers, setpoints and reference tracking, open-loop versus closed-loop control, observability and controllability, state estimation, model predictive control and receding horizons, actuator saturation, disturbances, and robustness. Be as faithful as you can, and do not let the choice of vocabulary settle the argument in advance.

Then give me a residue report. Which parts of the longtermist case and the totalitarian critique translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which cannot be expressed in control vocabulary at all? I want your committed view on this: is the underlying dispute best understood as a disagreement about the controllability and observability of the far future — about whether the long-run trajectory can be steered at all, and whether anyone could know they were on course — or does the control framing itself treat the future, and the people in it, as a plant to be steered, which is exactly what the critics say is objectionable? And where the original arguments have no counterpart in my vocabulary, is that because my vocabulary is missing something, or because the arguments are less substantive than their moralized language makes them look?
