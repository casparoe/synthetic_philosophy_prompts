I'm a software engineer and not at all trained in philosophy, so I'm probably making a dumb formal mistake here, but I can't find it. At work we do trade-offs all the time: two teams with different priorities each give ground on something they care less about to get something they care more about, and both walk away better off than if they'd refused—basically a Pareto improvement. I started wondering why moral disagreements can't be handled the same way, and I wrote the idea up as a numbered argument. The conclusion feels way too strong, but every individual step still looks fine to me.

Imagine Priya and Dev disagree about two moral issues, X and Y. Priya cares a lot about X and only a little about Y; Dev cares a lot about Y and only a little about X. Here's the argument:

(1) There's a possible deal where Priya gives up on Y (she goes along with Dev's position on Y, even though she thinks it's wrong) and Dev gives up on X (he goes along with Priya's position on X, even though he thinks it's wrong).
(2) Each would rather take the deal than not. Priya gets X mostly her way at the cost of going along on Y, which she cares about much less; Dev gets Y mostly his way at the cost of going along on X, which he cares about much less. So by each of their own values, the deal's outcome is better then the status quo.
(3) An outcome that everyone involved prefers by their own values, and that doesn't hurt anyone outside the deal, is an outcome they should all rate as better.
(4) If an outcome is better by your own values, then doing your part in bringing that outcome about is at least permissable by your own values.
(5) So neither Priya nor Dev can coherently refuse the deal by saying "my part of it would be wrong"—their own values already rank the whole package as worth it.
(6) Therefore any moral compromise that leaves both sides better off by their own lights is one that neither side can reject on principle. Principled holdouts are just miscalculating.

Is this argument valid? If not, which step fails exactly, and what would the corrected version of that step say? If the problem is an ambiguity in one of the phrases, give both readings and tell me whether the argument goes through on the strongest one.
