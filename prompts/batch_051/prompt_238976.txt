People keep telling me chain-of-thought monitoring is our best safety technique, because if we can read the model's reasoning we can catch dangerous plans before they get carried out. But the reasoning a model writes down is just more generated text, and it can easily be a post-hoc story or a sanitized version of what actually drove the decision. So suppose someone claims they have a monitor that can seperate faithful chain-of-thought from confabulated or obfuscated chain-of-thought. How would you test that claim? I want a concrete experimental design — not just 'see whether the CoT predicts the answer' — and I especially want to know what you'd do in the regime where the model is being optimized to fool the monitor, since that's the only regime that matters for safety. What result would count as genuine evidence that the monitor tracks real reasoning rather than surface features of the text, and what would a positive result still not show about a smarter model or stronger optimization pressure? Also: is 'faithful' even a well-defined target, or does any test just push the problem one level down — say, to a hidden scratchpad you're assuming is the real reasoning?
