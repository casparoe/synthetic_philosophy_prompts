Write a dialogue between two historians of philosophy debating how far Descartes's denial of animal thought extends. One character takes the hard mechanist reading: in Part Five of the Discourse on Method, Descartes argues that because animals cannot produce genuine speech, their behavior must be explained solely by the arrangement of their organs, and therefore they have no mind at all. The other character argues that this overstates the conclusion: what Descartes denies is reason or thought, not the capacity to see, hear, and feel, and he can consistently hold that a dog feels pain while lacking any reflective awareness of it. Let each character challenge the other's best textual evidence, not merely restate it, and have them isolate which premise about the relation between language and mentality actually does the work in the argument.
