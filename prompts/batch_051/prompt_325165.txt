I keep running into a situation in arguments about blame and I don't know what to call it. Someone becomes part of a wrong that is already fully underway — they accept a seat on a committee that has already decided to approve a rigged contract, or they join a group doing something bad and take a mostly cerimonial role — but the thing would unfold exactly the same if they walked away. Their vote or presence never changes the outcome. Yet we don't treat them like someone who just stands by and knows about it; we talk about them as mixed up in the wrong itself, not just as a person of bad character who kept quiet. Is there an established name for this? Aiding and abetting and accessory, as I understand them, seem to require that you actually helped the main wrongdoer in some way, and here the person didn't help in any sense that shows up in the outcome. If no single term fits, what concept comes closest, and where exactly is the line between being part of a wrong and merely failing to stop it? I'm less after the label than the carving.
