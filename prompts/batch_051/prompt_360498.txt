Consider a war in which one side’s cause is objectively unjust. Two soldiers face each other: A fights for the just side, B for the unjust side. B was raised entirely within state propaganda and has no feasible way to discover that his side is unjust; A’s evidence for his own side’s cause is equally compelling to him. Both fight only against uniformed combatants in otherwise identical circumstances. On the orthodox view, combatants are moral equals: B does not act impermissibly merely by fighting, and neither soldier is more liable to be killed than the other. On the revisionist view, B acts impermissibly and is liable to be killed, while A is not.

What is your credence in the revisionist verdict for this epistemically symmetric pair — that is, that despite the symmetry of their evidence, B acts impermissibly in fighting and is liable to be killed? Do not give a bare number. Decompose your credence into at least the following sub-questions, assigning a probability and a one-line justification to each and showing how you combine them: (1) whether liability to defensive force tracks objective wrongdoing or only wrongdoing relative to the agent’s evidence; (2) whether non-culpable factual ignorance negates wrongdoing or only excuses from blame; (3) whether the permissibility of fighting is determined at the level of the individual soldier or the level of the state or collective; and (4) whether B retains a right of self-defense against A once A becomes the immediate threat. Then identify the sub-question to which your final credence is most sensitive, and state what evidence or argument would most change that sub-question.
