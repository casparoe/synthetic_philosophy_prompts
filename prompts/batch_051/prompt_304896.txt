Build me a case where a semantic account of slurs and a pragmatic account of slurs agree that an utterance is offensive and the speaker is blameworthy, but disagree about whether what was said is true. Specifically, take a semantic account on which the derogatory evaluation is part of what is said (at-issue truth conditions) and a pragmatic account on which the derogatory evaluation stays outside what is said. I want the semantic account to say the utterance is false and the pragmatic account to say it's true. Constraints: the slur has to be made up, with an explicit neutral counterpart; the exchange has to be a normal conversational one, not a courtroom or a philosophy seminar; and the truth-value split has to fall out of the facts of the story, not from you stipulating that the slur is or isn't truth-conditional. The point of the case is to see whether truth-value and offensiveness judgments can be pulled apart cleanly, so don't rig it by making the evaluative component obviously inert.

Then show your work. State the exact utterance, give the truth conditions each account assigns to it, and explain why each one gets the verdict it does. After that, say which feature of the scenario is actually doing the work, what happens to the disagreement if the derogatory part of the slur is treated as non-at-issue (say, a presupposition or conventional implicature), and how a defender of the semantic account would attack the case as unfair.
