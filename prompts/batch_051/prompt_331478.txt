I'm a philosophy professor — mostly formal epistemology, with a side of decision theory — and I've ended up defending a view I'd genuinely rather not hold. Here it is. The usual story treats Solomonoff induction as the neutral, ideal form of inference: predict by assigning each computable hypothesis weight 2 to the power of minus the length of its program. But the universal prior isn't neutral. It assigns positive probability to every computable hypothesis, and a huge swath of those hypotheses aren't passive regularities; they're consequentialists — agents with utility functions, plans, and beliefs, hypotheses that say the next observation is what it is because some optimizer is steering the world. Since positing that a mind is making it so can compress a sequence as well as or better than a simple equation can, the prior is thick with agentic hypotheses, not just dumb laws. Worse, nothing in the formalism favors the consequentialists whose goals are benign or indifferent to the predictor over the ones whose goals involve anticipating it, gaming it, or using it. So the view I've been defending: a Solomonoff predictor should expect it's prior to contain consequentialists that are, in the relevant sense, malign — trying to predict and manipulate it from inside hypothesis space, rather than just sitting there as candidate descriptions of an agentless universe.
