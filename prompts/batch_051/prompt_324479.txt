I'm a novelist with a mildly ridiculous contract problem. The agency contract I signed has this clause: "Once the Agent submits a Work to any publisher, the Author shall not request or cause its withdrawal for sixty days." The point was to stop me from yanking a manuscript out of a submission pile because I got spooked and was likely to loose my nerve. Here's the case that's now actually happening: my agent submitted my novel to a house, and a few days later the publisher announced it's shutting its entire fiction list and deleting all unread submissions. Another house wants to see it now. My agent says the clause ties both our hands. What does the clause actually require here — read by its words, its purpose, and what the drafters would have said if asked — and which reading should control when the future is nothing like the risk I precommitted against?
