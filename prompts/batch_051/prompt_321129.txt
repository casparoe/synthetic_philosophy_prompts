Alright, here's one. Two people I know have been arguing for a year about how much rule-following a society should actually demand. One is a 'tight culture' person: she thinks strong norms, clear expectations, and low tolerance for deviance are what keep a society from falling apart, and that looseness sounds nice until the people you count on stop showing up, paying their share, or watching their kid. The other is a 'loose culture' person: he thinks permissiveness and tolerance are what let people experiment, dissent, and not be crushed for being weird, and that tightness is mostly a machine for punishing whoever doesn't fit. They've agreed to do an adversarial collaboration and produce one document they both sign.

Write the document for them. Use this structure:

1. Agreed claims (numbered): things they both accept about what tightness and looseness actually are, what each side is really worried about (phrased so the other side says 'yes, that's a fair description of my worry'), and what the empirical record does and doesn't show already. Don't make these vacuous.

2. The exact disagreements: for each one, state it in neutral wording, then give each side's own version, phrased so that side would happily sign it. Watch for places where theyre really arguing about the word 'norm' or 'rule' rather than about how much constraint is good — if part of the fight is verbal, say which part and why.

3. Cruxes: the sub-questions such that a settled answer would actually move at least one of them. I suspect one crux is whether tightness and looseness are a single spectrum or can be bought seperately by domain (tight on public health, loose on art), and another is whether loose cultures free-ride on a background of tightly enforced rules. Add cruxes of your own too.

4. For each crux, a concrete case, natural experiment, or kind of evidence both sides agree bears on it, plus what each side predicts about it in advance.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, then the crux section.
