I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about GPL and derivative works, because I keep flip-flopping depending on the day I'm asked. I hold all of the following, or at least I think I do: (1) copyleft is a legit way to keep code free, and if your program is derived from GPL code you should have to release the whole thing under GPL; (2) linking to a GPL library from a proprietary program makes the program a derivative work, because at runtime the two things are basically one program sharing the same address space; (3) but if my program just spawns a GPL command-line tool as a subprocess and parses its output, that's fine and I dont owe anyone my source; (4) also, what should matter is how much original expression actually got copied, not how integrated the pieces are at runtime; (5) and yet I'm fine with the idea that a tiny five-line function copied from GPL code into a giant proprietary codebase should still require the whole codebase to be GPLed. Which of these are actually in tension, which just look like they are, and what would I have to give up to make the set coherent? Rank the tensions by how severe they are, dont just tell me everything is equally messy.
