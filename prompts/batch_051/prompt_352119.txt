I run a small LessWrong-adjacent meetup on philosophy of mind. Usually around 14 people show up, a mix of programmers and people who mostly just read the sequences years ago, and we've got a single 90-minute session. The topic is sphexishness: the digger wasp that paralyzes a cricket, drags it to her burrow, goes inside to check the burrow, and if you move the cricket a couple inches while she's in there, she'll drag it back and re-check, on loop, never noticing. What I want people to leave with is the vertigo that behavior can look intelligent and goal-directed while being rigid at the gears level, and that the flexibility we credit to human agency might be mostly an environment that holds still. That feels like it should eat at the free-will intuition without actually settling what free will was supposed to be.

I want a runnable game for this, not a lecture. I keep picturing teams writing an explicit if-then procedure for a small physical task, like sorting blocks into a bin. Then they swap procedures and act as the "robot" while a saboteur quietly breaks one hidden assumption: a second bin, a glued-down block, a block that isn't a block. The moment I'm chasing is when the robot can see the obviously right move but the card they're executing says to go repeat a check step, and they feel themselves get pulled back to the card. I want that to happen before anyone says "sphexishness" out loud.

Give me exact rules, materials that cost less than twenty bucks, a scoring or payoff scheme that doesn't need real money (status or candy is fine), and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto the wasp, then onto the free-will question. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it on the fly. Also be honest about which part of the idea the game can't actually produce and I'll have to supply in the debrief, and how to keep the discussion from ending at the cheap "so we're all wasps, huh" shrug.
