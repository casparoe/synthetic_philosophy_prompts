I sit on the technical advisory committee of a regional port authority that must approve, by the end of the quarter, a design crest elevation for a new storm-surge barrier protecting a mid-sized coastal city. Our consultants have delivered downscaled output from a twenty-four-member global climate model ensemble, and the projections have split the committee. Local sea-level rise by 2080 is relatively tightly bounded across almost all members, but the extreme-storm activity that drives surge heights varies enormously: roughly a third of the members show little change in storm intensity at this location, a third show a substantial increase, and the rest fall between, with no clean relationship to skill over the historical record. Half the committee wants us to design to the ensemble mean plus a standard freeboard allowance, on the ground that every model deserves an equal voice and that giving more weight to some subset is cherry-picking. The other half wants us to design to the worst credible member, because the decision's cost function is asymmetric: undershooting by a meter could drown a city, while overshooting is a budget line. The consultants' spreadsheet is labeled 'likelihood-weighted,' but the ensemble was never constructed as a probabilistic sample — it is an ensemble of opportunity, assembled from whichever modeling centers volunteered runs, and several members share code, parameterization schemes, and even developers, so they are not independent witnesses. Underlying the dispute is a genuinely philosophical problem: what can the spread of a set of structurally related, imperfect models license a decision-maker to expect?

I need to circulate a short written opinion before next month's vote. Please draft the decision memo I will submit to the full committee. It should recommend a concrete design rule for the crest elevation — or, if you think that cannot be defended on the present evidence, a concrete procedural rule for choosing one with the materials we have — and it should explain what the ensemble can and cannot warrant. Treat the strongest version of the losing view seriously enough that its advocates will recognize their own reasoning in your characterization of it. And please avoid the familiar generalities that models are imperfect and uncertainty is high; the committee knows both, and our vote has to happen regardless.
