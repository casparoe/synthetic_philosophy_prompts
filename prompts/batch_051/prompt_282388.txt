I'm a journalist who mostly covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about the long standoff in cognitive science between enactivists and representationalists. The way I'll use the labels: representationalism says a mental act is, at bottom, the manipulation of internal states that stand for the world — the real business of perceiving and thinking happens in the head, and the body and environment are just inputs and outputs. Enactivism says the mental act is a kind of skilled doing, a whole loop between a living body and its environment, and that no inner item sitting in the head is doing the real cognitive work. I keep finding that the two camps can re-tell any example in they're own vocabulary without changing the observable facts, which makes the fight feel merely verbal to a reader, so I'd like you to build me a thought experiment that forces them apart somewhere visible.

Here's the spec. It should be a single ordinary-life scenario, no brain scans, no neural implants, no futuristic tech, set in a job where someone thinks with their hands — a mechanic, a piano tuner, a pastry chef, a midwife, your pick. There should be two conditions that differ in only one respect: in the active condition, the person controls their own sensing, they choose where to probe, tap, sample, listen and for how long; in the passive condition, feed them the very same sequence of sensory input — the same pattern of sound, touch, vibration, whatever modality makes your case work, in the same order — but they cannot act back on the scene, they just receive it on a fixed schedule. Keep everything else the same. The two theories will agree about everything the person says and does afterward; what I want them to disagree about is one sharply-phrased question, roughly: does the passive person have the same kind of perceptual-cognitive achievement as the active person, or did they merely have information delivered to them rather than perceive the thing? Engineer it so the representationalist has to say yes and the enactivist has to say no, but not cheaply — the representationalist shouldn't come out looking like they just defined perception as passive input, and the enactivist shouldn't come out looking like they've simply stipulated that passive reception can't count.

Give me exactly four sections. 'The scenario' (around 250 words, concrete, no jargon). 'Where they part company' (around 350 words: set out what each theory takes to be going on in each condition, then isolate the exact premise the enactivist rejects and the representationalist accepts — that's the part I want to show readers as the real disagreement). 'The load-bearing feature' (around 150 words: name the one element of the case that does the work, and what happens if you remove or flip it). 'The comeback' (around 150 words: the strongest thing the side that comes out looking worse can say to resist the scenario, and whether the case still carries weight in my article after that response).
