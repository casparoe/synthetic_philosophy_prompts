I head genetic counseling for a mid-sized fertility clinic network. We receive a steady stream of requests for preimplantation genetic testing (PGT) for traits that are not straightforwardly medical: sex selection for family balancing, selection against congenital deafness, and, increasingly, requests to rank embryos by polygenic scores for traits such as educational attainment or height. These come in several times a month, and the decisions are consequential—patients build their families around our answers, and we are exposed to charges of either disability discrimination or eugenic overreach. Our counselors now decide case by case, and the inconsistencies are becoming indefensible.

Please write a single reusable decision procedure a counselor can run during a 45-minute consultation, without philosophical training and without consulting me, to decide whether we facilitate a request, decline it, or refer it to our ethics committee. Ground the procedure in the contrast between procreative beneficence (roughly, that parents should choose, among the possible children, the one expected to have the best life) and the commitments of liberal eugenics (roughly, that institutions should not impose a single vision of a good child and should respect parental choices within broad limits). I need the questions asked in order, what counts as an adequate answer to each, where the procedure stops with "decline" or "refer," and which steps are genuine value judgments rather than factual or technical ones.

Then run the procedure on three requests: a Deaf couple selecting an embryo that carries a mutation for congenital deafness when other healthy embryos do not; a couple requesting sex selection for family balancing; and a couple requesting that embryos be ranked by an educational-attainment polygenic score. Say what the procedure cannot decide, and flag at least one case where it yields a result my clinic will be reluctant to accept. Commit to a single procedure and defend its stopping rules; do not offer me a menu of options.
