I am a high school student writing a paper for a philosophy of language unit on how slurs get their power to hurt. Before I can argue about whether a slur's offensiveness is part of its literal meaning or only something about its use, I need a working definition of the term slur itself, and the dictionary-style glosses I have found are too loose to be useful. I would like you to propose a definition, run it through the cases below, and tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer; where it gives a surprising verdict, either revise the definition or defend the verdict. I also want to know whether, on your definition, a word can be a slur all by itself, or whether being a slur depends on who says it and why, so that the same word can be a slur in one context and not another.

Clear cases, where I expect agreement:
- An anti-Black racial epithet of the historically charged kind, said by a white speaker to insult a Black person. A slur.
- A common anti-gay epithet said with contempt to a gay classmate. A slur.
- The word teenager. Not a slur.
- The word jerk. Insulting, but I do not think it is a slur.

Hard cases, where I am genuinely unsure:
- The same anti-Black epithet written out once in a linguistics article whose author argues that the word should be studied rather than hidden.
- The word queer used by a campus group as a neutral umbrella term, and the same word said as a taunt. Treat these as one case: does a single word have to receive a single verdict?
- The word gypped said by someone who does not know anything about its origins.
- Illegal used as a bare noun, as in a politician saying we should deport the illegals.
- Karen aimed at a woman who is treating a store employee badly.
- Scab used by a picketing worker for someone who crosses the line.

My own instincts are that the academic mention is offensive but not a slur, and that scab is a slur even though the group it targets is defined by a choice. I have no settled view on gypped or Karen. I particularly want to know which of your verdicts is least secure and why, and whether the biggest pressure on your definition comes from reclaimed uses or unknowing uses. Keep the whole response under 600 words.
