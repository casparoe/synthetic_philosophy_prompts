Okay, so you know the counterfactual mugging setup. Mugger flips a fair coin. Heads, he asks you for $100. Tails, he hands you $10,000, but only if he can tell that you would have paid on heads. In the original, he's got perfect knowledge of your counterfactual behavior, so the whole question is just whether you'd pay.

Now change one thing. Suppose the mugger's counterfactual knowledge is noisy: in the tails branch, if you actually would have paid, he gets that right with probability r; if you actually wouldn't have paid, he still wrongly hands over the $10,000 with probablity 1 - r. (So r is his accuracy at classifying your heads-branch behavior.) Heads is unchanged: he always asks for the $100.

Do the causal calculation (the coin has already landed heads, so paying now can't causally reach the tails branch) and the functional/updateless calculation (pick the policy across both branches). At what value of r does the updateless calculation stop saying you should pay? And does the variant still seperate CDT from FDT the way the original does, or is there a range of r where they end up agreeing?
