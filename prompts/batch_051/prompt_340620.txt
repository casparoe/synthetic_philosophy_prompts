I'm taking a course on the philosophy of logic, and I've been arguing with a classmate about Lewis Carroll's scene in which a tortoise accepts the premises of a modus ponens argument but refuses to take the step to the conclusion unless a new premise is added — and then refuses again unless still another premise is added, and so on. I proposed the following four criteria for a piece of reasoning to count as a genuine modus ponens inference rather than an adjacent but different act: (1) the person asserts or accepts the premises P and P→Q; (2) the person's acceptance of Q is produced by those acceptances rather than reached independently; (3) the person treats the move from P and P→Q to Q as licensed by the rule of modus ponens, in the way one uses a rule, rather than as requiring the additional belief that the transition is truth-preserving; and (4) the person does not make a special exception of this application without pointing to some difference in the case.

My classmate then described a borderline case. Tess applies modus ponens unreflectively all day: from the premise that the train leaves at six and the premise that if it leaves at six she will miss the reception, she concludes flat-out that she will miss the reception, and she asks for no bridge premise. But in her logic class, when the instructor writes P and P→Q on the board and asks her to detach Q, she declines. She asks the instructor to add the further premise 'if P and P→Q, then Q.' When the instructor concedes it, she still declines, and asks for 'if P and P→Q and (if P and P→Q then Q), then Q,' and so on. She insists throughout that she accepts modus ponens, is not questioning it, and that her only difficulty is that each pile of premises needs one more premise before Q can be detached from that pile. Does Tess count as having made a modus ponens inference in the classroom? Work through my four criteria, say where they underdetermine the verdict, and tell me whether the case shows that the criteria need revision — perhaps because criterion (3) is pulling too much weight, or too little. In your answer, please state how confident you are in your conclusions and single out the point you are least sure of.
