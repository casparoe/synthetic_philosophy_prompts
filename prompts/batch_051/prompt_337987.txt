I am a sixth-form student and the student member of my school's Quincentenary Planning Committee. The committee must decide which dating system will be used in the official anniversary timeline and the commemorative volume, and I have been asked to circulate a short decision memo before Friday's meeting. Three schemes are on the table. The first is the school's existing house style on local history displays: it counts years forwards and backwards from the school's foundation, so the present year is written as something like '+497' and earlier events as '−n'; this is natural for a volume about the school but gives no guidance for events before the foundation or for relating the school's history to national and international history. The second is CE/BCE, which the school has long described as secular and neutral, but which keeps the same pivot as the older Christian scheme. The third is a reformed scheme that would add roughly ten thousand years to the present numbering, so the current year becomes a five-digit date with its zero point near the start of the Neolithic; its supporters call it a genuinely neutral, global epoch, while its critics say it merely relocates the arbitrariness. The memo should recommend one of these options, or a hybrid with named conditions, and should treat the underlying question seriously: is the choice of an epoch a mere convention, or does it express a substantive view about which events matter? It should engage honestly with the strongest consideration on the losing side and give the committee a decision it can actually act on. Please write the memo in British English. At the very end, after the memo, include a final line beginning 'Verdict:' followed by exactly one of these labels: 'school epoch', 'CE/BCE', 'reformed epoch', or 'hybrid'.
