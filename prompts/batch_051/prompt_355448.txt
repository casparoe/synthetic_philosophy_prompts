A blog I was reading summarized Nozick's tracking theory of knowledge and I'm suspicious it's a bit off, but I can't tell where. The post said, roughly: knowledge is true belief that tracks the truth. You know p just in case p is true, you believe p, and your belief is sensitive to p — meaning if p had been false, you wouldn't have believed p. That's the whole account; theres no separate requirement of justification, just the right kind of hook-up between belief and world. The payoff, according to the post, is that you can know you have hands, because in the closest world where youd have lost them you wouldn't believe you still had them, but you can't know that you're not a brain in a vat, because in the vat world you'd still believe you weren't one. The post then said this forces Nozick to deny that knowledge is closed under known logical consequence, though it claimed he only denies closure for special skeptical hypotheses and that closure holds fine in all ordinary non-skeptical cases. Its big objection was that the view still refutes itself: if I know I have hands, and I know that having hands entails I'm not a handless brain in a vat, then I can deduce I'm not a handless brain in a vat and thereby come to know it — contradicting what Nozick says. So, how fair is this as a summary of Nozick? Go through it piece by piece and tell me what a careful Nozick would accept, what's an oversimplification, what's just wrong, and whether the self-refutation objection actually lands once the accurate parts are in place.
