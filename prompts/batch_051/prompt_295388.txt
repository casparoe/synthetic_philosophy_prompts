I have been trying to get clear on what it takes for a piece of mathematics to explain a physical fact rather than merely describe, measure, or predict it, and I want a working definition of mathematical explanation — not a slogan. This matters to me because cases like the ones below are often used as evidence that mathematics is indispensable to scientific explanation, and I want to know which of them can actually carry that argument.

The cases I want the definition run against are these. (1) The arithmetic case: if I have seven apples and am given five more, the fact that 7+5=12 does not explain why I end up with twelve apples; it just reports the count. I take this to be clearly non-explanatory. (2) The cicada case: North American periodical cicadas emerge in broods on 13- or 17-year cycles, both prime, and the standard explanation is that a prime period minimizes how often a brood emerges in the same year as predators or competing broods with shorter cycles, since a prime shares few common multiples with other periods; here a fact of number theory seems to be doing the explaining of a biological regularity. (3) The table case: a three-legged table never wobbles while a four-legged one often does, because three points determine a plane and four points need not be coplanar; the mathematical fact seems to be the whole explanation, even though no cause is being cited. (4) The honeycomb case: bees build cells that form regular hexagons, and the celebrated explanation is that a hexagonal tiling uses the least wall material for a given storage volume — a geometric claim now proved. But there is a wrinkle: actual cells may form because soft circular wax tubes get pressed into hexagons by physical forces, in which case the optimality theorem would not track the real mechanism. (5) The bridge case: Euler showed that no walk through the old Königsberg crosses each of its seven bridges exactly once, and this seems to explain why a certain route is impossible — puzzling if explanations must cite causes. (6) The storm case: Saturn's north pole displays a stable hexagonal jet stream that falls out of the fluid equations, and it is hard to say whether the mathematics explains the shape or merely solves the physics.

My suspicion is that the phrase 'mathematical explanation' does two different jobs: sometimes the mathematics picks out the actual difference-maker, and sometimes it shows that, given structural constraints, things had to be so; I expect the honeycomb, bridge, and storm cases to sort between those jobs. If you agree, give me two definitions and say which cases fall under which; if not, give one definition and defend it. Either way, run the definition through all six cases, say plainly where it gives the wrong verdict or no verdict, and revise it or defend the surprising result. I am confident only about the arithmetic case (not explanatory) and the cicada case (explanatory); the rest are the ones I most want pressure-tested. Finally, tell me how confident you are in the definition and in each verdict, and single out the point you are least sure about.
