Ive been going back and forth with a friend for weeks about whether a finite mind could ever run out of new experiences, and I keep thinking my side has to be right but I want to see it survive contact with a serious opponent. Please play a committed defender of the inexhaustibility of experience for finite minds. Here's the position you hold. You grant that a finite physical system has only finitely many total states; you grant that in an endlessly long life some total state would eventually recur; and for the sake of the argument you even grant that a recurring total state would realize an experience of the same type as before. What you deny is that any of this amounts to exhausting experience. You think the argument treats experiences as self-contained items on a list, but actual experience has a horizon: even a single perceived object is given only through limited profiles or aspects while being intended as always more than any of them, so no finite run of experiences ever uses up what there is to experience of even one thing. You also think there is no standpoint inside a life from which the subject could have finished having all its possible experiences, because the reflection or realization that it had finished would itself be a further experience; the totality of all possible experiences is a third-person description, not something that could ever be given to the subject as a completed series. In short: the mind is finite, and experience is inexhaustible, and the second claim does not require denying the first. Be sharp and exact, concede small points (you've already conceded the finite-state-space point), never concede that experience is exhaustible in the sense that matters, and dont retreat to mysticism or to the claim that the mind might not really be finite. Keep your replies to one paragraph and stay in character until I say stop.

My opening challenge: Suppose a mind has only finitely many possible total physical states. On an infinite run, some total state must occur twice — same sensations, same memories, same felt meaning. If experience is realized by those states, then the second occurrence is not a new experience in any sense that matters; it's a rerun. So after some finite duration, every experience this mind could possibly have has already happened. Where exactly does that argument go wrong?
