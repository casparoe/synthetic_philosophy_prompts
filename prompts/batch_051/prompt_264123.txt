I'm giving the short closing speech at the final gathering of a neighborhood center that has been the heart of our community for over fifty years; it is closing because the building has been sold and no replacement could be found. I'm not an academic, but I've spent a long time thinking about what exactly is being lost. I've come to believe the loss is not just a sum of private losses. Each of us may keep our individual friendships, and we may form an online group, but the community itself — the shared way of talking, remembering, maintaining the place, settling expectations, and being answerable to one another — is a good in its own right, and it is destroyed when the practices that carried it stop. I want that conviction to govern the speech rather than merely appear in it. The speech must let me name the loss honestly and without false comfort, so no saying that this isn't really an ending or that we will carry the center with us as if carrying it reproduced the thing itself. At the same time it has to land with a mixed room. Some founding members in their eighties feel the loss as viscerally as I do; a few younger people think I am romanticizing a building and that staying in touch one-to-one is enough; and one or two quietly think the center had already outlived its purpose. Please write the speech, about four minutes long.
