I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, so I may be using some of this vocabulary loosely. In my day job I work with objects that behave like infinite data structures — lazy streams, coinductive types, loops understood through invariants rather than final states — and I had always treated "infinite" as an idealization: a convenient way of talking about a process that never actually terminates. Then I worked through Hilbert's hotel and Cantor's diagonal argument, and I saw that mainstream mathematics treats infinite totalities as completed objects, actual infinities, rather than merely potential infinities that can always be extended one more step. What surprised me is that the finitist objection to this never really disappeared. There have always been serious mathematicians and philosophers who hold that an actually infinite set is not a legitimate object; that talk of "all natural numbers" is shorthand for an open-ended procedure; or that something counts as existing only if it could in principle be constructed or exhibited. The mainstream, meanwhile, went ahead and built set theory, classical analysis, and a good deal of theoretical computer science on the assumption that actual infinities are unproblematic. So the dispute looks to me as though it was settled by adoption rather than by persuasion: the minority never conceded, the majority never really answered the objection, and the two sides stopped addressing each other. I know Hilbert hoped to justify infinitary mathematics by strictly finitary means, and that Gödel's incompleteness theorems are usually taken to have closed off that project; but that seems to settle a different question than the finitist's. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not ultimately mathematical but concerns what "exists" is supposed to mean in mathematics, and what a proof is supposed to show — that a statement is true in a formal system, or that an object can actually be given. I'd like a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one about the words "exists" and "infinite," or are the two sides answering different questions under one heading? In giving the diagnosis, please engage with the positions as each side actually states them rather than a caricature. And what would count as progress — a formal result, a sharper distinction, a change of question — or is this a case where neither side should be expected to move the other?
