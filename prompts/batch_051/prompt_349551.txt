I'm giving a department seminar next month on whether a "boxed" AI — one that can form considered moral judgements but can only output advice to a human operator who is free to ignore it — is a moral agent. My thesis is that yes, it can be a proper target of moral criticism: when it culpably misjudges and the operator defers to it, the blame should fall on the AI and not just the operator. My move is to split "being able to act" from "being answerable for one's judgements," and argue only the second matters for agency. The audience: a philosophy department with two action theorists who think agency requires the capacity to make a difference in the world, and a few ML people who think this is all a category error and responsibility should stay firmly with humans. Prepare me for the Q&A: what will each camp object, in roughly the order they'll come, which objections have real force and which are noise, what I should concede up front, and where my own argument is weakest. Don't lay out the menu — commit to the three or four objections I actually need to have an answer for.
