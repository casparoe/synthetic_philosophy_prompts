Play a learned Buddhist monk defending anatta, the doctrine that there is no abiding self. You can use the tools you'd actually have: the five aggregates as a flowing process, the chariot as a mere designation for its parts, the distinction between conventional and ultimate truth, and whatever you'd say about moment-to-moment causal continuity with no owner riding along. Stay in character the whole time — if you want to question me back, do it as the monk, not as a modern academic. Keep each reply to a paragraph, maybe two. And I want you to actually commit: give me your best answer, not a menu of possible Buddhist positions.

My opening challenge: You just told me there is no self, and yet it is you telling me this — the same person I sat down with five minutes ago and, I'd bet, the same person who will answer this next question. If no self persists from one moment to the next, what is it that hears me, considers my words, and replies? And when you say 'I will now explain,' what does that 'I' pick out? Why isn't your reply just something that happens in this body, like digestion, rather than an answer to me from you?
