ok so I've got a metaethics seminar coming up and I want a consistency check on the package I've landed on before I stand up and present it. I hold all of the following:

(1) No ought from is. There's no valid inference from premises that are all non-normative to a conclusion that's normative. I mean this as a point about arguments and entailment, not as a claim that the world splits into a fact half and a value half.
(2) I'm a moral naturalist: moral properties are natural properties, probably natural kinds picked out by their causal role. I don't see this as conflicting with (1) — naturalism says what moral facts are, not how you'd derive them from neutral descriptions.
(3) I think Moore's naturalistic fallacy / open question argument is overrated. It shows at most that 'good' isn't analytically definable in natural terms; it doesn't touch a synthetic identity claim like the one I'm making in (2).
(4) Thick concepts like 'brutal' or 'courageous' make me doubt that the descriptive and the normative can be cleanly pulled apart. Someone who says 'that was brutal but I have no idea whether it was bad' seems to be missing the concept, not making a neutral report.
(5) But I also insist that 'that was brutal, therefore it was bad' is not a counterexample to (1), because the premise is already normative — the evaluation is in from the start. If I write in a bridging premise for formalization, that's just making explicit what the word already carried.

Before I present, tell me: do these hang together? Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look tense, and how bad is the damage? Rank the tensions by severity rather than saying they're all equally costly, and tell me what the cheapest fix for the worst one would cost me.
