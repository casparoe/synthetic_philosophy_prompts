I'm taking a philosophy of biology course, and we are working through the afterlife of Aristotle's four causes—in particular, what became of the final cause, the idea that natural things and processes can be understood as directed toward an end, once early modern natural philosophy drove final causes out of physics. The positions we have mapped so far are these:

1. Eliminativism: apparent teleology in nature has no place in natural science. Final-causal talk about non-human nature is, strictly speaking, false or empty; it survives only as a façon de parler, and any genuine explanation will be in terms of efficient causes.

2. The cybernetic or feedback reduction: goal-directedness can be translated, without remainder, into non-teleological causal language about systems whose behavior is corrected toward a set point by negative feedback. On this view, a final cause is just a state that a system is causally disposed to maintain.

3. The selected-effects account: a trait's function is whatever effect it was selected for in its evolutionary history. Final-causal talk is legitimate, but it points backward, to past selection pressures, not to anything in the present or future.

4. The causal-role account: a trait's function is whatever it contributes to some capacity of the wider system, considered independently of its history. Final-causal talk is legitimate, but it points to present organization and dispositions rather than to the past.

I want to know whether this map is complete. Give me a fifth position that is a recognized view in this literature, if there is one. It should be genuinely different in structure from all four: not a rewording, a special case, or a combination of items already on the list. If you supply one, explain what it claims, why it is not reducible to one of the four, and what problem with the four it is meant to solve—or, on reflection, whether it is any good. Finally, please flag which parts of this taxonomy are settled textbook material and which are genuinely contested among people who work on the topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
