I'm taking a philosophy of mind course and we've been reading around Metzinger's self-model theory, and there's this term, phenomenal transparency, that I keep half-understanding and then losing my grip on. The way I've had it explained is something like: a representation is transparent when you look through it at what it represents, instead of seeing it as a representation itself — the example people reach for is a window, or a tool you're using so fluently you stop noticing it. The claim is that the self-model is supposed to be transparent in this sense, which is why your own self doesn't show up as a model or a construct but just as, well, being there.

But here's what I don't get. My phone's touchscreen controller is also something I never experience as a controller; I just get taps and swipes. Is that the same sense of transparency, or a much thinner one? More generally, could you explain the conceptual difference between phenomenal transparency and the ordinary, boring fact that a lot of mediation goes unnoticed? And does saying the self-model is transparent amount to more then saying that you can't introspect the neural processes that build your sense of self? I have a suspicion the second reading is too weak and misses something important, but I can't quite articulate what.
