I'm an undergraduate taking a seminar on philosophy of language and artificial intelligence, and I've been trying to settle a distinction I keep appealing to in arguments but cannot find a stable name for. I have been calling it truth-mimetic assertion. By this I mean an utterance, or a piece of text, whose production is governed entirely by the aim of seeming like a competent, relevant, plausible contribution to the conversation, rather than by any attempt to track whether what is said is actually the case. As I am drawing the boundaries: a lie involves representing what you believe to be false with the intention to deceive; an honest error involves aiming at truth and missing; but a truth-mimetic assertion is neither, because the truth or falsity of the output never exerts control over its production at all.

The case that made me latch onto this is probably familiar to a lot of students now: a friend asked a large language model to summarize one of our course readings, and it produced a confident, well-organized paragraph attributing to the paper an experiment that, when we checked, was never in the paper and may not exist anywhere. We had the argument everyone seems to have. To call it a lie seems wrong, because there is no hidden intention to mislead in the model; to call it a mistake seems just as wrong, because the model was not attempting to be accurate and then failing; but some ordinary category seems needed to describe output that is merely shaped to be accepted as a competent answer. My worry is that my third category may be doing the same work as an existing idea I've heard only vaguely described, and that I may be smuggling mental-state language into a case where none belongs. I'd like an honest assessment of whether the category is coherent and genuinely distinct, or whether I have merely redescribed something already well known under another name. I'd also like to know whether saying that a language model produces truth-mimetic assertions commits me, at least implicitly, to treating the model as an agent with aims in a way that cannot be defended. If the whole framing rests on a confusion—for instance, that a system without intentions can be usefully sorted into categories built for intentional speakers—please say so plainly rather than working within my trichotomy.
