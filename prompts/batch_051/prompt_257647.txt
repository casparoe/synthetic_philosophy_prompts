Consider Max Black's two-sphere universe: an otherwise empty space containing nothing but two globes of iron exactly alike in every intrinsic qualitative respect—size, shape, mass, temperature, chemical composition, and so on. Black took this to be a plausible case in which two objects are distinct even though nothing intrinsic distinguishes one from the other, and thus a challenge to the non-trivial version of the Identity of Indiscernibles, the principle that no two distinct things can share all their properties.

Now vary the case. The universe still contains the two duplicate iron globes, exactly alike in every intrinsic qualitative respect. But it also contains a third body: a small iron cone, obviously different in shape, lying one mile from one globe and two miles from the other. Nothing else exists.

Work through how adding the cone changes the debate. Specifically: (a) Do the two globes now differ in some property, and if so, is the distinguishing property intrinsic or relational, and is it qualitative or identity-involving (involving a particular individual, as in the property of being this very globe)? (b) Does the modified case refute Black, or does it refute only a version of the Identity of Indiscernibles that counts relational properties, while leaving the intrinsic-qualitative version exactly where Black left it? (c) Which version of the principle—one restricted to intrinsic qualitative properties, one that also allows qualitative relational properties, or one that allows all properties including identity-involving ones—gives the most defensible verdict in the modified case, and does adopting that version force you to revise the verdict you would give in Black's original two-globe world? Conclude by saying whether Black's original challenge to the Identity of Indiscernibles survives this variation, is dissolved by it, or is merely relocated.

Begin with a one-sentence statement of your verdict, and keep the entire response under 800 words. At the end, identify the single consideration that, if it went the other way, would most change your answer, and state where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
