I've been trying to reverse-engineer Judge Okonkwo's defamation doctrine from her last few opinions, because she keeps getting the cases about star-ratings, scores, and analyst forecasts, and I want to see whether I can predict her next ruling. Here's her record as best I've reconstructed it (my glosses on the reasoning, but the outcomes are right):

- She dismissed a chef's suit over a critic's line that the tasting menu was "pretentious, joyless, and wildly overpriced": aesthetic verdict, can't be true or false.
- She let a hotel's suit go forward over a travel blogger's post, "This hotel has bedbugs, and the front desk lies about refunds": the bedbug sentence at least asserts a verifiable fact.
- She dismissed over a stock-forum comment, "Sell. This thing's going to zero, management is asleep": predictive puffery, not a factual claim.
- She dismissed over a "1.5 / 5 reliability" score that a contractor-review site gave an HVAC company, with no written explanation: a standalone score doesn't say what happened on any particular job.
- She let a bond issuer proceed against a rating firm that published a "CCC" rating with a report saying the rating "reflects our review of the issuer's audited financials and a liquidity stress test," where the issuer had evidence the firm had actually reviewed an unrelated shell company's statements. Apparently she said a rating can be actionable if the reader would understand the factual claim it implies as being reported.

He wants the score treated as a assertion of fact.

If Judge Okonkwo is being consistent with herself, does she dismiss or let it proceed?
