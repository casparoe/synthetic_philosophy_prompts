I work as a software engineer, mostly maintaining and refactoring a legacy monolith, and I recently fell down a rabbit hole reading about a philosophical program called conceptual engineering. The motivating thought seems to be that philosophers shouldn't only describe how concepts actually behave; sometimes they should revise them, for example by reworking an inherited concept of marriage, race, or mental disorder once that concept is judged to be doing harm or otherwise misfiring. The comparison that keeps coming up is with tools or technology: a concept is supposed to be like a tool that can be sharpened, repurposed, or replaced, and conceptual engineering is rather like refactoring a legacy codebase instead of just documenting its current behavior.

I would like to know how far that engineering analogy actually holds. Give me the correspondence table: what in conceptual work plays the role of the legacy code, the public API, the client code that depends on it, the bug or code smell, the test suite, the refactor itself, and the deprecation and migration process. Then lay out where the mapping breaks. To seed the discussion: I am suspicious that code gets to point at a specification or test suite in a way that concepts may not, that altering a concept's extension might just change the subject rather than honestly repair the same concept, and that client code has maintainers with explicit interests whereas the users of a concept are uncoordinated and often disagree about what it is for. But I am not in a position to judge which of those disanalogies actually matter.

The analogy is normally used to support the conclusion that we can, and sometimes should, deliberately revise inherited concepts instead of settling for descriptive accuracy about their current use. Audit whether that conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table: separate the breaks that are harmless from the ones that undermine the analogy's intended use, and say what a better analogy would be if this one cannot carry the weight. I am not looking for references or a reading list, just an analysis I can evaluate on its own terms.
