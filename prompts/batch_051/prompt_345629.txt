I've gotten fairly convinced by the AI-safety-via-debate story: you've got two systems arguing opposite sides of a question in front of a human judge, and if the judge is only a bit better at spotting good arguments than at producing them, the truth wins because it's easier to defend a true answer than to keep a false one alive against a competent critic. What would count against this? I'm especially interested in failure modes where a debater wins by being persuasive instead of right—exploiting biases the judge has, giving true but misleading arguments, or producing reasoning the judge finds satisfying but can't actually check. Are those failures of the underlying idea or just bugs in a particular setup? I'd also like to know whether the view is at serious risk of being insulated: does it predict anything its rivals don't, or has it been qualified so that no experimental result or demo could move it? Sort the defeaters from decisive to merely awkward, and be honest about how exposed the position really is.
