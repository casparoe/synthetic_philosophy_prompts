I am an undergraduate taking a course in the history of science from Greek mathematics through the early modern period, and I keep running into the same interpretive habit in our readings without knowing what to call it. An older result is translated into modern notation or modern concepts, and the translated version is then treated as what the original author was really doing. Our textbook credits the Pythagoreans with discovering the irrationality of the square root of two, although the sources themselves present a proof that the side and the diagonal of a square are incommensurable — a claim about ratios and magnitudes rather than about a class of numbers called irrationals. Priestley is often said to have discovered oxygen because he isolated the gas we now identify as oxygen, even though he took himself to have produced dephlogisticated air and drew conclusions from that identification that a modern chemist would reject. Archimedes' quadratures are routinely presented as an early form of integral calculus, as if the only thing he lacked was the notation.

I understand why these re-descriptions are attractive: they make old arguments quickly intelligible, and they let one say that an old result is really the same as a modern one. But they also seem to smuggle in distinctions and standards that the historical actors did not possess, so that questions like who really discovered oxygen become nearly impossible to settle honestly. I am trying to pin down exactly what this habit is, what it is not, and where its edges are. Is there an established term for it, and if so, does the term name this specific habit or only a broader family of errors of which it is one member? Is the phenomenon one thing, or a cluster of related moves — a credit-giving move, an evaluative move, and an explanatory move — that should be kept apart? And when is a modern reconstruction of this kind a harmless clarifying paraphrase or didactic convenience, rather than a distortion that changes the subject? If no settled name exists, I would also welcome a proposal: a term with a definition and some boundary cases. A bare label would not help me; I want the distinctions.
