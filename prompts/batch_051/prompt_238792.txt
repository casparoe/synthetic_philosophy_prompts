i've somehow became the person who settles 'canon' for a small theatre group doing oral tales from the island chain my gran came from. the first story exists in six collected tellings, and the spine is constant: a girl who understands birds follows a nightjar up to the mountain meadow, is taken as wife by something the tellers call the Mountain Weatherman (a voice in the mist in some tellings, a huge man made of hail in others), and comes back to the village speaking only birdsong. but the endings split. in two tellings she chooses to go back up the mountain, and the village hears birdsong every dawn after; in three her brother drags her home and she never speaks again; and in the one transcribed earliest, which everyone now treats as the reference version, she tricks the Weatherman into telling her his name, and the village learns to call the rain. half the group says we should stage 'the canonical version', the one the story really is. i keep saying the only thing canonical about that earliest transcription is that it got written down first, and the rest is accident. who's right? if i have to stage exactly one, what am i choosing between, and does it even make sense to ask which was the original? i want you to actually commit rather than lay out options. finish with your short answer and then a final line starting 'Verdict:' with the bottom line.
