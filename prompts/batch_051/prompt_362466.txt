A populist party gets the following rule adopted, on the grounds that politicians keep ignoring the will of the people. Whenever a bill passes the legislature, any bloc of members that voted no and makes up at least a third of the chamber can force a binding referendum on it—no signature collection, no cooling-off period. The referendum kills the bill only if a majority of those voting vote to reject it and the no votes add up to at least 30% of all registered voters. If the bill survives, it becomes law immediately and is exempt from another referendum for five years. Analyze this as a mechanism, not as a moral position: what do minority blocs start doing with their referendum trigger, how does it change which bills majorities even try to pass, when does the public actually turn out, and what second-order effects appear (referendum fatigue, gaming the turnout treshold, timing the vote)? And the big question: would this track anything that deserves to be called the will of the people, or would it mostly track something else, and could its failure modes be fixed without gutting it?
