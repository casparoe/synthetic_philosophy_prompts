Give me your credence, expressed as an imprecise probability, in the following proposition: by 2100, in the world's wealthiest economies, paid employment will no longer function as the default anchor of social identity and felt contribution for most working-age adults. Treat the proposition as true if, around 2100, representative survey evidence in those countries shows — across at least two consecutive decades rather than as a transient response to economic or policy shocks — that fewer than half of working-age adults, asked open-endedly what mainly supplies meaning and standing in their lives, point first to a paid job rather than to caregiving, community, craft, creative practice, or some other non-employment source.

By an imprecise probability I do not mean a confidence interval for a hidden frequency. I mean an interval of your own credal bounds: the lower endpoint is the value you can defend under the most skeptical readings of your evidence and assumptions, and the upper endpoint is the value you can defend under the most favorable readings. If you would ordinarily report a point estimate, widen it to the interval that honestly reflects the range of cases you would not want to rule out.

Decompose the estimate into no fewer than four sub-questions. Suggested terms, which you may replace or supplement: (a) the extent to which automation and machine intelligence will eliminate and alter the demand for human labor over the period; (b) the probability that material security and access to social goods become substantially decoupled from selling labor, through transfers, public provision, or equivalent mechanisms; (c) the availability and cultural legibility of non-employment sources of identity and esteem, such as care relationships, craft, voluntary association, and demanding leisure; and (d) the persistence of the ideological link between paid work and moral worth, including the probability of a successful reaction that re-centers employment. For each sub-question, give an interval and one or two sentences of justification. State explicitly how you combined the sub-questions — which terms multiply, which add, and which dependencies you ignored or approximated — and then give your resulting interval for the main proposition. Finally, identify the sub-question whose interval contributes most to the width of the final interval, and say what single piece of evidence available within the next twenty years would narrow your final interval the most.
