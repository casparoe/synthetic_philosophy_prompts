Start from the following working account of *wu-wei*, assembled from the standard marks given in the Daoist texts and their commentaries. An action is done in *wu-wei* when it exhibits all of these features:

1. *Non-deliberation.* The agent does not consciously canvass alternatives, set the act before herself as a goal, or try in any foregrounded way.
2. *Spontaneity or situational fitness.* The act answers the circumstances immediately and aptly, the way water follows the contour of the ground rather than being forced toward a planned result.
3. *A self-effacing agent.* There is no felt gap between an *I* that supervises and the action that is supervised; no self-monitoring, checking, reviewing, or second-guessing during the act.
4. *Effortless efficacy.* The deed is accomplished deftly, with little or no felt strain — the tradition's way of putting it is that nothing is done and nothing is left undone.

Now consider a deliberately constructed borderline case. L is by temperament a chronic self-monitor: every move she makes comes with an inner audit, and she finds this exhausting. She resolves to become someone who acts with *wu-wei*, and she treats this as a project. She keeps a daily spreadsheet on which she scores her degree of non-interference; she schedules unstructured blocks of time and protects them with a timer; and during those blocks she practices a technique in which she rests attention on a single sensory contact and lets her body respond before a thought about responding forms. After two years, the felt self-monitoring has disappeared. Those close to her say she now moves, speaks, and works with the economy of a practiced craftsman or musician. But there is a maintenance cost: she still needs a weekly session in which she deliberately audits the past week's lapses, revises the spreadsheet, and rehearses the letting-go technique. If she skips these sessions for several weeks, the old self-auditing returns.

This case sharpens the paradox of trying not to try: L deliberately cultivates the cessation of deliberate cultivation, and the result seems to survive only so long as she keeps deliberately maintaining it.

Does L act from *wu-wei* during the ordinary hours between maintenance sessions, or has she manufactured only a convincing imitation? Take a definite position and defend it. Work through the four features one by one; where a feature does not settle the matter, say so explicitly, and say whether that shows the feature, or the whole list, needs revision. In particular, address whether *wu-wei* should be judged by how an action unfolds in the moment or by the causal history and ongoing practices that produce and sustain it, and break your verdict apart temporally: does it differ for L's ordinary activity, for the weekly maintenance session, and for the underlying condition that she would relapse without the session? Argue for a single committed answer rather than laying out both sides and stopping.
