Longtime rationalist-forum lurker here. There's a move I keep seeing in moderation fights that I don't have a clean name for, and I'd rather carve it properly than just slap a label on it.

A subreddit has a rule against "low-effort posts." A Discord has a rule against "spam." A comments section has a rule against "bad-faith engagement." A mod removes someone's post, and the user doesn't defend the post itself. Instead they demand: draw the exact line. How many low-effort posts before someone's a low-effort poster? How many unsolicited links per hour is spam? Exactly how much snark flips a comment into bad faith? The mod, being honest, can't give a number, and the user acts like that settles it. If the mod does give a number, the user asks why one less isn't also spam and says the cutoff is arbitrary. If the mod says they judge case by case, the user says that's just unaccountable vibes. So every possible answer loses, and the user concludes the rule can't be enforced at all.

But the same user, a week later, will report an obvious spam bot or praise an obviously substantive post without any hesitation about where the line is. And they usually don't conclude that spam doesn't exist. They conclude something more like: no particular post can ever be actioned, because any line you draw will have a neighbouring case on the other side that's basically the same. Sometimes they'll even grant that the rule is doing useful work overall, but insist that each individual enforcement is illegitimate for exactly that reason.

I know there's a named fallacy for "there's no sharp boundary, therefore the distinction isn't real." But this feels different, or at least more specific: the person grants the distinction at the extremes and uses the fuzziness of the boundary as a veto on individual judgements, while never offering a workable alternative and never explaining why their own confident recognition of clear cases doesn't count. Is there an established name for this exact move? How does it differ from the no-sharp-boundary-so-the-distinction-is-fake fallacy and from motte-and-bailey? And if nothing fits, what would you call it? I want the carving — where the edges are, whether it's one move or two — not just a label.
