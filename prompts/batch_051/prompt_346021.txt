I'm on the board of a small (40-unit) housing co-op, and I've somehow become the person who vets all the "technology" proposals before the full board votes. Every few months a commitee brings us something: last year it was a key-fob entry system and a package locker with a camera; this year it's a face-recognition intercom and an app for booking the laundry room. The meetings keep splitting down the same line — half the board says a tool is just a tool, and the other half says its not neutral — and we talk past each other because we have no shared filter. A friend put the point to me this way: some devices basically force a particular arrangement of power if they're going to work at all (an entrance that's only reachable up three stairs doesn't have an opinion, but it has a policy), while others could work under many arrangements but quietly favor one because the default settings are doing a lot of the choosing, and a few really are close to neutral. That's the idea I want made usable, not turned into a reading group.

Write me a decision procedure we could actually run on a proposal, given that board members aren't tech experts, we meet once a month, a lawyer only checks legal exposure, and putting hardware in is hard to walk back. I want: the questions to ask in order; for each question, what counts as a good enough answer to move on; where the procedure stops with a no; where it hands the decision to the full membership as a values question rather than a facts question; and a sentence on what the procedure can and can't settle.

Then run it on the three proposals in front of us right now: (1) a key-fob entry system with the property manager keeping a log of who enters when; (2) a face-recognition intercom that unlocks the front door when it recognizes a resident; (3) an app-based laundry booking system that replaces the paper sign-up sheet and sends phone reminders. I suspect I'm going to like the procedure's answer on the laundry app least — let it say so anyway.

Hard cap: 500 words. If you're running over, cut the worked examples before you touch the procedure.
