Had a late-night argument with a friend about epiphenomenalism and now I cant stop thinking about why this debate never goes anywhere. Heres the shape of it. He kept pushing the self-stultification objection — the thing people also call the paradox of phenomenal judgment. If qualia are causally inert, then when I say that I'm in pain, the report is caused by physical processes, not by the pain itself. The pain is idle even with respect to my own saying. So my report would be exactly the same in a zombie twin with the same physical states but no pain at all, and therefore my report gives me no evidence that the pain exists. Epiphenomenalism, he said, cuts off the very judgments it's built on.

I kept pushing back with the tracking move you see in the literature. Sure, the pain doesn't cause the report, but the physical state that causes the report is the same state that correlates with the pain. In nearby worlds where that physical state occurs, the pain occurs too, so the report tracks it. The judgment isn't just lucky, even though the pain never pushes anything around. So my beliefs about my own qualia can still count as knowledge.

The weird thing is that we both accepted the exact same causal picture — qualia do nothing, judgments are entirely physical — and yet neither of us moved an inch in three hours. I've seen the same standoff in the professional debates too, and it never seems to resolve. My hunch is that this isnt really a disagreement about whether epiphenomenalism is true at all, but about when a report of a mental state can count as evidence for that state once the state is causally excluded — maybe a disagreement about standards of evidence rather than about the facts. My friend thinks that's a cop-out, and that it's just a plain incoherence the epiphenomenalist won't admit.

So what kind of disagreement is this actually? Verbal, substantive, different standards of evidence, different questions under one heading, or bedrock intuitions that neither side can argue for? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or should nobody expect progress, and why?
