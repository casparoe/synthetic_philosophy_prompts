Ok so I keep flip-flopping on whether to give up epistemic closure or Nozick's sensitivity condition, and I want someone to force me to price each option. Tracking has real systematic virtues: it gives a single mechanism that explains why I know I have hands (if I didn't, I wouldn't believe I did), why I don't know I'll lose a fair lottery before the draw (if I were going to win, I'd still believe I'd lose), and why I don't know I'm not a handless brain in a vat, all without conceding ordinary knowledge. But I also have a firm intuition that if I know I have hands and know that having hands entails not being a handless brain in a vat, then I can competantly deduce and thereby come to know that I'm not a handless brain in a vat. That deduction step is just how knowledge extends, and it doesn't feel like a parochial habit I can debunk. Nozick says no: my belief that I'm not a BIV isn't sensitive, because in the close world where I am one I'd still believe I'm not, so closure has to go. Which should yield? Should I debunk the closure intuition, amend or abandon tracking, or is there a reading where both survive (closure for justification, not knowledge, say, or sensitivity replaced by safety)? What would actually justify each of those moves, and what kind of case or consideration would settle which cost is worth paying?
