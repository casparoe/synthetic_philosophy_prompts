I spent most of my working life as a sedimentary geologist, and since retiring I have been running a small reading group for other former scientists on metaphysics. We have found ourselves going in circles on one of the oldest questions in the field: how many things are there, and which of them, if any, are basic. I would like you to act as a careful metaphysician who is also a good taxonomist, and build a decision tree that sorts readers into positions on monism and pluralism about what there is. It should behave like the diagnostic keys I used to teach with in field geology: each branch node must be a concrete claim or case a person can answer without already knowing the labels, not a question like "are you a monist?" Each leaf should carry a named position and a one-sentence note on its most awkward commitment.

These eight positions must appear as leaves, with rough glosses so that a smaller model without web access can work from them: existence monism (exactly one thing exists full stop); priority monism (exactly one fundamental thing, the cosmos, with many derivative things depending on it); Spinozist one-substance monism (one substance with infinitely many attributes and finite modes); mereological nihilism (only simples exist; there are no composite objects); atomism (the small parts are fundamental and wholes are nothing over and above them); moderate common-sense pluralism (many things, with parts and wholes equally real and neither prior); composition as identity (a whole just is its parts taken collectively); and hylomorphism (composite substances are matter shaped by form). Please seed the branch questions with at least these four cases: a pile of sand grains on a beach, a granite boulder and its mineral grains, a single quartz crystal, and the universe considered as a whole. Build from the logical space rather than from the list of names: first set out the yes/no questions, map the combinations of answers, locate each position in a cell, and for every unoccupied cell give a one-line diagnosis of whether it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Also flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate and say what extra question would pull them apart.

Before writing anything final, spend some private reasoning sketching the full logical space and checking that each leaf lands in a distinct cell or is explicitly flagged as not separable. Then return your complete answer as a single JSON object with these fields: preamble (one sentence telling a reader what the key does and that it sorts by commitments, not self-descriptions); branch_questions (an array of objects with id, question, allowed_answers, and distinction_tested); tree (an object with root and edges, where each edge has from, answer, and to); leaves (an array of objects with id, name, path expressed as a chain like "Q1.no → Q4.yes → Q7.no", one_sentence_description, and most_awkward_commitment); unoccupied_cells (an array of objects with answers, diagnosis being one of "incoherent", "unattractive", or "unexplored", and one_line_why); inseparable_pairs (an array of objects with positions, why_not_separable, and separating_question); and method_note (a short paragraph recording which combinations you collapsed or rejected and why).
