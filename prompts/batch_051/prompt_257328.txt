I retired a few years ago from a career in physical chemistry, and for the first time I'm actually reading philosophy instead of just saying I would. One thing I keep tripping over is the idea of incommensurable values and so-called hard choices. In my old line of work, if you said two results were incommensurable, that just meant somebody had failed to find the right converson factor, and once you found it the comparison snapped into place. So when I read that some values are incommensurable and that niether of two options is better than the other, I naturally think: fine, then they're equal for decision purposes, or the ranking is just unknown and I should go collect more evidence before choosing. But the literature seems to deny both of those, which leaves me a bit at sea. Here's a concrete case I've been chewing on: choosing between staying in a city where my grandchildren live and moving somewhere that would let me pursue a long-deferred research project in retirement. Suppose the family values and the intellectual values here are genuinely incommensurable. What exactly should I do? Is the rational choice to treat the options as tied and flip a coin, or to keep looking for some hidden common scale until one comes out ahead? I've also seen people say that in a hard choice you can 'create' reasons for one option by committing to it, but that sounds like dressing up an arbitrary pick after the fact.
