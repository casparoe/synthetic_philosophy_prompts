The Ashgrove Street Supper Club, a private dining society, adopted the following bylaw in 1987:

"Any member who accepts an invitation to a monthly supper and then fails to attend, without having notified the host at least six hours in advance, shall not be invited to the following month's supper."

A member has linked a calendar-management application to her email with instructions to accept any invitation that fits her stated availability. The application accepted the invitation to the supper on the 14th and placed it on her calendar, but she never saw the acceptance, and she never arrived. The host received no notice. Should she be treated, under the bylaw, as having accepted the invitation, so that she forfeits next month's invitation?

Argue this question from (a) the plain wording of the bylaw, (b) the purpose the rule serves in the club, and (c) what the 1987 drafters would most plausibly have said if asked about an auto-accepted RSVP. In doing so, address whether an RSVP is best understood as a promise, a forecast of one's own attendance, or a courtesy, and say which approach should govern here and why. If your answer would change on the assumption that the application could no longer be overridden once it had accepted, say so.
