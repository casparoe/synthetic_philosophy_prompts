A university adopts the following policy for contested grades. A student may appeal a grade to a departmental Appeals Committee, which must issue a written decision. But the policy refuses to treat any committee as authoritative: any decision of the Appeals Committee, whether it upholds or overturns the grade, can be appealed to a Faculty Appeals Committee, which reviews both the underlying grade and the lower committee's reasoning. The Faculty Committee's decision can likewise be appealed to a University Appeals Committee, and that decision can be appealed to a new Standing Review Commission, because the policy states that no body is final. More precisely, the policy lays down a general rule of ascent: for every body that issues a decision, there is a further body that may review it, and the same rule applies to that further body. There is no terminal body, and the drafters say this is deliberate: any terminal body might be mistaken, biased, or captured, so finality is rejected as an arbitrary stopping point. A grade is treated as settled only when no appeal is filed within the filing window; at each level the window is eight weeks. To keep the process from spiraling through endless tiers in obvious cases, committees are instructed to dispose of frivolous appeals quickly, but there is no ceiling on the number of tiers, since a ceiling would, by the drafters' own logic, reintroduce an arbitrary stopping point.

Treat this as a mechanism and analyze how it would actually behave. Walk through what it incentivizes among students, instructors, and the people drafted onto the committees; where contested grades actually come to rest in practice and why; what happens to the idea of a final grade and to equal treatment between students with different patience, resources, and appetite for procedural combat; and what new games open up at each level. Then give a definite verdict: does the policy deliver fair and timely resolution of grade disputes, and is the refusal of finality the real source of its problems or a red herring? If the mechanism is broken, say exactly what a terminal body would have supplied that the regress cannot, and whether the scheme can be repaired without merely reinstating an arbitrary final decision-maker.
