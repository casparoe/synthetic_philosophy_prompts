I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I keep running into a term I can't pin down: common knowledge. In the loose sense it seems to just mean that everyone knows something. In the stricter sense I half-remember from coordination problems and consensus protocols, it seems to mean that everyone knows that everyone knows it, and so on upward. I want a working definition that captures where the strict sense matters for coordination without losing the ordinary meaning.

Here are the cases I'd like the definition to classify:

1. In an all-hands meeting, a manager announces a reorg while everyone is in the room and can see that everyone else is there and listening.
2. In a poker game, a card is dealt face-up on the table in full view of every player.
3. Two engineers independently discover the same flaky-test bug, and neither has any idea that the other has noticed it.
4. On a team, everyone believes a project will be cancelled, and everyone has inferred from everyone else's behavior that everyone else believes it, but no one has ever said it aloud.
5. A decision is posted in a shared Slack channel that the whole team belongs to; one member has notifications disabled and never reads it, while everyone else reads it.
6. In the classic two-generals problem from distributed systems, two processes need to agree to commit at a fixed time; messages can be dropped, and every message is acknowledged, but each acknowledgement can also be dropped, so neither process can ever be certain the other received the latest message.

I'm confident that cases 1 and 2 should count and that case 3 should not; I honestly don't know how to rule on 4, 5, and 6, and I'd rather the definition decide them than my hunches.

Please state the definition up front in one sentence, run it through all six cases, flag any verdict where the definition feels wrong or needs defending, revise or defend as you see fit, and end by telling me which of your verdicts is least secure. Keep the entire answer to at most 500 words.
