I am a PhD student in political philosophy, working on reparations for historical injustice. In a draft chapter I have to set out the position I actually hold before subjecting it to criticism, and I would like you to audit the following set of commitments for coherence. The commitments, as I would currently defend them, are:

1. That the direct victims and direct wrongdoers are all dead does not by itself extinguish a reparative claim; the passage of time creates practical and evidential difficulties, but it is not an independent moral reason against repair.
2. The duty falls on the state as a continuing corporate agent; it can owe reparations even where no current citizen is individually at fault and none has personally received an identifiable benefit from the wrong.
3. Reparative justice is not distributive justice: a community may still be owed reparations even if it is no longer among the worst-off, and a community's present deprivation does not by itself create a reparative claim.
4. The quantum of reparations should be fixed by counterfactual loss — the difference between the descendants' actual position and the position they would now occupy had the wrong never occurred.
5. Where a specific stolen asset remains identifiable, reparations should take the form of the return of that asset or its equivalent value, and there is no separate entitlement to a global counterfactual accounting.
6. In the case of a confiscated estate later improved by the wrongdoer, the descendants are owed the land, but not the increase in its value attributable to improvements whose costs the wrongdoer itself bore.

I would like this treated as an audit, not a defence. Please structure your response in three sections:

1. 'Apparent tensions that dissolve' (about 200 words): identify pairs that look incompatible but are not, and say why.
2. 'Genuine tensions, ordered by severity' (about 400 words): for each, describe the conflict and what would have to give to remove it.
3. 'Cheapest repair' (about 200 words): recommend the single revision that preserves most of the position, and state what it costs.
