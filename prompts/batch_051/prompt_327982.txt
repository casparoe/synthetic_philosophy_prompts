I've been reading a bit about decision theory after building a small tool to log my own choices, and a mate who's really into the rationality scene sent me an argument he insists shows that anyone without a complete utility function over every possible gamble is just irrational. It's bugging me because the conclusion feels way too strong, but I can't find the step wich breaks. His version goes:

(1) The von Neumann–Morgenstern representation theorem says that if your preferences over gambles satisfy four conditions—completeness, transitivity, independence, and continuity—then those preferences can be represented as maximising expected utility for some utility function and probability assignments.
(2) If you violate any of those four conditions, there will be some chain of bets or trades, each of which looks fine on its own, that together guarantee you end up with a sure loss (a money pump).
(3) Completeness is one of those conditions, and it says that for any two gambles you either prefer the first, prefer the second, or are indifferent between them.
(4) But I don't have a ranking for plenty of pairs. For instance, I have no settled preference between a 30% chance of losing my job next month and a 10% chance of spending six months in a randomly chosen town I've never heard of, followed by a small chance of a prize I have no idea how to value, like being given a parrot. I've never considered that comparison, so there is no fact about which I'd pick.
(5) So, by (2) and (3), I'm irrational just because I've violated completeness, and there's some money pump out there I'd walk into.
(6) That would mean nearly everyone is irrational just for not having made up their minds about comparisons they've never even faced.

I'd really like to hang onto the idea that coherent preferences matter and that being predictably exploitable is a genuine failing, but I don't want to accept that I'm irrational until I've ranked every possible pair of gambles. Where does this argument go wrong, if it does? Or do I just have to bite the bullet? Also, could you write your answer in British English, since I'll be showing it to a study group over here.
