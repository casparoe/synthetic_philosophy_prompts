A friend who follows Hungarian politics insists that what's happened there since 2010 is democratic backsliding. My cousin says no, that's just what the losing side calls it when an incumbent wins a few elections and changes the rules it was elected to change — no different in kind from any party passing laws it campaigned on. I'd like to figure out wheter this dispute is even resolvable by evidence. Pick a country (Hungary, Poland, Turkey, the US, or another you know well) and design the best empirical test you can of the claim that it has undergone democratic backsliding, as opposed to ordinary partisan victory plus policy change. Be specific about which indicators you'd watch (formal rule changes, how often the government loses in courts or in genuinely competitive elections, media ownership, changes to electoral laws, treatment of opposition mayors or civil servants, that sort of thing), and what thresholds you'd set in advance to count as evidence one way or the other. Then say what a positive result would and would not show, and where no test can reach: does the word backsliding quietly do the normative work, and is that a flaw in the concept or the whole point of it?
