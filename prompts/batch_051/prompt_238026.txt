Consider the following distinction from ergodicity economics: when a risky prospect is faced once by many people (or by many parallel copies of one person), the relevant quantity is the ensemble average; when it is faced repeatedly by a single person whose wealth or wellbeing compounds along one path, the relevant quantity is the time-average growth rate of that path. Please describe the most important implications of this distinction for an individual deciding whether to accept a positive-expected-value risk that will recur across their life, such as a volatile career, a concentrated investment, or a small repeated chance of ruin. Lead with the single most important implication as your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning and the supporting implications, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
