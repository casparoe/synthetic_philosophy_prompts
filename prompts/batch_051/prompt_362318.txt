I run a small Minecraft server with about forty regular players, and I am its only moderator. The written rules are minimal: no destroying other players' builds, no taking items that are not yours, and no harassment. I have server logs, hourly backups for rollbacks, and a plugin that records who opened which chest. Every week I have to settle disputes—griefed builds, stolen rare items, harassment in chat or by players who follow someone around—and I want a principle I can stand behind rather than a gut feeling. The stakes are modest but real to the players: I can restore builds, return items, mute or ban people, and my decisions are not reviewed by anyone.

Please act as a professional philosopher working on applied ethics and the metaphysics of virtual worlds. First, briefly brainstorm the two or three candidate principles about when a virtual object or virtual harm should be treated as real, and say what each principle would imply for moderation. Then turn the strongest candidate into a reusable decision procedure I can run in under fifteen minutes per case with no formal training: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a game-internal response, and where it escalates to treating the case as real harm to a real person, such as a genuine penalty or involving a trusted adult. Be explicit about which steps are judgment calls that cannot be made mechanical. Then apply the procedure to these three cases and flag any place where the procedure gives an answer I will not like: (1) a player destroyed a build that another player had spent many hours on, and a complete backup exists from an hour earlier; (2) a player mocks and mimics a younger player in chat and in-game behavior without breaking any written rule; (3) a rare item that sells for about fifty dollars on third-party marketplaces was taken from a locked chest by exploiting a game glitch. Finally, say what the procedure cannot settle and where I should hand a case to an adult instead of deciding it myself.
