Accident investigation boards and hospital mortality reviews routinely list things like failure to inspect a boiler or failure to recognize sepsis among the causes of a disaster or death. So institutions that otherwise lean hard on physical evidence are also treating pure absences—things that didn't happen—as causes. What theory of causation does that practice presuppose? Does it commit them to saying omissions are real causes, or is it just shorthand for the idea that the outcome would have been different if someone had acted? And if you held that only events can literally be causes, what would these reports have to say instead, and what would be lost by rewriting them that way?
