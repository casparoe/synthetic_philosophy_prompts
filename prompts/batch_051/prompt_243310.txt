Consider the traditional example, made famous by Daniel Dennett, of the digger wasp Sphex ichneumoneus. The wasp paralyses a cricket, drags it to the mouth of her burrow, leaves it there, enters the burrow to inspect the nest, returns, and then drags the cricket inside. If an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches while the wasp is inside, the wasp locates it, drags it back to the mouth of the burrow, and enters to inspect once more; the sequence can be repeated indefinitely. Stipulate for the purposes of this question that the traditional description is exactly accurate.

Dennett uses the example to draw a contrast between sphexish rigidity and the flexibility he takes to be required for free action. On this view, the wasp is unfree not because its behaviour is caused, but because its behaviour is insensitive to a novel fact — that the cricket has already been inspected and moved — and it cannot treat that fact as a reason to alter its routine.

Now vary the example. Imagine a mutant strain of Sphex with a finite re-inspection counter. In every other respect its routine is identical to the wild type's. When the cricket is displaced at the burrow mouth, the mutant repeats the inspection routine; but after the cricket has been moved away three times during a single provisioning run, the mutant stops re-inspecting and drags the cricket straight into the burrow, even if the experimenter moves it again. The counter resets on the next provisioning run, and no amount of experience with the experimenter's trick shortens the run of three re-inspections; the counter is as fixed as the wild type's routine.

Does this variation force a qualification of the original contrast between sphexish rigidity and genuine flexibility? Is the mutant less sphexish than the wild-type wasp, or merely sphexish in a more complicated way? What, if anything, would have to be added before the mutant counted as flexible or free in Dennett's sense, and would that addition satisfy a libertarian who insists that free action requires the ability to do otherwise in the very same circumstances? Finally, state what you take the original example to show, and say whether the variant strengthens or weakens that conclusion.
