I'm pulling together a list of arguments for the view that effort — the striving, struggling, grinding part of an activity — is valuable in itself, not merely because of what it produces. The ones I have so far:

(1) The achievement argument. Climbing a mountain the hard way is a greater achievement than riding a cable car to the same summit, even with the same view at the top. The difference is difficulty, and difficulty consists largely in the amount and intensity of effort required. So if achievements have value over and above their outcomes, the effort inside the achievement must be carrying part of that value, not just pointing to it.

(2) The ownership argument. Effort is what makes an outcome mine rather than a gift or an accident. What I earn through effort seems to have a kind of value that windfalls lack, even when the windfall is bigger. If agency itself matters, effort is how agency shows up in the result.

(3) The meaning argument. A life without struggle, even a pleasant and successful one, tends to feel empty. Striving for an end gives a life a point in a way that having the end handed to you does not. So the struggle isn't just a cost paid in order to acquire meaning; it partly constitutes that meaning.

(4) The perfectionist or self-overcoming argument. Effortful action is the exercise of rational and volitional capacities against resistance. On perfectionist views, exercising distinctively human capacities well is good in itself; effort of the right kind just is that exercise. Nietzsche's self-overcoming is the vivid version of the thought.

I also know the character-building line — effort makes you stronger, more patient, tougher — but I've set it aside because it looks plainly instrumental: those traits are downstream effects. If you want to resurrect it, you'd need to show the traits aren't downstream effects but partly constituted by the striving, which sounds like it collapses into (4).

I have a lingering sense there's a fifth argument I'm missing. Give me an argument that effort is valuable in itself that is genuinely different from the four above — not a restatement, a special case, or a mashup of items already on the list.
