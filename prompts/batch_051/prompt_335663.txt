Consider the following argument about Kavka's toxin puzzle. A benefactor offers you one million dollars, to be deposited in your account tomorrow morning, on one condition: at midnight tonight you must genuinely intend to drink a nonlethal but severely nauseating toxin tomorrow afternoon. Nothing prevents you from changing your mind after the deposit, and once the money is in your account you will have no further reason to drink. Now evaluate this argument:

(1) At midnight, you know that tomorrow afternoon drinking will be purely costly: the money is already secured, and the toxin will make you ill.
(2) A rational agent does not perform an action she knows will leave her worse off overall when abstaining costs nothing; so you will not drink tomorrow afternoon.
(3) Since you know the setup and know you are rational, you know now that you will not drink.
(4) You cannot form a genuine, wholehearted intention to do what you know you will not do.
(5) Therefore, you cannot form the intention at midnight, and the condition for receiving the money cannot be satisfied.

Is the argument valid? If it is not, identify the exact step at which it fails and give the strongest reading of that step on which it is most plausibly true. If the argument is valid, say which premise you would reject and why. In particular, assess whether step (4) is best understood as a conceptual claim about the nature of intention, an empirical claim about human motivation, or something else, and explain what your answer implies about the difference between intending to act and predicting that one will act.
