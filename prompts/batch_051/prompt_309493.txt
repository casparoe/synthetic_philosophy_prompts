I keep ending up defending divine simplicity: the view that God has no composition or parts of any kind, so that God's existence, essence, and each of God's attributes (omnipotence, omniscience, goodness, and so on) are strictly identical with each other and with God — not just necessarily co-extensive, but literally one and the same reality. I want to stress-test this rather than defend it. What would have to be true for it to be false? Give me the arguments, consequences, counterexamples, and intuitions that count against it, sorted by how decisive they'd be — including things defenders themselves would concede are hard cases or real costs, not just what critics say. I'd be especially moved by a clean argument that it turns God into a abstract object, or that it makes God's free action impossible. And I want an honest verdict on whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that's a problem for a metaphysical position of this kind.
