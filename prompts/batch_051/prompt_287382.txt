I am on my high school's Lincoln-Douglas debate team, and our captain ends many disagreements by saying that no amount of "is" can ever add up to an "ought." Last week he invoked it against my argument that we are obligated to adopt a policy because doing so would measurably reduce suffering: he insisted that a reduction in suffering is a fact about how the world would be, and facts about how the world is cannot by themselves justify a conclusion about what we should do. I suspect this slogan hides several distinct claims under one sentence, some nearly undeniable and others far more demanding. What are the distinct precise claims it could be expressing, laid out as a menu from the most trivial to the most radical? For each, say whether it is clearly true, substantive and defensible, or likely false, and identify which reading or readings would actually license his move against my argument.
