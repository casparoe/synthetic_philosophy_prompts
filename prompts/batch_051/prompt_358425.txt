I teach an upper-level seminar in philosophy of mathematics and formal logic, 12 students, and I'm writing the final. I want one essay question on Skolem's paradox, worth 20 marks, that a candidate could answer in about 90 minutes. It should force them past reciting the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem: I want them to work through the specific case where a countable model of ZFC has a set it treats as uncountable — its powerset of the naturals — even though that set is countable from outside, and then say exactly how much of the paradox the standard "the bijection just isn't in the model" reply kills, and how much of the worry about absolute uncountability survives. Please write the question, a model answer around 800 words, and a marking scheme that seperates an A from a B by naming the moves each must contain, plus the one or two misunderstandings or slide-bys that should cost marks. In the model answer, keep a clear line between what's settled mathematics and what's still genuinely contested in philosophy of set theory, and don't present any contested reading as the consensus.
