Next week in my seminar on the philosophy of physics and information, we are reading Ted Chiang's story "Exhalation" alongside the Maxwell's demon literature: the original thought experiment in which a demon sorts fast and slow molecules, Szilard's one-molecule heat engine, and Landauer's principle that erasing a bit of information carries an unavoidable thermodynamic cost. The story's narrator is an anatomist in a world of mechanical beings driven by compressed argon, and he dissects his own brain to conclude that thought is carried by the flow of air from higher to lower pressure — a difference that is steadily equalizing throughout the story's closed universe, so the end of thought is already set. I would like a critical reading of the story through that physics-of-information lens. Does the story slide between thermodynamic entropy, the information-theoretic cost of erasure, and the idea that a mind requires a continuous stream of differences to remain a mind?
