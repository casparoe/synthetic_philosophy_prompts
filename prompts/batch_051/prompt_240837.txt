Construct a thought experiment, engineered to specification, in which epistemic contextualism and pragmatic encroachment give opposite verdicts on a single ordinary-life knowledge attribution. The case is for illustrating the precise point at which the two views, which agree on the familiar low-stakes/high-stakes bank cases, come apart: the subject's practical stakes are high while the attributor's conversational context remains low.

The scenario must satisfy all of the following:

(i) There is a subject S and a proposition p that S believes truly on the basis of evidence E.

(ii) S faces high practical stakes with respect to p: acting as if p true would be very costly for S if p is false.

(iii) An attributor A, in a conversation whose context imposes only a low epistemic standard, asserts "S knows that p." A's context must not inherit S's high stakes—whether because A is unaware of them, because they are not salient in A's conversation, or because of some other engineered feature; specify which.

(iv) Under contextualism, the truth of A's attribution is set by the low epistemic standard governing A's context. Under pragmatic encroachment, whether S knows is set by the threshold that S's own stakes impose on S's evidence. The evidence E must therefore be good enough to meet the first standard but not good enough to meet the second.

(v) The scenario must fit in one paragraph, be realistic and ordinary (no lotteries, demons, fake barns, brain-in-vats, perfect predictors, or radical skeptical setups), and must not stipulate the verdicts: the divergence must be generated from the two theories' rules, not from the story's declaring who is right.

Then do the check. State the proposition p, S's evidence and method, what makes S's stakes high and A's low, and why each theory gives the verdict it does: why contextualism counts A's utterance true, and why pragmatic encroachment says S does not know, so that A's utterance comes out false. Identify the feature of the case that does the work, say how the two verdicts converge if that feature is removed, and finish by saying which of the two verdicts is the harder one to accept in the case as built, together with the strongest attack a defender of that harder verdict would make in reply.
