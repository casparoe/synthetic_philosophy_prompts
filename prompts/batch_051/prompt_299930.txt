You are an expert philosopher of mathematics and biology. Please think carefully, work the arithmetic explicitly, and try not to fall back on the standard moral of a familiar example before checking how far it survives the variation below.

The familiar example is the periodical cicada of eastern North America, which emerges in huge synchronized broods only every 13 or 17 years. One widely discussed biological explanation of these prime-numbered cycles is that a prime period minimizes how often an emergence coincides with the population peaks of periodic predators or parasites. A 12-year cycle, for example, aligns with a 2-, 3-, 4-, or 6-year predator cycle at every single emergence because those numbers divide 12, while a 13- or 17-year cycle aligns with a shorter predator cycle of period p only every 13p or 17p years. More precisely, under the usual idealization of perfectly periodic cycles, for a cicada cycle of length c and a predator cycle of length p, the fraction of the cicada's own emergences that coincide with predator peaks is gcd(c, p)/p, where gcd is the greatest common divisor. The philosophical moral usually drawn is that the property doing the explanatory work is a mathematical one, primeness itself, and this supports the enhanced indispensability argument, on which mathematics that plays an indispensable role in a good empirical explanation should be accepted as more than a mere bookkeeping device.

Now vary the case. Suppose the only predators that matter have fixed cycles of 4 and 6 years, and the cicada-like lineage can evolve toward any integer cycle length from 10 to 30 years. Run the standard argument inside this simplified model. Show explicitly which cycle lengths the standard criterion counts as optimal, and in particular whether a composite period such as 25 does exactly as well as the primes in that range even though it is not prime. Then say what this does to the philosophical payoff: in the simplified model, is the explanatory property primality or merely being coprime to the actual predator periods, and does the enhanced indispensability argument still go through, and if so with what ontological conclusions? Finally, judge whether the simplified model refutes or merely refines the usual moral drawn from the real cicadas. Can the appeal to primality be defended as the only property that guarantees non-coincidence with every possible shorter predator cycle at once, and if so, is that defence doing biological or logical work? Walk through each step of the reasoning, then give your overall verdict.
