i'm a software engineer on a platform team and i keep running into design docs that describe systems as "Darwinian" or "evolutionary" or say a component "undergoes natural selection." I've been reading a little Karl Popper on the side, and I hit a passage from his 1977 Darwin Lecture, "Natural Selection and the Emergence of Mind," that made me wonder whether we're using these words way too loosely. The passage goes like this:

"The selection of a kind of behavior out of a randomly offered repertoire may be an act of choice, even an act of free will. I am an indeterminist; and in discussing indeterminism I have often regretfully pointed out that quantum indeterminacy does not seem to help us; for the amplification of something like, say, radioactive disintegration processes would not lead to human action or even animal action, but only to random movements. I have changed my mind on this issue. A choice process may be a selection process, and the selection may be from some repertoire of random events, without being random in its turn. This seems to me to offer a promising solution to one of our most vexing problems, and one by downward causation."

I know Popper also at some point said natural selection was almost untestable because survival of the fittest is close to tautology, and then I gather he softened that view. I don't have a philosophy background, so I need this translated into something usable.

What I want is a practical checklist I can run in a design review to decide when it's legitimate to describe a process in our software as a Darwinian selection process, in something like Popper's sense, and when it's just a metaphor or even an unfalsifiable redescription. I need questions to ask in order, what counts as enough of an answer for each, and where I should stop and admit that the call is a judgment rather than something you can read off the code. Then apply the checklist to three examples: a fuzz testing harness that mutates inputs and keeps the ones that find new code paths; a genetic algorithm that evolves network topologies; and an A/B testing pipeline that keeps whichever variant has the higher conversion rate. If the procedure says one of these is not really Darwinian selection, or says the label is doing no explanatory work, I want to know. And wherever Popper's tautology worry matters, flag where someone could make the claim unfalsifiable by redefining "fitness" after seeing the result.
