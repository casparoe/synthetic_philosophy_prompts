So I run a one-off workshop for about twenty undergraduates, mostly non-philosophy majors, and I've got 75 minutes. The aim is to make them feel the force of the problem of choosing an epoch for a calendar rather than just talk about it. The takeaway I'm after is that there is no neutral year zero: every candidate epoch is part pure coordination device and part substantive claim about which events, people, or processes get to sit at the center of shared time. By the end I'd like them to be able to pull apart three things the phrase 'year zero' smushes together: the choice of origin point, the numbering convention (is there a zero, and are earlier dates counted as negative numbers or as named eras?), and the era label attached to the count. I also want them to notice concretely that the usual BCE/CE count has no year zero — the year before 1 CE is 1 BCE — and that quietly inserting one would shift the labels on every earlier date by one. Its not a harmless bookkeeping tweak.

The idea should come out of what happens, not out of me lecturing afterwards, and I want the words 'epoch' and 'year zero' to stay out of my mouth until the debrief. Something like: split them into groups, give each group a different job or set of constraints — astronomers who want an origin event that could be identified without any written record, archivists who care about continuity with existing databases and documents, diplomats who want something every culture can live with, systems programmers who actually have to implement date arithmetic and don't want negative years or off-by-one bugs. Maybe give them a menu of candidates rather than letting them propose anything: a physical event, a political founding, a religious date, a nice round number, a natural cycle, that sort of thing. Then in plenary force the groups to merge on one shared scheme so they run into the fact that everyone agrees the choice is partly arbitrary and yet they can't agree on which arbitrary thing to choose. If there's a way to make the no-year-zero arithmetic bite during the game — a group computing the interval between a date just before the proposed origin and a date just after it and getting two different answers depending on whether they insert a zero — that would be perfect.

Please give me: the roles or rules with enough specificity that someone who hasn't read any background could facilitate, a 75-minute timeline, a debrief script that maps specific moments in the activity onto the conceptual points (arbitrary convention vs value-laden choice vs coordination), a short list of what predictably goes wrong when this is run and how to handle it, and an honest note on which part of the philosophical point the activity can't reproduce and where I'll have to just say it myself. If you think the frame is off — if an activity is the wrong medium for this, or I'm running together two different senses of 'year zero,' or the coordination problem and the value-ladenness problem can't both be made salient in one session — tell me directly instead of giving me something that looks like a lesson plan.
