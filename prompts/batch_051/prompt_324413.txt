I have been turning over a structural analogy between the sorites paradox and the statistical mechanics of phase transitions, and I cannot decide whether it is deep or superficial.

On the side of vagueness: one grain is not a heap, and adding a single grain never turns a non-heap into a heap; yet a heap is composed of nothing but grains. The predicate 'heap' seems to have no boundary located at the level of individual grains, even though the heap/non-heap distinction is robust in ordinary use.

On the side of physics: in a ferromagnet, bulk magnetization appears as the temperature drops below the Curie point. Yet in any finite collection of spins, the magnetization is a continuous function of temperature; no individual spin 'switches' at the transition, and the sharp change emerges only in the thermodynamic limit, through spontaneous symmetry breaking. The phase boundary is thus real at the macro level while having no micro-level locus.

The analogy would be this: a vague predicate stands to its individual grains as a phase stands to its spins. Adding one grain is like lowering the temperature an infinitesimal amount — no single step matters, but the accumulated steps do. If this were more than metaphor, then the lack of a micro-level cutoff would not by itself show that 'heap' lacks a sharp boundary at some coarse-grained level, any more than the smoothness of spin behavior shows that 'magnetized' and 'unmagnetized' fail to pick out distinct phases.

What I want to know is whether the structure genuinely carries over, and where it breaks. In particular: (1) Is there an analogue of the thermodynamic limit for a heap, or does the finiteness of any actual pile defeat the parallel immediately? (2) Phase transitions involve an order parameter and a broken symmetry; what, if anything, plays those roles for 'heap', given that its application depends on purposes, arrangement, and spacing rather than a physical observable? (3) If the parallel partially transfers, does it say anything about the standard options for vagueness — supervaluationism, many-valued accounts, epistemicism — or does it merely move the sorites up to the macro level, where the same puzzle about boundaries reappears? I would rather have an account of where the two phenomena come apart than a bare verdict one way or the other.
