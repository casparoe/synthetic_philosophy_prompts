I'm a PhD student in cognitive science, sitting in on a philosophy of perception seminar, and I've realised I can't actually state what cognitive penetration is in a way that matches my own verdicts. I know a lot of ink has been spilt over whether cognition 'reaches into' perception, but when I try to define it I just wave at 'top-down influence on perceptual experience,' which is way to loose. So I'd like you to reverse-engineer the principle from my case judgments, or tell me which of them have to go.

Here are the cases and what I think. (1) After I've measured the two lines in the arrowhead illusion and know they're equal, they still look unequal; I'd say that's obviously not penetration because the knowledge can't get in. (2) An experienced chest radiologist sees a faint nodule in an x-ray that a med student staring at the same film simply cannot see; I'd say that's perceptual learning, not penetration, because her past experience has rewired her visual system. (3) In a psych experiment, people asked to make a grey banana-shaped patch perfectly grey set it slightly blue, as if the remembered yellowness of bananas was leaking into the grey; this one I do want to call penetration, even though it also comes from past experience, which I realise makes (2) and (3) an uncomfortable pair for me. (4) People wearing a heavy backpack say a hill looks steeper than it does; I want to say that's not penetration, just effort biasing their report. (5) Thirsty people estimate a water bottle as closer than it is; here I lean yes, the desire makes it actually look closer — but I also see that (4) and (5) look like the same kind of case, so one of these verdicts may be doing the work.

So: give me a precise principle (or a small set of candidate principles) that best captures these verdicts, say explicitly what it predicts for a new case — say, someone who has just watched a horror film and 'hears' footsteps in an empty house — and then tell me which of my verdicts a simple version of that principle has to overturn, and which of my verdicts is doing the most to force complications. If you think the right move is a two-part account (e.g. penetration of perceptual content vs. mere biasing of report), formalize it.
