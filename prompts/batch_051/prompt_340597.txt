In evolutionary theory there is a familiar line of reasoning—call it the bottleneck argument—for deciding whether a collective counts as a Darwinian individual, a genuine unit of selection, rather than as a cooperating group or an ecological community. The argument runs roughly as follows. For a collective to evolve as one unit, its lower-level parts must share a reproductive fate; otherwise selection on the parts favors cheaters and tends to dissolve the collective from within. A developmental bottleneck is supposed to secure that shared fate: because the collective develops anew from one cell or a small propagule, its parts are clonally related and multiply only through the collective, which keeps internal conflict low. The criterion sorts a paradigmatic animal from a school of fish, and it has been used to ask whether an ant colony is one organism or many.

The development I want you to address is the holobiont. A holobiont is a host together with its persistent microbial symbionts: a coral with its photosynthetic algae, an aphid with its nutrient-providing intracellular bacteria, a lichen as a fungus together with its algae. Many holobionts are deeply functionally integrated—the host cannot persist or reproduce without a metabolic service the symbiont supplies—and yet the symbionts are often reacquired from the environment each generation rather than passed through the host's reproductive bottleneck. Write an essay on whether the bottleneck argument survives this development. Does the holobiont case show that a bottleneck is unnecessary for Darwinian individuality, or that integration plus shared fate can be achieved by other means such as partner fidelity, host policing, or co-dispersal, and the argument therefore needs repair? Identify which premise of the argument is actually under pressure and say whether a weaker, repaired version can handle obligate symbioses without collapsing into the view that anything functionally integrated counts as an organism. The essay should defend a definite verdict, not close on a survey of possibilities.
