Consider this passage from the opening of the nineteenth chapter of the Zhuangzi, in James Legge's translation, where the chapter is titled "The Full Understanding of Life":

"He who understands the conditions of Life does not strive after what is of no use to life; and he who understands the conditions of Destiny does not strive after what is beyond the reach of knowledge."

I want a working definition of wu-wei, not as a historical survey but as a concept that can do real classificatory work. The definition should explain why this passage is more than prudential advice about efficient effort: the person described is not simply avoiding wasted motion, but is said to stand in a different relation to action itself. It should also handle the familiar paradox that deliberately trying to be effortless seems to be a form of trying, so naively defining wu-wei as "not trying" makes the concept look either unattainable or self-undermining.

Here are the hard cases I want your definition to decide. Some I regard as clear, some genuinely unsettled; I want to see where the definition forces a surprising verdict. Classify each as wu-wei or not, say why on your definition, and flag any where your definition gives the wrong intuitive answer or no answer.

1. A parent yanks her child out of traffic. Afterward she says the movement came without a felt decision, and she only realized what had happened when she saw the child in her arms.
2. A novice meditator who has been told to let her breathing go at its own pace is trying hard to stop controlling it, with the result that her breathing becomes more forced and deliberate.
3. A chess program plays the opening move from a precomputed table: no deliberation, no felt effort, no resistance.
4. A person unreflectively checks his phone every morning from entrenched habit, even though he regrets it later.
5. A jazz improviser is actively monitoring the harmony, anticipating the next change, and steering himself away from a phrase he knows would be a cliché, but reports that his fingers feel as if they are choosing.
6. A person stuck on a problem deliberately sets it aside after intense effort, and then finds the solution arriving while he is doing something else.
7. An experienced craftsperson under time pressure mutters instructions to herself, every movement deliberate, yet the sequence flows from long practice and time passes unnoticed.
8. A person abandons a difficult project and tells herself she is "letting things happen," but in fact she is avoiding the discomfort of failing.

Please propose a definition, run it against each case, and then do two further things. First, state which of your verdicts is least secure and what would make it break. Second, explain how your definition ought to read the quoted passage: specifically, whether the passage's understanding of "not striving" should be understood as the absence of effort, the absence of self-assertion, the absence of resistance to circumstances, or something else.
