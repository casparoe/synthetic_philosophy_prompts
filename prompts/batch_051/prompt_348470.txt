I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about whether there's any intellectually respectable version of the thought that history has a moral direction. Everyone knows the slogan about the arc bending toward justice, but I don't want to lean on that because its usually used as rhetorical comfort, not as a claim with actual content. The position I keep getting pulled toward after interviews is a deliberately deflated one. Not providence — no plan, no guarantor, no secret force behind events. It's more like a ratchet: once certain institutional technologies show up (literacy, cheap long-distance trade, states that have to collect taxes and keep records, courts of appeal, mass publics), they change what it costs to be cruel. Some expansions of moral concern become self-reinforcing, and some reversals become expensive in ways they weren't before, so over long stretches the default drift is toward wider moral recognition even though decades-long reversals happen all the time. So the arc bends on average, moving from local and tribal toward more universal and impersonal forms of accountability, but not because anything wants it to.

For this piece I want to know where that position is actually exposed. What would have to be true for this deflated directional claim to be wrong? I can gesture at some candidates — a sustained reversal that doesn't just happen but sticks and spreads; evidence that the apparent expansion is mostly an artifact of what gets preserved, taught, and counted; historical cases where the same institutional technologies went the other way and made cruelty cheaper instead. But I'd like a real taxonomy. Which findings would be decisive, which would just be awkward, and which are only logical possibilities but dead? What would a defender of this ratchet view concede counts against it, that critics never bring up? And has the view been softened so much that it can't be wrong anymore — and should I treat that as a problem with the view or just as the price of doing history? Also, could you write your answer in American English.
