I am a database engineer who reverse-engineers legacy schemas and plans system migrations; I have no background in philosophy of science. I have encountered a position called structural realism, summarized roughly as follows: mature scientific theories warrant belief in their structure, the network of relations, symmetries, and dependencies captured in their equations and models, rather than in the intrinsic nature of the objects those relations connect; and when one theory succeeds another, it is this structure, not the relata, that persists. Some versions go further and say the relata are nothing over and above their place in the structure. Restate this position in the vocabulary I actually work in: schemas, entities, attributes, primary keys, foreign keys, relationships, constraints, referential integrity, and the difference between a schema and the rows that instantiate it. Work through the central notions one by one — structure, relata, theory change, continuity — and then give me a residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which require real distortion, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary. In particular, I want to know whether the claim that the schema survives while the entities do not is a fair database paraphrase of structural realism, whether primary keys are a fair analogue of relata, and whether the stronger claim that relata are nothing but their position in the structure is coherent in my terms or only looks coherent because database design already assumes the entities it is being asked to abandon. Does structural realism reduce to the familiar engineering point that the schema is what migrates across a rewrite, or is it trying to say something my vocabulary cannot express?
