I’ve provisionally adopted illusionism about phenomenal consciousness: the view that introspection does not reveal real, private, intrinsic qualitative properties, but instead produces a useful but non-veridical model of our own mental states. I’m not asking for the standard case for or against the view. I want a map of what would actually count against it. Which empirical findings, dissociation syndromes, altered-state reports, introspection-based arguments, or conceptual results should a committed illusionist treat as genuine defeaters rather than merely awkward complications? Which of these would the view’s defenders themselves concede as counting against it, and which would they dismiss as already absorbed? Where does illusionism bet on downstream claims about attention, global workspace, metacognition, cognitive access, or introspection that could simply turn out false? I’d like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely uncomfortable, along with an honest assessment of whether illusionism has been qualified into unfalsifiability—and, if it has, whether that should bother someone who holds the view.
