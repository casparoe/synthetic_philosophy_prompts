I'm a PhD student in philosophy of biology and I'm trying to get straight on Machamer, Darden, and Craver's account of mechanistic explanation before I lean on it in my dissertation chapter. I was reading a survey article that summarizes them, and I've got a feeling its not quite right in a couple of places, but the author is a professional philosopher of science so I don't trust my memory. The article's summary goes roughly like this:

"Machamer, Darden, and Craver think of a mechanism as an organized set of entities and activities that regularly produces a change from start or set-up conditions to finish or termination conditions. The entities are the component parts, and the activities are the things those parts do, such as binding or diffusing. To explain a phenomenon mechanistically is to identify the relevant entities and lay out the organized sequence of their operations; once the parts are correctly identified and the order of operations is right, the phenomenon has been explained. Because activities are just what entities do, the explanatory weight is carried by the entities and their spatial and temporal organization. And since a complete mechanism description bottoms out when you reach the smallest working parts, the account is in principle reductionistic, even if in practice scientists stop at whatever level their field finds convenient. On this picture, mechanistic explanation is an alternative to covering-law explanation: the organized sequence of steps does the work that laws were supposed to do."

Two things in particular are bugging me. First, the claim that activities are "just what entities do" and that entities carry the explanatory weight — I thought a big part of MDC's point was that activities are on an explanatory par with entities, not reducible to them, so calling them "just what entities do" would be exactly the kind of thing they argue against. Second, I didn't think they were reductionists in principle; I vaguely remember something about "bottoming out" being relative to the level of organization a given field cares about rather than the smallest physical parts. But maybe I'm importing that from Craver's later book or confusing them with someone else. I'm not after citations or a reading list — I can chase the texts up myself. What I want is an accuracy audit: go through the summary and tell me what a careful defender of MDC would accept, what they'd push back on, and whether the overall gloss is fair or misleading. And since I'll be relying on your verdict: please say what would change your mind about it — which claim, if the texts turned out one way rather than another, would flip your assessment — and where you think your reconstruction is most likely to fail.
