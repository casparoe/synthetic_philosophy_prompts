we read the Simone de Beauvoir bit in class (the one where she says you aren't born a woman, you become one) and I got into an argument with my friend and now I cant get out of it. One kid said it just means gender is a social thing. But another kid said that if it's a social thing, then being a woman is like being a goth or a theater kid—you can just stop doing it or get bored of it—and that can't be right, because Beauvoir also clearly thinks women are an oppressed group, and you can't be oppressed by a group you can just opt out of. I wrote out the reasoning and it looks like:

(1) If being a woman is something you become, not something you're born as, then womanhood is made by society, not biology.
(2) If womanhood is made by society, then it's a social kind, like a high school clique or an occupation.
(3) If it's a social kind, then whether someone is in that kind depends on social facts about them, so they could in principle leave the kind by changing those facts.
(4) But if someone could in principle leave womanhood, then "women are oppressed" can't mean what Beauvoir seems to want it to mean, because oppression requires a group you can't just exit.
(5) So Beauvoir's claim either makes womanhood basically a lifestyle choice, or it can't do the work she needs it to do.

I hate the conclusion but I don't know wich step is the bad one. I'd most like to keep (1) and (4), but maybe that's the problem. Which step do you reject, and what do you give up by rejecting it?
