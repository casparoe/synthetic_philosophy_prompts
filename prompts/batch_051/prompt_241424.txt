In my epistemology class we were doing the regress problem, and a guy in my discussion section kept dismissing foundationalism by saying it's turtles all the way down. His gloss was that any belief you try to treat as basic is going to need justification from something further, so the "foundation" is just an arbitrary place where you got tired of asking for reasons. I've seen the same phrase used to mean something more like "our reasons form a circle" or even "no belief is ever really justified," and those feel pretty different to me. He talked like the slogan settled the foundationalism vs. coherentism vs. infinitism debate all by itself, and I'm not convinced it did.

Could you lay out the different precise claims "it's turtles all the way down" might be expressing here — for instance, about whether the chain of justification terminates, loops, continues forever, or whether every stopping point is arbitrary — and for each say whether it's trivially true, substantive and plausible, or just false? More importantly, which of those readings would actually do the work he needed to dismiss foundationalism? Could you also seperate what's actually settled in epistemology from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this, and not present a contested view as the consensus.
