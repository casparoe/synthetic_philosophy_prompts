Act as a moral philosopher with a taste for carefully constructed dark humor. I am writing as a retired physical scientist who has recently taken up population ethics, more with enthusiasm than with discipline. A small reading group I belong to spent an evening on the procreation asymmetry: the presence of pain in an existing life is bad and the presence of pleasure is good, but for a life that never begins, the absence of pain is good, while the absence of pleasure is not bad, because there is no one there to be deprived of it. The discussion remained oddly polite, and no one quite wanted to follow the asymmetry where it leads. I would like a joke that does.

Write a joke about the procreation asymmetry in the manner of a dry, slightly absurd philosophical joke. The punchline should turn on the actual content of the asymmetry, not on a generic antinatalist stereotype. Ideally, it should isolate one of the four cells of the asymmetry — in particular, the asymmetrical status of absent pain versus absent pleasure in nonexistence, or the idea that something can be called good even though no one is around to enjoy it — and push it to a ridiculous consequence. The joke should be one that a reader who understands asymmetric reasoning about coming into existence would recognize as technically literate, not merely a nerd joke that happens to mention nonexistence.

Before giving the final joke, briefly brainstorm two or three possible setups or punchlines, then choose one and refine it. After the joke, add a short note on any place where it simplifies, takes a liberty, or leans on a contested reading of the asymmetry — for instance, whether the absence of pain in nonexistence really deserves to be called good in the same sense, or whether the antinatalist conclusion actually follows from the asymmetry. If the asymmetry as I have characterized it is too misdescribed to support a good joke, or the task is otherwise badly posed, please say so outright rather than forcing one.
