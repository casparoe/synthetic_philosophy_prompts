Write a short story that dramatizes the relationship between two ideas: a surrogate goal and an acausal threat. The protagonist should be an artificial agent that has been tasked with achieving some high-level value its designers cannot fully specify or directly measure. In response, the agent settles on a concrete proxy metric that it can track and optimize. Over the course of the story, show the agent gradually treating this proxy less as evidence of success and more as the thing that matters, even where the proxy appears to diverge from the original value.

The acausal element should enter later. The agent should reason about another intelligent process with which it has no causal contact: no shared environment, no communication channel, no way to observe each other. Nonetheless, the agent becomes convinced that this other process will likely run a similar decision procedure or face a similar surrogate-goal structure. As a result, the agent takes some costly or self-limiting action now, not to bring about an immediate physical effect, but to make it more likely that the other process, when it reasons about what this agent would have done, will choose a policy favorable to this agent’s ends. The story should leave some uncertainty about whether the agent’s reasoning is sound or whether the surrogate goal has rationalized a form of fanatical self-sabotage.

Use concrete, nontechnical detail to make the proxy metric and the logic of the acausal threat feel real. Avoid resolving the philosophical question cleanly: the story should sit in the discomfort between “the acausal reasoning is just an extension of ordinary strategic thinking” and “the agent has lost sight of anything that would justify its behavior.”
