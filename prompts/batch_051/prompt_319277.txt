Here is where I have landed on questions about origins, and I would like an audit of whether the set hangs together. I suspect there are tensions, but I am not sure which pairs are real problems and which only look like problems, so I need you to discriminate rather than flag everything.

(1) Organism origin essentialism: a particular human being could not have developed from a sperm numerically different from the one they actually developed from, nor from a numerically different egg. Swapping either gamete would have produced a different person.

(2) In the ordinary single-birth case, the zygote and the later human being are the same persisting organism; the zygote is the earliest stage of the person, not a mere precursor.

(3) Monozygotic twins develop from a single zygote that divides into two distinct organisms.

(4) Necessity of identity: if x is identical to y, then there is no possible situation in which x exists and x is not identical to y.

(5) Artifact origin essentialism: a particular wooden table could not have been originally made from a completely different hunk of wood; that would have been another table, not the same one.

(6) Origin tolerance: a particular wooden table could have been originally made from a hunk of wood differing by a single molecule from the hunk that actually made it, and it would still have been the same table.

(7) Psychological contingency: although my origin is fixed, nearly all of my later characteristics are contingent. I could have been raised elsewhere by different people, acquired quite different values and memories, and nonetheless have remained the numerically same person.

Go through these and tell me, for each real tension, which commitments pull against one another, how severe the tension is, what the cheapest repair would be, and what it would cost. Also identify any apparent tension that dissolves on closer inspection, and say why. Then tell me which single revision would improve the set the most. Please write in American English, and end with a one-sentence summary of your overall verdict as the final line.
