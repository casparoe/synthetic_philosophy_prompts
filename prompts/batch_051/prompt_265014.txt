I keep getting cornered in office hours about the socialist calculation debate, and it's starting to make me doubt whether the criteria I teach are doing any real work. Here's how I've been teaching it. The Mises–Hayek side, as I present it, says rational economic calculation requires decision-makers to steer by prices that meet three conditions: (i) the prices arise from actual exchanges, not from a formula or decree; (ii) the prices carry information that was originally scattered across countless individuals, including local know-how that can't be written down in a report; and (iii) someone genuinely eats the loss of being wrong, so prices function as an error signal. Against that, I teach Lange as saying prices don't have to come from real markets in capital goods: a central board could announce provisional prices, tell managers to treat them as given, and revise them by trial and error whenever shortages or surpluses show up. Fine.

Now a student asks about a planned economy with something I'll call PlanSage. Its a real-time planning system that ingests granular data — orders, stockouts, reported costs, worker flows, weather, all of it. Consumer goods and labor are bought and sold for actual money, so those are real market prices. But every producer good is allocated by PlanSage: it computes an hourly vector of shadow prices for each input, and managers are legally required to maximize against them. Managers bid for inputs with internal tokens, and a unit that uses inputs wastefully gets "bankrupted" — the manager is demoted and the unit dissolved. No one privately owns the means of production, and the tokens can never be converted into consumption goods. So here's the question: is the shadow price for a ton of copper a market price in the sense Mises thought was necessary for calculation? Does this economy solve the calculation problem, or does it just simulate the look of solving it? Go criterion by criterion, say explicitly where the three conditions underdetermine the verdict, and then say whether the conditions themselves need revising — and, if so, how.

After your argument, give me a JSON object with these fields: "verdict" (one of "calculates", "simulates", or "genuinely borderline"), "criterion_analysis" (an array of three objects, each with "criterion" as a string, "assessment" as a string, and "satisfied" as one of "yes", "no", or "unclear"), "misesian_reading" (a string stating what a Misesian should say), "langean_reading" (a string stating what a Langean should say), and "criteria_revision" (either null or a string stating how the criteria should be changed).
