I recently joined the grants committee of a family foundation that gives away about $4 million a year, and every quarter we get pitched two very different kinds of ask: fund this rent-relief program now, or fund this three-year campaign to change how eviction courts work. The discussion always collapses into someone saying "that's just a band-aid" and someone else saying "that's too abstract to measure," but we've never actually written down what makes something genuinely systemic, or when the marginal, direct thing is the better bet. I'd like a reusable decision procedure my committee can run on any proposal: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer at each step, where the procedure says "no, fund the direct thing instead," and where it stops and says "this is a values call, a person has to decide." We have about a afternoon per proposal, noone on the committee has a philosophy background, and the full board signs off on the final slate. Then run the procedure on two proposals: (1) a yearly grant to subsidize insulin for uninsured patients at one clinic, and (2) a grant to a small group lobbying for changes to state Medicaid drug-pricing rules. If the procedure gives an answer I won't like on one of these, don't soften it. Be honest about which steps are really judgment dressed up as procedure.
