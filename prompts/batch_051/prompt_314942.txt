So I recently blew up a small sports-betting bankroll and went down a rabbit whole on Kelly betting, and now I can't stop reading the "geometric rationality" people who talk like maximizing expected log wealth is a whole philosophy of life. I want to make fun of it in a way that would actually land with someone who knows the real math, not just generic fintech-bro bashing.

Can you write a satirical piece, around 700 words, that's the deadpan FAQ / support page for an app called something like LogWealth that implements geometric rationality across your entire financial life? Formatted as numbered FAQ entries, answered by a support rep named Dana. The questions are from users, and the answers stay perfectly calm and consistent with the app's worldview while quietly revealing how insane the whole thing is.

The jokes need to come from the actual Kelly framework, so someone who knows it laughs instead of wincing. Things you could use: the app lets the user input their own edge and then treats that number as gospel when it computes the Kelly fraction; it caps position size at half Kelly unless you pass a variance-tolerance questionnaire that always says "not yet"; it distinguishes between maximizing expected wealth and maximizing expected log wealth the way a wellness app distinguishes between cardio and strength; overbetting your edge destroys growth and the app has a whole support article about it; and the ensemble-average vs. time-average distinction shows up constantly. One entry should be a user whose account is down 75% complaining that Kelly is supposed to be optimal, and Dana explains that the user is confusing the ensemble average with the single path they actually lived, and also that entering p = 0.99 on a hunch is not an edge. Another entry could be about buying insurance, where Dana declines because the premium is negative expected log growth, and suggests upgrading to Pro if the user insists on having non-log utility.

The satire should stay deadpan and be fair: Kelly sizing for repeated bets is genuinely useful, the app just takes it way past where it makes sense and treats the user's self-reported probability as the only input that matters.
