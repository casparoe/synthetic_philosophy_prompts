I have been puzzling over a thesis that physicists and mathematicians often assert but almost never state with enough precision to be evaluated: that elegance is, by itself, evidence for the truth of a theory, a proof, or a conjecture. I do not mean the weak claim that elegance is a useful heuristic for discovery, or that it makes results easier to teach, remember, and extend. I mean the strong claim that if two candidate hypotheses are otherwise comparable in their fit to the data, the more elegant one is more likely to be correct.

I would like you to treat this as a testable hypothesis about the historical record of science and mathematics, and to design the best test you can of it. Be concrete. How would you build a sample of cases, such as competing hypotheses in physics before one of them won, or published mathematical conjectures whose truth was later settled? How would you operationalize elegance without letting the ratings be contaminated by hindsight, by familiarity, or by knowledge of which candidate turned out to be true? What would you compare against, and what result would count as evidence for the claim, what result would count as evidence against it, and how large an effect would you need before you would call it real?

Then I want you to be honest about the residue. Suppose the test came out strongly positive. What exactly would it have shown, and what would it still not show? In particular, would it distinguish the thesis that elegance itself is truth-conducive from the alternative that elegant theories merely get tested harder, or that simpler theories have fewer adjustable parts and therefore fail less often for reasons that have nothing to do with beauty? Would a positive result in empirical science transfer to mathematics, where truth is not settled by observation? And if the test came out negative, would that refute the thesis, or only show that the thesis is false at the coarse grain at which you managed to measure elegance? Finally, tell me which part of the original claim no historical or empirical test could ever settle, even in principle.
