Hey! I write a small blog about environmental and animal ethics and I want to make one of those "which view do you actually hold?" quizzes, but for how people think about killing pest insects. My readers already take animal welfare seriously, so dont waste a question asking whether insects matter at all as a raw yes/no—everyone taking it will have *some* ambient concern. The point is to sort them by what they say about concrete cases, not by which label sounds nicest. Can you write 8–10 questions, each a very specific scenario (houseplant aphids, termites in the joists, a mosquito on your arm, bedbugs, ants on the counter, an invasive beetle in the woods nearby, cabbage loopers in the vegetable patch, a moth in the living room, that sort of thing) with three answer options that differ in what you would actually do, and then a scoring key mapping the patterns onto something like: the no-kill/relocate insect sentientist, the humane-necessity welfarist (kills only when needed and insists on the fastest death), the territory view (different rules inside the home vs out in the wild), the ecological collectivist (individual bugs matter mostly through populations and ecosystems), and the minimal-concern nuisance view. The questions themselves shouldn't name any of these positions, just cases and actions, so someone can answer without already knowing the taxonomy.

A few specific things I want the quiz to get right. Please include at least one question that forces a choice between invasive-species control—like a city spraying for emerald ash borer or treating a wetland for mosquitos—and harm to individual insects; at least one question with an insect that is not a pest at all but that people still sometimes kill or relocate (the moth, a caterpillar that might turn into a butterfly), because that separates the pure no-kill view from views that only care once there is damage; and at least one question about *how* you kill rather than whether—squashing, drowning in soapy water, a glue trap, a systemic insecticide, releasing parasitoid wasps—since the humane-necessity and minimal-concern people will often answer "kill it" to the same cases and only come apart on method and speed. For each position in the key, give one sentence on its most awkward commitment (the no-kill view has bedbugs; the ecological view has slow-parasitoid deaths; etc.), and explicitly flag answer combinations your scoring key can't place in a principled way—like someone who uses slow poison indoors but refuses to harm a caterpillar outdoors is probably just squeamish about goo, not a secret theorist, and the quiz should say so rather than quietly mapping them somewhere.
