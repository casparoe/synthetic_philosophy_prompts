I am building a small formal framework for comparing the value of states of the world, where a state's value is meant to be a function of at least two dimensions of a mental life: how much sentience or consciousness is present, and how good or bad the experience is along dimensions such as valence, intensity, and duration. I keep meeting people who commit themselves to additive, multiplicative, or lexical rules for combining these dimensions on the strength of a single case, and I want the criteria fixed before the choice rather than after.

Build the requirement list first, and keep most of the weight there. For each entry, name the case or consideration that forces it, and situate it on a spectrum from non-negotiable to a commitment of one particular camp. Cover concrete cases (zero on a dimension, negative values on a dimension, tiny but nonzero quantities, saturation or ceilings) as well as structural constraints, such as whether the combining rule may depend on how the dimensions are measured, whether the components must be separable or may interact through necessity and gating, and whether within-subject and across-subject aggregation must obey the same rule. Flag any subgroups of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which trade-off looks hardest to avoid. Only after that, give a brief verdict on how the additive, multiplicative, and lexical approaches fare against the list, and identify the single requirement that does the most to eliminate candidates.
