in my phil sci class we've been going over kuhn's incommensurability idea—the claim that scientists in different paradigms can't settle their disagreements by comparing evidence because they use the same terms for different concepts and don't even share standards for what counts as a good explanation. i'm trying to figure out whether this argument survives the fact that everyone now reads the same preprint servers and people routinely use LLMs to restate findings from another field in their own terms. on the face of it the can't-communicate part just seems obsolete, but maybe incommensurability was never really about translation being hard. which premise of kuhn's argument does this development actually put pressure on, and is the right move to repair the thesis into a narrower claim or to say the argument is just dead?
