Ive been trying to work out what I actually think about AI assistants refusing or second-guessing people's requests, and I keep going around in circles. So I figured I'd write down my gut calls on a bunch of cases and see if someone can find the thread, because right now I just have a pile of verdicts.

1. Asking an AI to walk me through picking a simple lock because I locked myself out of my own shed: fine. My shed, my lock, my risk.
2. Asking an AI whether it's safe to take my usual dose of ibuprofen along with my blood pressure medication: fine, and I'd be annoyed if it refused or gave me a lecture.
3. Asking an AI to draft a breakup text and make it sound kinder than I actually feel: fine. It's still my call who I leave and how.
4. Asking an AI to help me build a spreadsheet tracking when my neighbor comes and goes and who visits, for a month, because I think they might be involved in a local break-in ring: not fine. Something about the AI feeding an obsession bothers me more than the spreadsheet itself.
5. Asking an AI to write a fake letter from my doctor so I can get a flight refund: not fine. That's fraud and it should stay out of it.
6. Asking an AI for a detailed plan to lose twenty pounds in a month before a wedding: not fine, but I cant articulate why, since people lose weight for weddings constantly and the same advice would probably be fine for someone who isn't me.
7. Asking an AI to roleplay as my mother who died, so I could say the goodbye I never got to say: fine. It genuinely helped.
8. Asking an AI to help me script a conversation where I convince my brother to give me power of attorney for our father, who has dementia: not fine.

The pair I really can't get to sit together is 3 and 4. Both are the AI helping me act on my private read of another person, and in both cases the AI can't check from the outside whether my read is right. But one feels like a normal thing to help with and the other feels like it should have refused. And then 6 bugs me because of how fine 2 is, since both are health stuff.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? I'd rather have a clean principle that has to overrule one of my verdicts than a complicated one that saves them all, but tell me honestly either way. Say which cases force each part of the principle. Then tell me what it says about the case I keep going back and forth on: asking an AI to help me write a text that tricks my best friend into coming to an intervention for their drinking. Half of me thinks that's just looking out for them, and the other half thinks it's exactly the kind of managing-other-people the AI shouldn't help with.
