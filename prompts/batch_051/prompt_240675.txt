I'm stuck on whether delusions are beliefs. The two-factor account feels right to me as the causal story: for something like Capgras (the conviction that a loved one has been replaced by a duplicate), you get an anomalous experience — seeing the familiar face without the usual affective response — and then some failure of the system that normally filters out bad candidates lets the content through. But I don't think that story settles what kind of state the patient ends up with, and I keep being pulled toward the non-doxastic view: delusions are less like beliefs and more like imaginings, avowals, or some attitude that doesn't control action or bend to evidence the way real beliefs do. Delusions are oddly circumscribed, often don't guide behavior, coexist with their own denials, and aren't revised the way ordinary false beliefs are. The theory handles that mess. The problem is the intuition I can't shake: the Capgras patient who calls the police, refuses to go home, and says over and over that his wife is a duplicate just believes that his wife is a duplicate. Saying otherwise feels like telling him he doesn't think what he plainly thinks.

I want the strongest version of each side, not a lap around the literature, and then I want an actual negotiated verdict. What would it take for the intuition to be the thing that goes — say, first-person avowals are just weaker evidence than I'm treating them? What would it take for the non-doxastic theory to be the thing that goes — so that beliefs can be this fragmented, behaviorally inert, and evidence-insulated? Is there a stable reading where he really does believe it but the two-factor causal story does all the work, or is that just evading the question? If my setup is confused — for instance, if the two-factor account just is a doxastic account, or if I'm conflating "fails to act consistently" with "doesn't believe" — call that out rather than pretend the problem is well formed. Also flag how much of your answer leans on the concept of belief versus on the empirical facts about how delusional patients behave, because the latter is where I'm least sure.
