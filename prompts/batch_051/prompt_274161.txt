I am preparing a handout for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on Kantian ethics, and I want a decision tree that sorts students into the competing readings of the Formula of Universal Law on the basis of the judgments they actually make about cases, rather than on the basis of which reading they say they accept. Please write the instrument to the following specifications.

Assume the Formula of Universal Law as the test directing agents to act only on maxims they could will to become universal law. The tree should treat two failure modes as separate and not collapse them: the case in which a maxim cannot be universalized because the universalized practice would undermine the practice or concept the maxim presupposes (the contradiction-in-conception family), and the case in which the universalized maxim is conceivable but could not be willed by a rational agent with ordinary ends (the contradiction-in-willing family). It should also take the maxim-specification problem seriously from the outset: any action can be described under many maxims, from a description at full particularity to one at high generality, so applying the test already involves a choice of description. The tree should mark explicitly where it treats that choice as settled by a fixed specification rule, where it treats the choice as discretionary, and where it treats the choice as fatal to the test.

The tree must route students on the basis of their verdicts on at least these five cases, and every question must be answerable by someone who does not already know the scholarly vocabulary:

1. Borrowing money while falsely promising to repay.
2. Refusing to develop a talent that is now dormant but would clearly be useful to others in a realistic future.
3. Refusing to help someone in distress when one is comfortable and the help would be cheap.
4. Voting only for candidates who promise the largest personal tax reduction, without regard to competence or character.
5. Reserving a concert ticket under a false name in order to keep one's attendance private, where the venue's terms require the true name.

Case 4 is meant to be genuinely borderline: some readings will treat it as a contradiction in willing, on the ground that a rational agent cannot will a world in which everyone votes solely on narrowly self-interested grounds because that world predictably delivers officials the agent has reason not to want; others will treat it as permissible, on the ground that universal pursuit of self-interest does not make the practice of voting impossible. Case 5 is meant to test how much latitude the tree grants in the specification of the maxim; the questions should therefore not be written so as to presuppose that either case is read only one way.

Each leaf must carry: a name, or unnamed if no one defends it; the pattern of case verdicts and specification choices that routes to it; and one sentence on the view's most awkward commitment. The leaves must include at least these: the strict contradiction-in-conception reading; the strict contradiction-in-willing reading; a reading on which the failure is practical rather than logical, because the agent could not pursue the ends on which both agency and the maxim's own point depend while willing the maxim universally; a publicity-style reading, on which a maxim fails if it could not be presented as a policy for a community of equals; a maxim-specification skeptic, who holds that the test yields verdicts only under a choice of maxim description it cannot itself justify; and a consequentialist-in-Kantian-clothing reading, on which the test appears to do independent normative work only because maxims get described at whatever level of generality flatters the antecedently preferred outcome.

Build the tree from questions first and positions second: state the splitting questions in plain language, lay out the combinations of answers they permit, locate the named positions in that space, and then diagnose any unoccupied but logically available combinations in a single clause as incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored. Where two leaves cannot be separated by the tree, flag the pair and state the further question that would separate them. Do not begin from the textbook labels and force the cases to fit them.
