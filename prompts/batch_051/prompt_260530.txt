ok so I've been asked to write up my views on meaning and understanding for a reading group, and I want a consistancy check before I hand it in. here's roughly where I've landed:

1. I'm a physicalist about meaning: there's no semantics floating around without the right causal machinery underneath it.
2. I think symbol-shuffling alone isn't enough for semantics. A system that just manipulates formal symbols with no causal connection to the world can't be said to mean anything, no matter how coherent its outputs look.
3. But I also think a brain in a vat wired into a perfect simulation of a normal body would still have genuine perceptual beliefs, including beliefs about what it calls the world outside.
4. I'm an internalist about content: if my molecule-for-molecule duplicate has the same internal states as me, our words and thoughts mean the same thing; what's outside the skin doesn't fix meaning.
5. I don't think a language model trained only on text understands anything at all, regardless of how well it talks.
6. But I do think a person locked in from birth, who could only read and write, would eventually genuinely understand words like red and walking — through the community's language use at least — even though they never had any of the relevant sensations.

do these hang together? I can feel a few places where they rub, but I can't tell which are real problems and which just look like problems. go through and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only seem to be, rank the real tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest revision would be for each. don't try to soften it into something consistent: if something has to go, say so.
