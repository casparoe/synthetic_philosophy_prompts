I keep going around in circles on what should count as "manipulation" when we're talking about app and website design, and I want an actual working definition I can use, not just vibes or a list of examples. I've got a pile of cases and can't figure out where the line is.

Cases that seem clearly manipulative:
1. An unsubscribe link that's grey, tiny, and buried under three menus, while the "keep my subscription" button is big and bright.
2. A wheel-of-fortune reward spinner in a game that always stops one slot short of the jackpot.
3. A notification saying "your friend just bought this" that gets generated even when no particular friend did.
4. An app that sends 11pm warnings that you're about to loose your 214-day streak.

Clearly fine:
5. A friend giving you a sincere, detailed case for quitting smoking.
6. A sign on a mountain trail saying the cliffs ahead are dangerous.
7. A waiter accurately describing how the special is made and why people like it.

Borderline:
8. Autoplay-next-episode, default on, two clicks deep to disable.
9. Candy at checkout, at kid-eye height.
10. A fitness app whose avatar frowns at you when you miss a workout.
11. A chatbot that infers from your writing that you're lonely and frames recommendations as "books that make people like you feel understood."
12. A site that charges you more because it infers from your device that you can pay more.
13. Surge pricing on a ride app during a storm.
14. Default organ donor registration with a one-click opt-out.

What I want: propose a definition of manipulation (or, if the honest answer is that we're conflating two different concepts, say that and define both), run it against every case, and flag where it gives a wrong answer or no answer. Then revise until it survives, or defend the surprising verdict. Tell me at the end which of your verdicts is least secure, and whether your definition actually does the work there or you're just back-forming it to the cases.
