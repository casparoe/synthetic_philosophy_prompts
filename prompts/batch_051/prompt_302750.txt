I'm drafting a short position statement for a seminar on consciousness and perceptual neurotechnology, and before I circulate it I'd like an audit of whether my commitments actually hang together. My rough position is this:

(1) I'm a physicalist: every conscious state is identical with some physical state of the brain; nothing nonphysical is added. (2) I nevertheless think the explanatory gap is real in the following sense: a complete physical and functional description of a brain state would not by itself let someone know what the associated experience is like, because phenomenal concepts are irreducibly first-personal. (3) I take the stabilised-image work on 'forbidden colours' at least as seriously as its defenders do: there can be experiences, for example of a homogeneous field that looks simultaneously reddish and greenish, that violate the usual red/green opposition, even though opponent-process theory treats redness and greenness as mutually exclusive channels. I do not think all such reports are artifacts, though I concede some may be. (4) I think brain interfaces — targeted cortical stimulation, future neuroprosthetic modulation, or comparable interventions — could in principle produce genuinely novel qualia: not merely new combinations or remixes of familiar sensory qualities, but experiences whose qualitative character has no counterpart in ordinary perception. (5) I hold that the only way to know what such a novel experience is like is to have it oneself; a subject's description of it cannot convey its character to someone who has not had it. (6) I also hold that there is a fact of the matter about whether a given report corresponds to a genuinely new quale rather than to a familiar quale under an unusual label, attention state, or demand characteristic.

I suspect some of these are in tension, but I can't locate the problems precisely and I don't want to rig the audit by guessing in advance. Please treat this as a consistency check: go through the commitments and identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to be, and order the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say what would minimally have to give and what the revision would cost. I would also like an explicit verdict on whether (3) and (4) really lean on the same line of thought or whether they sit awkwardly together, and whether (1), (2), and (5) are jointly tenable in the way I have been assuming.
