I keep hearing a move in internet atheism debates that goes something like: the burden of proof is on the person claiming God exists, just like in science where the null hypothesis is no effect and you need evidence to reject it. I get why its rhetorically useful, but the more I think about the actual machinery of null hypothesis testing, the less sure I am that the analogy carries any weight. The null isnt the default because its antecedently plausible; its special because it lets you control false positive rates, and failing to reject the null is not the same as accepting that the effect is zero. Also, picking a null, a test, and a significance level is full of theory-laden choices, whereas the atheist seems to want the default to be a pure absence of commitment, not a choice. i want someone to lay out what maps onto what, where the analogy breaks, and whether the conclusion it is used to support—that the theist alone carries the burden—survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If the statistical analogy were pushed all the way, would it commit an atheist to something like a prior near zero for God, or just to a convention about what counts as a licensed claim?
