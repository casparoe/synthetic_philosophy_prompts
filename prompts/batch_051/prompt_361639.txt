I am preparing a position statement in the free will debate for a seminar on compatibilism and incompatibilism, and I would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. My present commitments are these.

1. Source incompatibilism. An agent is ultimately responsible for an action only if the action is not wholly the causal product of factors that were in place before the agent existed. If determinism is true, no one is ever ultimately responsible for anything.

2. Frankfurt cases succeed. In cases where a merely counterfactual intervener would have made the agent do the same thing had she shown signs of doing otherwise, the agent can still be morally responsible, provided the action issued from her own evaluative outlook. Responsibility does not require the ability to do otherwise.

3. The reactive attitudes are insulated from the metaphysics. Resentment, gratitude, and indignation are justified attitudes within ordinary interpersonal life, and their justification is not hostage to a resolution of the determinism question.

4. Blame is desert-entailing. To blame someone is not merely to deter, rehabilitate, or express a demand; blame is fitting only when the person deserves it, where desert is a non-forward-looking relation between the person and the act.

5. Manipulation and upbringing are not on a par. Someone whose evaluative outlook was installed by direct manipulation is not responsible in the same way as someone who acquired the very same outlook through an ordinary upbringing, even though in both cases the relevant causal origins lie outside the agent's control.

I would like an audit rather than a lecture. Tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which merely look as though they are; rank the genuine tensions by severity; and for each say precisely which commitment would have to be revised and what the cheapest revision is. Then identify the single revision that would do the most to make the set cohere, and state whether it amounts to giving up, weakening, or reinterpreting one of the five commitments. Please do not stop at the thought that "responsible" is ambiguous; if a reinterpretation is the answer, show what it costs.
