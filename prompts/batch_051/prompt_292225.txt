I've been defending a position about fundamentality, and I'd like to know what would actually count against it — not the standard complaints about grounding talk in general, which I've heard a hundred times, but the weakest point in this specific view. Here it is. I hold that there is a fundamental level, but I don't think "levels" are a helpful picture, because the relation that does the work — grounding, the "in virtue of" relation — is not itself another fact in the world. It's more like the form explanation takes, or logical scaffolding. So when someone asks "what grounds the grounding relation?" or "is grounding itself fundamental?", I treat those as catagory mistakes, sort of like asking what color the number three is. Non-fundamental facts are real and fully grounded; grounding facts aren't items in the inventory at all — they're how the inventory is arranged. What I want from you is a map of my exposure. What observations, arguments, formal results, or plain counterexamples should make me abandon this rather than just add a qualification? What does the view risk that its rivals don't, and where have I insulated it by refusing to let grounding facts into the picture? If it turns out unfalsifiable in practice, is that actually a problem for a position of this kind, or is it fine? Be willing to tell me the specific place it would break first.
