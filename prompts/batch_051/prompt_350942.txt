My co-op board is fed up with the building's constant low-level friction—people leaving bikes in the hall, noise after ten, the walk not getting shoveled—and with the fact that every time someone complains we just hold a meeting where everyone agrees to try to be better neighbors and then nothing changes. So they're proposing to replace all of that with a ledger. Each unit gets the same number of points every month. The board publishes a price list: leaving a bike in the hallway costs 3 points a day, noise after 10pm costs 5, shoveling the front walk earns 1 point per snowfall, watching a neighbor's cat earns 4 points a day, and so on. Bounties are paid out of a house account and fines are paid into it; if the account runs short the co-op tops it up out of general dues. Points can be bought and sold between residents in an app, for cash. You can also buy an exemption pass for 15 points a month that makes your first three infractions of any kind free. At the end of the year, the top 20% of point balances get a month's rent rebated, and the bottom 20% pay an extra month. The board is explicit that the price list is exhaustive: if it isn't on the list, it's allowed, and the only way to raise a greivance is through the ledger—no appeals to fairness, being a good neighbor, or unwritten norms.

I have to vote on this next month, and I don't want a yes-or-no take yet. Walk me through how the thing would actually behave over a year or five. What does it incentivize, where does it get gamed first, what happens to helpfulness and decency that aren't on the list, and are the failure modes fixable without quietly bringing back the be-a-good-neighbor talk they're trying to kill off?
