ok so in the Locke I was assigned, original acquisition has three conditions: (1) mix your labor with something previously unowned or held in common; (2) leave enough, and as good, for others—the proviso; (3) don't let it spoil, so you can only take what you can actually use. Money is the usual escape hatch from (3).

The case I keep going back and forth on: a company scrapes nearly all of the digitized public domain—old novels, scientific papers, folk songs, local newspapers, everything—and does a huge amount of skilled labor to train a proprietary model on it. The originals remain public: nothing is deleted, and anyone can still read them. But the model itself—a file of weights, not the corpus—is kept private and sold; and in practice it has become the main way to productively use the whole commons at once, so a competitor who didn't do the same scrape can't build anything comparable. They've fenced the aggregate usefulness of the commons without removing the commons.

Does this count as legitimate Lockean acquisition, or does the proviso bite? Is leaving the raw texts untouched enough to satisfy the leave-enough-and-as-good condition, or should that condition cover the derived use-value too, not just the raw stuff? Go condition by condition, say where the criteria underdetermine the verdict, and finish with a definite yes/no on whether this passes the Lockean test, even if your answer is that the framework needs a fourth condition or a rewritten proviso. Don't just lay out both sides and leave me to choose—commit to one, and give me your reasons.
