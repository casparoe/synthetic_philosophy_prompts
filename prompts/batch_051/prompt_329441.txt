I sit on the clinical governance committee of a teaching hospital. The hospital is piloting a diagnostic decision-support system for junior doctors: when a trainee enters their case notes, the system immediately displays a ranked list of diagnoses and a suggested management plan, before the trainee has worked through their own reasoning, and it offers no explanation for its recommendations. The pilot shows that the system improves diagnostic accuracy, especially among trainees, but several committee members oppose any roll-out beyond the pilot. They argue that routinely seeing the answer before forming one's own clinical judgement will deskill junior doctors and erode their understanding of the underlying physiology, even when their final answers turn out to be correct. Others reply that clinicians already practise this sort of dependence when they consult a senior colleague or a reference text, and that a reliable tool which reduces error should be adopted. The disagreement seems to turn on whether dependence on an opaque system is relevantly different from dependence on a colleague who can explain, justify, and be questioned. Draft a short decision memo for the committee recommending whether to adopt the system and with what safeguards, and give a fair treatment of the strongest consideration against your recommendation. Please write the memo in American English.
