After reading Mill's chapter on the government of dependencies by a free state in Considerations on Representative Government, I reconstructed what seems to be his argument, and I cannot find the exit. (1) Representative government presupposes that a population already has certain moral and social habits—cooperation, self-restraint, acceptance of decisions made by others. (2) Where those habits are absent, the only workable form of government is despotism, and despotism is justified if the ruler governs for the improvement of the governed. (3) Mill describes India as a society that lacks those habits. (4) Therefore British rule in India may be legitimately despotic if it is for India's improvement. (5) But the same reasoning would justify any stronger, more advanced power in ruling any weaker, less advanced people despotically, while the ruled people are deemed least competent to judge whether they are being improved. (6) That conclusion conflicts with the value of self-government that Mill defends elsewhere. I would like to keep (1) and the value of self-government, but I do not see how to avoid (4). Please tell me which step to reject, or if the argument is valid, which premise is least costly to give up and what that costs. I am a retired scientist and new to philosophy, so I would like the diagnosis to be explicit about the conceptual moves. Please answer in American English.
