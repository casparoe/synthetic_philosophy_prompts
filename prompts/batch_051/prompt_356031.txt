I'm a novelist, so I spend most of my day building people out of words and then trying to make readers insist those people have inner lives. The zombie thing has been stuck in my head for years: a duplicate of me, molecule for molecule, same behavior, same winces and sighs, same long-winded "of course I'm conscious, what a strange question" speeches, but with no phenomenal consciousness—nothing it's like to be it, all dark inside. The move I keep seeing is that if we can conceive such a being, maybe even imagine it in perfect detail, that shows consciousness isn't entailed by the physical/functional facts, so physicalism has a leak. But I'm a novelist; I can imagine all sorts of things, and I've made readers imagine them too, so I've gotten suspicious of how much work conceivability is allowed to do. Heres what I want you to do. Design the strongest empirical test you can of the underlying claim—that the physical and functional facts don't pin down the phenomenal facts. Be concrete: what would you measure, in what subjects or systems, with what controls? What result would push you toward "yes, the conceivability argument is picking up on something real," and what result toward "no, it's just a failure of imagination, like being unable to picture water as anything but H2O"? Then tell me honestly what even a positive result wouldn't settle—in particular whether any test that runs from the outside could ever get a grip on the very thing the zombie is supposed to lack.
