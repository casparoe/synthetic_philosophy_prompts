I keep coming back to a grabby-aliens-style Great Filter position, and I want someone to actually try to break it rather than steelman it for once. The view, spelled out: the reason the sky is empty is that the filter is mostly behind us, spread across a few absurdly improbable transitions between lifeless chemistry and a galaxy-filling civilization. I'd put most of the probability mass on abiogenesis, eukaryogenesis (or something functionally similar), and the jump to tool-using general intelligence. Once a lineage clears those, the remaining step from intelligence to a durable, expanding civilization is the one filter a smart species has a halfway decent shot at surviving; expansion itself is fast enough that the universe looks empty mainly because almost nobody has cleared the earlier steps yet. On this picture a late-stage filter — nuclear war, engineered pandemics, unaligned AI, whatever — is the exception rather than the rule.

So: what would actually count against this? I don't just want the standard maybe-the-filter-is-ahead-of-us line; I want a map of the position's exposure. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and include the ones a committed holder of this view would themselves admit are real exposure, not just the outside critics' talking points. In particular I'd like pressure-tested: how much work observer-selection reasoning is doing (does treating our own existence as evidence make observers common or rare, and how sensitive is the view to that choice); whether fast durable expansion is being assumed rather than argued for; which actual results from origin-of-life chemistry or exoplanet biosignatures would move the needle and which wouldn't; whether the same empty-sky observation can be re-read as evidence that the filter is ahead of us; and wether the whole thing is just we-havent-looked-hard-enough dressed up in priors. End with a verdict on whether the view is at risk of being unfalsifiable, and if so whether that's a bug or just what a hypothesis about a single observable universe has to look like.
