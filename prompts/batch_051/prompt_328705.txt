I'm a retired toxicologist and only recently started poking around in metaphysics, and something about dispositions has started to irritate me. In the lab and the regulatory world, a chemical gets classed as, say, "acutely toxic" or "corrosive" on the basis of a fixed battery of standardized tests, and from then on the label gets read as a claim about the substance itself -- how it will behave, whether in a warehouse or in somebody's lungs. But then I read about finks and masks: a disposition can be present even when an interfering factor would stop the manifestation (a poison with an antidote in the blood is still a poison), and a thing can lose the disposition exactly when the trigger occurs (a live wire hooked to a failsafe). That makes me wonder what a Safety Data Sheet is even predicating. What does this classification practice presuppose about dispositions? Is it committed to the old conditional analysis -- if the stimulus occurs, the manifestation occurs -- or to dispositions as intrinsic properties of the substance, or just to a stable-ish regularity under "normal" conditions? Reconstruct the implicit metaphysics, say wether it hangs together in light of finks and masks, and tell me what would have to change in how we classify and warn about chemical hazards if we took finks and masks fully seriously.
