I want you to write a sonnet about it: a real 14-line sonnet, iambic pentameter, actual rhyme scheme, volta landing at line 9, and a closing couplet. In the octave, set up three judges voting on two premises P and Q, where liability R follows iff both P and Q are true: one judge accepts P and rejects Q, a second rejects P and accepts Q, and the third accepts both. Make it clear each judge is internally consistent, since each one only accepts R if they accept both premises. Then hit the volta with the tally: P passes 2-1, Q passes 2-1, but R loses 2-1 even though R logically follows from P and Q. Use the rest to bring out why this is disturbing: the group has majority-endorsed premises but majority-rejects their consequence, so it's collectively inconsistent even though no individual judge is. Let the closing couplet say exactly what the dilemma threatens (the idea that a group has a single coherent mind or judgment set of its own) or what the two escapes are (premise-based vs conclusion-based aggregation). Accuracy matters here: don't make the judges irrational and don't reduce it to 'people disagree'. After the poem, as the very last line, put 'Summary:' followed by a one-sentence plain statement of the paradox.
