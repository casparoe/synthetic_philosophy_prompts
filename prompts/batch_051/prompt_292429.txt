I'm a TA for an undergrad philosophy of science course, and we're second-grading a Kuhn exam. In lecture we covered the part of chapter 10 of _The Structure of Scientific Revolutions_ where Kuhn says, in effect, that after a paradigm change the scientist still looks at the same world but works in a different world, and that proponents of competing paradigms see different things when they look at similar displays. I don't have the book in front of me, so treat that as a paraphrase from my notes, not a direct quotation. The short-exam question I wrote was:

In chapter 10, Kuhn claims that after a scientific revolution scientists work in a different world, even though the world itself has not changed. Explain what he means by the word world here. Does this claim force Kuhn to say theory choice across paradigms is arational? Why or why not?

The student answered:

Kuhn means a perceptual gestalt switch. Just like the duck-rabbit drawing can be seen as either a duck or a rabbit without the lines on the page changing, scientists before and after a revolution see the same data but organize it under different concepts. Key terms like mass or planet change meaning, so the two paradigms are incommensurable. Incommensurability means they cannot be compared point-by-point. Because of that, there is no neutral evidence that could settle the debate between them, so Kuhn has to concede that theory choice is arational, basically a matter of conversion or social pressure. This is basically Duhem's holism plus history: all observation is theory-laden, so no single result can force someone to abandon a paradigm.
