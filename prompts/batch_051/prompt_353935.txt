I keep running into subsidiarity, and I still can't state exactly what it is as a test that decides cases. In a comparative federalism class we've looked at the US, Germany, Switzerland and the EU, and everyone invokes the word differently — some as a constitutional legal rule, some as a piece of political morality about decisions being taken close to citizens. I want a working definition, not a slogan: something I can apply to a new case and get an answer, or at least an honest "it depends on X."

Here are the cases I want the definition to handle:

- A municipality setting speed limits on ordinary local roads.
- A German Land deciding that primary schools should teach a particular handwriting script.
- The EU setting minimum crash-test standards for cars sold across the internal market.
- A Swiss canton refusing to apply a federal asylum ruling because it says it knows the local integration situation better.
- A city introducing a rent cap, where landlords and renters in the surrounding municipalities say their rents are affected too.
- An upstream state setting water-quality standards for a river that flows through five other states.
- A national carbon price.

My rough priors: the first two are clearly local, the third clearly higher-level, and the last four are where the definition has to earn its keep. Propose the definition, run it through all seven, and tell me where it surprises you, where it gives no answer, and where you'd have to revise it. Bonus if you can say whether subsidiarity is one principle or a pair of prinicples that happen to share a name.
