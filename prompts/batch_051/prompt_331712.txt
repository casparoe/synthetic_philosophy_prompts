I co-teach a course on early modern skepticism, from Descartes's evil demon through Hume's effort to reconcile his skeptical arguments with the fact that he cannot help forming ordinary beliefs. A colleague with whom I teach the course has, over several years, stated a set of crisp verdicts on the cases we use:

- A person in good viewing conditions who believes, on the basis of a clear look, that there is a barn in the field ahead: knows.
- A person who counts seven coins in her palm in good light and believes that there are seven: knows.
- A person whose entire sensory experience is invented by an evil demon and who, on the strength of that experience, believes she has hands, though in fact she has none: does not know.
- A person who holds one ticket in a fair million-ticket lottery with a guaranteed winner and, before the draw, believes purely on the strength of the odds that her ticket will lose: does not know.
- A person touring a countryside in which almost every barn is an indistinguishable papier-mache facade, who happens to look at the single genuine barn and believes it is a barn: does not know.
- A person who reads the time from a stopped clock that happens to display the correct time, and so forms a true belief about the time: does not know.

I have devised a further case to test whether one consistent principle explains all of this. In the new case, a researcher takes a bottle from its customary place in the solvent cabinet of a well-run laboratory, reads a label identifying the contents as ethanol, and believes that the liquid is ethanol. The liquid is in fact ethanol. She has not run any test that would distinguish ethanol from methanol, which would look exactly the same in the bottle, and she has no particular reason to suspect that the contents fail to match the label.

What should I expect her to say about this new case, and why? I want more than a label: state each plausible principle that fits the record, say what each principle predicts for the new case, identify the earlier case that is the closest analog and explain precisely how the new case differs from it, and give your best prediction with your reasons. If her record underdetermines the principle, say which additional case I should put to her next to discriminate the remaining candidates.

Please answer in no more than 350 words.
