After a long argument with a friend about whether someone who cannot produce a definition nevertheless has the concept in question, I no longer trust either of us with the word "concept," and the standard candidate accounts—prototype, exemplar, and theory-theory—each seem built to handle some cases and strained by others. I want a working definition of what it is for a person to have a concept, framed so that it gets all of the following cases right.

The cases:

1. A field birder who can identify hundreds of species, sorts any novel bird as bird, and says a penguin is a bird but not a typical bird, yet can offer no rule that admits penguins while excluding bats. I take her clearly to have BIRD.

2. A student who can recite a one-sentence definition of entropy as a measure of disorder but cannot use ENTROPY to compare two states, predict an outcome, or explain any phenomenon. I take him not to have full possession of ENTROPY.

3. A child who on Monday calls a sheep a dog, and by Friday, after correction, applies DOG only to dogs and no longer to sheep. I have no stable view about whether she had DOG on Monday, acquired it during the week, or had some unstable intermediate state.

4. A cashier who has given exact change for decades and spots an error instantly but says she simply sees the arithmetic and cannot explain it or teach it. Does she have ADDITION and the relevant number concepts, or only a skill that happens to use them?

5. A geneticist in 1900 and a geneticist in 1980 both use GENE fluently; the former treats genes as indivisible units of heredity linked to traits, the latter as DNA sequences with regulatory structure. Do they share one concept whose content changed, or is this two concepts under one word? I lean toward one concept, but I cannot defend the lean.

6. A person who, moments after a fire alarm, forms and applies the category THINGS WORTH TAKING FROM A BURNING HOUSE, though he never possessed it before. Does he now have that concept, and can any account based on stored representations handle a concept assembled on the spot?

Propose the definition, run it through all six cases, and say where it gives the wrong verdict or no verdict. If no single definition survives intact, tell me whether having a concept is doing more than one kind of work, and which cases force which part of the definition. I am less interested in a survey of the literature than in a proposal I could argue about.
