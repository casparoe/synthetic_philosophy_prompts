The clause in question reads:

"Section 4(c). A proposal shall be deemed adopted without further vote if the YES side of its associated market has traded above $0.65 for 72 consecutive hours. For purposes of this section, the associated market is the market on the proposition: 'This proposal, if implemented, will increase the treasury's U.S. dollar balance at the end of the 30 days following implementation.'"

Someone submitted a proposal called a 'Treasury Liquidity Bridge.' It says the DAO shall borrow 4,000,000 USDC from a lending desk at 5% APR, park it in the treasury's main USD wallet, and repay principal plus interest on day 31 out of that same wallet, with no other repayment source named. On day 30 the treasury's USD balance is up four million, so the associated market is trading around 0.78 and on a plain reading 4(c) would auto-execute it. But the DAO isn't actually richer; it just owes the money back the next day. The proposer says its covered by the plain wording and we're obligated to let it run. Half the multisig agrees, the other half says that can't be what the clause is for.

So: does 4(c) actually require us to let this through? Walk me through the four readings — plain wording, the purpose of the clause, what the drafters would have said if they'd seen this exact proposal when they wrote it, and what a reasonable DAO member would have understood the clause to mean.
