I'm on my high school's ethics bowl team and we were prepping a case about how to split a fixed education budget between a rich district and a poor one. One of the seniors explained prioritarianism to me like this: 'Prioritarianism is basically utilitarianism with a guilt tax. It says a benefit to someone who's badly off counts for more then the same benefit to someone who's well off — or, to make the same point another way, you get extra points for reducing inequality. But that means the view is wide open to the leveling-down objection: if we take money from the rich district and just burn it, we've made the distribution more equal, so a prioritarian has to admit the world is better in at least one respect even though literally nobody is better off. Since that's absurd, prioritarianism is refuted.' I have to respond to this in practice tomorrow but I don't know enough to tell where he's actually right. Which parts would a careful prioritarian accept, where does he misdescribe the view, and does the leveling-down objection actually land the way he says?
