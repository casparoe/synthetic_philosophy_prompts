Before a workshop on the foundations of quantum mechanics I have to submit a short statement of my views on probability in the many-worlds interpretation, and I want it audited for internal consistency before I present. Here is the position I currently want to defend:

1. The universal quantum state always evolves unitarily. Measurement is decoherence-induced branching, and after branching every branch is equally real: there is no collapse and no hidden fact about which branch is "the actual one."
2. The squared-amplitude weights in the formalism are not objective chances of the sort a stochastic collapse theory would supply. The fundamental dynamics contains no probabilities.
3. Nonetheless, I treat the Born weights as probabilities in deliberation and action. Facing a branching event with weights of 2/3 and 1/3, I would buy a ticket that pays a dollar in the 2/3-weighted branches for any price up to 66 cents, and a ticket that pays in the 1/3-weighted branches for any price up to 33 cents — exactly as if the weights were probabilities — even though I know descendants of me will exist in both sets of branches.
4. I also hold that before the measurement I have genuine self-locating uncertainty: I can rationally be unsure which branch I am about to experience, roughly as I can be unsure which of two identical-looking rooms I am in. I am not uncertain about which branch will be real, since all of them will be.
5. But I reject branch-counting as the basis for these attitudes. Branch number appears basis-relative and ill-defined, and the formalism supplies no preferred notion of "number of branches" to which equal credences could be assigned.
6. Finally, I am drawn to a decision-theoretic justification of (3): rational agents in branching worlds must, on pain of violating plausible axioms on their preferences, choose among futures as if weighting them by the squared amplitudes.

I am not confident this package hangs together. Which of these commitments are in genuine tension, which pairs merely appear to be in tension, and which single revision would do the most to restore coherence at the least cost? Please rank the tensions by severity and say, for each, what would have to be given up.
