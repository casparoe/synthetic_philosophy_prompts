For a workshop on multipolar traps and races to the bottom (the phenomenon sometimes personified as Moloch: a stable but widely disliked equilibrium from which no actor can profitably defect alone), I want a short self-diagnostic instrument—a "Which account of the trap do you actually hold?" quiz. Please write eight short scenario questions, each a concrete case: an arms race, overfishing on the high seas, credential inflation, regulatory competition among jurisdictions, ad bidding that raises costs without changing market share, safety shortcuts in a competitive tender. Each question should offer two or three answer options that capture rival causal stories rather than rival labels: (a) the harmful choice is individually rational given what others do, but everyone would be better off if all refrained; (b) the competition is over a fixed or positional reward, so the effort mostly cancels in aggregate; (c) most participants already prefer the better equilibrium and would choose it if they were confident others would too, so the real obstacle is assurance and common knowledge; (d) the trap persists mainly because the rules leave the harmful option cheaper than the responsible one, and a tax, liability rule, or default would reverse the choice. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto four positions—commons/prisoner's-dilemma, positional-arms-race, assurance/coordination-failure, and incentive-design accounts—plus an ecumenical category for mixed profiles. The questions should sort readers by their judgments about the cases, never by which position they identify with, and you should flag any pair of positions the instrument cannot cleanly separate.
