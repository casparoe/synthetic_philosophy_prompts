I've been trying to clean up my views on a cluster of values that feel intrinsic but, I've come to think, are probably just instrumental: truth-seeking, tradition, and purity. Take truth-seeking. It feels like I want to know things for their own sake, but whenever I push, the only story I can tell is instrumental — true beliefs let me act, coordinate, and avoid being fooled. The same move seems to work for tradition. A family recipe or a festival feels like it matters in itself, but we quietly drop traditions when they stop doing a job (binding people, marking time, giving an identity) and keep them while they still do. Purity feels the most irreducible — the twinge at contamination feels primitive — but it too seems to cash out as a proxy for real harms like disease or spoilage. So the position I've landed on is that these three are things that feel like ends but are really means, and the appearance of intrinsic value is a kind of afterglow from how reliably useful they've been. I also can't tell whether I'm running together two distinctions here — being valuable in itself versus being valued as an end — and whether my position should really be a claim about what makes the values valuable or about why we keep them.

I know the obvious counterexamples already: useless trivia, pure mathematics, the person who insists on knowing a painful truth they'll never act on. I can talk myself out of those. What I want is a map of what would really count against the position taken seriously — what it would take for me to have to change my mind about one of these values rather than just soften the story. Which cases, findings, or arguments should worry me most; which would be decisive versus merely awkward; and what should someone who holds this position count as evidence against it, rather than just what critics would say? One thing still nags at me. I can imagine being offered, for free, a useless but irreversible truth about someone I care about — something I could never act on and that would only make me sad — and I suspect I'd still want it. But I also suspect I'm rationalizing, because after the fact I can almost always invent a hidden instrumental reason for wanting a truth. Is the view in danger of explaining away every apparent counterexample that way? And if it is, is that a vice in a view like this, or just the normal price of holding that some apparently intrinsic values are not what they seem?
