I'm on the commitee at my university that decides whether an admissions scheme counts as a quota under our charter, which bans quotas but allows race-conscious review and outreach. Our own precedents so far: (1) we struck down a plan that set aside 30 of 100 seats for members of an underrepresented group regardless of how they scored against the general pool; (2) we approved holistic review where group membership can be a plus factor among many, with no target and no separate route; (3) we approved a plan that gave every member of the group a fixed 15-point bonus on the admissions index but guaranteed nothing and capped nothing; (4) we struck down a plan that required a 25 percent floor, achieved by re-ranking the waitlist until the number was hit. Now a dean proposes something else: any applicant from the group who clears a lower, fixed test-score threshold is admitted automatically, with no cap and no stated target, so the number shifts every year with the applicant pool. Is that a quota by our lights if we want to be consistent with ourselves? Which of our earlier cases is the closest analog, and if our precedents genuinely underdetermine it, what principle would we be adopting with each decision?
