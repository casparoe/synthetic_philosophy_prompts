I'm a PhD student working on whether the profit motive is really the root cause of AI risk, and I keep coming back to Marx. In Capital, Vol. 1, ch. 10, on the working day, he says this about capital as such:

"But capital has one single life impulse, the tendency to create value and surplus-value, to make its constant factor, the means of production, absorb the greatest possible amount of surplus-labour."

Here's the thing that's bugging me. That argument was built for a setting where the thing being used up was labor-power, and the countervailing force was the worker's own resistance plus state regulation. In frontier AI, the thing being used up seems different: not hours of a worker's life, but the margin of safety for everyone else. The "means of production" are the models and data centers, and "absorbing surplus-labour" looks more like shipping before red-teaming is finished, or treating uninsurable systemic risk as someone else's externality. Does Marx's argument survive that change, or does it just say something generic like "firms respond to incentives"? Specifically: which premise in his mechanism is doing the real work, and can it be repaired so it keeps identifying what gets sacrificed? I'm not asking about policy; I want to know whether the diagnosis transfers.
