The question was:

"Explain the grandfather paradox and assess whether it shows that backwards time travel is logically impossible. Mention at least one standard response to the paradox, and say what the paradox does and doesn't establish."

The student's answer:

"The grandfather paradox goes like this: a time traveler goes back and kills her grandfather before her father is conceived. Then her father never exists, so she never exists, so she never goes back to do the killing. But if she did go back and kill him, she both kills and doesn't kill him, which is contradictory. Therefore backwards time travel is logically impossible. The standard response, from David Lewis, is to deny that she can change the past: her going back (or failing to) is already part of the earlier time. So she fails to kill her grandfather; the past makes it impossible for her to do otherwise, and the universe has to block the inconsistent action (the gun jams, she slips, whatever). On this view her failure is forced, so she isn't free with respect to the killing. This is essentially Novikov's self-consistency principle, which says physics only admits globally self-consistent histories. The paradox therefore doesn't rule out backwards time travel; it just shows the traveler's options have to be consistent with what already happened."
