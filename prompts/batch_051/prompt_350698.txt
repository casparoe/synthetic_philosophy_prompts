I've been trying to nail down what I actually think about free-riding / social parasitism, because my gut verdicts on concrete cases keep pulling in different directions. I'll list them and I want you to find a principle that gets them, or close.

1. Using an open-source library whose license imposes no legal obligation whatsoever in a commercial app I sell, without donating or contributing a fix back. Fine.
2. Joining the office coffee pool without ever putting money in, but drinking several cups a day. Clearly wrong to me.
3. Living in a country, using roads/courts/police, while structuring your income so you pay zero tax. Wrong, and not just legally; feels like a bigger version of (2).
4. Listening to a listener-supported public radio station for a decade and never donating. Fine, or at worst a mild ding.
5. Declining to vaccinate your kids but relying on the high vaccination rate around you to keep them safe. Wrong, more than a mild ding.
6. Skipping every monthly neighborhood workday that maintains the shared playground, while your kids use it constantly. Kind of wrong, though I'm not sure it's as bad as (2).
7. Stopping to enjoy a street musician's set and then walking away without putting anything in the case. Fine.
8. Working at a unionized shop, taking the union-negotiated raise and the grievance process, but opting out of dues because you legally can. This one bugs me: I think it's wrong, but it looks an awful lot like (1) and (7), which I called fine.

My working theory is that taking-without-contributing is wrong when the good is rivalrous (the coffee, the union's money and effort) or when there's an explicit opt-in contribution norm. But (3) isn't obviously about a rival good, and (5) isn't about an explicit norm, so I don't think the priniciple is doing all the work.

And be honest about whether my line between (4) and (8) can be made principled or whether one of those verdicts has to go.
