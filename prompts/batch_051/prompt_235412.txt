here's where i've roughly landed on induction and projectibility, and i'd like to know whether the whole thing hangs together:

(1) Goodman's right that there's no purely formal or syntactic way to rule out 'grue' — by any logic-only standard, green and grue are on a par, so confirmation theory alone can't solve the new riddle.

(2) But I don't think it follows that all predicates are equal. Some predicates ('green', 'electron') carve the world at its joints, or at least better joints, and some ('grue', 'bleen') are gerrymandered in a way that should matter for induction.

(3) I like the idea that joints show up partly through the history of a predicate: entrenched predicates — ones that have been projected successfully for a long time — are better candidates for projection than freshly made-up ones.

(4) However, I also think entrenchment can't be the whole story, because a genuinely novel scientific predicate can be projectible almost immediately (say, a newly defined particle type, or a new clinical category), before it has any track record.

(5) I hold a deflationary view of laws: I don't think laws of nature are a special kind of thing over and above the non-accidental regularities, and I don't want to ground projectibility in lawhood.

(6) But I want to say grue-projections fail for a reason that isn't just "they're weird": a grue induction has a built-in time-dependence (before/after some date) that makes it non-robust — it changes its mind or breaks down as soon as the relevant boundary passes, whereas green keeps working.
