Heres an argument about embeddings I keep going back and forth on.

(1) Word2vec and BERT-style models are trained purely on co-occurrence statistics—predicting words from their contexts—so the only information a token's vector carries is distributional. (2) Yet the geometry of the resulting spaces seems to track semantics: vector differences like king − man + woman land near queen, and directions in the space correspond roughly to gender, tense, sentiment, and scale. (3) Weirder still, analogy quality often improves if you mean-center the vectors or delete the first few principal components before doing the arithmetic; throwing away some of the raw co-occurrence variation makes the semantic relations sharper, not blurrier. (4) But if removing a purely co-occurrence-based dimension makes semantic relations pop out more clearly, then those relations were already in the geometry, not projected onto it by the human evaluating the analogies. (5) So the geometry is not merely a convenient stand-in for word meaning; it's doing genuine semantic work. Conclusion: distributional geometry is at least partly constitutive of lexical meaning, not just correlated with it.

Is the argument valid? If not, exactly which step fails? For the weakest step, give the strongest defensible version of it and say whether the conclusion survives. Keep the response under 350 words.
