I have been trying to work out whether it is ever literally true that a company, a crowd, or a state is to blame for something, rather than that being merely a loose way of speaking about the people involved. I have no training in philosophy or law, so I have been approaching this from scratch by collecting cases and looking for a rule that fits all of them. I would like your help building a working definition of blameworthiness.

Please give me a definition of what it takes for an entity — a single person or a group of people, and perhaps something else — to be blameworthy for an outcome. The definition should give a definite judgment, yes or no, on each of the cases below. Where the definition gives what feels like the wrong result, or gives no result at all, I want you to say so, and then either revise the definition or defend the surprising judgment. At the end, tell me which of your judgments you are least confident about and why.

Cases whose verdicts seem obvious to me:

1. A building owner has known for months that a staircase is dangerous but does nothing because fixing it would cost him two months of rent. The staircase collapses and injures a tenant. The owner is clearly blameworthy.

2. Two people run a food business and together decide to use an ingredient they know makes some customers sick. I am confident the business itself is blameworthy, and not only the two owners separately.

3. A hurricane tears the roof off a house and injures the family inside. I do not think a hurricane is the sort of thing that can be blameworthy; there is simply no one to blame.

4. A bus driver has a sudden and unforeseeable medical event while driving and kills a pedestrian. The bus company kept the vehicle in good repair and screened the driver as it was required to. I take it that neither the driver nor the company is blameworthy.

Cases where I keep going back and forth:

5. A factory has strict safety rules and regular training. A technician, on his own initiative and against those rules, disables a guard on a machine so he can work faster, and a coworker is later injured. Is the company blameworthy, or only the technician?

6. The top managers of a drug company learn that their painkiller is far more addictive than they have claimed, and they agree to hide the evidence. Many patients become addicted. The managers are certainly blameworthy; but is the company itself blameworthy over and above them, or is saying so just shorthand?

7. A bank's bonus system rewards making loans and penalizes caution. Employees who raise concerns are quietly reassigned. No single person intends any harm, and each individual decision looks defensible when viewed alone. Together these practices make a local financial collapse very likely, and it then happens. Can the bank be blamed for an outcome that no one inside it decided to bring about?

8. At a sporting event, a crowd of mostly strangers attacks a visiting fan. Some throw punches, some shout encouragement, and some just stand filming. We normally say that the mob is to blame, but who exactly is the target of that blame, and does it reach the bystanders?

9. A small war cabinet orders an invasion after secretly falsifying the intelligence it presents. The country's citizens had no vote and no knowledge of this. Is the state, or the country, itself blameworthy, or only the officials in the cabinet?

10. Several landlords independently adopt the same automated pricing program. Without anyone coordinating or communicating, the program's recommendations lead each of them to raise rents, and evictions in the city rise. Is the blameworthy party each landlord, the group of landlords, the software, or no one at all?

One further complication. I have noticed that when people say that something is to blame, they sometimes seem to mean different things: that the thing is an appropriate target of resentment, that it owes an explanation, or that it is where repair should come from. These can pull apart in the borderline cases, so please make clear which of these your definition of blameworthiness is really defining, or whether it is deliberately meant to cover all of them.
