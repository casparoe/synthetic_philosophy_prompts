A policy blogger I follow keeps pushing the following scheme: replace caseworker-run conditionality with a public job-search log for every unemployment claimant — applications sent, interviews, training modules — viewable by any registered taxpayer. Any taxpayer can flag an entry as bogus within 30 days; three flags on the same entry triggers a hearing before a panel of five randomly drawn locals from the same district, three of whom are themselves on benefits and two who are not. The panel votes by majority on whether the entry stands; if they find it bogus, the claimant looses four weeks of benefit and the panel's reasons are published with the claimant's name. Treat it as a mechanism, not a moral position. What does it incentivize among claimants, flaggers, and the people who sit on these panels; how would it be gamed, including by people acting in groups; what happens to claimants whose job search is real but hard to document; and is there any single tweak that keeps the fraud-catching while avoiding it becoming a neighborhood reputation contest — or is that contest the whole point?
