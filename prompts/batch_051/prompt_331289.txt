I have been developing a position in the mental imagery debate that I call the indexical-access account of depictiveness, and I want it assessed honestly before I commit to it further. The account starts from a contrast that I find more basic than the usual one between depictive and propositional formats: a picture-like representation is one at which you can point, whereas a discursive representation is one from which you can only retrieve. When you inspect a mental image of a dog, you can direct attention to the region where the tail meets the haunch, or move from the left ear to the right ear, without first specifying those locations under a description such as upper-left quadrant or the feature cluster that encodes the tail. A mental image seems to contain places in a way that a propositional encoding does not. I therefore propose that depictiveness be defined by this capacity rather than by how the underlying information is stored: a representation is depictive just in case the system that uses it can demonstrate locations within it, move among them, and return to them by way of spatial address rather than content-based search. My hope is that this captures the force of the reaction-time results standardly cited in the debate, such as scanning time increasing with distance and rotation time increasing with angular disparity, while avoiding Pylyshyn's objection that a literal picture in the head would need an inner viewer to interpret it. It would also imply that much of the Kosslyn-Pylyshyn dispute is partly verbal, since the two sides could agree on what subjects can do with images while disagreeing mainly about storage vocabulary. But I can see two problems. First, pointing into a representation may just reintroduce the homunculus in a subtler form: something still has to know where the places are. Second, the entire phenomenon of spatial address might be an epiphenomenal feature of conscious imaging, perfectly compatible with an underlying propositional store, in which case my account has not located the real dispute. I want to know whether this is a genuine third position or only a redescription of one of the familiar sides. Give me the three strongest objections and tell me whether any of them is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable.
