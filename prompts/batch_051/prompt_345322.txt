I got promoted to dock supervisor at a regional food-service packaging distributor six weeks ago, and onboarding made me read the employee handbook cover to cover. Two parts are stuck in my head because they seem to point me in opposit directions.

Under "Decision-Making Standard," it says: "Choose the action that produces the best overall results for everyone affected—employees, customers, the community, and the company. Honesty, fairness, and loyalty matter because they tend to produce better results over time. But when a particular act of honesty would lead to worse results on balance, the right action is the one that produces the better outcome."

Under "Code of Integrity," it says: "Honesty, promise-keeping, and fairness are not tactics. Make every report, log, and statement truthful, and honor the commitments this company has made, whatever the cost. These duties apply absolutely, not as rules of thumb to be set aside whenever a calculation says the numbers point the other way."

Last week I found out my most experienced loader has been signing pallets of coffee cups as "visual inspection complete" without actually opening the shrink wrap when the dock is slammed. He taps the cartons and checks for obvious crush damage. In three years no damaged pallet has shipped, and our damage rate is basically zero. The customer contract promises every pallet gets a full visual inspection, and the log is our only proof. If I report him, he's fired under the zero-tolerance policy, and I lose my best person two weeks before peak season; orders will slip and the newest seasonal hires might get layed off. If I stay quiet, the practical risk to the customer is tiny, but we're letting a false log stand.

Are the two handbook passages actually in conflict in this case, or can they be reconciled? Give me the candidate reconciliations—different things "best overall results" could include, whether the two sections govern different kinds of decisions, whether one section is supposed to override the other or was just written by different people at different times—and tell me which reading is honest and which is special pleading. Then say which one I should follow here, and in particular whether honesty has weight on its own or only counts when it pays off in results.
