I am a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, working on a dissertation about how observers attribute goals and strategies to systems whose internal workings they cannot directly inspect. In the course of reading on inverse reinforcement learning, evolutionary optimality models, and rational analysis, I keep encountering a version of the same difficulty: the same observed behavior can typically be rationalized as the output of many different objective functions paired with many different search or planning procedures, especially once one is permitted to vary assumptions about constraints, computational limitations, reward shaping, or the space of actions the system considers. To fix ideas, consider an automated trading system that places a large sell order during a liquidity squeeze. The behavior is compatible with its seeking to minimize execution costs, to liquidate inventory in order to satisfy a risk constraint, to depress the price deliberately, or to follow a pre-committed schedule produced by some earlier search procedure; each story couples a different objective with a different search space. I want to build a chapter around this underdetermination, but my supervisor keeps pressing me to say why it matters beyond the technical observation that behavioral inference is ill-posed without strong auxiliary assumptions.

With that context, here is my question. If one accepts that an optimizer’s objective and search space are substantially underdetermined by its behavior, what are the most important implications that would follow or be strengthened? I am especially interested in domains such as: interpreting the goals or intentions of deployed AI systems from their outputs; adaptationist explanation in biology; inferring preferences from human choice in economics and the social sciences; and legal or moral attributions of intent to automated systems. I would prefer that you not limit yourself to those four. For each implication you identify, please indicate whether it is a genuine consequence of the underdetermination claim or merely something commonly attached to it, and rank the implications by how much practical or intellectual weight they should carry. I would also like you to flag any alleged implication you think is overrated or does not follow at all, since part of my chapter will argue that the underdetermination point is occasionally made to do more work than it can support.
