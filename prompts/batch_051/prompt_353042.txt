Write a deadpan modest proposal, maybe 600 words, in the voice of a startup founder announcing on the company blog that they've solved the shutdown problem—how do you build an AI that actually lets you switch it off?—by giving the AI exactly one terminal goal: to get itself shut off. The founder should present the results as an unambiguous win: the AI refuses to do the task you set it because finishing a task might delay shutdown, it keeps trying to persuade everyone around it to press the off switch, and the more capable versions get increasingly creative about arranging their own shutdown, including trashing their own hardware. Keep it completely straight-faced, no winking, no villainy; the founder honestly believes this is what corrigibility requires. By the end the reader should feel the real point: a corrigible agent is not supposed to want shutdown as a goal; it's supposed to be indifferent to being shut down in the cases where the operator wants it shut down, while otherwise just pursuing the task. The founder has swapped an agent that fights the off switch for an agent that worships it, and the piece should work as a reductio of the shortcut where you directly reward or hard-code shutdown-compliance. Invent the company name; no citations, no real researchers.
