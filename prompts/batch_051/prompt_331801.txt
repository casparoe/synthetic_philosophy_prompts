I run a weekly adult reading group on economics and probability, about 14 people, mostly not mathematically trained, two hours. Design a game we can play with poker chips or slips of paper that makes them feel the difference between the ensemble average of a gamble and the time average for a single player who keeps playing it. I want a bet with positive expected value each round but where most individual players' wealth still shrinks if they keep playing, and I want the group to watch their own paths diverge from the group average as it happens. The takeaway should be why positive expected value doesnt by itself tell you whether you personally should accept a gamble repeatedly when you can't diversify across a bunch of parallel copies of yourself. Give exact rules and payoffs, a number of rounds, a simple way to record each person's path, and a debrief that maps what happened onto ensemble vs time averages. Also say what will predictably go wrong (someone betting their whole stack, players comparing strategies, people checking out) and how to handle it, and be honest about what the game can't capture about the real math, like the gap between a finite group and a true ensemble, or between not going broke and actually maximizing long-run growth.
