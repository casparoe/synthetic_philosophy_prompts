For my high school math club, I want a sorting quiz called 'Which philosophy of mathematics do your answers commit you to?'; members will take it before they have read anything about logicism, formalism, or intuitionism, so every question must sort people by how they respond to concrete cases rather than by which school's name they prefer. Please write nine questions, each a short case with three answer options, covering at least these five points: whether Goldbach's conjecture has a truth value right now while it remains unproved; whether a nonconstructive existence proof really establishes that something exists; whether the natural numbers form a completed totality or only a process that can always be extended; whether numerals refer to objects or are empty marks moved by rules; and whether arithmetic is ultimately a branch of logic. Then supply a scoring key: first lay out the possible combinations of answers, place the three schools in that space, and for each combination no major school occupies, say in one sentence whether it is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely undefended. Add a fourth cell, 'mind-independent realism (not one of the three)', for the pattern that accepts objective truth values but denies that arithmetic reduces to logic, and state what the quiz cannot settle about that view. For each of the three schools, include one sentence naming its most awkward commitment.
