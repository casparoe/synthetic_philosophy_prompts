I've been collecting a friend's verdicts about when it is acceptable to use animal products, because her pattern keeps surprising me, and now I want to know what her track record commits her to. Here are the cases she has already ruled on:

- A deer killed by a car, found fresh by the side of the road: she thinks eating it is fine.
- A sealed container of cooked chicken taken from a restaurant's trash at closing time, which would otherwise be thrown out: fine.
- A leather belt left in a free box on the sidewalk: fine.
- Buying a new leather wallet from a store: not fine.
- A leftover steak from a dinner she did not order, which the person who ordered it is about to throw away: fine.

One gap in the record: every yes-verdict so far concerns an animal that is already dead or an object that would otherwise be discarded, and I have never heard her rule on anything taken from a living animal, such as eggs, milk, wool, or honey.

The new case is this: a neighbor keeps four rescued backyard hens as pets. The hens are healthy, will not be killed, and lay unfertilized eggs that the neighbor does not want and would otherwise throw away. The neighbor offers my friend the eggs.

What should she say about the eggs if she wants to be consistent with her earlier verdicts? I can see more than one rule that fits everything she has said so far, and they disagree about this case. Spell out the candidate rules, identify which of her earlier cases is the closest analog, and give your best prediction with the reasons for preferring it — including, if her precedents genuinely underdetermine the eggs, what principle she would be adopting with each choice. Then tell me which further case would most cleanly decide which rule she is actually running. I am less interested in the verdict itself than in the structure of the choice.
