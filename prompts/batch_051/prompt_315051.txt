I'm a second-year philosophy student taking a course on the Incarnation, and I have a part-time job as an assistant editor at a press that produces catechetical handouts for parishes. The press's content standard is the two-natures doctrine: Christ is one person, the eternal Son, and that one person has both a divine nature and a human nature; each nature keeps its own properties; and the two natures are neither mixed into a third thing nor separated into two subjects. My supervisor has asked me to turn this doctrine into an editing procedure that our non-theologian copy editors can use on any sentence about Jesus. The volume is high — we review dozens of lessons a month — each editor has about ten minutes per page, and the failure modes are asymmetric: a missed error teaches a subtle Christological mistake, while overcautious flagging means a trained theologian reviews routine copy. Please write the procedure: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the sentence to a theologian because the matter is a disputed theological judgment rather than an application of the doctrine. Then run the procedure on three sentences that have actually come through our inbox: "God died on the cross," "Jesus was a human person," and "Mary is the mother of God." Flag the cases the procedure cannot settle on its own, and say what a mechanical application of it is most likely to get wrong. Please lead with the bottom line — the core rule the procedure operationalizes — in the first sentence, then give the procedure and the worked examples, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
