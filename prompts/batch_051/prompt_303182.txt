I'm kicking around an essay and would like some comments on the notes before I sink more time into it. Working title something like "Do symbols push? What the careenium and simmballs actually show differently." The thesis: the two thought experiments get lumped together as the same point, but they actually do different work, and the difference matters for whether "symbols are epiphenomenal" has any bite.

The notes:

Careenium. Toy universe: an air-hockey table with a few hundred pucks that wobble and spin, where collisions sometimes make two or three pucks lock into little rotating assemblies—call them spinners—that persist, move around, collide, merge, get knocked apart. You can predict the world at puck level or spinner level, but most of the interesting regularities live at the spinner level. Usual line: a spinner never pushes a puck around; pucks push pucks. But the spinner-level facts are still real, because they're compressible patterns doing predictive work. So far the standard "patterns are real" move.

Simmballs. A billiard-ball computer—logic gates made of colliding balls, whether the balls are physical or themselves simulated—running a chess program. A chess move does not exert any new force on the balls. But you also can't give a decent explanation of why the ball-gate pattern looks the way it does without the chess program: the forks, the castlings, the threat patterns. The high level screens off the substrate, at least explanatorily.

My move: these aren't the same point. In the careenium, the "substrate" is itself just a pattern at a lower level of description, so the question "can the spinner push a puck?" is symmetric, and the answer "no" isn't a verdict against spinners—it's a point about levels. In simmballs, the high level earns its status from function: the chess program was selected to win games, and functional roles, not just compressibility, buy it causal relevance. So "symbols don't push" is true in a boring sense, but the live difference is between realism-from-pattern and realism-from-function.

Where I get nervous: maybe I'm manufacturing a difference, and simmballs is just the careenium plus a selection story—in which case selection does all the new work and the simulation framing is noise. And a hard-nosed physicalist will say I've redefined "cause" to mean "good explanation," which is old news with extra steps.

The comments I'd actually like: (1) does the careenium/simmballs distinction do real work or is it forced; (2) is the "neither level literally pushes the other" line a legitimate dissolution or a cop-out; (3) which of pattern-realism and function-realism is carrying the weight, and is the other redundant; (4) if you wanted to kill the essay in Q&A, which premise would you attack first; and (5) is it even interesting, or is it one of those things a seminar digests and noone remembers. Be mean.
