I'm a grad student in a philosophy/psych lab and I've spent the last year moderating an adversarial collaboration between two senior people who disagree about delusions as deeply as two people can while still working together. One is a doxasticist: delusions are genuinely beliefs — irrational, evidence-resistant, badly formed beliefs, but beliefs. The other is anti-doxastic: calling a delusion a belief imports the wrong cognitive kind; on his view many delusions are closer to imaginings, mislabeled avowals, or some in-between state that isn't a belief. We have been through the usual cases repeatedly (Capgras, Cotard, Fregoli, double-bookkeeping, monothematic versus polythematic, the jumping-to-conclusions findings), and the argument keeps collapsing into the two-factor theory. Both sides accept the broad two-factor architecture: a first factor produces the anomalous experience or content, and a second factor lets that content become a delusion. The dispute is over what kind of failure the second factor is — and that is where the belief question becomes impossible to dodge. For the doxasticist, factor two is a failure in belief evaluation, so a very bad candidate belief gets accepted and retained. For the anti-doxasticist, factor two is a failure to keep an imagining tagged as non-veridical, so the state was never in the business of being a belief at all.

I want you to write the document this collaboration should have produced after a year. Format it in three parts. First, a numbered list of agreed claims, written neutrally but not vacuously: include their shared description of the two-factor architecture, what double-bookkeeping consists in, and which clinical data both camps treat as constraints. Second, a disagreements section (a table is fine) where each disputed claim is phrased so that its own side would accept the wording of its view — no caricatures of the other side. Third, a cruxes section: three or four sub-questions such that if new evidence settled one of them, at least one of them would change position. For each crux, give a concrete case, manipulation, or kind of evidence both sides agree would bear on it, and pre-register what each side predicts. I'm not asking you to adjudicate; I need a text both could co-sign, with the residual disagreements made exact. Keep the doxasticist's and anti-doxasticist's predictions seperately labeled.
