I've been trying to understand the Rawls–Harsanyi debate and I keep hitting the same wall. As I understand it, Rawls says that behind the veil of ignorance you have no basis for assigning probabilities to ending up as any particular person, so rational choice means maximin: pick the arrangement where the worst-off position is as good as possible. Harsanyi replies that a fully rational person behind the veil should assign equal probability to being anyone, and then expected utility gives you something like average utilitarianism. I can see that these are two different decision rules, but I don't get what makes either one the "right" one. If the veil is supposed to strip away probabilities, Harsanyi's equal-probabilities move looks like he's just smuggling back in probabilities he admitted weren't there. But maximin also seems like one arbitrary rule among several. Why not minimax regret, which would presumably pick a different arrangement, or some other rule that also cares about the second-worst position? It all feels like a standoff where each side chooses the rule that happens to generate the politics it already held. Is one of them making a decision-theoretic mistake, or is the original-position setup just incapable of settling anything?
