I've been trying to get my actual view of collective action down on paper after a term of reading on shared agency, and I want a consistency check on the set. Here's where I've landed. (1) Joint action is a real difference in the world, not just a way of summarizing individual movements: two people dancing a tango are doing something jointly, while two strangers who happen to sway in time next to each other are not, even if the movements are identical from the outside. (2) What makes the difference has to be something in the participants' minds — in how they each represent what they're doing — since the bodily motions can be the same. (3) But I don't think there is any primitive we-mode or group consciousness: a shared intention is built entirely out of individual attitudes of the form 'I intend that we J,' meshed together and out in the open, so that each knows the others have them. (4) Still, a group can be blameworthy for an outcome that was in no member's head: a firm with a reckless culture causes a death that no manager or worker ever wanted or singly chose, and the organization itself — not just the individuals — is at fault. (5) And I insist that acting jointly doesn't require occurrent higher-order thoughts about the others' intentions most of the time: a parent and a small child tidying up together can count as a joint activity without the child representing anyone's intention as an intention.
