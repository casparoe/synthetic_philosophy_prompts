I am a software engineer, and I have been trying to make sense of a line of reasoning I have seen in online discussions of how to handle very small probabilities of very good or very bad outcomes. I can follow the probability, but I have no formal background in philosophy, so I do not know the literature and I may be mangling someone's careful position. The argument, as I reconstruct it, is this. (1) A rational agent should choose the option with the highest expected value, understood as the sum over outcomes of the value of the outcome weighted by its probability. (2) There is no principled upper bound on how valuable an outcome can be; whenever a good outcome is described, a still better one can be described, so possible values run arbitrarily high. (3) From (2) it follows that for any positive probability, no matter how small, there is a conceivable outcome valuable enough that its probability-weighted contribution is enormous, larger than any specified finite cost. (4) So expected value reasoning appears to say that one should be willing to pay any finite cost for a chance at some suitably enormous outcome, even when the chance is vanishingly small and the story behind the outcome is wildly speculative. (5) For instance, if a stranger says that unless I hand over my phone he will bring about an astronomically large number of simulated beings who suffer terribly, and I assign even one chance in ten to the hundredth power to his being able and willing to do that, the expected harm of refusing dwarfs the value of the phone, so I ought to hand it over. (6) That conclusion seems absurd, and no decision procedure should be held hostage by numbers that large. (7) But I do not want to give up expected value reasoning entirely, since it is what I trust when I decide whether to ship a change, run an experiment, or accept a wager with known odds.

Where does this argument go wrong, if it does? Please tell me which step or premise you reject, and what it costs to reject it. I would most like an answer that lets me keep expected value reasoning for ordinary decisions with well-defined probabilities while refusing to be governed by arbitrarily large values attached to arbitrarily small probabilities. Reply in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way you would write me an email.
