You've probably seen the cube-factory objection to the principle of indifference. A factory makes cubes, and all you know about the next one is that its side length L is somewhere between 0 and 1. By symmetry you'd say P(L < 1/2) = 1/2. But the face area A = L² also sits between 0 and 1, and the exact same symmetry logic says P(A < 1/4) = 1/4, even though those are the same event. Classic.

Now vary it. Instead of knowing nothing, you get one actual fact from a quality-control report: the mean side length is exactly 2/3, and that's all (no other moments, no distributional family). You decide to be a good objective Bayesian about it, so you run maximum entropy over L with the bound 0 < L < 1 and the constraint E[L] = 2/3. That's a truncated exponential, p(L) ∝ e^{βL}, with β chosen to force the mean.

Then run maxent again, but in the area paramtrization. The same information is E[√A] = 2/3 there. Do two calculations: (1) push the L-maxent density through A = L² to get a density over area; (2) run maxent directly on A with the constraint E[√A] = 2/3. Do you get the same family or not? If not, say exactly where the mismatch shows up (Jacobian factor, shape of the exponential, or both).

Now the philosophical bit. Does this show that maximum entropy still hasn't produced a unique rational prior, just moved the original parametrization problem one step back, even with a real constraint in hand? Or is this case relevantly different from the no-information cube factory? And what would a Jaynes-style defence have to add here — some privileged underlying measure or invariance group that has to be justified before the entropy calculation is even meaningful? Can you actually build one for this factory, or does that just push the problem up a level?
