Question: In the passage below, F. H. Bradley claims that relation and quality form a "vicious circle." Explain what this circle consists in, then show how the same difficulty generates the regress that has come to be called Bradley's regress when applied to the unity of a proposition. Conclude by stating what Bradley takes this to show about the truth and unity of relational judgments.

Passage (from F. H. Bradley, Appearance and Reality, Chapter III, "Relation and Quality"):

"The object of this chapter is to show that the very essence of these ideas is infected and contradicts itself. Our conclusion briefly will be this. Relation presupposes quality, and quality relation. Each can be something neither together with, nor apart from, the other; and the vicious circle in which they turn is not the truth about reality."

Student's answer:

"In the passage, Bradley is arguing that qualities and relations cannot be separated. Every quality is what it is only in contrast with other qualities, and that contrast is a relation. Conversely, every relation needs terms, and those terms must have qualities. So the two notions are mutually dependent: each presupposes the other. The 'vicious circle' is the circularity of definition: we cannot define 'quality' without invoking relations, nor 'relation' without invoking qualities, so neither concept is well-defined. When this point is applied to the unity of a proposition, Bradley's regress appears. If a relation R unifies the constituents a and b of a proposition, R is introduced as a further element alongside a and b. But then R must itself stand in a relation to a and b, so we need a further relation R1, and then R1 must stand in relation to R, a, and b, and so on indefinitely. Bradley takes the regress to show that no relational judgment can be true as stated: relations are only a way we organize appearances, not a feature of reality. The unity of the proposition is therefore not genuine; it is an appearance produced by our thought."
