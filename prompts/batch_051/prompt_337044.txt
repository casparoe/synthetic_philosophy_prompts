I run the modeling desk at a competition-economics consultancy. We build small simultaneous-move games — mostly 3x3 or 4x4 payoff matrices — for bidding, entry and pricing problems, a couple a week. The juniors get half a day per game, they've had one undergrad game theory course, and I sign off before anything reaches a client, so I need to be able to check their work fast. Before solving, we eliminate dominated strategies. The problem is they only remeber the pure-strategy rule: delete a strategy if another single strategy beats it against everything. So they miss cases where a strategy is strictly dominated only by a mixed strategy — say a 50/50 blend of two others — even though no pure strategy dominates it.

I want a written procedure they can follow for iterated elimination of dominated strategies on a small game, allowing mixed strategies as dominating strategies, and staying with strict dominance unless the weak-dominance case genuinely matters. Give me the actual tests in order, what counts as "yes, delete", when they should stop deleting, whether and why they need to record the order of deletion, and where they hand the thing to me or to a solver because the check is no longer doable on a spreadsheet. Then work the procedure through three cases: (1) a 3x3 where strategy C survives every pure comparison but is dominated by an even mix of A and B; (2) a game where a strategy only becomes dominated after another one is deleted, so the procedure has to say what gets rechecked after each deletion and whether the final surviving set depends on deletion order; (3) a game where the procedure ends up deleting the client's favorite strategy, the one the analysis was supposed to support.

Use four sections: Procedure (numbered steps, roughly 350 words); Worked examples (roughly 400 words); Where the procedure breaks (roughly 150 words); Judgment calls (roughly 100 words).
