Here's a pair of judgments that don't sit well together. If a middle-sized country—say one with a lot of coastline and a lot of coal—announced it was going to unilaterally start pumping sulfate aerosols into the stratosphere to cool the planet, people would call it a monstrous thing to do without asking the rest of the world. But when the same country announces a big expansion of coal mining and exports, with the climate consequences every bit as foreseable, it's treated as normal economic policy, not as one country unilaterally deciding to mess with everyone's climate. Is there a principle that generates both judgments, or is it mostly that one is new and legible as an intervention and the other is diffuse and familiar? I want the strongest possible defense of treating the two differently. In particular, don't just say the aerosols are risky: if you lean on unknown side effects, termination shock, or moral hazard, explain why the known and basically irreversible side effects of the emissions don't count in the same way. Then tell me honestly whether the asymmetry should stay, flip, or go.
