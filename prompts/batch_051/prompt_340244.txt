You are an expert in philosophical hermeneutics and the history of Christian theology, and you are comfortable doing close, charitable exegesis. Think carefully and begin by brainstorming the most literal readings before settling on an answer. Consider two New Testament passages, both from the King James Version, that seem to point in opposite directions on the question of whether a reader needs a human interpreter to understand Scripture. The first is often cited by those who stress the need for authoritative guidance:

Acts 8:30–31: "And Philip ran thither to him, and heard him read the prophet Esaias, and said, Understandest thou what thou readest? And he said, How can I, except some man should guide me? And he desired Philip that he would come up and sit with him."

The second is often cited by those who stress the sufficiency of the Spirit and the individual reader:

1 John 2:27: "But the anointing which ye have received of him abideth in you, and ye need not that any man teach you: but as the same anointing teacheth you of all things, and is truth, and is no lie, and even as it hath taught you, ye shall abide in him."

These two passages can be read as giving opposite answers to the question "who decides what the text means?" Do they genuinely conflict, or can they be reconciled? Work through the candidate reconciliations — for example, differences in situation (a pre-Christian outsider reading Isaiah versus already-taught believers), the difference between an initial need for a guide and a later independence from any teacher, or a difference between ordinary human instruction and the Spirit's teaching. Identify which distinctions are already present in the texts, which are imposed on them, and which reconciliation survives honest contact with both passages rather than flattening one of them. Then spell out what your best reconciliation implies for the principle of sola scriptura: if the text is the final authority, what settles the disagreement between these two texts about who may say what the text means? End by saying which reading is most honest and which amounts to special pleading.
