Take Hempel's raven paradox and alter it by making the background facts and the sampling protocol fully explicit. Let H be the hypothesis that all ravens are black. Its contrapositive equivalent is that all non-black things are non-ravens. Hempel's equivalence condition, together with the Nicod criterion, implies that an observation of a non-black non-raven (a white shoe) confirms H, because the white shoe is a positive instance of the contrapositive. Applied directly to the original hypothesis, by contrast, the Nicod criterion counts a black raven as a positive instance and treats a white shoe as irrelevant.

Now suppose a perfectly reliable scanner has inventoried a closed warehouse containing exactly 2,000,000 objects. The inventory establishes as background knowledge that exactly 200 objects are ravens and exactly 200,000 are black. We compare two simple alternatives: H0 says all 200 ravens are black; H1 says exactly one raven is non-black and the other 199 are black. Under both hypotheses, then, exactly 1,800,000 objects are non-black.

For each of the following reports, calculate the likelihood ratio P(report | H0) / P(report | H1), showing your reasoning:

(i) An object selected uniformly at random from the whole warehouse is reported to be a non-black non-raven.

(ii) The scanner samples objects uniformly from the whole warehouse, discarding each one, until it finds the first non-black object, and that object is reported to be a non-raven.

(iii) An object selected uniformly at random from among all non-ravens is reported to be non-black.

Also calculate the same ratio for the report that an object selected uniformly at random from the whole warehouse is a black raven.

Using these numbers, answer the following. In this precise finite setting, is it still strictly true that observing a non-black non-raven confirms the hypothesis that all ravens are black, and does the original paradox survive once the base rates are made explicit? What does the comparison between the white-shoe ratios and the black-raven ratio show about the thought that the two observations are on a par as positive instances? Finally, assess which standard diagnosis of the paradox is best supported by your calculations: the view that a non-black non-raven genuinely satisfies Hempel's confirmation relation, the Bayesian response that it confirms only negligibly when non-black things vastly outnumber ravens, or the response that what matters is the sampling protocol. Does any one of these diagnoses fail to capture something your three protocols reveal?
