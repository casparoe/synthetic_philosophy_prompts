Counterfactual: in 1990 Switzerland replaces its federal popular-initiative procedure—constitutional initiatives, 100,000 signatures in eighteen months, a double majority of the people and the cantons, and a parliamentary counter-proposal route—with a California-style direct initiative. Under the new rules, signatures equal to 5 percent of the registered electorate suffice to place a proposed federal statute on the ballot, and 8 percent for a proposed constitutional amendment, again within eighteen months. A simple national popular majority enacts either kind of measure; the majority-of-cantons requirement is abolished. Initiatives take effect exactly as drafted: no parliamentary counter-proposal, no implementing legislation, and the Federal Assembly may not amend or repeal them except by returning the identical question to the voters. A single-subject rule is the only real filter. Everything else about Swiss political structure remains unchanged: the multiparty Federal Council and concordance system, cantonal autonomy, directorial executive, and the existing mandatory and optional referendums on parliamentary legislation.

Predict, with reasoning, how Swiss politics and policy would have looked by 2020 compared with the actual record. I am less interested in a verdict of better or worse than in the mechanisms: which actors would move first (parties, interest groups, the Federal Council, cantons, courts, foreign partners), which policy areas would become initiative targets, and how the system would feed back on party discipline and coalition government.

Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, no introduction or conclusion. Each item should state one predicted development, the mechanism producing it, and a confidence judgment, in one or two sentences.
