so i've been chewing on this on my own and i cant get it to add up. everyone says we should reward hard work and that people should get ahead by what they do, not who their parents are. but the tax system here in the US seems to point the other way. if i work and earn a salary, a big chunk gets taken in income tax before i ever see it. but if my parents leave me a house or a pile of money, there's a huge exemption, so i might end up with it without paying a cent. and i read somewhere that if my parents bought a stock for a dollar and it's worth a million when they die, and then i sell it, i don't pay income tax on that gain at all—the whole gain over their lifetime just disappears, like it never happened. these rules didn't write themselves, somebody chose them. so what picture of fairness, or of the family, or of equal opportunity, do those rules actually presuppose? do they quietly assume that inherited money is more legitimately mine than money i earned, or that parents have something like an unlimited right to give their kids a head start? or is there a way to see the rules as not presupposing any of that, just neutral bookkeeping—and does that neutral reading hold up once you look at what the rules do to where different people start the race? and if the rules do presuppose that family transfers are just obviously fine, what would have to change in them, or in how people defend them, if we stopped assuming that?
