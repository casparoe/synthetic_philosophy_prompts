I'm a software engineer and I've gone down a rabbit hole reading arguments about climate, meat, and voting, particularly the "your individual contribution is too small to matter" thing. That claim feels right to me about some cases and obviously wrong about others, so I'm trying to reverse-engineer whatever rule I'm actually using. Here are my verdicts, and they don't all sit comfortably together.

(1) If a friend buys a factory-farmed chicken at the supermarket, I think that's wrong. I know the store orders in bulk and his one purchase almost certainly doesn't change how many chickens are killed, but I still think he shouldn't do it. (2) If he skips voting in a presidential election in a safe state, I don't think he's done anything wrong, even though his preferred candidate winning would be genuinely better for a lot of people. His vote won't decide the outcome, and I wouldn't blame him for staying home. (3) If he books a transatlantic flight just for a fun weekend, I think that's at least mildly wrong because of the emissions, even though his seat contributes a vanishingly small slice of the total harm. (4) But I don't have the same feeling about him running a beefy gaming PC all day, or driving an old car to the store instead of walking: those are also small contributions to the same problem, so I can't see why the flight counts and those don't.

My first-pass rule was somethig like: an act is wrong if it increases expected harm in the world, even by a tiny amount, and you should do an act if it increases expected value, even by a tiny amount. But that rule smashes straight into my own verdicts. It would make skipping the safe-state vote wrong (the expected value of voting is positive, just small), and it would make the gaming PC as wrong as the flight. So either my rule is wrong, or some of those verdicts are. I want to know: is there a principle—maybe a baroque one—that captures the whole pattern? If the clean principles all force me to overturn one or two verdicts, which ones should I give up first and why? And whatever principle you end up with, tell me what it says about a case I genuinely haven't settled: joining a large consumer boycott of a company where one person's withheld purchase won't be noticed and wouldn't change the company's behavior.
