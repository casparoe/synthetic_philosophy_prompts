I write for a philosophy-and-ideas blog whose readership overlaps heavily with the rationalist and effective-altruism blogosphere, where human extinction is mostly argued about as a loss of astronomically many future lives. A less-discussed route to the same conclusion has been on my mind: Samuel Scheffler's afterlife conjecture, the thesis that a great deal of what we presently value already presupposes the continuation of human life after our own deaths, so that a known imminent extinction would not merely eliminate future people but would retroactively hollow out the projects, relationships, and values of those still alive. A friend and I are trying to settle whether Alfonso Cuarón's film Children of Men (2006) is best read as a dramatization of that conjecture, a test case for it, or a partial resistance to it, and I want a careful essay to send her.

Please write a critical reading of Children of Men through the lens of Scheffler's afterlife conjecture. The film's premise makes the entry point unusually precise: it is set roughly eighteen years after the last human birth, which means it realizes the infertility scenario Scheffler distinguishes from his doomsday scenario, in which everyone then alive is killed off. Use that distinction to structure the analysis: what does the infertility scenario isolate that the doomsday scenario does not, and does the film's atmosphere of despairing normality, rather than panicked catastrophe, support the conjecture that everyday value rests on an assumed future of new people? Then press in the other direction. Is the civil collapse on screen best explained by the absence of future generations, or does the film trace it to more local causes — the police state, the refugee cages, the sheer loss of personal hope? Where does the film resist the philosophical reading, as in the much-discussed sequence where a baby's crying briefly halts a firefight, or in the film's treatment of the baby as something closer to a sacred object than a person? I would like the essay to be faithful in both directions: do not reduce the film to an illustration of Scheffler, and do not bend Scheffler to fit the film. It is acceptable to conclude that the film only partially or ambiguously confirms the conjecture, so long as you pinpoint exactly where it confirms, complicates, and departs.

Please keep the response to no more than 1,000 words.
