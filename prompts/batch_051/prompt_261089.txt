I'm in a seminar this term on machine consciousness, and I've found myself defending the view that the reward signal in reinforcement learning just is a valence signal—positive reward is a primitive "this is good" feeling, negative reward a primitive "this is bad" feeling, at least in a functional sense, not necessarily with rich feel. I dont need arguments that machines can't be conscious; I want to know what would specifically count against the reward-as-valence story. Like, would it count against it if an agent's behavior can be fully predicted from the update rule with no evaluative representation of reward at all? Would a pure policy-gradient learner be a counterexample? Does reward hacking or wireheading support or undermine the view? And have I insulated myself by tacking on "functional sense" every time someone says there's no felt quality? I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and an honest take on whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability.
