Okay, so I'm a novelist, and I need a working definition of violence — not the dictionary kind, the kind I can use to keep a whole cast of characters honest. My current book is about a woman who's been trying to live as a strict pacifist for decades, not just "war is bad" but as a daily rule. The plot turns on a fight inside her peace collective over whether smashing up a drone factory at night counts as violence. I've wrote that argument five times now and I keep flip-flopping depending on who talked to me last.

Give me a definition of violence with a principle behind it. It should be crisp enough that a person could actually steer their life by it. Then run it against the cases below. Some of these feel like obvious yeses, some feel like obvious noes, and the middle ones are the whole reason I'm asking. If the definition gives a weird verdict, either revise the definition or defend the weird verdict — don't just shrug. Also tell me whether your line is doing the work at the level of the act, the intention, or the consequence, because my characters would fight about exactly that.

The cases:
- A man punches another man during a bar fight.
- A surgeon cuts into an anesthetized patient to remove an appendix.
- A hunger striker refuses food until the state releases a political prisoner.
- A protester publishes a police chief's home address after a shooting.
- A member of the collective smashes the windows of a munitions factory at night; no one is inside.
- A landlord raises the rent on a long-term sick tenant, knowing she'll be evicted and will get worse.
- A woman shoves a man hard enough that he falls, because he was about to grab her in a parking garage.
- A parent screams at their child every morning until the child stops speaking in public.

Lead with the definition in one sentence, then run the cases in order, and then tell me which of your verdicts is least secure and what fact about the world would flip it.

Keep it under 750 words.
