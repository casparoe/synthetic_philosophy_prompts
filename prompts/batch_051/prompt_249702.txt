So my friend and I just spent a whole evening arguing about whether it even makes sense to call something "bad grammar." She's basically a descriptivist: languages are just different systems, so the best you can say is "wrong for the context," and a lot of the rules people enforce are just class markers. I kept insisting that no, if a student writes "I could of stayed longer" in a college paper they've made an actual error, in a way that's different from a speaker of African American English saying "He be working nights." By the end we were talking passed each other and I realized I can't actually state what I think a grammatical error is.

So give me a working definition of "bad grammar" (or "grammatical error" — whichever term can carry the weight). Don't just tell me to consult a style guide; I want to know what makes something an error rather than a difference. Then run the definition through every one of these cases, flag which verdicts you're least secure about, and patch or defend the definition where it starts to give weird results.

- "I could of stayed longer" in a college paper.
- "For all intensive purposes, the plan is fine" in an office email.
- "He be working nights" from a speaker of African American English.
- Chomsky's colorless-green-ideas sentence — perfectly grammatical but it means nothing.

- "I ain't done nothing" in casual speech.
