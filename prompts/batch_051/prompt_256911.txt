I want them to actually feel the force of it, not just see it as an artifact. I remember being struck by Russell's own early discussion in The Principles of Mathematics where he describes being led to the contradiction by reflecting on the class of all classes that are not members of themselves: the predicate seems perfectly clear, but the membership question flips either way. I don't want to lecture that at them; I want the activity to recreate it. Design an in-class game or structured activity where students construct sets from ordinary predicates, apply a membership-check procedure they can run themselves, and run into the contradiction before I name it. Don't use the barber version—it's too familiar and lets them file it under "logic puzzles." Give me rules, materials (no tech), a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened to unrestricted comprehension and the move to separation. Also tell me what predictably goes wrong the first time, and which feature of the real paradox the activity cannot reproduce. Finally, say how confident you are in the design choices and which element you're least sure of.
