I'm a philosophy PhD student working on population ethics, and I've been stuck on an asymmetry that shows up in my own judgments no matter how I try to talk myself out of it. It's probably the deepest reason I find myself drawn to suffering-focused views, but I cant find a clean statement of what would actually justify it, so I want someone to make the strongest possible case.

Here is the asymmetry, put concretely. Suppose I can prevent a child from stepping into a patch of stinging nettles by shouting a warning at no cost; if I stay quiet, people think I did something wrong, and I agree. But suppose I could instead, at no cost, give a child a few minutes of real delight—buy them an ice cream, point them to a litter of puppies—and I don't. Nobody treats that as a wrong; it's just not my job. The same split shows up with lives we might create. A couple who know their child would have a condition causing severe chronic pain are criticized for conceiving anyway; the pain counts against the act. But a couple who decide not to have a third child who would have been very happy are criticized by nobody, and the happiness doesnt count as a reason to have the child. So we treat the prevention, reduction, or non-creation of suffering as morally weighty—often as a duty—while we treat the promotion or creation of happiness as optional, supererogatory at best.

What I want is this. Find the strongest principle, or nested set of principles, that generates both halves of this pattern without also generating verdicts we'd reject. Test it against nearby cases: the same quantity of pain versus pleasure in a single existing person, tradeoffs between persons, cases where you must cause a small pain to secure a large pleasure, and the question of whether preventing a pain and preventing the loss of a future pleasure are symmetric. Then give me an honest verdict: should I keep this asymmetry and let it pull me toward something like negative utilitarianism, or is it just a psychological quirk—scope sensitivity, loss aversion, status quo bias—that has no good moral justification and ought to be given up?
