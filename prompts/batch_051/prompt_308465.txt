ok so my philosophy club is doing a unit on personhood and the rule is you have to write down what you actually think before anyone else gets to argue with it, and I'd rather find the weak spots first. here's where I've landed:

(1) I'm drawn to an ubuntu-ish picture: being a person is something you partly become through other people—being named, held, talked to, remembered, having a place in other people's lives. its not just a species thing, and its not something you bootstrap on your own.
(2) but I also think babies, people in comas, and people with serious intellectual disabilities have full moral standing and can be wronged, even though they can't participate in relationships much from their side.
(3) and I think dehumanization can't actually take someone's personhood away. if an entire society refuses to treat some group as persons, the wrongness is that real persons are being treated as less than they are. recognition doesn't create the personhood; at best it acknowledges something already there.
(4) but then I think a child who grows up with almost no human contact (like a feral child) is missing something person-related, not just missing social skills. so I don't think personhood is purely biological either.
(5) the way I currently hold it together is to split moral standing from full personhood: standing you have just from being human, full personhood you grow into through relationships.

so does that actually hang together? go through the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only look like they clash, and rank the real tensions by severity. then say which single revision would clean up the most and what it would cost. also tell me what would change your mind—which argument or consideration would flip your verdict, or where your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
