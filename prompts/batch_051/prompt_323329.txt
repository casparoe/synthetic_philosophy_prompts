I am trying to work out what I actually think about promising, starting from my reactions to particular cases rather than from a theory. Here are my verdicts, roughly as they come to me.

1. A colleague agrees to read a draft of mine by Friday. On Thursday evening she gets a more tempting invitation. I think she should still do the reading, or at least ask to reschedule; she should not simply skip it.

2. On his deathbed, my father made me promise to keep running the family shop. He is no longer alive to be disappointed, to rely on me, or to release me. I still think I am bound.

3. A close friend told me something in confidence, and I promised not to tell anyone. She has since wholly forgotten both that she told me and that I promised. I still think I am bound.

4. A mugger holds a gun to my head and makes me promise to bring him money the next day. I do not think this promise binds me.

5. I promise to meet an acquaintance for coffee. I think a real obligation exists, but it is a light one and fairly easily overridden; if I cannot come, I owe her notice rather than simply not appearing.

6. If the person to whom I made a promise later releases me from it, freely and without pressure, I think the obligation is dissolved.

7. I promise a neighbour to water her plants while she is away, and then break my leg. I think the promise is still broken and I owe her a substitute and an explanation, even though I am not to blame.

8. I make a New Year's resolution to myself to exercise. I do not think this has the same force as a promise to another; I may revise it without wronging anyone.

I keep going back and forth on one further case. A friend extracted a promise from me to go into a project with him because he had no one else; since then, through no fault of either of us, the project has come to matter to him not at all, though he has never released me. Am I still bound?

Is there a single principle — an account of what makes a promise bind, when it does — that captures all or most of these verdicts? If none captures all of them, tell me which verdict you would overturn and why. Please state the principle precisely, show how it handles each case, say what it predicts for cases I have not listed, including the one I am torn on, and be candid about which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate. I would like the answer in British English. Finally, please say what would change your mind: which step in your argument is most likely to fail, and what argument or consideration would lead you to a different principle or a different verdict.
