I'm teaching Billy Budd next term and keep getting stuck on the trial scene. The whole point seems to be that Billy didn't mean to kill Claggart—he can't even get the words out to defend himself, and the blow is almost a reflex—yet Captain Vere insists the court should look only at the act and it's effect, not at intention. I'd like a reading of the novella through that lens: does Vere actually have an argument from military discipline here, or is he just rationalizing? And finally, do you think the novella makes a point about punishing without mens rea that the standard philosophy-of-law debate misses, or is it just muddying a distinction the lawyers actually keep cleaner?
