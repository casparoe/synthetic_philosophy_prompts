Before I read any more theories of how scientific thought experiments work, I want to nail down what such a theory actually has to deliver. Write the desiderata for an adequate account of how thought experiments in science can produce knowledge or improved understanding without new observation or experiment. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement. At a minimum, work through these five: Galileo's falling-bodies reasoning (two stones tied together, one heavier than the other, on the assumption that heavier bodies fall faster), Newton's rotating bucket of water, Einstein's elevator in free fall, Maxwell's demon sorting molecules, and the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen scenario. I do not need you to summarize the cases; use them as constraints, and say explicitly what each one forces a theory to accommodate—for instance, the ability of an imagined case to refute a proposed law without a single measurement, to expose an unnoticed assumption, or to make a physically impossible idealization informative. Keep the central question fixed: how can a scenario run entirely in the head yield new belief or genuine understanding about the empirical world?

Then separate the genuine desiderata from the tendentious ones. Flag which requirements you take to be non-negotiable for any adequate account, and which are really the property of a particular school—empiricist reconstruction, rationalist or Platonist intuition, mental simulation, or the view that thought experiments are just arguments in disguise. I particularly want to know whether any pair of plausible requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, as would be the case if one requirement says the imagined scenario must be physically realizable and another says we still learn from impossible or infinite idealizations; if you find such a conflict, say how a good theory should handle it rather than pretending it away. Finish by briefly scoring the main families against your list, and identify the single requirement that does the most to narrow the field. The list itself is the deliverable, not the scorecard.
