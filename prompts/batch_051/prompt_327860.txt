I sit on the ethics committee of a mid-sized fertility network, and we're suppose to have a revised policy on preimplantation genetic testing ready for the board by Friday. The case that's been eating our meetings is a couple with no known single-gene disorders on either side who want to rank their IVF embryos using a polygenic score for educational attainment. The score is built from hundreds of common variants, and our medical director keeps reminding us that it only explains something like five percent of the variation in the population, but the couple's position is basically: we're already doing IVF, the test exists, and if it gives our kid even a slightly better expected shot at a good life it would be wrong not to use it. One member wants to approve it under plain reproductive autonomy, a few think it turns children into made-to-order products and will invite sex-selection and trait-selection creep, and another keeps saying that if the test really does indicate a better expected life then the question isn't just permission but perhaps a duty. I'm the one who has to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday so we can stop going in circles.

Draft that opinion as continuous prose, like an email or letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables. It should recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with an actionable bottom line, and it should take seriously the strongest argument on the losing side rather than just listing considerations.
