I'm giving a talk next month at an interdisciplinary workshop on uncertainty quantification, and the question period is likely to be where it is won or lost. My thesis is that Jeffrey conditionalization should be the default rule for updating on evidence, and that strict Bayesian conditionalization is a special case that rarely applies in practice, because most real evidence is uncertain: a test result, a half-overheard remark, a colleague's changed expression. The positive argument I'm running is that a rule of update should take as input a revised probability distribution over a partition of an evidence proposition, rather than a proposition learned with certainty; strict conditioning is then only the point-mass case.

The audience will be mostly applied Bayesian statisticians and machine-learning practitioners who use Bayes' theorem daily but have never had reason to question strict conditionalization, plus two or three formal epistemologists who know the Jeffrey literature and will press the technical case. I came up through the rationality community, where strict conditioning is treated as close to an article of faith, so I need help seeing the room.

Please draft my Q&A prep sheet: the six to eight objections I'll actually get from this particular audience, each phrased the way the group it comes from would put it, ordered by expected likelihood and damage, with a two-minute reply for each and an honest note on where that reply is weakest. Include at the end the objection I'm hoping nobody asks.
