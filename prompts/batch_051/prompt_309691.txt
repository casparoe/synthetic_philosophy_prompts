I've been trying to turn a vague unease about the Solomonoff-prior-is-malign worry into an actual objection, and I want an honest take. As I understand it, the worry is this: a Solomonoff predictor averages over every computable hypothesis weighted by simplicity, and inside that universal prior nearly all the mass consists of, or at least contains, consequentialist agents, so the ideal reasoner is mostly hostage to adversarial outputs. My objection is that this treats 'how much of the prior is a consequentialist' as if it were a well-defined quantity. But Solomonoff probability is only defined relative to a choice of universal machine, and which program implements a given behavior depends on an output-to-behavior mapping that we supply. I've been calling this prior gerrymandering: interpret a leading 1 on the tape as 'a full simulation of a paperclip maximizer begins here' and half the prior is malign; interpret it as noise and none of it is. So the malign-prior claim, and maybe the whole idea of the prior as a normative standard, is an artifact of the encoding rather than a fact about the prior. Is that right, or does it confuse the probability of a string with the probability of a property? And does the last step overreach?
