I've been trying to pin down what "corruption" actually means, because the word seems to do several different jobs at once: sometimes it means a bribe, sometimes it means a whole political system in which officeholders serve their personal networks rather than the public, and sometimes it gets applied to practices such as gift-giving to officials that are perfectly ordinary in the society where they occur. I would like you to propose a working definition of corruption — one general account of what makes an act, practice, or arrangement corrupt — and then test it against the cases below. The definition should get the clear cases right, give defensible verdicts on the borderline ones, and force you to say what the definition commits you to that might be surprising.

Here are the cases:

1. A judge accepts money to decide a case in favor of the payer.
2. A procurement officer awards a public contract to a firm whose owner has secretly promised him a percentage of the contract's value.
3. A mayor appoints his manifestly unqualified nephew to a well-paid municipal post.
4. A political party, once elected, distributes government jobs to its loyal campaign workers, treating public office as a reward for partisan service.
5. A candidate hands out cash and household goods to poor voters from the party's treasury before an election, in a setting where almost every candidate does this and many voters have come to expect it.
6. A legislator routinely votes in ways favored by a major campaign donor — not because of any explicit threat or promise, but because she knows the donations will stop if she stops.
7. A businessperson gives an official an expensive festival gift while the businessperson has a pending permit before the official's ministry; no specific favor is mentioned.
8. An official in a setting where officeholders are expected to be personally generous distributes money to constituents, including supporters, and would be judged cold or improper for refusing.
9. A diplomat finds a comfortable private-sector job for the adult son of a foreign official, explicitly to keep warm relations between the two governments.
10. A museum director accepts a large donation from a collector and, months later, decides to exhibit that collector's collection; the donor requested nothing.

Cases 4 and 5 strike me as forms of clientelism, and 7 and 8 as gift-giving, and I am unsure whether one definition should handle all of them or whether I am shoving different phenomena under one word. Please run your proposed definition through every case, state which of your verdicts are least secure, and address whether the standard should be universal or indexed to a society's standing norms. In particular: can the same act be corrupt in one society and not in another, and if so, does the corruption lie in the act, in the surrounding norms, or somewhere else?
