I'm a novelist working on a book set in a small town where a woman buys an abandoned chapel and turns it into a techno club. Half the town calls it desecration, and I realized I'm not sure what they're actualy claiming — is it the building, the history, the god, or just the loud music? I want a working definition of desecration (or the sacred, if that's the better place to start) that I can use to think through my plot and figure out whether my characters are having a real argument.

Here are the cases I've been stewing over:
- A protester burning a national flag during a demonstration.
- A family scattering a parent's ashes at sea because the parent loved sailing, even though another sibling insists the parent wanted a churchyard burial.
- An archaeologist carefully exhuming a medieval cemetery to study diet and disease, with the blessing of the parish that uses that churchyard.
- A collector buying a Medal of Honor at auction and displaying it next to baseball cards.
- A woman tearing up every photograph of her late husband in a grief-fueled rage, then taping one back together.
- The same chapel I just described being opened as a nightclub after it was formally deconsecrated and sold.
- A Holocaust memorial left to fall into such disrepair that the weeds keep visitors away from the stone.

I'm fairly sure the first one counts as desecration and the third one doesn't, but the rest I keep flip-flopping on. Give me a definition that gets these cases right, run it through all seven, and tell me which verdict you're least confident about. Then say whether you think "desecration" is a distinct moral category, or really just harm or disrespect wearing a religious-sounding coat — because if it's that, I suspect the town in my novel isn't actually disagreeing about anything real.
