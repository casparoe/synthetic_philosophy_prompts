I'm in a reading group that's split down the middle over whether history can be turned into a predictive science. The Turchin-style side runs demographic-structural models: elite overproduction (too many trained elites chasing too few positions) and popular immiseration build up over a century or so, then the state hits a crisis, and the secular cycle starts again. The other side says the whole field is curve-fitting — a few noisy data points, periods and boundaries chosen after looking at the outcome, and cycles that vanish out-of-sample. This fight has been going on for decades in the field, and for months in my group, and nobody has moved. Everyone agrees on the individual events, so the facts aren't obviously what's dividing us. I can't tell what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it a factual question about whether the cycles replicate? A disagreement about what an explanation in history has to do — mechanism and prediction versus narrative and singular causes? Or a deeper disagreement about whether large social systems have stable enough causal structure for anything like laws of history? My hunch is its mostly the second, and the empirical argument is a smokescreen. Diagnose the disagreement, and say what would actually count as progress given the diagnosis — and what would count as progress if your diagnosis is different.
