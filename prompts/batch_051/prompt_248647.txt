In chapter XIV of the Second Treatise of Government, Locke argues that because many accidents may happen in which a strict and rigid observation of the laws would do harm, the executive must sometimes be allowed to act for the public good even without the law, and sometimes against it; he calls this prerogative. He treats it as a necessary discretion in emergencies, not a blank license.

Now suppose a country writes a modern version of this into its constitution. The article says: "When urgent public necessity requires it, the executive may take measures inconsistent with existing statutes, including emergency border controls, quarantine and lockdown orders, seizure of medical supplies, and temporary restrictions on assembly, provided that within ten days the executive publishes a written justification identifying the law being derogated from and the specific harm to be prevented. A standing Emergency Review Commission, whose members are appointed by each major party in proportion to its seats, must be given the justification and may refer the measure to a randomly selected citizens' assembly of 150 people. The assembly may annul the measure by a two-thirds vote. If the measure is annulled, it is void from the start, but officials acting in good faith are immune from prosecution."

Treat this as a mechanism, not as a moral argument for or against executive power. What does it incentivize among the executive, the legislature, the parties that appoint the review commission, the agencies implementing the orders, and the citizens in the assembly? How would it actually get used as time passes—what kind of emergency gets declared, and when? Where does it get gamed first? Does the citizens' assembly become a rubber stamp, a permanent veto, a theatre for national arguments, or something else? How might the executive structure its justifications to survive review without giving up discretion? Walk through a few scenarios—for example, a genuine pandemic, a manufactured border crisis, a slow-moving economic emergency, and an emergency that looks real but is being used to lock in unrelated policies. Then say whether the failure modes can be fixed by modifying the commission, the assembly, the time limits, or the standard of review, or whether the Lockean premise itself keeps leaking through whatever box you put around it.
