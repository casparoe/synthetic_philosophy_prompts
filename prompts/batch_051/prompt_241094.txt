In a lot of U.S. school districts, a kid only gets learning accommodations—extra time, a quiet room, an aide—if there's a formal ADHD diagnosis on file, and that diagnosis is made mostly from parent and teacher rating scales. But the same school psychologists who make the diagnosis run teacher trainings warning that the label 'ADHD' changes how teachers read ordinary fidgeting and how children see themselves ('I can't focus, I have ADHD'). So the institution treats the diagnosis as a stable fact that warrants services while also teaching that assigning the diagnosis perturbs the very behavior it tracks.
