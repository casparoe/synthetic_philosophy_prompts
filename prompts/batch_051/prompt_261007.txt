I've been chewing on a passage from John Dewey's *Democracy and Education* (Chapter VII, "The Democratic Conception in Education") and an objection a classmate made. Dewey writes:

"Now in any social group whatever, even in a gang of thieves, we find some interest held in common, and we find a certain amount of interaction and coöperative intercourse with other groups. From these two traits we derive our standard. How numerous and varied are the interests which are consciously shared? How full and free is the interplay with other forms of association?"

The way I read him, he's trying to get a normative standard for evaluating forms of social life without inventing an ideal out of whole cloth: start from two features that show up in every actually existing group—shared interests and intergroup interaction—but then use them as dimensions of degree, so a better society is one where the shared interests are more numerous and varied and the contact with other groups is fuller and freer.

My classmate reconstructed Dewey's move as an argument and says it is either trivial or question-begging:

(1) Every existing social group, even a criminal band, has some interests consciously shared among its members.
(2) Every existing social group has some amount of interaction with other groups.
(3) The worth of any mode of social life is to be judged by how numerous and varied its shared interests are and how full and free its interplay with other groups is.
(4) Since the standard in (3) is derived from traits present in every group, it can only rank groups by degree along those two dimensions; it gives no independent reason to treat those dimensions as valuable.
(5) Therefore Dewey's criterion either just describes a possible ordering rather than justifying it, or it secretly assumes the conclusion that inclusive, open forms of association are better.

I think the conclusion is too strong, but I can't figure out whether the fault is in (3), (4), or somewhere else in Dewey's text. Is this a fair reconstruction? Is the argument valid? If it is valid but unsound, say exactly which step fails and what the strongest reading of that step would be; if it is valid and sound, say what that means for Dewey's project.
