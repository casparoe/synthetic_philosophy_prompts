Two members of a philosophy of language seminar have spent the term defending, respectively, epistemicism and supervaluationism about vagueness, and they have now agreed to an adversarial collaboration. Draft the joint statement the two of them could both sign. Open with a numbered section of claims both accept, covering what the sorites paradox does and does not show, what a borderline case is, what a semantics for a vague predicate must account for, and what role classical logic should play in the theory. Follow this with a precise statement of the residual disagreement, with each side's own view formulated in wording its proponent would endorse: the epistemicist's sharp but unknowable cutoffs together with a margin-for-error account of why we cannot know where they lie, and the supervaluationist's rejection of bivalence in favor of truth on some precisifications and falsity on others, with a sentence true only if supertrue. Then provide a disagreements table whose rows identify the genuine cruxes, such as whether bivalence may be abandoned, whether a sorites series contains a determinate boundary, and whether higher-order vagueness is better handled as precisifications of precisifications or as the unknowability of sharp second-order boundaries. For each crux, specify a concrete case or argument both parties would accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each side would predict or say about that case. Conclude by identifying any portions of the dispute that the collaboration revealed to be verbal rather than substantive, and explain why.
