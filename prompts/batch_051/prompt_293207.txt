I am setting the final exam for an upper-level seminar on intuitionistic logic and mathematical constructivism. The class is a mix of philosophy and mathematics students, and the recurring weakness in their written work is that they can state the BHK interpretation of the connectives but cannot say what, if anything, formal metatheoretic results about intuitionistic systems establish philosophically.

The question should be built around the following tension. Constructivists sometimes cite the disjunction property of intuitionistic logic — that if A ∨ B is provable, then either A or B is provable — and the analogous existence property of Heyting arithmetic — that if ∃x A(x) is provable, then A(n) is provable for some numeral n — as evidence that these systems capture what it is for a disjunction or an existence claim to be established constructively. A thoughtful student might reply that these are results about particular formal systems, and it is not obvious that they carry the philosophical weight constructivists place on them. The BHK clauses already say, semantically, why a constructive proof of a disjunction or an existence claim yields one of those things, so the metatheoretic property may be more of a consistency check than an independent argument for constructivism; and the properties by themselves do not show that a system has the right meaning-theoretic credentials, since a system might possess them for accidental or proof-theoretic reasons.

The question should force students to do at least three things: (a) spell out how the disjunction and existence properties relate to the BHK clauses for ∨ and ∃, including the direction of dependence between the semantic clauses and the metatheoretic properties; (b) explain why the corresponding properties fail for classical logic and classical arithmetic, and assess what that failure actually shows; (c) give and evaluate at least one reason to doubt that the disjunction property, by itself, demonstrates that intuitionistic logic is constructive in the intended sense, even if it is a desirable property for a constructive system to have.

Please provide three things. First, the exam question itself, worded so that a strong student recognizes that restating the BHK clauses is necessary but not sufficient; the question may state the two metatheoretic properties compactly, but must not supply the analysis. Second, a model answer of about 700 words. Third, a marking scheme out of 20 that separates an A answer from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain, together with a one-sentence note on the most common way students who understand the material still lose marks on this question.
