I've been chewing on this on and off for a few months and I want someone to kick the tires before I post it and get torn apart. The background question is: why would an AI want anything at all? The standard story for organisms is evolution—wanting food, status, safety, etc. bought fitness. But an AI isn't shaped by that. It could just be a calculator that solves a problem without wanting anything, and most current systems obviously are that. So what would it take for an AI to actually want something, rather than just behave as if it wants it?

My candidate is a distinction Ive been calling modeled wanting vs. behavioral wanting. Behavioral wanting is the revealed-preference story: the system's policy raises the probability of X, full stop. Modeled wanting is stricter. The system has a world-model that includes its own policy as a variable, and it represents the difference between X and not-X as a gap that its own outputs are supposed to close. It doesn't merely produce actions that tend toward X; it marks some of its own candidate actions as wrong relative to X, the way I mark the plan that loses my keys as the wrong plan.

The claim: modeled wanting is the only kind of wanting worth the name, and it can show up in an AI with no evolutionary history, because all it requires is (i) a self-model, (ii) a target variable made salient by training, and (iii) enough capacity to evaluate its own candidate policies along that variable. A thermostat behaviorally wants 72F, but it doesn't modeled-want it. A self-modeling RL agent, on the other hand, would literally want reward, not merely act as if it did.

I know this brushes against reward-vs-goal talk, instrumental convergence, and probably some philosophy-of-mind story where desire is just a functional state. What i actually want from you is: is there a new distinction in here, or am I relabeling something? And does "modeled wanting" do any work that "having a preference over one's own policies represented in a world-model" doesn't? Also flag the part, if any, that's worth keeping even if the full theory dies. Steelman me, then hit me with the three strongest objections. I especially want to know whether any objection is fatal rather than just uncomfortable—for example, whether self-modeling plus a target variable gives you a model of yourself as wanting but not wanting itself, or whether the account is circular because "target variable" smuggles in the wanting.
