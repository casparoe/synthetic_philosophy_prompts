Consider the following argument about what I will call sphexishness: the property of behaving in a mechanically rigid, cue-bound way. I have not been able to decide whether the argument is valid, and if it is, which premise gives way.

(1) An action is free only if it is guided by the agent's appreciation of reasons, not merely triggered by a configuration of cues.
(2) Any reason-guided action can be exhaustively represented as the activation of a system of condition–action rules: if certain cues, internal states, and feedback loops are present, the action tends to follow. Such a representation need not mention any additional, non-cue-like faculty.
(3) Hence, reason-guided behavior is not controlled in a different way from cue-guided behavior; it is simply cue-guided behavior implemented in a vastly larger and more interconnected rule set.
(4) The digger wasp Sphex ichneumoneus is a pure case of cue-guided behavior. Its prey-retrieval routine is a fixed sequence keyed to a specific triggering configuration, and it repeats the sequence whenever the configuration recurs, even when the repetition no longer accomplishes anything. We would not count such a routine as free.
(5) If the only difference between two systems is the number and interconnection of their cues and rules, then the two do not differ with respect to freedom; adding complexity to an unfree process cannot by itself make the process free.
(6) Therefore, human action is not free. What we call flexibility is just sphexishness with a larger lookup table.

The conclusion seems wrong to me, but I cannot locate the mistake, and I half-suspect there may be no mistake. Say exactly which step, if any, fails. If the argument is invalid, identify the first step at which it goes wrong and state what kind of mistake it makes. If the argument is valid but unsound, identify the false premise or premises and state what a corrected version of each would have to say.
