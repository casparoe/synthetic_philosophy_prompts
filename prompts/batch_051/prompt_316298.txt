Please explain the difference between Hobsbawm's idea of an invented tradition and Anderson's idea of an imagined community. I've got the rough gloss down — both are meant to show that nations are less old and less natural than they like to pretend — but I want the actual conceptual distinction rather than two adjacent summaries. Is it just a difference of scale (one about particular ceremonies and rituals, the other about whole nations), or are they claims about quite different things: one about elites deliberately fabricating continuity, the other about the epistemic situation of ordinary people who will never meet most of their co-nationals? To force the issue: could a nation be an imagined community whilst its central traditions are genuinly ancient and continuous? Could a tradition be invented in Hobsbawm's sense while the nation that performs it is not imagined in Anderson's sense? And if you were looking at the historical record, what would it take for the evidence to support one thesis but not the other — are there coherent worlds where one is true and the other false, and what does that tell us about what each thesis is actually doing?
