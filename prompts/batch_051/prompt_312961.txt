Two philosophers have spent a year arguing about whether forgiveness can be morally obligatory and whether it can be unconditional. One holds that forgiveness can sometimes be a duty and can be granted before, or in the absence of, the wrongdoer's repentance, apology, or repair; the other holds that forgiveness is never owed and is fitting only as a response to a wrongdoer who has taken responsibility. Draft the joint statement their adversarial collaboration would produce. Include: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign, including an account of what forgiveness is and is not — for example, how it differs from excusing, condoning, forgetting, and reconciling, and whether the victim's act and a third party's act are the same kind; (2) a table stating their residual disagreements in wording each side would accept for its own position, followed by (3) a list of cruxes — sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side — each paired with a concrete case or argument both agree bears on it and each side's prediction recorded in advance. If part of the dispute turns out to be merely verbal, say which part and why.
