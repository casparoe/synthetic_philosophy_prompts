I am drafting a novel in which several characters' devotions go wrong, and I want the moral psychology to remain consistent even where my own intuitions are not. I find that I trust certain spontaneous verdicts about idolatry and misplaced worship, but I cannot state the principle behind them.

Consider the following cases. A woman orders her life around her husband's judgments, takes his preferences as final, and treats her loyalty to him as the one commitment she would never renegotiate. I judge this a form of idolatry rather than intense love. A father says, without melodrama, that he would let everything else burn before his daughter, and acts accordingly. I do not find that idolatrous. A patriot treats his country's standing as the final standard against which other values are measured and will lie or break promises only when, as he sees it, the country requires it. This seems idolatrous. A painter treats a single unfinished painting as an unconditional demand and sacrifices friendships and honesty to it as though the work were an end in itself. That seems misplaced. A woman prizes her own moral purity so highly that she will allow serious harm rather than commit a small wrong herself. I read this as worship of her own cleanliness rather than of what morality demands, and so as misplaced.

My working rule has been that idolatry consists in treating something as an ultimate or unconditional value when its value is actually instrumental, derivative, or conditional. But the rule fits the patriot, the painter, and the moral purist better than it fits the devoted spouse and the father; in particular, it does not explain why those two cases strike me so differently, since both make a single human being ultimate.

What principle, if any, captures or repairs these verdicts? Please state it precisely, show how it fits each case, and apply it to at least one further case I have not listed: devotion to a teacher, to a dead beloved's memory, or to an art form rather than to a single work. Be explicit about which of my verdicts, if any, cannot be kept. Please answer in no more than 900 words.
