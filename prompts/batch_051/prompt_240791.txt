If Alice measures an electron and assigns it spin-up along z, while Bob assigns it a superposition because he only knows it came from a particular source, I don't want to say either of them is wrong — each has the state that's right given what they know. That makes the wavefunction sound like it's just a summary of information. Yet I also think that if the electron is spin-up and someone assigns spin-down, that person is simply wrong, full stop. So sometimes two different assignments can both be right, and sometimes only one is right, and the difference has something to do with measurement or interaction, but I can't say cleanly what the difference is.

I also think the wavefunction is real in some sense — interference patterns, tunneling, chemical bonds, they all seem to be guided by it — but I don't think it's a physical field spread out in ordinary 3D space, and I certainly don't think there are many worlds branching all the time. I'm fine with saying the wavefunction collapses, even instantly, when a measurement happens. I don't like positing hidden variables that already fixed the outcome before measurement, even though that's the obvious way to make the coin case and the electron case come out the same.

One more: suppose a friend of mine is in a sealed lab doing a measurement.
