For an essay for my English class, I'm trying to work out why sad art is enjoyable rather than off-putting, and I've collected my own honest reactions to keep me from settling on a theory too early. Here are the verdicts I'm trying to capture. When a novel ends tragically—two characters who have done nothing to deserve it are permanently separated, one of them dead—I finish it in tears, call it one of the best books I've read, and expect I'd reread it even knowing how it ends. When a documentary shows real people mourning after a real disaster, I don't feel any of that: I'm distressed, I look away, and I wouldn't rewatch it for pleasure. When a song describes my own recent breakup almost word for word, it is more painful than the novel, yet it is also the one I play on repeat; I feel exposed and, at the same time, understood. Being told the ending of a tragedy ahead of time doesn't spoil it for me—sometimes the dread makes it stronger—but being told the twist in a thriller or the punchline of a comedy ruins the whole thing. A puppet show in which visibly carved wooden figures stand in for a dying child still makes me cry, and I don't afterward think the tears were cheap. A horror film scares and disgusts me, and I can enjoy that too, but it feels like suspense and the relief of the jump scare ending, not the slow savoring I get from tragedy. A sad still image—a painting or photograph of an old man asleep on a bench—moves me once, but I don't feel any pull to stay with it, and I don't seek out copies the way I seek out sad stories and songs. And when something genuinely terrible happened in my own family last year, the grief had no savor at all; I would not call any part of it pleasing.

Is there a principle about why we enjoy and seek out sad art that delivers all, or nearly all, of these verdicts? Please state the candidate principle or principles precisely, tie each element to the cases that motivate it, and then tell me what it predicts about cases I haven't listed—say, a video game in which I myself make the choice that causes the tragedy, or a poem I am told was generated by a machine with no lived experience. Finally, be honest about which of my verdicts the principle cannot accommodate and would force me to give up.
