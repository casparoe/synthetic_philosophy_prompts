I’ve been trying to pin down a three-way distinction about what people actually mean when they say a prior “washes out” in Bayesian inference, and I’d like an honest assessment of it rather than encouragement. I distinguish self-correction, mutual convergence, and guaranteed convergence. Self-correction is the claim that a single learner whose prior assigned vanishingly little probability to the true hypothesis will nevertheless come to assign high posterior probability to it in the limit with probability one under the true distribution, provided the truth lies in the support of the prior. Mutual convergence is the claim that two Bayesians who share a likelihood but begin with different priors satisfying some overlap condition will see their posterior probabilities over a fixed collection of events draw arbitrarily close to one another almost surely as the data accumulate. Guaranteed convergence is stronger and less elegant: for a fixed tolerance and confidence level there is some finite sample size, specifiable before the data arrive, such that with at least that confidence and regardless of which prior each agent started from, the two posterior probabilities over the events in question lie within the tolerance. My working view is that these are genuinely distinct ideas, that ordinary talk about disagreeing experts eventually agreeing tacitly assumes the third, and that the standard asymptotic results deliver only the first two, or deliver the third only by smuggling in a rate condition. I also suspect that self-correction and mutual convergence come apart in both directions: a single agent can converge to the truth while two other agents merge toward a shared false consensus in a misspecified model, and two agents can merge with each other even when one of them would never have self-corrected from its own prior.

Tell me whether this distinction is real or collapses under pressure, whether it has already been drawn in the literature under established names, and which notion of washing out is doing the work in each case. I want the cleanest examples that separate the three notions, a case in which self-correction and mutual convergence both hold while guaranteed convergence fails, and a case in which my definition of guaranteed convergence would count something as convergence that no one should call that. If my claim about what ordinary expert talk presupposes is wrong, say so directly. Please write your assessment as continuous prose, the way you would write a letter or an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
