After a long argument with a friend who defends the precautionary principle, I've realized I don't know how to state the principle in terms a decision theorist would accept. My friend kept insisting the principle is neither a maximin rule nor ordinary cost-benefit analysis, but he could not tell me what the third thing is. I work with expected utility, subjective priors, ambiguity aversion, maximin and minimax-regret rules, asymmetric costs for type I and type II error, and value of information. Please take the strongest defensible version of the precautionary principle and translate it into exactly those terms. Distinguish carefully which claims come out as a maximin rule, which as a heavy-tailed prior over hidden harms, which as an asymmetric weighting of false positives and false negatives, and which as a claim about the option value of acting before an irreversible commitment is made. Where a piece of the principle only fits by distortion, or has no expression at all in my vocabulary — for example, if it rests on a notion of burden of proof, or on treating uncertainty itself as a reason rather than as a state of information — say so explicitly.

Then give me a residue report: are the untranslatable parts evidence that my framework is missing something normatively important, or that the principle is less precise than its defenders claim — perhaps just the maximin rule with extra rhetorical force? Please do the translation honestly; do not smuggle cost-benefit assumptions back in and call the result a successful translation. I am less interested in whether the principle is right than in locating exactly what my usual tools can and cannot say about it.
