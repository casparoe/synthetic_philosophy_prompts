i'm closing out a reading group on original sin and im trying to write something for the last session. Write a villanelle about inherited guilt — the thought that a fault can be passed down a line of descent and implicate someone before they've done a single thing. The two refrains should carry the two sides of the old Pelagian/Augustine fight: one refrain keeps insisting that guilt has to be something you choose or do, so you can't be on the hook for an act that happened before you existed; the other refrain keeps insisting that it's in you anyway, woven in before any choice. Make the form do the real work: each time a refrain comes around it should feel like the inherited thing reasserting itself in another generation, and the final couplet should bring the refrains together the way actual arguements about original sin do — holding the conviction of being implicated and the conviction that you never chose it at the same time, neither one absorbing the other. Don't name Pelagius or Augustine inside the poem itself; someone who knows the controversy should recognize the positions, and someone who doesn't should just read a good poem. Standard villanelle: nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, two refrains and the usual aba rhymes.
