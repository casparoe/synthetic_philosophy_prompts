For as long as I can remember arguing with people—online, at work, with relatives—I've been struck by how much of a conversation moves forward on the strength of what is quietly taken for granted. A coworker asks, "Why has this policy, which everyone can see is failing, lasted so long?" and the question bundles in the claim that the policy is failing, though nothing established it; it just rides along in the grammar. A friend says, "Now that you've come around on the proposal, what worries you about the details?" and if I don't object, we leave with a shared impression that I did come around. In response I've developed a habit: I refuse to answer the question as asked. I stop and challenge the embedded assumption first, even when that derails the exchange, because answering straight seems to let an unearned assumption be counted later as something we both accepted. I make exceptions when the assumption is trivial or when the stakes are low and the other person is plainly testing an idea rather than smuggling one in. But I've also watched people who do the opposite—go along with questionable assumptions to keep the conversation moving—and they sometimes build a shared picture that, though partly false, is useful enough to keep working with. I'm not sure my rule isn't just a way of never letting a conversation get anywhere without relitigating every plank.

What I want to know is whether this habit is rationally defensible, or whether it's a rhetorical shield dressed up as a principle. Under what conditions should a person refuse to accept what a claim or question presupposes, and under what conditions should they accommodate it at least temporarily, knowing that the shared background may get updated in their absence? Treat it as a policy: say where it is reliable, where it fails, what a better version of the rule would look like, and how I should decide in a particular case from inside my own head rather than after the fact. Please also say how confident you are in your answer and single out the point you are least sure of.
