ok here's a thing i keep going back and forth on. everyone talks like "word" obviously picks something out, but you go one layer into morphology and it turns into slush. i want a working defintion of "word" that gets the following cases right. i've flagged the ones i think i know and the ones i dont. part of what i'm circling is the inflection/derivation divide, since it's supposed to tell us why cats isn't a new word but blackbird is, but i've never seen anyone make it stick.

- cat — clearly a word. anchor case.
- the plural -s in cats — clearly not a word by itself. another anchor.
- cats vs cat — are these one word or two? i used to think two, but then i started worrying about ran vs run.
- run, ran, running — is that three words or three forms of one word? i lean three forms of one, but a normal english speaker would also say "run" and "ran" are different words.
- unhappy — clearly one word to me, and the un- inside it clearly is not a word on its own. so the definition has to explain why un- is not a word but unhappy is.
- blackbird vs black bird — first one word, second two. the definition has to see the difference somewhere other than spelling.
- kick the bucket — three words, but one unit of meaning. is an idiom a word? no? but then a word can't just be a unit of meaning.
- don't and i'm — one word each or two? i genuinely have no idea.
- ice cream — one word or two? i say one word that happens to have a space in it, but that feels like cheating.
- will have been running — four words or one verb? i'd say four words, but i couldn't defend it against someone who says it's one predicate.
- evlerinizden, turkish for "from your (plural) houses" — turkish says one word, english needs four. so is "word" just a language-specific spelling convention?
- king of england's hat — the possessive 's attaches to a whole phrase, so what is it? a word? a suffix? neither?
- german aufstehen / ich stehe auf — one word that splits in half, or two words that are secretly one.

propose the definition, run it through all of those, and tell me which ones it gets wrong or leaves undecided. then either fix the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. please actually commit to one definition and argue for it instead of giving me a menu of options and making me choose.
