I'm a PhD student in health informatics and I somehow ended up on our teaching hospital's clinical AI governance committee, and I've been asked to draft the decision memo for tomorrow's vote. Heres the case. We have a readmission-risk model for nurses that flags patients likely to bounce back within 30 days. There are two versions ready to deploy. Version A scores 93% on our holdout set and, when it flags someone, it auto-generates a short explanation that looks great in a demo (two recent admissions, no follow-up appointment, a creatinine trend) but the engineering team has been honest that these explanations are generated after the fact and weren't actually used to compute the score. Version B was trained with a legibility penalty: it can only issue a high-risk alert if it can point to a small set of chart features whose values are actually present, and the training loss includes a term for producing these checkable reasons; when it cant produce them it defers to a nurse rather than guessing. Version B scores 89%. The split on the committee is basically this: one faction says launching the less accurate model is knowingly causing extra missed readmissions, and accuracy is itself a safety property; the other says Version A's explanations are confabulations dressed up as reasons and will teach staff to trust a system whose errors they can't audit. I need a one-page memo with a clear recommendation: adopt A, adopt B, or a staged/conditional rollout, and what specific conditions if staged. It should lead with the bottom line, give the reasoning, and actually steelman the side I'm not adopting. Please cap it at 600 words, no citations or lit review—the committee just wants the argument.
