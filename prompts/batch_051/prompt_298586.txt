I've been trying to pin down what should count as a naturalistic explanation of religion, rather than just a story about religion that happens not to mention anything supernatural. The way I've been thinking about it, such an explanation has to do roughly four things: it should appeal only to ordinary causal mechanisms we already need elsewhere (so no sui generis religious sense organ), it should explain why religious beliefs and practices are widespread and persistent rather than occasional or confined to one culture, it should stay neutral about whether the beliefs it explains are true or false, and it should say something about the content of religion, not just the bare fact that people believe in something. My friend keeps telling me the byproduct account from cognitive science of religion passes all four. The account says our agency detection is hypersensitive: we're built to see agents in ambiguous stimuli because the cost of missing a predator is much higher than the cost of a false positive, and this bias makes us prone too posit unseen agents. Then minimally counterintuitive concepts do the rest: ideas that violate one or two of our automatic expectations about objects, animals, or persons (a person who is invisible, a tree that listens) grab attention and stick in memory better, so they spread more easily than fully intuitive or fully bizarre ideas. On this view religion is mostly an accumulated side effect of ordinary cognition, not an adaptation or a special domain of thought. What bothers me is that, while this seems to explain why god and spirit concepts are easy to form and remember, it says very little about ritual, costly sacrifice, moral codes, or why some traditions have no personal gods at all. So does the byproduct account actually qualify as a naturalistic explanation of religion under the criteria I listed? Go through them one at a time, and where it fails, tell me whether the problem is in the criteria or in the account. Also, is it fair to let an explanation off the hook for big parts of the phenomenon by saying those can be bolted on later with other mechanisms, or does an explanation of religion need to cover the whole thing from the start?
