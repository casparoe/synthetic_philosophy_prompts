I need a second pair of eyes on a position I'm assembling for a paper on love and reasons. The debate is mostly framed as appraisal versus bestowal, and I seem to want parts of both, along with a relational view. Before I commit, tell me whether these five commitmments can all be true together:

(a) Love is answerable to reasons — saying someone's love is unwarranted or mistaken isn't just a judgment about whether it's good for them.
(b) The reasons can't be the beloved's traits. A qualitatively identical person wouldn't inherit my reason to love, so whatever grounds it is something particular.
(c) Following Kolodny, I think the ground is relational: my reason to love this person is the relationship between us (partner, friend, sibling), not properties either of us has independently.
(d) But not just any relationship qualifies — an abusive one, or a merely biological tie, seems to me to ground little or nothing.
(e) And then there's a Frankfurtian pull: maybe love is a brute volitional necessity that creates reasons for me instead of answering to reasons that were already there.

Rank the genuine tensions by severity, distinguish them from the ones that only look like tensions, and tell me the cheapest way to restore consistency and what it would cost.
