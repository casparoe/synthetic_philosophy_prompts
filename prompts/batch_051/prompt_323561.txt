I am drafting an argument that grief can be intrinsically valuable, not merely instrumentally useful, and I need a thought experiment built to order. Construct a one-paragraph case comparing two possible lives of the same person after the same grave loss: the death of a deeply loved person. In the first life the person grieves in a way that is fully informed, deep, and sustained, but not disabling. In the second life the absence of grief is produced by a realistic post-loss intervention, such as a drug that blunts the affective response to the loss, rather than by ignorance, denial, or any difference in what the person believes. The case must be engineered so that hedonism and a simple desire-satisfaction theory of well-being both rank the non-grieving life as the better of the two, while a fitting-response view ranks the grieving life as the better of the two. By the fitting-response view I mean the following: a life goes better not only through pleasure or the satisfaction of desires, but also through its emotional responses being fitting to their objects—joy at the genuinely good, grief at a genuine loss. On this view, fitting grief is itself a constituent of a life lived in proper contact with what matters, and when the loss is of something of great value, the fittingness of deep grief can outweigh the felt pain of that grief; a post-loss emotional blank, however comfortable, is a defect in the life rather than merely the absence of a symptom. To make the desire-satisfaction verdict straightforward, build the case so that the person has no antecedent or second-order desire to grieve and no desire that grieving would satisfy; the only desires at stake are a desire for comfort and a desire for the loved person's welfare, with the second equally thwarted in both lives. Constraints: no supernatural assumptions and no science-fictional technology; the absence of grief must produce no further harms such as side effects, guilt, dulled positive emotion, or later regret; and the grief must produce no further goods such as deepened relationships, achievement, or acquired resilience. After presenting the scenario, walk through the reasoning that hedonism, the desire-satisfaction theory, and the fitting-response view each give about the two lives and why. Then identify which feature of the case is doing the work to make the fitting-response view peel off from the other two, say what happens if that feature is removed, and give the strongest hedonist or desire-satisfaction objection and how the fitting-response theorist should answer it.
