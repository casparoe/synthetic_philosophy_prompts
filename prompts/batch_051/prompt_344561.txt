I have been trying to work out whether choosing between causes is really just triage with an extra column added, or whether the resemblance is only a loose analogy. In triage, two questions appear to do nearly all the work: how bad will the outcome be if nothing is done, and how much can intervention change it? Cause prioritisation puts the same two questions first — importance and tractability — and then adds a third, neglectedness: how many people are already working on the problem. My first thought was that triage has no counterpart to neglectedness, but I no longer trust that. A medic who comes upon two patients who are equally urgent and equally treatable would go first to the one nobody else is treating. So I want to know whether that move — go to the patient nobody else is treating — is the same consideration as neglectedness, or only superficially similar. More precisely: is neglectedness a genuine third factor, or just a rough rule of thumb for spotting where importance or tractability has been misjudged? And if it is only a rule of thumb, where does the rule break down? Please answer in British English.
