I am trying to work out the principle behind my own judgments about when it is permissible to override a market-clearing price, and I would like you either to extract that principle or tell me that none is there. My verdicts are as follows. A minimum wage set modestly above the market rate in a tight urban labor market: justified. A minimum wage set at roughly twice the median wage in a region with high unemployment: not justified. Rent control that caps increases on all existing units in a city with very inelastic housing supply: not justified, even though it protects sitting tenants. Rent control that applies only to newly built luxury units and sunsets after ten years: I find this hard to object to, though I cannot say why it should differ from the previous case. A law banning price increases above pre-storm levels for bottled water and generators during a hurricane: justified. A wartime price freeze on staple foods that produces shortages and black markets: not justified. Is there one coherent principle about what makes overriding a market price wrong, and about the conditions under which it is nevertheless permissible, that delivers all or most of these verdicts? State it precisely, explain how each element is motivated by a particular case, tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to revise, and apply it to one further case of your own invention.
