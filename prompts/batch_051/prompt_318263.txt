I am a high school senior and a competitive archer. My coach keeps telling me to "stop trying so hard" before scoring ends, but when I try to stop trying, I only watch myself more closely and shoot worse. I have been reading about the Daoist idea of wu-wei—roughly, acting skillfully without forcing or self-interference—and about the paradox that deliberately trying to be effortless tends to be self-defeating. I want a reusable pre-shot routine, not a one-time tip, that operationalizes wu-wei for the two minutes before a scoring end and the twenty seconds before an individual shot. The routine has to work in competition, when no coach can speak to me and my heart rate is up, and it must not turn into one more command ("now perform effortlessness") added to the things I am anxious about. Lay out the checklist or decision procedure, run it through three realistic cases from my season, including at least one where the procedure tells me to do something I will not like, and mark the points where judgment cannot be eliminated.
