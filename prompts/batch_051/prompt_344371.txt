Suppose a consortium of synthetic biology labs wants to blunt the unilateralist's curse as it applies to the publication of risky methods. They adopt the following mechanism: any member producing a result above a jointly agreed risk threshold must deposit it in a private, time-stamped escrow and is barred from publishing publicly for ninety days. During the embargo, any other member may pay a fee to extend the embargo for another ninety days; each successive extension costs more on a fixed escalating schedule, and fees are split equally between the originating lab (as compensation for delayed credit) and a fund for third-party safety audits. After a maximum two-year embargo, publication is automatic. Analyze this as a mechanism rather than as a moral position. What does it incentivize among labs with different discount rates, risk estimates, and competitive standings? How would labs game the threshold, the fee ladder, or the compensation? What happens to the informational value of what gets deposited, flagged, or repeatedly extended? Then tell me which failure mode is most damaging, whether the scheme can be patched without gutting it, and whether you would prefer it to the baseline of unilateral publication.
