Lewis Carroll's tortoise accepts p and accepts p → q, but declines to accept q. When told that q follows by modus ponens, the tortoise is happy to add that rule itself as a further premise — (p ∧ (p → q)) → q — and then still declines to draw the conclusion, requiring yet another premise, and so on without end. Doesn't this show that modus ponens can only be justified by adding the rule as a new premise, so that any deductive argument needs an infinite string of premises before its conclusion becomes binding? If so, what remains of the idea that logic is normative for reasoning?
