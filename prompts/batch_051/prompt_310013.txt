You are a philosopher and research strategist working on the conceptual foundations of AI alignment. A foundation is deciding how to allocate roughly thirty researcher-years over the next three years between two approaches to making frontier AI systems safer.

The first approach pursues worst-case guarantees: results that hold for every input, environment, or model in some class, so that certain failures are ruled out altogether — for example, proving that a system cannot produce a specified dangerous output no matter how it is prompted or perturbed. The second pursues average-case guarantees: results that hold for typical, in-distribution, or high-probability cases, so that failures are rare rather than impossible — for example, red-teaming and benchmark evaluations showing low failure rates on realistic inputs.

The foundation suspects that the deepest disagreements between advocates of these approaches are conceptual rather than merely technical: disagreements about what a safety guarantee is supposed to buy, how to model an adversary or a distribution shift, whether being safe is a property of a system or of a system plus its deployment context, and when a worst-case result is so conservative that it fails to constrain any real system. Before committing funds, they want a defended agenda from you.

First think through a wide range of candidate conceptual questions at the interface of worst-case and average-case reasoning, and the criteria you would use to rank them: upstreamness, tractability for philosophers and theorists rather than ML engineers, neglectedness, and how much the allocation decision actually turns on the answer. Then select the five questions whose answers would most change how the foundation should divide its resources between worst-case and average-case work, and put them in priority order.

Format your reply as a numbered list of exactly five items, with no introduction or conclusion. Each item should be one or two sentences and should name the question and give the core reason for its place in the ranking, including what kind of answer would shift resources toward which approach.
