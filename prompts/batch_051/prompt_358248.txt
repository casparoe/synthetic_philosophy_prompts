I've been trying to figure out why the Molyneux debate never seems to actually resolve. Molyneux asked Locke whether somebody born blind, after recovering sight, could tell a cube from a globe by sight alone before touching them, and Locke reports the no answer. But then later in the same chapter he writes something I think holds the reason the dispute keeps stalling. This is Book II, chapter ix, section 8 of the Essay Concerning Human Understanding:

“When we set before our eyes a round globe of any uniform colour, v.g. gold, alabaster, or jet, it is certain that the idea thereby imprinted on our mind is of a flat circle, variously shadowed, with several degrees of light and brightness coming to our eyes. But we having, by use, been accustomed to perceive what kind of appearance convex bodies are wont to make in us ; what alterations are made in the reflections of light by the difference of the sensible figures of bodies ; – the judgment presently, by an habitual custom, alters the appearances into their causes. So that from that which is truly variety of shadow or colour, collecting the figure, it makes it pass for a mark of figure, and frames to itself the perception”

My hunch is that this passage shows the debate is less empirical than it looks: one side takes Molyneux's question to be about the raw idea before the judgment has altered it, while the other takes it to be about what a grown, experienced person would see, and Locke's whole point here is that grown perception already includes habitual judgment. That would make the two sides answer different questions, not disagree about the answer. But I'm not sure, because there are also clearly empirical versions of the question, like actual cataract patients and the role of visual experience. I keep goimg back and forth on whether my diagnosis is too quick. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this really is: verbal, empirical, conceptual, or some mix? And given that diagnosis, what would actually count as progress in the debate? I'm not interested in just a verdict on Molyneux's problem; I want to understand the structure of the impasse itself.
