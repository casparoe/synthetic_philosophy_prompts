Next month I'm giving a workshop paper I've been fighting with for a while, and I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a defense. My thesis is that the paradox of deontology can't be dissolved the way most deontologists now try to dissolve it. The setup: a constraint says don't kill one person even to stop five other killings, but the reason the constraint matters is that killings are bad, so the view seems to forbid the very act that would minimize the thing it calls bad. I argue the standard repairs — going agent-relative, denying that the relevant badness aggregates, saying the constraint protects a status rather than a value to be promoted — all make the same quiet retreat: they stop being about what the act does to the victim and become about the agent's relation to her own act. And once you make that move you've surrendered the thing deontologists actually wanted, namely that the act is wrong full stop in virtue of what it does to the person. So I defend the unpopular line that the tension between the constraint and the underlying value is real, and the honest deontologist lives inside it instead of pretending to dissolve it.

The audience is a mixed normative ethics workshop: about half are consequentialists who will treat 'so your view prefers outcomes with more killings' as decisive, there are two Kantian constructivists who will say I'm turning the categorical imperative into a value to be promoted and misreading Kant, and one Scanlonian who will claim the paradox dissapears once wrongness is about justifiability to others rather than producing outcomes. Give me the objections in the order I'll actually get them, phrased the way these people would put them, with how much damage each can do, a two-minute reply for each, what I should concede and what I should hold, where each reply is softest, and the one question I'm hoping nobdy asks. Also flag the weakest point in my own case that I should have a defense ready for rather than hope they miss.
