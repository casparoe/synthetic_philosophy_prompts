I'm a features writer for a science-and-ideas magazine, doing a long piece on laws of nature — specifically the stand-off between Humeans and anti-Humeans about whether the laws are nothing over and above the total mosaic of particular facts, or whether there's a real nomic necessity (a necessitation relation between universals, say) that makes a regularity a law and lets it govern its instances. I've spent a year listening to two philosophers, one on each side, talk past eachother, and I've finally persuaded them to do an adversarial collaboration: instead of another round of papers and replies, they'll publish a single joint statement they can both sign. It should say what they agree on, state where they part ways in words each accepts for their own side, identify the cruxes, and record in advance what each side predicts about the evidence that could bear on those cruxes.

Draft that joint statement for them. I suspect some of the real cruxes are the one-particle universe (does it have any laws at all?) and the familiar contrast between the regularity that all gold spheres are under a mile across and the regularity that all uranium-235 spheres are under a mile across, but you should include whatever cruxes you think do the actual load-bearing work, and you should flag any disagreement that is mostly about words. Return it as a JSON object with these fields: "agreed_claims" (array of strings; each a non-vacuous claim both camps can sign), "disagreements" (array of objects with keys "issue", "humean_position", "anti_humean_position", and "wording_both_accept", where the issue and wording are neutral enough that each side would sign them), "cruxes" (array of objects with keys "crux", "why_it_matters", "agreed_test_or_case", "humean_prediction", and "anti_humean_prediction", where the test or case is one both sides agree bears on the crux and the predictions are what each side commits to in advance), and "mostly_verbal_disputes" (array of strings, each noting a dispute that dissolves under paraphrase and what each side was really saying). Please write the whole thing in British English.
