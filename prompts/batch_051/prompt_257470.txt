The standard argument that priors wash out with enough data runs roughly as follows. Take two Bayesian agents with different priors over a fixed class of hypotheses or models. Suppose each prior assigns positive probability to the true model, and both agents update by exact conditionalization on the same sequence of observations. Under suitable regularity conditions, their posterior distributions merge: after enough shared evidence, their initial disagreement becomes irrelevant.

Now consider a more realistic setting. The true data-generating process is not inside the hypothesis class over which the agents have priors: the model is misspecified. Moreover, neither agent updates exactly; each one maintains an approximate posterior by optimizing a tractable objective, as in variational inference. Does the washing-out argument survive this development?
