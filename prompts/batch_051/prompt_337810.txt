I've been going around in circles on whether "meme" is a real thing or just a slick metaphor, and I want to stop reading paper-by-paper and instead get the full spec first. What would a theory of cultural variants have to get right for it to be true that memes replicate and undergo selection, rather than just being talked about as if they do? I keep seeing people pick one example — chain letters spread, so memes are real; recipes mutate every time they're passed on, so they're not — and I want the requirements list instead.

Write the desiderata. Include at least these, plus anything I'm missing:

- What is the unit, exactly — the performed behavior, the underlying instruction or recipe, the neural representation? And what makes two performances or two people's ideas instances of the *same* meme?
- How do you seperate copying from merely causing someone to produce a similar result on their own? If a kid watches someone use a stick to fish termites and then comes up with the same trick independently, that's not replication — what has to be going on for us to say the variant was inherited rather than re-invented? And does reconstruction from partial cues (hearing a song once and "recovering" the rest from what makes sense musically) count as copying or not?
- Cultural transmission is noisy and systematically distorted: people regularize stories toward stock plots, misremember lists toward what they already believe, and so on. Does the claim that memes replicate require a fidelity that transmission actually has? Or can a low-fidelity, mostly-reconstructive process still give cumulative adaptation?
- In culture, a lot of variation is directed — someone deliberately improves a recipe before passing it on. Does that break the selection picture, or just add another ingredient? And can "fitness" be defined non-circularly (not just "whatever spreads is fit")?
- Does blending kill it? If two recipes get combined, the variants merge; if inheritance is blending, variation tends to get washed out. Does meme theory have to make variants particulate (gene-like) to get selection, and can it?
- Is the thing that gets copied the recipe or the product? If you learn to bake by watching and tasting the cake, are you copying the cake or the idea behind it, and does the theory have to choose?
- Last one, probably the acid test: what would we actually observe in a transmission-chain experiment if memes were literal replicators, that we wouldn't observe if the whole thing is just a metaphor? In other words, where does the theory risk being wrong?

For each, say why it's a genuine requirement and not just one camp's hobbyhorse, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are parochial. Then flag any subset you think can't be jointly satisfied — if the requirements themselves conflict, that's the part I most want to know about. Finally, name the single requirement that kills off the most popular versions of the view.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble, no restating the question. A table is fine if it helps.
