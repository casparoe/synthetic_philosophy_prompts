i read einstein's essay on socialism a while back and it got under my skin more than i expected. the way i read it, he's not really making an efficiency argument, he's making a claim about what capitalism does to people: it trains them to treat their own labor and everyone else's as something to be sold, and the point of a planned economy isnt output but the free development of individuals. I've caught myself defending this to friends and getting waved off as naive, so I want to know what would actually count against it. If his core claim about capitalism's effect on character and the alternative were false, what would the world have to look like? Which evidence—about actually existing planned economies, about what markets do to motivation, about whether democratic control over a bureaucracy can survive—would a defender of the essay have to treat as fatal rather than merely awkward? Does the character-based version make any predictions the efficiency-based version doesn't, and would failing to find those count against him? I want the threats ranked, and I want to know if the argument has made itself unfalsifiable in a way that should bother me.
