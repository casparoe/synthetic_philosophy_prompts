I have been working through Searle's Chinese Room alongside Dennett's rejoinder that Searle's line, applied consistently, would also strip a humble two-bitser—the vending machine that accepts US quarters and rejects slugs—of any claim to recognise anything at all. I have tried to turn Dennett's rejoinder into a clean numbered argument, but I worry that I have either made it too easy or hidden a premise that Searle would refuse.

Here is my reconstruction. (1) Whatever feature would disqualify the two-bitser's coin-discriminating state from being a genuine recognition—being realised in a physical mechanism, being exhaustively describable without mental vocabulary, or lacking any inner 'aboutness' beyond its causal role—is equally a feature of a human being's recognition of a quarter. (2) If the disqualifying features are present in both cases, then either both the machine and the person genuinely recognise quarters, or neither does. (3) The person genuinely recognises a quarter; that is exactly the kind of original intentionality Searle wants to preserve for minds and deny to machines. (4) Therefore the two-bitser genuinely recognises quarters. (5) But the two-bitser is a paradigm case of merely derived intentionality, since its state has content only because we built it to accept quarters. So the original/derived distinction cannot be what separates genuine recognition from mere mechanism, and Searle's contrast collapses.

Go through these steps in order. Is the argument valid as stated? If it is valid but not sound, say which step should be rejected and why; if you think it is sound after all, say briefly what accepting it commits one to.
