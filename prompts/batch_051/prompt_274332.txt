I maintain the reputation system for a technical discussion site, and I am writing a design document that will lock in several invariants for the next version. I have been defending a list of commitments in design meetings, but I have never checked them against each other as a set, and I would like an audit before the document goes out. My current commitments are:

(a) Informational value: a karma score's primary job is to be a usable predictor of the expected quality of a user's future contributions, not merely a record of past activity.
(b) Cheap negative feedback: downvotes should be close to free and available to every account, because making them costly lets low-quality content survive.
(c) Open pseudonymity: account creation is unlimited and pseudonymous; we will not verify identities or bind accounts to real persons.
(d) Egalitarian verdicts: the community's aggregate judgment should operate on one member, one vote; no user should be able to buy a larger share of the verdict with money or accumulated karma.
(e) Merit-weighted input: when the system weighs which content to surface, judgments from accounts with strong contribution histories should count more heavily than judgments from new or low-reputation accounts.
(f) Spendable karma: accumulated karma should be an economic resource that users can spend, for instance to boost the visibility of a post, so that reputation is not a read-only badge.
(g) Collusion resistance: the system should be robust against small coordinated groups such as brigades or sockpuppet rings, without recurring manual intervention.

I suspect some of these are in genuine tension, while others may only look incompatible until some distinction is drawn. I would like the tensions ordered by severity rather than treated as equally serious: which commitments actually pull against one another, which apparent conflicts dissolve on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision, or set of revisions, that would make the whole list coherent?
