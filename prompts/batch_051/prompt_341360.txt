I'm a novelist, and building the politics for a near-future setting has made me lose faith in the left/right distinction entirely. My working definition: left is about equality and universalism — flatten hierarchies, redistribute, include outsiders; right is about hierarchy and particularism — preserve order, tradition, and in-groups. Now my problem case. I've invented a movement, call it the Commonwealth, that wants to abolish private property, hand the economy to elected workers' councils, open the borders to everyone, and legalize every drug. But they also want to ban divorce, keep women out of public life as a matter of divine law, give the clergy a veto over every council decision, and recriminalize homosexuality. On my criteria that's left on economics and right on culture, which makes the movement feel borderline to the point of contradiction. Is the Commonwealth left, right, neither, or is the one-axis picture just wrong? And if 'left' and 'right' still name something real, what is the one thing that actually holds each side's positions together — and does it hold my movement together? I want a committed verdict; dont hand me a tour of the options and leave me to pick.
