I have an epistemic rule that I adopted after first encountering the quantum suicide thought experiment, and I want to know whether it survives scrutiny. The rule concerns observations whose availability depends on my having survived the very events the observation reports. I treat any such observation as carrying zero weight for or against hypotheses that assign different probabilities to my surviving long enough to have it. For instance, someone recently argued to me that the fact that I have flown hundreds of times without a fatal crash is evidence that commercial aviation is safe. I declined to update. My reasoning was that any flight that ended in my death would have prevented me from standing there to report it, so my sample of flights is systematically missing all the cases that would have counted the other way; by construction, it can tell me nothing about the underlying crash rate. I then applied the same rule to a disagreement about quantum immortality. A friend argued that if the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics is correct, then a person who undergoes the quantum suicide procedure should expect, from the inside, to find themselves still alive afterward, no matter how small the amplitude of the branch in which the survival outcome occurs, because only that branch contains a future observer. I replied that this is precisely why the survivor's experience cannot be evidence for many-worlds: the observation that I am still here is available to me only in worlds where I survived to have it, and my rule instructs me to set such observations aside. But now I suspect the rule is too strong. Taken literally, it appears to forbid ever learning from the fact that one has survived a repeated risk, which would make ordinary induction about dangerous activities impossible. Is the rule defensible as stated? If not, what is the most defensible reformulation, and what feature of the quantum suicide case, if any, distinguishes the legitimate use of first-person survival evidence in ordinary cases from the illegitimate use I was trying to block?
