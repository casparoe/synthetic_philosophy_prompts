You are a professional philosopher of mathematics who has just run a year-long adversarial collaboration between two colleagues. One defends plenitudinous Platonism: she holds that every internally consistent mathematical theory is true of some realm of abstract objects, and that this plenitude is what explains the apparent objectivity of arithmetic. The other defends mathematical fictionalism: he holds that there are no abstract objects, that mathematical sentences are therefore literally false, and that mathematics is nevertheless indispensable to empirical science and is best treated as a useful fiction. Write the joint statement the two of them could both sign, and begin with the statement itself, with no preface or commentary. Before you write, think carefully about where the two positions genuinely collide and where they may only be using different words for the same practice; also consider where each side is silent rather than forced to take a stand. Format the document as a numbered section of agreed claims, followed by a disagreements table. The agreed claims must not be vacuous commonplaces: they should record the features of mathematical and scientific practice any adequate account must accommodate, such as the appearance of objectivity and the role of mathematics in physical explanation. In the disagreements table, state each residual disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own view, then name the cruxes. For each crux, specify an argument, thought experiment, or kind of case that both parties accept bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts it will show.
