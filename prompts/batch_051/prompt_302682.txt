Over the past term I have been convening a small working group on the political philosophy of church–state relations. The group has been comparing four settlements: England's established church with its formal role in the legislature, Germany's system of state-collected church taxes, the French tradition of laïcité with its strict separation of religion from the public sphere, and the United States' disestablishment combined with a strongly religious civil society. Two members of the group have reached a stable impasse. One defends a cooperative model: the state may properly recognize religious communities, collect church taxes, and otherwise support religious institutions, because religious exercise can be a public good and state neutrality need not mean state silence. The other defends a separationist model: the state's obligation to treat all citizens as equal participants requires it to avoid establishing, privileging, or administering any religious body, since such entanglement inevitably marks some citizens as outsiders. They have agreed to write an adversarial collaboration rather than continue the seminar debate.

Draft the joint statement these two could both sign. Please include: (a) a numbered list of agreed claims, including claims about what each of the four settlements gets right or wrong; (b) a disagreements table giving exact formulations of each residual disagreement, with each side's view phrased so that side would accept the wording; and (c) an explicit account of the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that a settled answer to at least one would move one or both of them. For each crux, specify what kind of institutional evidence, historical case, or hypothetical test either side would accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each side predicts that evidence would show. The statement should also separate genuine normative disagreement from merely verbal disagreement over terms such as neutrality, establishment, impartiality, and recognition.
