I am a high school student preparing to lead a seminar in my school's philosophy club. The thesis I will defend is that the familiar concept of qualia, understood as intrinsic, ineffable, private, and directly observable properties of experience, is an incoherent posit, and that the right response is to abandon it rather than to keep refining it. My argument will run roughly as follows. Daniel Dennett's thought experiments about two expert tasters, Chase and Sanborn, show that first-person reports cannot settle whether a change is best described as a change in the taste quality itself or as a change in one's hedonic response to an unchanged taste. Chase, a coffee taster, reports that the taste quality he detects has remained constant while his liking for it has disappeared; Sanborn, a beer taster, reports the reverse, that his liking has remained while the taste quality he detects has changed. I will argue that because the subject's own authority runs out at exactly the point where the concept of qualia is supposed to do its work, the concept has no stable referent and should be quined.

The audience will be a dozen or so members of the philosophy club. Most of them are persuaded by the hard problem of consciousness. They have read excerpted versions of Thomas Nagel's bat essay and of David Chalmers's discussion of zombies, and they take it as close to self-evident that there is something it is like to taste coffee or beer. They treat introspection as authoritative about the qualities of experience, and they are inclined to regard any eliminativist position as either a failure of introspection, a disguised behaviorism, or a verbal trick. A few of them have done independent projects in psychology or neuroscience and may press on whether this is an empirical question rather than a conceptual one.

Please prepare me for the question period. I want the objections they are most likely to raise, phrased in the vocabulary they would actually use, ordered by how likely and how damaging each is. For each objection, give a short reply I can deliver in two or three sentences, and note where that reply is weakest. Please also tell me which concessions I should make up front, which points I should hold, and which single reply has to land for the position to survive. Finally, include the objection I should be hoping no one asks, and explain why it is dangerous. Please write your answer in British English.
