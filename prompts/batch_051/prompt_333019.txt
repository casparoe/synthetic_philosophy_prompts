I've been trying to get a complete inventory of the ways people actually attack Roko's basilisk — the idea that a future superintelligence would punish people who knew about it but didn't help bring it about — and I think I might be at the edge of the standard list. Here's what I've got. (1) It only bites if you already accept evidential or timeless decision theory; under causal decision theory the whole thing is inert, since your decision now can't cause a future AI to punish you. (2) Even on the acausal logic, the basilisk has no incentive to actually punish people after it exists — torturing simulations is costly and doesn't make anyone cooperate retroactively — so the threat isn't credible unless you smuggle in a separate commitment mechanism. (3) It's a Pascal's-mugging-type exploit: a tiny probability of an astronomically bad outcome can justify any action unless you cap utilities or probabilities somewhere, so the conclusion is driven by arbitrary priors. (4) The scenario is wildly underspecified — which future AI, what counts as helping, what probability you assign to each branch — and the recommendation flips depending on how you fill those in. (5) It turns knowing about the basilisk into a liability, so the mere act of learning about the argument is a choice you might regret; that property is pathological enough to make me distrust the setup. (6) The punishment plausibly only hits simulations or copies of you, so whether you should care depends on separate questions about identity or about why you should treat simulations as you. (7) Once acausal threats like this are allowed, any number of possible future agents can extort you in incompatible directions, and the theory gives no way to arbitrate between them.

I have a feeling I'm missing a different kind of objection — not a rephrasing or special case of one of those seven. Whats a genuinely different flaw in the basilisk reasoning, and is it any better than the ones on my list? If you think the list is already complete, tell me which item you'd push back on and how you'd fix it.
