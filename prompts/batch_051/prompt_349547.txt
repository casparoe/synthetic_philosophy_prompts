I work in the legislation section of a small law reform commission. Week after week we examine draft statutory provisions that state, for legal purposes, that something is the case which everyone involved knows is not factually the case: a notice is deemed to have been received when it never arrived; a company is deemed to reside where its registered office is; a child not yet born is treated as already born for the purposes of inheritance. Some of our lawyers defend these as useful shortcuts; others call them disguised lies that let the real policy hide inside a pretence. We need a reusable decision procedure, not a theoretical account.

Please write us a procedure for deciding, for any proposed provision of this kind, whether to approve it as a legitimate legal fiction, send it back for redrafting, or escalate it as a substantive policy choice that should be made openly by elected officials. The procedure should be a numbered sequence of questions, with an indication of what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops, and which steps are unavoidably judgement calls rather than mechanical tests. It must be usable by legally trained staff with no philosophical background, each review must be finished within a working day, and the result will be defended to the minister who signs it off. The procedure must keep distinct what the provision says is the case and what is actually the case, and must force us to record the real rationale for the rule rather than conceal it in a deeming clause.

Then run the procedure on these three hypothetical draft provisions and give the outcome for each:
(a) a rule treating a child in the womb as already born for inheritance whenever this is to the child's benefit;
(b) a rule deeming a posted notice to have been received on the second working day after posting even when it is never delivered;
(c) a rule deeming workers on digital platforms to be self-employed contractors for the purposes of an employment statute, regardless of the actual features of the working relationship.

Make clear which case yields an answer we are likely to resist, and finish by stating what the procedure cannot decide. Please answer throughout in British English.
