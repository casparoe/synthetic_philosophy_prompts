I am a co-moderator of an online debating forum, and I keep seeing the phrase "nothing matters" used to terminate debates, each time apparently making a different claim. Recently, a long exchange about whether a powerful AI system's decisions could be criticized as wrong was closed with: "Nothing matters, so the question is moot." We are drafting a moderation policy that distinguishes cases where the phrase is doing real argumentative work from cases where it is only expressing a mood, and I need the slogan disambiguated first. Please lay out the distinct precise theses that "nothing matters" could be used to assert — including, if it is a genuine option, any reading on which the claim concerns what exists rather than what has value or purpose — and for each reading say: whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false; what someone who accepted that reading would be committed to about how one should live; and whether that reading would actually support treating the AI-criticism question as moot. The deliverable should be a menu of readings with verdicts, not a single interpretation argued at length.
