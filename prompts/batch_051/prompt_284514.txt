I am the undergraduate student member of my university's Academic Integrity Review Panel, and I am currently taking an ethics course in which we have been studying deontological constraints and the paradox of deontology. A case before the panel has forced me to turn that material into a vote.

The facts are settled. A student, A, discovered that their roommate, B, had obtained a copy of the upcoming final examination for a large course and was organizing a group to study from it. A copied the file from B's laptop without B's permission and submitted it anonymously to the instructor, with a note explaining how it had been obtained. The instructor cancelled the examination; the cheating stopped, and the students who would have used the file never did. The investigation confirmed A's account, but it also established that A had accessed B's device without authorization. The code of conduct classifies unauthorized access to another student's files as a violation in its own right, and the panel is required to rule on it.

The panel is divided. Some members hold that the prohibition on unauthorized access is categorical: A accessed B's files without consent, and the good outcome cannot erase that. Others hold that the outcome is exactly what should matter: A prevented a much larger breach of academic integrity, and punishing A would punish the very act that upheld the integrity the rule is meant to protect. I recognize the structure of this disagreement from the course. The rule against unauthorized access is supposed to protect privacy and prevent self-help; if we exempt A, we seem to admit that the constraint can be set aside when enough good would come of it; if we sanction A, we seem to insist that the constraint binds even when following it would have allowed far more serious violations. This is the paradox of deontology in a disciplinary file.

I need to draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete disposition: find A responsible, find A not responsible, or find A responsible but impose no penalty, with the reasoning the panel could act on and criteria we could use in future cases. It should engage the strongest consideration on the losing side seriously rather than dismissing it as rule-worship or as cost-benefit analysis. And please include, explicitly, what would change its mind: which argument or consideration would be most likely to push it to the opposite disposition, or where its reasoning is most likely to fail.
