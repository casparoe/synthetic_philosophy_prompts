Heres the summary as I remember it:

Williamson argues that no interesting mental state is luminous, where "luminous" means that if you're in the state, you automatically know you're in it. The core argument is a sorites: imagine you feel cold at dawn and the room slowly warms up. And once you accept that, it generalizes: you can never really know you're in pain, or that you're anxious, even in pretty obvious cases. So self-knowledge is way more limited than we like to think. She also said the same argument just refutes the KK principle, because "if you know P then you know that you know P" is basically the claim that knowing is a luminous mental state.
