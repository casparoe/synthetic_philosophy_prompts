Someone I know ends every argument about whether a person's words actually did anything by saying that saying makes it so. Most recently: a company issued a press release 'recognizing' its employees' union and 'apologizing' for years of ignoring them, and she insists the statement itself settled things. I get that 'I promise' or 'I resign' arent just descriptions of a pre-existing fact. But those four words feel like they cover a lot of different territory, from a promise that creates an obligation to a compliment that just changes how someone feels. Can you break 'saying makes it so' apart into the different precise claims it might be making — about promising, apologizing, declaring, describing, causing effects, or taking on commitments — and for each say whether it's boringly true, flat false, or only true under special conditions like the right institution or authority? Then tell me which of those precise claims, if any, would actually make her right about the press release — and if my own question assumes something confused about what a press release would have to do to settle that kind of dispute, call that out instead of going along.
