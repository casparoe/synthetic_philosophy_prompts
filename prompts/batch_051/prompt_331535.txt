For a seminar on paradoxes of motion, I am compiling a list of substantive solutions to Zeno’s arrow paradox, not merely restatements of it. As Aristotle presents the argument in Physics VI.9, Zeno says that at any instant a flying arrow occupies a space equal to itself, and what occupies a space equal to itself is at rest; therefore the arrow is at rest at every instant of its flight, and since its whole time consists of these instants, it never moves. Aristotle’s own reply is that the arrow does not move in the instant, and that time is not composed of instants but of intervals.

I already have these five solutions on my list: (1) the interval-based solution, which says motion belongs to stretches of time rather than to durationless instants; (2) the at-at theory, on which motion is simply being at different places at different times, so there is no further state of moving that must obtain at an instant; (3) the limit or calculus-based solution, which treats velocity as a limit of ratios over ever-smaller intervals, not as a property of an instant; (4) Bergson’s solution, which locates motion in an undivided experienced duration rather than in a series of spatial positions; and (5) discrete spacetime solutions, which deny that the arrow’s trajectory is composed of infinitely many durationless points in the first place.

Give me one more solution to the arrow paradox that is genuinely different from these five — not a rewording, not a special case or combination of them, and not a historical variant whose logical structure is already captured by one of the five. The new solution should also be able to handle a tightened version of Zeno’s argument: even if time is not composed of instants, at every moment during the flight the arrow is at some exact place and is not, at that moment, passing from one place to another, so there seems to be no temporal part of the flight in which the arrow’s being in motion is located. State the new solution, show exactly how it differs from the five listed, and assess whether it is a good solution or only an instructive failure.
