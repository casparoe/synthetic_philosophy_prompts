I'm taking a course on empire and liberal political thought, and last week we covered Mill on India. I was complaining about the reading to a friend, and he sent me his summary of Mill's position, which he says he lifted from a review essay and has been using as the backbone of his paper. I'm suspicious of it, but I dont know enough to say exactly where it goes wrong. Here's what he sent me:

"Mill spent about thirty-five years working for the East India Company, ending up in a senior examiner position in London, so he had an obvious institutional stake in defending colonial rule. In On Liberty he says pretty directly that despotism is an appropriate way to govern barbarians, provided it aims at their improvement, and in Considerations on Representative Government he treats India as a society not yet fit for representative institutions. So his real view is that Indians are racially incapable of governing themselves and will need British rule indefinitely. And since he is a utilitarian, what matters is the total balance of happiness over suffering, so if ruling India makes Britain better off on net, the empire is justified: colonial peoples don't get a separate veto over arrangements that benefit the metropole. That's also why he opposed transferring India from the Company to the Crown in 1858—he thought the Company's indirect rule maximized the good."

Where do I start pulling this apart? I do know he opposed the abolition of the East India Company, which sounds bad, but the 'racially incapable' part feels like it might be his dad James Mill bleeding in, and the utilitarian move to 'if Britain benefits, it's justified' seems way to convenient to be an honest application of Mill's own principle. Can you go through the summary piece by piece and tell me which claims are actually accurate, where it slides into misrepresentation, and whether the overall takeaway—that Mill's liberalism was basically just an excuse for empire—follows from the accurate parts?
