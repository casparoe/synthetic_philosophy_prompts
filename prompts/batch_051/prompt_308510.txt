Please explain the doing/allowing distinction (also discussed as the killing/letting-die distinction) from the ground up, by actually applying candidate criteria to hard cases rather than by reporting what the literature says. I have supplied the criteria and the cases below so that the task is self-contained.

First, give a concise preliminary statement of what the distinction is supposed to be and why it matters, drawing on, for example, medical ethics, criminal law, famine ethics, or trolley-style cases. Then work through the candidate criteria C1–C5, plus any you add, against the test cases T1–T8. Finally, use the pattern of successes and failures to answer this question: is the doing/allowing distinction best understood as a unified moral fact, a loose cluster of related distinctions, or a disguised proxy for other factors such as causation, proximity, difficulty, or intention?

Candidate criteria:
- C1 (Bodily movement): An agent does harm only if her body moves in a way that causally contributes to the harm; if no bodily movement of hers contributes, she merely allows it.
- C2 (Energy transfer): Doing requires the agent's body to transmit energy to the victim or to some object that then brings about the harm; allowing is the absence of any such transfer.
- C3 (Withdrawal of aid): Harm counts as doing if the agent removes or interrupts a protective or life-sustaining resource that someone had supplied; it counts as allowing if the agent merely fails to supply the resource in the first place.
- C4 (Counterfactual baseline): Doing makes the victim worse off than they would have been had the agent been entirely absent from the scene; allowing leaves the victim no worse off than that baseline.
- C5 (Intention/mode of agency): The distinction tracks whether the agent brings the harm about as something she acts upon the world to cause, especially as an intended end or necessary means (doing), rather than merely foreseeing it as something she does not prevent while pursuing some other aim (allowing).

If you think some further criterion does better than any of these, add it, label it clearly (e.g., C6), and run it through the cases on equal terms.

Test cases:
- T1 (Switch): A trolley is heading toward five workers. A bystander throws a switch that diverts it onto a siding where one worker is killed.
- T2 (Push): A bystander pushes a heavy man off a footbridge in front of the trolley; his body stops it and he dies, while five are saved.
- T3 (Ventilator removal): A doctor disconnects a ventilator from a patient whose continued treatment is futile, and the patient dies.
- T4 (Withheld antibiotics): In a drug shortage, a doctor withholds antibiotics from a patient with a treatable infection, and the patient dies.
- T5 (Silent passerby): A passerby sees a blind man about to step into a hidden pit and deliberately says nothing, knowing he will be injured.
- T6 (Drowning child): A swimmer sees a child drowning and does nothing, although she could throw a nearby life ring without any danger to herself.
- T7 (Two daughters): One daughter removes a feeding tube from her comatose mother, who then dies. A different daughter, in an otherwise similar case, never authorizes inserting the tube in the first place; the mother dies with the same timing and in the same condition.
- T8 (Budget cut): A legislator votes for a budget cut that eliminates a food-aid program, foreseeing that some current beneficiaries will die as a result.
