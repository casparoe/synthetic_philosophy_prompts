I retired from a career in chemical physics a few years ago and have recently joined a reading group that is working through some older philosophy. We are currently deep in F. H. Bradley's *Appearance and Reality* (1893), and I have been asked to write down my commitments on the problem of relations for the group to examine. The passage that set this off is from Bradley's chapter "Relation and Quality." As I understand his argument there, he asks what it is for one thing, a, to stand in a relation R to another thing, b. If R is a third item alongside a and b, then we need to say what relates R to a; if that connecting item is itself a relation, the same question arises again at the next level, and if it is not a relation, then R never actually related a and b at all. He seems to conclude that either we have a bare heap of terms with no genuine unity, or we have an endless regress of connecting relations, and in neither case do we get a single relational fact.

That argument has unsettled me because it touches the picture of facts, truth, and thoughts I brought with me into philosophy. Here is that picture, stated as a set of claims:

1. Relations are real and irreducible: the repulsion between two electrons is not simply a monadic property of each electron taken separately; the relation itself is part of the world.
2. When a stands in R to b, there is a fact that is a complex of a, b, and R; the fact is one thing, not a mere list or set.
3. The unity of a fact is not a further ingredient alongside a, b, and R; the fact's being unified is the way those constituents are together, not an additional entity.
4. Thoughts and statements have the same kind of unity as facts. When I think that the electron is in the ground state of the hydrogen atom, I do not entertain a list of constituents; I grasp a single state of affairs, and my thought is true in virtue of that fact.
5. Ontology should be explanatory: complex wholes should be intelligible through their parts and the arrangement of those parts. But "the arrangement" cannot be a brute primitive if we still want the explanation.
6. Relational facts are non-negotiable for fundamental physics, so any metaphysics that eliminates relations entirely is unacceptable.

I would like an audit of this set rather than a defense of Bradley. Which of these claims are genuinely in tension with one another, and which tensions are only apparent? Order the tensions by severity, explain what the least costly revision is and what it would cost, and tell me whether Bradley's regress forces a change within this list rather than merely a change in how I describe the list. I would rather know now than have the reading group dismantle it sentence by sentence.
