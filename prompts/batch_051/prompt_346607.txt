We're doing Hart and Fuller in my jurisprudence class and I keep coming back to a modern version of the debate. Fuller says law has an internal morality: it has to be general, promulgated, non-retroactive, consistent, and not demand the impossible, and a system that misses these becomes less like law. Now suppose a chunk of law is actually implemented as executable code—an open-source benefits calculator—where contradictions just won't compile, retroactive changes show up in version history, and the rules are as public as the source. Does Fuller's argument survive this, or does it just turn his moral principles into engineering constraints? Does Hart do better because the authority to write and approve the code remains a social fact? I want the conceptual question, not the automation policy: which premise of Fuller's argument is under pressure here, and what would a repaired Fuller claim look like if the original doesn't survive?
