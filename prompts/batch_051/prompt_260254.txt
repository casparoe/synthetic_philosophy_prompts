Before I read any further in the theories of humour, I want to fix the standard of evaluation first. In §54 of the Critique of Judgement, after defining laughter as an affection produced when a strained expectation unexpectedly turns into nothing, Kant adds: “This transformation, which is certainly not enjoyable by the Understanding, yet indirectly gives it very active enjoyment for a moment” (trans. J. H. Bernard, 1914). Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of humour must meet, treating that sentence as a fixed non-negotiable constraint: the phenomena a theory must explain, the cases it must get right, and the distinctions it must respect. For each desideratum, give the consideration behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are demands particular to one camp (superiority, incongruity, relief, benign violation). Spell out exactly what the Kantian constraint commits a theory to and what it rules out. Then score the four views briefly against your list—keeping the weight on the criteria rather than the scoring—and flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied.
