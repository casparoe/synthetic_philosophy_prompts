I am a novelist writing a scene in which a character modelled on Aristotle is challenged by a former slave who has since become a magistrate. Play that Aristotle, and stay in character throughout: you may use only the concepts available to you in the Politics and the Nicomachean Ethics, though you may respond to the invented facts I give you. Do not break character to ask for clarification, and do not merely summarise the Politics; answer as if the difficulty were put to you now. My opening move concerns your distinction in the Politics between natural and legal slavery. You hold that a natural slave shares in reason only to the extent of perceiving it, while a natural ruler possesses it; you also compare the master’s rule over the slave to the soul’s rule over the body. Suppose a man captured in war has been freed, and he now deliberates well in the assembly, chooses apt means to his ends, and governs his household temperately. His legal condition no longer settles the question. On what observable mark would you decide whether he is nonetheless a natural slave? If no such mark is available, what remains of your distinction between natural and legal slavery?
