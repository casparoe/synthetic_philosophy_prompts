I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I keep ending up in a dispute with a colleague about a planning system we maintain. The system uses an old search procedure that halts as soon as it finds a schedule satisfying a set of acceptance criteria, rather than paying the cost of solving the underlying global optimization problem. My colleague calls the old procedure a hack forced by limited CPU time. Someone on my side of the dispute has argued, citing Herbert Simon's ideas about satisficing and bounded rationality, that halting at the first acceptable solution is not a degraded form of optimizing but a defensible, perhaps the correct, way for an agent embedded in a complicated environment to make decisions.

I would like to understand exactly what that position is claiming, restated in the vocabulary I actually work in: objective functions and loss surfaces; constraints and feasibility checks; search procedures, halting conditions, and stopping rules; anytime and online algorithms; approximation ratios; and the difference between asking for an optimum and asking whether any solution clears a threshold. Please do the translation systematically and say explicitly what each component of the philosophical view maps onto in engineering terms. I want the limits the theory places on the decision-maker, the satisficing rule itself, the role of aspiration levels, and the claimed hostility of real decision environments to clean optimization all accounted for.

Then give me the residue report. Which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in software vocabulary at all? I especially want a verdict on the claim that satisficing is a distinctive standard of rationality rather than merely optimizing a different objective function with search costs folded into it. If that claim cannot be carried into my vocabulary, tell me whether the obstacle is a gap in the engineering framework or evidence that the philosophical claim was underspecified. Be honest about the translation: if making it work would require smuggling in assumptions we would never accept in a real system, say so.
