i spent forty years doing physics and only now, retired, have i started reading philosophy of mind. the thing i keep circling back to is what "physical" is even supposed to mean in "everything is physical." as i understand it, that's Hempel's dilemma: define "physical" in terms of current physics and physicalism comes out false (or at least hostage to every future edition of the phsyics textbook); define it in terms of a completed, ideal physics and the view goes soft and empty — just "whatever the final science ends up saying." could you turn that into a sonnet for me? a Petrarchan one, or at least one with a real hinge at line 9. the octave should sit in the first horn: current physics, its gaps, the way it keeps getting revised, the mind showing up nowhere in the equations. then the volta should turn into the second horn: the appeal to a future completed physics, and the worry that the doctrine then costs nothing. i want the turn to carry the argument, not just be a shift in mood. and please don't resolve it in the closing lines; leave the dilemma hanging, which is honestly where it leaves me.

if i've garbled the dilemma, or a sonnet is the wrong shape for it, say so plainly instead of writing around my mistake. otherwise a short note after the poem on where it takes liberties with the idea or leans on a contested reading of it would be great.
