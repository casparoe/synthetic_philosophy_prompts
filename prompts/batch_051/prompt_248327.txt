Me and my friend got into it last night about whether rights are trumps, side constraints, or just instruments, and I ended up inventing a view on the spot that I now need someone to check. I called it "seat-dependent rights" and I'll try to state it cleanly. The idea is that a right isnt one kind of thing: it works differently depending on which seat you're in. From the seat of the person whose right is on the line, a right really does act as a trump — you get to say no, full stop, and you're not being irrational or selfish for refusing to weigh aggregate welfare against it. From the seat of a third party or a judge, the same right shows up as a side constraint: you may not violate it even to prevent two identical violations of it. But from the seat of someone actually running an institution (a hospital administrator, a cop, a legislator), rights are just instruments — inputs among others, used to predict backlash, build trust, buy off constituencies, or protect the vulnerable because it happens to be efficient. My friend said that last admission makes me an instrumentalist who's just being sentimental about the other two seats. I dont buy it, because the three seats don't reduce to each other: the claimant's "no" isn't only a useful fiction, it's the correct grammar for being the person whose right it is. So my homemade claim is that the trumps/side-constraints/instruments debate mostly misfires — each one is a script for a different role, and the mistake is trying to crown one script as the whole play. Is this coherent? Does it already exist under a name? What does it get right and where does it fall apart? Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro or conclusion: coherence, nearest existing views, what's right, where it breaks, and whether it's actually distinct from instrumentalism or just rebranded.
