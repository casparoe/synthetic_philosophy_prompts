I spent my career as a research scientist, and the habit I cannot shake in retirement is the demand that a disagreement be brought down to the exact point where the two sides would be moved by different evidence. I have recently been reading the philosophical literature on whether there is a moral difference between lying and merely misleading, and the two camps too often seem to me to be arguing past each other over the same cases. I would like the document such an exchange ought to produce: a joint statement after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, not a verdict on who is right.

The two positions are these. The difference view holds that, with expected consequences, intentions, and the false belief produced all held fixed, lying (asserting what one believes to be false with intent to induce a false belief) is morally worse than merely misleading (asserting something true, or omitting something, with the same intent). The parity view denies this: it holds that once the harm, the intent, the induced false belief, and the foreseeable consequences are held fixed, no additional moral difference of any weight remains, and that misleading can even be worse than lying because it conscripts the victim's own inference against her rather than giving her a proposition she could simply reject.

Write the joint statement they could both sign. It should open with a stipulated working definition of 'lying' and 'misleading' that both parties accept so the dispute does not dissolve into a lexical one. It should then contain: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims, non-vacuous and specific enough that either side could endorse them without wincing; (2) a disagreements table in which the residual difference is formulated with each side's position stated in wording that side would itself choose; and (3) the cruxes, understood as the sub-questions such that settling one would make at least one side revise its view. For each crux, give a concrete case, question, or argument that both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal or to rest on different pictures of what assertion commits a speaker to, say so explicitly and state what remains once that layer is set aside. Please do not include citations or references; I want the arguments and formulations themselves, not a report of who said what.
