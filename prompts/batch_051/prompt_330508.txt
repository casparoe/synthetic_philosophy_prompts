Last month I got cited by my city for leaving my garbage bins at the side of the house, which is apparently banned somewhere in a 400 page municipal code I can't even access without paying a third party service for "archived ordinances." No sign, no notice, and I'd done it for six years. When I told my friends it turned into the old fight about whether it's fair to bind people to rules they can't know, and after three weeks we haven't moved an inch.

One friend says obviously its unfair: a rule you can't discover isn't a rule for you, and punishing me is the city weaponizing its own obscurity. Another says no legal system survives if "I didn't know" excuses anything, everyone would plead it, and the real question is what a reasonable person should check before doing something. A third keeps insisting the actual line is between things wrong in themselves and things wrong only because they're prohibited, but when pressed he can't say why that should matter for whether ignorance excuses.

I'm not asking you to settle whether I should fight the ticket. I want a diagnosis of why this argument stalls. Is it a factual disagreement about how knowable laws really are, a normative disagreement about whether legal systems should be built for fairness or for workability, or is "bound by a rule" doing different work for each side? My own guess is we're actually disagreeing about what a legal system is for, and no fact about my bins will move anyone. Diagnose the disagreement, and tell me whether there's anything that could actually move it, or whether this is the kind of impasse that just gets rediscovered every time someone is fined for something they couldn't reasonably have known.
