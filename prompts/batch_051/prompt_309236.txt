I cover math and philosophy for a general audience, so I've had to explain the Sleeping Beauty problem (fair coin; she's woken once under heads, twice with a memory wipe under tails, and each time asked her credence in heads) and the doomsday argument in the same month, and I noticed I treat them different. In Sleeping Beauty, the move behind the one-third answer is that when Beauty wakes she's sampling one of the possible awakenings (one on heads, two on tails), so waking is evidence for tails; I find this convincing. But the doomsday argument seems to make the same move: I'm sampling one of the possible humans, and a long-lived humanity has vastly more of them, so the fact that I find myself this early in the lineup favors a shorter future. I find that absurd, and so do most of the scientists I interview. I want a principle that lets Beauty update on 'I am awake now' but forbids me from updating on 'I am the 100-billionth human.' Is there one, or do I have to give up one of these two intuitions, and which? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble, and don't restate the question.
