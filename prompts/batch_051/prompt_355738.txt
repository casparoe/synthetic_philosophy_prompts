I spent last night arguing with a friend who works in statistics about whether numerical symbolism in scripture is a genuine object of interpretation or a dressed-up form of pattern-hunting. We never resolved it, but the argument left me convinced that the two positions mirror something in his own field, and I want to know whether the resemblance is deep or superficial. The parallel is this. When an interpreter claims that a recurring or conspicuous number—seven days of creation, forty years in the wilderness, twelve tribes, the 153 fish in John's resurrection scene, the number 666—was deliberately embedded as a symbol, the skeptic replies that a determined reader scanning any sufficiently long and varied text will eventually find frequencies, multiples, and sums that look significant, and that the interpreter chooses after the fact which patterns get counted and which do not. That appears structurally close to the methodological charge a statistician makes against data dredging: if one runs many analyses and reports only the ones that reach significance, chance alone practically guarantees that some will look meaningful, unless the hypothesis was specified before looking at the data. I want to know whether this is a real identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion. In particular, does authorial intention play the same role in exegesis that pre-registration plays in statistical inference, and is there anything on the exegetical side corresponding to the null distribution that makes the charge of p-hacking exact rather than rhetorical? I can already see disanalogies—a text is not a random data-generating process, an author can genuinely plant a pattern that a later reader might also have found by accident, and the costs of a false symbolic reading differ from the costs of a false research finding—so I do not want an answer that simply legislates one side of my argument with my friend. Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. The points should state the mapping precisely, say what would have to be true of both sides for rigor to transfer from statistics to exegesis, and identify at least one place where the parallel predicts something false.
