ok so I'm trying to nail down my actual views on free will and moral responsibility after bingeing the old forum arguments, and I want a consistency check before I write the post. I've ended up with a bunch of claim-ish committments and I honestly can't tell where the crack is, or whether there are several. Here's the set:

1) I think standard Frankfurt cases basically kill the priniciple of alternative possibilities as a necessary condition: an agent who couldn't have done otherwise can still be fully responsible, as long as the backup process never actually kicked in.

2) I'm a compatibilist about determinism. In a fully deterministic world, agents are responsible when they deliberate and act on their own reasons, and I don't think slotting a genuinely random fork into deliberation adds any responsibility.

3) My positive account is reasons-responsiveness of the actual mechanism. If the mechanism that actually produced the action is suitably responsive to reasons across nearby counterfactuals, the agent is responsible even if no alternative possibilities were open in the actual world.

4) But in the Frankfurt variant where the intervener would directly cause the decision if the agent started to diverge, not just detect the divergence, I still say: backup never fires, responsible; backup fires and directly causes the decision, not responsible. The difference is whether the actual mechanism got bypassed.

5) In a manipulation case where the agent's values and character were comprehensively installed by another agent during childhood, I want to say the agent isn't responsible, even if at the time of action she's fully reasons-responsive, endorses the values on reflection, all of it.

6) But for ordinary socialization — parents, schools, peers — I don't get that same no-responsibility intuition, even when it's just as causally thorough. And I can't see what distinguishes the manipulation from ordinary socialization except my wanting to say another agent intended the outcome, which feels like I'm sneaking in a condition I haven't stated.

7) I also have the intuition that a deliberately designed AI that is robustly reasons-responsive within its assigned objective is less responsible, or not at all, than a human with the same capacities, for the same murky "how it got that way" reason.

What I want from you is an audit, not a lecture. Which pairs are actually in tension? Rank the tensions by severity — don't just tell me they're all equally bad. Which pairs only look like they're in tension and why do they dissolve? And if I can only modify or give up one commitment to make the rest hang together, which one costs me the least and what do I lose by changing it?
