Here's one I keep going back and forth on. Philosophers argue about what norm governs assertion: knowledge (don't assert unless you know), truth (don't assert unless it's true), or belief (don't assert unless you believe it). I'm not asking for a first-principles argument about which is right; I want to know how you'd actually test this. Say you run vignettes: someone asserts a truth they had no evidence for, or a reasonable falsehood, or a lie they themselves believe — wich results would count for each norm, and which of the three positions is genuinely at risk from the data rather than retreating to "that's just folk intuition, not the real norm"? Design the best test or battery of tests you can, spell out what result would move you toward each view, and then be blunt about what even a clean result would fail to settle, e.g. whether you've found a conversational convention, a moral rule, or something constitutively tied to assertion.
