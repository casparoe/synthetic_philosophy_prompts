I'm writing a paper for a philosophy of space policy module and I keep going round in circles about what 'colonisation' actually means once you leave Earth. In the stuff we read, a colony in the morally loaded sense is taken to involve four things: (1) permanent settlement of a population from a metropole; (2) extraction of resources for the benefit of the metropole rather than the locals; (3) extension of political control over people or territory; and (4) subordination or displacement of an existing local community. Now consider a Mars settlement that has been running for a couple of decades. People are born and die there, it is largely self-sufficient, but it ships some rare metals and scientific data back to Earth, and it is governed under a charter written on Earth with no independence on offer. Mars, as far as we know, has no existing local community. So criterion (4) seems to fail outright, and criterion (2) is at best mixed, since most of the mining is there to keep the settlement alive rather than to enrich Earth.

My worry is that this lets quite brutal forms of control and exploitation off the hook simply because there are no Martians to displace. I'd like a criterion-by-criterion answer to the question whether the settlement is a colony in the morally relevant sense. Where the criteria themselves underdetermine the verdict, say so. More importantly, tell me whether the borderline case shows the four criteria need revising - for instance whether the wrong of colonisation is really about domination, extraction, and permanent control, with displacement being only one especially bad symptom rather than a necessary condition. Please write the answer in British English.
