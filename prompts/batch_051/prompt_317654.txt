I teach an upper-level cognitive science seminar on concepts and analogy, and this year we have spent several weeks on the letter-string analogy microdomain associated with the Copycat program. Students have worked through items such as abc → abd, xyz → wyz, and aab → aabb, and have discussed how concepts like successor, predecessor, same, and opposite can slip into one another under contextual pressure. For the 90-minute final, I want one essay question that forces students to engage with conceptual slippage itself rather than summarize the architecture. It should require them to explain where the pressure for a slippage comes from, how a slipped concept alters the mapping while leaving the underlying rule intact, and why the best answer to a problem can depend on the whole string rather than on a local rule. Please write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme worth 20 points that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain. The question should include a novel letter-string analogy that is not one of the standard examples, and the model answer should explain why a rigid rule-based system would get that item wrong.
