Since I retired from the lab I've been reading around in philosophy, and a friend in my reading group has prodded me toward Wang Yangming, the Ming-dynasty Confucian thinker. The bit that's got its hooks into me is the unity of knowing and acting. As I understand it, for practical matters there is no real gap between knowing what to do and doing it: action is the completion of knowledge, so if someone repeatedly fails to act on something he claims to know, the right diagnosis is that he doesn't really know it, not that he knows it perfectly well but his will let him down.

I've tried to build my own position around that, and I'd like a consistency audit. Here's my current list. (1) For practical and moral matters, knowing and acting are one in the sense just described: failure to act on a practical truth shows incomplete knowledge, not a seperate failure of will. (2) Knowledge of this kind is graded; a surgical trainee who can describe a cholecystectomy but can't yet do one isn't simply ignorant, she has partial knowledge. (3) I still want to say weakness of will is real, at least sometimes. A smoker who knows he ought to stop and lights another is often best described as knowing and not doing, not as merely knowing less than he thinks he does. (4) I'm not much bothered by the fact that I hold thousands of entirely inert factual beliefs — enzyme kinetics, say — because I take the unity thesis to concern practical knowledge, not knowing-that. (5) I also think people can be rightly blamed for culpable ignorance, as with a doctor who never reads the safety data and harms a patient: the blame lands on the not-knowing itself, not only on the harmful act.

Before I bring this to the group, I'd like to know whether it hangs together. Go through the list and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only seem to be, and how badly they conflict; rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equal. For each one, say which commitment would most cheaply be revised or abandoned, and whether there's a single change that would clear up the most. I'd also appreciate the answer in British English, as the group is in Edinburgh.
