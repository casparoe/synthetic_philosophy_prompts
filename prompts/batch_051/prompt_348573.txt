i keep going back and forth on heterophenomenology, in the dennett sense where the science of consciousness treats what subjects say about their experience as a text or a set of expressed beliefs, and stays neutral about whether that text is true. the appeal for me is huge: you get public, checkable data instead of trying to peer into someone's private show, you don't have to solve the hard problem before doing any work, and it even seems charitable rather than reductive because you reconstruct the subject's inner world in their own terms instead of telling them they're wrong up front. but then i hit my thumb with a hammer and the whole method suddenly feels like a con. when i say that hurts i am not narrating a fiction about myself, i am reporting on the pain itself, which i am directly in touch with in a way no story about my beliefs could capture. the method seems to demote the pain to a character in a text and hand the final say to the anthropologist reading it. and i cant tell whether that's a real objection to the method or just me being in the grip of a picture of inner perception — the little homunculus watching a screen. so, straight question: does heterophenomenology actually contradict the intuition that my pain-report is about the pain and not just a belief i am describing, or does it only contradict a philosophical gloss on that intuition? and if there is a genuine clash, which should give? should the intuition be debunked (written off as a user-illusion the method should teach me to stop trusting), should the method be amended (say, by giving first-person reports some non-fictional authority so auto-phenomenology is a partner rather than a text), or is there a reading on which they are compatible? i want the negotiated verdict, with what would justify each move, not just a winner.
