Here's something I keep coming back to. The mental imagery debate between Kosslyn and Pylyshyn has been running since the 70s, and as far as I can tell nobody has actually moved. Kosslyn thinks the format of mental images is depictive, quasi-pictorial, and he appeals to mental rotation and scanning-time data; Pylyshyn thinks the underlying representations are propositional descriptions and the experimental results just reflect subjects' tacit knowledge of what the scene would look like. What bugs me is that the same behavioural data get explained both ways, and even the neuroimaging stuff (early visual cortex lighting up) didn't settle it, because Pylyshyn can always say that's just the implementation, not the code. My working theory is that this isnt one disagreement but several stacked: a question about representational format, a question about what the phenomenology is actually showing, and a question about which level of Marr's hierarchy anyone is even talking about. Diagnose the impasse. Is it a substantive empirical disagreement that's just hard, a verbal dispute about the word 'depictive', or two camps answering different questions?
