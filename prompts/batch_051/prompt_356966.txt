I am a PhD student in philosophy of science, currently working as a teaching assistant for an upper-level undergraduate course. The course has just finished a unit on Duhem, Quine, and the debate over scientific realism, and the midterm contained the following question:

State the underdetermination thesis, and distinguish two senses in which the evidence might be unable to decide between theories: (i) the weak sense, according to which the available evidence cannot conclusively prove a theory, and (ii) the strong sense, according to which for any theory there is always a genuinely different rival theory that accommodates all possible evidence equally well. Explain the relationship between each of these two senses and the related doctrine of confirmational holism. Does underdetermination, in either sense, give us reason to reject realism about unobservable entities? Why or why not?

One student submitted the answer below:

Underdetermination, in the weak sense, says only that the evidence cannot conclusively prove a theory; this much is uncontroversial, since any finite body of data is logically compatible with more than one theory. Duhem adds the point that theories face observation only as wholes together with their auxiliary assumptions, so a recalcitrant observation can always be absorbed by revising an auxiliary hypothesis rather than abandoning the main theory. From this, Quine showed that for any accepted theory there must be another theory, logically incompatible with the first, that fits all the same evidence equally well. If two theories are empirically equivalent in this way, then all possible evidence supports them to the same degree, so choosing between them cannot be rationally grounded and becomes a matter of convention or taste. It follows that the unobservable entities our theories posit—electrons, quarks, and the like—are not things we can be justified in believing in, and realism is therefore untenable. The history of science confirms the point: Newtonian mechanics enjoyed centuries of predictive success and was still eventually abandoned in favor of relativistic physics, which shows that even our best-confirmed theories can always be reinterpreted or replaced. Underdetermination therefore gives a decisive reason to reject realism about unobservable entities.
