I run a small hardware store. A few weeks ago I noticed the back emergency exit was stuck shut from the inside. I made a mental note to fix it and then, like an idiot, forgot about it. Last Friday there was a small electrical fire near the register. The front doors were open and nobody died, but one of my employees got knocked down in the rush and ended up with a pretty bad burn on his arm. I keep thinking that if I'd fixed that exit, or even just told everyone it was jammed, he probably would have gotten out faster and might not have been hurt.

My sister says I'm being too hard on myself. I didn't start the fire, I didn't block the door, and not-doing-something isn't doing anything: it's a non-event, and a non-event can't cause a burn. My wife says that's convenient, but by knowing and staying quiet I made the injury happen, full stop. I'm not asking about legal liability. I want to know who's right about whether I caused the injury, and how much I should actually own it. One more wrinkle: if some stranger who happened to know about the exit had also said nothing, I wouldn't blame them the way I blame myself. So is my job the only thing that makes me responsible, or does simply knowing about a risk change the causal picture?
