There's this thought experiment that comes up constantly in my corner of the internet, and every time it's the same two camps talking past each other. You have to choose which is worse: one person being tortured for fifty years, or some astronomically huge number of people — a number so big people write it in up-arrow notation — each getting a single dust speck in their eye for a second. One camp says the dust specks are obviously worse, because each speck is a tiny real harm and if you actually add them all up the total dwarfs the torture. The other camp says the torture is obviously worse, because no pile of trivial annoyances stacks into a destroyed life, and if you think it does you've let arithmetic override what 'harm' and 'worse' even mean. I've seen smart people hold both positions with total confidence, and I've never watched anyone flip. My hunch is that the standoff isn't really about which option is worse at all — its about whether harms are the sort of thing that must be additive, and maybe about whether the scenario is coherent enough to trust our reactions to it in the first place. Is that the right diagnosis? What kind of disagreement is this actually — a substantive moral disagreement, a disagreement about how to model the choice, or a disagreement about the thought experiment itself? And is there any move that would count as progress, like a sharper distinction or a cleaner case, or should everyone just stop having this argument?
