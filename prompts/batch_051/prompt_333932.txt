I'm a novelist, and for years I've been fiddling with a book made of a half dozen parallel storylines that never touch. The characters are in different cities, different centuries sometimes, and no event in one storyline can causally bump into another — no letters, no shared ancestors, no common cause at all. But I want them to end up helping each other, in the sense that each does something that pay's off in someone else's storyline, and each does it because she's confident the relevant other character is doing the matching thing in her own storyline. I recently learned decision theory has a name for something like this: acausal trade, where two agents who can't causally interact and can't make a binding deal still cooperate, because each can predict or infer how the other will act — they each run the other's decision procedure, roughly — and so each is willing to do something that helps the other, knowing the other is symetrically doing the same, even though neither action can reach the other as a cause.

I want you to restate acausal trade in the vocabulary of my craft: plot and parallel plot, character, dramatic irony, foreshadowing, theme, the reader's knowledge versus the character's knowledge, point of view, the author's god's-eye position over all the lines at once. Be faithful; don't just say it's parallelism and call it a day. Then give me the residue report: which parts of acausal trade map cleanly onto something a novelist already knows how to do, which parts you had to fudge, and which parts have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. And the thing I most want to know: is what gets lost an artifact of how fiction works (one author and one reader already linking the storylines from above), or does it expose something in the decision-theory idea that only looks rigorous so long as nobody asks it to live in a story? After the translation, am I seeing real acausal trade in my parallel plots, or just a cheaper trick that happens to resemble it?

Also, I honestly can't tell whether acausal trade is different from two characters reading each other's character and acting out of mutual trust. If it is different, say what the difference is in my terms; if the difference can't be said in my terms, say that too.
