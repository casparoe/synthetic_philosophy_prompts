Frank Jackson's Knowledge Argument runs roughly as follows: Mary has never seen colour, yet from her black-and-white room she learns every physical fact about colour vision; on leaving, she learns something new about seeing red, so not all facts are physical. A later development seems to change the dialectical position: a language model trained only on text, with no visual input whatsoever, can produce detailed first-person reports of colour experience that an observer cannot distinguish from those of a sighted human. It has the words for what red is like without ever having seen red. I am not asking whether the model is conscious. I am asking what this development does to the Knowledge Argument itself: does it undercut one of the premises, strengthen the argument by showing that verbal mastery and phenomenal knowledge pull apart, or leave it untouched? Identify the specific premise it bears on, and say whether the argument can be restated so that it still goes through. Answer in British English.
