In discussions about whether an AI system could be a legal person, I keep hearing the same move: corporations are already legal persons even though they aren't human beings, so the fact that an AI has no body or inner life can't settle the question by itself. I'm trying to work out how far that analogy actually goes. Could you audit it — lay out what corresponds to what between a corporation and a proposed AI legal person, identify where the mapping breaks, and separate the disanalogies that matter from the ones that don't? I also want to know whether the conclusion survives. The corporation example is used both to show that legal personhood is a practical status rather than a fact about a being's capacities, and to show that we already know how to create and manage artificial legal entities; my worry is that a corporation's personhood ultimately bundles the acts and interests of the humans behind it, while an AI legal person might not be a vehicle for anyone's interests in that way. Does the analogy make AI legal personhood look like a modest extension, or was it quietly carrying the argument the whole time?
