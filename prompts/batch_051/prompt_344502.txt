Write a short story set in a near-future municipality whose arts council has adopted a strict Tolstoyan funding policy. Under the policy, a work is eligible for public support only if it satisfies three conditions: the emotion it conveys must be one the artist has genuinely felt; the work must transmit that emotion so directly that it requires no interpretive training or commentary; and it must be accessible to the widest possible audience, including people with no background in the art form. Show what happens when a serious artist working in a deliberately difficult or transgressive style submits an application, or when a council member struggles to apply the three conditions to a borderline case. The story should dramatize the conflict between Tolstoy's demand that art unite people through shared feeling and the modern valuation of novelty, difficulty, and specialized taste.
