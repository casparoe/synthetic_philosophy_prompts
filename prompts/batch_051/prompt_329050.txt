Here are the objections to treating populist appeals to 'the will of the people' as a real, unified political thing that I've already collected:

1. Social choice: individual preferences don't aggregate into a coherent group will without arbitrary aggregation rules, so there's no pre-existing people's will to discover.
2. Boundary problem: who counts as 'the people' is contested, and populists usually define it in a way that excludes part of the actual citizenry.
3. Manufactured will: polling methods, media framing, and the questions asked produce the preferences that then get cited as the people's will.
4. Temporal instability: even if a people wills X at t1, it can will not-X at t2; invoking a standing will papers over that.
5. Normative gap: even if there were a unified majority will, that doesn't by itself settle whether it should be enacted, since majorities can will illiberal or oppressive things.

Give me another objection that is not on this list and not just a variant, restatement, or combination of any of them. Then tell me honestly whether the new objection is actually any good.
