In popular and textbook discussions of molecular biology, DNA is often described as a code, a blueprint, or a program for the organism, and this way of talking is made to do real argumentative work: it underwrites claims that genes carry instructions or information for building a body, that mutations are errors in a text, and that genetic engineering is a kind of editing. Audit this analogy in detail. What exactly maps onto what, and which features of a human code or blueprint have genuine counterparts in the DNA–cell system? Which disanalogies are merely incidental, and which ones matter for the conclusions the analogy is used to support? In particular, consider whether the analogy imports intentionality, a distinction between a message and its physical medium, or an independent reader that the molecular system need not contain. State how far the code/blueprint analogy is still justified and, if a better analogy is available, say what it would be and how it would change the claims we are entitled to make about genetic information.
