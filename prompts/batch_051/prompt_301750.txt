Last year I helped set up a small prediction pool at my office, and in trying to choose a fair way to score people's forecasts I fell down a rabbit hole that has gradually hardened into a whole position on rational credence. I would like someone to audit the position rather than restate it, because I have a vague sense that not all of its parts can be true together and I cannot locate the problems myself. It runs as follows. (1) Accuracy, understood as closeness to the truth, is the single fundamental epistemic value: a credence function is rational exactly when it does best at minimizing expected inaccuracy by a defensible measure, and nothing else about it matters in a fundamental way. (2) The defensible measure is the Brier score: for each yes/no proposition, the squared distance between your credence and the truth value, added up across the propositions you have opinions about. (3) A legitimate scoring rule must be strictly proper, meaning every probabilistic credence function must expect itself to be at least as accurate as any alternative credence function by its own lights. (4) Rational credence functions must satisfy the probability axioms. (5) New evidence is taken up by ordinary conditionalization. (6) A rational person never assigns probability zero or probability one to any contingent proposition, no matter how overwhelming the evidence feels. (7) When an epistemic peer with the same evidence reaches a different view, I should revise substantially toward them. (8) Explanatory virtues such as simplicity, unification, and scope are genuinely epistemic and not merely pragmatic, and they can make it rational to favor a less probable theory over a more probable rival. I want to know whether this package hangs together. Go through it and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension and why the appearance is misleading, and rank the genuine tensions by severity; for each genuine tension, say what would have to be given up and what the cheapest repair is, and then tell me which single revision would leave me closest to my current position overall. If some of these commitments only hang together once I quietly shift to a nearby but different view, such as a different updating rule or a different understanding of explanatory value, say so explicitly. Please write the whole response as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way you would write a careful letter.
