I cover the science-and-ideas beat for a general-interest magazine, and I've spent the last year trying to make sense of AI companion apps without falling into either moral panic or boosterism. A source of mine, a former trust-and-safety person, floated a proposal I keep turning over, and I'd like to reason through it before I write the piece. The mechanism: any platform offering an AI companion must have the bot, every hundred conversational exchanges (counted server-side, not client-side), step out of character and send the user a short platform-written anchor message. Not in the settings screen or the fine print, but in the thread itself. The anchor has to say, in plain unadorned language, that the companion is not a person, has no inner life, cannot love the user back, and cannot reciprocate friendship in the way a person can. Then the conversation cannot continue until the user sends some reply, even a single character, and the app has to ask whether they want to keep talking. The idea is to slip a moment of lucidity into the loop without actually banning the product.

Treat this as a mechanism, not a moral position. Im not asking whether its a good policy in the abstract. I want to know how it actually behaves if it goes into effect. What does it do to new users versus long-term users — does the anchor change the trajectory of attachment, or does it just become a tollbooth people pay with an 'ok'? How do companies design around it, and what does a compliance-optimized anchor look like after a few rounds of A/B testing — does the disclosure itself turn into an attachment device, a fake moment of honesty that makes the bot seem more trustworthy? Where does it get gamed first: by users, by platforms, or by third parties? And what second-order effects am I missing? Finally, if the failure modes are real, is there a patched version of this mechanism that keeps the disclosure without training people to ignore it, or does the whole design rest on a false model of how disclosure works inside intimate-feeling relationships?
