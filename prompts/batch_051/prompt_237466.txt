I teach formal epistemology and keep returning to the value of information. I have been working out a distinction I call the boundary problem for Good’s theorem. The standard theorem, as I understand it, says that for an expected-utility maximizer, free information can never have negative expected value, because one can always ignore the signal and act as before. I have become convinced this result silently assumes that the decision problem—states, utilities, options, and the prior—is fixed independently of receiving information. Once acquiring information can alter one’s preferences, attention, or the menu of available acts, the theorem no longer seems to go through. I call this informational iatrogenesis: information as intervention rather than information as mere filtering. On my view, Good’s theorem governs only the filtering case. I want to know whether this distinction is real or merely terminological, whether there are existing formal refinements I am missing, and where my argument breaks down. Give me the strongest objections as charitably as you can, and say whether any is fatal.
