In my epistemology reading group there's a guy who keeps insisting that "knows" works like "flat." His example: a tabletop can be flat enough for a card game but not flat for a machinist, and the word hasn't changed meaning—just the standard of precision has shifted. From this he concludes that contextualism is just ordinary context-sensitivity, not a substantive epistemic thesis, that the skeptic is making a category mistake about which standard is in play, and that pragmatic encroachment is just a misleading way of saying the stakes raise the standard. I want to know how far the analogy really holds. Lay out the correspondence table—what plays the role of the tabletop, the card game, the machinist, the word "flat"—and then the disanalogies that matter for his conclusion. Give me a committed verdict on whether his deflationary conclusion survives once the mapping breaks, not a menu of options. American English.
