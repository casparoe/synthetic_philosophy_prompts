ive been reading Spinoza's Ethics and the way Part V opens is really throwing me. He writes:

"At length I pass to the remaining portion of my Ethics, which is concerned with the way leading to freedom. I shall therefore treat therein of the power of the reason, showing how far the reason can control the emotions, and what is the nature of Mental Freedom or Blessedness ; we shall then be able to see, how much more powerful the wise man is than the ignorant."

But earlier in the same book he is pretty explicit that nothing in nature is contingent, and that everything follows from the necessity of the divine nature. So how does he get away with talking about the power of reason "controlling" the emotions, or about achieving freedom and blessedness? I know he doesn't mean free will in the usual sense, but I still can't shake the feeling that a mind that is just as determined as everything else can't really "control" or achieve anything. Can you explain the conceptual issue: what does Spinoza think the mind's power actually consists in, how is that compatible with his necessitarianism, and how does it connect to his idea of the intellectual love of God as the highest state we can attain? Don't just say it's a metaphor—I want the actual mechanics of how he thinks this works.
