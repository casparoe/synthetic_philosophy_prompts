A donor club of rich countries is considering scrapping its aid budgets and replacing them with this. Each year, every recipient country's share of a common pot is set by a citizen-retention score: adults on the national registry at the start of the year, minus adults who left during the year, plus adults who came back, divided by adults on the registry at the start of the year. 'Left' means the person appears in donor-country entry records, visa overstay lists, or asylum claims — legal or not. The donor governments don't share there migration data until after the allocation is locked in, and recipient governments are free to keep their registries however they like. There's a cap and a floor so no one gets literally nothing and no one gets more than 5% of the pot. The stated rationale is that aid should reward governments whose people find life worth staying for.
