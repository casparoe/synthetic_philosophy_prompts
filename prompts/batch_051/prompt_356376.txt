I'm a novelist, and I need a scene argued out properly before I can write it. The novel's set in the 1640s, in an English parish just as the altar's about to be pulled down. I've got two clergymen who can't leave each other alone about the Prayer Book ceremonies. One is a Laudian rector: his line is the normative principle of worship — whatever Scripture doesn't forbid, the church may order, so long as it's edifying and decent and serves order, so he'll defend kneeling at the rail, the sign of the cross at baptism, vestments, set holy days, the lot. The other is a Presbyterian lecturer: his line is the regulative principle — in public worship you may only do what God has actually commanded, and whatever isn't commanded is forbidden; to him the ceremonies are will-worship, however piously dressed up.

I keep finding I've made one of them an idiot, usually the one I disagree with on the day. Could you write the dialogue between them? Make them equally good at this, and make it a real exchange: each response has to meet the other's point, not just re-preach his own position. I'd like them to go at each other over the second commandment, over Nadab and Abihu and the strange fire, over Colossians 2 and Romans 14, and over that elements/circumstances distintion the Presbyterian falls back on. The rector should genuinely think the presbyterian is binding consciences with a new law, and the presbyterian should genuinely think the rector is smuggling popery in through church order. End the scene unresolved — candles still lit, neither man moved — becuase the chapter needs them both to walk off still certain. Write it as dialogue in the scene, not as a summary of the positions.
