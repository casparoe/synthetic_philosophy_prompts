Write a deadpan, formal product-recall notice of about 550 words from Tectum Vision Systems, a fictional bioengineering firm that manufactures visual modules for autonomous robotic frogs. The recalled product, the Anura V3 optic module, is a faithful copy of a real frog's optic system and has begun firing in response to small, dark, moving objects that are not flies: BB pellets, drifting lint, the shadow of a circling heron. The notice should show, without ever saying so outright, that the company is handling the problem by revising the module's content downward rather than repairing it. Outside consultants, who insist that a state's content is fixed by its proper function and that proper function is fixed by a device's selection and design history, advise management to announce that the module never meant 'fly' at all — only 'small, dark, moving object' — so that the current behavior counts as correct rather than defective, and no refund or repair is owed. The humor must come from getting the machinery of a teleosemantic account right: etiological proper functions and the Normal conditions under which they are discharged; the way selection history is supposed to make misrepresentation possible in the first place; the ease with which content can be redescribed downward until error becomes impossible; the difference between a designer's intention and a selection history as competing sources of function; and the awkward case of a duplicate module assembled without any history, which would turn out to have no content at all on this view. The satire should land the standard objections to this style of theory — that content is underdetermined, that determinacy is restored only by quietly relying on a notion of what a device is 'supposed to' indicate, and that detaching the theory from its historical, teleological apparatus yields absurd results — without stating the objections, naming the theory, or citing anyone. Write in American English; keep the corporate idiom straight-faced and precise throughout.
