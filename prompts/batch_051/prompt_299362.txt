I've been calling these "self-throttling memes" and I want an honest verdict on the idea, not encouragement. The standard Darwinian account says cultural variants spread because they're good at spreading—catchy, useful, emotionally sticky. But I keep noticing cases where something seems to survive precisely because it holds its own spread back. A secret society's secrecy rule keeps the group small and committed; if everyone adopted the rule the group would lose its point. A slang term dies the moment adults pick it up. An initiation ritual filters members and that filtering is part of why members stay. What gets copied in these cases isn't just a belief or a practice, it's a restriction on copying. So the throttle is part of the meme itself, and the meme's continued existence depends on its reproduction rate staying low. Is this a real distinction or am I just restating frequency-dependent selection under a worse name? If it is real, does it expose a fault in the Darwinian analogy for culture, or is the analogy loose enough to absorb it? And where does the construct break—what would it wrongly count as self-throttling, or where is the throttle not actually doing the explanatory work?
