my philosophy of mind professor is one of those Merleau-Ponty people, and all term she's been making us vote on where the lived body ends and an instrument begins — she says its not the skin, its the body schema, motor intentionality or whatever. so far she's given her verdicts on a pile of cases. a blind person's cane: part of the body, full stop — the person feels the pavement at the tip of the cane, not the cane in their palm. a hat: not part of the body, just a thing you feel sitting on your head. a car after years of daily driving: part of the body — you feel the curb through the wheel and know the width of the car like your own hips. a first-person videogame avatar after 300 hours: not part of the body — it's a picture you steer with a controller, even if you don't think about which button is jump anymore. a surgeon's scalpel after 20 years: part of the body — the instrument disappears and the surgeon feels tissue at the blade. a loaded backpack on a long hike: not part of the body — it's a weight you carry, never something you feel through.

next week she's going to give us a new case: a teleoperated surgical robot, where the surgeon sits at a console and the remote arm mirrors her movements with no lag, and the force feedback is so good that experienced surgeons say they feel the tissue at the gripper, not the handles in their hands, and after enough hours they stop noticing the console. will she say the robot arm is part of the surgeon's body the way the cane and the scalpel are? predict her verdict, say which rule shes actually running — transparency of the instrument, a continuous mechanical link to the body, a habit that's just become automatic, or something else — which of her earlier cases is the closest analog, and if her earlier verdicts underdetermine the new one, what further case would settle the rule.
