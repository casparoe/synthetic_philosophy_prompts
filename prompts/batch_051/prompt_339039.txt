Here's what's chewing at me in the probability part of my modal logic class. I'm really taken with a clean regularity principle: a proposition is possible only if it deserves at least some nonzero probability, and assigning it zero just is treating it as impossible. If that held, possibility and probability would line right up, and we could read off what can happen from a probability assignment with no leftover modal residue. But then I picture a fair spinner on a continuous dial: the chance it lands exactly on 90.000... degrees is zero, yet that outcome feels possible in a way a round square doesn't. Wether I like it or not, the principle says those two are on a par, and I can't make myself believe that. I don't want a verdict by fiat. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take to revise the principle (and what a less tidy but workable possibility–probability link would look like), and whether there's a reading of 'treating as impossible' on which both survive. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, don't restate the question.
