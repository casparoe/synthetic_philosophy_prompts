I am a high school student preparing a proposal for a model constitutional convention, and I need to understand how the institution I am drafting would actually behave before I have to defend it. The proposal creates a Chamber of Later Generations in a country of roughly fifty million people with a unicameral parliament. The chamber has one hundred and twenty members chosen annually by lottery from the adult population, paid a full salary, and convened for one week each month. Every bill submitted to parliament must be accompanied by a posterity impact statement from the national budget office, classifying the bill as shallow if its projected effects end within sixty years and deep if any material effect is expected to persist beyond that horizon. The classification can be reviewed only by a special administrative tribunal, not by the ordinary courts. A bill classified deep cannot be enacted until the chamber has considered it. By a two-thirds vote, the chamber may adopt a generational objection that suspends the bill for two years. If the chamber adopts a second generational objection to substantially the same bill, parliament may enact it only by a four-fifths majority. Chamber members are under a legal instruction to decide as though they did not know which generation, from the present one through the year 2200, they would be born into, and they must record in writing the generation-specific assumptions they relied on. I am less interested in whether this is good in the abstract than in how it would actually operate. Walk me through the incentives it creates for the budget office, for members of parliament, for the lottery-selected chamber members, and for outside interests. Where is it likely to be gamed first, and what form will the gaming take? What becomes of the veil-of-ignorance instruction in practice, and which failure modes could be repaired without recreating the same problem elsewhere? Finally, tell me whether it would in practice function as a real representative of later generations or as a new route by which present-day actors exercise control while claiming to speak for the future.
