An ethics textbook I am using summarizes Bernard Williams's integrity objection to utilitarianism in one paragraph, and I suspect the paragraph is not a fair summary, but I do not know Williams well enough to say exactly where it goes wrong. The textbook's account runs as follows. Williams argues that utilitarianism demands that agents treat their own deepest projects and personal relationships as nothing more than inputs to the maximization of overall welfare. In the Jim-and-the-Indians case—a visitor is told that unless he himself shoots one of twenty captives, all twenty will be killed—Williams is said to show that utilitarian reasoning commits Jim to shooting. According to the textbook, Williams's deeper point is that if Jim refuses, he is not responsible for the deaths of the other nineteen, because a person is answerable only for what he actually does, and failures to act are never his doing. The textbook says Williams then extends the point to personal attachments: when a man saves his drowning wife rather than a stranger, Williams holds that if the man could justify the rescue only by reflecting that it is permissible to prefer one's wife, that reflection would be one thought too many, since her being his wife should move him directly rather than through the filter of impartial justification. The textbook concludes that Williams has thereby refuted utilitarianism, because utilitarianism leaves no room for integrity, understood as staying faithful to whatever a person happens to care most about.

I would like an accuracy audit of this summary. Which parts would a careful Williams accept as fair, which are common simplifications, and which are actual distortions of his view? In particular, I want to know whether the last two moves—the account of Jim's responsibility if he refuses, and the conclusion that the integrity objection refutes utilitarianism—follow from what Williams actually argues, and what exactly gets exaggerated or conflated along the way. Please also state, as precisely as you can, what the integrity objection accurately stated does and does not show.
