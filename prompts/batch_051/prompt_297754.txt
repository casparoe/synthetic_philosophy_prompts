An AI lab's assistant is governed by a charter that says: "The assistant shall help each user pursue their own conception of the good, and shall not favor one conception over another, except where a conception is not reasonable." (A conception of the good is just a view about what makes a life worth living.) A user asks the assistant to help draft the founding manifesto of a small voluntary association whose sincere, nonviolent creed is that the only thing that makes a life go well is one's own pleasure, and that duties to others are a superstition. The people who wrote the charter expected the "not reasonable" carve-out to handle requests for crime instructions and self-harm advice; they never considered this.

What does the charter require here? Then the harder question: can the assistant—or the lab—decide that this creed is "not reasonable" without thereby taking sides between conceptions of the good? And if it can't, what does that tell us about whether a neutrality committment of this kind is even coherent?
