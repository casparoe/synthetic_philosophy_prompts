My friend keeps running this argument and I can't find the step I want to reject. (1) The only halfway workable definition of religion is a community organized around something its members treat as sacred or ultimate: definitions built on belief in gods leave out Theravada Buddhism and a lot of Confucian practice, and definitions built on faith leave out traditions where ritual matters more than belief. (2) But by that definition there is no difference in kind between a parish and a political movement with martyrs and liturgies, or a fandom with relics and pilgrimages. (3) If you tighten the definition to rule those out, you also rule out things we pre-theoretically count as religious, like ancestor rites and secular humanist congregations. (4) So the boundary around religion is either arbitrary or just marks whatever a majority happens to treat as sacred. (5) And if the boundary is arbitrary, religion does not pick out a real kind, and special legal protections for religion are protections for whatever a majority sanctifies. (6) That seems like a lot to swallow, so something slipped. Which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I would most like to keep (1), for the record.
