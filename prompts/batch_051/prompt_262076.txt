So I've been trying to nail down what people actually mean when they say the outgroup argues in bad faith, and every definition I come up with is either way too broad ("defends a false thing") or way too narrow ("knowingly lies"). Give me a working definition of arguing in bad faith that gets these cases right:

1. A columnist files an op-ed she privately thinks is false because her editor assigned the take, and she writes it as if it's her own view.
2. A policy researcher deliberately crops a graph so a program looks more cost-effective than it was; caught, he says everyone does this.
3. A college debater draws a side at random and makes the strongest case she can.
4. A flat-earther who's spent years going through what he takes to be the primary evidence and sincerely believes it.
5. An activist inflates a death toll by a factor of ten, telling herself the official count is a cover-up and the cause is important enough that it doesn't matter.
6. A cable pundit who has flipped positions twice, each time after switching networks, and each time seems fully sincere.
7. Someone reposts a viral quote without checking it, and when shown a debunk says "even if the quote is fake, the point holds."
8. Someone who walks into every argument assuming the other side is lying; when an opponent asks a genuine question, she treats it as a trap.

My current intuitions: 1 and 2 are bad faith, 3 and 4 aren't, and 5 through 8 are where I keep waffling. I also want the definition to seperate bad faith from plain motivated reasoning, if you think they come apart, and it obviously can't just mean "argues for the side I dislike." Propose the definition, run every case through it, flag where it gives a weird answer, and then either revise it or defend the weird verdicts.
