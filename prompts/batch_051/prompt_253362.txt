The classic statement of transubstantiation leans heavily on a distinction between substance and accidents: the substance of the bread and wine is replaced by the body and blood of Christ, while the accidents—taste, texture, everything you can measure—remain exactly as before. But that whole substance/accident framework came out of Aristotle, and almost nobody in metaphysics or the sciences treats hylomorphism as a live theory of the world anymore. Does the real-presence doctrine survive that change, or was it always a claim that can only even be stated inside the old ontology? I want a careful answer that identifies which part of the doctrine is actually under pressure from letting go of substance-talk, whether there's a repaired version that keep the "real" as opposed to merely symbolic without smuggling the old metaphysics back in, and whether that repaired version ends up weaker, stronger, or just different. Please write the answer in American English.
