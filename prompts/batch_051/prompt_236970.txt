Write a dialogue between two members of a sentencing-guidelines commission. The first, a compatibilist close in spirit to Daniel Dennett, holds that ordinary adults possess the self-control, responsiveness to reasons, and reflective capacities that make them morally responsible for much of what they do, and that a modest, non-vindictive retributivism — in which the hardship of punishment is partly justified because it is deserved, never because it gratifies the victim — belongs in a just sentencing scheme. The second, a free will sceptic close in spirit to Gregg Caruso, holds that no one is morally responsible in the basic-desert sense (the sense that would make punishment intrinsically deserved rather than merely useful), that the retributive element of punishment is therefore unjustified, and that criminal justice should be rebuilt on a public-health/quarantine model concerned only with prevention, rehabilitation, and incapacitation.

The dialogue should be a genuine engagement rather than a set piece: each character should state the other's strongest argument before answering it, each should make at least one real concession or modification of a subsidiary position, and the exchange should turn throughout on a single practical question — whether 'culpability' may remain a determinant of sentence length or must give way to risk and treatment need alone. Do not let either side become a strawman, and end with the characters jointly identifying the one residual disagreement they regard as deepest.
