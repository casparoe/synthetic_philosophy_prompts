I keep flip-flopping between two ways of thinking about inequality. On one side I'm genuinely drawn to egalitarianism — at least the kind that says inequality in status or standing is itself bad, and not merely a rough proxy for someone having too little; a society with a smaller gap is better in that respect, the way a society with less humiliation is better. On the other side I can't shake the leveling-down intuition: making some people worse off while making nobody better off can't be an improvement in any respect, so if my egalitarianism says it is, maybe my egalitarianism has to go. Prioritarianism looks like the grown-up compromise — weight benefits to the worse off more, but drop the idea that equality itself matters — and it dodges leveling-down cleanly, but I honestly can't tell weather it's a real resolution or just a way of changing the subject away from the thing about inequality that actually bothered me. I don't want to be told which side wins. I want a negotiated verdict: what would it take for the leveling-down intuition to be debunked and dropped, what would it take for the equality-of-status view to be amended or abandoned, and is there a reading of "better off" — say one that counts relative standing or self-respect as part of someone's position — on which the egalitarian intuition and the leveling-down intuition can both come out true?
