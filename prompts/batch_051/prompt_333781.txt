I'm running a small workshop on risk and environmental policy, and I'd like to hand out a document that forces the two loudest camps in the room to stop caricaturing each other. One camp defends a strong precautionary principle — the kind in the Rio and Wingspread statements, roughly: when an activity threatens serious or irreversible harm, the absence of full scientific certainty is not a good enough reason to delay protective measures. The other camp defends standard risk governance: quantify what you can, put probabilities on the rest, weigh expected costs and benefits, and don't let vague worst-case talk hijack decisions. I can't tell how much of the fight is real and how much is them talking past eachother about words like "uncertainty," "serious," and "cost-effective."

Write the joint statement these two could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want it to include the claims they genuinely agree on (including what the principle actually says and what it doesn't, and what both sides think a regulator owes the public); the residual disagreement stated precisely, with each side's view worded so that side would accept it as its own; and the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of them. For each crux, say which case or kind of evidence both sides would accept as bearing on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance. If part of their argument is verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why. Please keep it continuous prose, like an email: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, and no tables.
