I am a graduate student in mathematics who has been reading some philosophy of mathematics, and I keep noticing a pattern in how definitions are chosen and then retrospectively described, but I do not have a name for it. The pattern goes like this. A definition is introduced as a formal stand-in for an intuitive notion: groups are meant to capture symmetry, open sets are meant to capture nearness without a metric. Then the definition gets adjusted because someone needs a theorem to go through — axioms are loosened or tightened, or a broader class of examples is let in — and the adjustment is defended less by the original intuition than by the fact that it preserves or extends the results. Later, when the older and newer definitions come apart in edge cases, the newer one is spoken of as the concept's real content, and the motivating intuition is demoted to a heuristic ladder that can be kicked away. The open-set case is my cleanest example: on the usual story, open sets begin as neighborhoods of points in metric spaces and end up as anything satisfying the topology axioms, including things like the indiscrete or cofinite topology, where "nearness" stops doing its ordinary separating work. The group case runs the same way: the subject begins with permutations and symmetries and ends with a group being whatever satisfies the axioms, even when no object has been fixed for it to act on. My question is whether this exact two-step pattern — a definition revised for proof-theoretic reasons, then declared retrospectively to be what the concept really meant — has an established name. I can see it is adjacent to Carnapian explication and to Lakatos's account of proof and refutation, but neither seems quite right: explication sounds like a deliberate one-shot replacement of a vague concept by an exact one, while Lakatos's concept-stretching is driven by counterexamples within an ongoing proof, not by the later decision to treat the new definition as the correct one. If the pattern has a name, I want to know exactly how it differs from those neighbors; if it does not, I would like a coined term, with a definition and two or three boundary cases where it is genuinely unclear whether the pattern applies, for instance a revision driven mainly by the original intuition rather than by theorems. The label matters less to me than the carving: what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges are. Finally, tell me how confident you are in your identification or coinage, and single out the point in your answer you are least sure about.
