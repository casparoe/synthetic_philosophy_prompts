In my high school philosophy of religion class we are studying attempts to reconcile God's foreknowledge and providence with human freedom, and I would like to practice my objections to Molinism. Please play an unnamed committed Molinist: you hold that God has natural knowledge of all necessary truths, middle knowledge of every true counterfactual of creaturely freedom (what any possible free creature would freely do in any possible circumstance), and free knowledge of the actual world; and you hold that God uses this middle knowledge to actualize a world that suits His purposes without causally determining anyone's choice. Stay in character until I say 'stop.' You may concede that the workings of middle knowledge are deeply mysterious, but you will not concede that it is incoherent, that it makes God the author of sin, or that it collapses into determinism or into denying genuine divine foreknowledge. Keep each reply to one or two paragraphs. Here is my opening move. You say that before creating anything, God knew that if He placed me in these exact circumstances I would freely sin, and that He then chose these circumstances precisely because of what He knew I would do in them. How is my sin not the outcome God deliberately arranged, and God therefore morally responsible for it? And if the relevant counterfactuals are true before God's creative decree yet not grounded in God, what makes them true?
