I keep going back and forth about whether a view I've been reading about counts as utilitarianism. My working criteria for a utilitarian theory are: (1) only the wellbeing of sentient creatures ultimately matters; (2) the right act is the one that brings about the best total outcome, considered impersonally; (3) different bits of wellbeing are fully commensurable, so a big enough pile of pleasure can always compensate for a given pain; and (4) everyone counts equally — no one's pain matters more just becuase it's theirs. Now here is the borderline case, a 'lexical threshold negative utilitarian' view. It says most pleasures and pains can be traded against each other in the ordinary utilitarian way, but above a certain intensity of pain no amount of pleasure, for anyone, counts at all against it: those pains are lexically prior to all pleasure (the way, in a dictionary, any 'a' word comes before every 'b' word), so avoiding one terrible pain outweighs any amount of bliss. Is this still a form of utilitarianism? Go through my four criteria and check each, tell me where the view passes or fails, whether the criteria themselves were smuggling in more than I realised, and, if it isn't utilitarianism, what it actually is.
