I keep catching a move in arguments about vague cases that I don't have a word for. Someone will say there's no fact of the matter about whether a 5'10" guy is tall, so 'he is tall' just isn't true. Then a minute later they reason as if the same sentence is false—they say he's not tall, or that it's untrue that he's tall, and build the next step on that. Either they've got two different senses of 'not' running, or they've quietly switched from 'there's no answer' to 'the answer is no.' I've ocasionally seen the exact same thing with future-tense claims: a person says 'there will be a sea battle tomorrow' is neither true nor false right now, then treats 'it's not true that there will be a sea battle' as if it meant 'there won't be one.' Is there a name for this slide from neither-true-nor-false back to plain falsehood? I don't just want a label—I want the boundary pinned down. What distinction does a person need to keep the two apart, is there a standard way of making that distinction explicit, and are there any cases where the two really do collapse into each other? If there's no established name, what would you call it?
