I'm writing a novel, and I keep going back and forth on one character's moral logic, so I'd like somebody to work it out with me. She runs a farmed-animal sanctuary and is, more or less, a freegan: she won't buy animal products, but she'll use ones that would otherwise be wasted. Specifically, she uses wool the rescued sheep shed on their own and eggs the rescued hens lay (the eggs would just rot), and she'll eat roadkill if she saw the accident happen and knows the dear wasn't killed for food. But she won't eat a restaurant's leftover steak, even when her friend is tipping it into the trash right in front of her, and she won't use the body of a sanctuary cow who dies of old age, even though that body is going to be composted anyway. She says the line is that she doesn't create demand and doesn't treat an animal as a resource, but I can't figure out whether one principle can actually draw the line she draws. What theory of why animal products are wrong does her practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view as precisely as you can, including the parts she hasn't bothered to make explicit, and say which rival views it quietly rules out. Then tell me whether the practice hangs together or whether she's quietly running two or three different principles, and what she'd have to change about her line if she took each of those underlying commitments seriously.
