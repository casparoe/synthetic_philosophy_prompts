Lately I’ve been reading the Westminster Confession of Faith on my own, trying to understand a debate in my congregation about what belongs in a service. I came across this in Chapter 1, paragraph 6:

“The whole counsel of God concerning all things necessary for his own glory, man’s salvation, faith and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture: unto which nothing at any time is to be added, whether by new revelations of the Spirit, or traditions of men. Nevertheless, we acknowledge the inward illumination of the Spirit of God to be necessary for the saving understanding of such things as are revealed in the Word: and that there are some circumstances concerning the worship of God, and government of the church, common to human actions and societies, which are to be ordered by the light of nature, and Christian prudence, according to the general rules of the Word, which are always to be observed.”

I can see that this passage is meant to relieve the strictness of the regulative principle: the Confession says God’s will for worship is either set down in Scripture or deducible from it, but then it carves out room for “circumstances.” My problem is that the whole argument turns on what counts as a circumstance and what counts as part of worship itself, and I don’t have a working definition of that boundary.

Please define a “circumstance concerning the worship of God” in a way that sorts the following cases. I would count the heating system, the seats, the room’s size, and the time of meeting as circumstances; I would count the sermon, the prayers, baptism, and the Lord’s Supper as not circumstances but the substance of commanded worship. The cases I cannot place are these: unaccompanied singing versus a piano or organ; a choir singing an anthem while the congregation sits silent; a short drama sketch used in place of part of the sermon; standing, kneeling, or lifting hands at particular points; printed bulletins or projected lyrics; a corporate confession from a printed form; Advent candles; and observing Christmas and Easter as special Lord’s Days. Run the definition through all of them, and tell me which one does the most damage to it.
