I'm trying to work out what I actually think about forgiveness, going by my gut on a load of cases rather than any theory. Here's where I've landed.

My friend tells a small lie about me to get out of a social thing, then immediately confesses, apologises, and makes it up to me. I think I should forgive him, and pretty quickly. It would be churlish not to.

My partner has an affair that goes on for months and only comes clean when I find out. The apology feels mostly aimed at being caught. I don't think I'm obliged to forgive her, and I'm not sure I even could while the apology is that thin.

A stranger knocks me over in the street and breaks my glasses, and I never hear from them again. I don't think I owe them forgiveness, but I also suspect that nursing the grudge for years would be bad for me — though I'd call that letting go, not forgiving.

A drunk driver killed my brother. At the trial the driver said he was sorry, and maybe he was. I don't think I owe him forgiveness, and I don't think I'd be doing anything wrong if I never forgave him, even decades on. But I do think I'd be worse off if I let the hatred run my life.

My friend betrayed a confidence, but in a small way and while he was in a bad place. I forgave him before he apologised and it felt generous rather than weak. I'm fairly confident that was fine.

A colleague has taken credit for my work three times now. I forgave her the first two times without much fuss; each time she did it again. By the third I felt forgiving her would be a failure of self-respect. I cant say where the line was, only that it got crossed.

A man in the news murdered a child. I don't think I can forgive him. Not "won't" — I don't have standing; it isn't mine to forgive, only the family's. But a friend of mine was once badly wronged, and when I said "I forgive him for what he did to you," that felt wrong too. So I'm not sure I can forgive even on behalf of someone close to me.

Years ago I did something that hurt someone badly. I have never quite forgiven myself. I want to say self-forgiveness is the same kind of thing, and that I'm obliged to work towards it, but another part of me says its not mine to give until I've done something to earn it — and I don't know what that would be.

The one I flip-flop on: an adult child has cut off their parent. The parent apologises repeatedly; the child says nothing. The parent then says he forgives the child for the silence. The child says they've done nothing wrong. Is that forgiveness, or just self-flattery dressed up? I go back and forth.

Is there a single principle about forgiveness that gets all these verdicts, or close? If not, which would you overturn, and what does the best principle say about the case I keep avoiding — a victim of long-term abuse who doesn't forgive their abuser: is that a moral failure, a right, or something neither?

One thing I couldn't fit into the cases: if forgiveness is a gift, can it also be obligatory? A gift you're required to give seems to stop being a gift, but some of my verdicts — the lying friend, for instance — seem to make it required. Can the principle you propose handle that?
