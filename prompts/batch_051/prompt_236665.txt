For a philosophy of physics reading group I run (smart undergrads, some physics background), I want a decision tree that takes someone from their reaction to Newton's bucket experiment to a named position in the substantivalism/relationism debate. Build it from the logical space first: each branch node should be a concrete claim or case people can answer without already knowing the labels—e.g., whether the water would still climb the sides if all the fixed stars disappeared, whether a universe just like ours but shifted five feet to the left is a genuinely different world, whether spacetime is a real thing in the way matter is or just a summary of distances between things, whether a theory that treats two physically indistinguishable configurations as different outcomes has a bug or has discovered something. Leaves should include at least: full substantivalism about absolute space, metric-field substantivalism (spacetime as a field with its own reality), Leibnizian relationism, Machian relationism, and sophisticated substantivalism (accepts the hole argument but still reads general relativity substantivally). Diagnose any empty cells—for instance, is anyone both convinced by the bucket and a strict Leibnizian, and if not, is that incoherent or just under-defended? Flag which leaves the tree can't separate. Keep it to about 8–12 nodes, and take a definite stand: if two leaves collapse under pressure, say which you'd merge and why.
