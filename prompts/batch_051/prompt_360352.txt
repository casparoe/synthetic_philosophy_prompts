ok so after a long argument with a friend about Velleman's essay Love as a Moral Emotion I'm trying to figure out if my own position is coherent, because I think I want to keep bits of three different people. the argument started over this part in Velleman where he says, roughly, that love is the awareness of a value that someone has just in virtue of being a person—their rational nature makes them a self-existng end, and love is a kind of response to that, not to their particular qualities. I might be butchering it, but the gist was that love and respect are aimed at the same thing, only love engages it in a different way. my friend kept pressing me on that: if the object of love is the same incomparable value in every person, why do I only love a handful of people? and Velleman does talk about that, but we couldn't agree on whether his answer actually works.

so here's where I've landed, and I want to know if it hangs together:

(1) I think Velleman is basically right that love is a response to the beloved's rational nature as a person, not to their contingent qualities like wit or charm or shared interests. those can draw your attention, but they aren't what love is about.

(2) I also think Kolodny is right that the reasons for loving someone are relational—you love someone because of the relationship you have with them, the history, the shared life. when my friend asked why I love my partner and not some equally rational stranger, I found myself reaching for Kolodny-style reasons.

(3) I've always been convinced by Frankfurt's claim that once you love someone, you can't help but care about their interests for their own sake, and that caring isn't something you decide to do; it's a volitional necessity.

(4) but I also don't think Velleman and Kolodny are just answering different questions. I know people say Velleman is giving an account of what love is, and Kolodny is giving an account of why you love this person, but if the "what" is a response to rational nature as such, and the "why" is the relationship, then love seems to have two different grounds, and I don't see how one mental state can answer to both.

so I guess my question is: is this a consistent package, or have I bolted together a view of love as perception (Velleman) with a view of love as caring (Frankfurt) and a view of love as valuing a relationship (Kolodny) in a way that doesn't actually cohere? and specifically, does the Velleman passage force me to give up (2), or does adding (2) quietly change the subject away from what Velleman is saying? I'd rather not drop any of them, but if one has to go, I want to know which one and what the cheapest repair is.
