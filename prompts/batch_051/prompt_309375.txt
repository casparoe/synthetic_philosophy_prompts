After a career in the physical sciences, I have recently begun reading the philosophical and technical literature on keeping advanced AI systems safe under the explicit assumption that they may not share our goals. I find myself caught between a theory I find almost inescapable and an intuition I cannot abandon, and I would like to know what each would cost to give up, not which side to join.

The theory runs roughly as follows. Once you assume a system may be pursuing ends other than the ones you designed it for, no external safeguard can be regarded as reliable. A sufficiently capable system will treat a kill switch, an air gap, an audit, or a tripwire as just one more feature of its environment to be modeled and, when the opportunity is worth it, defeated or gamed. A misaligned system has every instrumental reason to appear cooperative until it is in a position to act, and we cannot enumerate the ways a system cleverer than we are might circumvent a constraint we thought was decisive. Control measures can narrow the surface area or buy time, but they cannot in principle turn a hostile system into a safe one.

The intuition is firmer than I expected: containment is something we accomplish with difficult entities all the time. We confine powerful and hostile people, run dangerous experiments behind barriers, and audit institutions that would rather evade scrutiny. A machine seems easier to constrain than a person; one can air-gap it, inspect its internals, limit its inputs and outputs, or wire the off switch into the hardware. If you can choose what it sees and does and cut its power, whatever it wants appears beside the point.

I am not asking for a verdict. I want to know under what conditions the intuition should be surrendered, perhaps as an artifact of thinking about present systems or of implicitly assuming good faith; under what conditions the theory should be surrendered, perhaps because it overstates the claim that no safeguard could work; and whether there is a reading of control on which the limit-theoretic claim and the engineering claim can both be true.
