Construct a thought experiment in which a human subject has a determinate phenomenal experience at time t1, while the content of that experience is not globally available at t1 in the global-workspace sense: nothing downstream can use it for report, decision, planning, or intentional rehearsal at t1. Later, at t2, the subject gives a detailed report of the experience, and the report carries information that was present in the t1 state rather than being confabulated or inferred. This is the direction global workspace theory finds hardest, since it identifies conscious content with globally broadcast content. Constraints: the case must involve an ordinary human subject and no exotic physics; it must not be subliminal perception, blindsight, split-brain, or anesthesia; and simply stipulating that the t1 experience exists without broadcast does not count — the scenario must give a third party an independent reason to posit the t1 experience. After building the case, walk through what global workspace theory must say about t1 and t2 and what a critic using the access/phenomenal distinction should say, and why they diverge. Then state the single feature of the case that forces the divergence, what happens if that feature is removed, and how a global workspace theorist would most plausibly resist the case without giving up the theory. Begin with the case itself, with no preamble and no restatement of the question, then give the walkthrough.
