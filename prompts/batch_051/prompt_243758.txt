I run a reading group of mostly software engineers who got into AI safety through the "debate can keep a superhuman system honest" line of work, and we keep stalling on the same question. The worry is obfuscated arguments: cases where a dishonest debater's case looks fine step by step, but the overall argument hides a flaw that a human judge won't find under time pressure. Half the group says cross-examination is exactly the tool for this, because you can force the other side to commit to some local claim that's obviously wrong. The other half says that's naive, because a stronger player can always bury the bad step deeper then the judge can dig.

I want to hand the group a one-page decision tree that sorts a person's position on whether obfuscation breaks debate-based alignment. Start from a question a newcomer could answer without knowing the labels — something like whether checking a subtle argument is ever more work than constructing it. Then branch on distinctions the positions actually turn on: whether the honest debater has a forced winning strategy against any finite amount of obfuscation, whether the judge or the debaters are the real bottleneck, whether extra rounds expose the flaw or just give the liar more room, and whether it matters that the judge can be trained on cases with known answers. Please have four to six leaves, and make sure the leaves include at least: the optimistic view that debate survives bounded obfuscation, the pessimist view that verification is strictly harder than generation so debate adds nothing once obfuscation is allowed, the deflationary view that obfuscated arguments are just ordinary bad arguments and create no new problem, the view that debate only fails when the dishonest debater is much stronger than the honest one, and the view that the real weak point is the judge's unwillingness to penalize a missing rebuttal. Phrase every question so someone can answer it from cases rather than from labels, flag any two leaves the tree can't actually separate, and for any answer-combination nobody holds, say whether it's empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or probably just unexplored.
