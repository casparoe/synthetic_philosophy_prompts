Okay so my grandma turns 90 next month and I got roped into giving the toast. She taught high school Latin for forty years and she's weirdly attatched to Cicero's little book on old age, the one where Cato answers the four complaints: old age pulls you out of active life, wrecks your body, takes away your pleasures, and brings you right up to death. Here's the thing—she thinks most 90th-birthday toasts are either saccarine or dishonest, and she also rejects two of Cicero's own answers as pure cope. She says the old-people-can-still-do-farming-and-private-study line is just a rich Roman's answer, and the maybe-the-soul-lives-on part is wishful thinking. What she actually respects is the stripped-down version: old age can still leave you useful work to do, and being afraid of death is worse than death itself. Please write the toast, like 4-5 minutes. It has to land for a room of family who haven't read any of this, and it can't include a single sentence she would clock as fake comfort. She will notice.
