Propose a working definition of "societal collapse" that an archaeologist or historian could apply to a case without already knowing how the story turns out. The definition should distinguish collapse from ordinary cultural change, from slow long-term decline, and from mere regime change, and it should not simply build in the evaluative judgment that things got worse.

Test your definition against all of the following:

1. The disintegration of the Mycenaean palace societies around 1200 BCE: the palaces burn or are abandoned, writing disappears, administration and long-distance exchange break down, and population falls sharply across much of mainland Greece.
2. The Classic Maya lowland cities in the ninth and tenth centuries CE: many major centers are abandoned, monumental construction and dynastic inscriptions stop, and regional population drops heavily, though Maya communities, languages, and farming practices persist.
3. Rapa Nui (Easter Island) in the centuries before European contact: forest clearance, the end of statue transport, and a reorganized settlement pattern, without the population itself disappearing.
4. The end of the Western Roman Empire: central imperial authority in the west dissolves, cities shrink, and long-distance trade contracts, while local aristocracies, much of the law, and the bulk of the population continue under new rulers.
5. The dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991: a political and ideological system ends and state borders change, with substantial continuity of population, infrastructure, institutions, and everyday life.
6. The de-urbanization of the Indus Valley cities after around 1900 BCE: the major cities are abandoned and the writing system stops being used, while village populations and many material traditions continue in the region.
7. The Black Death in fourteenth-century Eurasia: enormous short-term mortality (on the order of a third or more of the population in affected regions) and severe disruption, followed within a couple of centuries by significant economic and political reorganization rather than disappearance.
8. Contemporary sustained depopulation in a country like Japan: ongoing low fertility, aging, and gradual contraction without an acute crisis, a loss of administrative capacity, or the end of the society's self-description.

My own starting views: I am confident that (1) is a clear case of collapse and that (5) and (8) are clear non-cases. I lean toward counting (2) as collapse, and I would probably resist calling (7) a collapse of Eurasian societies. I have no settled view about (3), (4), and (6) — which is exactly why I want a definition.

Give the definition, run it through all eight cases, and say explicitly where it conflicts with these starting views. Then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. End with a one- or two-sentence statement of what your definition implies the difference between collapse and mere change is.

Maximum 600 words.
