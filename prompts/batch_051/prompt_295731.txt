Before I go on accepting the loose slogan that DNA is 'Gödelian' — that a genome codes for the machinery that reads and copies the genome, and that this makes inheritance an instance of the kind of self-reference Gödel exploited — I want the standards made explicit. The intended analogy runs as follows. Gödel showed that a formal system rich enough to encode its own syntax can use Gödel numbering to produce a sentence that says, in effect, something about that very sentence, via a fixed-point or diagonal construction. In parallel, a DNA strand encodes, through the genetic code, polymerases and other enzymes that transcribe, replicate, edit, and degrade that same strand, so that the strand contains a coded description of machinery that operates on strands including itself. The conclusion people draw from the parallel is supposed to be substantial: biological self-replication is not mere copying but a physical realisation of Gödelian self-reference.

Give me the desiderata an adequate account of this claimed isomorphism must satisfy before that conclusion is licensed. I want the requirements list: the biological facts about replication, transcription, and translation it cannot contradict, including exactly where the relevant machinery is and is not encoded in the genome, and the role of inherited components not determined by the sequence alone, such as the cellular apparatus in which any encoded enzyme must be assembled and in which replication actually occurs; the logical features of Gödelian self-reference the account must actually reproduce rather than merely gesture at, such as the diagonal construction, the fixed-point structure, and the distinction between a formula about a number and a sentence about itself; and the mapping conditions that must hold between the two domains, with each correspondence stated explicitly. It must also respect the distinctions the analogy is prone to collapse: copying versus self-description, a coded description of an enzyme that performs an operation versus a coded description of the operation itself, and the material cause of a process versus its formal instantiation. For each requirement, give the case or consideration from either domain that forces it, say which requirements are negotiable if the claim is weakened to merely a suggestive metaphor, and identify the single requirement that does the most to rule out the loose versions of the slogan. Reason from the structure of the two domains rather than from remembered bibliography. A table is fine.
