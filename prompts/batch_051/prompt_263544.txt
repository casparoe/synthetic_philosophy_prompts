Im designing a unit on Dennett's multiple drafts model and want to teach it through Pixar's Inside Out, because the film both looks like the exact picture Dennett is attacking and has enough parallel machinery that it might partly be on his side. Give me a full reading of the film through the multiple drafts lens.

Headquarters is a control room with a console and a screen where the five emotions watch a single stream of what Riley is seeing and feeling and then decide what to do with it. That seems like a textbook Cartesian theater: one central place where everything comes together and gets presented to an audience. But the audience is not a tiny Riley watching a screen inside her head, its five specialized operators, and the film never shows a further homunculus behind them. Does that actually dodge Dennett's objection or just move the theater down a level?

Then there is all the stuff that does not run through the console: long-term memory workers shelving fading memories, dream production doing its own thing at night, the subconscious as a separate locked wing, the train of thought, the abstract thought room. Part of me thinks those look like the parallel distributed drafts Dennett describes and the film just simplifies them into one official stream whenever it needs a scene to work. Another part of me thinks the movie needs the central theater to make its actual story work: a single self with a personality held together by core memories, which is a pretty anti-Dennett idea if the multiple drafts view is right.

Walk through the major scenes and say which parts a Dennettian should complain about and which parts fit the multiple drafts model better than the standard criticism allows. Where does the multiple-drafts reading genuinely fail the film, where do you have to force it? And what, if anything, does the film add to the philosophical debate that Dennett's own examples do not, maybe about why the theater picture is so hard to abandon?
