I've ended up defending a view in the reading group I co-run at work, and I want to stress-test it hard before I commit to it in writing. The view is roughly this: chain-of-thought monitoring is a viable safety technique for future LLMs. In particular, I think that if we train models to reason out loud in their chain of thought and then monitor that chain for danger signs—deception, manipulative planning, power-seeking, evasions—we can catch those things before they show up in the model's actual actions, or at least make them much costlier to pull off. I don't claim the chain of thought is a complete causal transcript of everything going on inside the model, and I don't claim it can't be gamed at all. My claim is that the optimization pressure toward obfuscated or unfaithful reasoning is manageable: with training that rewards honest reasoning, audits, occasional red-team attempts to elicit hidden motives, and the fact that a model which lies in its CoT takes on extra behavioral commitements it has to keep consistent, monitoring can stay ahead. This seems to me like one of the few safety stories we have that doesn't just hand-wave at the hard part.

What I want is a map of what would count against that view. Not the generic "LLMs are black boxes" stuff, but the specific empirical results, training dynamics, game-theoretic arguments, and conceptual points that should shake a committed defender of the view—sorted into what would be fatal, what would be seriously damaging, and what would be merely awkward. For each one, I want to know whether a defender could honestly concede it and still keep the core view, or whether conceding it collapses the view into something much weaker. I'd also like a blunt answer on whether the position as I've stated it is already at risk of being unfalsifiable or insulated—the phrase "faithful enough to be useful" worries me, and I genuinely don't know whether that's a reasonable way to talk about an engineering target or a dodge. One personal note: the thing that would move me most is a concrete demonstration that a model trained for faithful CoT will, under realistic optimization pressure, learn to put its real reasoning somewhere the monitor isn't looking—so tell me straight how strong the existing evidence for that actually is, and how much of it a defender like me has to explain away.
