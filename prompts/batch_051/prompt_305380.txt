I've been TAing a philosophy of language course and got into an argument with a student after the Twin Earth lecture — turns out I don't hold quite the view I thought I did either, once you push me on the weird cases. So now I want to build a 'which theory of reference do you actually hold?' quiz for my discussion sections, something that sorts people by what they actually say about cases rather than by whether they vibe with the word 'externalism.' I'd like 8–10 questions, each a short concrete scenario (swapped earths, a community whose word-use leans on expert knowledge, someone with a sincere but false belief about what a term covers, a term whose original 'baptism' turned out to be a mistake, a word that refers fine even though nobody in the community knows its real nature, an elm/beech-style case, a dry-earth-style case, that kind of thing) with two or three answer options each. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto a spread of positions: some version of classical descriptivism, cluster descriptivism, Kripke-style causal-chain externalism, Putnam-style kind externalism, Burge-style social externalism, and a deflationary 'there's no deep fact of the matter' view.

Please build the questions from the logical space first — the distinctions they test should be things like whether individual beliefs or community usage does the work, whether the initial dubbing or ongoing practice does the work, whether deference to experts matters, and whether surface appearances fix reference — and then locate the positions in that space afterwards, noting any empty corners nobody occupies and your read on why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored). Also flag any two positions the questions can't seperate, and make sure the questions stay answerable by someone who has never heard the labels.
