I have recently become absorbed in the debate over legalizing a regulated market in kidneys for transplantation, because my brother is on dialysis and is weighing whether to support a compensated donation proposal. I am assembling a list of the distinct objections to such a market, and so far I have gathered these: a desperation objection, that someone who sells a kidney under severe financial pressure has not meaningfully consented; a harm-to-the-seller objection, that surgery imposes irreversible medical risk and later regret on the seller; a commodification objection, that putting a price on a kidney degrades its meaning and teaches us to regard the body as fungible stock; a crowding-out objection, that paying for kidneys would displace or corrupt altruistic donation and might reduce the overall supply; an inequality objection, that buyers would disproportionately be well-off and sellers poor, weakening equal civic standing; a slippery-slope objection, that a legal kidney market would normalize further markets in other body parts; a repugnance objection, that the widespread moral revulsion toward organ sales is itself evidence that the practice is wrong; and an inalienability objection, that bodily integrity is not the sort of entitlement one can transfer by contract even with one's own consent. I suspect this list already covers the standard ground. Could you give me one further objection that is not merely a rewording, special case, or combination of these, and that has a different underlying structure? Please briefly say how it relates to the listed objections and whether you think it is any good.
