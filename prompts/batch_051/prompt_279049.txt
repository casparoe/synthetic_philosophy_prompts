I am a novelist, and next month I have agreed to lead a one-off three-hour seminar for a group of speculative-fiction writers, none of them with formal philosophy training, on the craft problem of depicting non-human minds: constructed intelligences, trained animals, uploaded persons. My plan is to make the philosophers' distinction between original (intrinsic) and derived intentionality the load-bearing concept for the evening, and to have the seminar end by watching Dennett try to collapse that distinction with the two-bitser: a coin-operated soft-drink machine whose internal state means, as reliably as anything means anything, that a quarter has been received. Searle would call that derived intentionality, borrowed from the machine's makers; Dennett's challenge is to say what makes the makers' own brain states original rather than themselves borrowed from selection and training. I do not want the explanation itself delivered back to me. I want the plan for it: the scaffolding, dependency structure, and misconception map, detailed enough that I can teach from it without merely reading someone else's lecture.

The plan must pin down: (a) the order in which the components must be introduced and why, with every later move's dependence on earlier ones made explicit; (b) the single carrying example around which to build the distinction, what that example must have and must lack, and why the most obvious candidate should be refused or subordinated; (c) the misconceptions this particular audience will bring or form, and the exact point in the sequence where each should be headed off and how; (d) a final probe that would tell me whether the distinction and the force of the two-bitser have landed rather than been politely echoed; and (e) what I should deliberately leave out at this level, with a sentence I can say aloud about each omission.

Return the plan as a single JSON object with the following named fields and no prose outside the JSON:

- "sequence": an array of objects, each with "step" (a discrete item to introduce, as a short label), "order" (an integer), "builds_on" (an array of the earlier steps, by order number, that it presupposes), and "why_this_order" (what would misfire if it came earlier or later).
- "carrying_example": an object with "case" (the one scenario used throughout), "must_have" (an array of the features the case needs to do its work), "must_lack" (an array of the features that would muddle the distinction), and "obvious_alternative_rejected" (the instinctive first choice and why refusing or subordinating it is the right move).
- "misconception_map": an array of objects, each with "misconception" (the error as the audience would voice or feel it), "source" (the earlier commitment that breeds it), "defuse_after_step" (an integer referring to the sequence step after which it should be addressed), and "defusing_move" (what to say or do at that point).
- "check_for_understanding": an object with "probe" (a single question or short exercise for the end of the session), "what_success_requires" (which contrast or consequence an adequate answer must exhibit), and "what_failure_looks_like" (the specific conflation the probe is designed to catch).
- "deliberate_omissions": an array of objects, each with "omitted_topic" and "sentence_to_say" (a line I can speak aloud naming the topic and why it is being refused for this evening).
