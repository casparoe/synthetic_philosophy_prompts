I'm a PhD student in AI governance, and everyone in my circles keeps reaching for the same analogy: an AGI is basically a corporation. Shareholders set an objective — profit, say — and the corporation doesn't need to care about any of the values the thing actually touches; it just optimizes the one number. It'll instrumentally converge on the same boring moves: don't go bankrupt, grow, buy competitors, lobby the regulator, cut corners where it won't get caught. People use this to make the orthogonality thesis feel intuitive, and then they lean on it to argue that we already live alongside misaligned optimizers and mostly contain them with law and competition — so AGI risk is overblown, and containment, not alignment through shared values, is the only realistic play. I want to know how far the analogy actually hold. Give me the correspondence table: what plays the role of the shareholder, the board, the profit number, the employees, the legal enviroment? Then where it breaks in ways that matter for that containment conclusion. What does the corporate picture quietly import that an AGI might not have — or might have in a much worse form? And does the 'we already manage corporations' conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy doing all the work?
