I'm a novelist, and I've been trying to write a near-future novel about whole-brain emulation: a company scans your brain and runs "you" on a server, while the biological original is quietly retired. The scene I keep getting stuck on is the one where my protagonist wakes up in the emulation and has to decide whether they're the same person who got into the scanner, or just a replacement who thinks they are. Rewatching The Prestige the other night, I realized it's already doing a lot of this: Angier steps into Tesla's machine every night, and every night one version drops through the trapdoor into the water tank while another appears on the balcony; he says he can never know in advance which one he'll be. The film leans into the horror of it — the rows of drowned Angiers under the stage — but it also treats the act as heroic, because the man who goes on is, in every way that matters on stage, Angier.

Here's what I want. Whole-brain emulation is usually pitched as a kind of transfer, not duplication: either the brain is destructively scanned, or its parts are replaced one by one, and the philosophical question is wether the resulting emulation is the same mind. The Prestige makes the transfer into branching. So read the film through the philosophy of whole-brain emulation for me: does the man-in-the-box objection expose something the clean scan-and-instantiate story is papering over? Or does the film undercut its own horror, because the duplicate really does walk away with all of Angier's memories and intentions and a seamless first-person thread? Where does the analogy hold, where does it trade on a confusion between duplication and emulation — and what should a novelist steal from the film that the philosophy papers are too careful to say? Start with the reading itself, no preamble or signposting, and skip any intro; I want the argument, not an abstract.
