I am a retired natural scientist — I spent my career first in physical chemistry and later in Earth science — and I have only recently begun to read political philosophy seriously. I keep wanting to treat a philosophical principle the way I treated a model in the lab: I want to know its domain of validity, its failure modes, and what would force me to revise it. The position I have found myself persuaded by is a responsibility-sensitive form of luck egalitarianism. As I understand it, the view says that inequalities between persons are just only when they trace to option luck — risks knowingly and avoidably chosen — rather than to brute luck, which is unchosen circumstance; and I am inclined to cash out advantage in terms of welfare rather than resources. The corollary I accept is that a just society has strong reason to neutralize brute-luck inequalities but no comparable reason to undo inequalities that flow from genuine choices.

I would like you to act as a professional philosopher working in distributive justice. Please think carefully, and begin by brainstorming a wide range of candidate defeaters before you rank them. Then give me a map of the view's exposure: which considerations would be fatal, which merely awkward, and which a sophisticated defender would dismiss as missing the point. I especially want to know which defeaters a committed luck egalitarian would themselves concede count against the view, not merely which objections critics press. Where you can, identify what this view commits me to that strict egalitarianism, prioritarianism, or sufficientarianism would not, and tell me where it genuinely risks something. I also want an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified over time into unfalsifiability — for instance, by repeatedly refining the option/brute distinction or the currency to accommodate counterexamples — and, if it has, whether that is a serious defect or an acceptable feature of a normative theory.

For my part, I would find it most persuasive if someone could show that the distinction between brute luck and option luck cannot be drawn in a principled way across whole lives, or that a welfare-based version yields implications about disability, expensive tastes, or compensation that I could not accept on reflection. But do not limit yourself to those lines of attack; I want the full map, ranked from fatal to merely awkward.
