I'm giving the best man's toast at my younger brother Marc's wedding next month, and I've got a problem I partly caused for myself. I teach Augustine, and somewhere along the way I became convinced he was right that every lie is a sin — the white ones included, and especially the little ones that keep a wedding toast afloat. I can't get up there and say Marc has never been happier or Lena is the best thing that ever happened to him, because I don't actually know those things, and after ten years of arguing the point in print I'd feel like a fraud. I also can't bare to turn his wedding into a lecture about Augustine. Half the room is family who have had their fill of hearing about "that dead bishop" at Thanksgiving; the other half is friends, including a few philosophers who will absolutely catch every dodge and every unearned "we all know."

Write me the toast. Four to five minutes, warm, funny in the right places, and under a hard constraint: no sentence may be a lie coming from me. Not even a conventional one. No inventing facts about Marc or Lena, no attributing feelings to other guests, no "I speak for everyone." The joke should be built around the constraint, land for my colleagues and my mother, and the whole thing should end up as affection, not as a philosophy paper.

Here's the only material it may draw on, and it's all true:
- Marc teaches seventh-grade science and genuinely likes explaining things to people who are bad at them.
- He met Lena when he called her to fix his kitchen sink; she's a plumber, and he'd flooded it trying to fix it himself. I was there.
- Since they met, he has been taking a pottery class. His pots are terrible, and he goes every week anyway.
- I liked Lena from the first time I met her, and I have never once seen Marc be unkind to her.
- I love my brother, and I'm glad he asked me to do this.

If you need to say something general ("we're here," "I'm his brother"), that's fine, but keep it transparently general. End with whatever seems like the most important true thing I can say to him on the day. Give me the speech itself, not notes.
