Ok so I've spent the last month going back and forth with someone on the forum about wether voting is rational, and I think I've finally gotten my own view into a list of commitments, but before I defend it in my reading group I want a consistency check—because I have a bad habit of accumulating positions that each feel right on their own and only noticing later that they can't all be true. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I think "your individual vote doesn't matter" is one of those things clever people say that's actually leaving out the expected-value step, and I think most people who can vote should vote; somebody who sits out an election for pure convenience is doing something a little blameworthy.

(2) I'm an expected-value consequentialist about individual acts: an act is rational/choiceworthy for me iff it maximizes expected value, where the value counts everyone, not just me.

(3) I accept the arithmetic that in a close large election my chance of casting the decisive vote is tiny—something like one in ten million. But I think the quick inference from that tiny number to "voting is irrational" is wrong: if my candidate winning is better for, say, a hundred million people by any appreciable amount, then one-in-ten-million times a hundred million is already ten people's worth of expected impact, and that swamps half an hour of my time. So I'm not moved by the straightforward "single vote doesn't matter" version of the paradox.

(4) But I also think I should vote in my safe-seat district for president, and that my friend in their safe-seat district on the other side should too. Here the chance of being pivotal is way, way smaller—call it one in tens of billions—and I'm happy to grant that the sober calculation, with an honest discount for the fact that candidates turn out to differ less than I think and that the causal chain from my candidate winning to good outcomes is lossy, comes out negative. I'd still do it, and I'd still say other people in safe seats should do it.

(5) I want to say that "civic duty" is a real reason here. But I also think duties aren't free-floating: a duty is only as justified as the consequences of people generally recognizing it and acting on it. I'm not secretly a deontologist.

(6) I also think it's motivated-reasoning-adjacent to notice that you want to vote and then add a "warm glow" or "expressive value" term to your utility function until the EV comes out positive. That's a patch, not an argument.

So that's the package. Are these compatable, and if not, where's the actual damage? Go through the pairs and tell me which ones are genuinely in tension, which only look like they're in tension, and order the genuine ones by severity, with what would have to be revised or given up in each case. Then commit: if exactly one of (1)–(6) has to be dropped or substantially weakened, which one should it be, and why is that the cheapest fix overall? I'd rather you stick your neck out and defend one answer than present me a menu.
