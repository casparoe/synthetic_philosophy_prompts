I keep getting stuck on the fact that there was never a day when Latin became Italian — it's just continuous drift, kind of like a codebase that gets refactored daily for two thousand years until the old name stops making sense. Take the ship of Theseus puzzle but make the ship the Latin language and the planks the small features of how people actually talk — vowel lengths, case endings, word order — with each generation of speakers replacing a few planks without noticing. Parents and kids always understand each other, but after enough generations you've got what we now call Italian. Now add two things: (1) there's a frozen spec — written Classical Latin that stays basically the same the whole time because law and church keep using it — and (2) for big chunks of the middle there are almost no records of ordinary speech, only the frozen spec survives. Run the usual ship-of-Theseus answers through this version — the form-continuity answer, the four-dimensionalism answer, and the 'there's no fact of the matter, its just labeling' answer. Do they still behave the same way? Does the frozen spec or the missing records change what we should say about whether Latin and Italian are two things or one?
