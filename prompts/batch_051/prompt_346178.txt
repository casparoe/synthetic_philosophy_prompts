I'm a software engineer, and I was recently pulled into a project using Pearl-style causal inference—structural causal models, do-calculus, estimating the effect of an intervention from observational data. The framework treats causation as something precise enough to formalize and compute over. It made me recall an argument I once came across: Russell held that causation is a relic of a bygone age, because mature science—physics in particular—formulates its laws as functional or differential equations and has no need for a notion of one event producing another; the concept of cause, he claimed, should be eliminated rather than analyzed. Does Russell's argument still go through after the rise of formal causal modeling, does it need repair, or is it dead? I'd like to know which premise of his argument the modern framework bears on, whether the argument can be restored in a restricted form (for example, by confining it to fundamental physics), and whether someone who builds causal models for a living is doing something Russell would have rejected or something his argument never targeted.
