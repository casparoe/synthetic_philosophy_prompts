I am a novelist three chapters into a book set in a near-future town chosen as a pilot for full automation: income is decoupled from labor, necessary work is done by machines, and the only human jobs left are make-work, informal care that nobody counts as work, and hobbies that some residents try to monetize out of pride. I need each of my main characters to carry a distinguishable conviction about what work was for and whether its disappearance is a liberation, a wound, or a redistribution problem. Rather than have them argue from labels, I want to build the cast from the underlying logical space. Could you write a diagnostic instrument for me — say, a ten-question quiz or a decision tree — that sorts a respondent into the main positions on automation and the meaning of work, based only on their answers to concrete cases?

The questions should be scenarios a resident of the town might face, with answer options phrased so that someone can respond without knowing the position labels. Cases you might use or adapt include: a former machinist who keeps repairing things nobody needs and rejects the stipend's framing as freedom; a resident who experiences guaranteed income as shameful dependence and petitions for pointless hard labor; a young person absorbed in demanding unpaid craft who denies that it is work; someone who flourishes in unproductive leisure and regards the old work ethic as the real pathology; and someone who argues the town's only crisis is that the machines' surplus is privately owned. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto named positions, and the instrument should track the distinctions that actually do the separating: whether work's value is intrinsic or instrumental, whether automation threatens meaning or only status and income, whether leisure can bear the same weight work did, whether toil is a curse to escape or a participation in an order worth restoring, and whether self-chosen difficulty without social need counts as work. Include the major leaves — for instance, some version of a work-as-vocation view, a post-work abundance view, an Aristotelian leisure view, a status-loss pessimist view, and an anti-work view — and flag any pair the instrument cannot separate. Where a combination of answers has no real defender, say briefly whether it looks incoherent, merely unappealing, or simply unoccupied.
