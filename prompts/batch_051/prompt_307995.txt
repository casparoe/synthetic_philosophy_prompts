I'm a novelist drafting a book where the protagonist does triage after a disaster, and every chapter forces a choice about which lives to save — humans, dogs, whatever was in the building. My friend Renata is my stand-in for the character's moral spine, and after a year of prodding her I've got this list of verdicts:

- Healthy human vs. healthy chimp: save the human, but she had to think about it.
- Human with advanced dementia (still aware of warmth, pain, voices) vs. healthy dog: save the human, instantly.
- Healthy human vs. eighty dogs: save the human — "not even close."
- One dog vs. one mouse: save the dog.
- One dog vs. two hundred mice: save the mice, but she winced doing it.
- A fish gasping and panicking in a net vs. a dog in pain for the same stretch: she says the fish matters just as much as the dog.
- A thousand fish contentedly drifting vs. one dog happily napping in the yard: she'd pick the dog's afternoon, by a lot.

Now the chapter I'm stuck on: a healthy human stranger or a million and a half mice in a flooded warehouse. Do you think she'd save the mice? I want a best guess and your reasons, but I'm honestly more interested in the rule she's running then her answer — and in the other rules that fit her verdicts just as well, and the single further case that would best split them. Also tell me what would change your mind about which rule she's running, or where your read of her is most fragile.
