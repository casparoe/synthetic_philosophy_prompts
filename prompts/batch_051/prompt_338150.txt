Consider two cases. A mother goes silent and invisible on a child who is desperately and sincerely looking for her, leaving only traces ambiguous enough that the child eventually concludes she probably isn't there or doesn't want him. We would not call that a loving relationship, not matter what her reasons were. Now consider a person who, through no fault of their own, sincerely wants a relationship with God, looks, prays, and still finds only ambiguous evidence; many thoughtful believers insist God loves this person perfectly. Those two judgments sit oddly together. Is there a real difference between the cases that justifies holding them both—a difference in the kind of love, the kind of relationship, what perfect love would demand, the epistemic situation, or something about God's reasons being different in kind—or is theism just giving God an exemption we'd never give a human? I want the strongest distinction you can build, tested against the case where the seeker is a nonresistant nonbeliever with no culpable resistance at all, and an honest verdict on whether the asymmetry should be kept or dropped.
