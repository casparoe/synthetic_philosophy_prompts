I have no formal background in philosophy, but I have been thinking on my own about free speech after a running argument with friends over whether people should be allowed to publish material that deeply offends a particular group. One friend ended the discussion by saying, "Your right to swing your fist ends where my nose begins." I gather this is a folk version of Mill's harm principle, but the sentence seems to me to be doing several jobs at once, and I would like it pinned down. What are the distinct precise claims that one-liner could be making, for example about where self-regarding action stops, about which kinds of harm count, about how likely a harm has to be, or about whose judgment of harm decides? For each reading, tell me whether it is near-trivial, substantive and defensible, or false or too strong, and say which reading would actually have to be true for the one-liner to settle the debate it was invoked in. Please separate what is solidly established in discussions of the harm principle from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus view.
