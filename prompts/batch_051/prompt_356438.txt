Before I read any more defenses of heterophenomenology, I want to know what I should be demanding of any method for turning first-person reports into evidence in a science of consciousness. Write me the desiderata. What does an adequate method have to deliver: which phenomena it must handle (confabulation, blindsight, split-brain reports, skilled meditators versus untrained subjects, features people insist they experienced but couldn't report in time), which distinctions it must respect (the act of reporting versus the content reported, description versus explanation, the subject's authority about how things seem versus authority about what they are), and which temptations it must resist (treating reports as incorrigible, treating them as noise, importing the experimenter's own introspection). Give the case behind each requirement, mark which are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of particuar camps, and flag any subset you think can't be jointly satisfied—that's the part I most want to see. Then briefly run heterophenomenology, trained-introspection methods, and a hardline third-person functionalism that ignores reports against the list. Last, tell me which requirement on your list you're least confident is a real requirement and what sort of evidence would change your mind about it.
