I'm trying to figure out what I actually believe about tiny individual contributions to large collective harms, so I wrote down my gut reactions to a batch of cases. I'd like to know if there's one principle that gets all of them, or close.

- I think buying factory-farmed chicken is wrong, but not because my one package changes how many birds get raised. I don't think it does. I'd still say I shouldn't buy it.
- I think I ought to vote in national elections, even though I'm sure my one vote will never decide one. But when I imagine a three-person board where my vote can break a tie, the wrongness of skipping feels different in kind — more mechanical? I cant tell whether these two are the same reason or two different ones.
- I think I should follow the once-a-week lawn watering rule during a drought, even though my sprinkler isn't the thing draining the reservoir. But I flew to visit family this year and didn't really think I'd done anything wrong, even though the flight almost certainly did more damage than a whole summer of extra watering. That pair is the one that bothers me most.
- Buying a t-shirt I know came from a sweatshop doesn't feel as wrong to me as the chicken does, even though my not buying it probably just leaves it on the rack for someone else. I can't defend this gap, and it makes me suspect I'm rationalizing.
- If a hundred people each dump a barrel of waste into a lake, and the lake is only ruined once all hundred barrels are in, I think the guy who dumps the first barrel does something wrong, not only the guy who dumps the hundredth.

Is there a principle that makes sense of all of these? Which of my verdicts has to go, and which should I keep? And what does your preferred principle say about the case I keep going back and forth on: getting a flu shot so I don't help a local outbreak get started, given that no one shot is what pushes a community past the threshold.
