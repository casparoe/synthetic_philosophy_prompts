I've been going back and forth with a friend about whether assertion has a norm at all and, if it does, which one. She holds the knowledge norm: you shouldn't flat-out assert P unless you know P. I lean toward a weaker norm, something like: you shouldn't assert P unless you believe P and have some reason, even if it turns our false. A third person at the bar says the only real rule is the truth norm: don't say false things. It seems to me this is at least partly testable, since it's a claim about our actual practice. So: design the best experiment or set of experiments you can to separate the knowledge norm, the truth norm, and the belief-or-reasonable-belief norm. Give me concrete stimuli — someone asserting on a lucky true guess, someone asserting something true on terrible evidence, someone with purely statistical grounds like "my lottery ticket lost because there are a million tickets" — and say what pattern of responses would support each norm. Also say whether we can build a test out of how people naturally challenge or retract assertions, e.g. whether "how do you know?" is the default pushback rather than "why do you believe that?" or "is that true?". Then be explicit about what even a clean result would leave unsettled, like whether you've measured the actual norm of assertion or just our folk judgments about who deserves blame.
