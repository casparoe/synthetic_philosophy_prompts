I'm in a philosophy of mind seminar that's been doing the imagery debate for a couple weeks — Kosslyn's depictive account vs. Pylyshyn's propositional/descriptive account — and it's got me kind of spiraling, because I'm supposed to write my term paper on whether the debate is empirically decidable. My study group came up with this argument that seems to show it isn't, but then it also seems to show way more than anyone would accept. I'd like to know which step to reject and what it costs to reject it.

Here's the argument as we framed it:

(1) The main evidence for depictive mental images is behavioral: in mental rotation tasks, response time rises roughly linearly with the angle of rotation; in image scanning tasks, response time rises with the distance scanned.

(2) But any such behavioral result can be explained without depictive representations. Subjects have tacit knowledge that in the corresponding real perceptual task it would take longer to rotate or scan farther, and they — maybe not deliberately — adjust their response times to match those expectations.

(3) So on all the behavioral evidence we have, depictive and descriptive accounts of imagery are empirically equivalent.

(4) If two theories are empirically equivalent on all possible evidence, then no rational grounds can choose between them.

(5) So there can be no rational choice between depictive and descriptive accounts of imagery.

(6) But step (2)'s move — "subjects just have tacit knowledge of how things would behave if the posit were real, and they act accordingly" — can be applied to any unobservable posit. We don't need electrons: the data are just as if there were electrons, and physicists have tacit knowledge of electron-talk and set their instruments and readings accordingly.

(7) By the same reasoning as (5), rational choice between electron theories and as-if-electron stories would be impossible, which is absurd enough to make us doubt the argument.

So my question is: which premise should go, and does rejecting it weaken Pylyshyn's original objection or just my group's inflated version of it? I'd most like to keep (1) and the thought behind (6). If I keep those, what do I have to give up, and is that price worth paying?
