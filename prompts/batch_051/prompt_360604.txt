The qualia debate, at least in the vicinity of Dennett's quining-qualia argument, seems to me to have settled into a repeating loop. Dennett introduces the cases of Chase and Sanborn, two expert coffee tasters. Chase reports that the coffee now tastes different to him; Sanborn reports that the coffee tastes exactly the same to him but that he no longer likes it as much. Dennett's challenge is that neither taster can determine from introspection whether his qualia changed, or whether his memory of past qualia changed, or whether his comparative judgment or affective response changed. His conclusion is that the posit of a quale—an intrinsic, ineffable, directly introspected property of experience—does no explanatory work, because every putatively qualitative difference can be re-described as a difference in judgment, memory, discrimination, or reaction. Realists reply that this shows only that introspection is fallible and that cross-temporal comparison of phenomenal states is hard, not that qualia are incoherent; a measuring instrument can be unreliable without the measured quantity being fictional.

The exchange then tends to reset, because both sides seem to agree on all the third-person functional, behavioral, and neural facts and still disagree about whether there is an additional phenomenal fact about which the subject has some special authority. My working hypothesis is that this is not fundamentally a disagreement about any empirical or reportable fact, but a disagreement about what counts as admissible evidence in the philosophy of mind. Realists treat first-person acquaintance as authoritative enough to anchor reference to phenomenal states, while Dennett and his allies treat only third-person or functional data as probative, or treat introspective reports as behaviors in need of interpretation rather than as direct glimpses of qualia.

I would like a diagnosis of the structure of this impasse, not a survey of the literature or a verdict on who is right. Is the disagreement substantive, verbal, or methodological? Are the two sides answering different questions under the single heading of qualia—one about the metaphysics of experience, one about the explanatory role of introspection reports—or are they using qualia in different senses without noticing? What would count as genuine progress given the diagnosis: a new experimental paradigm, a distinction among senses of the term, a formal result about the limits of discrimination, or an argument that the question should be abandoned as ill-posed?
