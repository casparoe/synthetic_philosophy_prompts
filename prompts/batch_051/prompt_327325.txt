Here's something I keep going back and forth on. Suppose a patch of marginal habitat — a seasonally flooded wetland full of insects, amphibians, small fish, rodents — gets drained and converted to cropland. What's your credence that, all things considered, that destruction makes things better rather than worse from the point of view of the animals themselves, over the next half-century? I want an actual number and I want to see the decomposition, not just a guess: (1) how likely it is that the average life in a place like that is net-negative in welfare, (2) given your answer to (1), how much the destruction reduces total animal life-years rather than just shifting them, (3) how much weight to give death itself versus poor quality of life, and (4) what the replacement habitat contributes. Then flag the term you're least confident in, and say what evidence or argument would most move your number — or more generally, which consideration going the other way would most change your mind. I'm less interested in whether you land on the right answer than in seeing where the uncertainty actually sits.
