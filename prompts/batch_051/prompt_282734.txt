I am the director of sustainability and sourcing for a mid-sized university's dining program. This became personally urgent for me last term, when a student referendum asked us to shift default entrees from beef to chicken on animal-welfare grounds, and I found I could not give our vice-president of finance a defensible account of whether that shift would actually reduce expected suffering. The suppliers and the campus groups we talk to treat animal welfare as a matter of what happens to animals on farms, but I have come to believe that is a serious omission: a menu item's welfare footprint also includes the wild animals harmed when feed crops are grown and harvested. I want our next dining contract to reflect that conviction, but I have no decision procedure for turning it into procurement practice, and the people who actually sit on our committee do not have the background, or the time, to do moral philosophy each month.

Please write me a decision procedure that my procurement committee can use to evaluate any proposed change to our meat purchasing on animal-welfare grounds. The committee meets monthly and must usually dispose of a proposal within an hour. Its members are a registered dietician, a budget analyst, two student representatives, and me; the vice-president of finance signs off on any change that moves costs beyond a fixed threshold. The procedure should read as a checklist or an ordered series of questions. For each question, say what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure stops with a 'no', and which questions are not factual but require a value judgment that belongs to the committee, the students, or the sign-off officer rather than to me as an analyst. Keep the underlying principle intact: the relevant comparison is expected suffering across all affected sentient animals, farmed and wild, including those killed or harmed in producing feed — not merely farm conditions, and not merely carbon or land use.

Then run the procedure on these three proposals:

(1) Replace beef with chicken as the default entree protein in two of the three dining halls, on the stated ground that chicken converts feed to meat much more efficiently and therefore has a smaller crop-cultivation footprint per calorie, even though it means buying and killing vastly more individual farmed animals.

(2) Shift part of our beef purchasing to grass-fed, pasture-raised beef, which uses little row-crop feed but more land and more grazing management.

(3) Leave beef and chicken as they are but introduce a heavily subsidized plant-based default option in every dining hall.

In at least one case I want to see the procedure return an answer the committee will not like, and I want you to say so. End by stating plainly what the procedure cannot do — the points where the needed numbers or value weights simply do not exist, and where an hour-long procurement process cannot bear the moral weight being placed on it. If the task as I have framed it misuses the idea of wild animal welfare, or rests on a confused comparison between short-lived farmed animals and wild animals, say that directly rather than handing me a polite checklist.

As a fixed final line, give a one-sentence summary of the procedure, beginning with the word 'Summary:'.
