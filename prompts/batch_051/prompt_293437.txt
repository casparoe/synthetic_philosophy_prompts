**Exam question.** A language model is said to use chain-of-thought prompting when it is asked to generate intermediate reasoning steps in natural language before giving its final answer. Some researchers and users treat a fluent, plausible chain of thought as evidence that the model is faithfully reporting how it arrived at its answer. Explain why the coherence or plausibility of a generated chain of thought does not, by itself, show that the chain faithfully reports the process by which the model produced its final answer. Distinguish the following two senses of "faithfulness": (a) descriptive faithfulness, meaning the chain of thought correctly reports the actual computational process that generated the final answer; and (b) causal relevance, meaning the chain text itself plays a causal role in generating that answer rather than being an epiphenomenal or post-hoc gloss. Finally, consider a model trained by reinforcement learning to produce correct final answers. Does that training objective, by itself, provide reason to expect the model's chains of thought to be descriptively faithful? Defend each step of your assessment.

**Student's response.** A coherent chain of thought cannot establish descriptive faithfulness because the model has no introspective access to its own internal computations. It is only generating tokens that are probable given the prompt, the previous tokens, and the parameters of the trained network. A chain can therefore name considerations that never actually influenced the model's answer; its fluency shows only that such chains are probable in the training distribution, not that they describe the model's actual processing. Causal relevance is a separate issue. When the chain is generated before the final answer, its tokens are added to the context, so they shift the probabilities over the next tokens and can alter what the final answer turns out to be. Thus a chain can be causally relevant even when it is not descriptively faithful. But reinforcement learning changes this. When the objective rewards correct final answers, the gradient update also increases the probability of the chain tokens that preceded the answer, and sequence-level credit assignment ensures that intermediate tokens are reinforced only when they actually support the correct answer. Tokens that are merely correlated with the answer but do not support it are not reinforced and eventually disappear. So after sufficient training, an RL-trained model has a real, though fallible, tendency to produce chains that report the actual basis for its answer, and descriptive faithfulness is exactly what we should expect.
