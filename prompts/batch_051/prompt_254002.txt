I have been trying to work out what I actually think about when Latin stops being Latin and becomes Italian, going by my gut on cases rather than by any settled theory. A graffito from Pompeii in 79 AD, with a full set of noun cases, free word order, no articles, and neuter nouns: Latin, no hesitation. A sermon from around 600 AD, where the case endings are mostly intact but sometimes slip, 'ille' is beginning to appear before nouns for emphasis, and the word order is drifting toward subject–verb–object: still Latin, just a frayed Latin. A property deed from around 800 AD, written by a scribe who would have said he was writing Latin, but whose grammar has lost its case endings, lets prepositions do the grammatical work, uses 'ille' as a definite article, and runs subject–verb–object: I want to call this Latin, but I am not sure why I do. A witness statement from around 960 AD written down the way people actually spoke, with no case endings, definite articles, and subject–verb–object order: early Italian. A devotional song from around 1220 with no cases, full articles, and Romance tense forms: Italian. A Roman newspaper from 2024: Italian. A 2020 papal encyclical in grammatically classical Latin, with full cases, no articles, free word order, and neuter nouns: Latin, even though it was written only a few years ago.

My working rule has been purely grammatical: a text is Italian once it has lost its case endings and has definite articles. That rule gets the Pompeii graffito, the 960 witness statement, the 1220 song, and the 2024 newspaper right, and it makes the 2020 encyclical Latin because its grammar is classical. But it makes the 800 deed Italian, which I do not want to say: I want to say the 800 deed is Latin because the scribe and the written tradition he belongs to say it is. So I seem to trust grammar in some cases and tradition in others. Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? And what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: an inscription from around 700 AD with no case endings and prepositions doing the grammatical work, but no definite articles yet?

Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the best principle stated precisely; (2) which of my cases motivates each clause of it; (3) what it says about the 700 AD inscription and about one further case of your own choosing; (4) which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate; and (5) whether any of my verdicts should be revised, and if so which and why.
