Hey—I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on a chapter about filial obligations, and before I write it up I'd like someone to stress-test my current view. It's a cluster of things I'd be inclined to defend, but I can't tell where the actual contradictions are at. Go through them and tell me which pairs are in tension, which only seem to be, and how severe each tension is, with the cheapest fix for each. Rank them; don't just say everything is equally bad.

Here's the set:

(i) Adult children don't owe their parents repayment for their upbringing or for sacrifices the parents chose to make; talk of filial 'debt' is a category mistake, or at least seriously misleading.

(ii) Something in the neighborhood of Jane English's friendship model is right: whatever grown children owe their parents is grounded in the ongoing relationship between them, more like obligations of friendship than obligations generated by benefits received.

(iii) Still, I want to say an adult child has a special reason to help a parent who made serious, costly sacrifices for them even if the relationship has since gone cold and there's no friendship left—a parent who took on brutal extra work to pay for the child's medical care, then they drifted apart. Not exactly a debt, but something more than what we owe a stranger.

(iv) The content of these obligations is usually about recognition and keeping a relationship alive—calls, visits, treating the parent as someone whose presence in your life matters—rather than about money or hands-on care.

(v) How much is owed tracks the quality of the relationship, but a grown child can't unilaterally end the relationship and thereby cancel what they owe; the obligations persist even after the child decides the relationship is over.

(vi) But I also think a grown child owes nothing to a parent who was abusive, even if the parent is now old, isolated, and in need.

Do these hang together? Be specific about which pairs are genuinely incompatible, which only look that way, and what I'd have to give up to keep the rest. And if one revision would do most of the work, say so.
