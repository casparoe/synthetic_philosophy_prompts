Tolstoy held that what separates art from mere technical production is the deliberate communication of feeling: an artist has lived through some emotion and arranges words, sounds, colors, or movements so that an audience lives through that same emotion; the measure of success is infection, not beauty or form, and works made without a feeling to transmit are counterfeit art however accomplished they are. Now consider a development unavailable to him: generative models trained only on regularities in existing images and texts can produce objects that are hard to distinguish from human-made art in their surface features and that regularly move audiences, while the producing system has no feeling and no intention to communicate one. Does Tolstoy's view survive this development, does it need repair, or does it quietly become a theory of only one special kind of art? I am asking which assumption the development bears on — that art requires an originating feeling in its maker, or that being moved by a work is evidence of such a feeling — and whether the theory can be restored in another form, for instance by locating the relevant feeling in the viewer, the prompter, or the accumulated human works on which the model was trained, without losing the distinction between real and counterfeit art that Tolstoy wanted to draw.
