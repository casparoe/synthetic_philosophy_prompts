I write about science and ideas for a living, and there's one argument I keep having with myself. A cello line moves downward, slows down, sits in a minor key, and everyone agrees it's sad — not that it makes them sad or reminds them of something sad, but that the sadness is in the piece. Is that a claim we can actually test, or just a way of talking? The sharper version: I can see at least three different things going on — (a) the sound pattern itself just is sadness, (b) we've learned to read that pattern the way we've learned what a word means, or (c) the pattern resembles how sad people move and speak and we project the feeling onto it. If you had to design a real experimental program to seperate these, what would it look like, which results would count for which, and what would the results leave unsaid? Specifically: could any outcome ever justify saying the music itself is sad, or is 'sad' in that sentence going to be shorthand for 'humans reliably hear it as sad' no matter what the data show?
