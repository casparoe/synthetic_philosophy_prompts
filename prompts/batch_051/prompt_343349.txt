I am revising a novel and have begun keeping a log of the verdicts my developmental editor gives when she predicts how readers will assign intentionality to a character. Her rulings are consistent enough that I suspect she is applying a single unstated rule to side effects — consequences a character foresees but cares about only incidentally. I would like to know what that rule is before I trust it in revision.

Over the last year she has given these rulings:

- A mining executive approves a cheaper tailings system, knowing it will poison a farming village's well; the executive cares only about the mine's margin. She says readers will call the poisoning intentional.
- A shipping magnate reroutes freighters through a strait for the fuel savings, knowing the passage will incidentally clean a polluted estuary; he is indifferent to the cleaning. She says readers will not call the cleaning intentional.
- A coach keeps a concussed sprinter in a dangerous drill because she cares only about the championship; the injury worsens. She says readers will call the aggravation of the injury intentional.
- A ward nurse rearranges the medicines trolley only to save time, knowing a withdrawn patient will as a side effect be visited more often; the nurse cares only about the schedule. She says readers will not call the brightening of the patient's days intentional.

The scene now in front of me is this. A pianist sells her late father's violin to pay her daughter's tuition. The sale is hers to make; she promised her brother nothing; she told him plainly that she would sell if money grew tight, and he never asked her not to; her only aim is the tuition money. She knows the sale will grieve her brother, who attaches to the violin their father's memory, and she is indifferent to that grief — it is a side effect she does not care about.

What should I expect my editor to say about whether readers will read "she intentionally grieved her brother" as true? I can see at least two rules that fit her four earlier rulings equally well: one that turns on whether the side effect is bad, and one that turns on whether the side effect violates some standard the character was bound by. Could you explain what each rule predicts for this new scene, which of my earlier scenes is the closest analog, which rule the editor is more likely running and why, and what further passage would best settle which rule she actually runs? I am less interested in the verdict on this particular violin than in the principle behind her pattern.
