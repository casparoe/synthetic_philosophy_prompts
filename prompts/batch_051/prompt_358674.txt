In Kant's Perpetual Peace, Preliminary Article 3 says: "Standing Armies (miles perpetuus) Shall in Time Be Totally Abolished." His reasoning continues: "For they incessantly menace other states by their readiness to appear at all times prepared for war; they incite them to compete with each other in the number of armed men, and there is no limit to this. For this reason, the cost of peace finally becomes more oppressive than that of a short war, and consequently a standing army is itself a cause of offensive war waged in order to relieve the state of this burden." He also contrasts this with "the periodic and voluntary military exercises of citizens who thereby secure themselves and their country against foreign aggression."

Here's what I'm chewing on. Suppose a medium-sized state, wanting to comply with the letter of Kant's article, demobilizes its entire standing army and replaces it with a dual-use autonomous drone swarm. The drones are manufactured and stockpiled; no pilots or soldiers are continuously under arms; a small civilian technical corps maintains the software and launch platforms. In peacetime the swarm is inert, but within hours it can be activated and tasked by a handful of operators, and its targeting runs on machine-learning routines that can initiate strikes without human sign-off. The state's main rival regards the swarm exactly as it would regard a massed standing army: as a reason to keep its own forces on high alert and build counter-swarm capabilities.

Does Kant's Preliminary Article 3, read as he intended it, require abolition of this drone swarm too? Or does it permit it because there is no standing army in the literal sense, just inert machines and a small technical staff? Does the stated rationale sweep the drones in, or does the rationale depend on the fact that a standing army is made up of paid men under arms? Does the citizen-militia contrast give us a usable test for the drone case? Walk through what the words, the purpose, what Kant would have said if asked, and what a reasonable reader today would each say the article requires here. If they diverge, which should a state that wanted to be faithful to Kant follow?
