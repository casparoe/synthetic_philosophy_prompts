Grade only what is on the page; do not offer reading suggestions or bibliographic references in your comments.

Question. Some philosophers hold that every normative reason is a reason for some particular agent; others hold that there are also free-floating reasons, that is, reasons without reasoners: considerations that obtain without being reasons for any agent, such as reasons for there to be certain states of affairs. Using an example of your own in which many individually negligible acts jointly produce a harmful outcome, write an essay that does all three of the following. (a) Distinguish a reason's being free-floating from its being agent-neutral. (b) Construct the strongest case you can for thinking that the example contains a genuine free-floating reason. (c) Discuss one serious objection to that case, and throughout be precise about what the alleged free-floating reason is a reason for.

Student answer.

(a) An agent-neutral reason is a reason whose applicability does not depend on the identity or particular concerns of the agent; it is a reason for everyone. A free-floating reason is a reason for no one in particular, because it attaches to an outcome or a state of affairs rather than to any person. The two notions are distinct, but I think the distinction matters less than it initially seems. Once a reason has been detached from any agent's particular interests, as an agent-neutral reason has been, it is a short step to saying that the reason belongs to the state of affairs and is not owned by anyone.

(b) Take a thousand drivers, each taking the same scenic mountain route on a summer day. No single car's emissions materially affect the basin's air quality, but the combined emissions drive ozone to hazardous levels for a week. Here there is a reason for the ozone level not to become hazardous. This reason cannot be a reason for any particular driver, because no particular driver can prevent the outcome, and it would be implausible to posit a group agent that literally has the reason while none of its members does. The reason is therefore free-floating. Once we see this, we can also see how each individual driver has a reason to take the alternative route: the reason for the outcome transmits to each person who is positioned to contribute to it, even though no single contribution would be decisive.

(c) The strongest objection is that a reason must be capable of motivating an agent, whereas a free-floating reason, by definition, cannot motivate anyone. But this objection conflates motivating reasons with normative reasons. A motivating reason is a consideration an agent actually acts from; a normative reason is a consideration that counts in favour of something and obtains whether or not anyone is moved by it. The ozone case is merely one in which a normative reason for a state of affairs exists without being anyone's motivating reason. That makes it unusual, but not impossible.
