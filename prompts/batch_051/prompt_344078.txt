I recently read an article in a general-interest philosophy outlet that summarized Locke's account of property, and I want to know how much of it I can trust. According to the article, Locke argued that because each person owns his own body and his labour, whenever someone works on an unowned thing and thereby mixes his labour with it, that thing becomes his property. The article explains this by saying that labour is part of the person, so an object that has absorbed a person's labour becomes an extension of that person, and taking it from him would be like taking a part of him. The only limitation the article mentions is that one may not appropriate more than one can use before it spoils. It concludes that Locke showed that the first person to labour on any unowned resource acquires it outright, with no further obligations to others, and that inequalities arising later from free exchange are therefore morally unproblematic. How fair is this as a presentation of the labour theory of acquisition and the Lockean proviso? I'd like an accuracy audit: go through the article's claims one by one, identify which parts a careful reader of Locke would accept, which are subtly wrong or conflate distinct claims, what a defender of Locke's view would object to, and whether the article's concluding claim actually follows once the details are corrected.
