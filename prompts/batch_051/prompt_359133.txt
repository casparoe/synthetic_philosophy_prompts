My professional background is in the physical sciences, and I suppose I still carry the assumption that a well-posed problem with a well-defined numerical payoff structure ought to have a well-defined numerical answer. In retirement I have been reading through decision theory, and I have found the expected-value framework compelling in its generality: probabilities weight outcomes, and the rational choice is the one that maximizes the resulting sum. Yet I have become preoccupied by the Pasadena game and its relatives. In the standard version, a fair coin is tossed until it first comes up heads. If the first heads occurs on the nth toss, the player receives a payoff whose magnitude is \(2^n/n\), but with alternating sign — a gain for odd n, a loss for even n. The natural way to write the expectation yields the series \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^{n-1}/n\), which is conditionally convergent. That series can be rearranged to produce any finite value, to diverge, or to oscillate, so the expected value of the game is undefined in the usual sense.

I am genuinely torn. On the one hand, the expected-value framework is the most unified and elegant account of rational choice under uncertainty I know, and I am reluctant to abandon it or to complicate it merely to accommodate one contrived game. On the other hand, I have a stubborn intuition that the Pasadena game has some determinate fair price — that there is a fact of the matter about what a rational agent should pay to play it, and that our formalism is failing to represent that fact rather than revealing that the question has no answer. I would like you to give me the strongest version of both positions and then deliver a negotiated verdict. Specifically: under what circumstances should I treat the intuition as a debunkable artifact of the formalism's ordering problem, and under what circumstances should I amend or replace the expected-value framework so that it assigns a determinate value to this game? And is there a stable interpretation on which both the theory and the intuition survive? Please answer in American English.
