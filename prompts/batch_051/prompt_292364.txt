A colleague told me this four-way scheme is essentially Belnap's four-valued logic; another insisted it is just Nāgārjuna's tetralemma, as used in Madhyamaka discussions of emptiness, refurbished as software. I now realize I do not actually know whether these two fourfold schemes are the same idea or merely look alike. Please compare Nāgārjuna's tetralemma with Belnap-style four-valued semantics. In particular, I want to know whether the four alternatives line up one-to-one (especially the both and neither cases), whether each framework treats its four alternatives as exhaustive, and what it would mean for Nāgārjuna to reject all four alternatives if the four are exhaustive. Answer as a numbered list of exactly five points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
