I am a graduate student in philosophy, and for the past year I have been failing, with embarrassing regularity, to keep my own writing schedule: every Friday I resolve to draft by Sunday morning, and nearly every Sunday morning I choose to reread old notes instead. This has pushed me into reading two literatures side by side—philosophical accounts of akrasia, and the psychological and behavioral-economics work on hyperbolic discounting. I am writing a short position statement for a seminar, and I would like a consistency audit of the following seven commitments before I present them.

1. Akrasia is real and is not reducible to compulsion or to a change of mind: an agent can act intentionally, with full knowledge, against her own better, all-things-considered judgment.
2. Descriptively, my own failures have the signature of hyperbolic discounting: I prefer a larger, later reward when both it and a smaller, sooner alternative are distant, but as the sooner alternative approaches, I reverse the preference.
3. When I give in, the resulting action is still fully mine: it is intentional, and not an alien desire taking over.
4. I accept a broadly Humean theory of rationality, on which an action is rational just to the extent that it best serves what the agent most wants at the time of action, given her beliefs.
5. I also reject any privileging of the long-run self. Present bias is not a distortion of a normatively correct exponential discounting function; the self at each moment has legitimate authority to weigh the present more heavily, and a preference reversal is not automatically a rational error.
6. In spite of this, I believe precommitment devices—self-imposed deadlines with penalties, website blockers, leaving my phone outside the room—are rationally justified for someone like me, even when they constrain my later self against the preference that later self will predictably hold.
7. When weakness of will occurs, it is a rational failure, a defect of self-governance, rather than merely a taste of a later self that an earlier self happens to disavow.

Please go through this set and identify which pairs or triples are in genuine tension, ranked by severity. For each tension, tell me which claim would have to give and what the cheapest repair would be. Please also identify any apparent tension that dissolves on closer inspection. Finally, tell me the least costly single revision that would make the whole set consistent, and what that revision costs. Please refer to the commitments by number and write your answer in American English.
