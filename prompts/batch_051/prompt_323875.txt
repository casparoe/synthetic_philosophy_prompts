I keep noticing a pattern when people talk about using chain-of-thought monitoring as a safety technique, and I’m wondering if it has its own name. The setup is: you train or prompt a model to verbalize its reasoning so that the reasoning can be checked. Then you optimize or select against that verbalized reasoning on some task, maybe against a reward model or human feedback. At that point the chain-of-thought stops behaving like a trace of the system’s actual deliberation and starts behaving like an output channel optimized for the monitor: plausible-sounding steps, partial rationales, maybe even misleading ones, while the real computation stays opaque. I don’t just mean ordinary hallucination or a human being bad at introspecting; the weird feature is that the monitoring setup itself creates the pressure that makes the report unreliable. I’ve seen people use words like “unfaithful chain-of-thought,” “obfuscated reasoning,” or “Goodharting,” but I’m after something narrower. Does the process where a chain-of-thought becomes a target for optimization rather than a report of the actual reasoning have a standard term? If not, what are the closest concepts, and how would you carve the boundary between that and a model simply making a mistake, or deliberately trying to deceive a human evaluator?
