Two friends of mine run a weekly rules-game where the rulebook can be amended mid-game, and last week they hit an argument I've been unable to settle. The current Rule 8 says every rule-change proposal needs a two-thirds vote to pass. Rule 2 says no proposal may conflict with the existing rules. Someone proposed changing Rule 8 to require only a simple majority, and the proposal included the sentence: "This change itself takes effect by a simple majority."

Here's how the argument went.

Samir said the proposal was invalid on its face. It directly conflicts with Rule 8's two-thirds requirement, so Rule 2 voids it. Attaching a sentence that says "this applies to itself" doesn't make a forbidden change legal.

Priya said that's confusing two moments. The proposal isn't being adopted by simple majority; it's being voted on under the old two-thirds rule. If it gets two-thirds, it validly becomes a rule, and only then does the threshold drop. Repealing a rule is not conflicting with it.

Samir came back that if the proposal passes by two-thirds, then the sentence saying the change took effect by simple majority is false; if it passes by simple majority, it never became a rule. So the self-applying clause is either false or inert. And if you drop the clause, what remains is an attempt to use the old rule to delete itself, which is bootstrapping.

Priya said, fine, treat the self-applying clause as inert. The substantive proposal is just: lower the threshold for future amendments. That can be validly adopted by a two-thirds vote under the old rule. If an ordinary repeal of Rule 8 is allowed, this is allowed.

Samir replied that then the two-thirds rule is no constraint at all—one two-thirds majority can convert the game to majority rule permanently, and every other rule becomes equally vulnerable. An amendment procedure that can be used to erase itself at will doesn't really govern anything.

Priya's last move was that Rule 8 never said it was entrenched. The players gave themselves the power to change rules, and unless a rule expressly protects itself, it's just another rule. Samir is treating Rule 8 as a constitution with special status, but nothing in the rulebook says that. Any rule for changing rules will be self-referential when it's changed, so the circularity bug he's pointing at is just the normal cost of having an amendment rule at all.

They never resolved it, and I've gone over the conversation enough times to loose track of who's actually right. Please adjudicate move by move: for each exchange, who has the better point, and does anyone make a clearly mistaken move? Then say what, if anything, both of them are missing about how self-amending rules work. I'm fine with the answer being messy; I just want the scorekeeping to be precise.
