I am a doctoral student in decision theory, and I have realized that the word "utility" is doing too many jobs at once in the revealed-preference literature, so before I continue I want to fix the specification of what an adequate account must do. The field seems to slide among at least three senses — a Benthamite quantity of felt satisfaction, a real-valued index whose only job is to represent whatever a person chooses, and a normative measure of how well the person's own life is going — and arguments that choice reveals welfare often switch senses without saying so. I want a list of requirements that any adequate account of utility, and of what choice can reveal about it, has to meet, so I can hold the next several dozen papers to the list rather than being carried along by each author's local usage.

The list has to go beyond the slogan that choices reveal preferences just when the consistency axioms are satisfied, and it should say something definite about the hard cases: choices that are inconsistent across time or across descriptions of the same options, preferences that look constructed on the spot rather than retrieved, choices made under false or partial information, and menu effects in which adding an irrelevant alternative changes what is picked. It should also register the Sen-style objection that satisfying the consistency axioms is compatible with being systematic but reliably self-defeating, and say whether that is a problem for the theory or a bullet it is entitled to bite.

Please give me exactly five numbered requirements, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. For each requirement, state the case or consideration that makes it a requirement and mark it non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. Be committed: identify the single requirement that does the most to rule out the view that utility is nothing more than whatever a consistent choice function maximizes, and flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Skip the scorecard of named theories against the list; I want the criteria themselves. Write in American English.
