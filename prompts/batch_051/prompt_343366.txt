My friend just started at a neurotech company working on direct stimulation of visual cortex, and it's been making me think about which experiences are genuinely new as opposed to just old pieces rearranged. I'd like to know if there's a principle behind my gut on these cases. Hearing a chord after hearing each note separately: new-ish, but still made of old parts. Smelling a lemon–vanilla blend after knowing both odors: not a new smell; I can still pick out the two. Seeing orange after only seeing red and yellow: same thing, just a blend. But my friend says their device will someday let you see reddish-green, which is supposed to be impossible because of how the opponent channels work. There my gut splits: partly it sounds like a note that is both C and C-sharp, partly I think if you directly stimulate the right neurons you're just adding a channel, and that would be a genuinely new color. Then I think of mantis shrimp with their extra photoreceptors and I want to say they see colors I can't, not merely finer versions of mine. And I'd call an implant that gives someone magnetoreception a genuinly new kind of experience, not a combination. Is there a principle that captures all or most of that, what does it predict about the reddish-green case, and which of my verdicts have to go?
