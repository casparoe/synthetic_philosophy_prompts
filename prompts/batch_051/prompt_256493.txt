I'm a philosophy professor, and I've ended up as the independent ethics advisor on the board of a mid-size AI lab. The board is split over a unilateral safety policy: the lab would refuse to train or deploy models above a certain capability threshold unless a named competitor has already crossed it, and would reserve a fixed share of compute for alignment work, accepting the resulting 'alignment tax' (the capability penalty for doing safety work). One faction says the 'competitiveness constraint' makes this self-defeating — rival labs won't wait, so we'd be ceding the frontier to less careful actors and the net effect would be worse, not better. The other side says that's exactly the argument every lab uses to do nothing, and it treats the fact that someone else will do it as a get-out. I need to circulate a short written opinion before Thursdays board meeting. Draft it as a decision memo: a concrete recommendation (adopt, reject, or adopt with conditions) with a time frame, an honest treatment of the strongest version of the competitiveness objection, and some view on whether duties to employees and shareholders change the picture. Lead with the bottom line, keep it under 500 words, and write in British English.
