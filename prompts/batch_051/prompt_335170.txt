I got into a way-too-long argument with a friend last night about Russell's paradox, of all things, and I'm still not sure who had the better case. He did a couple years of math before switching to econ, and his line the whole time was that the paradox is overrated. His version: Russell's paradox is just the barber puzzle. You know the one — a barber shaves all and only the men in town who don't shave themselves, and then you ask whether he shaves himself. My friend says the paradox is the boring observation that no such barber can exist; it's a contradictory job description, not a discovery about mathematics. And he says the set version is exactly the same: there's just no set of all sets that don't contain themselves, full stop, and treating it as a crisis is drama. The popular book we'd both been reading does something similar — it introduces Russell's paradox through the barber and then concludes there cannot be such a set, and that's the whole point. But something about this feels off to me. The barber case seems easy to shrug off in a way the set-theoretic one isn't, like the stakes are different, but I couldn't articulate it in the moment.

Could you act as a philosopher of mathematics working on set theory and give me a careful audit of the barber analogy for Russell's paradox? To keep me honest, maybe start by brainstorming the intended mapping explicitly — what is the town supposed to be, who are the men, what does shaving correspond to, what is the barber — before you test it. Then lay out where the analogy genuinely illuminates the paradox, and where it quietly imports features of barbers and villages that don't carry over. In particular, I want to know whether my friend's conclusion (and the book's) survives once the disanalogies are on the table: is it fair to say naive set theory was committed to something as obviously impossible as a contradictory barber, or does the set-theoretic paradox bite in a way the village story can't capture? And if the barber really is a broken analogy here, is there a better one — or is the honest move to just drop the analogies altogether?
