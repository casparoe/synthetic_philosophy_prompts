I have no academic background to fall back on, but I have spent years running a large internet forum and building the reputation and moderation systems that keep it civil. Through trial and error I have arrived at a practical vocabulary for conflict: reputation as a tracked quantity; status as something people pursue through costly signals; third-party enforcers and the rules they apply; audiences that reward or punish certain responses; escalation loops; and design levers such as public upvotes, anonymous reports, strikes and bans.

Recently I came across the popular three-way distinction between honour, dignity and victimhood cultures, and it struck me that I already think about all three patterns every day, only in my own language. As I understand the distinction, it runs roughly like this. In an honour culture, a slight must be answered personally and visibly, or one's standing falls. In a dignity culture, the correct move is to absorb small offences and hand serious ones to impartial authorities. In a victimhood culture, the way to gain support is to publicise one's injury and appeal to onlookers, so that the party who can present as most aggrieved tends to attract the most backing.

Please restate this distinction faithfully in the vocabulary I have just described. For each culture, give me: what a slight is; what moves are available after a slight; who the relevant audience is; what equilibrium the community tends to settle into; and what the characteristic failure mode of that equilibrium is.

Then give me the residue report. Which elements of the three-way distinction map cleanly onto reputation systems, signalling and equilibria? Which ones survive only if I distort them? Which have no counterpart at all in my vocabulary? Most importantly, I keep wanting to ask whether any of these cultures is morally superior to the others. Can that question even be stated in my vocabulary, or does a clean translation quietly turn a moral question into a technical one about which equilibrium is more stable or cheaper to run? If something drops out, tell me whether the loss is a defect in my vocabulary or evidence that the moral question was less precise than it first appeared.
