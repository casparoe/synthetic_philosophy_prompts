I'm drafting a position paper for a workshop on the history of technology and AI governance, and before I circulate my argument I want an audit of whether my own methodological commitments about historical analogy actually cohere. The bundle of commitments I've ended up with is roughly this. (1) An analogy between a past technology and advanced AI is informative only to the extent that the causal mechanism operating in the historical case is present in the AI case; surface resemblance counts for little. (2) By that standard, the nuclear weapons analogy is the single most informative analogy for AI risk, because both cases involve a technology that lets a relatively small set of actors produce system-level catastrophe and that shifts the offense–defense balance. (3) I also believe the printing press is the right analogy for open-weight AI development, since it lowered the cost of copying and distributing information and thereby weakened gatekeepers; by contrast, I regard the Industrial Revolution as a poor analogy for AI, because mechanization replaced task-specific human capacities whereas AI is general-purpose, so the relevant mechanism does not carry over. (4) I hold that a technology's political consequences are largely fixed by its economic character — above all by whether it is a general-purpose technology — so once we classify that correctly, much of the downstream politics is predictable. (5) At the same time, I hold that institutions, strategic choices, and accidents play a decisive role in whether a technology's dangers actually materialize; that is how I explain the fact that nuclear weapons have not been used in war since 1945. (6) Finally, when two analogies point in different policy directions and neither has a clearly better mechanistic fit, I think the tie should be broken by the severity of the worst-case outcome associated with each analogy.

I cannot tell where this set comes apart. Please audit it: identify which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and say for each what would have to be given up to resolve it. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole bundle coherent, and what accepting that revision would cost.
