I have been trying to work out whether the following parallel is genuine or merely verbal. In the New Hume debate, the question is whether Hume took causation to consist in nothing more than constant conjunction in experience, or whether he took constant conjunction to be the observable shadow of real necessary connections in the world, which we can never apprehend. I keep seeing the same shape of dispute in contemporary causal inference: one can remain at the level of a joint probability distribution over variables, or one can posit a structural causal model whose equations and invariances are meant to underwrite counterfactual and interventional claims that the bare distribution alone cannot support. On this mapping, regularity and constant conjunction correspond to the joint distribution, whilst the hidden causal powers of the New Hume reading correspond to the mechanisms of the structural model. Is this a deep structural analogy, or does it collapse once both sides are stated accurately? If it is genuine, what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer, and what does the analogy predict that turns out false on one side or the other?
