I'm teaching a seminar on sacrifice and blame this semester, and while putting the syllabus together I realized I don't actually have a crisp account of what a scapegoat is. The stock example is the biblical rite where the people's sins are confessed over a goat and it's driven out into the wilderness: the goat is innocent, it isn't being punished, and it carries something away. That's clearly in. But then consider a mid-level manager fired after a scandal even though the executives above her were at least as involved; the ritual killing of an animal to cleanse a community of pollution; a kid who takes the blame for something his brother did because he thinks his brother will get beaten otherwise; an internet mob destroying one person for an old insensitive post while thousands who wrote the same thing go untouched; and a politician who resigns claiming to take responsibility for a systemic failure in a way that conveniently shields the party. I keep flipping on whether these are all one kind of thing or whether the word is doing too much work.

So here's what I'd like from you: give me a working definition of "scapegoat" that sorts these cases. I'm especially interested in whether the definition should require that the target be innocent, whether it should require that something actually gets transferred or cleansed, and whether it should require that blame is displaced from someone in particular rather than just from a group's discomfort. Run the definition through each case, flag which verdicts you're least confident about, and tell me whether "scapegoat" names a real uniform phenomenon or just a loose family of blame-practices. Also, if you can, draw the line between a scapegoat, a sacrifice, and a plain fall guy—or explain why the line can't be drawn.
