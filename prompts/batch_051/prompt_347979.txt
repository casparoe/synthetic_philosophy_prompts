Yesterday I had a long argument with a friend about the language of thought view, and she kept returning to an analogy she treated as decisive: the mind is to the brain as software is to hardware, and thinking is literally the execution of computations over sentence-like representations in mentalese. She used the analogy to support three conclusions: that any genuine thinker must operate on discrete, compositional symbols; that connectionist and vector-based models are at best mere implementations of a classical symbol system rather than alternative accounts of cognition; and that pre-linguistic infants and non-human animals can have full propositional attitudes only if they already possess an internal symbolic medium. I would like an audit of how far this analogy actually carries weight. Please set out an explicit mapping of correspondences — what is meant to play the role of the program, the instruction set, the data structures, the hardware, and so on — and then identify where the analogy breaks down, separating the disanalogies that matter for her conclusions from those that are merely superficial. Finally, assess whether each of the three conclusions survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and indicate what a better analogy would be, if there is one. Please write the answer in British English.
