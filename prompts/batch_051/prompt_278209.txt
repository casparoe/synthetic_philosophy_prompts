I have a research habit I'm half-defensive about and half-suspicious of. When a prediction of mine fails, my rule is: the central claims of my account are not up for revision; I instead go down the usual list of peripheral suspects — measurement error, boundary conditions, an unmodeled interaction, a bad manipulation, a weird sample. I tweak one of those and rerun. My colleague says this makes my core beliefs unfalsifiable. But I once read that mature research programmes work a bit like this: there's a protected hard core and an adjustable outer belt of auxiliary assumptions, and you don't throw out the whole programme just because an anomoly shows up. The trouble is I can't tell whether my habit is the defensible version of that, or just motivated reasoning wearing the same clothes. From the inside, how would I tell the difference? Is there a principled line between the okay kind of keeping-the-core-fixed and the not-okay kind?
