I've been reading about the "Cartesian theater" idea — the claim that there's no one place in your brain where everything comes together and a little "you" watches the show. Instead there's the multiple drafts model: lots of parallel interpretations get edited and revised, and whichever one ends up shaping what you say or do is the one that counts, with no central audience. The theory has alot going for it: it avoids the infinite regress of a homunculus watching a screen inside another homunculus, it handles weird timing effects and change blindness better than the theater picture, and it fits how messy and parallel the brain actually is. But I can't shake the intuition that there IS a single me in here, watching my experience unfold all at once. When I look across a room it doesn't feel like competing drafts; it feels like one seamless movie with an audience. I'm not asking which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing I give up, what it would take for the theory to be the thing that goes, and whether there's a reading of the intuition — maybe not as a literal place in the brain but as something about what a self is — where both can survive. Please spell out what would justify each of those three moves instead of just picking one.
