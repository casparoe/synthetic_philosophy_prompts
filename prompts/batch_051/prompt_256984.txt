Some moral psychologists draw the guilt/shame distinction this way: guilt responds to a bad act, shame to a bad self; guilt motivates repair and apology, shame motivates hiding or withdrawal. From this they infer that guilt is the morally better emotion, and some go further and claim that a culture can, within limits, choose which of the two it cultivates by how it responds to wrongdoing. I am not sure whether either claim is really testable or whether the normative conclusion is being smuggled into the operationalization. Take (a) the claim that guilt is the better moral emotion and (b) the claim that a culture can choose which of the two to cultivate. For each, design the strongest empirical test you can, specify what result would count for and against it, and be explicit about what even the best test could not settle — for example, whether "better" survives once it is reduced to something measurable, and whether a culture's observed emotion profile shows that the culture chose it.
