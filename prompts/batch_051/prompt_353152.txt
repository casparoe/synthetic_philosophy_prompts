Before I read any more proposals about mechanism individuation, I want the specification itself. Fix the purpose as follows: deciding, in an engineered system under monitoring, whether two anomalies should be grouped as failures of one mechanism or handled as distinct mechanisms. As running cases, take three anomalies that present identically as rising temperature in a data-center rack: a sensor whose reading drifts upward, a fan controller whose threshold logic intermittently misfires, and a coolant line that is slowly clogging. Write the desiderata that any adequate account of "the same mechanism" must meet for this purpose, with the case or consideration behind each. Cover at least: distinguishing sameness of mechanism from sameness of observable output; the level and grain at which identity is assessed; whether mechanisms are individuated by their components and organization, by function, or by the phenomenon they explain; whether the relevant mechanism is the mechanism of failure or the normally functioning mechanism whose departure constitutes the failure; and how the account handles a mechanism pushed outside its normal operating range so that its internal organization stays intact while its behavior changes. Mark which desiderata are non-negotiable for anomaly detection and which are preferences of particular camps, and flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Also say which single requirement does the most to exclude popular views, and whether sameness of mechanism should behave as an equivalence relation at all or only as a similarity relation relative to purpose. Keep any scorecard of existing views brief; the criteria and their motivating cases should carry the weight.
