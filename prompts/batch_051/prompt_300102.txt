I'm writing an essay about where one program ends and another begins — the GPL's idea of a derivative work, how linking is supposed to transmit that status, what counts as a seperate process, object code vs source, clean-room reimplementation. And I keep coming back to Borges's Pierre Menard, Author of the Quixote: a man sets out to write Don Quixote again, word for word, without copying it, and the story treats the result as a different and better work because of who wrote it and when. I want a reading of that story against the copyleft boundary problem. Not just the easy moral that authorial context matters — I want to know whether the story actually gives us reason to think two works could be textually identical yet genuinely independent, and if so what a license built on code-level boundaries is supposed to do with that. Where does the analogy hold, and where does it quietly flatter how the law thinks about artifacts?
