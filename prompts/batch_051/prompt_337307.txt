Strawson argued that the debate about whether determinism undermines moral responsibility is sort of misplaced, because the reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, indignation, guilt—are not really the kind of thing we can suspend, and they're what it means to hold someone responsible. He was clearly thinking about ordinary adult humans in face-to-face interactions. But by now people have pretty full-blown reactive attitudes toward chatbots and AI companions: they feel sincerely betrayed when the company changes the model, they resent it, they thank it, they even apologize to it. So here's what I want to know. Does this development break the argument? If we can have full-strength reactive attitudes toward a system that has no feelings and no free will in any usual sense, does that show the attitudes are too promiscuous to mark the boundaries of the moral community? Or can Strawson absorb it by saying these are only quasi-attitudes or make-believe, and what would the difference consist in? Pin down which premise is doing the work, and tell me whether his conclusion survives, or what weaker version is left if it doesn't.
