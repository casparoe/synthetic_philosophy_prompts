The other said that's a nonstarter: a person who genuinely loved everyone the same would be either a monster or a robot, and the Confucian point isn't that we should hoard care for our own, it's that the capacity to care has to be trained up in close relationships before it can reach outward, roughly the way Mencius talks about the child at the well. First came back that this confuses the order in which we learn to care with the order in which care is owed, and that Mozi never said to treat a wrongdoer exactly like your son; he said need and desert, not kinship distance, should set the priorities. Second replied that impartial care makes everyone responsible for everyone, and with scarce time and attention that means no one is actually cared for by anyone. They asked me to settle it and I honestly couldn't.
