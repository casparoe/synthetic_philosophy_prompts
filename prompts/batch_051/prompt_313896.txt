I'm preparing for a philosophy department colloquium next month, on a campus visit, and I need a Q&A prep sheet. The paper I'll present defends the claim that Dutch book arguments cannot, by themselves, establish probabilism: they can dramatize an inconsistency among the prices a person would post, but they cannot show that any particular credence or betting disposition is irrational unless we already grant a synchronic norm of coherence, something like a prohibition on holding a set of credences that licenses a collection of fair bets you would accept one by one and disown as a package. I came to this view partly because I spent two years before graduate school compiling odds for a betting exchange, and I noticed that the people I worked with cared about avoiding arbitrage without ever taking themselves to be receiving a theory of rationality; the distance between those two concerns is the whole paper.

The audience is a department strong in formal epistemology and decision theory, with one historian of Bayesianism and one behavioral economist cross-appointed in economics. The formal epistemologists are mostly accuracy-first and already suspect Dutch books are just heuristic props for the real mathematics; the decision theorists split between those who think sequential choice carries no global coherence requirement and those who accept diachronic Dutch book arguments against simple conditionalization; the behavioral economist is suspicious of any norm that ignores the cost of thinking. I expect the objection I'm hoping nobody asks to be about whether my own argument, if it runs, also undercuts the diachronic Dutch book argument for conditionalization, which I lean on elsewhere in the paper.

Please draft the prep sheet: the likely objections, say eight or ten of them, ordered roughly by how likely each is and how much damage it would do, phrased the way someone from this particular audience would actually put it, each with a two-minute reply and an honest note on where that reply is softest. For each objection, indicate which kind of audience member is most likely to raise it. End with the question I'm hoping nobody asks and tell me how to handle it if it comes anyway.
