I am working on a piece about how early and mid-twentieth-century philosophical objections to Darwinism have aged, and I keep returning to one that seems to be repeated far more often than it is updated. Karl Popper famously argued that natural selection is not a genuinely testable scientific theory, on the grounds that the principle of the survival of the fittest is close to a tautology: if fitness is identified with actual reproductive success, then the survivors are by definition the fit, and the theory rules out no conceivable outcome. He tempered the charge by treating Darwinism as what he called a metaphysical research programme, a framework that suggests questions rather than a falsifiable theory that competes with others, and late in life he recanted, conceding that natural selection does have testable consequences.

What interests me is a specific later development that seems to change the terms of that old objection. From roughly 1970 onward, two things happened in the philosophy and formal theory of biology. First, fitness was redefined as a propensity: an organism's expected reproductive output in a specified environment, rather than its actual realized success. Second, the Price equation supplied a general partition of evolutionary change into a covariance between trait value and fitness, read as selection, and a transmission term covering everything else, including drift and mutation bias. On this machinery, selection is no longer defined by the outcome; it is one measurable component of the outcome, and the theory makes quantitative predictions that can fail, just as the breeder's equation predicts a response to selection that disappears when heritability is absent or correlated selection opposes it.

My question is whether Popper's original objection survives that development, and in what form. Does the propensity interpretation plus the Price equation actually defeat the tautology charge, or does it merely relocate it, given that the covariance term is still an algebraic identity and a surprising result can always be redescribed as hidden covariance or transmission bias? I want an answer that identifies the specific premise of Popper's argument that this development bears on, says whether that premise can be repaired rather than simply abandoned, and judges honestly whether the objection is dead, refitted to bite again, or waiting for a different target.
