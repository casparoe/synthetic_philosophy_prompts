Write the joint statement that a pair of people in my reading group could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on sexual exclusivity. One of them — I’ll call her the conventionalist — holds that monogamy’s bindingness comes only from what partners actually agree to, explicitly or through a clear tacit convention, and that absent such agreement there’s no default moral requirement to be sexually exclusive. The other — call him the defaultist — holds that in a romantic relationship of the relevant kind there’s a standing moral default of sexual exclusivity, which partners can waive or renegotiate but don’t create from nothing. They’ve been arguing for months, and I suspect a lot of the fight is actually about whether the conventionalist can capture the felt sense of betrayal without smuggling in a default norm, and whether the defaultist can keep his position from collapsing into treating local expectations as moral facts.

I want you to draft the document they should have produced. Make it a numbered list of claims both accept about promising, waiving, silence, and what counts as “relevant agreement.” Then give a table of exact disagreements, with each side’s view phrased so its own side would accept the wording. Then list the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — and for each crux give a concrete case or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it, with each side’s prediction written down in advance. The point isn’t to resolve the dispute. It’s to make the remaining disagreement exact enough that someone outside the argument can see which bits are genuinely normative, which bits are empirical, and which bits were just people talking past each other about the word “expectation.”

Don’t cite authors or books; just write the document itself.
