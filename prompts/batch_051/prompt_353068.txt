I keep seeing people insist the alignment problem should be defined narrowly: a system is aligned iff it does what its operator actually intended, and whether the operator's intentions are good is a seperate question about what values to install. But I don't see how these come apart as cleanly as that. If I align a system to the intent of an operator who wants it to manipulate vulnerable people into buying junk insurance, haven't I produced a system that is badly misaligned in the only sense that matters? So isn't so-called intent alignment just value alignment with a particular (possibly awful) value chosen as the target, and calling it narrower is either naive about what alignment talk smuggles in or a cop out? Give me the actual argument that the narrow definition is doing real conceptual work and not just dressing up make-the-AI-do-what-the-boss-says as neutral engineering. Write it in American English, and lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence, then give the reasoning with no preamble and no restating the question.
