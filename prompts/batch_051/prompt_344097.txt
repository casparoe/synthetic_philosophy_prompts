I'm a teaching assistant for an introductory philosophy class at my high school, and I need to grade a short exam answer. The exam question was: "Could you have come from a different sperm and egg? Explain origin essentialism and present the strongest argument for your answer." The student wrote:

Origin essentialism says that an organism's origin in a particular bit of material is essential to it. Kripke argues for this with the example of a wooden table: a table made from one particular block of wood could not have been made from a completely different block, because then it would not be this table; the same pattern cut from new wood would be a different table. The point carries over from artifacts to organisms. A person comes from a particular sperm and a particular egg, so just as the table is tied to its original wood, a person is tied to those particular gametes. That is why your siblings, who came from other gametes, could never have been you.

The clearest reason is genetic. If a different sperm had fertilized the same egg, the resulting zygote would have had a different genome. Since your genome determines who you are, that zygote would have developed into a different person, not you. So you could not have been any of the other possible children your parents might have had.

A more rigorous version uses Leibniz's Law. You actually have the property of having originated from your actual sperm and egg; a person who originated from a different gamete would lack that property. Since identical things share all their properties, that person cannot be you. This proves, for every person, that they could not have had a different origin.
