I've been going back and forth on causation lately, and I keep finding myself pulled in two directions. On paper, the regularity view looks almost undeniable to me: all we ever observe is one event following another, over and over; we never observe some extra glue or "oomph" in the world itself. The idea that causation just is constant conjunction plus the mind's habit of expecting the effect seems to explain everything the evidence actually licenses, and it's beautifully economical. But then I watch one billiard ball crack into another, or I flick a switch and a light comes on, and I cannot shake the thought that when the first ball hits the second, the second has to move—that something in the collision itself produces, forces, or necessitates the motion, rather than merely preceding it. It doesn't feel like a projection of my own expectations; it feels like I'm detecting something out there, however much the arguments tell me I can't be.

I don't want a canned answer. I want to know what it would actually take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, and what it would take for the regularity theory to be the thing that goes. Is the intuition just a well-documented psychological byproduct—something a good Humean can debunk—or is it tracking a real feature of the world that regularity theories are simply blind to? And is there a reading of 'necessary connection' on which both sides survive, where the regularity story is right about what we can observe and justify but the world still genuinely contains causal powers or dispositions? Basically: when a systematic theory and a stubborn intuition collide like this, which one should yeild, on what grounds, and what would count as evidence one way or the other?
