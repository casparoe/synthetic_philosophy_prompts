My friend keeps ending arguments with "question everything," most recently when I said I didn't see why I should second-guess the nurse who read me my lab results. She said that's exactly the sort of thing you shouldn't just accept — question everything. But I honestly can't tell what she's claiming. It could mean any belief might turn out false, which is obviously true. It could mean you're not allowed to believe anything until you've checked it yourself, which sounds impossible to live by. It could mean believing on someone else's say-so is morally wrong, which is a much bigger claim. And if you really question everything, shouldn't the slogan itself be first in line? I want the options before I push back. Lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making, from the trivial to the radical, say which are platitudes, which are substantive, and which are false or self-undermining, and tell me which reading she'd acutally need for it to settle the lab-results argument — and whether that reading is one of the ones worth keeping.
