In my ethics reading group the particularism/generalism fight has basically stalled. Both sides agree that rules like "don't lie, other things equal" are fine as rules of thumb, and both sides agree context can swtich what you should do. But the particularists say no unqualified principle survives contact with actual cases, and the generalists say the particularists are just smuggling in unstated principles whenever they offer reasons for anything. My hunch is the actual disagreement is about what it takes to be a reason: the particularist thinks the same feature can count for an action in one case and against it in another, while the generalist thinks that only shows we've misdescribed the feature. Is that the real dispute, or is it something else — a verbal issue about the word 'principle', a difference about moral psychology, clashing starting intuitions? Give me a definite diagnosis, not a menu. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating the question.
