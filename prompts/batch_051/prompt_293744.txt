I'm putting together a short position statement on what the moral psychology of political difference shows, if anything, about whose political judgments are better justified. It is for an interdisciplinary working group on political epistemology, and I have been asked to write down my commitments as plainly as I can so the others can pressure-test them. I have ended up with the list below. Each item strikes me as true on its own, but I suspect the set does not fully cohere, and I would rather locate the cracks now than in front of the group. I genuinely do not know which item I should give up.

1. The central descriptive claim of moral foundations theory is broadly correct for the contemporary Western ideological spectrum: self-identified liberals' moral judgments are dominated by care/harm and fairness/cheating considerations, while self-identified conservatives' judgments also draw substantially on loyalty/betrayal, authority/subversion, and sanctity/degradation, and this is a difference in which considerations get treated as morally relevant rather than merely a difference in factual beliefs or rhetoric.

2. I do not think that descriptive breadth counts in conservatism's favor. Moralizing more domains is not itself a form of moral sensitivity, and could equally be overreach.

3. I accept an impartiality constraint on moral and political justification: a consideration counts in justification only if it could be accepted from a standpoint that does not give extra weight to the interests or standing of the judge's own group, rank, or way of life, and departures from that standpoint are at best instrumentally justified.

4. But I also think loyalty, authority, and sanctity are not reducible to care and fairness in disguise. They answer to real normative relations — special obligations to particular persons and projects, the justifiability of at least some structured asymmetry, and a kind of respect for what a community marks off as inviolable — and a morality built only from harm and fairness would flatten something genuine.

5. The fact that a moral judgment is fast, affectively loaded, and traceable to evolved intuitions is not by itself a mark against it. Correct judgments have to be produced by some machinery too, and good moral cognition need not be slow, cool, or effortful.

6. Still, I accept a debunking principle in outline: when the best explanation of why someone holds a moral judgment is a process that is insensitive to the considerations cited as justifying that judgment, that is a defeasible reason to distrust it.

7. As I currently see it, that undermining story applies more readily to conservative judgments in the loyalty, authority, and sanctity domain than to liberal care/fairness judgments. Outgroup-directed loyalty, deference under threat, and purity policing look more often driven by disgust, fear, and conformist signaling, with justification produced after the fact, whereas care and fairness judgments more often track the suffering or cheating they claim to be about.

8. But I do not think liberals are exempt. Plenty of left-coded moral judgments are plausibly explained by elite messaging, self-presentation, coalitional psychology, and disgust at outgroups' taboo violations, and the same debunking test should be run on them without special pleading.

9. All of this leaves me on the left, but less certain and less moralistic. I do not think the psychology forces a move toward conservatism; I think it makes entrenched moral disagreement look less like one side failing at inference and more like a conflict among differently weighted sensibilities.

What I want is an audit of this set. Go through the commitments and say which pairs are genuinely in tension, which are only apparently in tension and dissolve once the wording is read carefully, and which are compatible despite seeming otherwise. Rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each, say what minimally has to give and what the cheapest repair is, including what that repair costs elsewhere in the set. Then say which single revision would do the most to make the whole profile coherent, and defend that choice. I would rather have a definite verdict from you than a menu of options I would then have to choose from.
