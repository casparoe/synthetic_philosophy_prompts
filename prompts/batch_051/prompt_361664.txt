I have recently returned to the practice of prayer after a long absence, and I find I can no longer set aside the puzzle that used to bother me only in the abstract. If God already knows everything I need and cannot be moved by anything I say, then petitionary prayer looks either pointless or incoherent. I came across a passage in Thomas Aquinas's Summa Theologiae (Second Part of the Second Part, Question 83, Article 2, "Whether it is becoming to pray?") that states the standard solution in compressed form:

"we pray not that we may change the Divine disposition, but that we may impetrate that which God has disposed to be fulfilled by our prayers in other words "that by asking, men may deserve to receive what Almighty God from eternity has disposed to give," as Gregory says"

Before I read further in the contemporary discussion, I want a worked-out specification of what any adequate theory of petitionary prayer to an omniscient and immutable God must deliver. Write the desiderata: each requirement, the case or consideration behind it, and a clear view of which are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of one camp. I want the criteria themselves to be the deliverable, so keep any scorecard of existing views brief or skip it entirely.

In particular, I need to know whether the Aquinas passage imposes a non-negotiable requirement that a theory must not represent prayer as informing God, bending the divine will, or changing the divine disposition; and whether that requirement can be jointly satisfied with the requirement that prayer be genuinely efficacious — that it really obtains goods that would not otherwise have been obtained, rather than being only a psychological exercise or a piece of self-management. Also flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most to exclude candidate theories. The cases I most want the list to get right are these: the apparently unanswered petition, the prayer that coincided with what would have happened anyway, the prayer for inward change, and the petition for a specific external good.
