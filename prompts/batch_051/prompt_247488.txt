The standard Condorcet-jury-theorem argument for majority rule says: if each voter is independantly more likely than not to get a question right, then a large enough group will get it right with probability approaching 1 under majority rule. The usual gloss on independent is just that voters aren't copying one another or all reading the same newspaper. Now suppose the judgments are being made by people who all consult the same LLM — sometimes applying their own judgment, but with their errors now sharing a common cause (same model, same training data). Does the theorem still go through, does it need repair, or is it dead for this setting? If it survives, show why the common-LLM correlation isn't fatal. If not, say exactly which premise is under pressure and whether there's a repaired version that says something weaker but still worth having.
