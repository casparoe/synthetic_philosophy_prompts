Since retiring from a career in the natural sciences, I have been reading the philosophy of statistics, and I keep returning to a disagreement about how much of the replication crisis is genuinely a statistical-epistemology problem and how much is an incentive problem dressed in statistical language. One camp argues that the core defect is the field-wide habit of treating a p-value below 0.05 as if it certified a finding: the null-alternative asymmetry, the neglect of base rates and prior plausibility, and the reliance on long-run sampling behavior that the actual research process almost never supplies. On this view, practices like p-hacking, optional stopping, and the file drawer do their damage mainly by corrupting the sampling distributions that frequentist significance testing pretends to operate on, and a community that instead reported posterior probabilities against explicit priors would have been less likely to take seriously and later abandon many findings. The other camp argues that the statistical formalism is nearly epiphenomenal: keep the incentives fixed — publish-or-perish, novelty bias, journals that reward clean stories — and an unreliable literature will reappear under whichever statistical philosophy is in fashion, with only the technical vocabulary changing.

I would like to know how one could actually test this dispute rather than simply argue about it. Design a study — observational, quasi-experimental, or a coordinated prospective comparison — in which the statistical epistemology varies while the incentive structure is held roughly fixed, or the reverse. Be concrete about the units you would compare, the contrast, and the outcome variable. What pattern of results would favor the view that the statistical philosophy is doing real causal work, and what pattern would favor the view that incentives dominate and the formalism is mostly cosmetic? Then state exactly what a favorable result on either side would and would not settle: which part of the disagreement is empirical, which part remains a prior philosophical commitment, and where the two readings cannot be separated in practice because the counterfactual is unobservable or the concepts themselves blur. I am not looking for a survey of positions — I want a concrete test design and an honest account of its limits.
