Please argue that a rational agent with imperfect knowledge of her own preferences can sometimes be justified in honouring sunk costs — not as a second-best concession to weakness of will, but because the fact that she's already paid the cost is itself evidence about how much she values the project. Then do the harder thing: say exactly where this stops being a genuine exception and starts being bog-standard escalation of commitment. I'd like you to seperate out the conditions carefully and steelman the exception rather than survey the literature; a counterintuitive conclusion is fine, but I don't want special pleading.
