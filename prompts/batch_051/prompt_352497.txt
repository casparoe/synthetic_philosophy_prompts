Take Singapore from independence through roughly 1990 under Lee Kuan Yew and run it through the performance-legitimacy idea: that a government can earn the right to rule by delivering growth, stability, and public goods rather than (or not mainly) through elections. I already know the broad story — the PAP's near-total dominance, the HDB housing push, the economic takeoff, the Internal Security Act detentions, the controlled press — so skip the retelling and give me the case analysis: what the theory predicts the arrangement should look like when it's working, and where it stops explaining what actually happened. In particular, does the theory understate the role of coercion and the way early coercion keeps doing work after growth arrives? Can performance legitimacy really be distinguished from fear of falling back into disorder, or from simply getting lucky on the economy? And what does Singapore do to the theory — is it the cleanest case the theory has, or does it show the theory is mostly a story people tell about whoever delivers competence? Be explicit about where the theory goes silent rather then forcing it to speak.
