A survey article about the philosophy of time offers the following one-paragraph summary of J. M. E. McTaggart's argument in "The Unreality of Time," Mind (1908):

"McTaggart's core argument is simple. Time requires change. The B-series alone cannot give us change, because every event keeps its permanent slot in the earlier–later order; all the B-series can do is record that events stand in changeless relations. For change, you need the A-series, in which an event's tense is always shifting from future to present to past. But the A-series involves a contradiction: one and the same event must be future, present, and past, and that is impossible. Since the only thing that could make time real is contradictory, time is unreal."

Next to that summary, the article prints this passage from the paper:

"If, then, a B series without an A series can constitute time, change must be possible without an A series. Let us suppose that the distinction of past, present and future does not apply to reality. Can change apply to reality? What is it that changes?

Could we say that, in a time which formed a B series but not an A series, the change consisted in the fact that an event ceased to be an event, while another event began to be an event? If this were the case, we should certainly have got a change.

But this is impossible. An event can never cease to be an event."

The article presents the summary as a reliable digest of the passage, but the match between them feels off to me in ways I cannot quite articulate. Please audit the summary sentence by sentence. For each sentence, tell me what is faithful to the passage and to McTaggart's larger argument, what is an acceptable compression, and what would make McTaggart or a careful defender of his argument wince. Then say whether the summary's conclusion—that time is unreal because the only thing that could supply change is contradictory—actually follows from anything in the passage, and what a defender of the B-theory would be entitled to say in reply.
