Explain exactly what Descartes's cogito establishes. Does it establish only that I exist whenever I think, or does it also establish something about what kind of thing I am—for instance, that I am a thinking substance, or that I am distinct from my body? Be specific.

And heres the student's answer, verbatim:

Descartes's cogito is supposed to survive the most radical doubt. Even if an evil demon is deceiving me about the external world, my body, and mathematics, it can't deceive me about the fact that I, the one being deceived, exist. What the cogito strictly proves is just that this thinking subject exists at the moment of thinking. Some people object that at best there is thinking going on, not an I, but that's too quick: the thinking is given to me as mine, and that is enough for Descartes to conclude that I am a thinking thing, which is distinct from any body. Since thinking is an attribute and attributes need a substance, the cogito secures a thinking substance, although it doesn't by itself show that this substance can survive death. Descartes later uses the clarity and distinctness of this perception to argue for the real distinction between mind and body, but the immaterial self is already on the table from the cogito alone.
