I'm a philosophy professor putting together my considered view on autonomus systems and responsibility for a workshop, and I'd like a consistency audit before I circulate it. I don't want a reading list — I want you to actually push on the commitments. Here's where I've landed:

(a) Moral responsibility is fundamentally about quality of will: whether the conduct expresses the sort of disregard, negligence, or ill will that makes reactive attitudes like blame apt. Whether the person could have done otherwise is neither here nor there.

(b) Genuine responsibility gaps exist. When a self-driving car causes a serious collision through an interaction nobody designed or foresaw, it can be the case that no individual engineer, no manager, and no corporation is morally blameworthy, and the car itself isn't a candidate for blame either.

(c) Even so, the victims are owed a public accounting of what happened and repair for the harm. Someone identifiable has to answer for it.

(d) The right legal response is strict liability on the deploying company, but we should be careful not to dress that up as moral blame.

(e) The engineers aren't blameworthy for emergent behaviour they couldn't reasonably have foreseen, however bad the outcome.

(f) If an AI system became genuinely responsive to moral reasons, it could be a proper target of moral blame even though it can't suffer.

(g) Retributive punishment is justified only toward someone who is morally blameworthy, so in the gap cases nothing retributive should happen.

(h) A corporation can have a blameworthy quality of will — through its policies, testing regimes, and incentive structures — even when no individual employee is blameworthy.

Audit this set for me. Go through the commitments, identify which ones (or pairs) are in tension, and order the tensions by severity. Be clear about which tensions are only apparent and dissolve on closer inspection, and which are real. For each real tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision is. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set hang together, and what it costs.

Keep it under 900 words, and write in British English. I'd rather have fewer points made properly than a map of every possible objection.
