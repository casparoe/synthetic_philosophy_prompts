I've been trying to work out whether information about me is the kind of thing I can own, and my gut keeps handing me verdicts that dont obviously fit one principle. Here are the cases and what I actually think about each:

1. A fitness-tracker company takes heart-rate and sleep data from its users, strips off names, and sells the aggregate to a health insurer. Fine.
2. A data broker scrapes my public social-media posts and public records, builds a profile of me by name, and sells it to advertisers. Not fine, even though all the source material was public.
3. A hospital uses my de-identified medical records in a study that leads to a profitable drug. I get nothing. Fine.
4. A friend writes a memoir that accurately recounts conversations we had and some things I told her in confidence about my divorce. Not fine. But I keep going back and forth on whether the problem is that she betrayed a confidence or that she's selling my life without cutting me in. Treat that as part of the puzzle.
5. A company photographs me in the background on a public street and uses the photo in a stock image for an ad campaign. Fine, basically.
6. An AI company scrapes all my public blog posts to train a model that can pastiche my writing. Fine.
7. Someone takes a model like that and sells "new essays by [me]" without my involvement. Not fine.
8. I sell my genetic data to a consumer genetic-testing company, which later licenses it to a drug company for a study. I signed the form. Begrudgingly fine, but the begrudging part feels important.

My rough principle so far is: you own information about you when it's identifying and it was collected in a context where you had a reasonable expectation of control. But that principle seems to misjudge case 2 (it's identifying, and it's from public records, so I should be fine with it, but I'm not) and it might let case 4 off the hook for the wrong reason. I want you to tell me, as precisely as you can, what principle actually captures these verdicts—or most of them. If no clean principle gets all eight, tell me which of my verdicts I should overturn and why. Then apply your principle to a case I still can't settle: a company pays my grocery store for my purchase history, keyed to a persistent loyalty-card ID that isn't my legal name but can be linked back to me with a little work. Does that cross the line? Please actually commit to a position and defend it, not just lay out options. And write the answer in American English.
