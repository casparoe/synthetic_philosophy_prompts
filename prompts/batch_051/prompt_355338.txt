Please give me your credence in the following thesis, with the reasoning decomposed rather than compressed into a single number. The thesis: for any future-tensed assertion made now about a specific contingent occurrence — Aristotle's sea-battle case is the canonical example, an assertion made today about whether a naval battle will be fought tomorrow — the assertion is already either determinately true or determinately false now, even though nothing in the present state of the world together with the laws of nature settles whether the occurrence will take place. If you regard the thesis as ambiguous between a semantic claim about truth values and a metaphysical claim about settledness, distinguish those readings and assign a credence to each.

For the reading you take to be the substantive one, I want the decomposition. Among the terms I expect to see are these, though you may add or merge them if you think that better captures where the real work is: (i) whether a proposition can be true at a time with no present truthmaker; (ii) whether the sea-battle argument's move from present truth to the non-contingency of the future event is a modal fallacy rather than a genuine implication; (iii) whether assigning future contingents some third status, such as neither true nor false, does genuine explanatory work rather than relabeling the difficulty; (iv) whether the openness of the future is best located in the world, in our knowledge, or in the semantics of the future tense; and (v) how much weight to give the retrospective practice in which, once an event has occurred, we say it was true all along that it would occur. For each term give a rough probability and one or two lines of justification, and then show how the terms combine into your final number.

Then tell me which single term your final credence is most sensitive to — the one whose movement would move your answer most — and state what argument or evidence would most likely move that term. For context: my own credence in the thesis is around 0.55, and a colleague's is around 0.15. On your decomposition, where is a difference of that size most likely located?
