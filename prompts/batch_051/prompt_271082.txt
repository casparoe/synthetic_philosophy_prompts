I am drafting a term-paper proposal for a philosophy of mind seminar — around 6,000 words over one semester — and I keep circling a worry I cannot sharpen on my own. When I say my dog wants to go outside, part of what seems to license the attribution is that wanting is the kind of state natural selection builds: a mechanism that moves an organism toward outcomes because organisms wired that way left more descendants. When I say a reinforcement-learning agent 'wants' to maximize expected return, I suspect I am speaking only metaphorically. The designers supplied the objective function; the training dynamics made the policy persist; nothing in the system's history made any outcome something it was selected to secure, as opposed to something it is built to pursue given its present causal organization. And yet it would be strange if wanting were a historical honorific rather than a claim about what a system is doing now. So I cannot tell whether such a system literally wants what it pursues, merely exhibits a want-like pattern, or whether 'want' is simply the wrong category for it.

I would like you to turn this into the proposal itself. State the question so that it has a determinate answer — something like: is the property of wanting an outcome constituted by the causal-functional role of a state within the agent's present psychology and action-governing economy, or is it partly constituted by the historical process that produced that role, and if it is partly historical, can deliberate design stand in for a history of selection, or does design merely borrow its intentionality from the designer? Then explain why the existing positions leave this open, lay out why a philosophy-of-mind-and-action approach makes progress here where a purely engineering or formal decision-theoretic treatment does not, say what the possible conclusions are and which considerations would decide among them, identify what would count as a failed project, and give a realistic scope for one semester. Please describe positions by their argumentative shape rather than by citation; do not include a bibliography, as I will fill in the literature myself. Begin with the proposal itself, with no preface or commentary.
