My friend tried to summarize illusionism about phenomenal consciousness for me, and I can't tell how much of it to trust. Here's what he said, roughly:

"So illusionism is basically the view that consciousness is fake — not 'fake' like a trick, but fake in the sense that there is no such thing as what-it's-like-ness at all. Bite into a lemon and it seems to you that there's this sharp, private, citrusy zing, but according to illusionists there isn't: there's only a neural state, and your introspection somehow reads that state as having this extra 'phenomenal glow' that no such state actually has. The trouble is the whole story runs on the word 'reads.' An illusion is a conscious experience — something it's like to be taken in — so the view quietly assumes the thing it says doesn't exist. And once you push on that, it collapses into a boring terminological point where they've redefined 'consciousness' to mean whatever their science can explain, and then declared the real thing an illusion."

I suspect at least one of those moves is a caricature, but he states it all with such confidence that Im not sure which parts a careful illusionist would actually sign off on.
