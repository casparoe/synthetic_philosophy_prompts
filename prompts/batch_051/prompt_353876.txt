Retired physicist here, only recently started working through the Epicurean argument about death. The famous line gets all the attention, but the passage that actually stops me in Epicurus's Letter to Menoeceus is this: "We must remember that the future is neither wholly ours nor wholly not ours, so that neither must we count upon it as quite certain to come nor despair of it as quite certain not to come." If I take that seriously, I dont see how an Epicurean can coherently accept life-extending treatment. Choosing surgery or chemo seems like counting on future time as if it were ours, which is exactly what he warns against. Should a consistent Epicurean refuse the treatment and just enjoy whatever time is left?
