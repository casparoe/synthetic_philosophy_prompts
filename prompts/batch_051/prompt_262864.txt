I run the analytics team at a clinical commissioning group. Maybe once a month we get an evaluation claiming a treatment is better overall, but when you split by age or severity or site the effect flips or disappears — the classic Simpson's-paradox shape. I want a decision procedure my analysts can run before anything goes to the medicines optimisation committee. Please write it as an ordered list of questions: what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure says stop and only report the pooled result, where it says report the subgroup results instead, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of judgement rather than arithmetic. Assume my analysts have stats training but no proper causal-inference background, and each report gets at most half a day. The stakes are whether a treatment goes on the formulary or gets decommissioned, and at least one worked case should come out with an answer my committee won't like. Then run the procedure on two cases: (1) a new anticoagulant that looks better overall but worse in every risk band once you stratify, and (2) a surgical pathway that looks better in each hospital but worse overall because the sickest patients are sent to the best-resourced sites. Also say what the procedure cannot protect us from.
