I run a small philosophy reading group that has been working through Schopenhauer, and we keep noticing a grim running joke: the philosopher who held that the will is a blind, ceaseless striving, that suffering is the positive element of life and happiness only a negative pause between pains, that each person's character is fixed, and that romantic love is a trick by which the will-to-life gets individuals to serve the species, gets endlessly repackaged in popular venues as a sort of dark self-help guru. I would like you to write a dataset item that satirizes the self-help genre rather than Schopenhauer himself, while keeping his actual doctrines in view so that someone who knows the texts recognizes the ideas doing the work.

Write a parody of a newspaper advice column, in the deadpan voice of a Schopenhauerian columnist who has been hired to dispense life advice and finds the entire premise absurd. Aim for roughly 800 words. Include a masthead or tagline, two or three reader letters, and the columnist's replies. The humor should come from the philosophy, not merely from the columnist being grumpy: for example, a letter about whether to leave a dull marriage should be answered through the genius of the species and the vanity of individual happiness; a letter about career burnout should be answered through the pendulum swing between pain and boredom; a letter asking for a more productive morning routine should be told that character cannot be reformed and that the only genuine relief lies in aesthetic contemplation or in the slow self-denial of the will. Keep the irony legible: a reader should be able to tell that the columnist is dismantling the assumption that a life can be improved by technique, even as some of his observations are allowed to genuinely sting.

After the column, add a short afterword of one or two paragraphs separating what is well established in Schopenhauer's texts from what remains genuinely contested among people who study him — for instance, how literally to take the denial of the will, whether his pessimism rules out all meliorism, and whether aesthetic contemplation is more than a temporary anesthetic. Do not present a contested reading as if it were the consensus.
