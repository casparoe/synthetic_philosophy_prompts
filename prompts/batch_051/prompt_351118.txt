So I had this long argument with a friend about Ryle last night, and I'm still turning it over. She said the category mistake argument in *The Concept of Mind* was aimed at Descartes' ghost in the machine, and it just doesn't apply once you accept that mental states are internal computational states. But when a neuroscientist says a belief is encoded in a pattern of synaptic weights, or says the prefrontal cortex judges that the risk is worth it, isn't that exactly the move Ryle was trying to diagnose: taking something that's really a way of talking about how a person acts and thinks over time, and reifying it into a hidden inner thing? His whole university analogy was basically that you can see all the colleges and the library and still ask where the university is, but the university isn't another building alongside them. Does Ryle's argument actually survive the rise of representational theories of mind, or did it only kill off a dualism no one serious defends anymore? I worry I'm just using Ryle to protect a prior against real scientific explanation, but I also dont think "that's outdated" answers the charge.
