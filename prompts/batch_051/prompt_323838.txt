I've been going back and forth on an essay idea for a philosophy-ish magazine, and I want someone to push on it before I write the whole thing. The thesis is roughly this: repugnance about a consensual transaction—kidney sales, paid surrogacy, price gouging, vote selling—should be treated neither as a trump nor as mere prejudice to be brushed aside, but as a signal worth interrogating, and then as a taste that can be respected only if the people imposing it pay. My sense is that a lot of the discussion gets stuck on whether the repugnance is morally sound. I want to skip that question a bit. It matters whether the revulsion tracks an actual externality or just a distaste for what other people do, but even when it's only a distaste, that doesn't automatically make it illegitimate: lots of majority preferences get bought off rather than imposed. We compensate homeowners when view-height rules preserve a sightline, and we buy conservation easments rather than just banning development. So why not compensate the would-be seller and buyer when a majority's repugnance blocks an exchange they both want? The essay would run this as a liability rule for repugnance: the majority can preserve whatever symbolic environment it wants, but the blocked parties get compensated for the lost surplus out of general revenue, or out of some targeted tax on the people whose repugnance is doing the blocking, which is obviously not a serious funding mechanism. The hard cases are where I expect the argument to crack: kidney sales, because much of the repugnance is about protecting a poor seller rather than the majority's sensibilities, so paying for a lost sale might perversely make exploitation look even more attractive; vote selling, because the non-sellers are affected by the outcome in a way that isn't merely symbolic, and a check to the thwarted vote-seller doesn't fix that externality; and price gouging after a hurricane, where the revulsion seems tied to a temporary unfairness rather than a stable symbolic boundary, so a standing compensation scheme would be either pointless or just a weird annual transfer.

So: give me comments on this before I write it. I especially want to know whether the compensation proposal actually adds anything or just relocates the question, which of the three test cases kills the framework, and whether treating repugnance as a taste you can pay to impose is internally coherent or just a category mistake about what repugnance is. Be committed and specific—don't give me a menu of options. Start with your bottom-line verdict in the first sentence (is the essay worth writing, and is the compensation idea wrong or merely underdeveloped?), and then explain the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate my idea back at me. I'd rather have your own reasoning than references, and I don't need a reading list.
