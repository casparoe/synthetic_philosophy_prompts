Here is my opening move. The relativity objection, in its classic Rietdijk–Putnam form, runs as follows: special relativity entails that simultaneity is not absolute, so different inertial observers in relative motion disagree about which spatially separated events occur at the same time. There is therefore no frame-independent fact of the matter about what is happening now across space, and hence no global, privileged present for presentism to be true of. As a physicist I was trained to take Minkowski spacetime at face value, and on that picture there is only the four-dimensional manifold, with the present apparently nothing more than an observer-dependent slice. So how does a presentist avoid making existence frame-relative—and does frame-relative existence not amount to abandoning the view?
