Here is a spectrum argument meant to undermine the transitivity of evaluative comparisons. Consider worlds W0 through W1000. W0 contains one person with no headache. At each step, the person's headache increases by an amount too small to notice and too small to ground any reasonable preference for Wk over Wk+1. W1000 contains an excruciating headache. The argument runs:

(1) For each k, Wk+1 is not worse than Wk; an increase too small to notice or to fault cannot make the later world worse than the earlier one.
(2) "Not worse than" is transitive: if X is not worse than Y and Y is not worse than Z, then X is not worse than Z.
(3) By repeated application of (2) to the chain in (1), W1000 is not worse than W0.
(4) But W1000 is worse than W0.
(5) Therefore "not worse than" is not transitive.
