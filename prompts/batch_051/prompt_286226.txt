A magazine piece I read summarised Mill's harm principle this way: "Mill held that the only legitimate ground for restricting anyone's liberty is preventing harm to other people. Since offending someone is not harming them, speech can virtually never be restricted; accordingly, a consistent Millian must oppose hate-speech laws in all but the most unusual cases, and Mill's defence of free speech is simply this principle applied to discussion." I suspect this is too neat, but I cannot tell exactly where it goes wrong, if it does. Is this a fair summary of Mill's view?
