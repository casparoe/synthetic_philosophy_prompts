I am a philosopher of mind with competing commitments. I am attracted to a deflationary, multiple-drafts view of consciousness: there is no single moment or place at which a content enters awareness, no canonical version of an experience, and questions about the exact timing of awareness often have no determinate answer. This view earns its keep by dissolving otherwise baffling findings. In the colour-phi phenomenon, a red spot is flashed at one location and a green spot shortly afterwards at another; subjects report a single spot moving continuously across and changing colour midway, even though the second flash occurs only after the point at which the colour change is reported. The theory's diagnosis is that later information feeds back into an ongoing, revisable process of construction and report, so there is no privileged original experience to be contradicted. The Orwellian description (the original experience was overwritten after the fact) and the Stalinesque description (the experience was not fixed until both flashes were in) are, on this view, empirically equivalent redescriptions of one and the same process. Yet I cannot shake the conviction that there is a fact of the matter about what I was conscious of at a given moment, and that the difference between having an experience and retrospectively revising one's memory or report of it is a real difference rather than a difference of labelling. Present both sides at their strongest and tell me what should yield. Specifically: what would have to be true for the intuition to be the thing I abandon, what would a theory that preserves the intuition have to look like and what would it give up, and is there a reading on which the deflationary account and the conviction in determinate temporal experience are compatible? Please answer in British English.
