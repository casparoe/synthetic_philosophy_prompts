I run a small political-philosophy blog and I've noticed my readers sort themselves into the wrong camps. They call themselves rawlsians or inclusionists but then answer concrete cases in ways that cut against the label. So I'd like to make a "which view of religious reasons in politics do you actually hold?" quiz. The quiz should sort by what people say about cases, not by which label they like. The positions I want as leaves are: (a) exclusive public reason, meaning public officials shouldn't offer religious justifications for coercive decisions, since mere citizens can't be expected to share them; (b) Rawlsian public reason with the proviso, meaning religious reasons can be introduced in public debate but by the time it comes to supporting the actual law, proper public reasons have to be on the table too; (c) convergence liberalism, meaning what matters is that each citizen can accept the law from within their own comprehensive view, and there doesn't have to be one common set of public reasons; (d) permissive inclusionism, meaning ordinary citizens and maybe officials can use whatever reasons they actually have, and public reason is only a demand on institutions, not on individual speech; and (e) secular exclusivism or laïcité, meaning the problem isn't shareability but religion as such, so public institutions should be cleanly secular.

I'd like eight to ten concrete multiple-choice scenarios, each with two or three answer options that actually discriminate among those positions. Examples of the kind of case I mean: a school-board member voting on a sex-ed curriculum partly because her church teaches a particular view of adolescence; a legislator supporting assisted-dying legislation partly because he thinks God's mercy requires it; a judge writing a dissent that cites Aquinas; a citizen voting in a referendum on approving a mosque or a Jewish eruv; a public-health official deciding whether to allow a religious exemption to a vaccine mandate. Don't use all of these, and don't use only Christian examples; include at least two questions about non-Christian minority practices. Make the cases small and administrative rather than grand wedge issues. Then provide a scoring key mapping answer patterns to the leaves, and flag any pairs of views your questions cannot seperate and why. The whole point is for readers to discover what they actually think, so no question should just ask "do you think religious reasons are okay in politics?"
