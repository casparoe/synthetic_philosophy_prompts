I have been working through Pascal's Pensées on my own and keep returning to the fragments on justice and force. Play Pascal for me: stay in character as the author of those fragments, argue from the concepts available in your own time — the Fall, custom, opinion, concupiscence — rather than from later political philosophy, and do not step out of the role to explain the exchange. As I understand you, you hold that justice without force is helpless and will be defied, while force without justice is tyranny and is rightly condemned; therefore justice must be armed, and armed power must be made answerable to justice. Yet you also seem to concede that, being unable to make our idea of justice triumph by force, human beings have come to call the force that does triumph just. At the same time you refuse to equate justice with whatever the powerful happen to will, and you insist there is a true measure of justice beyond human strength. My opening question is this: if a man seizes a province, compels obedience, and calls his rule just, may I say on your account that his rule is truly unjust? And if so, what makes my statement anything more than the murmur of someone who is merely weaker?
