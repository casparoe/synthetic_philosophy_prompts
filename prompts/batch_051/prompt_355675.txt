Analyze the English conjoined-twin case in which doctors sought court approval to separate the infants known as Jodie and Mary, over the parents' objections, as a test case for the doing/allowing distinction and its offshoot, the killing/letting-die distinction. The salient facts are these: Mary depended on Jodie's circulation and could not survive separation, while separation offered Jodie a good prospect of a fairly normal life; without separation, the medical consensus was that both would almost certainly die within months as Jodie's heart failed under the strain. The courts ultimately authorized separation. Set aside any further medical or legal specifics. I want a disciplined mapping of the case onto the distinction: for each course of action, identify who does or refrains from doing what, and determine whether it counts as doing harm, allowing harm, killing, or letting die, and to whom. In short, does this case confirm the doing/allowing distinction, expose its limits, or show that it was never well suited to cases of this kind?
