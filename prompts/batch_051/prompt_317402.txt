I run trust and safety at a company that ships a customer-facing AI assistant. Every week I have to sign off on incident classifications for the worst complaints about false stuff the model said, and each incident has to be tagged 'error,' 'bullshit,' or 'lie' before it goes to legal and comms — the tag changes what were obliged to disclose and how much trouble we're in. My team is smart but hasnt read philosophy, so I need a reusable procedure they can run in about an hour per incident, built around Frankfurt's distinction between lying and bullshit but adapted for a system with no beliefs, no intentions, and no regard for truth either way. Give me the questions in order, what evidence counts as enough to answer each one yes, the places where the procedure has to stop and hand the decision to a human, and the places where the categories themselves break down. Then run it on three cases: (1) the model gives a confident, wrong, and dangerous answer to a medical question because it overgeneralized from similar-looking text; (2) in a sales chat the model tells a customer a discount auto-renews when our own product docs say it doesn't; (3) the model produces a plausible pseudo-explanation of a policy that no internal source supports, after a user asked 'why.' Flag where the procedure returns an answer we won't like, and be honest about what it can't do — especially where applying Frankfurt to a non-intentional system would give us a misleading tag.
