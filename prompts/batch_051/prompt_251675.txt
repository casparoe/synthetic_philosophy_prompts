I am a PhD student in astrobiology, and I am trying to form my own judgment about the great filter hypothesis: the idea that somewhere between the chemistry of a young terrestrial planet and a galaxy-spanning civilization there is at least one transition so improbable that it explains why we do not see clear signs of extraterrestrial intelligence. I would like you to rank the following candidate transitions by how likely each is to be the principal bottleneck, and to defend the ranking.

The candidates are:

1. From prebiotic chemistry to the first self-replicating molecule (the origin of life).
2. From self-replicating molecules to encapsulated cellular life with a coupled DNA–RNA–protein metabolism (the last universal common ancestor).
3. From anaerobic prokaryotic life to oxygenic photosynthesis and an oxygen-rich biosphere.
4. From prokaryotic cells to eukaryotic cells via endosymbiosis.
5. From single-celled eukaryotes to complex multicellular organisms with differentiated tissues.
6. From complex multicellular animals to advanced cognition with symbolic communication and cumulative culture.
7. From cumulative culture to a technological civilization capable of astronomy, energy-intensive industry, and deliberate long-range signaling.
8. From a technological civilization to a durable, expanding, galaxy-colonizing civilization.

Please structure your response as follows:

- Tier list (roughly 150 words): present three tiers—"most likely to be the great filter," "plausible but less likely," and "least likely"—and assign the transitions to them.
- Reasoning per transition (roughly 600 words in total, about 70–80 words per transition): justify each placement, including the considerations that most affect your estimate.
- Robustness (roughly 150 words): identify the two placements you are least confident about and say what kind of evidence would change them.
