For an advanced undergraduate seminar on the boundary problem in democratic theory, produce a diagnostic quiz that assigns respondents to an account of who belongs to the demos on the basis of how they judge concrete cases. It should have eight to ten questions, each built around a short case with two or three answer options about who gets a vote or a formal say. Cases should include at least: a non-citizen permanent resident of many years; a citizen who has lived abroad for decades and pays no taxes; a sixteen-year-old working full-time; a prisoner serving a sentence; residents of a neighboring country whose lakes are acidified by the home state’s coal plants; future people affected by current climate policy; a foreign firm disrupted by the state’s tariffs; a tourist who must obey local traffic laws for a week; and a stateless long-term resident. The questions must never name the theoretical positions, should avoid terms like "demos," "all-affected," or "all-subjected," and should be answerable by someone who has not read the literature.

The scoring key should map answer patterns onto the main positions: the all-subjected (coercion) principle in both a strict version and a residency-threshold version; the all-affected-interests principle; a weighted-stakeholder view; and a civic-membership or particularist view. Build the key from the logical space rather than from this list of labels. First set out the central splits—whether the basis of inclusion is subjection to coercion, affectedness of interests, or membership; whether affectedness warrants equal or interest-proportional influence; whether any subjection suffices or only pervasive, ongoing subjection; and whether competence or civic maturity overrides the other principles. Then locate each named position among the available combinations, mark any unoccupied combination with a one-sentence diagnosis of whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely underexplored, and flag any pair of positions the instrument cannot distinguish. The result should be self-contained and usable as a handout, with no bibliography or citations.
