I'm taking a seminar on Schopenhauer's pessimism and the metaphysics of the will. In The World as Will and Idea, Schopenhauer defends the view that the will is an endless, restless striving that can never achieve a final satisfaction, and that every satisfaction is only the removal of a pain. The standard arguments for his pessimism that I already have are: first, the argument from the structure of desire, according to which all willing arises from a lack, so life is essentially want and privation; second, the argument that suffering is positive and pleasure is merely its temporary negation; third, the argument from boredom, according to which once desires are satisfied, life reveals itself as empty and unbearable; and fourth, the point that the will's striving is not directed at any attainable final state but only at its own continuation. Give me one more argument for Schopenhauerian pessimism that is not on that list and not a mere variant of these, and then assess whether it genuinely contributes something the standard arguments miss.
