Write a sestina (six six-line stanzas, then a three-line envoi, same six end-words rotated in the usual way) about the quantum suicide thought experiment. Let the six end-words be drawn from the fixed parts of the set-up — trigger, spin, branch, alive, dead, and so on — so the return and rotation of the same words across stanzas does the work of the experiment being run again and again, each stanza a new branching. The envoi should hold together the precise puzzle: the experimenter keeps finding himself alive run after run, while branch weights (not just branch counts) pull against any quick inference to immortality. Keep that tension open rather than resolving it. Don't make it a spooky poem about parallel selves; someone who knows the many-worlds interpretation should find it accurate about superposition, first-person survival and the Born rule, and someone who doesn't should still get a decent poem. British English, please.
