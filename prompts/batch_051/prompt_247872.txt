So I'm a self-taught programmer, the only logic I really know is the stuff you pick up from types and tests, never took a class in any of this. Lately I keep stumbling on descriptions of intuitionistic logic and constructivism in math, and it sounds weirdly like opinions I already formed from writing code, but I cant tell if I'm actually understanding it or just pattern-matching it onto my own habits.

The vocabulary I think in: types as collections of values, functions from A to B, an inhabited type being one that has at least one value you can actually produce, the bottom type as a type with no values (in a sound language), exceptions and nontermination as the ways a function can fail to really deliver a B, and decidability/the halting problem as the built-in limit on what code can compute.

Restate the constructive/intuitionistic position in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report. I already know the easy mappings: implication is a function from proofs of the premise to proofs of the conclusion, which lines up with a function type; conjunction is a pair or record; disjunction is a tagged union like Either, where you have to say which side you actually have; negation is a function from P to bottom. And I can see why double negation elimination fails: a function from (P -> bottom) -> bottom is not a value of P, it just shows the refutation is absurd. Similarly excluded middle would be a function that, for any P, hands you either a P or a not-P, which would be a decision oracle for everything, and that crashes into the halting problem.

The interesting part is what does not survive. For example, when constructivists say the only sense in which a mathematical claim is true is that there is a proof for it, or that there is no fact of the matter before the thing has been decided one way or the other — is that the same as my everyday view that a type is uninhabited only if no value of it exists, whether or not my language is able to exhibit one? Or am I sneaking in my own realism, treating inhabitation as settled independently of anyone constructing anything, which might be exactly what these people are denying? Also, they seem to disagree among themselves over a principle I half remember: something like if you can prove that a search cannot fail to terminate, then you are entitled to say it has terminated. Does that disagreement correspond to a real distinction in programming, or is it a genuinely mathematical conflict my vocabulary is too coarse to capture? And the stuff about the continuum not being a finished set, or real numbers being generated over time, I can only wave at with lazy streams or codata — is that gesture carrying it or dropping most of it?

The meta question is what I actually want: after the translation, is the stuff left over just a gap in my vocabulary, or is the philosophical side of constructivism genuinely fuzzier than the Curry-Howard side, and translation into my terms exposes exactly where it gets fuzzy? Translate honestly — don't flatter me by writing off every mismatch as my ignorance, and don't pretend the mapping is cleaner than it is.
