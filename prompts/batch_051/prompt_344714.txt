I've recently inherited a small piece of ex-farmland in the Welsh borders — about thirty acres of poor grazing plus a bit of scrub — and I'd always assumed the right thing was to let it rewild: keep the sheep off, plant nothing, and watch it turn into woodland over a couple of decades. But a friend who works in the effective altruism world sent me some stuff on wild animal welfare, and now the less obvious the right thing seams. The worry I can't shake is that a 'healthy' ecosystem isn't obviously a happy one: more habitat means more voles, more songbirds, more insects, and a lot of those lives are short and end in starvation, disease, or being eaten alive. So I keep coming back to whether destroying or simply not creating habitat could reduce suffering on net, even though that runs against everything I was raised to believe about biodiversity. The decision is mine to make next autumn, and I've got about a year and a bit of spare money to think it through before I have to commit.

Rather than ask for the answer — I know there isn't a settled one — I want a defended sense of which questions underneath all this are actually load-bearing and worth my time. For example, is the central uncertainty about which animals can suffer (insects? nematodes? small mammals?), about population dynamics and whether destroying habitat just shifts suffering elsewhere, about the baseline quality of wild lives, about how to weigh the welfare of animals who would exist under each option, including future generations, or about something I'm not seeing? I'd like you to rank maybe five or six candidate questions by how much they'd change what I do with the land if I could answer them, how tractable they are for a non-biologist with a year, and how neglected they seem. Also tell me which ones you'd deprioritise even though they're philosophically interesting — questions where the answer, whatever it turned out to be, wouldn't actually change the decision. If your ordering depends on a specific view about, say, insect sentience, say so and tell me what would flip it.
