An engineer who removes his own permission to deploy changes outside work hours because he expects his late-night judgment to be bad is seen as admirably self-aware; no one says he has wronged his future self. But when a political community does the analogous thing collectively — a constitution that entrenches rights, requires supermajorities to amend them, or puts monetary policy in the hands of an independent body — the same move is widely criticized as undemocratic: the current majority is tying the hands of future citizens who never consented. Both are precommitments against a predicted failure of judgment. Is there a principled difference that makes the first admirable and the second objectionable, or is the asymmetry something we should give up? I want the strongest justification you can build for whichever side you take, tested against the hardest nearby case, and then a definite verdict: keep the asymmetry, reverse it, or drop it.
