Construct a thought experiment about standing to blame in which two tests for standing come apart in the direction usually thought hardest to obtain: an agent with dirty hands has standing while a clean-handed third party does not. Treat the following tests as stipulated and exhaustive.

Clean Hands test: whether one has standing to blame someone for a wrong W is fixed entirely by whether one is implicated in a wrong of the same kind as W. An implicated agent never has standing, regardless of how W affects them; a clean-handed agent always has standing, regardless of how remote they are from W.

Stake test: whether one has standing is fixed entirely by whether one is W's victim or stands in a morally significant relationship to the victim that W directly damages. A stakeholder always has standing, even if implicated in the same kind of wrong; a non-stakeholder never has standing, even if well-informed and sincere.

Now construct an ordinary-life scenario — a friendship group, a workplace, or a family, not a trolley case and no courts — one paragraph long, with one wrong W, one victim, and exactly two would-be blamers: D, who has a clear stake but is implicated in a recent, widely known wrong of the same kind as W, and C, who is clean-handed but has no stake. Add two further constraints: C's evidence about W is better than D's, and C has a long public record of opposing wrongs of W's kind. The point of the case is to test whether standing is fundamentally a relational position or a record of conduct, by making the Clean Hands test look as attractive as possible before it fails.

After the scenario, verify that the case meets each specification: state W, confirm that D passes Stake and fails Clean Hands, confirm that C passes Clean Hands and fails Stake, and identify the single structural feature doing the work. Then say what would happen if that feature were removed — which test wins, and whether the case collapses into the familiar hypocrisy case. Finish with the strongest reply a defender of Clean Hands could give to deny D's standing while still granting C's, and say whether that reply undercuts the Stake test or simply advertises its cost.
