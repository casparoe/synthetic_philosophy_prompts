I'm trying to get a better grip on Du Bois's line in that 1926 essay where he argues that all art is propaganda, whether the purists like it or not. I don't need a summary of the whole piece or its historical context.

Here are the objections to his propaganda thesis that I already have, so don't just repeat them:
- The aesthetic objection: it dissolves the difference between good art and rhetorically useful art, so a crude protest piece can outrank a subtle one as long as it serves the cause.
- The freedom objection: it turns black artists into employees of a political program and cannot make room for material that is willfully strange, private, or unbeautiful.
- The censorship objection: if black art has a duty to uplift, then work that centers vice, failure, or intra-communal conflict gets treated not just as flawed but as disloyal, which matches Du Bois's actual fights with some Harlem Renaissance writers.
- The respectability objection: it leaves the white gaze in charge by requiring black artists to produce edifying counter-images rather than refusing the demand for respectable representation.
- The monolith objection: it assumes a unified black audience with one shared political need, flattening differences of class, region, gender, sexuality, and politics.

It can come from later criticism or from something you think his position is vulnerable to on its own terms. Then tell me honestly how much damage it does to what he actually says, not to a strawman. Be committed: pick the strongest candidate you can and argue for it rather than giving me a menu.
