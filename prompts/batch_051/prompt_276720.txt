In his essay Freedom and Resentment, P. F. Strawson draws a contrast between the participant reactive attitudes, such as resentment and gratitude, and the objective attitude, in which one regards another human being not as a person from whom goodwill is demanded but as an object of social policy, as something to be managed, handled, cured, or trained. Suppose I take the following four conditions to be individually necessary and jointly sufficient for an attitude to count as a Strawsonian reactive attitude: first, it is a response to the quality of another person’s will as manifested in conduct towards oneself or someone one cares about; second, it is tied to a demand that the other reciprocate a minimal form of goodwill or at least refrain from ill will; third, it is suspended in the ordinary way by excusing considerations such as coercion, accident, or nonculpable ignorance; and fourth, it belongs to the participant rather than the objective standpoint, so that adopting it treats the other as a member of the moral community rather than as a case for management.

Now consider this case. A psychotherapist learns that a long-term patient has for months been deliberately deceiving her in order to obtain prescriptions, and that the deception has caused her serious professional and personal harm. She does not report feeling resentment or moral indignation. Instead, she records that the patient appears to meet the criteria for antisocial personality disorder, revises the treatment plan, and describes her own state as one of calm clinical concern. A colleague tells her that her response is precisely what Strawson called the objective attitude rather than a reactive attitude. She replies that her professional role makes the objective standpoint the appropriate one, and that the distinction between participant and objective attitudes is too rigid to capture her situation.

Does the therapist’s attitude qualify as a reactive attitude according to the four criteria? Work through the criteria one by one, indicate where they underdetermine the verdict, and then say whether the case shows that one of the four criteria needs revision or that Strawson’s contrast between participant and objective attitudes is less clean than he suggests. Please answer as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, in British English, with no introduction or conclusion.
