I've been teaching the assurance view of promises—the one that says a promise binds because the promisor voluntarily induces the promisee to rely on a stated intention, and thereby creates an expectation the promisee is entitled to. Here's a development I want to test it against. I don't see the message until later. Does the assurance view say I'm bound? If yes, where does the voluntary assurance come from—the standing authorization? some duty for one's agents? If no, is the argument failing at the made-by-me step or at the induced-reliance step? Give me a clear verdict, and also tell me which consideration, if it went the other way, would flip your answer, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
