I'm drawn to the Milgrom–Stokey no-trade theorem because it seems to give a systematic account of why purely speculative trade should unravel: if two Bayesian agents share a prior and know each other to be rational, then the very willingness to trade at a given price is itself information that should make both revise until neither wants to trade. But I also can't shake the intuition that when I act on my own research and buy a security I judge undervalued, I'm doing something rational, and that such trades are how dispersed information actually gets impounded into prices. I don't want to be told which side simply wins. I want a negotiated verdict: what would it take for the intuition to be the thing that gives, what would it take for the theorem's applicability to be the thing that gives, and is there a precise reading of the idea that information gets into prices on which both survive?
