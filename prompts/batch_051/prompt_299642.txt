Question: Explain Popper’s use of falsifiability to demarcate science from non-science. Then present the strongest version of the objection that hypotheses cannot be tested in isolation, and evaluate whether Popper’s methodological rules adequately answer that objection.

Student answer: Popper’s criterion is that a theory is scientific just in case it is falsifiable: there must be some possible observation that would contradict it. He treated Einstein’s general relativity as scientific because it risked a definite prediction about the bending of starlight by the sun, whereas he treated Freudian psychoanalysis as unscientific because its adherents could reinterpret any apparent counterexample as actually confirming the theory. A theory that cannot conflict with any possible experience is compatible with every course of events, and therefore tells us nothing about the world.

On this view, scientific practice proceeds by bold conjecture followed by attempted confirmation: researchers look for observations that agree with the theory, and when the theory survives many such tests it is corroborated, which means it has been shown to be probably true. This is what makes science rational.

The strongest objection is the one usually attributed to Duhem: a hypothesis is never tested on its own. Deriving an observation from a hypothesis requires auxiliary assumptions about instruments, background conditions, and other theories, so a failed prediction shows only that at least one member of the whole set is false, never which one. For example, if an astronomical prediction fails, the fault might lie with the telescope calibration rather than the physical theory.

Popper’s reply is to admit that testing is holistic but to add a methodological rule: scientists may modify auxiliary assumptions when a prediction fails, but the modification must be independently testable, and ad hoc modifications that merely protect the theory from refutation are illegitimate. This rule is generally considered to solve Duhem’s problem, because it permits the legitimate auxiliary adjustments scientists actually use while excluding the protective reinterpretation that makes a theory unfalsifiable.
