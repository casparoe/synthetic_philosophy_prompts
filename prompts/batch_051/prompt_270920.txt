I have an argument I cannot settle, and I would like you to identify the exact point of failure. It purports to show that clicking an "I agree" button cannot by itself create a contractual obligation.

(1) A person can acquire a contractual obligation only through an act by which they knowingly and voluntarily assume that obligation.
(2) An act is a knowing assumption of contractual terms only if, at the time of acting, the agent actually knows what the terms say and knows that the act incorporates those terms into the relationship.
(3) In clickwrap contracts of adhesion, the great majority of users click without having read the terms, without knowing what they say, and in an interface designed on the assumption that they will not read them.
(4) Therefore, for the great majority of users, clicking "I agree" is not a knowing and voluntary assumption of the terms.
(5) Therefore, for the great majority of users, clicking "I agree" imposes no contractual obligation.

Since the conclusion conflicts with the way such terms are routinely enforced, at least one step must fail. Tell me precisely which step or steps fail, on what reading, and whether the failure is best diagnosed as an equivocation between legal assent and genuine psychological agreement.
