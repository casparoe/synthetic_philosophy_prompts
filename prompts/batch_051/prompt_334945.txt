I've been trying to get a handle on Wang Yangming's claim that knowing and acting are one, and I keep swinging between thinking it's trivial and thinking it's obviously false. I'm not after the history: I want a working definition of knowing—or maybe really knowing—that makes the unity thesis true but still substantive. It should count a person who can ride a bike without thinking about it as knowing how to ride, and count a parrot trained to squawk 'look after your parents' as not knowing filial piety. It should give a definate answer either way for each of these: (a) a smoker who can spell out in detail why smoking kills but still smokes; (b) someone who sincerely believes they ought to visit a lonely friend, feels guilty about never doing it, and never does it; (c) a student who has just followed and understood a proof but can't reconstruct it the next morning; (d) an actor who behaves with perfect compassion on stage; (e) someone with locked-in syndrome who fully endorses an act of kindness but can't perform it. And bonus if the definition explains why knowing here feels like it comes in degrees.
