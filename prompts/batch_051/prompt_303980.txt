I'm a novelist sketching a near-future book where automation has eaten most jobs, and I keep noticing my own gut verdicts about what still counts as meaningful work. A woodworker hand-carving chairs even though chairs get printed for pennies: meaningful. A lab tech whose entire job is loading samples into an analyzer and pasting the readout into an email: not meaningful, feels like a ghost job. A doctor whose diagnoses all come from an AI but who sits with the patient and delivers the news eye to eye: meaningful. An artist who types prompts into an image generator and signs the ouput as his own: not meaningful. A call-center worker paid to train the AI that will replace her: also not meaningful, but grimmer somehow than the lab tech. A night watchman guarding a fully automated plant where nobody works at all: I keep flip-flopping. Is there one principle that captures all this, or close? What does it say about a maintenance worker who lubes robots that do a job nobody needs, a teacher whose classes are all run by software, or a human-only marathon when a robot could run faster? And if the principle can't fit every verdict, which of mine should I chuck, and why? Keep the answer under 450 words and in American English.
