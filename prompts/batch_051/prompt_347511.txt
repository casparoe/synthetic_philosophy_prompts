Next month I'm giving a talk to my department's philosophy of physics reading group defending the claim that the Rietdijk–Putnam argument really does force a block universe on anyone who accepts special relativity. My line is going to be: the people who try to defuse the argument by saying simultaneity is "just a convention" are smuggling in a notion of what's real "for a frame" while also denying that frames tell you what's real, and they can't keep both. I'll run the standard version of the argument first (for any event spacelike separated from my here-and-now there's some inertial frame where it's simultaneous with me; chains of overlapping frames spread "now" across all of spacetime; so past, present, and future are all equally real), and then I'll spend most of the time defending the metaphysical reading against the conventionality objection instead of just asserting it.

The audience: about fifteen people, mostly philosophers of physics and metaphysicians. Three are committed presentists who know the relativity literature and still think metaphysics gets the last word. Two are growing-block people who think the past and present are real but the future isn't. One is a philosopher of language who thinks this entire debate is people confusing tensed language with ontology and will try to deflate the whole thing. There's also one physicist who gets visibly annoyed whenever a philosopher says "really exists" and thinks the whole question is settled by drawing a Minkowski diagram. The rest are agnostic grad students who will go with whichever argument sounds cleaner on the day.

Give me a Q&A prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get from these people, ordered by roughly how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way each of them would put it, with a short reply I could deliver in a minute or two and a note on where that reply is soft. Tell me what I should concede up front and what I should hold. Include the question I'm praying nobody asks. Don't pad it: I want the prep sheet, not an essay on the block universe.
