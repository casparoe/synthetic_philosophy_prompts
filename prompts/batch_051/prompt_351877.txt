Okay so I've been going down a rabbit hole with Goodman's grue problem — the one about why "all emeralds are green" is a better induction than "all emeralds are grue" even though both fit everything we've observed so far, because grue just means "green if examined before t, blue otherwise." No philosophy background here; just somebody who reads Wikipedia and a few old blogs. I think I've got the standard five answers down. One, Goodman's own: entrenchment — green has a long history of successful projection and grue doesn't, so we get to keep projecting green. Two, the natural kinds or natural properties move: grue isn't a real property, it doesn't carve nature at the joints, so it can't show up in laws. Three, simplicity: green is simpler than grue, and grue is a gerrymandered mash-up that overfits. Four, the "purely qualitative" move: grue is disqualified because its definition mentions a particular future time t, so it's positional rather than qualitative, and induction should stick to qualitative predicates. Five, the Bayesian move: just assign green a higher prior and bury grue down in the priors.

Now heres my problem. Each one strikes me as having the same shape of failure. Entrenchment is just habit, and a community that talked in grue/bleen terms from the start would have entrenched grue instead. The natural kinds, simplicity, and qualitative moves all seem to depend on which language you start in — in a grue/bleen language, green is the weird, time-indexed one. And the Bayesian move just pushes the question into choosing the prior. I want a response to the new riddle that is not on that list and isnt a variant, special case, or combination of those five — so not "simplicity done with Kolmogorov complexity," not "natural kinds but grounded in evolution," nothing like that. If you've got one, lay it out plainly and tell me honestly whether it avoids the language-relativity problem the others get stuck on, or whether it just relocates it. If you don't think there is one, say that too and tell me why these five really do exhaust the options.

Also, please answer in American English.
