My ethics professor has been ruling on a series of harm-prevention cases. So far she has said the following. It is permissible to divert a runaway trolley from five workers onto a siding where one worker will be struck and killed. It is permissible to stop a trolley headed for five by releasing a heavy beam from an overhead bridge, where the beam blocks the track but also crushes a bystander; the beam, not the bystander, is what stops the trolley. It is impermissible to push a large man off a footbridge into the trolley's path, even though his body would stop the trolley and save the five. It is impermissible to cut up one healthy patient to obtain organs that would save five transplant patients. Next week's case is the trapdoor footbridge: a trolley is heading toward five workers, and you can pull a lever that opens a trapdoor under a large man, causing him to fall onto the track below, where his body stops the trolley; you never touch him. Predict her verdict on this new case. Say which rule she is implicitly running — the doctrine of double effect, a personal-force rule, or something else — and give your best guess with your reasons for preferring it, then name the case that would settle it. Reply as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
