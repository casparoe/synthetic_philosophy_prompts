i've been chewing on an idea I first saw in Nishida and I need someone to pressure-test where i'd actually part ways with it. the view is that experience doesn't start out divided into a subject over here and an object over there; at the bottom there's 'pure experience,' a kind of undivided doing/seeing/being that's there before any thought of 'me' facing 'the world.' on this picture the subject-object split — even the felt sense that i'm a self standing over against a tree — is something that gets generated when reflection comes along and tries to make an object out of its own activity. later he put this in terms of a kind of 'place' (basho) that includes both sides: reality isn't a relation between two things that could come apart, it's a place in which the contrast between self and world shows up. this strikes me as a deeply attractive theory. it dissolves the skeptical problem of how a subject can reach out to an object, because there was never a fundamental gap to bridge; it also stops treating the self as a substance and makes it something more like an organizing point inside a field. but the intuition is stubborn: when i look at the tree outside my window, i cant make myself believe the looking and the tree are the same kind of thing. i am the one aware, and the tree is given as other, as facing me. and i can't tell whether that intuition is a datum the theory has to answer to, or just the reflection-generated illusion the theory predicts i'd have. i want the strongest version of both sides and then a negotiated verdict: what would it take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what would it take for the theory to be amended or abandoned, and is there a reading of 'prior' or 'place' on which both honestly survive? don't give me a survey of the kyoto school — tell me which one i should give up and why, or how to keep both without redefining the intuition into something i don't recognize.
