In the course I am currently taking — a seminar on the conceptual foundations of SETI and the far future — we keep treating "advancement" as though it named one ladder, but the two canonical scales clearly measure different things. Kardashev's scale ranks civilizations by the total useful power they can command, moving outward from planetary to stellar to galactic levels, while Barrow's inverted, "microdimensional" scale ranks them by the smallest length scale at which they can deliberately manipulate matter, from macroscopic fabrication down toward molecular, atomic, and nuclear control. The two can come apart: a civilization could be very high on one and ordinary on the other. Before I read any further proposals, I want the full specification that the proposals will be judged against — that is, not a scorecard of existing scales but the desiderata: the requirements that any adequate measure of civilizational advancement must meet, with the case or consideration behind each.

Take the role of a philosopher of science with a background in physics who works on measurement and the conceptual foundations of astrobiology and SETI. Think carefully, and before settling on your final list, brainstorm more candidate requirements than you will use and stress-test each against both scales above.

Then present exactly five numbered requirements, each one or two sentences, with no introductory or concluding remarks. In each item, use the first sentence to state the requirement and mark it as either a settled adequacy condition or a genuinely contested commitment; use the second to give the motivating consideration or case. Do not present a contested view as the consensus. If two of your requirements are in tension, note the tension inside one of the numbered items.
