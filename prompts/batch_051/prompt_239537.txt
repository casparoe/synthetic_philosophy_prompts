I want you to restate Abelard’s position as faithfully as possible in that vocabulary: which elements of an offense correspond to his notion of the morally laden act, which to consent, and how he would classify cases that criminal law treats under attempt, impossibility, or mistake. Use examples such as a person who resolves to kill someone and takes what he believes are the final steps but fails; a person who performs the outward act without any consenting state of mind; and a person who consents to what he believes is a forbidden act, but where the act as it actually occurs is not what he took it to be.

Then give me the residue report: which parts of Abelard’s view map cleanly onto criminal law concepts, which parts you had to distort to force into the framework, and which parts have no legal counterpart at all. In particular, I want to know whether Abelard is effectively proposing a kind of strict liability for mental acts, or whether something in his account of consent resists the law’s way of separating act and intention. I’d also like a verdict on whether the places where the translation fails are places where the law is missing something Abelard saw, or places where Abelard’s distinction is less stable than the law’s.
