I run a reading group that pairs a novel with a problem in the philosophy of mind, and next term we are doing Lois Lowry's The Giver alongside material on novel qualia and impossible experiences. Read the novel through that lens. In it, Jonas begins to see the colour red while everyone around him sees only grey; the community has deliberately suppressed colour, strong emotion, and most memory; and the Giver can transmit whole experiences to Jonas by touch. The novel plainly dramatises the idea that a subject can acquire a genuinely new phenomenal quality, and that such a quality cannot be conveyed by description but only by being given. I would like you to sort out what the book is actually committed to here. Specifically: (a) does the novel treat colour experience as a private quale, as transferable information of the sort a neural interface would have to deliver, or as something else; (b) is the Giver's transmission best understood as a solution to the Mary's-room problem, an evasion of it, or a change of topic; and (c) how does the novel bear on the stronger thesis that there are impossible experiences, the idea behind discussions of reddish-green and bluish-yellow, and could the community have engineered those away, or could the Giver transmit one? Be faithful in both directions: say where the novel genuinely illuminates the philosophy and where an honest critic would say it leans on a contested or confused picture of experience. This is for a reading group, so please write in British English, do not rely on verbatim quotation from the text, and mark clearly which points are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who work on this.
