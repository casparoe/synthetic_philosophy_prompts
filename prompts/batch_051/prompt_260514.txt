Act as an expert philosopher of science who works on causation and is fluent both in the philosophical literature on interventionism and in the causal-modeling / do-calculus tradition. I want you to think before you write: begin with a brainstorming pass in which you generate candidate requirements and stress-test them against hard cases, then settle on a final, argued list rather than reproducing the standard treatment.

I keep seeing interventionist accounts of causation treated as obviously correct in causal-inference circles and as obviously circular in metaphysics seminars, and I want the list of criteria that would let me adjudicate between those reactions. So I am asking for the specification of the job, not a defense of one member of the family. Write the desiderata: what must any adequate interventionist or manipulationist account of causation deliver, where "adequate" means good enough to underwrite the causal claims scientists actually make in the special sciences, and to guide experimental design, observational causal inference, and policy intervention?

For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a taste, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of one camp. Be concrete about the hard cases: preemption and overdetermination; omissions and double prevention; causes that cannot be physically manipulated, such as sex, ancestry, macroeconomic aggregates, or distant historical causes; probabilistic causation; cycles and feedback; distinguishing a common cause from a direct cause; and the distinction between actual causation and type-level difference-making. In particular, say explicitly what invariance under interventions is supposed to add over mere robust correlation, given that both can be invariant within the actually observed range.

Then address the circularity worry head-on. Interventions are themselves characterized partly in causal terms, for example an intervention on X must not influence Y through a route that bypasses X. I want to know whether this circularity is a defect the account must remedy, a tolerable feature of a non-reductive account, or a serious problem that limits what the account can explain. State the worry precisely, and say what it would take to answer it on the theory's own terms.

Finally, flag any subset of the desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied; the joint-unsatisfiability claims are the most valuable part, because they turn the list into an argument. Also identify which single desideratum does the most work in excluding popular views, and give a one-paragraph scorecard for a pure counterfactual account, a Humean regularity account, and a Woodward-style interventionist account against that one requirement. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scorecard.
