I work in water-supply planning for a medium-sized city utility. Every few years our analysts come back with downscaled climate projections out to 2050 — ensembles from several global and regional models, showing streamflow ranging from about -30% to +15%. The board keeps asking for a single number to plug into our long-range plan, and I keep telling them the models dont give us one. I need something better than "it's complicated." Write me a decision procedure my analysts and I can actually run each time we get a new batch of model runs: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure should stop and say "these model outputs alone can't support a decision," and where it should hand off to me or the board because the remaining call is about risk tolerance rather than science. Make sure it handles the messy parts: when an ensemble spread can and cannot be read as a probability distribution, when agreement between models means something and when it's just the same model dressed up twice, and how we decide whether our decision cares about the spread at all. Then actually run the procedure on two cases we face: sizing a new reservoir where the high end of the ensemble would justify a much larger capital spend, and deciding whether to enter a 40-year water-rights contract that only makes sense if streamflow doesn't drop more than 10%. Give me at least one case where the procedure tells me something I won't want to hear, and be straight about what the procedure can't do — what it has to leave to judgment no matter how good the workflow is.
