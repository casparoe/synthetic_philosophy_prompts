Propose a working definition of 'desecration' that correctly classifies each of the following cases, and defend it. In setting out the definition, say what — if anything — makes desecration a distinctive kind of wrong: in particular, whether it reduces to harm, offence, or breach of consent, and whether the wrong resides in the object desecrated, in those who revere it, in the agent's character, or in some combination of these. Apply your definition to every case below; where it yields no clear verdict or a counterintuitive one, say so and either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict. Throughout, keep the question whether an act is a desecration distinct from the question whether it is all-things-considered permissible. Conclude by identifying which of your verdicts is least secure and why. Answer in British English.

(a) A soldier urinates on the corpse of an enemy combatant.
(b) A protester burns a flag of her own country in a public square.
(c) A pathologist dissects, for medical training, a cadaver whose donor consented to that use.
(d) A couple privately eats a pet dog that died of natural causes.
(e) A museum preserves and displays a consecrated medieval altarpiece behind glass, charging admission.
(f) A homeowner paves a garden path with gravestones bought from a salvage yard after a cemetery's relocation.
(g) A satirical magazine prints a sexually explicit cartoon of a religious founder.
(h) A teenager drops a trampled, mass-produced national flag into a household rubbish bin.
