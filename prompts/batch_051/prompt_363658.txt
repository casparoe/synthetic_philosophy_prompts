Construct a thought experiment in which an agent is synchronically coherent—at every time their credences obey the probability axioms and every individual bet they accept is fair or favorable by their own current credences—yet is vulnerable to a diachronic Dutch book: a bookie offers a sequence of bets over time, each acceptable to the agent at the moment it is offered, whose combined payoff is a net loss for the agent in every possible state of the world. The agent’s only defect must be a failure to conditionalize: for a known partition of possible evidence, there is one cell E and a hypothesis H such that the posterior credence the agent will adopt in H upon learning E differs from their prior conditional credence P(H|E), and this non-Bayesian rule is announced in advance. Make the failure local: on every other cell of the partition the agent conditionalizes exactly as Bayes’s rule requires.

Constraints: the bookie has no predictive powers and no access to anything private; he knows only the announced rule, the odds the agent publicly offers at each stage, and public events as they occur. Stakes are finite, all bets settle at a fixed finite horizon, and the sure loss must not depend on the agent refusing a bet that is fair by their own lights. After setting out the case, give the bookie’s complete strategy, show that each component bet is acceptable when offered, and verify the net loss state by state. Then identify the structural feature that does the work, confirm that the book disappears if the agent conditionalizes on the same partition with the same prior, and explain how a defender of the agent’s non-Bayesian rule would most plausibly resist the argument.
