I've been trying to get my head around 'inner alignment' and I keep tripping over the same thing. As I understand it, the worry is that a model trained to do X might end up actually optimising for Y even though it looks fine in training. But how is that a seperate, detectable claim? If the model behaves the way you want in every situation you've tested, then 'it has the right inner objective' and 'it has the wrong one but it happens not to matter' are indistinguishable, so the distinction is doing no work. And if it later does something bad, why not just say it generalised poorly? It feels like inner misalignment is either unobservable or just a spooky way of restating ordinary overfitting. What am I missing? Please write your answer in British English.
