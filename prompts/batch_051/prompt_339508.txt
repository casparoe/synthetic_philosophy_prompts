I was reading a blog post that tried to explain the intent alignment vs. value alignment distinction, and I can't tell how much of it to trust. Here's the gist of what it said: the alignment problem is just the problem of making AI do what we want, and it splits into a narrow and a broad version. The narrow version, intent alignment, means the system reliably does what its operator actually intended rather than gaming the literal instruction. The broad version, value alignment, means the system acts on the right values — whatever humans would endorse on reflection, or at least what's genuinely good for them. The post then claimed that the serious technical problem is the narrow one, because the broad problem is a moral and political mess with no agreed answer; if you can make a system robustly do what you intend, it said, then whether your intentions are good is a separate problem that AI researchers don't need to solve. It also said RLHF is basically intent alignment, since you train the model on human feedback until it internalizes your goal. The distinction feels clarifying, but those last two steps strike me as too quick — especially the RLHF part, since I thought RLHF just made models predict what humans would approve of. Is this a fair summary of how people actually draw the intent/value distinction, and do the conclusions it draws about which problem is "the real one" survive the accurate parts? Please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested here, and don't present one camp's position as if it were the consensus.
