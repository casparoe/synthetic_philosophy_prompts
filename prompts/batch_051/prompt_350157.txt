Hey, I help run a smallish internet forum and we're redesigning our reputation system. Here's the proposal: posting earns you no karma at all. Everyone starts with 100 karma and no more is ever minted. For the first 24 hours after a post goes up, any logged-in user can stake a little of there own karma on a yes/no question — will this post still be up, and not hidden by mods, a week from now? Guess right and you get your stake back plus a pro-rata share of the losing side's stakes; guess wrong and you lose it. Your karma then does exactly one thing: it sets the default sort position of it's own posts — higher karma means your new posts start higher in other people's feeds. You can post with zero karma; you just start at the bottom. There are no normal downvotes, just the 'will be removed' side of the bet. Moderators still remove posts, and a removal pays out everyone who bet that way.

Treat this as a mechanism, not as a policy we've already decided on. Walk me through how it actually behaves: who votes and why, what kind of posting it selects for, how the fact that mod decisions are now the payoff event gets gamed, and where the system breaks first. Be concrete about failure modes and say which ones, if any, can be patched without turning it into a completely different scheme. Also, as you go, separate what's fairly settled among people who study reputation systems and prediction markets from what's genuinely contested or just your own guess, so I don't mistake a controversial read for the consensus.
