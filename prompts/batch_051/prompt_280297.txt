After a long and inconclusive argument with a friend about animal ethics, I have been trying to pin down the exact shape of the following kind of case. A person who holds that using animals for food is wrong nevertheless eats animal products when doing so appears not to support the industry: eggs from a neighbor's rescued hens, milk from a rescued goat, or a meat dish a restaurant will otherwise throw away. The defender claims that consuming what would otherwise be wasted creates no new demand and therefore involves no complicity in the harm of animal agriculture; the critic replies that it still treats animals as resources and publicly signals that eating them is acceptable. Is there an established name for this specific configuration, and how does it relate to, yet differ from, the broader practice of avoiding an industry by consuming only what that industry discards? I am asking less for a label than for a precise delimitation: what makes a given case true waste, and whether the signaling or expressive dimension can be separated from the consumer's private reasoning.
