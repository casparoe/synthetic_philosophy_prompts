I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about altering other worlds and building off Earth, going by my gut on cases. A crewed mission to Mars that drills geological cores and leaves behind a small, sealed habitat: fine. A company strip-mining a near-Earth asteroid for platinum, under international monitoring: fine. Building a large rotating habitat in high Earth orbit, assembled from lunar material: fine, even though it amounts to manufacturing a new environment from scratch. Terraforming Mars over centuries by thickening its atmosphere so plants could grow: not fine, though I can't say why it's worse than the asteroid. Introducing engineered microbes to Mars as a first step toward that terraforming: not fine, worse than the drilling, and close to unforgivable if it got loose. A permanent Mars settlement raising children who will never set foot on Earth: fine, assuming the adults went freely and the settlement is genuinely survivable. Leveling a flat area of the Moon for a radio telescope: fine, but I'd be furious if the same work disturbed one of the Apollo landing sites. Finding microbial life in Europa's ocean and drilling through the ice anyway to sample it: not fine; after that discovery, leaving Europa alone is the only option that doesn't feel like vandalism. My working rule is something like: extract and build where there is nothing already there, but don't transform a world that has, or might have, its own history. Test that rule against these cases and repair it. Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close, and what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: melting ice in a permanently shadowed crater on Mercury to supply a settlement, which would end the crater's stability after billions of years?
