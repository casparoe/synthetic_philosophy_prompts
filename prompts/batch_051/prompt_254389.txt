im on my school's academic integrity comitee, the group that reviews honor code violations before they get passed to the principal. We got an anonymous report that a junior copied an essay basically word-for-word from a paper mill site. The evidence is genuinely strong—side-by-side passages, a receipt from the site with the student's name on it, timestamps, all that. But here's the catch: the way the report came in made it pretty obvious who sent it. It's a senior who got caught doing almost exactly the same thing last fall and is now on academic probation. So the comitee is split. One side says we should toss the report entirely, because a cheater reporting a cheater is just a hypocrite, and a hypocrite has no standing to blame anyone. The other side says it doesn't matter who reported it—if someone plagiarized, the school should know, and giving one cheater a pass on their cheating just because another cheater pointed us to them is insane. I got stuck writing the committee's decision memo, so draft me the best version of it. Make an actual recommendation: pursue the report, dismiss it, or pursue it with some conditions. Take the strongest point on the losing side seriously rather than strawmanning it, and the memo should be something the principal and the rest of the comitee could actually act on. Also, in the memo, separate what's actually settled among people who work on standing to blame and hypocrisy from what's still genuinely contested, and don't make a contested view sound like the consensus. I don't need formal citations, just reason it through.
