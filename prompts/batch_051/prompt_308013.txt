I'm on the review panel for a small climate foundation that funds solar geoengineering work, and we've got a proposal to decide. Our own precedents: we funded a purely computational study comparing sulfate aerosol injection scenarios, with no governance component; we rejected a small outdoor aerosol release because it had no independent oversight board; we approved a social science study of how people in three countries react to the phrase solar radiation management; we rejected a proposal to build a prototype deployment aircraft, on the stated ground that it would create a constituency for deployment before anyone had decided to deploy; and we approved with conditions a modeling study of what happens if a large deployment shuts off suddenly, the condition being that the team publish a plain-language summary of termination shock. The new proposal is a real-world field campaign that releases a tiny amount of calcium carbonate in the stratosphere, has an independent monitoring board and a sunset clause, but did no prior public consultation in the launch region. I keep going back and forth on how our own rules extend to this one. What should we decide if we want to be consistant with ourselves, which precedent is the closest analog, and if our precedents genuinely underdetermine it, what principle would we be adopting with each choice?
