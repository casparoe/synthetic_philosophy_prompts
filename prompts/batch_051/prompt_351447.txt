I'm a journalist who covers the intersection of science and ideas, and I'm writing a piece on how Descartes' doctrine that nonhuman animals are unthinking machines looks beside animal-cognition research he could not have anticipated. I want you to reason your way, as faithfully to Descartes as possible, to what he himself would most likely have said about each of the following developments — not what a modern Cartesian would say, and not what we now think he should have said.

1. In 2024, a group of scientists and philosophers issued the New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness, holding that there is strong scientific support for conscious experience in all vertebrates, and at least a realistic possibility of conscious experience in some invertebrates, including insects, decapod crustaceans, and cephalopods.
2. A small reef fish, the cleaner wrasse, has been reported to pass a mirror-mark test, orienting to a mark on its own body only when it can see its reflection.
3. Octopuses solve novel problems, escape from enclosures, manipulate objects, and show something like play, despite a nervous system organized very differently from a mammal's.
4. In 2022, animal-welfare law in the United Kingdom extended a statutory recognition of sentience to decapod crustaceans and cephalopod molluscs as well as vertebrates.

For each one, say whether Descartes would most plausibly class it as mechanically produced passion and outward motion, or as something that would force him to concede thought. Which of the four, if any, puts more pressure on his position than the behaviors he already knew about from animals such as dogs and horses? And what would he take as the threshold for conceding a mind to a nonhuman animal — still language plus domain-flexible reason, or something else?

Throughout, keep two things separate. On one hand, what is textually settled: Descartes denied language and reason to beasts and took their cries and movements to be explainable entirely by the disposition of their organs. On the other hand, what is genuinely contested among Descartes scholars: whether he denied all feeling and sensation to animals, or denied only thought while granting animals a corporeal sort of sensation. Where your prediction turns on that contested choice, give the prediction under each reading and say which reading you are adopting, rather than presenting either as the settled consensus.
