I'm a philosophy of religion PhD student, and in our seminar on the fine-tuning argument I got hung up on a varient of the firing-squad analogy that nobody had a clean answer to. The stock version: ten trained marksmen shoot at you from close range, and you somehow survive. Under 'chance' that's ten independent low-probability misses, astronomically unlikely; under 'something arranged this' (blanks, a payoff, a deliberate plan to spare you) survival is very likely. So surviving is strong evidence for the arranged hypothesis — which is meant to mirror how a bunch of independently fine-tuned constants point to design.

Here's my twist. Suppose you later learn the rifles were all sighted-in together, as a batch, by one armorer. The shooters' errors are now correlated: with probability q the whole batch is off in a way that makes every shot miss; with probability 1-q each shooter has only his usual tiny independent miss probability p. Set p = .01 and q = .05. Under chance, your survival probability is roughly q + (1-q)p^10, which is about 0.05 — not 10^-20. Under the arranged hypothesis survival is still near 1. So the likelihood ratio drops from roughly 10^20 to roughly 20. The evidence has not vanished, but it's been wrecked.

Now map that back to fine-tuning. If the seemingly independent fine-tuned constants are actually outputs of one deeper law or symmetry (one armorer), then a single upstream fact — the one law happening to land right — could produce all the downstream 'coincidences' at once. Does the fine-tuning argument survive that possibility? What exactly goes wrong with the independent-miss calculation in this variant — does it give a lower bound on the chance probability and therefore a too-friendly upper bound on the likelihood ratio, or is it just the wrong model? Does the arranged/design hypothesis lose anything by having to say the whole batch was doctored rather than each rifle individually, e.g. by becoming less specific? And what should someone who likes the original analogy say the relevant q is here, and can they say it without already peeking at the fact that we survive?
