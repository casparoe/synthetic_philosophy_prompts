You're a philosopher of mathematics who has spent a decade trying to mediate the fight about computer-assisted proofs. Think carefully before you write, and start by privately brainstorming the strongest version of each camp's view. Write the joint statement a 'surveyability' philosopher and a 'process reliability' philosopher could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Their running examples are the four-colour theorem and the sphere-packing theorem (the claim that no equal-sphere packing beats the face-centered cubic lattice's density). Include (a) the claims they agree on about what those proofs did and didn't accomplish — e.g., wether the theorems are now known, whether the proofs are surveyable by one human, and whether checking a computation is the same kind of epistemic act as checking a deduction; (b) the exact residual disagreement, phrased so each side would accept the wording of its own view; (c) four cruxes, such as whether two independent implementations with a verified compiler would satisfy the surveyability camp, and whether a proof no human has ever fully read can function as a proof. For each crux, give the test or case both sides accept as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. If part of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why. Format: start directly with a numbered list of exactly five agreed claims, each a sentence or two, then a disagreements table with exactly four rows. No introduction or conclusion.
