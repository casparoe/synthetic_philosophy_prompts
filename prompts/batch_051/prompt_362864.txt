ok so for my war-and-responsibility seminar I've ended up with a stack of views about the run-up to the First World War and I'm not sure they actually hang together, or whether I'm just switching registers depending on the reading that week. heres where I've landed:

(1) I think Germany was more responsible than any other single power for the war actually breaking out in 1914, not just one of five roughly equal contributors.
(2) But I don't think a nation is the kind of thing that can be morally responsible at all; blame has to attach to individuals, not to "Germany."
(3) I'm also persuaded by the structural story: the alliance system, the arms race, mobilization plans with rigid timetables, and the general expectation that a European war was coming made the July crisis massively overdetermined, so zeroing in on any one government's choices distorts what happened.
(4) At the same time I resist saying "the system made them do it"; that turns the decision-makers into puppets and lets their actual choices off the hook.
(5) And I find the Fischer line convincing — that German civilian and military leaders had expansionist war aims and in July 1914 preferred a general war to backing down — even though I wouldn't say "Germany wanted the war," since that smears millions of ordinary Germans who had no say.
