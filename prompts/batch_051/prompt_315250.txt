I'm a novelist. I'm writing a book set in a breakaway republic that has a flag, a border, a police force, a tax office, the works, but exactly two countries on earth treat it as a real state and everyone else calls it a rebellious region of the bigger neighbor it broke away from. My characters keep slipping between calling the place a country and a would-be country, and I want to actually understand the difference before the next draft. I also run a civics club at a high school, around 18 kids, one 75-minute block a week. Could you design a game or role-play that makes them feel the pull of both sides: that statehood is something you achieve by governing territory, and that it's also something that only exists once other states decide it does? They shouldn't hear the word recognition until the end. I'd like a rough timeline for the 75 minutes, plus roles and rules with payoffs. The set-up should have some students as aspirant states with unequal starting resources, some as established states who can grant or withhold recognition, and maybe a neutral assembly that will only let a newcomer in if enough established states vouch for it. Recognition should have mechanical payoffs (access to a shared fund, trade routes, passports, seats at the table), so the question do you even exist as a state? comes up naturally when someone gets blocked from something. Try to include at least one aspirant that is well-run and mostly peaceful but widely shunned, and one established state that barely controls its claimed territory. The pressure should come from those asymetric cases, not from a facilitator explaining definitions. Then a debrief that maps what happened onto the declaratory and constitutive views, and onto the weaker idea that recognition is evidence of statehood rather than a criterion. Tell me what predictably goes wrong when people run this kind of thing, and say honestly which part of the legal-philosophical mess the game cannot reproduce (for instance, the gap between having a right on paper and being able to use it, or formal versus de facto recognition). No lecture up front.
