For a philosophy of social science project, I need the specification before I form any view: what would a theory of generational membership have to get right if it is to settle whether Boomers, Millennials, and Gen Z are real social kinds rather than merely useful labels for market research? Give me the desiderata. Begin with the cases it must classify correctly: a birth cohort that lives through the same upheaval but never acquires a shared political self-understanding; a generational label imposed retroactively that its supposed members later adopt; a narrow age band that claims to be split off from its surrounding generation. Then state the distinctions it cannot collapse, among a demographic cohort fixed by birth-year boundaries, a generation as a shared social location formed by historical events encountered at a formative age, a self-identified generational label operating as an identity, and a mobilized faction within a generation. Include the demographic and sociological results it must respect, such as the age-period-cohort identification problem (age, period, and cohort effects are confounded in aggregate data and cannot be cleanly separated) and the typical finding that within-cohort variation on attitudes exceeds between-cohort variation. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a preference. Then say which single requirement most undermines strong generational essentialism, and flag any pair or subset of the desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied.
