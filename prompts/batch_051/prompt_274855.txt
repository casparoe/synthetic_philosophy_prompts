I have been recording the judgments of a philosopher of science who teaches confirmation theory. Her past pronouncements are as follows:

1. A theory in particle physics predicted, in advance of observation, that a new particle would be found within a narrow mass window; it was found. She called this strong confirmation.

2. A model was adjusted until it reproduced a long-known anomaly in galactic rotation curves. She said the anomaly provides no confirmation, because the model was built to fit it.

3. A theory about the evolution of a trait, developed for independent reasons, later turned out to entail a correlation that had been published years earlier but had never been explained. She called this modestly confirmatory.

4. A model of voter behavior predicted in advance that heavy rain lowers turnout. She said this was only negligibly confirmatory, because nearly any plausible model of voting and mood would have predicted the same thing.

5. Researchers searched a large dataset for patterns, selected the strongest one, and reported it as though it had been predicted in advance. She said the selected pattern provides no confirmation.

6. A theory derived for independent reasons was found to entail a known phenomenon that every rival then in play failed to accommodate. She called this strongly confirmatory, even though the phenomenon was known before the theory was proposed.

Next week she will rule on this case: a theory was designed with a long-standing, previously unexplained regularity explicitly among its desiderata, so that accommodating the regularity helped guide the theory's construction. The theory is not a curve-fit to the regularity, it has independent theoretical motivation, and no current rival can accommodate the regularity.

Predict what she will say about whether the regularity confirms the theory, and with what strength. Then identify the account of confirmation she seems to be running—for example, a severity account, a use-novelty account, a Bayesian account on which old evidence can confirm, or a temporal-novelty account—explain which of these also fit her record and which are ruled out, and describe the further case that would most cleanly separate the remaining candidates.
