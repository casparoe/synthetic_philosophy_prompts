I spent my working life in an analytical chemistry lab, and since I retired I've got into the philosophy of law — mainly the rule-of-law question of how precise a criminal offence has to be before it's fair to punish someone under it. As a little retirement project I've been sending made-up statutes and byelaws to an old friend who used to be a circuit judge, and asking whether he'd strike each down for vagueness, purely on instinct and whatever reasons he gives. His record so far:

- A byelaw making it an offence "to do anything in a public park which a constable reasonably believes is likely to cause annoyance to any other person." — void.
- An airport security regulation making it an offence to "pass through a designated screening point carrying any container holding more than 100 millilitres of liquid." — upheld.
- A statute making it an offence to "deposit in any inland waterway any substance specified in Schedule 4 as harmful to aquatic life." — upheld.
- A statute making it an offence to "keep, breed or sell any dog of a type specified in Schedule 9." — upheld.
- A statute making it an offence to "behave in a public place in a manner that a substantial number of members of the public would regard as discreditable or improper." — void.
- A byelaw making it an offence "knowingly to feed or attempt to feed any wild animal within a designated conservation area." — upheld.

Next week I'm going to send him this one, and I keep going back and forth on how he'll rule:

"It is an offence knowingly to sell or supply to a person under the age of eighteen any article whose primary purpose is to facilitate the taking of a controlled drug."

Whilst I can see at least two rules that fit his track record. Maybe he only cares whether an ordinary person can work out the boundary of the offence from the text itself. Or maybe he thinks a knowledge-or-intent requirement cures vagueness, because the defendants own awareness supplies the standard that the words lack. There might be a third rule about whether there's an ascertainable core. Could you treat this as a little prediction excercise? I'd like: (1) the candidate rules and how well each fits his verdicts so far, (2) what each rule predicts for the drug-paraphernalia statute, (3) your best guess about his verdict and why, and (4) a further made-up statute I could send him that would settle which rule he's actually running. No need for real cases or citations — I'm after the reasoning, not the library.
