In my environmental ethics class we've been talking about wild animal suffering, and I keep noticing a move people make that I can't find a name for. If a person deliberately starts a fire and animals burn to death, everyone counts those deaths as bad; if lightning starts the same fire and the same animals burn, it's treated as sad but not really a moral issue. Similarly, a disease that sweeps through a herd is "just nature", but if a farmer's negligance let the disease in, it suddenly matters. Same injuries, same suffering, but the source changes how much the suffering seems to count, not just who's responsible for it. It isn't the usual appeal-to-nature thing where nature is good or animals don't really feel pain; people will agree the animal suffers just as much. They're discounting the badness because it came from a nonhuman source. Is there an established name for this? I'd like to know whether it's best thought of as a bias, a baseline in moral accounting, or a substantive claim about responsibility. And if there's no exact term, what the nearest concepts are and how this differs from them, and where the edges of the phenomenon are. Also, tell me what would change your mind about the diagnosis, or where you're least confident.
