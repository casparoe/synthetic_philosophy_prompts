Consider a small but pervasive asymmetry in how people treat promises. Someone who promises to bring a dessert to a dinner, to return a borrowed book by the weekend, or to water a neighbor's plants while she is away, and then fails without explanation, is usually described as having broken a promise: a modest failure, but a promissory one. Now suppose someone promises, "I'll be there at six," and arrives at 6:40 after losing track of time. The same people will typically describe her as late—annoying, inconsiderate, a minor social friction—rather than as having broken a promise. The temporal commitment was no less explicit, no less voluntary, and no less avoidable than the commitment to bring the dessert, and the wasted time may cost the host as much as the missing dessert would have. Ordinary practice grades undertakings indexed to a clock much more leniently than undertakings indexed to an object or action. Is that asymmetry justified, or is it just a collective habit of indulgence toward our own timekeeping?

I am not looking for a flat "contexts differ" answer. I want you to construct and defend the strongest principle that could generate both the strict judgment and the lenient one—for example by appealing to human unreliability about duration estimates, to what everyone knows "six o'clock" really means, to the difference between a deadline for an act and a commitment to occupy a window of someone else's attention, or to what lateness signals about future trustworthiness—and then test that principle against nearby cases. What should the principle say about someone who is late to a dinner after the host has explicitly said, "Please be on time; I have to leave by 6:45"? About the person who is never late for work but always 20 minutes late to family dinners? About a promise to call "around five" that is fulfilled at nine? And when you have tested it, render a verdict: should we keep the asymmetry, reverse it, or collapse it into a single standard by which "I'll be there at six" is no different in kind from any other small promise?
