I am trying to pin down, before I evaluate any particular proposal, what a theory of the rationality of betting on cryonic revival would have to get right. By a theory I mean a systematic answer to the question: under what conditions, if any, is it rational for an individual to spend a meaningful share of income and attention on arrangements aimed at raising the probability of being restored after current legal death? I would like you to produce the desiderata for such a theory — the requirements it must satisfy, the cases and distinctions it cannot collapse, and the failure modes it has to avoid — treated as conditions of adequacy rather than as items on a scorecard.

For each requirement, give the consideration behind it. The theory should, for example, explain how to handle extremely small probabilities of astronomically large payoffs without thereby licensing every structurally similar wager, and how to respect the distinction between legal death and the irreversible destruction of whatever physical features underlie memory and personal identity. It should take a position on whose preferences count — the present person, the possible revived person, or the future society that would perform and underwrite the revival — and it should specify whether the relevant probabilities must be objective single-case chances, subjective credences, or stable across plausible reference classes. It should also say what the theory implies when the best use of a dollar for raising revival probability is not buying a suspension contract but funding research, improving the credibility of the field, or changing laws and norms that currently obstruct revival. Where a requirement reflects only one camp's commitments rather than a constraint on any honest theory, flag it as negotiable.

I am most interested in whether some subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable. An explicit expected-value calculation, for instance, may sit uneasily with the deep uncertainty about future technology and about what future agents will prefer to do with their resources; a requirement that the theory not depend on contested metaphysics of personal identity may sit uneasily with the requirement that the bet guarantee the right person gets restored. State these tensions precisely rather than trying to resolve them. Finally, identify the single requirement that does the most work in excluding the common arguments for signing up, and say briefly whether the strongest version of the theory you can construct clears the non-negotiable requirements or instead stalls at one of the tensions you identified.
