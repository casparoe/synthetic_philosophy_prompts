Exam question (20 points):

Explain Kant's distinction between acting from duty and acting merely in accordance with duty. Then apply it to the three cases below. For each case, state (i) whether the action is in accordance with duty, (ii) whether it has moral worth in Kant's sense, and (iii) what role, if any, the agent's feelings play in your assessment.

(a) Amelia, a nurse, works extra shifts during a public-health crisis. She genuinely enjoys the sense of purpose the work gives her, but she also believes the extra work is morally required; if the enjoyment disappeared, she would still do it.

(b) Ben, a cashier, finds a wallet full of cash left at his register. He returns it to the owner because he pities the owner and finds the thought of keeping it unpleasant.

(c) Celia, a juror, votes to acquit because the prosecution has not met the burden of proof, but her primary motive is that she expects the defendant's family to be grateful to her.

Student answer:

Kant's distinction is between doing what duty requires because it is required, and doing the same outward action from some non-moral motive such as self-interest or sympathy. Only actions whose motive is respect for the moral law have moral worth. In (a), Amelia acts both from duty and from enjoyment. Kant requires that an action from duty be entirely free of inclination, so the presence of enjoyment means her action cannot have moral worth; as long as she enjoys it, the duty motive is contaminated by inclination. In (b), Ben acts from pity, which is a feeling and not the rational motive of duty. His action is in accordance with duty but lacks moral worth, and so Kant would say his action deserves no moral praise at all. In (c), Celia reaches the legally correct verdict, but her primary motive is self-interested. Her action is in accordance with duty, yet because her motive is not duty, the action lacks moral worth. In general, an action has moral worth only when the agent's sole motive is duty, and any feeling alongside duty means we must withhold the ascription of moral worth.
