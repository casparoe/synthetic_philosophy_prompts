I'm a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, and I've been trying to get a grip on cognitive penetration by cutting the phenomenon at a slightly different joint. My hunch is that the whole debate between penetrability and impenetrability talks past itself because it lumps together two very different kinds of top-down influence. I'm calling them retouch and rework, and I'd be really grateful for a candid assessment of whether the distinction is coherent, whether it's already out there under another name (it probably is), and where it breaks.

Here's the idea. Retouch: a higher-level state (a fear, a desire, an expectation, an aesthetic preference) alters the salience, ordering, or emphasis of features but doesn't alter which distal properties the experience purports to present. The effect can be undone by sensorimotor variation: move closer, change the lighting, walk round the object, squint, look again. Mood making a hill look steeper than it is, until you actually start climbing or measure its angle with your thumb, is retouch. Same for being hungry and suddenly noticing every bakery sign on the street; the signs were in the retinal image all along, you just now notice them.

Rework: the higher-level state alters the experience in a way that stays stable under normal sensorimotor variation, so the subject keeps having the same sort of perceptual content even after moving, re-checking, and changing vantage point. The only thing that corrects it is a change in the background cognitive state. A racial bias that makes an object in someone's hand keep looking like a gun even as he comes closer and the details are in plain view would be rework.

My claim is that only rework deserves the name cognitive penetration. Retouch is what the modularists have always allowed under headings like attention and adaptation, and it doesn't touch the impenetrability of early vision. Rework, if it exists, is the problematic case for modularity. I suspect a lot of the famous alleged cases only show retouch, whilst the real argument should be about whether rework ever happens under ecologically normal conditions.

I know this sounds a bit too tidy, but its been nagging at me. What are the strongest three or four objections, and is any of them fatal rather than just embarrassing? In particular, am I just redefining 'penetration' by stipulation, or does the distinction actually track a difference in the underlying processing?
