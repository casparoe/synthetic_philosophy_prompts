Act as a philosopher of mathematics with a working command of proof theory. Think it through carefully, and begin by brainstorming several candidate defeaters before you sort them into a structured assessment.

The position I hold is this: the standard first-order axioms of arithmetic (Peano arithmetic, PA) are consistent—there is no finite derivation of 0 = 1 from them—and our very high confidence in that claim is rationally well-grounded, even though Gödel's second incompleteness theorem says that, if PA is consistent, PA cannot prove its own consistency. I mean the specific consistency claim about that formal theory, not the observation that arithmetic has worked well in practice.

Tell me what would count against this position, and rank the defeaters by how decisive they would be. Which developments would be fatal, which merely awkward, and which would a committed defender concede as genuine counterevidence rather than dismiss as irrelevant? Please address at least the following. (1) How should I weight a short, fully verifiable derivation of 0 = 1 against a contradiction whose shortest proof is astronomically long or beyond any feasible verification? (2) Would an inconsistency in a weak subtheory such as Robinson arithmetic be decisive, and what would an inconsistency in a strong extension such as ZFC or one of its large-cardinal extensions tell us about PA itself? (3) Would failures in our supporting apparatus—say, a collapse of the standard consistency proof by transfinite induction up to epsilon-zero, or the discovery that the background theory used in such a proof is itself inconsistent—count against PA's consistency, or only against our grounds for believing it? (4) Could any direct empirical evidence, such as a physically executed computation that appeared to derive 0 = 1 from the axioms, be dispositive, and if so under what conditions? (5) Has the position been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if it has, is that a defect in a claim of this kind or simply the normal situation for claims about abstract objects? Conclude with an honest assessment of how exposed this consistency claim actually is.
