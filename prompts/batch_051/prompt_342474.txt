I keep feeling like the Münchhausen trilemma is just what happens when justification is a recursive function and somebody forgot to specify the base case. Here's the mapping that's been stuck in my head: a recursive function needs a base case or it recurses forever; a bunch of mutually recursive functions can define each other fine as long as some call chain eventually hits a base case; and just hardcoding a base case by fiat is normal engineering, not a scandal. That maps suspiciously well onto the three horns — infinite regress is unbounded recursion, circularity is mutual recursion with no base case anywhere, and dogmatism is the fiat base case. Foundationalism would be well-founded recursion, coherentism a cycle that never bottoms out. Is there something genuinely deep about grounding and justification here, or am I just pattern-matching on the word 'ground'? In particular, does the fact that programmers are completely fine with hardcoded base cases tell us anything about whether dogmatism is acceptable, or does the analogy break exactly because code only has to terminate while beliefs have to be justified? What would have to be true on both sides for results to transfer from one to the other, and what does the parallel predict that turns out false?
