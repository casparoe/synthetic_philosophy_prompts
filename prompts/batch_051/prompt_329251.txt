ive been going in circles on my beliefs about chance and deference, and i'd like an outside audit rather than just re-deriving the same loop myself. here's the cluster as it stands:

(1) the principal principle, roughly: if the objective chance of A at the present time is x, then conditional on that chance fact plus any admissible background information, my credence in A should be x. admissible information is information that doesn't give me a backdoor to A over and above what the chance already gives.

(2) chance is a real feature of the setup or the world, not a measure of my ignorance. a fair coin's 0.5 chance of heads is true of the coin-plus-toss, not just of my state of information.

(3) the past is settled. once the coin has been tossed and i've seen heads, the present chance that it landed heads is 1 and my credence in that is 1. i don't think this violates (1), because the relevant chance is the present one, not the pre-toss chance.

(4) i'm a two-boxer in newcomb's problem, and more generally a causal decision theorist: i evaluate acts by the outcomes they cause, not the outcomes they merely predict. i think this is compatible with (1), because the proposition that i will two-box is not admissible background information about the million — it's correlated with the contents of the box without causing them, so the principal principle doesn't require me to conditionalize on it. but sometimes i worry that this is a dodge and (1) actually does reach into my own acts.

(5) i'm also drawn to a broadly humean view of chance: chance facts are made true by the whole mosaic of events, the full history of outcomes, with no extra primitive propensities bolted on top. that's why i take the undermining-futures problem seriously: a chance theory stated at a time can assign nonzero chance to a future history in which that very theory is false.

(6) and that's why i've ended up thinking the right version of the principle is the new principle as a friendly patch: my credence should defer to the present chance of A conditional on the chance theory itself being true, not to the unconditional present chance of A.

so does this package actually hang together? i can feel that something is off but i genuinely dont know which pairs are real conflicts and which dissolve once the wording is cleaned up, and i don't know which commitment i'd give up if forced. give me an audit: go through the tensions in order of severity, say what has to give or get qualified in each, and tell me whether the whole set is actually tenable or whether i've smuggled in a contradiction somewhere.
