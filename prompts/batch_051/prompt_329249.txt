In my philosophy of language seminar we've been working through Harry Frankfurt's old distinction between the liar and the bullshitter, and a student brought up ChatGPT the other day, wich got us talking about the 2023 mess where a New York lawyer filed a brief full of fake case citations that the model had invented — remember, the judge asked to see the cases, the airline's attorneys said none of them could be found, and sure enough they didnt exist. The lawyer got sanctioned. I know that part of the story; what I'd like is a careful analysis of the episode through Frankfurt's lens, not a retelling. Spell out the trichotomy first. Lying, for Frankfurt, isn't just saying something false; the liar has to know the truth and deliberately steer away from it. The bullshitter is different: he's not especially trying to get the world wrong or right, he just isn't tracking truth at all, because his eye is on how the performance lands on the audience. Error, by contrast, is the person who cares about truth and simply misses it. Now map the episode onto that. Who are the relevant agents at each stage — the model, the lawyer who signed the brief, the firm, maybe the developers — and which of the three categories fits their conduct? Can ChatGPT literally lie or bullshit if it has no beliefs or intentions of its own, or does the bullshit have to be located somewhere else in the sociotechnical pipeline? Then run it in the other direction: what does this case do to Frankfurt's framework? Does it confirm his distinction, strain it, or show that a system producing text with no concern for truth at all is a fourth thing he never anticipated? If you think hallucination needs its own category — or that it's bullshit with no bullshitter — argue for that and explain what it does to his account.
