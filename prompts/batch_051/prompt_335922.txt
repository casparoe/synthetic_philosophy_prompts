After a long argument with a friend last weekend, I have been turning over a question about the ethics of my own family life. I was raised in a devout household, and I am now raising my two young children without that faith. To protect the relationship with my parents, I have not told them outright that I no longer believe; when they press me about baptism and Sunday school, I say we are still deciding, and I let them assume the children will be raised in the church. My friend insisted this is a kind of false witness. I replied that the commandment was aimed at the courtroom and at malicious slander, not at privacy and family diplomacy. The verse we kept returning to is Exodus 20:16: "Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour." The case now before it, an adult child who no longer shares her parents' faith and preserves belonging through omission, is plainly not a case its authors foresaw. I do not need a practical resolution; what I want is to know what each standard approach to reading the verse commits one to. Take the plain words, the original purpose, what the drafters would probably have said if asked about my situation, and what a reasonable reader today would understand the verse to require. Then say whether any coherent reading reaches my case, and if so, which one does and why.
