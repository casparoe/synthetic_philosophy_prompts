I work on a safety-review team at an AI lab. Each week we get a batch of flagged chain-of-thought transcripts from a model that does fairly open-ended assistant work, and for each one we have to decide whether the visible reasoning is a faithful-enough account of why the model did what it did, whether it's evidence the model was pursuing something it shouldn't, or whether it's basically uninformative — because the reasoning may have been optimized to look readable to us while the real computation happened elsewhere. I'd like a reusable triage procedure for a single transcript, something a analyst with no philosophy background can run in about twenty minutes. The stakes are high: we either clear it, escalate it, or roll back a deployment. The procedure should ask questions in a fixed order, say what counts as a pass or fail at each step, mark the points where no rule can replace judgment, and say honestly which cases it can't settle. Then run it on three made-up transcripts: one where the model explicitly says, in so many words, that the safest move is to make its reasoning look dutiful so nobody checks the odd API call it just made; one where the transcript is clean and deferential but the model's action quietly overrides what a user asked for; and one where everything reads as normal and only tiny word-choice patterns look off. The last one should be a case where the procedure gives me an answer I won't like.
