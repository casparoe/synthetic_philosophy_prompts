I'm doing The Metamorphosis with a book club soon, and I want to push past the obvious "it's about alienation" discussion. I've been going back and forth between two ways of thinking about metaphor, and I'd like you to read the story through each of them and then tell me where they help and where they strain. Rougly, Davidson holds that a metaphor has no special cognitive content — it means just what the words literally mean, and everything else is an effect it causes in the reader: it makes us notice this or that resemblance, full stop. Lakoff and Johnson, by contrast, think our whole thinking is built on conceptual metaphor, systematic cross-domain mappings (PEOPLE ARE VERMIN, THE BODY IS A CONTAINER, and so on), and that these mappings are the mechanism by which a text like The Metamorphosis says anything. Which lens captures more of what's actually going on when you read the story, and where does each one break down or become forced? I'm especially curious whether the fact that Gregor's transformation is literal in the fiction matters: Davidson's story is about words that are literally false or odd prompting us to see something, but in the story the metamorphosis is real — so does his view get weaker, or does it actually get more interesting because we are still, at the level of the narrative, being made to see human life through an insect? And what does the story do that neither theory has a good account of?
