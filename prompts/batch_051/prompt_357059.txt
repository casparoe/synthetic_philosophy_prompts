I've been interviewing two camps that mostly talk past each other, and they've agreed to an adversarial-collaboration-style sidebar instead of a debate, so I need the document they could both sign.

The two positions:
- The "reward-as-valence" camp: negative reward, when it's functioning as a common currency for evaluating states and actions in a sufficiently complex agent, is a primitive form of negative valence. On this view the in-principle "it's just a number" objection misses the point; whether a trained RL system suffers is a matter of degree and architecture, not an automatic no.
- The "bookkeeping" camp: a reward signal is just a scalar training signal for credit assignment and policy search. It isn't a state of the system at all, so asking whether the reward "feels bad" is a category error. They'd say a thermostat minimizing an error signal isn't suffering, and the only apt question is whether the trained system's own internal states have a valence-like role.
