Consider the following proposal. A regulator establishes a Transparency Bond Scheme covering nudges and similar forms of choice architecture. Before an organisation may deploy a default rule, opt-out arrangement, framing change, anchor, or social-comparison message in any setting affecting the public, it must file a brief statement of the target behaviour, the mechanism by which the intervention is meant to operate, and the population affected, and must lodge a bond scaled by the expected number of people exposed and the organisation's record. The bond is returned only if the intervention passes a disclosure test: in a pre-deployment trial, the intervention's effect on a representative sample of the affected population is measured both with and without a plain explanation of how the intervention works and what it is designed to achieve. If the effect among the informed sample is less than four-fifths of the effect among the uninformed sample, the bond is forfeited to a fund supporting independent testing, the intervention may be deployed only with the disclosure made at the point of choice, and the organisation's bond rises for future filings.

Work through how this scheme would actually behave, not whether it is a good idea in the abstract. What would it select for in the design of nudges, and would the selected interventions be the ones the scheme's sponsors intend? Reconstruct the main strategies by which a determined deployer could obtain the refund while nonetheless steering choices in ways the scheme is meant to catch, and identify which parts of the trial, definition, threshold, and disclosure procedure would bear the strain. Pay particular attention to the possibility that a nudge's effect can survive disclosure because it works through inertia, opt-out costs, or complexity rather than through the ignorance the disclosure is meant to remove. Explain who would be effectively outside the scheme's reach and what that does to the distribution of influence over choice. Finally, say which of the failure modes could be repaired by amending the scheme and which are intrinsic to the project of separating acceptable nudges from manipulative ones by means of a disclosure test. Please answer in British English.
