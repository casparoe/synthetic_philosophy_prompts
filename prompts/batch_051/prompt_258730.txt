I keep going back and forth on an old problem and I want you to take a side, not give me a survey. The doctrine I'm talking about says creation was originally good — that before the Fall there was no death, no predation, no animal suffering — and that all of that entered the world through human sin. But we now know from evolution and the fossil record that animals killed and ate each other, got sick, starved, and went extinct for hundreds of millions of years before any human existed. So predation and suffering look like they were built into the world from the start, not caused by anything a human did.

My question is whether this doctrine of a good creation survives that discovery, and if so in what form. Which specific claim is under the real pressure — the claim that the original creation was good, the claim that death entered the world through human sin, or the claim that the Fall is an event in history — and what is the strongest version that can still be defended? If the move is to say that 'good' always included predation and parasites eating their hosts alive, show me that this isn't just a redefinition that makes the doctrine unfalsifiable.
