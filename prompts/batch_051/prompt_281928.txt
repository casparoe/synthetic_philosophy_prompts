I chair a small foundation's grant board. We meet monthly and fund maybe two of every five proposals, so the rejections are high-stakes and our own inconsistency drives everyone crazy. Reviewers rate four criteria — feasibility, significance, fit to our mission, and budget — as sound or unsound, then we take a majority vote on the proposal overall. We keep getting outcomes where a majority rates every criterion sound but a majority votes to reject, or the reverse, and it's become obvious the aggregation rule is deciding cases rather then just reporting what we think. Please turn what judgment-aggregation theory knows about the discursive dilemma into a procedure our non-expert panel can actually run in a two-hour meeting. Give it as exactly five numbered checks, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Work these two recurring scenarios through the checks: (a) all four criteria get majority "sound" but the overall vote rejects; (b) the panel votes to fund even though two members insist the shared reasons don't support that verdict. At each check, flag whether it's mechanical or a judgment call, and in the fifth check say what the procedure can't settle for us.
