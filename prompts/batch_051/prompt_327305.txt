I'm an undergraduate computer science major currently taking a philosophy seminar on well-being, and I'm more comfortable than most of the class with formal machinery: reward functions, policies, value functions, discounting, exploration-exploitation trade-offs, gradient-based optimization, local maxima, model-based versus model-free control, reward hacking, wireheading, and Goodhart's law. Last week we read about the paradox of hedonism — the claim that deliberately making pleasure one's aim tends to undermine the very pleasure one seeks, so that pleasure is most reliably achieved as a byproduct of pursuing other ends without treating them merely as instruments to pleasure. We spent the session debating whether this is a deep objection to hedonism as a theory of value, and I left with the nagging sense that the dispute is really about optimization and that my own vocabulary might dissolve it.

What I want is a systematic translation, not an analogy. Restate the paradox of hedonism — and the argumentative use philosophers make of it against hedonism — in the language of reinforcement learning and control theory, as rigorously as the material permits. Two distinctions matter to me. First, how would you encode the difference between pleasure as a reward signal that scores states or trajectories and pleasure as a feature of the agent's own state that can be altered by the act of attending to it or pursuing it? Second, is the paradox best modeled as reward misspecification, as wireheading or reward hacking, as a nonstationary reward environment, as a credit-assignment problem, or as something none of those categories cleanly captures?

Then give me the residue report. Tell me which parts of the paradox and its anti-hedonist use map cleanly onto those RL concepts, which parts you had to fudge to make them fit, and which parts have no counterpart at all in the RL vocabulary — for example, the phenomenological character of enjoyment, the evaluative claim that only pleasure has final value, or the difference between an agent whose policy literally maximizes a returned reward and a person who adopts the policy of not dwelling on her own pleasure. Be explicit about whether each failure of translation signals a limitation in the RL vocabulary or an unclarity in the philosophical formulation. Finally, assess my suspicion directly: I suspect the paradox of hedonism is an optimization pathology — real, but informative about how pleasure is produced rather than about whether pleasure is the only thing of final value. Is that suspicion mostly right, or does the residue report show that the part that will not translate is doing the real philosophical work?
