My apartment building recently installed smart doorbell cameras, and my downstairs neighbor and I now argue constantly about what's actually private. He says it's no different from looking through a peephole; I keep saying it has to be different, because the camera records and keeps a searchable log of every time I come and go, who I bring over, when I leave for work. So I've been trying to actually define what a privacy violation is, instead of just having a feeling about it, and the word turns out to be a lot slipperier than I expected. I keep flip-flopping between thinking privacy is control over information about you and thinking it's about certain realms of life being off-limits to scrutiny, and neither one seems to survive all the cases below.

I'd like you to act as a professional philosopher who specializes in privacy and information ethics. Please think carefully and start by brainstorming a few candidate definitions before settling on the best one, then walk me through how it handles each case.

Give me a working defintion of a privacy violation — not privacy as a vague value, but what specifically makes an action a violation of someone's privacy. Then run that definition through these cases and tell me where it gives a surprising or wrong-seeming verdict:
- a stranger glances at you for a second on the sidewalk
- your friend secretly reads the diary you left on your kitchen table
- your therapist repeats something you told her in confidence to a friend at a dinner party, but she leaves out your name
- your landlord gets a key-fob system that logs exactly when you enter and leave the building, and he never looks at the logs
- a hardware store whose loyalty card you use notices you've been buying boxes, packing tape, and a moving dolly, infers you're about to move, and sells that guess to a moving company that starts calling you
- an old roommate tells a long story at a party about the time you had a panic attack in college, which you'd never told anyone else about
- someone on the bus positions their phone so it films you specifically, without you noticing, for twenty minutes
- a journalist includes in a profile of you a minor shoplifting arrest from when you were nineteen that was technically public record but that no one has thought about in decades

Then tell me: does any single definition actually get all of these right, or do some intuitions have to give? If it gets one wrong, either revise the definition or defend the counterintuitive verdict. And which of your verdicts are you least confident about?
