I've spent most of my career keeping production systems alive, and half my mental furniture is stuff like legacy root accounts, single points of failure, break-glass credentials, backwards compatability, and deprecation warnings. Lately I can't stop reading about constitutional monarchies — the UK, Sweden, Japan — democracies that still keep a monarchy around. And the debate about them maps weirdly well onto the arguments I have at work about whether to finally delete some ancient root account or keep it around because nobody knows what would break.

The monarchist side, as I understand it, says: keeping a hereditary head of state seperates the symbol from the politician. The monarch absorbs the ceremony and the national-unity stuff, so the prime minister can be a normal partisan that people argue about without the state itself feeling up for grabs every election. They also point to reserve powers as a kind of constitutional backstop — mostly unused, but there if everything goes sideways. The republican side says: that's insane in a democracy. Nobody should hold state office by birth, the reserve powers are a fire hazard rather than a safety feature, and anything the monarch does for identity or continuity could be done by an elected or rotating head of state, or by nobody.
