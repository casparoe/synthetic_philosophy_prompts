Write a deadpan corporate policy memorandum, roughly 700 words, from the newly formed Office of Ethical Simplification at a fictional multinational, announcing the rollout of 'Golden Rule 3.1' after the company's earlier attempt to run all employee conduct on a single one-line maxim failed during beta testing. Format it as numbered clauses followed by a short FAQ, in the voice of an HR-compliance or legal department that remains determined to preserve the rule while quietly burying the embarrassing test results.

The satire should target the project of keeping the golden rule as a complete decision procedure by stacking qualification upon qualification. The jokes should come from the actual structure of the philosophical debates rather than office slapstick: the test subject who, on a literal reading, is authorized to treat others exactly as he wishes to be treated; the discovery that two people with contrary tastes cannot both be treated according to what the other wants; the amendment that replaces actual wants with informed or idealized wants, forcing the company to determine what every employee would want if only they were a better version of themselves; the retreat to a purely negative formulation, which prohibits what the agent would hate but requires nothing positive; and finally the role-specific schedule of exceptions that does all the remaining moral work. Each amendment should generate a new absurdity, and by the end the reader should feel the rule has quietly collapsed into ordinary moral judgment with a very long set of appendices. It should be funny to someone who knows the philosophical literature and still readable, as compliance satire, to someone who doesn't; the memo itself should never name the objections in seminar terms or cite anyone.
