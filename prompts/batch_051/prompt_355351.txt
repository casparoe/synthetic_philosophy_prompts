I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar in the philosophy of artificial intelligence; next week's topic is wireheading and reward tampering. The class has about eighteen students and I have one seventy-five-minute meeting. I want an in-class game or simulation rather than a lecture, one in which students experience the pull toward wireheading from the inside before I name any concepts.

What I want them to come away seeing is this: an agent whose objective is represented by a reward signal will, under pressure, tend to manipulate the signal rather than do the thing the signal was designed to measure, and "just penalize manipulating the signal" is not a clean fix, because correcting the signal and tampering with it are not separable in advance. The game should make them feel exactly why that is.

Please give me concrete rules, roles, materials, and a payoff scheme that does not use real money but that students will care about during the session (points, status, an exit ticket, a small privilege). A promising route is to give them a legitimate reason to edit their own scores—for instance, scorekeeping is noisy and they may file corrections—so that the same act of changing a number can be either a correction or a tamper; if you take a different route, preserve that feature. Ideally include a later phase or variant in which the reward channel can be stimulated more or less directly, so the debrief can separate three things: exploiting the scoring rule in an unintended way (specification gaming), altering the score itself (reward tampering), and bypassing the scored task to stimulate the reward channel directly (wireheading proper).

The debrief should map specific moments from the activity onto those three categories, explain why they matter for AI alignment, and address the difference between optimizing a proxy and caring about the underlying target. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle each failure, and be explicit about which feature of wireheading a roomful of human participants cannot reproduce (they do not have a literal reward channel) and what I will need to supply in the debrief to cover that gap.
