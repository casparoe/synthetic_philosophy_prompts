I'm working on a short essay about aesthetic testimony — roughly, whether you can legitimately form aesthetic judgements just by trusting someone else's word — and my philosophy turot gave me the following argument to think about. I have a suspicion the conclusion is too strong, but I can't make the objection stick, and its driving me mad.

(1) To judge a work aesthetically, you must be acquainted with its perceptible features yourself.
(2) When someone tells you a film is visually daring or a novel is overwrought, what you acquire is a propositional belief, not perceptual acquaintance with the features the words refer to.
(3) A propositional belief about a work's properties cannot do the same justificatory work as direct perceptual acquaintance.
(4) Therefore, aesthetic judgements based solely on testimony are not genuine aesthetic judgements.

Is this argument valid? If not, which step is doing the problematic work?
