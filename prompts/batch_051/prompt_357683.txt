i've noticed something weird about how we hold people responsible for what they say, and I can't tell if it has a name. First case: a friend tells me my flight leaves at 6 because she misread an email she trusted; it actually leaves at 7 and I miss it. I'm annoyed, but mostly at her for being careless, not for saying something false—she had every reason to think it was true. Second case: a guy at a party announces confidently that a certain country's population is 42 million, a number he invented on the spot, and it turns out to be exactly right. Something about his saying it still bothers me even though it was true. The first failure seems to be about the content being false, the second about the speaker having no basis—two different wrongs. But every single rule I try for when someone is allowed to say something ('only say what's true', 'only say what you actually know', 'only say what you believe') cleans up one case and misfires on the other. Does this pattern have an established name, and what's the right way to carve it—one rule, two rules, or something else? Please commit to a view and defend it; I don't want a menu of options.
