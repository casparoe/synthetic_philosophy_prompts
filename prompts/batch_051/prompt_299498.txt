I'm a PhD student in philosophy of science, and I've been going in circles on laws of nature for months, so I want someone to help me figure out what I'm allowed to keep. The view I'm attracted to is Humeanism, roughly Lewis's best-systems account: the laws are just the axioms or theorems of whichever deductive system best balances simplicity and strength in systematizing the whole mosaic of local matters of fact. Its virtues are real: no primitive nomic necessitation, no modal glue beyond the actual facts, and laws stay epistemically accessible because they're just patterns in the very facts we can go look at. It also gives a clean story about the law/accident distinction: the fact that all the coins in my pocket are silver is an accident because adding it to the system would cost complexity without buying much strength, while the fact that all electrons have unit negative charge earns its keep by covering a huge range of phenomena cheaply.

But I have an intuition I can't shake: that laws don't just summarize how things happen, they're part of why they happen. If the law is a theorem of the best system, then this electron's charge is not explained by the law; the electron's charge is part of the mosaic that makes the law true. The explanatory arrow points up from the instances to the regularity, and that feels exactly backwards. It's like saying the periodic table explains why this lump of sodium fizzes in water, when the table is just a summary of how lumps of sodium behave. It also makes me unsure how laws underwrite counterfactuals the way they seem to: everyone agrees that if this bit of sodium had been dropped in water it would have fizzed, and I want to say the law is what makes that true. A non-governing summary seems to make it true only in the loose sense that the closest worlds where the summary holds are worlds where the thing happens, and I can't tell whether that's a real explanation or just a paraphrase.

So here's the fork. Should I (a) debunk the intuition as leftover anthropomorphism about governing — the universe doesn't legislate, it just is, and I should get over it; (b) keep the intuition and amend or abandon Humeanism, maybe by treating laws as irreducibly relational facts about properties (Armstrong-style) or as primitive facts about how the universe produces its next state, and pay whatever metaphysical price that costs; or (c) take a compatiblist reading on which laws explain their instances in a deflationary, non-causal sense, like explanation by unification or structural explanation, that a Humean can honestly live with? I want a negotiated verdict, not just a side: what does each move cost, and is (c) an actual reconciliation or just re-labeling the intuition so it stops hurting?
