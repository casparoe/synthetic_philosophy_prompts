I am a PhD student in metaphysics, and before a seminar session on metaontology I need to present the position I actually hold about the status of ontological disputes. I have written the position out below, and I would like an outside audit of whether it hangs together before I defend it in front of the group.

1. Quine's criterion. A theory is ontologically committed to whatever entities must fall within the range of its first-order variables for the theory's assertions to be true; the surface nouns of ordinary language carry no ontological weight by themselves.

2. The dispute between universalism and nihilism about composition — the view that any two things compose a further object versus the view that no two distinct things ever compose — is merely verbal. The two sides are not disagreeing about any fact about the world, and the appearance of a substantive question dissolves once their terms are made precise.

3. The dispute between platonism and nominalism about numbers is substantive. There is a genuine question whether numbers exist, and settling it is not just a matter of choosing a convenient language or deciding how to speak.

4. Quantifier variance is false. The existential quantifier has a single best, univocal meaning — roughly the ordinary one — and it is not the case that the universalist and the nihilist are simply employing two equally good but different meanings of that quantifier.

5. The right method for settling substantive ontological disputes is Quinean: regiment the rival theories into first-order logic and compare them on theoretical virtues such as simplicity, explanatory power, and conservatism, rather than by consulting pre-theoretic intuitions about what really exists.

I cannot tell whether these commitments jointly describe a stable position, and I would rather not find out during the seminar. Please audit the set: identify where the real strains are, distinguish them from places that only look strained, and order the genuine strains by how serious they are. For each genuine strain, indicate which commitment or commitments are doing the pulling and what the least costly adjustment would be. Close by saying which single change would do the most to make the position coherent, and what that change would force me to give up elsewhere.
