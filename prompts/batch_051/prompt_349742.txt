A friend and I have been stuck for months on a dispute about negative utilitarianism, and we've reached the point where each of us can predict the other's moves and nobody changes their mind. His position is what I've seen called lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: once suffering crosses a certain intensity — the kind a person would do almost anything to end, roughly torture-adjacent — it can't be traded off against pleasure at all. No amount of mild contentment, spread across any number of people or years, makes a world containing even one such episode better than a world without it, because on his view that suffering is in a different "unit" than mild pleasure. My view is that pleasure and pain are in principle comensurable: they sit on one scale of how good or bad experience is, so for any given pain there is some amount of pleasant experience that would compensate for it, even if the amount is astronomically large and would never actually occur. We agree on every concrete case we've tried — we'd both prevent the torture, both prefer a painless world, both agree pinpricks aren't in the same league as agony. So the disagreement seems to be entirely about the structure of value, not about any practical choice.

What bothers me is that the argument cycles through the same three moves. I say: if you accept that, you lose any total way of comparing lives or worlds. He says: comparability can be partial, and the threshold case is exactly where comparison breaks down. I say: that's just an intuition dressed up as a structural claim. He says: and your claim that enough mild pleasures outweigh torture is a worse one. Around and around. My hunch is that we might be using "outweighs" in different senses — I mean something like "figures in a rational preference ordering," he means something like "is genuinely felt as compensation from the inside" — or maybe we secretly disagree about whether the intensity of an experience is cardinally measurable in the first place. I'd like a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is (verbal, about measurement, about which intuitions get to count as axioms, about aggregation across persons, something else), and, given that diagnosis, what would count as progress — or whether the sensible move is to stop expecting it to resolve.
