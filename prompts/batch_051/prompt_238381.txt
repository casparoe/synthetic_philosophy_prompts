Here is the variation I've been chewing on with a friend. Keep every feature of the original beetle example except one: the beetles are still sealed in boxes, nobody ever sees anyone else's beetle, and each person knows only their own. But now the private beetles have public downstream effects. Once a month the whole community gathers and each person, behind a screen, tips their still-hidden beetle into a shared pot. The pot is heated, and the resulting broth has plainly public properties — bitter, sweet, salty, a particular smell. People talk about beetles in connection with this ritual all the time: they predict "today's broth will come out bitter, my beetle is the bitter kind," they blame or credit particular people for how the communal broth tastes, they keep records of past broths and argue about whose beetle did what. No one can ever verify which physical beetle caused which feature of the broth, since the contributions go into one pot, but the correlation between certain people attending and certain broths is stable enough that the community speaks confidantly about there being a bitter-beetle person or a sweet-beetle person.

Does 'beetle' still fail to do any real work in the language in the way the original example is meant to show, or has the added causal connection to a public practice quietly given the word a public criterion?
