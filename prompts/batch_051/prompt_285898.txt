I have recently retired from the natural sciences and have been helping a philosophy department mark undergraduate essays. This week's question concerned Freud's account of religion. The question on the paper read:

"Freud's account of religious belief is sometimes summarized as the claim that religion is wish-fulfillment. Explain what Freud means by this: identify the underlying wish or wishes, the developmental story Freud gives for their origin, and the precise sense in which he calls religious beliefs illusions. Then present and assess one standard objection to this account."

One student wrote:

"Freud says religious belief grows out of the helplessness of early childhood. The infant is dependent on its father for protection and comfort; adulthood does not end this dependence, because nature and fate are indifferent and terrifying. Under that pressure, the grown-up recreates the childhood father on a cosmic scale: God is, in effect, an exalted father who watches over us, provides for us, and guarantees a moral order in which the good are rewarded and the wicked punished. Religious doctrines are thus fulfillments of the universal human wish for protection, guidance, and eventual justice. They are illusions because they are false beliefs that people hold in spite of the evidence. Psychoanalysis, by showing that these beliefs are generated by childish wishes, shows that they are unjustified, and Freud therefore thinks we should abandon religion in favor of science. The most serious objection to this account is that it commits a genetic fallacy: even if a belief originates in wish-fulfillment, it might nevertheless be true. Showing how a belief was caused does not settle whether it is true, so psychoanalysis cannot by itself refute theism."

Please grade this answer and give the specific comments that justify the grade. I want a point-by-point assessment rather than a general impression: identify exactly where the answer is accurate, where it subtly overreaches or misstates Freud's position, and what a corrected version of each problematic passage would have to say. Assign a mark on a standard 0–100 scale, and make the grade commensurate with the severity of the errors relative to the strengths of the answer.
