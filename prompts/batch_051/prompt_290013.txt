I'm a philosophy professor, and I've somehow ended up on the hook for a two-page research proposal for a workshop on time and consciousness; a colleague dropped out and I said I'd cover it, which is looking hoplessly optimistic now. The seed is the colour phi setup: two brief flashes in different places, the first red and the second green, and with the right timing subjects report a single moving dot that shifts from red to green roughly halfway across—so the colour change is reported as having occurred before the second flash appears. Dennett has argued that there is no fact of the matter between the Orwellian story (the two flashes are consciously seen, but memory is quietly rewritten afterward, so what gets reported is the moving dot) and the Stalinesque story (conscious experience is delayed until after the second flash and assembled into the moving dot before it is ever experienced). He takes this to undermine any picture on which there is a definite place or time where consciousness comes together.

I want the proposal to push past that and ask whether the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction is itself well-formed enough to be genuinely undecidable, or whether it's a false alternative produced by a bad picture of experiential time. No citations or bibliography, please—I'll add those myself. What I need is: a precise statement of the question; an account of why the existing positions leave it open; how a conceptual project rather than another empirical study could make progress, and why that approach beats the obvious alternatives; what a useful deliverable would look like for someone working on the timing of conscious experience; and an honest note about what would count as failure. Keep it scoped to a single term, and write it as a proposal rather than an essay defending an answer.
