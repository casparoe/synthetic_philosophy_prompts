Consider the proposition: a measure of civilizational advancement is inadequate if it ranks civilizations solely by energy-harnessing capacity, and an adequate measure must include at least one further dimension—information-processing capacity, or the finest physical scale at which the civilization can deliberately manipulate matter (Barrow's downward-looking dimension, as opposed to Kardashev's upward-looking one). What is your actual credence in that proposition? I don't want "it depends on the definition of advancement"; I want a number and the decomposition. Break it into the sub-questions: whether "advancement" is one univocal dimension or a cluster of related capacities; whether energy capture is a reliable proxy for the other capacities; whether information-processing capacity and fine-scale manipulation are themselves two independent dimensions or collapse into one; and how much of our preference for energy-based scales reflects theoretical adequacy versus observational feasibility. Give rough probabilities for each sub-question, show how they combine, and identify the sub-question your final number is most sensitive to. Then state what evidence or argument would most move that number.
