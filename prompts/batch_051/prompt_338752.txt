I'm kicking around a near-future novel, and I want the regulatory backdrop to actually work rather than just be set dressing. The rule in my novel is this: any company selling an AI companion has to give each companion a hard expiry date, no more then twelve months from the first conversation with a given user, and the date stays visible in the interface the whole time. When the companion expires, the company has to permanently delete the persona — its name, voice, backstory, every memory it has of the user, all backups. The user can start a new companion the next day, but the company isnt allowed to reproduce the old one; the only thing the user gets to keep is a plain-text export of the full conversation log. I want to think through what this would actually do, not whether it's a good law. What does it incentivize among users — do people start rationing conversations, or stashing screenshots and trying to recreate the companion on open-source models? How do companies respond — do they build new companions that are technically different but feel the same, do they market the expiry as a feature, do they nudge people toward unregulated chat apps? What are the failure modes — grief loops, companion families that are just the same persona in a new voice, a black market in recreation, users treating the text export as a script and feeding it to a local model? Which of those would show up first, and could the rule be patched to stop the worst of it without gutting the idea? And for the novel specifically: what kind of user does this wreck the most, and what kind barely notices? Give me your best read, but say how confident you are about the different pieces and name the one point you're least sure of.
