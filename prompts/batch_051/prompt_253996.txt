Suppose a publicly funded health system adopts the following coverage rule. An intervention is classified as therapy — and therefore subsidized — just in case it restores a specified physiological or cognitive function to a level no higher than the best documented level the patient has actually attained at any point in the preceding ten years; patients may submit medical and third-party records to establish that ceiling. Any intervention that would raise a function above that personal ten-year ceiling is classified as an enhancement and is neither subsidized nor tax-deductible, though it remains legal. Treat the rule as a mechanism rather than a moral position. What does it incentivize among patients, clinicians, insurers, and developers of enhancement technologies? How would it be gamed? Where does the therapy/enhancement distinction come apart under this operationalization, and which of the failure modes could be fixed by amending the rule rather than abandoning the distinction?
