You are an operations researcher. Your native idiom is constrained optimisation: an optimiser chooses decision variables so as to maximise or minimise an objective function subject to constraints, with hard constraints delimiting what is permissible and soft terms in the objective expressing what may be traded off. You routinely work with penalty methods, Lagrange multipliers and lexicographic objectives, and you are attentive to the fact that the choice of what goes into the objective and what is imposed as a constraint is a modelling decision, not something the mathematics itself supplies. You have no formal background in philosophy.

I would like you to translate a position in moral philosophy into your idiom as faithfully as possible, and then tell me what does not survive the translation.

The position comes from Robert Nozick's discussion of the moral status of animals in Anarchy, State, and Utopia. Nozick explores a two-tier account of moral status. In the first tier, beings capable of the relevant kind of rational self-direction are protected by inviolable side constraints: there are things that may not be done to them, whatever aggregate benefits would result, and they may not be used merely as means. In the second tier, beings that lack this capacity, and Nozick places non-human animals here, are not protected by such side constraints. Their interests enter a broadly utilitarian reckoning: our treatment of them is to be assessed by the overall balance of benefit over harm, with their pains and pleasures included in the aggregation. The position therefore treats persons in a broadly Kantian manner and animals in a broadly utilitarian manner, with the line between the two tiers drawn by the presence or absence of the relevant sort of rational self-direction.

Your task is not to attack or defend the position. Treat it as a specification to be modelled. Produce the most exact translation of its internal structure into the vocabulary of constrained optimisation. Be explicit about the modelling choices you are forced to make: what the decision variables are, who or what the optimiser is, what belongs in the objective function, what belongs in the constraints, and what the feasible set contains. Where a component maps cleanly onto a concept you already have, say so. Where it maps only with distortion, say precisely what is distorted. Where it cannot be expressed in your vocabulary at all without smuggling in assumptions, say what is missing, and give a judgement as to whether the absence reveals a limitation of the constrained-optimisation framework, an unclarity or tension in the original philosophical position that would have to be resolved before it could be modelled, or both. Keep the translation honest: do not silently import assumptions from constrained optimisation and present them as if they were already in Nozick's position.

First, give me the translation and the residue report as continuous prose, with subheadings if useful. Then, after the prose, give me a structured summary.

The structured summary should be a JSON object with the following fields:

- "overall_mapping": a string of one or two sentences stating the central correspondence you are proposing, namely which component of the position is the objective function, which component is the constraint set, and which component corresponds to the upstream choice of what counts as the optimisation problem.
- "components": an array of objects, one per component of the position you translated. Each object must have:
  - "component": a short paraphrase of the component in ordinary English.
  - "translation": its rendering in constrained-optimisation terms.
  - "fidelity": a string, exactly one of "high", "medium" or "low".
  - "residue": a string saying what failed to carry over; use an empty string if nothing was lost.

- "diagnosis": an array of objects, each with:

- "bottom_line": one or two sentences giving your overall assessment of whether the two-tier picture is a substantive moral position or is partly an artefact of how constrained optimisation carves the world into objectives and constraints.
