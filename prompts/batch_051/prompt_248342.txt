I'm a high school student taking a programming course and also sitting in on a philosophy reading group, and I keep getting caught on an analogy I would like checked. The group has been reading about dialetheism and paraconsistent logic, and the part that stuck with me is the way paraconsistent logics are built to survive contradictions. In classical logic, a contradiction—a statement and its negation both true—triggers the principle of explosion, which lets any sentence whatsoever become derivable; one inconsistency trivializes the whole system. Paraconsistent logicians try to design consequence relations in which a contradiction can sit inside the logic without everything following from it. The part that interests me is the containment: the contradiction is real, or at least tolerated, but it does not poison every derivation.

The thing that struck me is that floating-point arithmetic does something structurally similar with a special value called NaN. Under the IEEE 754 standard used by most programming languages, an operation like 0/0 or taking the square root of a negative number does not necessarily abort the program; it returns NaN, a value meaning not a number. Once NaN gets into a computation, it propagates—operations involving NaN return NaN—while the rest of the program keeps running as usual. NaN also has strange comparison behavior: it is not equal to itself, and most comparisons involving it come out false, so the clean picture of a proposition being either true or false seems not to fit it. That reminded me of dialetheism's truth-value gluts, except that NaN might look more like a gap than a glut.

I cannot tell whether this is a genuine structural parallel or just me pattern-matching. Could you spell out the mapping in both directions—what corresponds to a contradiction, to explosion, to the paraconsistent consequence relation, and where NaN sits? More importantly, what would have to be true on both sides for any result to transfer from how IEEE 754 handles NaN to how a paraconsistent logician should handle contradictions, and what is one prediction the analogy makes about paraconsistent logic that turns out to be false? For example, if NaN is best understood as a truth-value gap while a dialetheia is a glut, does that wreck the comparison, or is the containment idea strong enough to survive the mismatch?
