And this is the student's answer:

Student answer: Aristotelian syllogistic works by parsing sentences into a subject and a predicate, and its quantifier words — all, no, some — tie two such terms together. Frege replaced that subject-predicate analysis with a function-argument analysis and introduced variables bound by quantifiers, including nested quantifiers. That is what allowed logic to represent relations and not just monadic properties. For example, the argument from Every horse is an animal to Every head of a horse is a head of an animal turns on the relational expression is-a-head-of. Syllogistic cannot even state that argument, but in Frege's notation it comes out as a straightforward valid inference. Frege also showed that his system was sound and complete; that metatheoretic result is the real reason modern logicians prefer his logic over syllogistic. So the main change wasn't extra notation so much as a proof that the whole system works.
