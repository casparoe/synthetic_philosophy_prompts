I manage a small apartment building, and my screening policy quotes the city's fair housing ordinance, which says: "An owner may not reject an applicant on the basis of race, color, religion, sex, national origin, ancestry, disability, familial status, or source of income, and may not use any screening procedure that has the effect of doing so, unless no less-discriminatory procedure would serve the same legitimate business purpose." I bought a commercial tenant-screening score from a data broker. The score doesn't take any protected category as an input—its built from rent payment history, eviction filings, and past addresses. But it turns out the score is systematically lower for applicants whose rental history includes addresses in a heavily immigrant neighborhood, because landlords there file evictions at higher rates. I just rejected someone whose score was below my cutoff, and I didn't know about this pattern when I bought the score. Am I in violation of the ordinance? I'm less interested in the practical legal answer then in what each approach to interpreting the clause—the plain words, the purpose, what the drafters would have said if asked, what a reasonable applicant would understand—says about whether it reaches a facially neutral algorithm with a disparate effect the landlord was unaware of. Does it change anything if I later learn about the pattern and keep using the score?
