I am a retired natural scientist who has recently taken up the philosophy of mind, and I have been working through Dennett's ladder of creature-types. As I understand it, the ladder runs from Darwinian creatures, whose only hypothesis-testing is natural selection over reproducing lineages; through Skinnerian creatures, which during one lifetime prune blindly produced behaviors by reinforcement; and Popperian creatures, which can let candidate behaviors or hypotheses be eliminated in an internal model rather than in the world; to Gregorian creatures, which enrich that internal model with culturally inherited thinking tools such as words, numerals, and formal techniques. I would like to analyze the rise of large language models—the release of ChatGPT in late 2022 and the ensuing debate over whether such systems actually understand—through this ladder. Please be strict about the mapping: for each rung, identify the unit on which selection acts, whether that unit is even well-defined for an LLM, and where the correspondence is merely metaphorical rather than explanatory; consider how pretraining, reinforcement learning from human feedback, chain-of-thought generation, and the inheritance of language as an already built thinking tool map onto the rungs. Then run the analysis in the opposite direction: say what this case exposes about the taxonomy itself—whether an LLM belongs on a single rung, straddles several, requires a fifth rung, or reveals that the rungs are a useful heuristic rather than a natural-kind hierarchy, perhaps because the relevant unit of analysis is no longer a single continuing mind but a distributed artifact, a training lineage, and an institution. Where the ladder is silent, do not force the case to fit; say so plainly. Throughout, please distinguish what is settled among people who know this material from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
