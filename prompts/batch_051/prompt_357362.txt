I lead the policy team at a large social media platform. We must determine, at high volume and under serious time pressure, whether a violent incident is classified as terrorism in our internal safety systems, content-removal decisions, and public transparency reports. The label triggers a separate rule set and carries substantial political and legal consequences, so consistency matters, but the incidents are heterogeneous and often ambiguous. I want a reusable decision procedure that a trained policy analyst with no background in political theory can run on a single incident in about ten minutes, grounded in the conceptual criteria that distinguish terrorism from other forms of political violence—in particular, whether the targets are civilians or noncombatants, whether there is a political or ideological aim, whether the violence is meant as communication to a wider audience, and whether state and non-state actors are to be treated symmetrically. The procedure must say which distinctions are load-bearing and which can be compressed, must flag every step that is a judgment call rather than a mechanical test, and must identify where the standard criteria themselves are unsettled. It must handle the hard cases: state forces attacking a market; an insurgent attack on a military checkpoint; infrastructure sabotage with no casualties; a leaderless attacker whose manifesto is politically incoherent; and public figures who praise or justify an attack without directing it. Give three worked applications, including one in which the procedure classifies an incident differently from ordinary public usage and one in which the procedure cannot return a determinate verdict. The final procedure will have to survive review by our legal and public-policy teams.
