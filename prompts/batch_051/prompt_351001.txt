"Objective Bayesians deny that priors are just subjective opinion. They hold that for any body of evidence there is a unique rational prior, and you get it by maximizing Shannon entropy subject to the constraints the evidence supplies. Entropy measures how much information a probability distribution contains, so maximizing it means assuming exactly as much as the evidence forces you to and nothing more. For instance, if all you know about a die is that its long-run average is 4.5, the rational prior is the flattest distribution over the six faces with that mean. This makes the prior fully objective: two people with the same evidence cannot rationally disagree, because the constraints pick out a single distribution. In fact any distribution can be represented as the maximum-entropy distribution for some choice of constraints, so objective Bayesianism doesn't just solve the problem of priors; it solves the problem of induction."

I'm suspicious of that leap at the end — and the line about entropy measuring how much information a distribution "contains" sounds backwards to me, like higher entropy should mean I know less about the outcome, not that the distribution contains more information.
