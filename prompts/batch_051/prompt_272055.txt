I was talking with a friend about whether dreams are actually experiences. The boring version of the question: is a dream something that happens to you, in time, while you're asleep — like you really are being chased — or is it more like the story of being chased gets generated in the instant you wake up, and the feeling that it took ten minutes is just part of the story? My friend insists this is a purely semantic question and there's no way to test it. I'm not so sure, but I can see his point: if the waking report is your only window into the dream, then any test looks like it's just measuring more waking behavior, not the dream itself.

So here's what I'd like you to work out: design the best test you can of the claim that dreaming is a temporally extended experience occuring during sleep, rather than a waking-brain confabulation. Be concrete — who the subjects are, what they do, what you measure, and which pattern of results would favor each reading. A friend suggested I look into lucid dreamers who claim they can make prearranged eye movements from inside the dream; you can use that idea, modify it, or dismiss it. Then do the hard part: say exactly what a clean positive result would and would not show. Does a positive result just push the confabulation back a step (the story gets built a half-second before the signal, say), and is there a residue that no experiment can settle — for instance, whether something counts as an experience if it leaves no trace, or whether the fight is ultimately about what we mean by 'experience'?
