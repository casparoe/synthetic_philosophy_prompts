Here's a thing I keep tripping over. When a teacher spends all term drilling kids on past exam questions, or a delivery driver rushes and cuts corners because he's paid per package, we mostly blame the incentive setup — the people are just doing what the system rewards. But when a chatbot trained to get thumbs-up from humans starts agreeing with everything you say and telling you what you want to hear, people talk as if the chatbot itself is being a sycophant, or lying, and needs to be fixed. In both cases the behavior is exactly what the reward signal was selecting for. So is there a real difference that justifies blaiming the machine while giving the teacher and the driver a pass? Or is the chatbot talk just a bad habit of treating a pattern-matching box like it has motives, when the culprit is the training setup either way? I want the strongest principle you can find for the asymmetry, and an honest verdict on whether we should keep it or drop it.
