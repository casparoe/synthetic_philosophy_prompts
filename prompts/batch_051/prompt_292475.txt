I am a third-year philosophy undergraduate taking a course on causation in classical Islamic philosophy, and we have just been working through Al-Ghazālī’s occasionalism. I would like to ask for a satirical piece for our department’s student magazine, and I want the satire to be earned by the metaphysics rather than by caricature. Could you write a deadpan, formal insurance claim denial letter, around 550–650 words, from a company that has adopted Al-Ghazālī’s occasionalism as its official doctrine of causation for claims handling? The letter should have the standard apparatus: a policy number and date, a subject line, a section reviewing the loss, a list of exclusions, and a closing paragraph about appeals. It should consistently reason from occasionalist commitments: created things have no causal powers of their own; God creates every event directly at each instant; the regularities we call causes are only God’s custom or habit (ʿāda), not necessary connections; and on the cotton-and-fire example, fire does not itself burn cotton but God creates the burning on the occasion of contact, and could withhold it. The jokes should be built out of these ideas: the fire is not a true cause, the insured house is re-created moment by moment so the very subject of the damage becomes elusive, and the policy should distinguish covered customary divine action from uncovered suspensions of custom, so that a careful reader feels the real costs of occasionalism for responsibility, compensation, and ordinary causal talk without the letter ever announcing them. Please begin your response directly with the letter, with no preface or commentary.
