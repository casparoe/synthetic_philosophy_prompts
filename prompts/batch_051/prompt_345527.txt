Me and my friend went in circles for an entire afternoon about whether rights are trumps, side constraints, or just instruments, and I came out with a little homemade theory which I want checked before I try it on him again. His line was that rights are frozen cost-benefit analysis — institutional shortcuts we could in principle replace with the underlying consequentialist sums. But the trump picture and the side-constraint picture both left me itchy too, because they all still make a right into a consideration that sits in the same balance as utility and either wins or loses. My idea, which I'm calling rights as tripwires (yes, dont judge the name), is that a right is not a weight at all. It's a tripwire: once a specified kind of action crosses the line, it doesn't just add a very large negative entry to the consequentialist ledger. It switches which kinds of reasons can even be counted. Below the wire, you can weigh costs and benefits normally. Once the wire is tripped, cost-benefit talk about the protected interest goes inert — you cannot 'buy off' the violation with utility, no matter how much utility there is. But the right can still give way if you can produce a same-type consideration, meaning another right, or the same right of many other persons, and at that point it is not that the right got outweighed; we have moved to a different justificatory question entirely. The part my friend hated: rights on this view are insensitive to magnitude until a different category of reason shows up. A million units of convenience cannot touch one person's bodily integrity, but a credible threat to another person's life changes the question — and the number of lives does not do the switching; the category does. Example: weighing my noise and convenience against my neighbor's wish for quiet before 10pm is ordinary reasoning because no wire has been tripped. Once it is her kidneys or her blood, no amount of aggregate convenience gets a hearing; only another right gets a hearing, and then the question is not 'how much utility' but 'whose right, and why does it bind me.'

Honesty mode, please. Is this basically Raz's exclusionary reasons wearing Nozick's shirt, and have I reinvented something already in the literature? Does the category-switch idea actually do any work beyond saying rights have lexical priority over one class of reasons? Give me the three strongest objections, and tell me whether any of them is fatal rather than merely awkward. Also, does the concept of a right earn its keep on this view, or have I accidentally explained rights away?
