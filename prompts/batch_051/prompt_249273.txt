I am a PhD student in twentieth-century international history, and I have been puzzling over why the debate on the origins of the Cold War seems to have stalled. The orthodox school treated the conflict as a response to Soviet expansionism and the breakdown of the wartime alliance; the revisionists inverted the story and located the driving force in American economic and strategic ambitions, above all the pursuit of an open world economy; the post-revisionists, in turn, tried to step back from assigning blame, emphasizing mutual misperception, the security dilemma, and the way each side's defensive decisions registered as offensive in the other's eyes. Yet in seminars the old divisions reappear almost unchanged, and newly opened archives tend to be absorbed into whichever framework a historian already holds rather than shifting anyone's position.

My hypothesis is that the field is stuck because the question "who started the Cold War?" is really several questions wearing one name: a chronological question about what happened and in what order; a causal question about which decisions or structures most raised the probability of confrontation; a counterfactual question about what would have happened if one side had acted differently; and a moral question about blameworthiness. If that is right, the schools may be talking past one another less about the record than about which of these questions is the real one and what kind of answer would settle it.

I would like this hypothesis tested rather than simply accepted. Diagnose the disagreement in terms of what the three schools actually claim. Is the impasse substantive, or is it largely a matter of different questions sharing a single heading? Is there a hidden empirical question underneath it, a dispute about the standards for assigning responsibility in history, or a difference in bedrock intuitions about agency and structure? And given the diagnosis, what would count as progress—new evidence, a better account of causal responsibility, a separation of questions, a change of question, or something else—or should we stop expecting this debate to converge?
