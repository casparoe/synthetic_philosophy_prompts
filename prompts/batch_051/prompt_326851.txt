I'm a novelist, and I'm trying to make a fictional institution hold water instead of just being a thesis in a costume. The community in my draft — a small, wealthy, extremely earnest guild — has a founding rule: every public statement a member makes must be literally true, and a single false sentance gets you fined or expelled, enforced by a rotating panel of auditors. The rule binds literal content only; nothing is said about implication, omission, knowing winks, selective examples, true-but-beside-the-point answers, or the timing of a truthful sentence. They've essentially drawn the lying/misleading line at the level of law rather than etiquette. What would this do to how people actually talk, who becomes trusted, and who climbs to the top? I half-expect the best misleaders to flourish while the straightforward ones become marks, literal truth to lose all its signaling value, suspicion to crawl into even trivial statements, and the auditors to become the real government. But maybe it goes somewhere less obvious, so tell me where the scheme breaks first and whether the failures are fixable — say, by policing 'the spirit of the rule' or punishing known misleading — without turning the oath into something no longer about truth at all. I want the incentives and second-order effects made concrete, not a morality tale.

Please commit to a definite trajectory for the institution and argue for it; don't lay out the options and leave me to choose. End with a single line: \answer{...} containing a one-sentence summary of your verdict.
