I am currently taking a philosophy of science course, and after our unit on scientific realism I have been trying to make an objection to the pessimistic meta-induction precise, but I cannot tell whether the argument is sound or where it goes wrong. I would be grateful for a step-by-step audit.

The argument runs as follows.

(1) The pessimistic meta-induction reasons from a catalogue of past theories that were strikingly successful yet whose central posits turned out not to exist — the caloric theory of heat, the phlogiston theory of combustion, ether theories of light — to the conclusion that our current successful theories are also probably false in their central claims.

(2) Every theory in that catalogue is classified as false by our current best science. It is current physics and chemistry that tell us there is no caloric fluid, no phlogiston, and no luminiferous aether.

(3) If the pessimistic meta-induction is good reasoning, then our current best science is likely to be false in its central claims.

(4) If our current best science is likely to be false in its central claims, then the negative verdicts it issues about caloric, phlogiston, and the aether cannot be relied upon.

(5) If those verdicts cannot be relied upon, then the catalogue invoked in premise (1) has no securely established instances; the induction would rest on data points that, by the induction's own lights, we are not entitled to trust.

(6) An induction resting on data points we are not entitled to trust gives no reason to accept its conclusion.

(7) Therefore, the pessimistic meta-induction is self-undermining: if its reasoning is good, it destroys the evidence it needs, and if its reasoning is not good, it gives no argument against scientific realism.

Is this argument valid? If it is not, which numbered step fails, on which reading of it, and what is the strongest reconstruction available to a defender of the pessimistic meta-induction? If it is valid, does the conclusion really damage the pessimist, or does it only show that the induction must be stated more carefully?
