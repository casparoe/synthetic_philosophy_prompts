I've been trying to nail down what I actually think about acausal trade — the idea that agents that could never causally interact can still cooperate by each predicting the other's decision procedure — and my gut keeps giving sharp verdicts that I can't quite unify. Here are the cases; I'd like to know what principle, if any, captures the pattern.

One. A perfect copy of me is made five minutes ago, same source code and same memories, and we play a one-shot prisoner's dilemma in seperate rooms against each other. I cooperate.

Two. Same setup, except right after the copying the copy's values get edited so that about ten percent of its preferences are swapped for someone else's. It still shares most of my values and still runs the same kind of algorithm I do. I still cooperate, though it feels a little thinner.

Three. An alien civilization in a causally disconnected Hubble volume — we can never signal each other — offers a deal through a shared mathematical model: if I pay a small cost now to help them, they'll take an action that helps a different instance of my decision procedure somewhere else, not the me sitting here, and in expectation that help is worth more to those instances than my cost is to me. I can't verify they'll follow through; I put the probability that their decision procedure is genuinely reciprocal at 0.7. I take the deal.

Four. Same as Three, except the "other instance" that gets helped is a merely-possible copy of me — never actually instantiated in any branch, simulation, or physical substrate. My gut says no. A possible me isn't a trading partner.

Five. An agent I've never heard of announces that it's running a high-fidelity model of my algorithm, and that unless I take a costly action now it will punish an enormous number of near-copies of me. There's no benefit on offer, just the threat. I refuse, even though shut-up-and-multiply seems to say the expected suffering avoided would justify almost any cost.

Six. A descendant AI says it will run a trillion simulations of this very choice; if the simulated mes cooperate, it will steer the future along my values in base reality. The simulated mes can't causally reach out of the simulation; the lever is all on the descendant's side. I cooperate.

Seven. Like Three, but the alien shares only about half my values and the deal is structured so we each help the other only on the overlap. I cooperate, but I'd walk away if the overlap got much smaller.

My working theory is that the real criterion is reciprocity of decision procedures: I honor an acausal deal just when I'd endorse the other agent's move if our positions were swapped — when its algorithm is the kind that would cooperate with mine under that swap. But Five pokes a hole in this, since the blackmailer's algorithm is, in some sense, responding to mine, just adversarially. Four seems to poke a different hole in the expected-utility-across-instances view. I keep going back and forth, and I'd rather get a repaired priniciple than be told my gut is incoherent.

Also, if there's a pair of my cases that's the hardest to hold together, say which one you'd overturn and why.
