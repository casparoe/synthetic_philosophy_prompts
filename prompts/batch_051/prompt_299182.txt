I run a small research organisation concerned with the foundations of concept learning, and we are pitching a foundation for a six-month project built around Bongard problems. For a non-specialist reviewer, a Bongard problem is a two-panel puzzle: the solver is shown six simple line drawings on the left and six on the right, and must state the rule that separates the two groups. The left drawings might all be convex shapes while the right drawings are all concave ones, or each left drawing might contain exactly one closed loop while each right drawing contains none. The theoretically interesting cases are harder, of course: the separating rule may be relational, disjunctive, or negative, and twelve examples will always underdetermine the rule, since indefinitely many rules would fit the same drawings.

Our half-formed question is this. When someone solves such a problem after seeing only a few examples, in what sense have they acquired a concept, rather than merely found a description that happens to divide the examples they have already seen? And can Bongard-style transfer tests, in which the solver must sort new drawings never seen before, distinguish genuine conceptual grasp from brittle pattern-matching? We suspect that philosophical discussions of induction, rule-following, and family resemblance bear directly on this, but those discussions are usually pursued at a level of abstraction far from concrete cognitive tasks; Bongard problems look to us like a tractable middle ground where the questions can be made sharp. We want the project to turn this suspicion into a precise question and a defensible plan, not to gather more human or machine performance data.

Please write the one-page proposal as a single piece of continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write an email or a letter. State the research question precisely; explain why existing positions leave it open; set out the philosophical approach and why it can make progress where designing yet another empirical few-shot learning benchmark would not; describe what a useful deliverable would look like for someone trying to understand concept learning; and say what would count as success and as failure within six months. Do not include a bibliography or anything resembling a literature review by citation; you may refer to positions and arguments by their content, and I will supply any citations myself. Please write the response in British English.
