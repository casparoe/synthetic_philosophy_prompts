A friend and I got into the weeds about whether sola scriptura actually has the resources to stop the regress of interpretation, and he leaned on the Westminster Confession of Faith (1.7): "All things in Scripture are not alike plain in themselves, nor alike clear unto all; yet those things which are necessary to be known, believed, and observed, for salvation, are so clearly propounded and opened in some place of Scripture or other, that not only the learned, but the unlearned, in a due use of the ordinary means, may attain unto a sufficient understanding of them." I replied with this argument, but now I'm not sure it's as tight as it sounded. (1) For the unlearned to know they've reached the "sufficient understanding" the passage talks about, they have to actually use the ordinary means—reading, comparing passages, maybe listening to preaching or teachers. (2) In any genuinely disputed case, applying those means means deciding which passages are the clear ones, what those clear passages themselves mean, and whether the obscure passage has been properly brought into line with them. (3) Each of those decisions is itself an act of interpretation performed by a fallible human mind. (4) So before Scripture can resolve the original dispute, a fallible act of judgment has to resolve how Scripture bears on that dispute. (5) If that fallible judgment is the thing doing the settling, then the final authority is not Scripture but the judgment, which is just the regress objection wearing different clothes. (6) If the judgment is protected from error, then we've made the unlearned reader (or their teacher, or their tradition) an infallible interpreter, which the Confession itself never does. (7) So either the clarity claim leaves the regress in place or it smuggles in an infallible interpreter. Is this argument valid? If not, exactly which step fails, and what would the correct version of that step be? If it is valid, which premise does someone committed to the Confession have to reject, and what does rejecting it cost them?
