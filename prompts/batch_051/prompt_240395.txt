I keep trying to explain the liar paradox to people and it always comes out as a diagram with three boxes — bivalence, the T-schema, self-reference — and a note saying pick which one to give up. What I actually want is a poem that does the paradox rather than gesturing at it. Write a villanelle about the liar. The two refrains should themselves form a two-sentence liar cycle: the first refrain says the second is false, and the second says the first is true (swap it if you want, as long as the cycle has no stable assignment in principle). The villanelle is the right form here because the refrains return on schedule and reassert the exact thing you thought you'd settled two stanzas ago. When the refrains make their final return as the closing couplet they should land the diagnosis instead of just reasserting the cycle: commit, precisely, to which piece the poem has been forcing you to give up — the unrestricted T-schema, bivalence (gaps), non-contradiction (gluts), or self-reference in the object language. Someone who has read Tarski or Kripke or Priest should be able to tell which fix you've picked without the poem naming it. It should still be an actual poem rather than a versified encyclopedia entry, and yeah, its meant to be read aloud at the end of a seminar, so don't let the rhyme scheme drive the content off a cliff.
