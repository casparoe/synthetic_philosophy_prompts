I've been going down a rabbit hole about causation lately — not for a class, I have zero background in this, just late-night reading — and I keep oscillating between two things that both feel right. On one side there's the Russell-style view that causation isn't really part of the worlds fundamental inventory. When I actually learn physics, 'cause' never shows up in an equation; the rock and the window just exchange momentum and the glass ends up broken. The laws don't even care which event came first in the simple cases, so there's nothing in the equations that marks the throw as the 'cause' and the break as the 'effect'. From that angle, saying the throw caused the break starts to look like us projecting an action-story onto events, not like a deep fact about what the collision consisted in. The view is attractive because it's so clean: I don't have to answer what the causal glue is made of, and I stop multiplying weird relations beyond what physics seems to need.

But then I push a mug off my desk and watch it smash, and I cannot make myself believe that the one-way-ness of it is just something I'm adding. The push makes the smash happen; the smash does not make the push happen. If I wiggled the push, the smash would change, but wiggling the smash wouldn't change the push. And it doesn't feel like a projection: the asymmetry is sitting right there in the world, before I arrive to tell a story about it. So I'm stuck between a theory that seems suspiciously tidy and an intuition that seems suspiciously homemade.

What I'd like is for you to walk me through the strongest version of each side, and then give a negotiated verdict. Is the one-way-causation intuition the kind of thing I should debunk and drop — and if so, what's the actual debunking story, not just 'it's an illusion'? Or should the Russell-style view be amended so that causation stays real but isn't basic, like a higher-level phenomenon built out of something else? Or are the two compatible on a careful reading? I want to know what would justify each move, not just which label I should pick. And can you write the answer in American English?
