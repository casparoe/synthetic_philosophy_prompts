Consider the following variant of the Mary's room knowledge argument. As in the original, Mary has spent her whole life in a black-and-white room and has mastered every physical fact about human colour vision. The new element is this: the room contains a device that directly stimulates her visual cortex and can produce, in a controlled and repeatable way, the full range of colour experiences that ordinary perceivers have when looking at red, green, blue, and intermediate hues. Mary has used the device many times, so she is intimately familiar with each of these experiences considered from the inside. But the device's stimulation settings are unlabelled: while she is in the room, neither she nor anyone else can tell her which stimulation setting corresponds to which public colour word, and she has never seen a coloured object or a non-white light.

On the day of her release, she is shown a ripe tomato and told that its colour is red. It would be natural for her to say something like "So this is what red looks like." Work through what the standard positions on the knowledge argument should say about this variant. Does Mary learn something new when she sees the tomato? If so, is it a new phenomenal fact, a new recognitional ability, an old fact under a new mode of presentation, or something else? If she does not learn a new phenomenal fact, does that weaken the original Mary's room argument, or does it merely show that the variant has changed the very thing the original scenario was designed to test? Which of the standard replies becomes easier or harder to motivate once Mary already has the experiences themselves and lacks only the link to public colour words? Finally, say how confident you are in your analysis and single out the point you are least sure about.
