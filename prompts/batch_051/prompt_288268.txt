Exam question (20 points): State and explain each condition of Nozick's truth-tracking account of knowledge, being careful about how the conditions are relativized to a method. Then explain why Nozick takes the account to imply that knowledge is not closed under known logical implication, illustrating the point with a perceptual belief and a skeptical alternative that the perceptual belief entails is false. Finally, consider this case. Sameera looks at an ordinary-looking wall clock currently displaying 3:00, and it is in fact 3:00; she believes it is 3:00. Unknown to Sameera, the clock contains a hidden mechanism that would hold the display at 3:00 if the actual time were anything other than 3:00. According to the tracking account, does Sameera know that it is 3:00? Give the reasoning.

Student's answer: On Nozick's view, S knows that p via method M just in case (1) p is true, (2) S believes p via M, (3) if p were false, S would not believe p via M, and (4) if p were true, S would believe p via M. Condition (3) is sensitivity, and condition (4) is the safety condition — roughly, the belief could not easily have been wrong. Relativizing the conditions to a method matters because, otherwise, a grandmother who sees her healthy grandson would be counted as not knowing, since his family would have told her he was healthy if he were ill; Nozick's method clause says she still knows, because her method is seeing him rather than hearing reports.

Nozick rejects closure because sensitivity, unlike truth, is not preserved under known implication. Consider Mina, who sees her hands and believes she has hands, and the proposition that she is not a handless brain in a vat. Mina knows she has hands: in the nearest worlds where she lacks hands, she would not believe she has hands. But she does not know she is not a handless brain in a vat: if she were such a brain, her experience would be indistinguishable and she would still believe she was not one. So the tracking account allows one to know the entailing proposition while failing to know the entailed one.

For Sameera, the tracking account says she knows it is 3:00. Her belief is true and formed by reading the clock, and had it been 3:00 she would have believed it was 3:00, so the fourth condition holds. The third condition also holds once the method is held fixed. Her method is reading the clock, not checking the clock against the actual time. In the nearest worlds where it is not 3:00, the hidden mechanism would have kept the display at 3:00, so the same method would still have produced the belief that it is 3:00. But the method itself would not have changed or malfunctioned in those worlds, and that is what the method-relativization is meant to rule out. Since the method is the same and the belief is produced by that method, Nozick's sensitivity condition is satisfied. All four conditions are therefore met, so Sameera knows.
