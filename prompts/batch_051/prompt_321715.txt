I chair the clinical ethics committee of a large teaching hospital. We are revising our ethics-consultation policy, and the sticking point is the word dignity. Our current policy tells consultants to respect the dignity of the patient but does not define it, and in the last year it has been doing opposite work in hard cases. In one case, the adult children of a patient with advanced dementia demanded continued artificial nutrition and hydration, arguing that he has intrinsic dignity as a human being and that withholding feeding would treat him as disposable. In another, the care team for a competent patient with end-stage ALS argued that she should be supported in refusing ventilation because a life prolonged in a condition she experiences as degrading would violate her dignity. Same word, opposite conclusions. Two committee members want us to adopt a substantive definition of dignity and make it a standing consideration in consultation; two others think dignity is a rhetorical amplifier that adds nothing to the more specific values it gestures toward and want the policy to replace it with precise terms such as autonomy, welfare, and respectful treatment. Draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete policy: define dignity substantively, use it without defining it, or drop it in favor of more precise value terms. It must treat the strongest point on the losing side seriously, not as a strawman. Format the opinion with these sections in this order: Background and the two cases (about 120 words); The case for retaining a substantive concept of dignity (about 140 words); The case for replacing the term with more precise values (about 140 words); My recommendation (about 160 words), including the exact wording you propose for the policy's dignity clause; Dissent handled (about 80 words). The recommendation should be one the committee could actually adopt with minimal editing.
