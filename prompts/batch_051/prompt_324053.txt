I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background, and I keep getting into this argument with a friend about pre-AGI bargains — deals people try to lock in now about how the post-AGI world gets run: a lab promising to share the benefits, countries agreeing to limits, that kind of thing. My current view is that these deals are basically worthless. After AGI exists, the people or system holding power wont be bound by whatever we agreed before, because the enforcement mechanisms that make promises stick — courts, contracts, reputation, governments — all assume a world where nobody has decisively more power than everyone else, and AGI breaks that assumption. So a pre-AGI bargain is just a promise made in a world whose rules are about to stop applying. I want to stress-test this rather than just have it confirmed. What would have to be true for a pre-AGI bargain to actually matter? Which mechanisms could make one stick, and which of those are live options rather than merely logically possible? What evidence or arguments should actually move me, sorted by how much they'd change my mind? And am I insulating the view by saying any bargain that does hold was never a real AGI transition — and if so, is that a bug or just how claims about one-off events have to work?
