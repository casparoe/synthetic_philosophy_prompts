I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about objective chance — the idea that some probabilities are really out there in the world, like the chance of a fair coin landing heads or a radioactive atom decaying within the hour — and how that's supposed to constrain what a rational person believes. One analogy I keep running into, going back at least to David Lewis's work on the Principal Principle, is that chance is like an expert advisor: if the chance of heads is 0.5, you ought to set your own degree of belief to 0.5, the same way you'd defer to a trusted meteorologist who says she's 30% on rain. The principle itself is usually put as: conditional on the full history of the world up to now and the true theory of chance, a rational initial credence in a proposition should equal its present chance.

I want to know how far that analogy actually holds, the way you'd audit 'the market is an ecosystem.' What corresponds to what? The expert's stated degrees of belief presumably correspond to the chances, but what about her track record, the fact that she has evidence you lack, the moment you receive her report — do those have counterparts, and if so what are they? Where does the mapping break down? I'm especialy interested in the ways an expert can be wrong, biased, hedging, or answering a slightly different question — features chance itself can't have — and what those asymmetries do to the work the analogy is meant to do. What is the analogy doing in Lewis's argument: is it just an intuitive gloss, or is it actually carrying the normative claim that deferring to known chances is rationally required? And does it survive the fiddly cases people bring up right away — inadmissible information, where you've seen the coin land heads so the chance is no longer your guide, and the undermining problem, where a chance theory assigns positive probability to futures that would make that very theory false? If it breaks under those, is there a better analogy, or would the principle be better off stated with no analogy at all?
