Please argue for the following position: when deciding under risk, the correct extension of the Pareto criterion is the safe, or ex post, version, according to which a reform is an improvement only if it leaves every affected individual at least as well off in every state of the world and makes at least one affected individual strictly better off in some state, rather than the ex ante version, which requires merely that each individual's expected welfare rise. Treat the ordinary deterministic Pareto criterion as the special case in which the state space is a singleton. Your argument should defend the safe criterion against two familiar objections: first, that it conflicts with the preferences of individuals who would willingly accept a possible loss in some states for an expected gain; and second, that it is so restrictive as to rule out nearly every worthwhile reform, including insurance-like arrangements. Present the argument as exactly five numbered steps, each one or two sentences long, with no introductory or concluding sentences.
