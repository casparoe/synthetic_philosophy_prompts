I’ve been arguing with a friend about Dōgen’s fascicle Uji, and I came up with a reading I’ve been calling the local-clocks view. The idea is that Dōgen is not saying that all beings share a single time continuum; rather, each being is its own time, like a local clock that runs in its own way. A mountain’s being-time includes the weathering of rock and the return of seasons; a person’s being-time includes memory, anticipation, and aging. There is no master clock that makes these clocks agree on a common now. Instead, their times only partially overlap when the beings interact causally, perceptually, or karmically. I think this is why Dōgen can say both that time passes and that it does not pass, and why he can call each thing an instance of time without making time a container or a mere dimension.

I want an honest assessment. Is this a coherent reading of uji, or have I just dressed up the relational theory of time in Buddhist language? Does it capture anything distinctive about Dōgen’s treatment of practice, impermanence, and nonduality, or does it make Dōgen too much like a modern philosopher of physics? Please name the nearest existing interpretations or concepts if you know them, and point out exactly where my local-clocks view breaks down — for example, for causally isolated or nonsentient things, or when I try to explain how language and remembrance work across local times.
