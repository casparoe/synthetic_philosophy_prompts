A structural analogy has been nagging me, and I can’t tell whether it is deep or superficial. In the Ship of Theseus, planks are replaced one at a time until no original wood remains, and the puzzle is whether the continuously repaired ship or a rival ship rebuilt from the discarded planks is the original. I see the same shape in software: a codebase is refactored module by module until no original line survives, while version control lets someone check out an early snapshot and develop it as a rival project. The mapping is: original planks to original lines; replacement to refactoring; the reassembled ship to a fork built from old code; and sameness of ship to sameness of project. In both cases one candidate is defined by continuity through replacement and another by preservation of the original material. But in software we usually treat repository identity, not code preservation, as what settles which project is canonical, whereas the ship case is standardly taken to pose a question of fact about identity, however hard to answer. Is the analogy a real structural match, or does it break exactly at the point where codebase identity is stipulated rather than discovered? What would have to be true of codebase identity for the puzzle to transfer, and what does the parallel get wrong? Please say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you are least sure of.
