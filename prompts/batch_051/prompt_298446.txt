I sit on the ethics committee for a neurotechnology lab developing a noninvasive occipital-stimulation device, and we are drafting the safety clause that will govern studies in which the device is used to deliberately induce unusual color experiences. The working text reads: "The investigator shall not administer any stimulation protocol that produces pain, acute emotional distress, or a persistent alteration of the participant's pre-study perceptual baseline; nor shall a protocol be offered unless its expected perceptual effects are described in advance in terms the participant can understand."

A pilot case has since come to us. A participant consented to "altered color perception of uncertain character" and was exposed to a sequence calibrated to her retinotopic map. She reported no pain and no distress. She did report, for several minutes, a stable, saturated color that was no familiar hue and that she could not locate among mixtures of any colors she had previously seen; she later described it as a red that was green at once, while insisting that before the session she would have called such a color impossible. She rated the experience as fascinating and said she would repeat it. At the six-week follow-up, however, she said that ordinary reds now sometimes strike her as faintly bracketed by that strange color, as if the memory of it sits beside them; a standard battery nevertheless found no measurable decline in her hue discrimination.

Does the working clause permit or rule out repeating this protocol with future participants? Give the best reading under the clause's words, under its purpose, under what the drafters would have said if this case had been put to them, and under what a reasonable participant would understand from the consent process; then say whether and how the clause should be amended. Structure your reply in six titled sections in this order, roughly 80–100 words each: "Bottom line", "The letter", "The purpose", "The drafters in hindsight", "The reasonable participant", and "Amendment"; lead with your bottom line.
