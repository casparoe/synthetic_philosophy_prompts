I've been going back and forth on the transparency thesis about photographs, the idea that a photo is more like a mirror or a telescope than like a painting: when you look at one you literally see the thing photographed, not a picture that stands in for it. The theory keeps winning me over on reflection because it seems to explain a bunch of things at once — why a photo counts as evidence in court in a way a sketch by a courtroom artist wouldn't, why we say "that's Nana" when we point at a snapshot instead of "that's a picture of Nana," and why seeing a photograph of the Grand Canyon can make someone say they've seen the Grand Canyon even if they've never stood there. It also gives an elegant story about the difference between photos and paintings: the photo's content is counterfactually dependent on the scene through a mechanical process, so it inherits the kind of perceptual contact a mirror has, whereas a painting passes through a person's beliefs and intentions at every stroke.

But I have a stubborness intuition that won't shut up. When I'm holding a photograph of my grandmother, who died years ago, I'm clearly looking at a flat piece of glossy paper with blobs of dye on it, not at her. Saying I'm seeing her feels almost ghoulish. And the intuition gets stronger the further the process gets from an ordinary snapshot: a heavily processed Hubble image with false color and stacked exposures doesn't feel like a window, it feels like an artifact somebody made; a long-exposure photo of star trails shows a scene no human eye could ever have seen. Yet a live CCTV feed on a screen feels transparent even though it's arguably just as processed, just less visibly so. I dont know whether the intuition is tracking a real feature of these cases or is just an artefact of how much processing I can picture happening.

So give me the strongest case for each side. Should I debunk the intuition and keep the theory whole? Should I narrow the theory so that only some photographs are transparent, and if so by what principle — the amount of counterfactual dependence, the absence of human intervention at certain stages, the kind of causal chain, something else? Or is there a reading on which both are right — I see my grandmother through the photo while also seeing the paper, the way I see through a window while seeing the glass — and does that reading actually save the theory or just restate it? Walk me through what each move costs, and which one you would give up.
