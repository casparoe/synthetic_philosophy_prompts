I just finished season one of Severance, and its got me going in circles about an idea I keep coming across in pop-science videos: that consciousness is a kind of user illusion. The way I understand it (and I might be butchering it), what feels like a rich private movie playing inside my head is really more like a computer desktop—a simplified interface the brain shows itself, with icons and folders standing in for all the messy machinery underneath. And the me who seems to be sitting there watching the movie is itself part of the interface, not a little person in a theater.

The innies in Severance seem like a perfect test case. Same brain, same body, but a self that basically gets booted up from scratch on a conference table: no memories, but still feelings, preferences, attachments. Lumon practically runs the feelings—the numbers on the screen that just feel scary for no reason, the wellness sessions, the break room where you repeat an apology until it supposedly sinks in. That all looks like the show is saying the self is manufactured, a story the system spins.

But the show also pushes against that idea. Helly would rather hang herself than keep living as an innie. Irving falls for Burt. Dylan flips out just from hearing one thing about his outie's kid. That doesn't look like a desktop icon protecting itself—that looks like a person. So give me a reading of season one through the user illusion lens: where does the show actually dramatize the idea, and where does it resist it or show the idea's limits? Does it end up saying the innie is just as real as the outie, or that "real" is the wrong question to ask? Point to a scene or two a true believer in the user illusion idea would cite, and at least one scene a skeptic would cite back.
