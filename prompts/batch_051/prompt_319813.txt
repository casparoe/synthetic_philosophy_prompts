I've got to circulate a short statement of where I stand on truth and knowledge for a workshop on anti-realism, and someone suggested I get it stress-tested before sending it round. So here's the set of claims I find myself committed to:

1. A sentence has content only if there is some possible procedure for finding out whether it's true. If no possible experience or piece of reasoning could settle it one way or the other, it isn't really saying anything.
2. For any true proposition, it is possible that someone knows it. I've always thought of this knowability principle as falling out of 1.
3. Knowledge is factive: you can't know something false.
4. If you know a conjunction, you know each conjunct.
5. There are truths nobody currently knows (exactly how many grains of sand were on Brighton beach at noon last Tuesday, or something like that).
6. Possibility is classical in the relevant way: if a claim entails a contradiction, then the claim isn't possible.

Could you go through that list and tell me which pairs or larger subsets are pulling against each other, how severe each tension is, and what the cheapest fix would be in each case? I'd also like to know which apparent tensions I should stop worring about, and whether there's a single revision that would clean the most of this up.
