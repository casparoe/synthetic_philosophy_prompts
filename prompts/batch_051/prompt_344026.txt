I've got a workshop paper next month defending a Mu'tazili-flavored position in Islamic ethics, and I want to go in with a clear picture of how the Q&A is actually going to go. My thesis is that at least some acts are good or bad in themselves, and that human reason can grasp some of those moral facts before any revelation is on the table — so "God commands the good" comes out as informative, and "God wouldn't command the bad" is a substantive claim rather than a definition. The audience is the tricky part: it's a department colloquium where the two senior commentators are trained in kalam and lean Ash'arite. They hold that good and bad just are what God commands and forbids, and that any independent standard of goodness would make God answerable to something outside Himself. There's also a philosopher of religion who works on the Christian Euthyphro and thinks the usual dilemma is a false choice, plus some grad students who know the broad strokes but not the Islamic specifics.

My argument, roughly, is that the Ash'arite still needs a non-trivial notion of justice to say God's command is just. If "just" just means "commanded by God," the sentence says nothing, and the Ash'arite's own habit of praising God's justice seems to presuppose a standard that isn't identical with the commanding. I also want to argue the Mu'tazili view doesn't actually subordinate God: it doesn't posit some external boss God has to obey, only that God's own nature, or the rational order, fixes what "good" means. I know there are soft spots. Give me a Q&A prep sheet rather than a full defense: the objections this audience will actually raise, in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way an Ash'arite-trained specialist would put them, each with what I should concede, what I should hold, and a note on where my reply is weakest — and include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
