I keep going in circles on the old Kosslyn/Pylyshyn fight and hitting the same wall. A friend is convinced the depictive side basically won: mental rotation time tracks angle and image scanning time tracks distance, so images must be picture-like. But I don't see why a propositional representation plus the subject's tacit knowledge about how real seeing works wouldn't produce the same reaction-time curves — they're not introspecting a format, they're simulating a world. Is there any experiment that could actually seperate a depictive representation from a propositional one that encodes the same spatial information and has the same expectations? I want the strongest possible test, the pattern of results that would count for each side, and then the part I care most about: what would a decisive result still leave open about whether mental images are picture-like in any deep sense, rather than just functionally similar to pictures? Or is that gap unfillable because the whole dispute is about what we should mean by representation?
