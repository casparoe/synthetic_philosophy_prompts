I was recently appointed to a municipal commission that reviews proposed ordinances against the city charter and enabling statutes, and I tried to write down the interpretive commitments I actually hold. The list: (1) the controlling meaning of a statute is its ordinary public meaning at the time of enactment; (2) the intent of a multimember legislature is not a genuine object, and talk of what the legislature 'wanted' is at best a short-hand; (3) where the text is genuinely ambiguous, the best reading is the one that advances the scheme's evident purpose; (4) the absurdity canon is legitimate, so a judge may depart from the plainest reading when that reading yields a result no reasonable lawmaker could have endorsed; (5) an interpretive method is, everything else equal, better when an untrained reader can derive what the law requires from the enacted text alone, without legislative history; (6) where a term is vague, settled and consistent agency practice over time can fix its legal meaning. Most of the time these feel like one coherent position, but occasionally they rub against each other, and I cannot tell which of the apparent conflicts are real. Which commitments are in tension with which? Order the tensions by severity, say what would have to give in each case, and identify any apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection.
