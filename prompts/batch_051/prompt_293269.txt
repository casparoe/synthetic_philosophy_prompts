I'm applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship and need to submit a two-page research proposal. My half-formed idea centers on transcendental arguments. The secondary literature seems trapped between two readings. On the first, a transcendental argument refutes skepticism by showing that the skeptic must already accept the very claim she denies, because that claim is a necessary condition of something even the skeptic cannot coherently abandon. On the second, the argument shows only that we must treat some framework or commitment as indispensable, which appears to be a fact about us and our cognitive needs rather than about how things are. The standard moves are either to accept this modest reading or to add a roughly verificationist premise; neither has satisfied the other side. I suspect the debate stalls because 'necessary condition' is being treated as one relation when it may be several: a condition without which a proposition could not be true; a condition without which a concept could not be correctly applied; a condition without which experience could not be the kind of experience it is; and a condition without which we could not function as agents. Whether a transcendental argument can support an anti-skeptical conclusion may depend on which of these relations is doing the work, and on what exactly the skeptic is being asked to abandon.
