I keep getting asked to design short courses for our company's internal learning platform, mostly for working engineers who need to pick up a skill fast (like reading a database schema, or the basics of a regulatory domain). I've been rereading Whitehead's The Aims of Education, and the bit in "The Rhythm of Education" where he lays out the cycle of romance, precision, and generalisation keeps feeling like it should cash out into an actual design procedure, not just a vibe. So what I want is something like a checklist or flow I can run when I sit down with a proposed course: what questions do I ask about the material and the learners, in what order, to decide whether the romance stage is even possible? What counts as a real romance stage versus "teacher does a fun demo"? How do I know when the precision stage is too early or too late, and what does the generalisation stage look like for a 4-week professional course? You don't need to argue for Whitehead's view; take it as given and operationalize it. Then run it on two proposals: a 4-week course called "Intro to SQL for people who have never coded" and a 4-week course called "Negotiation for engineers." I'd honestly like to see a case where the procedure tells me not to run the course in its proposed form, or to restructure it in a way the subject-matter expert will hate. Be concrete about the indicators I'd look for.
