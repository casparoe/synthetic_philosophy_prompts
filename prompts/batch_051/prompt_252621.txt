I'm in a course on Tolstoy's theory of art, and we've been reading What Is Art? in the Maude translation. In chapter five he gives this example — I'm paraphrasing, not quoting — of a boy who once met a wolf in the forest and was terrified; later he tells the story so vividly that his listeners feel the same terror, and Tolstoy says that's art in miniature, because one person's feeling has infected another. I wrote down my immediate verdicts on a bunch of cases, and now I'm trying to see whether there's a single principle behind them or whether I'm just using Tolstoy when it suits me.

Here are the cases:

- A cereal commercial jingle makes me genuinely nostalgic for childhood summers. I don't think it's art, even though I felt infected by something the ad maker probably didn't feel.
- The last movement of a Mahler symphony leaves me with a heavy, specific feeling I'd call the composer's resignation. I don't know exactly what was transmitted, but I'd call it art.
- A stranger's post on an anonymous site describes his father's funeral and I end up crying at my desk. I don't think that's art.
- A child's crayon drawing of our dog with three legs and a huge smile gives me the kid's affection. I'd call it art.
- A polictician's campaign ad with swelling music and flags gives me a wave of patriotic feeling. I want to say it's not art, but I can't tell whether that's because I dislike the content, and by Tolstoy's infection test it passes.
- A urinal in a gallery (or any readymade) doesn't infect me at all, but I still want to say it's art, just not the kind Tolstoy would value. This is the one I'm most confused about.
- A friend's performance piece where she sits silently in a chair for an hour transmits no clear feeling, but the situation as a whole leaves me lonely. I'm torn.
- A randomly generated chord progression on a website happens to give me a pang of sadness. Not art, because no one was transmitting anything deliberately.

So my question: is there a single principle that gets all or most of these, and is it just Tolstoy's infection criterion plus a sincerity/intention clause? Or do I need to give up some of the verdicts? Please also say what your principle would say about a case I haven't listed: an AI that composes a piece after being trained on millions of emotionally charged works, and the listener is infected but the AI had no lived feeling. And can you separate what's settled Tolstoy scholarship from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic?
