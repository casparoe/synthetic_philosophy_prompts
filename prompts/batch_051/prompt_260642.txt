I've been messing with a distinction for dispositions and I want it stress-tested. I call them loud and quiet triggers. A loud trigger is the ordinary stimulus: the glass gets struck. A quiet trigger is everything else that has to cooperate for the manifestation to show up: the glass isn't wrapped in packing material, no sorcerer changes its molecular structure the instant it's hit, there's a hard floor under it, and so on. My claim is that finks and masks are not two extra problems for analyzing fragility; they're just quiet-trigger failures. A fink pulls the disposition out during the loud trigger, a mask blocks the manifestation while the disposition stays put, but both are really the quiet trigger going false at different times. So I've been thinking the right analysis is: x is disposed to M when S, provided the quiet trigger holds. The provided-clause does all the work the literature gives to finks and masks. I realize this smells like a ceteris paribus clause with a new name, and maybe that's all it is, but I can't tell whether that's a feature (dispositions just are ceteris paribus) or a bug (the view goes empty). Is this already someone's view under a better name? And where does it break? Give me the strongest objection and tell me whether its fatal or just uncomfortable.
