Before I read another defense or condemnation of a particular biopic, I want the full specification first. Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of what we owe the real dead when we dramatize them—in historical fiction, film, and television—must meet. Fix the purpose as: evaluating whether a dramatization of a specific real person has discharged its obligations to that person and to the historical record, for a general audience that will absorb some of what it sees as history.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and mark which requirements you take to be non-negotiable and which are really only the preferences of one camp (strict documentary fidelity, loyalty to the family, maximal artistic license, and so on). I especially want to see where the list is internally tense: which plausible pairs of requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, or can be satisfied only by qualifying one of them. Address the distinctions a theory cannot collapse, such as those between private individuals and public figures; between inventing events and filling gaps the record leaves open; between falsehood, omission, and misleading framing; and between harm to the living and wrong to the dead. Also locate the wrong itself: does the obligation run to the dead person, to survivors, to the integrity of public memory, or to the viewer, and can the theory say so without quietly collapsing that distinction into one of the others?

Then test the list briefly against three problems, and say whether any of them shows that a requirement needs revision: (1) a fictional account that preserves every documented public fact about a historical statesman but inserts a wholly invented private act of cowardice to make a thematic point; (2) a film drawn from a writer's private letters, published against her estate's wishes, that dramatizes scenes she never intended to become public; (3) a production that uses licensed footage and a synthesized performance to make a dead civil rights leader appear to endorse a present-day political program. Keep the weight on the desiderata and their motivating cases rather than on a long scoring of existing positions. Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding inadequate views, and which requirement or tension is the best pressure point against your own list—that is, say what would change your mind about how the desiderata should be drawn or ordered.

Use American English.
