Here's the spec.

Construct a one-paragraph scene from ordinary life where the relief theory clearly predicts laughter, but the benign violation theory predicts no humor — there's no violation of any norm, benign or otherwise. And here's the hard part: the laughter has to be genuinely comic; it should read like humor, the kind of thing a reader would recognize as funny, not just a nervous spasm or a sob. I'm not interested in the easy version where a character giggles after a scare and everyone agrees it wasn't really funny. If you can't meet the spec, say so up front and build the closest thing you can, and explain which part of the spec broke. Don't cheat by stipulating that the laughter is funny or by wheeling in a prop joke from outside the scene.

After the scene, walk me through what each of the four theories says about it and why — including incongruity and superiority, which I left out of the spec on purpose. Then tell me which feature of the scene is doing the work, what happens to the verdicts if I cut that feature out, and how a defender of the benign violation theory would attack your case (they'll probably say you've smuggled in a violation somewhere, or that the laughter isn't actually humor). Be honest about whether the attack lands.

Since you're my philosopher, think carefully and start by brainstorming three or four candidate scenes you rejected and why, before giving me the one that survives. Oh, and write the whole answer in American English.
