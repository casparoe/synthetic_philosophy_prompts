Hi—I'm a PhD student working on population ethics, and I've been turning over a modification of the procreation asymmetry that I can't get to come out clean. The standard thought experiment compares two worlds: one in which a child exists with a life containing both pain and pleasure, and one in which that child never exists. The asymmetry says that in the no-child world, the absence of the child's pain is good, while the absence of the child's pleasure is not bad. So there's a reason not to create someone whose life would be net-negative, but no parallel reason to create someone whose life would be net-positive—at least not a reason grounded in the possible child's interest in existing.

Here's the variation. Suppose there are three options: create child A, create child B, or create nobody. A and B can't both be created. A would have a firmly net-negative life: serious pain, tiny pleasures. B would have a firmly net-positive life: tiny pain, serious pleasures. Now work through the standard asymmetry against this choice.

For A-versus-nobody, the table seems straightforward: nobody wins, because A's pain is bad and its absence is good, while A's small pleasures, once absent, don't count against nobody. For B-versus-nobody, though, the asymmetry still says B's tiny pain is bad and its absence in the nobody world is good, while B's large pleasures are absent but that's "not bad." So does nobody beat B too? That already sounds like the asymmetry refuses to create even lives that are obviously worth living, unless you allow B's present pleasures to outweigh the absent-pain good—and the standard table never tells you whether those are commensurable.

And for A-versus-B, there isn't even an column in the original table. If I create B, A's serious pain never happens, which is good; if I create A, B's tiny pain never happens, also good but lesser. Both choices leave the unchosen child's pleasures absent, which is supposed to be not bad in both cases. It's starting to look like the asymmetry can only say: minimize pain among whoever exists, and treat pleasure as adding nothing to the comparison once a child goes uncreated. That's suspiciously close to a negative utilitarian verdict across possible people.

So my question: does the procreation asymmetry, taken strictly on its own, yield a determinate ranking of A, B, and nobody in this three-option case? If not, where exactly does it go wrong—is the asymmetry being misapplied, is it incomplete about how to compare less-pain-plus-pleasure against more-pain-plus-pleasure, or does it need an extra principle (say, a person-affecting restriction) to keep it from collapsing into "only pain matters"? And if a defender has to bite the bullet and say nobody beats B as well, what exactly is the bullet?
