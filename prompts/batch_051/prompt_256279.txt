I've been having a running arguement with some friends about whether an endless afterlife would actually be good, and it's gone in circles so long I've started to wonder what we're even disagreeing about. One friend says no, because anything that goes on forever has to become boring eventually: after you've had every conversation, heard every song, seen every sunset enough times, you'd want it to end. He always brings up Bernard Williams's Makropulos case, where a woman who's been alive for 342 years on some elixir has got so bored and indifferent that she stops taking it and dies. Another friend says that's a mistake: boredom is a feature of our current brains and bodies, not of persons as such, so God could just make us so that we never get bored and always find things fresh. A third friend says even if God could do that, it would be a kind of lobotomy, because our capacity to find things interesting depends on having limited time and limited possibilities, so removing boredom would remove the very thing that makes life worth living. I notice we all seem to agree on the facts about boredom, but the argument hasn't moved at all.

What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it a factual one about human psychology, a theological one about what God can or would do, or a conceptual one about whether a life without boredom would still be recognisably ours? And given the diagnosis, is there anything that would actually settle it or move it forward, or is this one of those arguments where the real problem is that the question is too badly posed? Please answer in British English.
