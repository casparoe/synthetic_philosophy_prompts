i'm a software engineer with zero philosophy background, so forgive me if this is basic. i have a rule for miracle reports and i honestly can't tell if it's defensible or just a bias i'm attached to.

the rule: whenever someone reports an event that would require the laws of nature to be violated -- a faith healing, someone back from the dead, a weeping statue, a fulfilled prophecy -- i treat the report as false by default. and i don't mean 'not proven yet.' i mean i'm effectively certain there's a natural explanation, even if i never find the flaw. when a coworker told me his uncle's terminal cancer vanished after a pilgrimage, i didn't investigate at all; i quietly filed it under 'misdiagnosis, exaggeration, and people not telling the story straight' and moved on. i apply this discount no matter how many witnesses there are or how reliable they seem.

i think i reverse-engineered this from hearing about hume on miracles, never actually read him. the intuition being: it's always more likely that the report is somehow wrong than that the laws of nature took the day off, so testimony can never overturn the regularity of nature.

my worry is, couldnt a flat-earther use the exact same structure? 'a photo of a round earth from space? nasa faking it is more likely than everything i know about how the world looks being wrong.' from inside my head my rule feels like it tracks reality, but from inside his head, so does his. am i just running a biased classifier? i know enough stats to see this as setting my prior to zero, which is exactly the point, and maybe exactly the problem. should i actually be updating a small amount on multiple independent miracle reports, even if i think theyre almost certainly bogus? and is there a version of this rule that keeps the bullshit filter but doesn't turn into dogmatism?
