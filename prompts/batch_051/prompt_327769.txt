I run a small design studio that does pictogram and wayfinding systems for city agencies—bus stations, park systems, recycling programs, flood-evacuation signage, that kind of thing. We get a new icon request every few weeks, the stakes are real (people have to find an exit or not drink bad water), and our users include alot of people who dont read English well or read at all. Nobody on the team is a philosopher, but we've half-absorbed Otto Neurath's picture-language ideas—the ISOTYPE stuff—and we keep arguing about how to apply them. I want a decision procedure we can actually run on a new icon brief before any drawing starts.

Work from these Neurath rules as our studio understands them: a pictogram is more like a word in a language than a drawing; quantity gets shown by repeating a unit, not by stretching or enlarging it; flat silhouettes, no perspective, no decorative detail; a sign only gets its meaning from the system of signs around it; and there's a separate "transformer" step where someone decides what information the picture has to carry before an illustrator touches it. I dont need you to correct my reading of Neurath—just assume those are the rules and make them usable.

The deliverable is a checklist a project manager can run in under an hour with a designer and a client rep. For each step, say what question to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer, when the answer is an automatic no, and where the step is a judgment call we have to own rather than a rule. Also mark the points where the procedure hands the decision to the client board because its a value choice.

Then run the procedure on three briefs: (1) a "public defibrillator here" sign for a rec center; (2) a "no batteries in the trash" sign for a transfer station; and (3) a client who wants a friendly cartoon mascot pointing the way to the family restroom because they think the standard figures feel cold. I suspect that last one gets rejected, and I want to see whether the procedure actually says so and why. End by saying what the procedure cannot do.
