I've been rereading Nozick recently and I've become convinced that the Wilt Chamberlain argument stands or falls on what we mean by voluntary. So give me a working definition of a voluntary transfer (or voluntary exchange, whichever is the right unit) that handles these cases:

- A fan pays 25 cents to watch Wilt Chamberlain play.
- A hiker lost in the desert pays $500 for a bottle of water from another hiker.
- A worker takes a dangerous factory job because the only alternative is starving.
- A tourist buys a counterfeit watch from a street vendor who knows it's fake and the tourist doesn't.
- A patient signs a consent form for a surgery they don't actually understand.
- A landlord raises the rent 40 percent and the tenant stays because moving is too expensive.
- A mugger says your money or your life, and you hand over the wallet.
- A parent gives a child $10 as a gift.

I suspect the word is doing at least two jobs: one descriptive (nobody literally forced your body through the act) and one normative (the menu of options wasn't rigged by an unjust background). And I think Nozick's entitlement theory needs the first but is quietly helping itself to the second. If you agree, give me the two seperate definitions and say which cases flip between them; if you disagree, give one definition and defend the surprising verdicts. Either way, run the definition against every case and tell me which verdict is least secure.
