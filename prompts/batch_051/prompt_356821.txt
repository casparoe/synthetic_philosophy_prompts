A country emerging from three coups in ten years drafts a new constitution containing this interregnum clause:

"When the offices of President and Prime Minister are both vacant or both incumbents are declared unable to act, executive power passes to a Council of Continuity consisting of the five most senior judges of the Constitutional Court. The Council may take only such decisions as are necessary to preserve the constitutional order; it may not amend the Constitution, appoint judges, or extend its own authority. A decision of the Council lapses after sixty days unless the judges of the Constitutional Court who are not on the Council certify that the decision was necessary and that all of its effects can be fully reversed within one hundred and eighty days of the date it was made. A decision refused certification is void from the moment it was made."

Analyze this as a mechanism, not as a drafting problem. Walk through how it would actually operate in a prolonged interregnum: what the Council would be driven to do and to avoid; whether "necessary" and "fully reversible" can be made to mean anything stable for decisions like closing the borders, freezing bank accounts, postponing elections, or arresting a coup leader; who would effectively govern; where it would be gamed first; and whether the failure modes can be repaired without giving up the reversibility requirement. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
