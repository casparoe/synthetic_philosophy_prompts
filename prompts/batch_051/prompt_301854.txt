Many people would regard it as seriously wrong to kill a pig or chicken by slowly suffocating it, or to cut off a breeding animal's eye to make it reproduce faster, if either were done without pain relief. Yet standard aquaculture does both kinds of thing: farmed fish are routinely killed by asphyxiation in air or by live chilling in ice water, processes that can take many minutes to over an hour to produce insensibility, and female shrimp in commercial hatcheries routinely have one eyestalk cut or crushed off to stimulate spawning, typically without anesthetic. The usual defense is that fish and decapod crustaceans cannot feel pain, or feel it far less than birds and mammals, so there is nothing in the neighborhood of the wrong. But a category-difference of exactly this kind has been invoked before and later abandoned often enough that it cannot, by itself, carry the weight. What principle, if any, justifies the asymmetry? Give the strongest candidate you can find — a real difference in sentience, in the value of the lives involved, in human interests, in feasibility — and then say whether it succeeds, or whether our treatment of terrestrial farmed animals and our treatment of farmed fish and crustaceans should be brought into line in one direction or the other.
