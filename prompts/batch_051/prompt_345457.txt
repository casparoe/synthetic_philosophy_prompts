Last night I spent several hours arguing with a friend about free will, and I came away less sure than I started that the view I defended actually hangs together. I wrote down the commitments I found myself leaning on at different points in the argument, and I would like a careful audit of them before I put the position forward again. Here they are:

(1) Free will is compatible with determinism, and we possess it even if determinism turns out to be true.

(2) The free will worth wanting is not the ability to do otherwise in exactly the same circumstances. It is a bundle of capacities that come in degrees: self-control, foresight, responsiveness to reasons, and the ability to step back from a desire and ask whether to act on it.

(3) Moral responsibility does not require the ability to do otherwise. Frankfurt-style cases, in which a failsafe would have forced the action had the agent not decided on her own, persuade me that an agent can be responsible when her action issues from a reasons-responsive mechanism even if she could not have done otherwise.

(4) No one is the ultimate cause of her own self. By the time a person is in a position to shape her character, the capacity to shape it is itself the product of temperament, upbringing, and luck she did not choose.

(5) That fact does not undermine ordinary blame and praise, because these practices need not track ultimate desert. Holding one another to norms is justified largely forward-lookingly — it cultivates better agency — even though it also registers what was done.

(6) Still, constitutive and circumstantial luck should sometimes temper blame. Two people who both now meet the bar for reasons-responsiveness can, I find myself thinking, be blameworthy to different degrees for the same act if they arrived at that point by very different routes, one of them through advantages she never earned.

(7) If the difference between my acting one way and acting another were settled by a genuinely random event in the brain after everything else about my deliberation was fixed, that would not add to my freedom. It would make the action less mine, not more.

Please go through these systematically. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? Rank whatever genuine tensions you find by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be. Then tell me whether there is a single revision that would remove the most strain while changing the position the least.
