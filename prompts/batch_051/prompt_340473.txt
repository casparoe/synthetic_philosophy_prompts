I work as a backend engineer at a company that builds water-quality, feeding, and audit software for aquaculture farms, mostly shrimp and salmon. I have no training in animal welfare science or ethics, but I have ended up sitting on our internal welfare-standards committee, which reviews what our software may legitimately say about animal welfare and what its audit flags mean. The committee is deadlocked on one decision, and I have been asked to bring a short written opinion to the executive team before next week's meeting. Our welfare module already treats salmon as welfare-relevant and requires that salmon be stunned before slaughter. The disputed question is whether the same logic should extend to farmed shrimp. At present the module presents chilling shrimp in an ice slurry as an acceptable slaughter method. The proposal before us is that we stop doing that: a farm would be marked compliant only if it applies electrical stunning immediately before the shrimp are put on ice, and direct chilling without prior stunning would be flagged as non-compliant. Because large buyers use our software to audit their suppliers, a compliance flag has real commercial consequences.

One side argues that we should adopt the stunning requirement. They point to behavioral findings on decapod crustaceans, including avoidance learning, prolonged grooming of an injured antenna or limb, and responses that outlast the immediate stimulus, and they say these are difficult to dismiss as bare reflexes. Their view is that we are not entitled to assume there is nothing it is like to be a shrimp when the evidence points the other way, and that where the stakes are large and the facts uncertain, the responsible default is to require the less painful method. The equipment exists, it is fast, and at scale the added cost per animal is small relative to the suffering we would otherwise be tacitly licensing across millions of animals. They also note that several jurisdictions and certification schemes have begun moving toward protecting decapods, so a stricter default is probably coming anyway.

The other side says the science is genuinely unsettled and is disputed among the researchers who study it: many of the observed responses can be read as nociception without felt experience, and no one has yet established that crustaceans possess what would be needed for conscious pain of the sort that would justify this intervention. They add that we are a software company, not a physiology laboratory or a regulator, and that requiring stunning on contested science means imposing equipment costs, training burdens, and audit friction on producers, many of them small operations with thin margins. The plausible worst case, they say, is not that farms quietly upgrade; it is that they leave our platform for a less demanding buyer or certification body, and the same shrimp are chilled exactly as before, just outside our sight. We would then have done no good while staking our credibility on a welfare claim we cannot fully defend.

On my own I would normally try to settle this by writing a specification, a measurable standard a farm must meet to pass, but the dispute is upstream of any specification: it concerns whether the evidence about what shrimp experience is strong enough to make stunning a requirement rather than an option. What I need from you is the short written opinion I can circulate before the meeting. Please recommend a concrete course of action, whether adopting the requirement now, keeping the current default, or some phased or conditional option, and attach a definite time frame rather than deferring to further research indefinitely. Lead with the recommendation in the first sentence and then give the reasoning. Engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side, and say why, given it, you still recommend what you do. Please write the entire opinion as continuous prose, the way one would write a considered email or letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
