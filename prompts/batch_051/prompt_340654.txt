Define 'grammatical rule' so that it correctly classifies the following five cases: the default English past tense in -ed, including its extension to novel verbs (to google → googled); sing/sang/sung and the broader ablaut classes; go/went; Norse loans into English such as skirt and skin, which retain /sk/ where inherited words like shirt and shall shifted to /ʃ/; and the Neogrammarians' claim that sound change is exceptionless, with Verner's Law reclassifying the apparent exceptions to Grimm's Law as a further regular pattern once an accent condition is added. I am fairly sure that -ed counts as a rule in some sense, that go/went does not, and that the Neogrammarian regularity counts as a rule only in a different sense; I do not know what to do with sing/sang/sung or with skirt and skin. I suspect the phrase is doing at least three jobs at once — a description of what speakers do, a claim about their mental machinery, and a historian's claim about what changed when — and that these five cases force the jobs apart. Propose the definition, or propose a split if one definition cannot do the work, run it through all five cases, and say which verdict is least secure.
