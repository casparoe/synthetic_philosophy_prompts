I'm a philosophy PhD student, mostly working on tense and modality, and I'm trying to pin down my view on future contingents before my next meeting with my supervisor. I've got a package of commitments and I honestly can't tell whether it hangs together — every time I think I've found the inconsistency I find some way to squirm out of it, so I need someone to be a bit less charitable than I'm being with myself.

Here's the package:

(1) Bivalence. Every future-tensed statement like "there will be a sea battle tomorrow" is either true or false right now. No truth-value gaps, no third status.

(2) Truth-maker maximalism. Any contingent truth needs something in the world that makes it true. A truth with no truth-maker is one I don't want in my ontology.

(3) Open future, branching, no thin red line. From the present there are multiple mutually incompatible futures that are all genuinely possible, and no single branch is marked out in advance as the actual one.

(4) Present stuff does the truth-making. When "there will be a sea battle" is true now, what makes it true is the present state of the world — ships, orders, winds, whetever. I don't want to appeal to future facts.

(5) The lazy-argument verdict. The old argument that since it's already true you'll recover it's pointless to call the doctor is clearly bad. It seems to confuse truth with necessity, and I want my view to leave room for rational delibarating even when there's already a truth about what I'll do.

(6) The admiral verdict. Suppose the night before, the truth of "there will be a sea battle tomorrow" is made true by present facts about the world. I still want to say the admiral could call the attack off. It shouldn't turn out that he was free only until the truth-maker showed up.

Which of these actually conflict, which only seem to, and which conflicts are worse than they first appear? For each real tention, rank it by severity, say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would be, and then name the single revision that costs me the least overall. If I've garbled a technical notion (truth-maker maximalism, the thin red line, whatever) or the task is badly posed, just tell me that instead of playing along.

Keep it under 800 words.
