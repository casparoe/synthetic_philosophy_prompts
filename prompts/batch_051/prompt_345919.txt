I've been reading around in Purva Mimamsa lately and I've come around to something like there standard line on testimony: cognitions are valid by default (svatah-pramanya), error is always something that has to get introduced from outside by a specific defeater, and the Veda is authorless and eternal, so there's no author whose competence or intentions could even be in question. On this view you don't need to independently verify what a source says before you trust it; you start out warranted, and you only revise when something concrete trips the alarm. I want to know what would count against this position. Not just the obvious objection that we never actually encounter authorless texts. Which intuitions about lying, propaganda, testimony from strangers, language acquisition, or how we correct our own perceptual mistakes does the position rise or fall with? Which possible findings or cases would a committed Mimamsaka have to concede are genuinely awkward, and which would they dismiss as question-begging? I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and an honest verdict on whether the position has made itself unfalsifiable by treating every apparent counterexample as just another defeater being detected.
