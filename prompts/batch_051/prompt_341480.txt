The pessimistic meta-induction (PMI) in the philosophy of science runs roughly as follows: the history of science provides many theories that were empirically successful by the standards of their day yet were later abandoned as false in their central claims, so by induction we should expect our own successful theories to turn out false as well. I am interested in whether this argument survives a particular change in how scientific success is now achieved. A growing share of the most impressive predictive work — in protein-structure prediction, weather forecasting, and materials discovery, for example — is done not by explicit, interpretable theories whose central entities and mechanisms scientists can state, but by high-dimensional machine-learning models trained on large datasets, whose internal workings remain opaque even to their designers. Does the PMI apply to such models at all? Are they the kind of thing that can later be abandoned as false in their central claims, and does the historical sample of superseded theories include anything relevantly similar? If the argument does not transfer, should that weaken the PMI for traditional scientific theories, or does it simply show that realist commitments never covered such predictive instruments?
