I am a PhD student in philosophy working on formal value theory, and I am currently drafting a chapter on how the value of a world should be understood to depend on the conscious experiences it contains. The discussion repeatedly circles around two quantities — roughly, the amount of consciousness a world contains, and how good that consciousness is from the inside — and around three candidate rules for combining them. The first is additive: the value of a world is some weighted sum of the amount of consciousness and its average valence. The second is multiplicative: value is the product of the two quantities, as in the idea that the good a world contains is something like consciousness times happiness. The third is lexical: one dimension receives strict priority, so that no gain along the subordinate dimension can compensate for even a small loss along the dominant one. I can manipulate the formal expressions without difficulty, but I find it hard to say what the choice among them is ultimately a choice about.

Could you explain, in a way that remains precise about the formal structure but foregrounds the philosophical stakes, what each combination rule commits one to concerning the tradeoff between bringing more conscious life into existence and raising the average quality of conscious life? I would especially like to understand: what each rule implies about the separability of the two dimensions, that is, whether the value contributed by one depends on the level of the other; which populations or worlds the different rules treat as tied, and which they can compare only through strict priority; what each rule says about indefinitely many barely positive experiences, a single extremely intense experience, and experiences whose valence is zero or negative; and whether the apparent disagreement between the additive and multiplicative rules is partly an artifact of how happiness is defined, since if happiness is average valence per unit of consciousness, multiplying it by amount of consciousness seems to return something equivalent to a total, additive quantity. Put differently, how should I distinguish between cases in which these rules reflect genuinely different axiological positions and cases in which they are merely alternative parameterizations of the same underlying view?
