I serve on my high school's student-faculty conduct board, and we are deciding a case that has split the board. A student has been buying out the cafeteria's entire limited supply of a very popular food item just before the lunch rush each day and reselling it to classmates at roughly three times the cafeteria price. The student's defense is that every sale is voluntary, the buyers are glad to get something they otherwise could not, and the school should treat this as harmless enterprise, perhaps even a useful lesson in how markets work rather than a discipline issue. The counterargument on the board is that the profit exists only because the student deliberately creates artificial scarcity, adds nothing of value, and turns classmates into customers; on this view the behavior is greed rather than enterprise and should be banned. A third group wants to allow it with a markup cap.

Draft the board's short written decision: recommend one concrete course of action (allow without restriction, ban, or allow with conditions) and give the reasons, engaging seriously with the strongest argument for the losing position rather than only restating the winning one. The board is ultimately split on whether this sort of acquisitiveness is a character flaw the school should discourage or a legitimate engine of mutually beneficial exchange, so your memo should speak to that question as it arises in the facts. Keep it to no more than 400 words.
