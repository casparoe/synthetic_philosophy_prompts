I've been trying to work out what I actually think makes something funny, because the four theories I keep hearing about—superiority, incongruity, relief, benign violation—each fit some of my reactions and whiff on others. Here are my gut calls on cases. Someone slips on a banana peel: funny, though I feel a little bad after. A terrible pun where someone "loses interest" in banking: funny, but in a different way, almost because it's awful. A comedian doing an impression of a pompous politician: funny. The same comedian doing that same impression, voice and all, of a specific coworker who is clearly struggling: not funny, it feels mean. A toddler calling a horse "big doggy": funny and a little sweet. Being tickled: I laugh but it doesn't feel like humour, just a reflex. A dark joke a stand-up tells a week after a disaster: not funny, to soon. The same joke two years later: I honestly can't decide. And the case I keep going back and forth on: a joke I don't get at first, then someone explains the mechanism, and then I laugh—was it funny before the explanation, or did the explanation make it funny? Is there a single principle, or a plausible hybrid of these theories, that gets all or nearly all of these, and what does it say about cases like the explained joke? Which of my verdicts would that principle force me to revise, and is there a clean way to avoid just writing a separate clause for each one?
