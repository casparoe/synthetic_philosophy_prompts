Please give me your credence in the following proposition and decompose the reasoning.

Proposition. Let "unowned excellence" be an act or work of genuine excellence — a novel, a mathematical theorem, or a performance at the top of human ability — that is never known or appreciated by anyone, produces no welfare gains for anyone (no pleasure, no preference satisfaction, no relief of suffering), and gives the creator no positive experience. Holding everything else fixed, a world in which such a work is produced is all-things-considered better than a world in which it is not produced; the excellence itself makes the difference.

Decompose your overall credence into at least the following terms, giving a number and one line of justification for each: (a) the probability that there is some final value that does not reduce to welfare; (b) conditional on (a), the probability that achievement or excellence is among these non-welfare values; (c) conditional on (b), the probability that such value can attach to an achievement that is unobserved and makes no difference to anyone's felt life; and (d) the probability that, given all of the above, the stated world-comparison is true. Show how these terms combine into your overall credence. Then identify the term you are least confident in and say what evidence or argument would shift it most.
