I'm a commissioning editor at a popular history magazine. Three or four pitches a week lean on big non-human causes — plagues, climate shifts, volcanic eruptions — and my reviewers can't agree on where taking the environment seriously ends and environmental determinism begins. One reviewer says never let the weather explain a decision a person made; another says a cause doesn't need intentions to do real historical work. I need a reusable checklist my team can run on a pitch or draft: ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, which answers kill the piece, which send it back for reframing, and where the checklist stops and a person makes the call because it's a craft or values judgment rather than history. Mark honestly which steps are just judgment dressed up as procedure. Constraints: 45 minutes per piece, no climate historian on staff, the editor-in-chief signs off but wants consistency. Ground the checklist in the actual conceptual difference between a non-human factor as a historical actor (if that idea even makes sense for a bacterium or a volcano), a trigger, and a background condition — not office vibes. Then run it on three pitches: (1) a piece arguing the Black Death ended serfdom in England by killing enough laborers to shift the balance of bargaining; (2) a piece arguing the Little Ice Age's harvest failures caused the European witch hunts; (3) a piece saying the 1815 Tambora eruption caused the cold wet summer of 1816 and therefore caused Frankenstein. At least one should end with a verdict I'll hate (and yes, I'd love to run pitch 3 for the headline). And say plainly what the checklist can't adjudicate.
