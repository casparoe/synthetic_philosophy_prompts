I'm a novelist, mid-draft on a book where two characters — a painter and a neurologist — keep arguing about whether the idea that there's something it is like to be in a mental state is real or just a bad habit. I've read enough Dennett to know the Chase and Sanborn coffee-taster move: two people both say the coffee got worse for them, one says the taste itself changed, the other says the taste is the same but his standards got stricter, and there's no way to tell which is right. That scene is going to be the hinge of the book, and I dont want the argument to be the cartoon version.

So before I read any more of the qualia literature, write me the desiderata. What would a theory of qualia actually have to get right to count as adequate — not for a lab, but for someone who has to write smart characters disagreeing about it? I want the full spec: the cases it has to handle (Chase and Sanborn, inverted spectrum, any others you think are load-bearing), the distinctions it can't collapse — a quale changing versus a judgment changing, no fact of the matter versus a fact we just can't reach — and the evidence from introspection or neuroscience it can't simply wave off. For each requirement, say the consideration behind it, and tell me which ones are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's taste; I keep seeing ineffable, private, intrinsic, incorrigible listed as if they were a single package, and I doubt that. Also flag any pair of requirements you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, and say whether the list itself shows qualia are an incoherent posit or just that some of the usual properties have to go.

Then tell me which single requirement does the most to knock out the popular positions, and do a brief scorecard of Dennett's quining, a straight qualia realist, and a representationalist against the list.

Last thing: I want to know what would change your mind about your own list. Which requirement are you least sure is a genuine requirement, and what argument or consideration would make you drop it or add a new one? Where is your reasoning most likely to fail?
