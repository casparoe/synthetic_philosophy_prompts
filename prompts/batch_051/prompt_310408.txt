Write the joint statement that a committed rights-as-side-constraints theorist and a committed rights-as-instruments theorist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Keep the focus on how rights figure in practical reasoning—their deontic structure—rather than on their legal or historical content. The statement should give: (1) a numbered list of substantive claims both camps accept, avoiding vacuous or merely verbal agreement; (2) a disagreements table in which each camp's view is formulated in wording that camp's own proponents would accept; and (3) the genuine cruxes, specifying for each the sub-question whose answer would move at least one camp, a concrete case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, and each side's prediction recorded in advance. At minimum, address whether a right functions as an ex ante constraint on an agent's conduct or as a marker of which outcomes matter; whether the fact that an agent violates a right matters over and above the resulting harm; and whether rights-talk is eliminable in favor of talk about interests or welfare. If you conclude that part of the residual dispute is merely verbal, identify which part and why. I would rather you commit to definite formulations and judgments than survey the options.
