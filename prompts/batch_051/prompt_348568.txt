So my friend Dana and I argue about fairness constantly — specifically about who gets helped when things go bad — and she has this strikingly consistent set of calls she's made over the years that I've, like, kept a mental list of. If a kid is born with a serious genetic disease, she says of course society pays for treatment. But a wealthy guy who smoked two packs a day for forty years and got lung cancer? That's on him, she doesn't think we owe him a cent. Victims of an earthquake: obviously we rebuild their homes. A guy who lost his savings at a casino but still owns his house and can work: tough luck, you chose to gamble. A child who got a rare cancer from factory pollution in the town where she grew up, even without proof of exactly which factory did it: she'd pay for her care. A rich mountaineer who ignored three weather warnings and got helicoptered off the mountain: rescue him, sure, but send him the bill. And someone born into a poor family in a town with basically no jobs, who is still poor at forty: she says we owe him real support because he never had a fair starting line.

Here's what I want you to predict. A man grew up in the same kind of dead-end town, and the only decent-paying work was at a chemical plant where everyone knew some workers eventually got a lung disease from the exposure, though it wasnt guaranteed. He took the job, worked there twenty years, got the disease, and now he cant work and needs expensive ongoing treatment. Would Dana say we should pay for his treatment, if she's being consistent with her earlier calls? What I actually want to understand is what rule she's running — is it a "you chose it, so you own it" rule, a "nobody should end up below a decent floor no matter how they got there" rule, or something about whether a choice was really free given your starting point? I can see a few rules that fit everything she's said so far. Give me your best guess at her answer, identify which old case is the closest analog, and tell me one more case I could put to her that would force these rules apart.
