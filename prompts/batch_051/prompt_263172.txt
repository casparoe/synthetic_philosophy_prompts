I sit on a state-appointed reconciliation commission in a country whose previous authoritarian regime committed mass atrocities before a transition to democracy about two generations ago. We must recommend to the current government whether to issue an official apology and, if so, in what terms—specifically, how the statement should characterize the responsibility of today's citizens, almost all of whom were born after the transition. Draft the short decision memo I will circulate before the next meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action, draw the distinctions among criminal, political, moral, and metaphysical guilt rather than merely invoking them, and engage the strongest argument on the losing side directly: the commission is split between the view that a state can inherit guilt across generations and the view that guilt attaches only to identifiable individuals. Please close with a note on which consideration would most likely overturn the recommendation and where the memo's reasoning is most exposed.
