One of my colleagues has gone fully accuracy-first. His line is that epistemic rationality is nothing over and above expected accuracy — a credence or full belief is rational exactly when it does as well as possible, from the agent's own point of view, at getting the world right — and that every epistemic norm, from probabilism and conditionalization to norms about when to stop gathering evidence, can be derived as a theorem about accuracy. I honestly cant tell whether this is a substantive claim or a disguised piece of empirical psychology wearing a normative hat, so I want to know whether it can be tested at all.

Design the best test you can of the claim that accuracy is the sole fundamental epistemic value, as against a pluralist who says understanding, calibration, or the relevance of a true belief matter independently. Be concrete: what would you measure or manipulate, what result would count for the accuracy-first view and what for the pluralist, and — this is the part I care about — is your test actually tracking what people do, what they judge to be rational, or what is rational in fact? Say what a positive result would still leave open, and tell me where your proposed test is most likely to mislead, plus what evidence or argument would change your mind if you were defending the accuracy-first side (or convert you if you werent).
