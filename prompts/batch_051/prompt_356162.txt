ok so I'm a PhD student working on foundations of probability, sort of between philosophy of science and statistics, and I've ended up with a set of commitments that each feel right on their own but I'm no longer sure they hang together as a package. Last week I was grading undergrad stats homework and caught myself explaining why a confidence interval isn't a probability statement about the parameter, and then the same week in my reading group I was defending the Principal Principle, and it made me want to actually audit the whole combination instead of just assuming its fine.

Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm a subjectivist about credences: they're degrees of belief, the basic norms are coherence (the probability axioms) and updating by conditionalization, there's no uniquely rational prior, and the only rational constraint on a person's degrees of belief are coherence plus conditionalization.
(2) I also think there are objective single-case chances. A radium-226 atom has a chance of about 1/2 of decaying within 1600 years, and that's a fact about the atom or its setup, not a measure of anyone's ignorance.
(3) I accept the Principal Principle as the bridge: if I know the objective chance of E is x, then my credence in E should be x, setting aside cases where I have inside information that trumps the chance.
(4) I think the reference class problem is fatal for frequentism — any single event belongs to indefinitely many reference classes, and there's no principled way to pick the one whose relative frequency is supposed to be 'the' probability of the event.
(5) In practice, though, I'm fine with p-values and confidence intervals in scientific papers, as long as nobody reads a 95% CI as 'the parameter is in this interval with probability .95.' I use them and I teach them, and I don't think they're just cargo-cult ritual.

So: is this a consistent package? I honestly can't tell weather I'm a run-of-the-mill eclectic or whether one of these commitments has to give. Walk me through which pairs pull against each other and which only look like they do, ordered by how serious the tension is, and tell me what the cheapest fix costs.
