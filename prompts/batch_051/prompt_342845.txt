I have been going back and forth with a colleague about the semantics of vagueness, and we have reached the stage where we agree on every standard piece of linguistic data and disagree only about what explains it. He defends a version of epistemicism: for a predicate like "tall," there is, relative to any concrete context of use, a sharp cutoff between the tall and the not-tall, fixed by the totality of usage and the relevant worldly facts. On his view, when a competent speaker calls someone "borderline tall" and declines to assert either that the person is tall or that she is not, the speaker is registering her own ignorance of where that hidden cutoff lies. I have been defending a supervaluationist alternative, on which a borderline case is one in which the sentence is true on some admissible sharpenings of the predicate and false on others, so that it is neither true nor false outright; the hesitancy registers a genuine gap in what has been said rather than ignorance of a fact that already exists. We can each accommodate the familiar data—unwillingness to assert either disjunct, acceptance of "either she is tall or she isn't," sensitivity to comparison classes, and so on.

I want to know whether any observation could break this tie. Design the strongest empirical or experimental test you can of the claim that borderline hesitation tracks a hidden sharp boundary in the way that ignorance tracks a fact, rather than tracking the absence of any fact of the matter. Be concrete about the task, the manipulation, the result that would favor each account, and the interpretation each side should accept if the data came out the other way. (One thought I had: tell subjects that a reliable machine has measured the context's threshold, then see whether their confidence, assertion thresholds, or betting behavior shift accordingly. But I worry that this merely stipulates a sharpening rather than reveals one.) Then say exactly what a positive result would establish and what it would fail to establish, and whether the part that remains untested is a limitation of the experimental design or a sign that the original dispute is not fully empirical. Please write your answer in American English.
