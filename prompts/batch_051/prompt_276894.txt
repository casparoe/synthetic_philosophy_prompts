After retiring from a career in physical chemistry, I have begun working through philosophical logic, and I find myself repeatedly returning to the sorites paradox because it resembles the threshold phenomena and phase transitions I used to model. The classical argument, as I understand it, runs on bivalence: for a vague predicate such as bald, one assumes

(a) a person with zero hairs is bald, and
(b) for any n, if a person with n hairs is bald, then a person with n + 1 hairs is bald.

Since each conditional must be either true or false, and a finite chain of modus ponens steps preserves truth, one is forced to conclude that a person with 100,000 hairs is bald, which is false.

Now take the development that has always seemed natural to me as a scientist: degree-theoretic semantics. Let bald take values in the real interval from 0 to 1, let the conditional take the Łukasiewicz value min(1, 1 - p + q), and let validity preserve some designated degree. On this picture, each conditional in the chain can be almost but not perfectly true — say, degree 0.99 — and the long chain of deductions degrades so that the unacceptable conclusion receives a low degree. The argument no longer goes through as a proof.

I cannot tell whether this is a genuine dissolution or a relocation. The sharp boundary seems to have moved into the metalanguage: one still must say which degree counts as bald enough, where exactly the conditional becomes too far below 1 to license an inference, and why 0.98 is acceptable while 0.97 is not. If that is right, the paradox may reappear in a repaired form, as a higher-order sorites about degrees rather than hairs. But perhaps the correct moral is different — perhaps what the degree-theoretic treatment shows is that the classical argument was always an artifact of forcing a vague predicate into a two-valued language, and that its real load-bearing component is the insistence on bivalence rather than the inductive premise or modus ponens.

I would like a candid assessment. Does the classical sorites survive the shift to degree-theoretic semantics? If it survives, state the repaired argument as explicitly as you can. If it dissolves, identify exactly where the old argument fails and what residual problem, if any, remains.
