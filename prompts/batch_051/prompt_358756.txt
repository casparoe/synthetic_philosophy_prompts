I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so apologies if I'm starting from the wrong vocabulary. My current book is about two sisters who fall out over moral questions. One has become a strict vegan and treats eating animals as transparently wrong; the other keeps saying this is just a disgust reaction that got promoted into a moral conviction — a bias, not a preference. I'd originally planned for one sister to be the reasonable one, but the longer I write the less sure I am whether the story should treat that distinction as real.

Here's what I'd actually like to know. Suppose someone says: my opposition to eating animals is a genuine moral value, not a bias. Is there any test that could actually settle that — an experiment, an intervention, a longitudinal design, anything? I want you to set out the strongest concrete test you can think of, say exactly what result would favor the bias reading, what result would favor the value reading, and then say what even a clean result would leave open. In particular, I want to know whether a positive result would show we've found a real difference in the person, or whether we'd just be re-labeling something after the fact. And is the line between a preference and a bias the kind of thing evidence can settle at all, or is part of it just a compliment we pay to views we approve of?
