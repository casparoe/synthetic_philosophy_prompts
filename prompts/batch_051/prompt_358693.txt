There's a passage in Book III, chapter VII, where Mill makes roughly this point: money is a contrivance for sparing time and labour, not a genuine source of wealth, and people mistake it for wealth because it is the instrument by which they get command over other things. I won't quote it exactly here; I'll put the exact paragraph up after the game. The central question I want them to hit is: when a bank or government prints money, what exactly gets created—wealth, liquidity, or simply someone's ability to bid real goods away from someone else? Include exact rules and roles, a timeline, how to keep 18 people engaged, a debrief that maps each game moment onto the Mill passage and onto the money/liquidity/wealth distinction, plus a note on what predictably goes wrong the first time. Be honest about one feature of Mill's idea the game can't reproduce—I'm especially curious whether it can get at why more circulating medium might feel like more wealth even after prices adjust.
