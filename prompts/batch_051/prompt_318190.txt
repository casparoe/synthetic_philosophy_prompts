In the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's entry on dispositions, the entry's author writes: "For its disposition, it seems, has to do only with its possibly shattering in certain conditions." That sentence looks right to me at first, but my verdicts on particular cases keep pulling against it. Here are the verdicts I am trying to reconcile. I would like you to tell me what principle, if any, captures the pattern, what it says about further cases, and which of my verdicts, if any, it forces me to drop. Please begin with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question. As you work through this, I would also like the response to say how confident it is in what it claims and to single out the point it is least sure of.

1. A fragile wine glass sits on a shelf and is never struck. I say it is fragile now.

2. The same wine glass is wired to an electro-fink: a device that would detect any incoming strike and, an instant before contact, alter the glass's microstructure so that it would deform rather than shatter. If it were struck, it would not shatter. I say the glass is still fragile before the strike.

3. The same fragile wine glass is packed in thick shock-absorbing foam, so that if it were struck, the foam would absorb the blow and the glass would not shatter. I say the glass is still fragile while packed.

4. A rubber ball is wired to a reverse fink: if it were struck hard, the device would first make it rigid and brittle, and it would then shatter. If struck hard, it would shatter. I say the ball is not fragile before the strike.

5. A sugar cube is sealed in a vacuum chamber and never put in water. I say it is water-soluble anyway.

6. A live wire is fitted with a safety relay that would make it dead if a conductor touched it. If touched by a conductor, it would not deliver current. I say the wire is live now.

7. A fragile glass is placed in a chamber whose air pressure is so high that, if struck, it would not shatter; the fragments would be forced back together before they could separate. I say it is still fragile, but I am less confident here than in the foam case.

If you think the best account would overturn one of these verdicts, say which and why, and say what the repaired account then predicts about the other cases.
