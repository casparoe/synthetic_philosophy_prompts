Im a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I've been reading up on griefbots — chatbots trained on a dead person's texts and messages so the bereaved can keep talking to them. A friend who writes about tech ethics gave me his confident summary of the debate, and I wanted to check it before I put anything like it in an article. He said something close to this:

'The griefbot panic is mostly a category error. A griefbot is just an interactive archive of someone's words; it is no more a fake person than a photograph is a fake face. The critics who say it stops people accepting that the person is gone are still working with an outdated model in which healthy grief means detaching and moving on. Modern bereavement research points the other way: a continuing bond with the dead is normal and usually healthy. Sure, consent is a genuinely hard problem — the dead never agreed to be turned into a chatbot. But beyond that, the rest is moral panic.'

I find his view oddly persuasive, maybe because it's unsentimental, but I worry it's a bit to neat. Can you give me an accuracy audit? I'd like you to separate what's fair from what's subtly wrong in his summary, say which parts a thoughtful critic of griefbots would object to and which parts they'd accept, and tell me whether his overall conclusion survives if the errors are corrected. Also happy to hear which bits are common simplifications rather than outright mistakes.
