I am putting together a handout for an advanced undergraduate seminar on quantified modal logic, and I want a decision tree or flowchart that sorts students into positions on the Barcan formulas, ∀x□Fx → □∀xFx and its converse □∀xFx → ∀x□Fx, without asking them to recognize the formulas or the conventional labels in advance. The tree should have eight to twelve branch points, each phrased as a concrete question that can be answered independently of the formalism: whether a merely possible but nonactual sibling is included in the domain of “there are things such that,” whether an ordinary artifact still belongs to the domain at worlds where it was never made, whether a way the world could have been can involve objects absent from the actual world, whether the existential quantifier should be read as expressing existence or a broader notion of being, and whether something can bear properties at worlds where it fails to exist. The leaves should include at least constant-domain possibilism, necessitism, and a Kripke-style actualism that rejects both formulas, plus whatever other leaves the answer combinations generate; empty or unnamed leaves should be explicitly noted and briefly diagnosed as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Each leaf should carry one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate should be flagged. I want the distinctions chosen in this order of priority: first whether quantifier domains vary across worlds, then the direction or directions in which they can vary, then whether existence is a quantifier or a predicate, then whether non-existents can be subjects of predication. I especially want the tree to show whether accepting the converse Barcan formula while rejecting the Barcan formula is a stable stopping point, since I will use it in a unit on whether everything that exists exists necessarily. Keep the questions accessible to someone who has taken an introductory modal logic course but has never seen the Barcan debate.
