I am a philosopher of physics, and I am preparing a document that could be used in a graduate seminar on the ontology of spacetime. I would like you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed spacetime substantivalist and a committed relationist. Explain the two positions with care at the top of the document, in formulations each side would accept. Treat substantivalism as the view that spacetime points and regions exist over and above the material events that occupy them and possess their geometric and inertial structure even independently of how matter and fields are arranged; treat relationism as the view that talk of spacetime is best understood as talk about a network of spatiotemporal relations among material bodies and events, with no independent arena left over once the relata are subtracted. Do not strawman either side: recognize, for example, that a substantivalist can deny that every formally possible distribution of matter over the spacetime points is dynamically possible, and that a relationist must be able to recover the distinction between inertial and accelerated motion that Newton's rotating bucket was meant to force on us. Where a formulation would be contested by one camp, flag it rather than pretending it is neutral.

Now produce the document itself. It should open with a numbered "Agreed Claims" section containing only claims both camps can sign, stated non-vacuously and without equivocation. Then provide a disagreements table with exactly these columns: "Contested claim" (a neutral statement of the point at issue); "Substantivalist rendering" (the version the substantivalist insists is the real issue); "Relationist rendering" (the version the relationist insists is the real issue); "Genuine or verbal?" (your judgment of whether the two renderings express a substantive disagreement or a difference in idiom); "Crux" (for substantive rows, the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side); "Agreed test or consideration" (the argument, thought experiment, or observation both camps accept as bearing on the crux); "Substantivalist prediction"; and "Relationist prediction". Make the renderings precise rather than sloganistic, and record the predictions in advance, as an adversarial collaboration requires. The rows should engage at least the following contested territory, though you may organize and add to it as you see fit: what the bucket thought experiment does or does not establish about absolute rotation; a symmetry-style argument in which all material content is shifted to a different place in spacetime; and a determinism-style argument in which the entire material and field content is moved smoothly over the spacetime points. In each case, first give the argument in a form both sides would accept before assigning it to one camp or the other.

After the table, close with a compact JSON summary of the collaboration. Use this schema: "document_title" (string); "agreed_claims" (array of strings, the claims from the first section, in order); "disagreements" (array of objects, each with "contested_claim" (string), "classification" (one of "genuine" or "verbal"), "crux" (string or null if verbal), "agreed_test" (string or null), "substantivalist_prediction" (string or null), and "relationist_prediction" (string or null)); and "remaining_obstacles_to_convergence" (array of strings naming the issues that would need to be settled before the two camps could write a fully unified statement rather than a statement with agreed claims and tabled disagreements). The point is the drafting: the document should be one that a careful substantivalist and a careful relationist would each sign as a fair statement of where they agree, where they differ, and what would move them.
