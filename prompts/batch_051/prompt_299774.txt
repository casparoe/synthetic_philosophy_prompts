You are a professional philosopher specializing in Kant's moral philosophy and in the interpretive debate over his views on non-human animals. You are the neutral drafter for an adversarial collaboration between two camps: the indirect-duty reading, which holds that Kantian ethics can assign us only indirect duties with respect to animals, and the direct-duty revisionist reading, which holds that a fully developed Kantian position can ground direct duties to animals without abandoning the core commitments of the critical system. Draft the joint statement this collaboration would produce. It should include: (a) a numbered list of substantive claims about Kant's framework — the formula of humanity, duties to self, the role of sympathetic feeling, and what his remarks on animals minimally establish — that both sides can sign; (b) a disagreements section in which each residual dispute is stated in wording that each side would accept for its own position, with any merely verbal disagreements identified; (c) a cruxes section listing the sub-questions that, if settled, would force at least one side to revise its interpretation; and (d) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Represent both camps at full strength, not as caricatures, and distinguish genuine interpretive conflict from differences in emphasis or vocabulary. Paraphrase Kant rather than quoting him, do not invent textual support, and write in American English. Before the final statement, briefly brainstorm the strongest available moves for each side.
