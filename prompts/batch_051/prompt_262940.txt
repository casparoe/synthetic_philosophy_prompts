I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I am preparing a column on AI paternalism: when a general-purpose assistant should refuse or override a user's request. Please construct a thought experiment to my specifications. Keep it ordinary and near-term: a text-based assistant, a competent adult user, no exotic technology and no perfect predictors. The centrepiece should be a matched pair of requests, identical in what is asked and in the non-trivial harm that will predictably follow if the request is granted, but differing in one respect only. In one variant the user is fully informed and acting voluntarily, though imprudently; in the other, the user is materially misinformed or acting under pressure. After presenting the pair, show how a hard paternalist and a soft paternalist would each rule on whether the assistant may refuse or override the request in the two variants: the hard paternalist should be committed to doing so in both, the soft paternalist only in the second. Then state which single feature does the work, say how the pair collapses if that feature is removed, and give the strongest objection a hard paternalist would press against using the pair to defend soft paternalism. The verdicts must be generated by the theories rather than stipulated. Please answer in British English.
