So I just spent the whole walk home after a long argument with a friend about Peirce vs James on truth, and I want someone to poke holes in the side I ended up defending. I was arguing for a roughly Peircean line: a proposition is true just in case there is a belief that any sufficiently persistent, honest community of inquirers would eventually converge on, if it kept investigating forever. Truth is what inquiry is destined to settle on; its not what happens to be useful to believe at the moment, and definitely not just what the current evidence says. My friend kept telling me I was smuggling in a "long run" that will never arrive and a "we" that doesn't exist, and I hate that he landed a few hits.

The hits that stung, roughly in order: first, the lost-fact problem — if a jar of coins gets melted down before anyone counts them, there's an exact number but no investigation can ever recover it, so either my view says there's no truth of the matter or it admits truth outstrips any possible inquiry. Second, the built-in-bias problem — if every possible inquirer shares a hardwired cognitive distortion, inquiry could converge on something false even in the idealized limit. Third, the no-convergence problem — plenty of domains look like they fork or oscillate forever rather than settle. Fourth, the inquiry-changes-the-fact problem — some facts get destroyed by being investigated, so they can't be stable fixed points of inquiry.

I want an honest map of what would count against my view. Which of these are fatal, which are just awkward, and which can the Peircean absorb by qualifying the view — and at what point does the qualifying turn it into something with no content? Is my view even falsifiable, and if it isn't, is that a problem for a claim about the nature of truth, or just what you'd expect? Say what a defender like me should themselves admit counts against it, not only what my friend threw at me. And be specific about what a Jamesian would have to be right about about for me to switch teams: what would the world have to look like, or which case would I have to find intuitively compelling, for the convergence view to be dead rather than just nicked?
