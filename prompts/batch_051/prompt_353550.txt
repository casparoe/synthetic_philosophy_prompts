I'm a PhD student in cognitive science writing a dissertation chapter on computational sentience — roughly, on which computations constitute experience of the sort that matters morally, and which systems can be wronged. My advisor keeps pressing me to defend a specific criterion, but I think the more neglected task is to fix the constraints that any adequate criterion must satisfy, since those are closer to being shared ground. I'd like to impose that discipline on my own chapter, and I'd like you to write the specification I'll work against.

Write me a list of desiderata for a theory of computational sentience: an assignment rule that says which computations are morally significant experiences and which computational systems are subjects. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say why it is a genuine constraint rather than a camp-specific commitment. Please ensure the list covers at least the following. (1) It must keep experiential status distinct from information-processing sophistication, outward behavioral competence, and possession of a self-model. (2) It must specify minimal binding or integration conditions, so that it does not count a large aggregation of independent computations as a single subject. (3) It must take an explicit position on invariance — substrate, time-scaling, duplication, and replay — so that it can rule on slowed or sped-up copies, functionally equivalent non-biological implementations, many identical copies, and a recorded pain state replayed without causal contact with behavior as opposed to a live one poised to guide action. (4) It must assign behavioral evidence a supporting but not decisive role. (5) It must remain continuous with the best evidence about pain-like processing in nonhuman animals and with gradual developmental and evolutionary transitions, rather than drawing an abrupt line at an arbitrary computational capacity. Then mark which desiderata are non-negotiable and which could be relaxed under pressure, and identify the single requirement that, if taken strictly, would exclude the most currently influential approaches.
