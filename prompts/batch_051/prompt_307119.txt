In the course of covering consciousness research, I have often encountered the view that the brain is a finite-state system: it can occupy only finitely many distinguishable total states. If each such state just is a possible experience, then a human mind would seem to be a finite catalogue of experiences. Suppose a person lived long enough to undergo every entry in that catalogue. What would be the right way to describe the rest of their inner life? Would there come a last genuinely new experience, after which every subsequent moment is only repetition—and could the person ever know that the catalogue had run out?
