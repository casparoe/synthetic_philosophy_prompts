Here is my half-formed idea. The standard way of reading results like the incompatibility of calibration with groupwise error-rate parity is as a negative discovery: no risk-scoring system can have both properties at once, except in highly constrained special cases. I suspect this is the wrong emphasis. The same result can be read as a feasibility result. It identifies a region of achievable fairness profiles and a boundary along which gains in one criterion must be paid for by losses in another. A physicist might call this a constraint surface rather than a contradiction. I want the proposal to ask whether that boundary can be characterized in a principled way, and whether the exchange rate between the conflicting fairness criteria is determined by something like the difference in base rates between groups. If that turns out to be right, the impossibility results would become less like dead ends and more like tools for explaining why the conflict is structural, and for comparing what different algorithms are actually trading off.
