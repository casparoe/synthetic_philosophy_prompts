I'm preapring a short statement of my position on religion in public life for a political philosophy reading group, and I want a consistancy check before I have to defend it. I've been working through Rawls on public reason and have ended up with the following commitments:

(a) On constitutional essentials and matters of basic justice, citizens owe each other a moral but non-legal duty of civility: when they advocate laws, they should be ready to offer reasons that people of other religious or secular worldviews could accept, and not rely only on their own religious doctrine.

(b) The duty applies only in the public political forum — roughly, the reasoning of judges, officials, legislators, candidates, and the way citizens vote — and not in the background culture (sermons, newspaper columns, family rows, seminars), where I can argue from doctrine as much as I like.

(c) I also accept the wide view of public political culture: even in the public forum I may introduce my religious reasons, as long as I am genuinely prepared to give a sufficient political reason in due course, and that political reason need not be a translation of the religious one.

(d) But I suspect that for some issues — abortion is the clearest — there is no political reason that does the same justificatory work as the religious reason; forcing me to supply one eventually often amounts to forcing me to argue from a reason that is not my real reason, or to shut up in the public forum about a law I think unjust.

(e) Finally, I think a stable liberal society can be held together by an overlapping consensus: each reasonable worldview can endorse the society's political conception of justice from inside its own resources, so no one has to be insincere or split into a public and a private self.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest single revision would be and what it costs. If I've set the whole thing up in a way that makes the audit incoherent or not worth doing, say that outright rather than playing along. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my five commitments.
