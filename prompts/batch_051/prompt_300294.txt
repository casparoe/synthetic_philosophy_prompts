For the democratic theory course I'm taking, I'm making a handout for my study group—one of those which-position-do-you-actually-hold style things—except about the epistocracy versus democracy debate. The point is to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they already like, so no question should just be "do you think only smart people should vote?" The leaves I want are about five: (a) plain equal-franchise democracy, where every adult citizen gets one equal vote with no test at all; (b) an equal-franchise-but-deliberative view, where the fix for ignorance is better civic education and well-designed public deliberation rather than barring or weighting anyone; (c) restrictive epistocracy, where you have to pass a knowledge test to earn the franchise in the first place; (d) plural voting, where everyone gets at least one vote but better-informed people get extra weight; and (e) an enfranchisement lottery, where everyone above some minimal competence bar is eligible but the actual voters are then selected randomly from that pool.

I'd like eight to ten questions, each a concrete case or claim where these genuinely come apart, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the leaves (or "unclassified" if someone is all over the place). Build it forward from the fault lines rather than backwards from the labels: whether political ignorance is a problem to fix by exclusion, weighting, or information; whether voting is for tracking some right answer or for respecting equal standing; whether the people who design the tests can be trusted not to rig them; whether low-stakes values questions and high-stakes technical questions should have different voting rules. Also flag any pairs of leaves your questions cant really pull apart—I suspect plural voting and restrictive epistocracy mostly differ on whether every person has a right to at least one vote, so tell me if the quiz can't separate them reliably—and if some cell in the logical space is empty, say so and whether its empty because the view is incoherent or just unattractive.
