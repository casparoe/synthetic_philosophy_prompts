I serve on the collections committee of a municipal art museum. We have been offered a bequest of a painting that was attributed for most of the twentieth century to a seventeenth-century Dutch painter and displayed under that attribution; technical analysis in the last decade has shown it to be a twentieth-century forgery, and the attribution has been formally withdrawn. No one can tell it from the real thing by looking: only the laboratory evidence — anachronistic pigments, the chemistry of the craquelure — exposed it. The donor, who bought it in good faith, will give it to us only on two conditions: it must carry a permanent wall label identifying it as a forgery, and at least once each year it must be hung next to one of our two secure autograph works by the same painter so that visitors can compare them directly. The donor is also offering the forger's technical notebooks, which document how the piece was made.

The committee is divided. Some members argue that accepting the bequest, however honestly labeled, amounts to the museum lending its authority to a fake, taking gallery space that could go to authentic works, and turning a room into a lesson about the art market rather than an encounter with art. Others argue that the ensemble — forgery, autograph work, and notebooks — has a pedagogical and philosophical value that originals alone cannot supply: it makes the question of why originals matter, or whether they do, something the public can investigate in the gallery itself.

Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate before next month's meeting, recommending that we accept, reject, or accept with conditions. The memo should give the donor a clear yes, no, or conditional yes; attach any concrete conditions such as display, signage, education, rotation, or deaccession policy; and state explicitly what our decision commits us to about the value difference between an original and a forgery — in particular, whether the original has a kind of value that the forgery lacks, and what that value is. Treat the strongest consideration on the losing side seriously.
