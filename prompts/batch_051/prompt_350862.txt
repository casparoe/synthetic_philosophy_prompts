"Kelly betting says that when you face a sequence of favorable bets, you should size each bet to maximize the expected logarithm of your wealth rather than the expected profit. The reason is that wealth compounds multiplicatively, so arithmetic averages mislead: a fifty-fifty bet that returns five dollars per dollar staked when it wins and nothing when it loses has an arithmetic expectation of 2.5, but a gambler who repeatedly bets too large a fraction will go broke, and the strategy that dominates in the long run is the one with the highest geometric growth rate, which maximizing expected log wealth identifies. This is the core of what rationalists call geometric rationality: a rational agent maximizes expected log wealth rather than expected wealth, because losses hurt more than gains and only logarithmic growth compounds correctly. A full Kelly bettor never goes broke, since the formula always stakes less than the entire bankroll, and in the long run full Kelly dominates any other fixed betting fraction. So if you know the odds, any deviation from full Kelly is simply irrational. The usual caveat is that in practice you don't know your edge, so you should bet below the Kelly fraction; but with exact odds, betting less than full Kelly just leaves money on the table without buying you anything."
