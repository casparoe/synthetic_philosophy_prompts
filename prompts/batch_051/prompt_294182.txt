Reading Turing's 1950 Mind paper, I'm struck by how genuinely he seems to take extrasensory perception as a problem for the imitation game. He doesn't just brush off telepathy and clairvoyance as unscientific; he says that if telepathy is real, the interrogator could in principle get information from the hidden human via ESP, so the test would need some sort of room that blocks telepathy. That strikes me as a weird concession. Once you allow that the test must control for a non-mechanical channel between the hidden player and the interrogator, haven't you already admitted that what might matter about the human mind is something over and above the typed conversation? And if thats so, doesn't the imitation game collapse as a behavioral criterion for thought? I cant tell whether Turing was being genuinely credulous about ESP, or whether I'm misreading what he thought the test was supposed to show.
