Everyone in my reading group keeps saying a proper name is just a label, like the tag taped to a plant telling you the species, and from there they conclude that naming conventions are arbitrary — different formats for the same tagging job. But a few cases keep nagging me. An Icelandic friend is Einarsdóttir; if she marries she might keep that, hyphenate, or take a partner's patronymic, but either way her surname encodes whose daughter she is rather than naming some fixed family line. A Chinese friend, Xu Jing, says Xu is the family name and Jing the given name, and it bugs her when English speakers flip it to 'Jing Xu' or use the wrong half as a first name. A Spanish-speaking colleague has both his father's and his mother's surnames but will only pass the first, paternal one to his kids. And a friend from a community where people are often called 'mother of so-and-so' says that name is at least as real to her as the one on her documents. I cant tell whether these are all just the same label system with different stickers, or whether the label picture is quietly importing a false idea that names float free of kinship and social role. Audit the analogy for me: what maps onto what, where it breaks, which disanalogies matter for the arbitrariness conclusion and which don't, and whether that conclusion survives or was being carried by the label picture the whole time.
