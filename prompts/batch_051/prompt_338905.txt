I've been trying to fix my tennis serve lately — or, more precisely, trying not to "fix" it, because the moment I start monitoring my wrist and my toss the whole thing falls apart. That got me reading about wu-wei and the paradox of trying not to try. Could you write me a sonnet (14 lines) about that? I want the octave to be the speaker working hard at being effortless — self-conscious, coaching himself, checking whether he's being spontaneous — and the volta to be the moment he realizes the checking itself is what's in the way. Then the sestet should show the shift into unforced action, but without pretending the paradox is solved once and for all: the minute you congratulate yourself on being effortless, you loose it again. It should be accurate enough that someone who knows the wu-wei literature would recognize the real paradox and not just a generic "go with the flow" poem. American English, please.
