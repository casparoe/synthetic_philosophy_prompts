Here's a debate I've never seen anyone actually crack. The doomsday argument, in its standard form, says: treat your birth rank among all humans who will ever live as a random draw; conditional on doom soon, a rank around 60 billion is completely ordinary, whereas conditional on trillions more humans it's a fluke, so your position in the sequence shifts the posterior towards doom soon. The main rival reply is that this is only one sampling scheme: your existence is itself evidence favouring hypotheses with vast numbers of observers (the self-indication assumption), which cancels the shift. And each principle can be pushed to a daft conclusion by some thought experiment, so neither side gets to claim the other is the only one biting a bullet. This has run for decades and, as far as I can tell, nobody moves. I want a diagnosis rather than another verdict. Is this a substantive disagreement about priors over doom, a quasi-normative or verbal disagreement about the right reference class or sampling scheme, or are the two sides answering different questions — one about evidential updating, one about indexical probability — under a single heading? If you had to bet on one of these, say which, support it from what each side actually says, and then tell me what would count as progress given that diagnosis: a formal result, a new thought experiment both sides accept, a change of question, or none at all.
