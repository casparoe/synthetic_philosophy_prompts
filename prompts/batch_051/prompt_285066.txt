I have been thinking about the phrase meaningful human control, which comes up often in debates about autonomous weapons. I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, so I prefer to treat it as an engineering problem: how do you keep a human genuinely in the loop without requiring a person to approve every individual shot, and without reducing approval to a rubber stamp?

Here is a concrete proposal I would like analyzed. A military adopts the following rule. An autonomous weapon may fire without a human approving that particular shot only while its onboard control margin is positive. The margin is a number maintained by the targeting system and capped at 100 units. Each autonomous engagement deducts between 1 and 20 units, with the amount set by a novelty estimator that compares the current situation with the distribution of situations in which the system's targeting behavior was previously reviewed and certified by a human. A human operator may add to the margin at any time by reviewing recent engagements: certifying one as correct adds 1 unit; rejecting one adds 3 units and automatically flags it for retraining. If the margin reaches zero, the system must route every prospective engagement to a human for pre-approval or stand down until a human adds margin; the system itself cannot request a top-up.

I want this treated as a mechanism to be analyzed rather than as a moral position to be evaluated. How would it actually behave if implemented? What would it incentivize on the part of commanders, operators, and the designers of the novelty estimator? How would people game the margin or the estimator, and what would that do to the distribution of engagements the system actually takes? Where would meaningful human control be lost in practice, and where would the rule produce the appearance of control without the substance? Finally, could the failure modes be fixed by adjusting parameters such as the deduction formula, the cap, or the review credits, or is a scalar control margin the wrong abstraction altogether?
