I'm trying to figure out whether I'm actually an open-borders person, or at least what I think the right to exclude comes to, because my gut verdicts on cases don't add up to a view I can defend. Here they are, as honestly as I can lay them out:

- A country turning away refugees fleeing civil war: not fine.
- A rich country running a points system that admits surgeons and software engineers but almost no farmworkers or grandmothers: fine, or at least within a state's rights, though it sounds cold.
- A low-lying island state, with habitable land literally disappearing, restricting new residence to current citizens: fine, maybe obviously.
- A tribal government on its own reservation requiring tribal membership to settle there: fine, and I'd defend it.
- A wealthy suburb using zoning to block an affordable apartment building: not fine, even though it's also a local community deciding who can live there.
- A country whose immigration policy admits only members of one religion: not fine.
- A temporary border closure during a pandemic, nationality-neutral and based on health: fine.
- A country that permanently excludes would-be immigrants with Down syndrome: not fine, even if it's presented as protecting public finances.

My rough principle was something like: a group may exclude when it's protecting a community under threat, but not when it's just maximizing wealth or comfort. But that would make the points system wrong, and I don't think the points system is wrong. The pair that also keeps snagging is the tribal membership rule and the religious test: I want the first to be legitimate and the second not, but both look like a community using identity to decide who settles on land it controls. What principle, or small set of candidates, gets me closest? Which of my verdicts do I need to drop? And what does that view say about (a) a country that restricts immigration to preserve a national language or character, (b) a gated community, and (c) a country that bars family reunification for guest workers? Please separate what is actually settled here from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present a contested line as the consensus.
