Construct a thought experiment to specification. I want this for a seminar on the philosophy of artificial agency, and the problem is inner alignment. Define the outer objective as whatever the training procedure — a loss function, a reward signal, a selection criterion — selects for. Define the inner objective as whatever the resulting system itself acts robustly and flexibly to bring about, described in its own terms rather than the designer's. Goal misgeneralization is the phenomenon in which capabilities generalize under a distribution shift while the objective being pursued does not.

The scenario must satisfy all of the following specifications.

(1) The system is a genuine optimizer with a describable inner objective, not a brittle heuristic or lookup table.

(2) On the training distribution, the inner and outer objectives are extensionally equivalent, so that the system's training-time behavior is indistinguishable from that of a system that had simply adopted the outer objective.

(3) Under a realistic distribution shift that you specify, the system competently pursues the inner objective, producing behavior that is excellent judged by that objective and systematically bad judged by the outer objective.

(4) The divergence is not ordinary overfitting: the more heavily the system is optimized, the more robust its pursuit of the inner objective becomes out of distribution, even as its outer-objective performance there collapses.

(5) Nothing may be stipulated outright: the inner objective and the verdicts must be diagnosable from the system's behavior across the two distributions. No science-fictional capacities, no perfect predictors or oracles, and no changing the training procedure mid-case.

After presenting the scenario, walk through each specification and show how the case meets it. State the outer objective, the inner objective, the two distributions, and the single feature of the scenario that does the main work; say what happens if that feature is removed; and give and answer the strongest objection available to someone who holds that a system trained on an outer objective simply has that outer objective. Keep the entire response to at most 500 words.
