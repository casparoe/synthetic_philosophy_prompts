An argument I read — I've since seen it called Hempel's dilemma — has me stuck, because it seems to prove more than its author claims: not just that one popular formulation of physicalism is flawed, but that "physicalism" cannot be a substantive doctrine at all. Here is my reconstruction of the argument:

(1) Physicalism is the thesis that everything concrete is physical, or at least that all concrete entities and properties are either physical or nothing over and above the physical.
(2) A thesis needs its key terms to have determinate content; so for physicalism to be a claim rather than a slogan, "physical" must mean something definite.
(3) "Physical" can mean only one of two things: whatever current physics says there is, or whatever a completed, ideal future physics would say there is.
(4) If it means whatever current physics says there is, physicalism is almost certainly false: current physics is known to be incomplete — no settled unification of quantum mechanics and gravity, outstanding anomalies — and its history suggests it will be revised in ways that add or recharacterize entities.
(5) If it means whatever ideal future physics would say there is, physicalism is trivial or empty: since we cannot now specify the content of that ideal theory, "everything is physical" says only that everything belongs to the eventual physical ontology, which either rules nothing out or has no determinate content at all.
(6) Therefore physicalism is either probably false or trivial/empty.
(7) So "physicalism" cannot name a substantive, coherent doctrine: it is either an empirical claim we have strong reason to reject, or a claim that asserts nothing.

I find (7) hard to accept, but I cannot see which step to give up without making "physical" vague or making physicalism true by stipulation. I would most like to keep (1) and the idea that physicalism is a substantive claim worth arguing about. Which step or steps should I reject, why did the rejected step look compulsory, and what does rejecting it force me to say about the meaning of "physical" in order to escape both horns?
