Before I read yet another account of why fields with fast, unambiguous feedback (programming, some kinds of engineering, certain parts of medicine) pull ahead of fields with slow or noisy feedback (nutritional epidemiology, macroeconomics, most of social psychology), I want to know what an adequate theory of this is even supposed to account for. Write me the desiderata for a theory of how verifiability drives progress: the cases any such story has to get right, including the awkward ones — fields where feedback was fast and clear and the field still spun it's wheels for decades, and fields with basically no direct feedback that nonetheless converged on something later vindicated. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it. Then flag which of these demands are non-negotiable and which are just camp loyalties (e.g. prediction-market people vs. artifact people), whether any pair of them can't be jointly satisfied by a single mechanism, and which one or two requirements do the most work in ruling out the lazy answers. Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases behind them, not on scoring existing theories — I'll do the scorecard myself.
