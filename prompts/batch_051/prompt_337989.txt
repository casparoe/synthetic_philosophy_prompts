Act as the drafter for an adverserial-collaboration document between two positions in the philosophy of classical physics: substantivalism (space is a real thing that exists independently of the matter in it) and relationism (space is nothing over and above the material bodies and their spatial relations). The two camps have been going round in circles for a while, so the piece has to be one they could both actually sign.

Write the document, staying in the classical Newtonian setting. Cover the two standard pressure points in your own words: the bucket experiment (Newton's bucket), where the water's surface ends up concave even when bucket and water are not moving relative to each other, so the concavity isn't tracking that relative motion; and the shift argument, where shifting the whole material world three metres east changes nothing observable but the substantivalist has to call it a different situation. Then go further and have them record, in advance, each side's prediction for the one-bucket-in-an-otherwise-empty-universe case, and say whether they'd both treat that case as probative.

Use these sections with rough lengths:

1. Agreed claims (numbered, about 200 words) — the setting, definitions and observed facts both sides can sign.
2. Point of departure (about 150 words) — the first claim they genuinely split on, with the sentence each side would accept as its own version.
3. Cruxes (about 200 words) — the sub-questions where a settled answer would move at least one side.
4. Tests and predictions (table, about 150 words) — per crux, a case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded.
5. Genuine vs. verbal (about 100 words) — which part, if any, of the remaining dispute is about wording (e.g. the use of 'frame' or 'real') rather than how things are.
