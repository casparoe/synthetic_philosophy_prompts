I am a high school student preparing a philosophy club session on legal proof, and I want a careful ranking of the leading explanations for a familiar puzzle about what is sometimes called naked statistical evidence. Suppose a pedestrian is injured by a bus, and the only evidence against the Blue Bus Company is that it operates 80 percent of the buses on that route at that hour; no witness can identify the bus, and no other evidence ties this company to the accident. Most people, including many judges and legal scholars, think the plaintiff should not win on that basis alone. A parallel example appears in L. Jonathan Cohen's gatecrasher case: 1,000 spectators attend a rodeo, only 499 pay, and no attendant can identify which specific person did not pay; a judgment against a randomly selected spectator on the bare statistical evidence seems wrong. In criminal law, the analogous thought is that a merely statistical case cannot meet proof beyond reasonable doubt. Yet courts do use background frequencies and circumstantial evidence freely. The puzzle is to explain the asymmetry.

Please rank the following six explanations from most to least convincing as an account of the core puzzle. For each, assign a tier (A, B, C, or D), a plausibility score from 0 to 10, and a one-sentence justification. Use these explanations:

1. Naked statistical evidence is not sensitive: the verdict would not track the truth across nearby possible worlds because a small change in the base rates would flip the outcome.
2. Naked statistical evidence can produce high credence but cannot produce a safe or justified belief about this particular defendant, and legal proof requires something closer to knowledge.
3. Naked statistical evidence treats the defendant as a mere instance of a class rather than as an individual whose particular role in the event must be established.
4. The resistance to naked statistical evidence is mostly a psychological quirk, not a principled rule, because otherwise similar statistical evidence is accepted in many legal settings.
5. Legal verdicts are speech acts that publicly license coercion and blame, and bare statistics cannot ground that kind of act because they fail to assert a particular fact about the defendant.
6. Corrective justice explains the resistance: tort law requires a transaction between this defendant and this plaintiff, not merely a probability that one occurred.

Then, after the table, give one paragraph identifying which explanation you think is the best candidate for a consensus account and which you think is most likely overrated, and why.

Finally, provide a concise JSON summary with this exact schema: ranked_explanations (array of objects, each with explanation_number, tier, and plausibility_score), best_consensus_candidate (one number from 1 to 6), most_overrated (one number from 1 to 6). The JSON should summarize your ranking, not add new arguments.
