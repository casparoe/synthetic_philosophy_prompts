Build me a criminal offence (or a municipal bylaw, either is fine) that is void for vagueness under the fair-notice prong but would survive the arbitrary-enforcement prong. Thats meant to be the hard direction, since the two usually travel together: language loose enough that a normal person can't tell whats banned is normally the same language that lets police decide who to nick. I want them pulled apart.

Constraints: a realistic provision with its actual wording, an ordinary enforcement situation (a noisy flat party, an off-lead dog, that sort of thing), and the separation between the two prongs has to come out of the structure of the offence itself, not from assuming a spiteful or incompetent officer. No sci-fi or made-up measurement tech, and the law should be one a real council or legislature might plausibly pass.

After you've given the scenario, do the check: state the conduct the law catches, say what a reasonable person does and doesn't know at the moment they act, and say what the officer knows or can measure at the moment of enforcement. Then tell me which feature of the case is doing the work, what happens if you remove that feature (does it collapse into an ordinary vague statute?), and how a defender of the law would attack the fair-notice verdict.
