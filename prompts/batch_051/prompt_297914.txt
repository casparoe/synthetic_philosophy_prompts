I've been arguing with a friend about ancient atomism, specifically what the solidity and impenetrability of atoms really commit one to. My friend, who is more sympathetic to the ancients than I am, keeps saying that Lucretius already anticipated a combinatorial account of matter: the same basic atoms can produce radically different things just by their arrangement, and he leans hard on the letters-of-the-alphabet analogy. He gave me this passage from De Rerum Natura, Book 1, in William Ellery Leonard's public-domain translation:

"Nay, thou beholdest in our verses here
Elements many, common to many worlds,
Albeit thou must confess each verse, each word
From one another differs both in sense
And ring of sound- so much the elements
Can bring about by change of order alone.
But those which are the primal germs of things
Have power to work more combinations still,
Whence divers things can be produced in turn."

His conclusion is that the ancient atomist picture is basically correct modulo modern physics: what matters for material difference is arrangement, and the atoms themselves are just invariant building blocks. I want a careful audit of this analogy, because it seems to be doing a lot of silent work. What exactly maps onto what: the atom to the letter, the thing to the word, the void to the spaces, the properties of visible bodies to the sense of a verse? What does the analogy import from language—in particular, is there an analogue of syntax, of a discrete inventory, of rules of combination, of semantic properties that exist only at the level of the whole—and which of those are actually present in the atomist ontology? Where does the mapping break, especially around impenetrability, solidity, and the void, which are precisely the features my friend says are essential to atoms? And then the bottom line: does Lucretius's analogy support the conclusion my friend wants, or does it smuggle in more structure than the atomist picture can justify? In your answer, lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
