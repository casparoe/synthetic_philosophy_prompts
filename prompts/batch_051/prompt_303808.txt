I'm writing the final exam for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on Nietzsche's early writings, and I want an essay question that separates students who actually understand the architecture of On the Uses and Disadvantages of History for Life (the second Untimely Meditation, 1874) from those who have merely memorized the labels monumental, antiquarian, and critical. In the opening portion of the essay, Nietzsche makes a claim roughly like this: a surfeit of the historical sense is harmful, and the capacity to forget, which he calls the unhistorical, is as necessary to the health of an individual, a people, and a culture as the capacity to remember. We spent a full class on the image of the animal that immediately forgets, so the students can connect the unhistorical to that image, but I want them to do something harder: use that opening claim to explain the internal logic and the characteristic degeneration of one of the three modes of history.

Please draft an exam question that requires this, then provide a model answer of roughly 700 words and a marking scheme totaling 20 marks. The marking scheme should list five must-hit points, the marks each earns, and a one-line note on how candidates typically get each wrong. I would also like a short note separating an A answer from a B answer.
