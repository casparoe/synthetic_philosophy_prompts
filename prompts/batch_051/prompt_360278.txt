I work on the playback and export side of a video editing product, so I spend an absurd amount of time thinking about latency, cuts, buffering, and what happens when somebody tweaks a timeline after the stream has already gone out. Lately I've been poking at the color phi phenomenon (I think thats the name): a red dot flashes, then a green dot flashes a little later at a nearby spot, and subjects report seeing one dot glide across and turn green partway along the path, which is strange because the green part seems to require the second flash but shows up in the experience as already there before it. The explanation I keep running into is a video-editing analogy. Either the brain edits the memory after the fact, the way someone quietly fixes the archive after the broadcast has aired (the Orwellian story), or it delays the feed a bit and edits the footage before anything reaches consciousness, the way a producer cuts a live feed during a broadcast delay (the Stalinesque story). People then push the analogy one step further: since both stories leave the person with the same final report and the same memories, the Orwellian versus Stalinesque distinction is supposed to be a difference that makes no difference, neither decidable nor worth deciding.

I can't tell whether that conclusion is right or whether its the analogy doing the work for it. In my world editing-before-air and editing-after-air are very different operations with different failure modes and different intermediate states, even when the final file comes out identical, so I'm suspicious of any picture that treats them as the same thing just because the output matches.

Could you audit the analogy for me? Walk me through what corresponds to what in the video pipeline: the footage, the editor, the viewer, the live feed, the delay, the archive. Then tell me where the mapping breaks or misleads, what the analogy quietly imports about which differences are supposed to matter, and whether the conclusion that the two revision stories are a distinction without a difference actually survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If there's a better analogy I'd want to hear that too. Please keep the whole thing continuous prose, like an email: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
