I'm a novelist by trade, and I'm the token novelist on the board of a small historical society. We've received a bequest to award one grant to whichever work most truthfully represents the 1984–85 miners' strike. The front-runner is a manuscript that reconstructs the exact conversations and private thoughts of people on both sides, mostly from inference and imagination, and the author argues that this gets closer to the truth of the strike than the surviving documents, which he calls partial and dead. Half the board says its just a novel wearing a historian's coat and we shouldn't fund it; the other half says all history is interpretation anyway, so why not fund the most vivid account. I've been asked to draft the decision memo — award, reject, or award with conditions — with a clear recommendation, the reasoning for it, and an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side. British English, please.
