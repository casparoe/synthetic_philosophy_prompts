I'm a PhD student in computational neuroscience and I've spent way too much time reading the back-and-forth about the free energy principle over the last few years. The debate between the people who think FEP is a deep unifying principle for all of cognition and life, and the people who think its either unfalsifiable, trivial, or just a convenient modeling framework, seems completley stuck. Everyone knows the moves at this point: the critics say you can put a Markov blanket around basically any coupled system and fit it with a generative model, so the principle rules nothing out; the defenders say that's the point, FEP isn't supposed to be a specific empirical hypothesis, it's a mathematical statement about what a system must be doing if it persists in a far-from-equilibrium state, and the falsifiable content lives in the process theories layered on top, like predictive coding or active inference. Then the critics say those process theories aren't really derived from the FEP anyway, and round and round it goes.

My working theory is that the argument isnt actually about brains or biology at all—it's about what an explanation in cognitive science is supposed to do. One side wants a principle that tells you the form any adaptive system must take; the other wants a hypothesis that could turn out to be false, and on that criterion a principle that applies to everything explains nothing. Is that diagnosis right? Or is there a substantive empirical or formal question hiding under the rhetoric that someone could actually settle? I want you to diagnose the structure of the disagreement itself: are these people answering different questions under one heading, disagreeing about standards of evidence, or is one side just confused about what the other is claiming? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress here—a theorem, a clear counterexample, a sharper distinction between the principle and the process theories, or just abandoning the phrase "the free energy principle" in favor of smaller claims?
