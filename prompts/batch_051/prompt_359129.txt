I'm putting together an in-class exercise for a cognitive science course, and I want a decision tree that sorts people into the position on the modularity of mind they actually hold rather than the label they walked in with. The class read the Fodor-style input-modules picture and some of the massive modularity replies, and the discussion kept producing people who said "I think the mind is modular" while meaning completely different things by it. I want the tree to begin with a single opening question about whether the mind is best thought of as one general-purpose machine or a collection of specialized systems, and then branch from there. Aim for six to eight leaves and no more then about eight branch nodes; I don't want a twenty-node monster. Please build the tree out of the logical space first: take the distinctions below, figure out which combinations of answers are actually coherent, put named positions at the occupied leaves, and briefly diagnose the empty ones (is nobody there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just underexplored?). I want at least these leaves represented: a Fodor-style view where input systems are modular but central cognition isn't, massive modularity where even high-level reasoning is an interaction of many specialized systems, a no-modularity or general-purpose view, and the developmental view where modular structure is something expertise builds rather than something the brain starts with.

The branch nodes should each be a concrete, answerable question, not a request to pick a school of thought. I'd like the distinctions of domain specificity, informational encapsulation (including whether encapsulation is all-or-nothing or graded), mandatory operation, innateness, and the input/central split to show up as nodes, but phrased through cases. Use these cases somewhere in the questions: a visual illusion that persists after you measure it, learning to read, chess expertise, and the difference between recognizing a face and solving a logic problem. End each leaf with a one-or-two-sentence statement of the position plus one claim that position makes about a case from the list. Also flag any pairs of positions the tree can't really separate, and say honestly what further question would be needed to split them.
