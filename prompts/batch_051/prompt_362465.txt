I keep having some version of this argument with my brother, who is one of those people who think 'sphexishness' is just a name for failing to imagine a big enough lookup table. You know the setup: wasp drags a paralyzed cricket to the burrow, goes inside to check, comes back out, and if you nudge the cricket an inch while she's inside, she just re-runs the whole checklist from the top instead of doing anything new. His line is that no amount of apparently flexible behavior — a crow solving a novel puzzle box, a transformer handling held-out tasks it wasn't trained on — buys you a real distinction in the world, because you can always post-hoc redescribe the flexible-looking output as a gigantic pile of context-specific reflexes. So 'sphexish' just marks a fact about the observer, not a joint in the behavior itself.

I don't buy it, but I can't point to the experiment that would make him shut up about it. So design the strongest test you can of whether some target system is 'merely sphexish with more states' versus behavior that deserves a different word — flexible, model-based, agentic, whatever. Pick a specific system and a specific intervention, say what observation counts as a positive result and what would count as a null result, and then be brutally honest about what even a clean positive result leaves open (for instance, wether it says anything about the system rather than just your model of it, and whether it earns words like 'choice' or 'agency'). I want a committed design, not a menu: stand behind one protocol and tell me what you'd bet if it were run. Hard cap 700 words.
