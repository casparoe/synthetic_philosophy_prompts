Some advocates of radical life extension call their critics "deathists"; those critics deny that the label fits anything they actually believe. Please provide a working definition of "deathism" that can settle when the term is rightly applied rather than merely used as a slur. Test your definition against the following cases, and tell me the case on which you are least confident: (1) a bioethicist who argues that a normal human lifespan gives a life its shape and that deliberately doubling it would be hubris; (2) a terminally ill patient who declines further life-prolonging treatment; (3) a public-health official who opposes public funding for longevity research on the ground that it would widen inequality; (4) a transhumanist who calls anyone raising such cautions a deathist; (5) a severely depressed person who says they wish they had never been born; (6) a palliative-care physician who takes relief of suffering, rather than extension of life, as the goal of care; (7) an environmentalist who opposes longevity research because of population pressure; (8) a healthy seventy-year-old who says she has no desire to live past ninety; (9) a theologian who holds that immortality is not something humans should pursue; and (10) a disability-rights advocate who argues that life-extension advocacy implicitly treats aging and disabled bodies as horrors to be escaped. Your definition should distinguish deathism from reasonable caution, personal preference, and psychological illness, and should not collapse into "any opposition to life extension."
