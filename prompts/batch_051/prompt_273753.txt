i keep coming back to the old paradox of tragedy question and id rather see it argued out than summarized. Write me a short dialogue between two philosophers who have both just rewatched the wordless opening montage of Up (Carl and Ellie's whole life in a few minutes), and who disagree about why people deliberately choose art that makes them sad.

Character A is a control/meta-response theorist. On their view nobody actually wants to be sad; what people enjoy is feeling their own emotions run inside a frame where nothing can get them — the way horror fans enjoy fear in a fireproof room. The sadness is real, but the payoff is second-order: sitting with grief while remaining in charge, knowing you can pause, rewind, lower the volume, and walk out when it ends. For A this is why spoilers don't ruin tragedy and why people rewatch the same tearjerker: the controlled exercise of the emotion is the point, not the sadness itself.

Character B is a valuing/rich-experience theorist. They think A has smuggled the paradox's premise into the solution — the assumption that we only seek what gives us some sort of pleasure. On B's view people don't enjoy the montage, exactly; they value the experience, the way you can be glad you visited a dying friend without having enjoyed the visit. The sadness isn't a toll you pay to get at insight; it's part of what makes the work significant. A version of the montage that left you dry-eyed would be a worse work, not a more efficient one, and "I needed a good cry" is a better guide to what's going on than "I enjoyed it."

Make them actually go at each other rather than taking turns. Each should attack the other's favorite case (rewatching, spoilers, the good cry, the fact that we click the video knowing exactly what it does to us), and each should have to concede or amend something under pressure. Dont strawman either side, and don't have them settle by agreeing to disagree — follow the argument, and land it wherever it seems genuinely stronger.

Please start with the dialogue itself — no preamble, no framing line, no commentary after.
