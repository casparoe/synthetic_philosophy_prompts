In my Bentham seminar we're reconstructing a single principled line from his private writings on sexual morality, and the instructor keeps giving us little case-verdicts she says Bentham is committed to. Her list so far: rape is punishable; consensual sodomy between two men in private is not punishable; ordinary fornication between unmarried adults is not punishable; public sex is punishable as a nuisance to everyone forced to see it; and a wife's adultery is a private wrong against the husband but not a criminal offense. The new case is two adult siblings, both consenting, in a private relationship and using contraception so no children are possible. What should the instructor say Bentham's verdict is, if she wants to stay consistent with her own list? Which listed case is the closest analog, what priniciple would she be committing to either way, and is there a further case that would settle which principle she's been running the whole time?
