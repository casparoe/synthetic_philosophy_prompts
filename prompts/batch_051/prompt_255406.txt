A friend and I spent last evening arguing about Hobbes’s distinction between a commonwealth by institution and a commonwealth by acquisition, and we stalled on a case I want to settle. In Leviathan, Hobbes describes the institutional commonwealth as arising when a multitude confers all their power and strength on one person or assembly, through a covenant of every person with every person, so that each authorizes all the sovereign’s acts as his own; the sovereign is not party to the covenant, and the end is peace and common defense. My friend treats this as a list of criteria; I suspect it is an idealized founding story that overstates what actual political communities need.

The criteria we took from that part of Leviathan are these: (1) a multitude of individuals; (2) mutual covenants, each with each, not merely parallel promises to a leader; (3) authorization of a single sovereign’s acts as though each subject had performed them; (4) the sovereign standing outside the covenant; (5) the end of peace and common defense; and (6) once erected, the sovereign holding undivided rights that subjects may not reassume.

Now consider this case. After a pandemic and the collapse of a national currency, a river basin roughly the size of a small country loses its state. Neighborhood councils form for water, food, and defense. Over several years they send delegates to a standing Coordinating Committee, which acquires a paid police force, a tax in labor and fuel, and a court of appeal. The Committee’s rules are enforced; people obey partly from fear of punishment and partly because the lights stay on. The Committee was not party to the original council pacts, and its members are bound by its own rules. Many residents arrived after the first year or were children then. No one ever stood in a general assembly and covenanted with everyone else, yet the inhabitants treat the Committee as their government and its commands as law.

Under the criteria above, is this a Hobbesian commonwealth by institution? Work through the criteria one by one. Where the answer is no, say whether that is because the criteria are doing real work or because Hobbes’s founding-covenant picture is too narrow. Then answer this: does the case reveal an unoccupied place in Hobbes’s dichotomy between institution and acquisition, or would he have a way to absorb it into one of the two? Finally, say whether the criteria themselves need revision in light of the case.
