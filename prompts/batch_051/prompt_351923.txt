Question 4 (20 marks):

Adam Elga, 'Self-locating belief and the Sleeping Beauty problem' (Analysis 60.2, 2000), writes, concerning the move after Beauty learns that the toss outcome is Tails:

"If (upon first awakening) you were to learn that the toss outcome is Tails, that would amount to your learning that you are in either T1 or T2. Since being in T1 is subjectively just like being in T2, and since exactly the same propositions are true whether you are in T1 or T2, even a highly restricted principle of indifference yields that you ought then to have equal credence in each. But your credence that you are in T1, after learning that the toss outcome is Tails, ought to be the same as the conditional credence P(T1| T1 or T2), and likewise for T2. So P(T1| T1 or T2) = P(T2 | T1 or T2 ), and hence P(T1) = P(T2)."

Elga then claims that P(H1 | H1 or T1) = 1/2, where H1, T1, and T2 are the centred possibilities Heads-Monday, Tails-Monday, and Tails-Tuesday. Explain how these two moves are meant to jointly support P(H1) = 1/3, and assess whether the indifference move in the quoted passage is as restricted as Elga claims and whether it actually licenses P(T1) = P(T2).

Student's answer:

Elga is defending the thirder answer. The first stage gives a conditional credence: once Beauty has learnt that the toss landed Tails, she has no reason to favour T1 over T2, because the two wakings are subjectively indistinguishable, so the principle of indifference forces P(T1 | T1 or T2) = P(T2 | T1 or T2), which yields P(T1) = P(T2). The second stage is P(H1 | H1 or T1) = 1/2, because conditional on its being Monday the coin is still fair, so Beauty's credence in Heads is 1/2. Since H1, T1, and T2 are exhaustive on waking, these two stages force P(H1) = P(T1) = P(T2) = 1/3. So Elga is right. The halfer's mistake is to think that self-locating beliefs are about possible worlds; actually they are about centred worlds, so once that is granted the 1/2 answer just disappears.
