I have been working through Locke's account of original acquisition for a paper on whether any version of the labour theory can survive its standard criticisms, and the familiar objections have begun to feel like a closed set. The ones I already have, stated in my own words, are these. (1) The mixing argument cuts the wrong way: pouring what I own into something unowned, like the ocean, looks more like a way of losing my labour than of gaining the thing. (2) Even if labour creates most of an object's value, that seems to entitle the labourer only to the increment of value she added, not to the whole previously unowned thing. (3) The theory gives no principled way to identify the labour that counts, since digging, fencing, gathering fallen fruit, and merely marking a thing as one's own could all be called labour by an interested party. (4) Introducing money as a durable store of value lets people convert perishable surplus into lasting wealth, sidestepping the spoilage proviso and allowing exactly the indefinite accumulation that the proviso was meant to restrict, so the proviso is quietly abandoned while its conclusion is kept. (5) The requirement of leaving enough and as good in common for others cannot be met in a world where the accessible supply of good land is limited, so the theory's own condition fails just when it would matter. (6) Even if some original appropriation were justified, nothing in the theory shows how it comes to bind people born later, who never performed the labour and never consented to being excluded from what was once common.

I set these out so you know which standard moves not to repeat. What I want is a further objection to the labour theory of original acquisition that is not on this list and is not a rewording, special case, or combination of anything on it — one that a competent reader of Locke would have to take seriously rather than dismiss as a quibble. It may come from any direction: the metaphysics of mixing, the theory of justification, the economics of labour, the role of the commons, or elsewhere. After you give the objection, tell me honestly how strong it is, and give the best reply a careful defender of the labour theory could make.
