I've basically ended up with a personal rule for what I let myself believe is safe: I treat any one-off activity below about 1 micromort as 'background noise' and don't think about it, and I only actually start worrying when something is above maybe 100 micromorts in a single go. My friend says this is completely arbitrary, that risk is a sliding scale without natural cut-offs, and that my 1 and 100 are just numbers I picked to make myself feel ok about stuff I already wanted to do, like driving. I get her point, but I also cant check the underlying data for everything I do, and I need some shortcut so I'm not doing a full probability work-up every time I get in a car or decide whether to try something new. So my question is whether a micromort-threshold habit like mine is actually defensible, or whether it's just a bias with a number attached. And if it is defensible, how should I set the thresholds in a principled way rather than just vibes?

There's one more thing bugging me. I'll happily drive because any single trip comes in under my threshold, but over a lifetime those micromorts presumably add up, and it seems like driving might be one of the bigger risks I take overall. Is it rational to treat lots of tiny repeated risks as completely different from one big risk that has the same total number of micromorts?
