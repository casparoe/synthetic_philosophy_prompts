im making a quiz for a reading group on the ethics of procreation, we just did Benatar's asymmetry and I want something that forces people to actually take positions. I want a "which view about creating people do you actually hold?" quiz: eight questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions. The positions that need to show up as results are: total utilitarianism, average utilitarianism, narrow person-affecting (only existing or inevitably existing people matter), wide person-affecting (people who exist if you create them matter too), procreative beneficence, and a Benatar-style anti-natalism built on the asymmetry. Dont let the questions name any of those labels; the whole point is sorting by cases, not self-description.

Please build it from the logical space rather than from the list of names: first the questions and which answer-combos are coherent, then locate the positions in that space, and say for any combo that no named position occupies whether it's empty because it's incoherent, because it's just deeply unattractive, or because nobody's bothered to defend it. Also flag any two positions your questions genuinely can't separate.

The cases I care about, make sure all appear: (1) the couple who could easily conceive a child whose life would be happy but decide not to; (2) the same couple but the child's life would be barely worth living; (3) the basic asymmetry probe: compare not-creating with creating a life that has 10 units of pain and no pleasure, and then compare not-creating with creating a life that has 10 units of pleasure and no pain; (4) waiting three months would mean a different child with lower but still clearly positive expected wellbeing, and the only reason to wait is convenience; (5) a one-shot choice between creating child A, whose life would be good (+10), and child B, whose life would be much better (+50), where you can't create both; (6) the repugnant conclusion choice between 10 billion people at +100 and an astronomically larger number at +1. The audience is a smart reading group that isnt scared of numbers but has no formal philosophy background, so keep jargon in the questions down and save the technical vocabulary for the scoring key.
