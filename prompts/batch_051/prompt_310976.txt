I've been going back over the ring of Gyges stuff in the Republic lately, and I keep thinking it would be interesting to run an actual corporate scandal through Glaucon's challenge rather than just leaving it as a thought experiment. You know the part I mean: Glaucon says people are just only because they can't get away with injustice, and if you gave an ordinary decent person a way to do wrong with no chance of being found out, they'd do it — and anyone who didn't would be thought a fool. No need to recap the argument; I know it.

So take the Volkswagen diesel scandal. The facts as I remember them: in 2015 VW admitted it had installed defeat device software in roughly eleven million diesel cars; the software could detect when the car was being emissions-tested and switch the engine into a cleaner mode, so the cars passed in the lab but on the road they put out nitrogen oxides far over the legal limit. The company payed billions in fines, some executives were charged, and it came out that a number of people inside had known or suspected for years.

What I want is a careful mapping between that story and Glaucon's picture. Who plays Gyges here — individual engineers, managers, or the company as a whole? What actually functions as the ring: the software, the gap between lab tests and real driving, the regulatory setup, or the corporate culture? What would Glaucon's account predict about why the cheating started and why it lasted, and where does that prediction break down — for instance, what do you do with the fact that some engineers raised concerns internally and got ignored, or that plenty of employees presumably never cheated? And what does the episode show about the theory: does it confirm that fear of being caught is the real thing holding people back, or does it show that Glaucon's psychology is too individualistic to explain how wrongdoing becomes institutional? I'm less interested in a moral verdict on VW than in an honest account of which parts of the story the theory lights up, which parts it has to strain to explain, and which parts it is just silent about.
