At a barbecue my brother-in-law defended dragging his kids to church by saying, basically, "what have you got to lose?" When I pushed, he spelled it out as a 2x2: if you believe and God exists you win everything; if you believe and no God exists you lose a few Sunday mornings; if you disbelieve and God exists you lose everything; if you disbelieve and no God exists you gain a few Sunday mornings. He says the disbelieve row never beats the believe row, so its not even an expected-value argument—its dominance. I can think of several objections (other gods with different requirements, a God who rewards honest doubters over people running a religious cost-benefit analysis, the fact that you can't just decide to believe something you don't actually believe), but he keeps treating each one as a small tweak to the matrix rather than a problem with it. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims hiding in "what have you got to lose?" here, and for each reading say whether it's a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or just false, and whether it actually gets him to "so you should go to church"? I want the whole menu of readings with a verdict on each one, not a single picked-out interpretation.
