I've been reading around animal communication on and off, and there's a distinction I keep needing that I can't find a clean name for—at least not one that seems settled. Let me lay out a few cases so it's concrete.

A gazelle spots a lion and does that stotting thing, jumping vertically instead of bolting. The usual story is that it's honestly advertising fitness: the jump costs energy and time, and a weak or sick gazelle couldn't afford to send the same signal. So the honesty comes from the cost of sending it, and the signaler sort of chooses how much to invest.

Compare that with a baboon's estrus swelling, or the bright red of a male cardinal where the red depends on carotenoids in his diet. These seem to be honest for a seperate reason: the signal is just physically tied to the condition it advertises—you can't produce the signal without having the condition, regardless of cost. There's no real strategic choice to invest in it.

Where I get stuck is that these two things blur. A deer's antlers presumably grow partly from nutrition and health, but stags also seem to invest in them as a threat display, and they're expensive to lug around. Is that one kind, the other, or both? And what would you even call the second kind, if the first kind is roughly what people mean by the handicap principle?

What are the nearest named concepts, which of my cases falls squarely on each side, and how should the blurry antler case be classified, if it even can be?
