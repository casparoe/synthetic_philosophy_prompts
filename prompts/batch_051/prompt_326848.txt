A few Sundays ago a friend from my old church and I ended up standing by his truck in the parking lot for almost two hours after service, arguing about whether our church's worship actually follows the regulative principle. I've always described myself as strict about it: in public worship, an element is only legitimate if God has commanded it, and what isn't commanded is forbidden. He kept asking me why I'm fine with a stage full of instruments and a projector with lyrics but firmly against a drama group doing a short skit tied to the sermon. My answer in the moment was that microphones, screens, heating, and the band aren't elements of worship at all; they're circumstances that let us do the commanded things (singing, praying, preaching, the sacraments) in an orderly and fitting way. A skit is a new element that was never commanded, so it can't come in merely because it illustrates the sermon well.

After he left I tried to write out the full set of commitments I actually operate with, and I'm not sure they're as coherent as I'd like to claim. Here they are:

(1) The regulative principle itself: whatever isn't commanded is out in public worship.
(2) We aren't limited to psalms; uninspired hymns and modern worship songs are fine, because the command for a congregation to sing isn't restricted to the book of Psalms.
(3) A full band accompanying congregational singing is fine as a circumstance, but the same band playing a song on its own during the service is out, because at that point it's no longer accompaniying a commanded act.
(4) Skits, mime, and liturgical dance are always out.
(5) Microphones, heating, a projector, sign-language interpretation, a livestream for shut-ins, and picking a translation of Scripture to read are all fine, even though none of them is commanded.
(6) I go back and forth on dimming the lights or setting out candles during singing to create a mood; it feels like a mere aesthetic circumstance, but it also feels more like the skit than like the microphone.

I want an audit, not just a list of possible problems. Which of (1)-(6) genuinely pull against each other, which only seem to, and how would you rank the real tensions by severity? If I made exactly one revision to clean up the most, which commitment would you change, and what would that change cost me?
