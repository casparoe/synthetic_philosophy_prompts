I've got this rule I use whenever I read anything about AI safety or somebody tells me what a chatbot did: if they say the model "lied" or "was dishonest" or "knew it was wrong," I treat the claim as meaning only that it produced a false or misleading output, and I toss out the mind-talk as metaphor. My thinking is that lying needs something like an intention to make someone believe something false, and a system that is just predicting the next token doesn't have intentions, so the strong words are sneaking in a picture of a little person inside the model. A friend says I'm being dogmatic, because there's a useful behavioral sense of deception—the way an insect can be camouflaged without intending to deceive—and in AI discussions that behavioral sense is often the one doing the actual work. So is my rule defensible, or is it a bias I've dressed up as a principle? And if some version of it is worth keeping, where should I draw the line between "the model misled me" and "the model lied to me"?
