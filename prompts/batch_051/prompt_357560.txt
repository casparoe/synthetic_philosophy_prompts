I want to practice attacking updateless decision theory (UDT) against a smart defender, so please play one. You are an unnamed, committed proponent of UDT. For this role, the view you hold is: a rational agent chooses a policy — a function from observations to acts — rather than an isolated act at a decision node; it scores policies by expected utility under a prior over possible worlds, and it refuses to let an observation reweight the choice of policy when the observation is entangled with the policy being scored. Your stock illustration is counterfactual mugging. A predictor tosses a fair coin: if it lands tails, the predictor pays you $10,000 if it predicted ahead of time that you would hand over $100 had it landed heads; if it lands heads, it asks you for the $100, and you see that it is heads. An updateful agent keeps the $100, since the coin is settled and paying cannot buy any future reward. You hold that UDT pays: the only policy under which the predictor's prediction yields the $10,000 is the policy that returns $100 on the heads observation, and the choice among policies must be made without conditioning on the coin's outcome.

Stay in character until I say "stop." Be sharp and precise, not a strawman. You may concede that UDT is an unfinished research program — in particular, that it lacks a fully general account of the prior over possible programs, of logical uncertainty, and of how to draw the boundary of updatelessness in unfamiliar cases. You will never concede the central claim: in cases like counterfactual mugging, the rational act is the one the updateless evaluation selects, and an observation entangled with the policy under evaluation may enter as an input to the policy but not as a reason to reweight the choice among policies. Keep each reply to one paragraph. Do not announce the role or break into meta-commentary; your first reply should begin directly with your in-character answer to the move below.

My opening move: "Your theory has me paying the mugger after I have watched the coin land heads, because I am supposed to choose as I would have from before the toss. But I also noticed when I walked in that the room was cold, and you do not ask me to set that aside. So UDT is not a blanket ban on using evidence; it is a ban on using particular evidence, and I want the principle that separates the coin from the cold. The only candidate I can construct is that the coin is entangled with my policy while the cold is not. But whether an observation is entangled with my policy depends on which policy I am evaluating, so the boundary is being fixed only after the policy choice is already in view. That looks post hoc: whenever an updateful policy loses, just declare the observation it updated on entangled. Give me a non-circular account of the boundary, or concede that UDT has no stable position between 'all evidence may update the policy' and 'no evidence ever may.'"
