Two friends of mine got into it at a party after one of them brought up Dennett's brain-transplant puzzle, the "Where Am I?" scenario where a guy's brain is taken out and kept alive in a vat while his body walks around wired to it by a radio link. I read the essay years ago but not carefuly, so I'm just going on what they said.

(1) Lu said the guy is where his brain is, period. Cut the link and the body drops, but if the man is still around at all, he's in the vat. The body is a biological drone.

(2) Gemma said that's rigged, because "the guy" got identified with the brain before the argument even started. From the inside, he sees through the body's eyes and hears through its ears; if you asked him where he is, he'd point at the body, not the vat. For the subject, here is where the body is.

(3) Lu said the felt sense of "here" is exactly the kind of thing VR can fake, so it can't tell you where someone really is. What matters is where the seeing and deciding happen, and that's the vat.

(4) Gemma said that's a fact about an organ, not an answer to the question. The case is built so that two things that usually travel together, the nervous system and the acting body, come apart; once they've come apart, "where the person is" only has an answer relative to which one you're tracking.

(5) Lu said that can't be right, because if the signal gets relayed through a hub in Nebraska, it would just be wrong to say the guy is in Nebraska; but on Gemma's view you could always pick that tracking scheme and make it come out true.
