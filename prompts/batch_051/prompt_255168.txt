I'm on my city's Public Integrity Board, the body that interprets our local clean-government ordinance, and we've got a complaint to decide at next week's meeting. I don't want to just vote my gut; I want to know whether our own past rulings force an answer or leave it genuinely open. So here are the cases we've already decided. A car dealer gave the mayor a $500 birthday gift; the two are old friends and the dealer had no business before the city at the time—no violation. A developer paid $8,000 for the mayor's airfare to a real-estate conference in Miami while the developer's site-plan application was pending; no explicit promise was ever shown, but we found a violation, 3-2. A union spent $100,000 on independent ads supporting a candidate, with no coordination with the campaign—no violation, since we treat independent spending as outside the ordinance. A lobbyist gave the planning commissioner's spouse a $2,500 spa weekend while the lobbyist's client had a variance pending—violation. A community group paid a councilmember's $600 flight to speak at a flood-control conference, with nothing pending before the city—no violation. A company paid the mayor's sister $10,000 for a "consulting report" nobody ever read, while the company had a contract before the city—violation.

The new complaint is this. A former campaign volunteer who now works as a lobbyist gave the mayor a $1,500 gift certificate to a local restaurant for the mayor's 25th wedding anniversary. The lobbyist has no client with a matter currently pending before the city, but told the mayor, "when something does come up, I hope you'll take my call." The gift was properly disclosed on the lobbyist's public filing. I can see at least three rules we might be implicitly running—something like quid pro quo only, something like "pending matter is what matters," and something like "buying access is enough"—and I'm not sure our precedents actually commited us to any one of them. If we want to be consistent with ourselves, how should we rule, which of our old cases is the closest analog, and if the precedents underdetermine the answer, what principle would we be adopting either way? And what further case, not yet before us, would most cleanly tell the leading candidates apart?
