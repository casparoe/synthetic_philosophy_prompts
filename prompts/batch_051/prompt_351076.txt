Im a software engineer and I keep chewing on this thing about dreams. The question I want to get at is whether a dream is an experience — something it's actually like for you while it's happening during the night — or whether the whole thing is just a log line that gets generated at wake-up and back-dated to look like it happened. From my day job, this is the difference between a process that really was in state X while it was running, and a process that crashes and then writes a postmortem claiming it was in state X all along. The postmortem is the dream report.

Ive seen this framed as a Malcolm vs Dennett standoff. On the Malcolm side, as I understand it, the point is that being asleep already means you're not conscious in the relevant sense, so calling a dream an experience is a kind of category mistake: a dream report can't be like a report of seeing a dog. On the Dennett side, as I get it, the point is less about the concept and more about the mechanism: maybe the brain just loads the memory at the moment you wake, and theres no nightly experience at all. Both make me suspicious of taking dream reports at face value, but for different reasons, and I want to know if evidence can break them apart.

For each possible result, say which side it favors and why. Then do the uncomfortable part: given a clean positive or negative result, how much would actually be settled? Where does the disagreement survive any test because the remaining gap is conceptual rather than empirical?
