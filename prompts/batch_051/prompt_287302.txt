Next week I'm giving a short talk at our lab's research meeting. The thesis I'm defending is that, in reinforcement learning agents, the reward signal is best understood as a primitive valence signal — the system's most basic good/bad register, functionally analogous to pleasure and pain — rather than as an inert training-loop variable; and therefore, at least for agents with rich world models and some rudimentary self-model, strongly and persistently negative reward should be treated under uncertainty as a live candidate for suffering, in roughly the way we treat pain-like behavior in animals. My core moves are that the response that a reward is just a scalar mistakes the signal's implementation for its functional role, and that the burden of proof should be symmetric between animal welfare and RL welfare. The audience is mostly ML engineers who build RL systems and see reward strictly as a training objective, plus two alignment researchers whose focus is specification gaming and wireheading, and one philosopher who works on animal consciousness and the problem of other minds. Prepare my Q&A prep sheet: the six or seven objections I'll actually get from them, ordered by how likely and damaging they are, each phrased the way this audience would put it, with a reply I can deliver in about a minute and a one-sentence note on where that reply is weakest, plus the question I'm hoping nobody asks. Keep the whole sheet under 1,200 words.
