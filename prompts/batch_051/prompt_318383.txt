I am trying to work out a definition of funny that does not simply equate it with whatever makes people laugh, since laughter also accompanies tickling, nervousness, and relief. The standard theories I know — superiority, incongruity, relief, and benign violation — each seem right about some cases and blind to others. Propose a working definition of funny (or argue that no single definition can do the job), drawing on whichever parts of those theories you think survive contact with hard cases, and test it against these seven: a colleague slips on an icy step and lands hard; a pun so formulaic you can see the punchline coming; a comedian's cruel mimicry of an immigrant's accent that brings the house down; a two-year-old solemnly mispronouncing spaghetti as pasketti; being tickled; the liar paradox; and a surgeon's grim joke just before a risky operation. I am fairly confident the first case is funny and the tickling is not, and I have no settled view about the pun, the accent mimicry, or the paradox. Run your definition through all seven, say where it forces you to revise the theories or my verdicts, and tell me which of your final classifications you would least want to defend.
