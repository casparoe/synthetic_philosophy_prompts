I am an economist in the country diagnostics unit of a development finance institution. Every few weeks I review growth narratives that trace a country's income or recent performance to one deep cause — institutions, geography, or culture — and those narratives then shape our lending priorities and the conditions we attach. I need a reusable procedure I can run on any such narrative, in under an hour and without new empirical work, to decide how far I should rely on it in a board memo that senior management must sign. Write the procedure as a sequence of questions I ask in order, with what counts as a sufficient answer to each, a clear stopping point where the narrative is not actionable, and the steps marked where the decision is a judgment call rather than a test. Make sure it handles at least these: (a) an institution is credited as the cause when the institution itself may have been caused by earlier geography or culture; (b) a historical natural experiment — a colonial boundary, a disease environment, a resource shock — is treated as identifying one factor's effect but then silently generalized into an account of why the nation is rich as a whole; (c) culture is invoked as the residual after institutions and geography fail; and (d) the difference between explaining a country's current income level and explaining a recent change. Then run the procedure on two claims: that Botswana's comparative success is due to its institutions, and that Argentina's long decline is due to culture. Add one further example of your own choosing where the procedure would reject a governance-centered narrative that an institution like mine would very much like to act on. Say explicitly what the procedure cannot settle without new causal identification — what it lets me write in the memo and what it does not. End with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing a one-sentence summary of the procedure's scope and limits.
