Ordinary speakers are far more confident in rejecting some uses of a word than in settling others. If someone points at a kettle and calls it a game, most people will say that is simply wrong; if someone asks whether a nearly self-playing idle app is a game, those same people will usually treat it as an open boundary question. Yet they cannot give necessary and sufficient conditions for 'game', and they would mostly accept that uses of the word hang together only through overlapping family resemblances and through the activity in which the word is being used. What, if anything, justifies the asymmetry between the kettle case and the idle-app case once sharp definitions have been given up? Is the kettle case a genuine error in the way that using a variable outside its scope is an error, or is it just a change of use we are very confident the surrounding practice will not absorb? I want the strongest principle you can find for keeping the distinction between a wrong use and a new use, tested against nearby cases such as metaphor, technical jargon, category change, and a word moving from one practice to another, and an honest verdict on whether the asymmetry should be kept or abandoned.
