Here's a set of calls I find myself making about what archaeological stuff means, and I'm trying to see if there's a single principle behind them rather than just vibes.

A dozen unused, polished axe heads buried together in a bog, neatly arranged in a circle: I'd call that a votive deposit, pretty confidently. The same axes found in a shallow pit under a burnt house, jumbled up with charcoal and daub: I'd call that a hidden hoard from a crisis. A child's grave with a single ivory braclet: I don't infer the child was high-status. The bracelet probably says something about the mourners, or about the community's resources, not about the kid's own standing. An adult man's grave with a sword, a shield boss and a horse bit: I infer he was a warrior, or at least that his community wanted to present him as one. Notice I can't say why I'm so reluctant in the bracelet case and so quick in the sword case. A large building with a low bench at one end, clay sealings, and dozens of identical small bowls, but no hearth: I infer this was communal or administrative, not a family home. A pit at the edge of the settlement full of sheep bones, smashed pots and ash: I call it ordinary rubbish, even though I know some pits with similar contents get called ritual deposits.

I keep reaching for a principle like: ritual when things are arranged and intact, practical or domestic when they're broken, mixed and scattered. But that feels far too crude, and I know there are messy ritual deposits and tidy hoards. Is there a better principle that captures all or most of these verdicts, and which ones does it force me to give up? And what would it say about a case I genuinely can't settle: a human skull carefully reburied in the foundation of a house?
