I chair my university's animal research ethics committee. We are deciding whether to revise our species-level oversight rules. Under the current rules, extra review is triggered mainly by legal or taxonomic status: certain mammals receive heightened scrutiny, while all other vertebrates are treated alike. A working group has proposed replacing this with a brain-based trigger: any study using animals whose adult brain weight or cortical neuron count exceeds a specified threshold would require investigators to submit a short "moral status" justification. The working group argues that brain size and neuron number track the capacity for welfare-relevant experience better than an animal's place in a classification scheme does. Opponents answer that the threshold is arbitrary, and that moral status should track sentience itself, which brain metrics only approximate. Draft the committee's decision memo — adopt the proposal, reject it, or adopt a modified version — with a clear recommendation and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side.
