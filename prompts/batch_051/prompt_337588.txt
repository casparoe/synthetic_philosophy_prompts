I have recently agreed to convene a small seminar on the ethics of war, and the group has asked me to begin by putting my own position on the moral equality of combatants into writing so the others can push back on it. Before I circulate anything, I want an audit of whether the view actually hangs together, because colleagues on both sides of the debate have told me it borrows the attractive half of each and dodges the difficult half. Here is the position as it stands. (i) I affirm the equality thesis: combatants on either side of a war hold the same permissions and prohibitions in the field, whatever the justice of their cause — they are permitted to kill opposing combatants and forbidden to intentionally kill non-combatants. (ii) I also believe that an ordinary soldier who fights in a clearly unjust war — an unprovoked invasion of a weaker neighbour, say — does something genuinely wrong simply by participating, even when every shot he fires stays within the rules, and that this wrongness is not merely a violation of professional discipline. (iii) I hold that the laws of armed conflict should remain strictly symmetrical, and that it would be a practical and moral mistake to require front-line soldiers to condition their conduct on a judgement about whether their side is just. (iv) I think contribution to an unjust war effort can make civilians responsible in a way that matters: the civilian official who organises the invasion and the factory manager who arms it may bear more responsibility for the resulting deaths than the eighteen-year-old conscript who fires the shots. (v) Yet I treat the immunity of non-combatants as absolute: no amount of such contribution makes a civilian liable to be intentionally killed. (vi) Finally, for blurred conflicts — insurgencies, occupations, civil wars — I would draw the combatant–non-combatant line formally rather than by moral evaluation: anyone who directly participates in hostilities is a legitimate target, regardless of whether their cause is just.

I would be grateful if you would go through these commitments and tell me which genuinely pull against which, which only appear to, and how severe each tension is once it is stated precisely. Order the tensions from most to least serious, and for each say what the least costly revision would be and what it would cost me.
