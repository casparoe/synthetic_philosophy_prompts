Last year the candidates mostly wrote out the standard examples from memory — Makinson and Kyburg, label them, job done — and the external examiner was not impressed. So I need a 90-minute PhD comp question that makes them construct the paradoxes from scratch and grapple with a structural issue rather than summarise the literature.

The thing I want the question to force them to confront is whether the preface paradox really is just another lottery paradox, or whether it's deeper. In particular: the lottery paradox seems to depend on a fixed acceptance threshold (a Lockean thesis with the cut-off set below the chance of any single ticket losing), so one can dodge it by abandoning the threshold or setting it very high. But the preface paradox can be generated without any threshold at all — the author accepts each individual claim on ordinary evidential grounds, and accepts the preface line as an inductive inference from her own fallibility, and the awkwardness is that a long conjunction of fallible claims can be much less likely than any of its conjuncts. So a top answer should be able to say which assumption each paradox minimally needs, and whether the standard moves against one transfer to the other.

The question shouldn't require candidates to cite articles or give a reading list — they should be marked on the reconstruction and the argument.
