I've spent the last decade building and fitting population and ecosystem models — limit cycles, carrying capacities, regime shifts, stochasticity, all of it. Recently my dad has gotten deep into Spengler and Toynbee as a retirement project and keeps arguing with me that civilizations have a life cycle the way populations do: they rise, overshoot, decay, and either collapse or cycle. I can sort of see it — Ibn Khaldun's claim that a dynasty's cohesion decays after a few generations sounds a lot like a slow state variable with it's own dynamics. But I can't tell how much of that is me pattern-matching and quietly bending their terms to fit my toolbox.

So here's what I want. Take the core claims of the cyclical-history camp — Polybius's cycle of constitutions, Ibn Khaldun's asabiyyah and generational decay, Spengler's organic stages of a culture, Toynbee's challenge-and-response with creative minorities turning into dominant minorities — and translate each one as honestly as you can into the vocabulary of dynamical systems / theoretical ecology. For each, say what carries over cleanly (as an attractor, slow variable, logistic phase, hysteresis loop, whatever), what only fits if you distort it, and what has no expression in that vocabulary at all. Then give me the residue report: do the untranslatable bits point to something real my framework is blind to — meaning, freedom, scale differences — or do they point to the original claims being looser than they look, closer to curve-fitting than to a mechanism? Keep me honest: if asabiyyah becomes a state variable, tell me what the equations would have to specify and what the theory doesn't pin down.
