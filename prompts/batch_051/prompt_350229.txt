I am taking a course on the philosophy of mind, and we have recently discussed the experience machine thought experiment. I would like to consider a variation of it that bears on a theme the course has touched on: the exhaustibility of experience by a finite mind.

Suppose that a human mind can discriminate only finitely many experiential states, and that the designers of the experience machine have compiled a complete finite catalogue of them: every sensory quality, emotional tone, and combination the nervous system can tell apart, including second-order states such as remembering or anticipating a first-order experience. The machine can induce any item in the catalogue, and the catalogue is exhaustive; nothing a human mind can experience is missing from it, and the machine cannot deliver anything beyond it. Now suppose that you plug in for long enough to have undergone every item in the catalogue at least once. From that point on, the machine can offer you only repetitions of items you have already had.

How does this modification alter the standard assessment of the machine? In the original version, the hedonist is committed to plugging in, while the opponent replies that we want actually to act, to be a certain sort of person, and to be in contact with reality, rather than merely to have experiences. Does the finite-catalogue version strengthen or weaken the opponent's case? Does it damage the hedonist's case by showing that a hedonic life is in principle completable and then merely repetitive? Or does it leave both positions intact, because the value of a pleasant experience does not depend on its novelty? More broadly, if a finite mind really could exhaust its own experience in this way, would that show that what we value is not the experiences themselves but something further — novelty, growth, or an open-ended relation to a world that can surprise us — or would that be an illicit inference from an artificial stipulation? Work through what each of the main positions on the original thought experiment would say about this variation, and identify which assumption of the original the variation puts under pressure. Please write your answer in British English.
