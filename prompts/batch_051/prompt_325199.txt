I cover fisheries and aquaculture for a general science outlet, and I've been trying to work out what I actually think about killing fish and crustaceans — mostly farmed stuff, but wild catch and recreational fishing keep sneaking into the question. I've read enough of the sentience literature to know that nociception isn't the same as pain, that the old line about fish not feeling pain has gotten a lot shakier, and that decapod crustaceans sit in a weird grey zone. But none of that adds up to a policy, or even a consistent personal ethic.

So here are my gut calls on a bunch of cases, and I want you to tell me what principal, if any, is behind them.

1. Farmed salmon raised in overcrowded net pens and covered in sea lice: not okay. I've reported on this and it's grim.
2. Wild cod dragged up in a trawl and left to suffocate on ice: not okay, though I find it less viscerally upsetting than the lobster case, which I realize is probably just about how the death looks.
3. A lobster dropped live into boiling water at a restaurant: not okay, and if I'm honest I'd support bans like the ones a few countries already have.
4. Oysters eaten raw: fine. I just can't locate a possible someone in there, nervous-system-wise.
5. Farmed shrimp killed in an ice slurry with no stunning: this is the one I go back and forth on. I suspect it's about as bad as the salmon case if shrimp are sentient, but I keep eating shrimp.
6. Catch-and-release carp fishing: I did it as a kid, and I now think it's probably not okay as a sport, even though the fish usually survives.
7. Trapping invasive crayfish to protect a native stream and leaving them to die in buckets: I feel like this is okay, or at least the least bad option, even though its not done for food or sport.
8. Farmed fish killed by percussive stunning before slaughter: fine. If we're going to eat them, that seems about as good as slaughter gets.

Do these add up to a principle? I'd like the principle to explain why oysters and lobsters come apart, to say whether my shrimp-lobster asymmetry is defensible or just a bias about how a death looks, and to predict what I should think about a case I didn't list — say, farming and killing crickets or black soldier fly larvae at scale for food. I'd also like to know how much the principle depends on the crustacean-sentience science: if the evidence shifted one way, which of my verdicts would change, and which would stay put.

And if no single principle gets all eight, tell me which verdicts I should drop, or which one is doing the most hidden work.
