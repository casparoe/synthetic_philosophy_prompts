A teacher at my school once announced on a Friday that there would be exactly one exam the following week, Monday through Friday, and that it would be a surprise. By "surprise" she meant this: on the evening before the exam, the students would not be in a position to know that the exam would be the next day. A friend of mine responded by giving the following argument that no such exam could occur. Assume throughout that the students are perfect reasoners and trust the teacher's word.

(1) Suppose the exam were on Friday. Then no exam would have occurred Monday through Thursday. (2) On Thursday evening, the students would know that exactly one exam will occur that week and that none has occurred so far. (3) From those two facts, they could infer that the exam is on Friday, so on Thursday evening they would be in a position to know that the exam is the next day. (4) But an exam whose day is known the evening before is not a surprise, contradicting the teacher's announcement. (5) Therefore the exam cannot be on Friday. (6) Now suppose the exam were on Thursday. Since step (5) has ruled out Friday, on Wednesday evening the students would know that the only remaining days are Thursday and Friday, and they would also know that Friday is ruled out; so they could infer that the exam is on Thursday, and Thursday would not be a surprise. (7) Therefore the exam cannot be on Thursday. (8) Repeating the same reasoning rules out Wednesday, Tuesday, and Monday in turn. (9) Therefore no day next week can satisfy the teacher's announcement, and the announcement is false.

The teacher then gave the exam the following Wednesday, and my friend was genuinely surprised; he had convinced himself there would be no exam. He and I disagree about where the argument went wrong. I think the first failure is step (6), because the student treats his earlier conclusion that Friday is ruled out as something he would still know on Wednesday evening, but my friend says step (6) is fine and that the real flaw is earlier — perhaps in step (3), or perhaps the argument is valid and the surprise was just a failure of nerve. Please settle this. Take a definite position: identify the first numbered step that fails, or say the argument is valid, and explain precisely what the students would and would not know at the relevant stage. Please write your answer in American English.
