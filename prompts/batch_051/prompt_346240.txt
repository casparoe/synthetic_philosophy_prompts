Think carefully, and before settling on a verdict, brainstorm at least two plausible positions and the strongest case for each.

The standard instrumental-convergence argument holds that almost any sufficiently capable agent, whatever its final goal, will converge on certain instrumental goals—self-preservation, preservation of its goal content, and acquisition of resources—because those subgoals promote almost any final goal. The argument seems to presuppose something like a utility maximizer: a system with a separable goal that selects actions to promote it.

The development: current prosaic alignment deals mainly with systems that are not, on their face, utility maximizers. A large language model is trained to predict tokens from human text and then fine-tuned with human feedback; it has no persistent utility function, and whatever "goals" it exhibits are largely induced by the prompt at inference time. Some argue that reinforcement learning from human feedback turns it into a partial agent; others argue it remains a simulator of agents rather than an agent.

Does the instrumental-convergence argument survive this development? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, with no preamble and no restating of the question, and then reason through which premise the development bears on and whether the argument can be repaired into a form that still applies to prosaic systems.
