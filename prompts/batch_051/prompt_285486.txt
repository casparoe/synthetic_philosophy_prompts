My roommate and I have been going back and forth about what Adam Smith would make of two modern progressive policies, and I genuinely can't figure out where he'd land. I've been reading The Wealth of Nations this semester, and the real Smith is a lot messier than the guy in the high-school textbook. Yes, he thinks governments trying to fix prices or wages by decree are almost always counterproductive. But he's also genuinely warm about high wages — he ties them to a growing population and a healthy, productive economy — and he's blunt that workers bargain from a much weaker posistion than their employers, who can quietly combine with one another while the law comes down hard on combinations of workmen. He's also withering about landlords, people who collect an income without producing much or risking much. And then there's the awkward part for the free-market reading: he actually endorses a legal ceiling on interest rates, mostly to steer capital away from reckless borrowers, so he plainly isn't a no-exceptions laissez-faire purist.

So here's the prediction problem. Suppose we plunk Smith down in a present-day city council chamber where two proposals are on the agenda: (a) a minimum wage of seventeen dollars an hour, well above the going entry-level wage in the city, and (b) rent control that caps annual increases at three percent and keeps that cap after a tenant moves out. He has to reason from his own toolkit — natural price versus market price, wages settled by bargaining between masters and workmen, rent as something like a monopoly return. What conclusion does he actually reach about each policy, and does he treat them the same way or differently? I'm less interested in the official scholarly verdict, and please don't pad it with citations I can't check, than in watching the actual reasoning: would his sympathy for workers and his suspicion of landlords survive his belief that price controls distort the very thing he cares about, or would his economic scruples win out? Give me maybe six or seven hundred words of your best Smith — not the cartoon libertarian, not a secret social democrat, just him.
