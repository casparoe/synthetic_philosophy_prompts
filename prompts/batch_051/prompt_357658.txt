I'm presenting a proposal to the board of a mid-size shrimp farm, plus one of their investors, arguing that we should start electrically stunning the shrimp before slaughter. It'd add something like two to four percent to operating costs, and my pitch is split between welfare grounds and the fact that a growing segement of European buyers are starting to ask suppliers about it. The board is four people: the operations director, who's been in aquaculture for twenty years and is convinced shrimp "don't feel pain the way we do"; the finance person, who is going to ask what it does to price per kilo; a sustainability lead who mostly cares about mangrove damage and water quality and thinks animal welfare is a luxury topic; and the investor's rep, who won't spend a dollar without seeing evidence of consumer demand. Prepare me for the Q&A: the objections I'll actually get, in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each of these four would actually say them, which ones have real force and which are just gut reactions, what I should concede up front, and the one reply that has to land for this to survive.
