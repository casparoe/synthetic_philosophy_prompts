I just came out of a long argument with a friend about a parallel I keep noticing, and I want a committed verdict on whether it is deep or superficial. Heidegger's analysis of equipment: a hammer in fluent use is ready-to-hand, absorbed into the task and not an object of explicit awareness; when it breaks or goes missing, it becomes obtrusive and shows up as present-at-hand, and in that same movement the background of equipment and the practical activity that had been carrying the user become visible. Predictive-processing theories of mind: the brain continuously generates top-down predictions about sensory input; smooth, absorbed coping is a low-prediction-error regime in which precision assignments keep the model fixed and the agent acts, whereas a surprising rupture forces prediction error up, inhibiting action and driving an update of the generative model, which makes hidden causes and the structure of the situation explicit. The structural claim I want to test is that these are two descriptions of one phenomenon: smooth functioning depends on the absence of explicit representation, and breakdown — of a tool in one case, of predictions in the other — is what makes the world's structure, and the machinery coping with it, manifest.

My friend's objection is that the analogy must be superficial because prediction error is a subpersonal, information-theoretic quantity while Heidegger's breakdown is a first-person phenomenological event. I suspect that is the very question rather than an objection.

Give me the exact mapping: what corresponds to the hammer, to the equipmental background, to the failure, and to the shift into explicit awareness. Say what would have to be true of predictive processing for Heidegger's point about world-disclosure to carry over to it, and what would have to be true of Heidegger's account for the computational story to count as an implementation of it. Then name at least one thing the parallel, if taken seriously, would predict that probably turns out false. Finally, take a definite position: is this a genuine structural resemblance, a loose analogy, or a confusion, and defend it.
