Act as someone who works at the intersection of philosophy of mind and long-term technology forecasting, and please think carefully rather than giving me a snap take.

Imagine a concrete counterfactual. It's 2036, and a company rolls out destructive whole-brain emulation. Here's the deal: a patient's brain gets vitrified, sliced, and scanned at roughly 10 nanometer resolution; the scan gets turned into a running simulation on a neuromorphic computer; and the biological brain is destroyed in the process. So there's never a copy sitting next to the original — there's only the simulation, which wakes up, recognizes its family, passes every behavioral test the clinicians throw at it, and reports one unbroken stream of consciousness leading right up to the procedure. By every external measure it preserves memory, personality, skills, even the person's weird little habits. It costs about as much as a house, and there's a small risk the sim destabilizes in the first year.

Now give me your best reasoned prediction of how the next twenty-odd years play out. In particular:

(a) Do institutions — and ordinary people — treat the emulation as the same person? How much does the fact that the original was destroyed (so it's a transfer, not a copy) matter, versus the fact that it's running on silicon rather than meat?

(b) Walk me through the first fights: the emulation tries to inherit property, stay married, or gets sued for something the biological person did before the scan. What do courts latch onto, and how does that precedent shape everything after?

(c) Who signs up for this and why? What does that selection effect do to the politcs around it?

(d) Where are you most confident in your predictions, and where does everything hinge on facts we just don't have (for instance, whether the simulation is actually conscious, or which theory of personal identity turns out to be doing the work)?

Before you answer, briefly brainstorm the distinct ways one could even define "same person" here — continuity of substrate, continuity of psychology, continuity of a particular causal chain, social convention, something else — and then use those as a menu to organize your predictions. I want visible reasoning and calibrated confidence more than a bottom line.
