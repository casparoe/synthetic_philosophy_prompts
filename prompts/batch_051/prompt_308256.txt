I have been working through the debate between the Mohists and the Confucians about the structure of care, and I keep coming back to Mozi's impartial care (jian'ai) because of its systematic virtues. It gives me one principle that applies to everyone in the same way, a clear decision procedure for cases where my attachments pull me in one direction and someone's need pulls me in another, and an elegant diagnosis of nepotism, factionalism, and nationalism as the same error of letting an arbitrary boundary count as a reason. The Confucian response, that care is naturally graded and properly radiates outward from the family, has started to strike me as question-begging: the graded structure of care is exactly what needs to be justified, so pointing to it as the natural order of moral development does no justificatory work.

What stops me from simply accepting Mozi's view is a conviction I cannot dislodge. I believe, before any theory, that I owe my mother something different in kind, not merely in intensity of feeling or in practical convenience, from what I owe a stranger. If I had to choose between a substantial benefit to her and an equal benefit to a person I have never met, it would not merely be permissible for me to choose her; it would be a failure of a specific duty not to. And the Confucian picture is not merely a rationalization of that conviction. It offers a real account of how the dispositions that eventually reach strangers are formed and sustained in the particular, disproportionate attachments of family life, and it suggests that impartial care demanded without that developmental structure would remain abstract and ineffectual rather than becoming actual.

I am not asking which side should win. I want an analysis of the costs of each way of resolving the conflict. Under what conditions should I treat the conviction about my mother as an intuition to be debunked and given up, and under what conditions should I amend or abandon impartial care instead? Is there a reading on which both can be kept—perhaps one that distinguishes equal consideration at the level of justification from differential outward duties—and what exactly would that reading have to affirm about the ground of my particular obligations? Tell me which resolution you would adopt first, and what argument or consideration would justify reversing it.
