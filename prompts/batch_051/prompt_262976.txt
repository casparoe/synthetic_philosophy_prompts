I have spent nearly a decade managing successful bars for other people, and I am now deciding whether to leave my job to open my own small cocktail bar. My plan is unusually detailed: I have secured a lease on a corner space in a neighborhood that is just beginning to gentrify, I believe I have identified a real gap between the city's expensive craft-cocktail rooms and its ordinary neighborhood pubs, and I have tested parts of the menu as pop-ups that sold out. My financial model shows I could break even at only forty percent of capacity. None of this is fantasy; each element is something I can point to and explain. And yet I keep running into the same discouraging fact: a large share of new bars and restaurants fail within their first few years. When I hold the plan in my head, those statistics feel as though they describe other people's businesses, not this one, because I have concrete reasons this one should succeed that a generic new bar does not have. But I suspect that "my case is different" is exactly what the owners of the failed bars said, too. I cannot tell whether my detailed knowledge is genuinely diagnostic evidence, or just a vivid story that makes a risky bet feel safer. How should I think about whether to trust my detailed picture of this one project over the general track record of similar ventures? What would it actually take for my case-specific knowledge to legitimately outweigh the statistical record, rather than merely feel as though it does?
