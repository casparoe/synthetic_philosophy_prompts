I am a PhD student in machine ethics, and much of my work involves casting historical moral debates into the modelling language I actually use day-to-day: reward functions, discount factors, agent-neutral versus agent-relative objectives, multi-agent equilibria, and inverse reinforcement learning from observed human behaviour. I have been working through the classical dispute between the Mohists and the Confucians over impartial care versus graded love. The Mohist position, as I understand it, is that the ethical agent should care equally for every person's welfare and that partiality toward kin and countrymen is the chief source of social conflict; the Confucian reply, developed most fully in the Mencius, is that care properly begins with the parent–child relation, is felt most strongly there, and is then extended outward by graded degrees, so that a doctrine denying this structure cannot make sense of even ordinary human motivation. When I try to formalise the two sides, the Mohist view becomes an agent-neutral reward over all persons, while the Confucian view becomes an agent-relative reward with something like a kinship-and-acquaintance discount schedule. But I am not sure this formalisation is faithful, and I would like you to press on that question.

Please restate the debate systematically in the vocabulary I have just described, including how you would encode each position in a learning or evaluation framework, and then tell me what does not survive the translation. In particular, are there elements of the Confucian claim that the graded structure is partly constitutive of virtue rather than a mere heuristic for allocating limited attention, or of the Mohist appeal to Heaven's will as the ground of impartiality, that have no counterpart in my vocabulary? Is the residue a defect of my framework, or a sign that the historical disagreement was less sharp than it appears? I am less interested in historical fidelity than in an honest translation that does not smuggle in reinforcement-learning assumptions and call them clarifications.
