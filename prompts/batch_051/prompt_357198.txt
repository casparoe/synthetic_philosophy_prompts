a guy in my reading group keeps saying the demos (the set of people with a right to participate in collective decisions) is basically a club: the people already inside decide who else gets in, and thats not a bug because even a vote about who gets to vote would have to be run by somebody already entitled to run it. he uses this to argue that the boundary problem — who belongs to the demos? — is fake, and that its fine for existing citizens to just set the borders of the demos themselves, the way a book club decides its own membership. I'm suspicious the club picture is doing all the work. audit the analogy: give me the correspondence table (members, the membership committee, joining and quitting, the people outside on the sidewalk, the club constitution, the stakes of a typical decision), then the places it breaks, and tell me which disanalogies actually matter for his conclusion and which are just aesthetic. does his conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or is the analogy carrying it? and if the analogy is doing the work, what would be a better one?
