I'm putting together a list of inference moves archaeologists use to get from objects to beliefs and institutions—not conclusions, but repeatable patterns of reasoning where a type of material evidence licenses a type of social or ideational claim. So far I have:

1. Patterned destruction of valuables left in place (bronze swords bent in half before they were deposited, mirrors smashed and not recycled). The move: if the damage is patterned and the valuable material was left behind, infer ritual killing of the object rather than plunder or casual discard.

2. Workshop refuse with half-finished pieces whose mistakes cluster at the same stage, plus finished pieces too good to have been made by the same hand. The move: infer an institutionalized apprenticeship or training sequence, not just a lone craftsperson.

3. Standardized weights, seals, and tallying systems across multiple sites. The move: infer an enforcing administrative institution, because standards like that dont maintain themselves.

4. The same elite iconography on seals, walls, and textiles, absent from common households. The move: infer a shared legitimating cosmology and its restriction to an elite.

5. Separated refuse, storage, and display contexts in a single building. The move: infer the residents' own categories of clean/unclean, hidden/visible, or dangerous.

Those five feel like they cover the whole genre, which makes me suspicious that the next one I get will just be a re-skin. I want one more that has a genuinely different structure: not a special case or combination of these, and preferably one that targets a belief, an institution, or a social fact none of these can get at. Make it up if you have to. Then tell me whether it's any good as an inference or just unfalsifiable storytelling.
