Before I evaluate any proposal that tries to make coherent extrapolated volition — the idea of taking what an agent would come to want after knowing more, thinking faster, and becoming more fully the person they wish they were — into a usable target for aligning a system with human interests, I want the standard of adequacy fixed first. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of an agent's idealized preferences must meet, with the consideration behind each requirement. At minimum, the list should handle the following pressures: an agent with inconsistent all-things-considered preferences; an agent who would revise a preference after correcting a straightforward factual error but not after a mere change in mood; an agent whose preferences are partly adaptive, as with a satisfied addiction or adaptive preference formation; an agent who currently endorses acquiring a preference they do not yet have; an agent for whom iterated idealization has no fixed point because each stage of reflection produces a self that wants a further change; and the worry that the procedure quietly substitutes the designer's values for the agent's under the description of what the agent would want. For each desideratum, state whether it is non-negotiable or a merely optional commitment of particular approaches, and explain what forces it. Then flag any subset of these desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied without significant qualification, and identify the single requirement that does the most work in excluding naive candidates — current-preference satisfaction, preferences as they would be if fully informed now, and one-shot reflective equilibrium. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring the candidates, and close with a concrete statement of what would count as progress once the list is accepted.
