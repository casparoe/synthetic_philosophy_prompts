Next month my partner begins the final stage of her gradual replacement: over the coming year, each of her remaining cortical columns is to be replaced one at a time by a functionally equivalent prosthesis, which learns to perform its role before the biological tissue is switched off, until no original neural tissue remains. She holds that what matters in survival is psychological continuity and connectedness — memories, values, intentions and character carried forward in overlapping steps — not numerical identity. She regards being told that she will 'still be the same person' as at best irrelevant and at worst false, and she has asked that the toast not rest its reassurance on that idea. I am giving the toast at her continuation party the week the final stage begins. Half the room are her colleagues, who will wince at any empirical overstatement about the brain; the other half are her parents and siblings, who are frightened and want to hear that she is not dying. Please write the toast — roughly five minutes — that reassures the family while saying nothing she would consider false about her survival, and that the scientists present would not find embarrassing. British English throughout, please.
