Here's my opening. You say a report of a miracle should convince only if it would be more miraculous for the report to be false than for the miracle itself to happen. But that looks circular the moment a miracle just means a violation of the uniform experience we have of nature, because the very witnesses I bring are supposed to count against that uniformity, and you have ruled them out in advance. A Bayesian doesn't have to do that. I can start with a prior of one in a billion for a resurrection, then conditionalize on a dozen independent, ordinarily reliable witnesses all reporting it. The chance of that agreement if no resurrection happened is much lower than one in a billion, so my posterior is near one. No circularity, no shoddy probability. So where is the mistake?
