I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I have been reading about Derek Parfit's teletransporter thought experiment and the question of what matters in survival. In my work I write requirements documents, and I want to apply that discipline to this debate. Please produce a specification of what a candidate relation must satisfy in order to count as what matters in survival. By that I mean the relation between me now and some future person such that the future person's experiences should rationally concern me in the way my own future does: I can anticipate them, feel special concern for that person, and reason prudentially about their welfare. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark it as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I want the structure of the problem, not a map of the literature.

The cases I want the specification to handle include the standard teletransporter, which scans and destroys my body and builds a physically identical, psychologically continuous replica on Mars; the branch-line variant, where the machine fails to destroy the original and two people are psychologically continuous with me; and a fission case in which each hemisphere of my brain is transplanted into a different body. Treat the branch-line and fission cases as edge cases the specification must handle rather than set aside. Add any further cases or distinctions you think the specification needs.

I have also noticed that people in the group keep insisting that the relation in question must be all-or-nothing rather than a matter of degree, one-to-one rather than one-to-many, and independent of the cause of psychological continuity. Please rule on each of those: is it a genuine requirement or a camp-specific preference, and which case or consideration decides it?

The part I most want is the conflict analysis. Tell me which requirements are jointly unsatisfiable, and which candidate relations, such as numerical identity, psychological continuity and connectedness, or bodily continuity, fail which requirements. In particular, tell me whether the requirement list itself already forces the conclusion that identity is not what matters, and if so, which single requirement does most of the excluding. Keep the weight on the criteria and the reasoning behind them rather than on scoring the candidates.
