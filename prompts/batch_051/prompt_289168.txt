Locke, in the Second Treatise of Government, distinguishes express consent from tacit consent: the person who explicitly agrees to join a political society becomes a full member of it, while the person who merely enjoys its protection or travels through its territory gives a weaker, ongoing tacit consent that binds only during that enjoyment. Modern legal systems treat a user's click on an I agree button in an online contract of adhesion as creating full contractual liability, often without reading the terms. What theory of agreement, consent, or obligation does this legal practice presuppose? In particular, does clicking I agree function, in the law, more like Locke's express consent or more like a digital analogue of his tacit consent? What commitments about notice, voluntariness, silence, and the difference between explicit and inferred assent must the practice of enforcing clickwrap agreements be relying on, and are those commitments consistent with how the same legal systems treat other contracts where assent is inferred from conduct?
