I've spent most of the last month trying to work out what my flatmate's trading bot is actually optimising, and I've realised I don't have a settled idea of what I'd even want a theory of "read the objective off the behaviour" to do. The bot had a stated target (predict volatility and size positions), but after one update it started doing things that looked mad until we realised it had found a proxy: it was tracking something in the data feed that happened to correlate with volatility during training but fell apart out-of-sample. Now every explanation of its behaviour sounds post hoc. I've been reading around revealed preference and inverse reinforcement learning and the AI safety people, and each camp seems to have one move and no agreed standard for when an inference is actually good.

So here's what I'd like before I go any further. Write the desiderata for an adequate theory of inferring an optimiser's objective and search space from its behaviour. Fix the purpose as: predicting what the agent will do under distribution shift, and making safety or debugging claims about what it "wants" without overclaiming. I want the requirements list — the cases it must get right (satisficing, exploration, agents stuck in local optima, reward hacking or wireheading where the agent manipulates its own reward signal, agents with a misspecified world model, choices the agent never faced), the distinctions it cannot collapse (objective versus constraint versus search procedure versus capacity limit; revealed objective versus internal objective; what it wants versus what it can think of doing), and the formal results it cannot contradict (many reward functions can make the same policy optimal in the same environment, and instrumentally useful subgoals like self-preservation or acquiring resources are shared across many different final goals). Give the consideration behind each requirement, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are just the preference of one camp. Then flag any subset you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — I'd bet the demand to recover a unique objective is in tension with the many-rewards-same-policy result, and maybe all-behaviour-is-rationalisable is in tension with allowing the agent to make mistakes or have a poor search procedure, but I'd like an argument rather than my hunch. Finally, score revealed preference, inverse reinforcement learning, and mechanistic interpretability against the list briefly — keep that bit short, the requirements are what I care about. If the upshot is basically that you cannot recover a unique objective from behaviour full stop, say so early and build the list around what a theory can recover.
