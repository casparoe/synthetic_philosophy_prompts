ok this has been bugging me. i flip a fair coin ten times and write the result down. if i get H T H H T T H T T H i'll happily call that random. but if i get H H H H H H H H H H i'll say that's not random, even tho both exact sequences are equally likely (one in 1024). so i can't just be tracking probability. one move is to say randomness is a property of the process, the tossing, and not of the string itself, and any string can come out of a random process. that seems right to me, but then why do we keep judging the strings themselves as random-looking or not, and why does that judgment feel like it's tracking something real? i know theres also a related idea where you measure how hard a string is to describe, something like the length of the shortest program that prints it, and you call it random if it can't be compressed much. but that makes randomness a matter of degree and the cutoff seems arbitrary. and ive seen people go all the way and say no finite sequence is random because any finite sequence could be the output of some deterministic rule, which sounds like it makes the word 'random' useless for anything we ever actually observe. so what i want is an explanation of whether a finite sequence can be random in any strict sense, or whether we should give up on that and say 'random' only applies strictly to infinite sequences, while for finite ones we just have graded judgments about how patternless or incompressible they are. use american english.
