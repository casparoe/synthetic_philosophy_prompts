The canonical multiple realizability argument for functionalism ran roughly as follows. A mental state such as pain cannot be numerically identical to any particular neurochemical state, because creatures with quite different nervous systems — an octopus, a being from another planet, a hypothetical machine — could be in the same mental state while sharing none of the same physical states. The natural conclusion is that pain is a functional state: what makes a state pain is the causal role it plays, paradigmatically being produced by tissue damage or signals of injury and tending to produce avoidance, attention, and distress. In its canonical form the argument leaned on a computing analogy: a functional role is something we can specify cleanly, the way a program is specified apart from the hardware that runs it, and the hardware matters only because it realizes the program.

Now consider a modern deep-learning system — a large language model or a deep reinforcement-learning agent trained end to end by gradient descent rather than built from a designer's decomposition of the task. Its capacities were not assigned as discrete functional states, and its internal states are distributed patterns of weights and activations. There is no obvious point at which the network is literally in a determinate belief state or desire state, even though its behavior is usefully described in those terms from the outside. The competences appear continuous, distributed, and in many cases inscrutably mixed together, rather than decomposable into distinct roles fixed in advance.

Does the multiple realizability argument for functionalism survive this development? Which premise is really under pressure: the claim that one and the same mental state can have many physical realizers, or the prior claim that there is a determinate functional level of description to be multiply realized at all? Is there a repaired version of the argument that still earns its keep, or should the development push us away from functionalism rather than toward it?
