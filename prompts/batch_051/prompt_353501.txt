I have been reading about intuitionistic logic, and I keep encountering the claim that it rejects the law of excluded middle and, with it, the method of proof by contradiction. I am struggling to see how mathematics could survive under that restriction. Consider the standard proof that the square root of two is irrational: one assumes that it is rational, derives a contradiction, and concludes that it is irrational. That looks like a textbook proof by contradiction, invoked precisely to establish a negative result. If intuitionists really disallow proofs by contradiction, how do they prove that the square root of two is irrational? Must constructivists simply forgo familiar negative theorems of this sort, or do they have to settle for some weakened surrogate for them?
