After a career in behavioral ecology, I have recently been working through the philosophical literature on free will, and I keep circling back to a single vignette: a digger wasp of the genus Sphex stings a cricket, drags it to the mouth of her burrow, leaves it on the threshold while she goes inside to inspect, and only then pulls it in. If an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches while she is inside, she does not simply take it in when she comes out; she drags it back to the threshold and goes in to inspect again. Repeat the interference, and she repeats the loop without end. The wasp has become the standard example of mechanical rigidity, what is called sphexish behavior, and the question it raises is not whether the wasp is determined but what exactly she lacks that a flexible, self-governing agent has.

I have collected the following candidate answers, and I am not asking for a restatement of any of them:

(1) A shared workspace for goal states. Her routines seem to be separately triggered modules, and the fact that the burrow has already been inspected is never written into a representation that the retrieval routine consults. A flexible agent updates every active sub-goal when one of them is satisfied.

(2) Metacognitive monitoring. She cannot represent her own sequence of steps and compare the current step with a record of what she has just accomplished, so she cannot detect that she has entered a loop and step back from it.

(3) Forward simulation. She does not preview alternative continuations, such as carrying the cricket straight in once the inspection is complete, and choose among them by predicted outcome; she runs a chain of local triggers.

(4) Inhibitory control. She cannot suppress the prepotent inspect-before-retrieving routine, tolerate its non-completion, and select a lower-priority routine, the way an executive control system overrides an automatic response.

(5) Responsiveness to reasons. Her movements are elicited by stimuli rather than guided by considerations that would justify one continuation over another; a flexible agent is moved by features of the situation that count in favor of acting a certain way.

(6) A contra-causal power to do otherwise. On this view the real difference is modal: given exactly her prior state and the incoming stimuli, no other continuation was possible, whereas a free agent has a power of alternative choice that is not fixed by antecedent conditions.

I am now looking for a seventh candidate that is not a rewording, special case, or combination of any of (1) through (6). It should identify a distinct capacity whose absence in the wasp would help explain the rigidity, and a defender of genuine flexibility should be able to advance it as a serious proposal. State the candidate precisely; explain what it adds that the six items above do not already capture; and then give an honest assessment of whether it survives the standard objection, namely, that it merely relocates the difference between rigidity and flexibility one explanatory level deeper rather than accounting for it.

Please answer in American English.
