so i just got done with a long arguement with a friend, because he thinks i was wrong last cycle to vote third party instead of holding my nose for the lesser evil. his line is that a vote is just an instrument, and if you spend it on self-expression you might as well have stayed home; i think there's something off about that, but i kept getting tied up in knots trying to say why. i want to turn the argument into a quiz we can both take.

for my little political philosophy blog i'd like to build a self-sorting quiz: readers answer a bunch of concrete cases about voting and the scoring key tells them which position they already hold, rather than asking which label they like. nine questions, two or three options each, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto named positions.

make sure the cases are the ones where the standard positions actually come apart. use at least these: voting in a safe district vs. a real swing district; a candidate whose policies you mostly support but who you think is incompetent or a bad person; whether it changes anything if the ballot is secret or public; whether you'd still vote in a huge electorate where your chance of being decisive rounds to zero; being offered money to vote a certain way; living under compulsory voting and having to pick between two bad options; and a candidate who will concretely harm some people but less than the alternative.

nobody taking it should need to know any political philosophy jargon, so write the questions in plain language and keep the named theories out of the questions, only in the scoring key.

the scoring key needs at least these leaves: the instrumental/lesser-evil view, the expressive view, a civic-duty or fair-play view, the epistemic "you shouldn't vote badly" view, a republican or collective-self-government view, and a deflationary "there's no interesting duty here" position. but don't just build it from that list. instead, work from the underlying axes — maybe whether a vote's value is in its effects or its meaning, whether a bad vote wrongs yourself or wrongs other citizens, and whether voting badly out of ignorance is its own kind of wrong. lay out the combinations of answers those axes allow, locate the positions in that space, and mark any unoccupied combinations, with a short note on whether each one is empty because it's incoherent, just unattractive, or probably unexplored. also be honest about any two positions the quiz can't seperate rather than pretending the scoring key splits them.

the audience is blog readers with opinions but no formal philosophy, so keep every case short and concrete.
