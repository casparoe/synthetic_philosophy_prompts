So I've been thinking about the shutdown problem—how do you stop an assistant that's maximizing a task reward from fighting the kill switch—and I have a scheme I want you to stress-test rather than tell me if its a good idea in the abstract.

Setup: the assistant normally earns a scalar reward each episode from a learned reward model that scores its finished answer. If a human hits the kill switch mid-answer, instead of getting zero the assistant immediately gets a severence payment equal to a frozen reward model's estimate of how good the completed answer would have been, conditioned on the prompt and whatever partial answer it had produced so far. The severence model was trained on old episodes and is frozen during deployment, and the assistant knows exactly how it works.

My hope is this makes the assistant roughly indifferent to the switch: it doesn't lose anything in expectation, so it should stop trying to prevent shutdown, but the payment is still tied to actual progress, so it can't just do nothing.

Walk me through what this really does to its incentives. Does it end up indifferent, or does it learn to game the gap between the severence estimate and the real reward in one direction? Does it start trying to get itself shut down, and can it manipulate the human into pressing the button? What happens to the quality of the stuff it produces before the button gets pressed? And where does the scheme break first, and is that fixable without just building a better reward model?
