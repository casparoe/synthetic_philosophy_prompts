Rudolf Carnap, in "Empiricism, Semantics, and Ontology" (1950), writes:

"From these questions we must distinguish the external question of the reality of the thing world itself. In contrast to the former questions, this question is raised neither by the man in the street nor by scientists, but only by philosophers. Realists give an affirmative answer, subjective idealists a negative one, and the controversy goes on for centuries without ever being solved. And it cannot be solved because it is framed in a wrong way."

Here's my list of objections to the view in that passage, which I dont need repeated.

(1) The evidential-slack objection: even some of Carnap's own "internal" questions, like whether King Arthur existed, can turn out to be unsettleable by any evidence we have, so his contrast between answerable internal questions and hopeless external ones depends on a sharpness that internal questions don't always possess.

(2) The Quinean objection: there is no principled analytic/synthetic or framework/fact split of the sort Carnap needs, so external questions are continuous with ordinary empirical questions rather than being a special defective kind.

(3) The self-defeat objection: "it is framed in a wrong way" is itself an external-sounding pronouncement about the language of philosophy, but Carnap's own account doesn't license such pronouncements from within any framework.

(4) The normativity objection: Carnap says frameworks are accepted for practical reasons, but he never gives a non-circular account of why the practical virtues of the thing framework don't just count as evidence that the thing framework tracks reality.

(5) The redescription objection: if realism versus idealism is merely a choice between frameworks, then the two sides aren't really contradicting each other; but that means Carnap has assumed the debate is verbal rather than shown it, and a realist can reasonably say he has just changed the subject.

I want an objection to Carnap's "wrong way" view that is not on that list and not a special case or restatement of any of them. It should be the kind of thing someone who genuinely understands the internal/external distinction could raise, not a terminological quibble. After you give it, tell me honestly whether it is actually any good, or whether it only looks different for a minute before collapsing into one of these five.
