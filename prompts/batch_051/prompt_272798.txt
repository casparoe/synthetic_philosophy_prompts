I'm writing up my views for a philosophy of mind seminar and want a consistency check before I present. I'm basically an epiphenomenalist about qualia. William James, in "Are We Automata?", describes the conscious-automaton view as saying: "Feeling is a mere collateral product of our nervous processes, unable to react upon them any more than a shadow reacts on the steps of the traveller whom it accompanies. Inert, uninfluential, a simple passenger in the voyage of life, it is allowed to remain on board, but not to touch the helm or handle the rigging." I endorse that picture for phenomenal consciousness. Here's the package I'm holding: (1) qualia are causally inert; (2) my introspective judgments about my own qualia are generally reliable; (3) a reliable judgment that I'm in pain would be impossible if the pain itself never caused the judgment; (4) I know I'm in pain when I'm in pain. Are these in tension, and if so which tension is worst and what's the least costly revision? I want a ranked audit, not a lecture.
