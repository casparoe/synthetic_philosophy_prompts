Write the joint statement that a committed mathematical platonist and a committed mathematical nominalist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The platonist is roughly Quinean about indispensability but needn't accept every piece of Quine's package; the nominalist thinks there are no abstract objects and treats number talk as a useful fiction or paraphrase.

Don't give me a list of bland commonplaces. I want a real document: first a numbered agreed-claims section, worded so both sides can genuinely put their names to it (about mathematical practice, proof, truth, what the disagreement is not about, etc.); then a seperate disagreements table where each side's position is stated exactly, in wording that side would accept; then a cruxes section.

I suspect the cruxes include something like: (1) whether the indispensability of mathematics in empirical science is best explained by the truth of the mathematics or by its mere usefulness; (2) whether a nominalist paraphrase can cover all the mathematics used in science without changing the subject or smuggling in abstract objects; (3) whether the platonist's epistemic access problem (how do we reliably know about causally inert objects?) is a genuine problem or an artefact of a bad causal theory of knowledge. Revise, add, or drop cruxes as you see fit, but keep them sharp.

For each crux, give a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about it. Work the North American periodical cicadas in somewhere: broods emerge every 13 or 17 years, and the primeness of those periods is often claimed to explain why they so rarely overlap with predator or competitor cycles, and the crux is whether that explanation has to quantify over numbers. If you think the case is irrelevant or question-begging, say so and replace it with a better one.

Also say explicitly which residual disagreements you judge to be merely verbal rather than substantive, if any. And write the whole thing in British English (analyse, not analyze, and so on).
