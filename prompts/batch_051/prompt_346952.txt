Here's a thing I can't get straight. Almost everyone thinks a person has a fundamental right to leave there country — if a state stops its citizens from emigrating, we call that tyranny, and were right. But almost no one thinks a person has a right to enter any country she likes; we treat it as completely normal that a state gets to decide who comes in, and that turning someone away at the border is just the state exercising its sovereignty. So exit gets treated as a human right and entry as a privilege. Is there an actual principled difference between the two, or is it just that one judgment flatters us and the other costs us? Give me the strongest principle you can find that generates both halves, then test it against the obvious nearby case: if every state may exclude you, isn't your 'right to leave' hollow — you can leave, but only into the sea? I want an honest verdict: keep the asymmetry, reverse it, or drop one of the halves.
