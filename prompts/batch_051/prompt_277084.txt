I have spent a long time training in a traditional martial art, and for the past two years I have been applying its model of practice to my own reasoning. In the dojo, a technique becomes available under pressure only after it has been drilled as a kata thousands of times, and the form is supposed to surface on its own when a live situation calls for it. So I have built myself a set of rationality katas. Most days I do Fermi estimates while commuting, write out expected-value tables for purchases or commitments above a certain size, run through a fixed checklist of cognitive biases before changing my mind about anything that matters, and rehearse the structure of a sound argument before difficult conversations. My fluency in these drills has improved a great deal, and I take that as evidence that the underlying abilities are being built; I now believe transfer into ordinary judgment is mostly a matter of further repetition. I am aware that this is exactly the usual criticism of physical katas — my instructor used to say you can polish a form forever without being able to use it against a resisting opponent — but I have assumed reasoning is different, because the drills themselves are mental and should therefore carry over to other mental activity more directly. On that assumption, which of my rationality katas should I prioritize to get the greatest transfer into everyday decisions, and how would I recognize that one has actually been internalized rather than merely performed well? I would like a committed answer: please take a definite position on what I should do and why, rather than laying out the options for me to choose from.
