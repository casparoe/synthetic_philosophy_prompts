I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, so I'm drawn to positions that minimize primitives and give a single decision rule. Recently I've found myself attracted to hedonism about well-being: the view that pleasure is the only thing good in itself, pain the only thing bad in itself, and that everything else matters only through its contribution to one or the other. What I like about it is that it feels like a clean API — one value type, one operation for evaluating any situation, and every hard case becomes a measurement problem rather than a conceptual one. No separate columns for achievement, authenticity, relationship, or meaning with incommensurable exchange rates.

The problem is an objection I can't stop pressing on myself — what I've since learned people call the heterogeneity problem for hedonism. Pleasure doesn't seem to name a single thing. The relief when an on-call incident finally resolves, the satisfaction of getting a failing test suite green, the physical pleasure of a cold drink when I'm overheated, and the absorbed enjoyment of three hours in flow on a hard problem don't feel like the same internal state varying only in intensity and duration. They have different textures, different relationships to wanting and attention, different typical durations and afterlives. When I ask what exactly the theory wants me to sum, I can't point to the common ingredient. If the primitive is that fugitive, hedonism starts to look less like a clean design and more like a function whose return type was never specified.

So I'm stuck between a theory with systematic virtues I still find compelling and an introspective intuition that pleasures are a mixed bag with no common felt core. I don't want a verdict handed down without the alternatives being taken seriously. Present each side at its strongest. On one side: the case that hedonism survives heterogeneity, whether by identifying pleasure with a common functional or neurocomputational role rather than a common felt quality, or by treating felt hedonic tone as a single dimension that can underlie superficially different experiences. On the other: the case that the heterogeneity intuition is a genuine refutation of the single-primitive picture, and that it forces either a richer theory of well-being or a much less ambitious hedonism. Then give me a negotiated verdict: which should yield, under what conditions, and at what cost? Should I amend the theory, abandon its single-primitive version, reinterpret the intuition so it no longer conflicts, or treat the intuition as a confusion about what a concept has to be? Be explicit about what would justify each move and what each one would give up.
