I've been trying to pin down what wisdom is, as opposed to just being smart or knowing a lot of stuff, and a weird comparison keeps turning up that I cant shake. A wise person seems to be doing something a lot like what regularization does in machine learning. An overfit model memorizes the quirks of its training data and then falls apart on anything new; a person who has a rule for every situation, or who learned lessons that only made sense in the very specific world they grew up in, looks like the same failure mode. Wisdom often makes the person worse at the local details — the wise old person gives advice that ignores some specifics of your exact situation but somehow generlizes to all the situations you haven't lived yet. Life experience is the training data, proverbs and principles are the priors, and the bias-variance tradeoff is the difference between cleverness and wisdom. But I'm aware this might just be me seeing the one metaphor I actually know in everything.

I want to know whether the mapping is deep or superficial. Where does it line up and where does it snap? If it were a real structural identity, we'd expect some specific things: that in a perfectly stable, unchanging environment, the "overfit" clever person beats the wise one; that more life experience doesn't add much once you've seen enough variety; that wisdom can't be transferred because you'd need your own training set. Do those predictions actually hold, or are they where the analogy quietly dies? And is there something about wisdom — caring about what matters, not just predicting outcomes — that the ML picture leaves out entirely, or is that just another regularization term?
