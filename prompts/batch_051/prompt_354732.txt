I'm the secretary of a small residents' committee that looks after a flood barrier on the river at the edge of our village. Every household behind the barrier is asked to pay £40 a year into a maintenance fund, and almost everyone does. One neighbour, Dave, stopped paying a few years back. He's not sneaky about it. He says the barrier will be maintained whether or not he pays, because if the fund falls short by forty quid the committee will find the money elsewhere or the council will top it up. So his payment never actually changes the chance that anyone's house floods. He sends the £40 to a malaria charity instead, on the grounds that it does more good there than it ever would in our maintenance account. He still lives behind the barrier, benefits from it, and will happily say so if you ask. One neighbour says if everyone followed Dave's logic the fund would collapse, but Dave replies that he's not everyone, and him not paying hasn't stopped anyone else from paying.

The committee is split. Some want to stop inviting him to the village fete until he pays up; they think he's a sponger. Others say he's actually being more moral than the rest of us, since he's thought harder about where forty quid does the most good and nobody is worse off for his choice. I keep going back and forth. Is Dave doing something wrong, and if he is, what is it exactly? If he isn't, is there still anything wrong with the rest of us putting social pressure on him? I'd like something more thought-out to say at the next meeting.
