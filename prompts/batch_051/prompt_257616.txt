hey — I'm a backend engineer at an AI company (mostly inference serving, never took a philosophy class) and I somehow ended up on our model release review group. We meet Thursday and I'm supposed to bring a draft decision memo, but I'm going in circles. The case: our team just finished a general-purpose 70B model — call it Turing-70B — that isn't available from anyone else yet. Our red team found that with a little fine-tuning on public phishing data it writes personalized spear-phishing lures that, in a small internal A/B test, got a click rate about 40% higher than the human-written ones. The decision is whether to release the weights openly under a permissive open-source license with no restrictions, or keep it behind a gated API plus a structured-access program for researchers, at least for now.

The group is split down the middle. The release side says: if we don't release, some other lab or hobbyist collective gets there within a year anyway, and all we'd have done is keep the capability in the hands of people who won't study it responsibly or build defenses. The other side — one member keeps calling this the unilateralist's curse, which I had to look up — says that's exactly the trap: when a bunch of actors can each unilaterally put a capability out into the world, and each one decides "my estimate says it's net-positive," the probability that at least one of them pushes the button approaches 1, even if the median informed view would have said hold off. So us releasing because "someone else will anyway" isn't a defense; it's laundering the judgment of the most careless actor into our own decision.

Write the group's decision memo: recommend release, no release, or release with conditions, with an actual bottom line the company could act on. Make sure the loosing side's best argument is on the page, not a strawman. Treat yourself as someone who works on risk governance and collective-action problems, think carefully, and before you commit, brainstorm at least three distinct ways this decision could go wrong, then show how your recommendation handles them. Keep it under about 900 words.
