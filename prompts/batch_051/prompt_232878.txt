I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am preparing a podcast interview with a historian of the French Revolution. My guest is an invented stand-in for the revisionist school: she holds that the Revolution is best explained by the internal logic of revolutionary ideology rather than by class conflict, and that the drift toward the Terror was made likely, perhaps overdetermined, by a theory of sovereignty that identified the nation with a single general will and could not, on its own terms, recognize legitimate opposition. On her account, the Terror was not primarily a defensive response to invasion, civil war, and economic crisis, but the working out of a demand for unanimity that was present from the start.

Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond asking her to restate this thesis. I want at least one question, with a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give, on each of the following pressure points:

- whether her account can explain the Vendée and the federalist revolts without treating every form of resistance as counter-revolutionary by definition;
- the place of the sans-culottes and the enragés, the popular radicals to the left of the Jacobins, whose grievances were material as much as ideological;
- what it means to say that revolutions devour their children, and whether that is a mechanism internal to revolutionary ideology or only a retrospective metaphor;
- the counterfactual about war: whether she is committed to saying the Terror would have happened even without the war that began in 1792;
- why, if revolutionary ideology made the Terror inevitable, the Revolution moved away from it after Thermidor, and what sort of evidence would show that her explanatory priority is mistaken.

In addition, include one question that tests whether her account of 1789 transfers to 1848 or to the Paris Commune of 1871, and one question that presses her on the difference between explaining the actors' self-understanding and endorsing it as a cause.

Each follow-up should engage the answer the guest is most likely to give rather than merely restate the original question; where the likely reply is a known move in this debate, such as treating civil war as evidence that revolutionary ideology had already won a decisive symbolic victory, the follow-up should ask what would falsify that move.

Please write the interview questions in British English.
