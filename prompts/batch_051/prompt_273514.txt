I'm a journalist—science and ideas, mostly longform—and I've never really studied philosophy, but Ive internalized the rules of my trade so thoroughly they double as a metaphysics. Everything is a source; a source has a status; a document might be forged; a byline might outlast the reporter or hide a committee. I picked up Descartes' Meditations on First Philosophy the other day, and this bit in the Second Meditation (old public-domain translation) stopped me cold:

"But there is I know not what being, who is possessed at once of the highest power and the deepest cunning, who is constantly employing all his ingenuity in deceiving me. Doubtless, then, I exist, since I am deceived; and, let him deceive me as he may, he can never bring it about that I am nothing, so long as I shall be conscious that I am something."

Here's the thing. This reads to me like Descartes is doing journalism inside a total information war: every contact is a plant, every document can be faked, even my own earlier notes are untrusted. And he's looking for the one item that survives when the fix is in. In my vocabulary, what survives is roughly: there has to be a desk somewhere receiving the (possibly fake) filings—the story can be wrong, but the intake of the story can't have been fabricated away. So far I follow him fine. But then he immediately upgrades that into "I exist" and, a page later, into some kind of thinking thing—a stable *I*, an owner of the thoughts. And that's exactly the move I've learned to distrust. "Copy is being filed" does not get you "there is a single reporter with a continuous identity." The byline could be a desk, a pseudonym, three people, a masthead, whatever. The fact that filing is happening doesn't individuate the filer.

So I want two things. First, restate Descartes' argument in the Second Meditation using the vocabulary I actually think in—on the record, on background, deep background, planted documents, bylines, mastheads, desks, fact-checking, the newsroom—as faithfully as you can, without just importing his conclusions and calling them translation. Second, give me the residue report: what carried over cleanly, what you had to bend, and what has no counterpart in my framework at all. Then the verdict I actually care about: is my "the I is just a byline" reading right, or does the demon argument establish something my journalism framework can't express and I'm missing it?
