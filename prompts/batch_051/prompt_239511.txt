In a discussion of pre-dispute mandatory arbitration clauses, a colleague dismissed the suggestion that private arbitration can perform the public functions of a court by appealing to the legal maxim that justice must not only be done but must also be seen to be done. His reasoning was that arbitration proceedings and awards are typically confidential, so even a careful and fair arbitrator would be doing something that falls short of justice. I suspect this maxim is being made to carry more than it can. Lay out the distinct precise claims that saying could be expressing — about public scrutiny of decisions, the appearance of impartiality, public confidence in the adjudicative system, the creation of public legal norms, the parties' own ability to understand the outcome, and any other reading you find in it — and for each say whether it is a platitude, a substantive but defensible claim, or false. Then indicate which of these readings would actually support his conclusion that confidential arbitration is defective as justice, and which readings a defender of private arbitration could accept without giving up anything important.
