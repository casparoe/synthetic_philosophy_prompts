i've been trying to turn my free-speech views from a pile of vibes into something i can actualy defend, and every time i write it down i get a list where each item feels right on its own but the whole thing maybe doesn't cohere. i'd like an audit of the set rather than a lecture on the Correct Position. here's where i've landed:

1. i basically buy a Mill-style harm principle: the only legitimate reason for the law to restrict speech is to prevent or punish a concrete, wrongful setback to someone's interests — incitement, credible threats, defamation, fraud, invasion of private facts, that kind of thing. feeling offended, unsafe, degraded, or worried about the social fabric doesn't count.
2. the way the skokie litigation came out was right: a nazi group should be allowed to march through a jewish suburb full of holocaust survivors, and the state shouldn't ban holocaust denial or other hateful views.
3. harassment and cyberstalking laws are fine even when the offense is a course of conduct assembled entirely out of speech acts that would each be protected in isolation — a few hundred texts and emails, months of posting about one particular person, repeatedly calling their workplace — even though no single message is a threat or defamatory. the harm is real and the law should reach it.
4. i oppose hate speech laws, because dignitary harm is too spongy to justify criminal penalties and i don't think a group has a right not to be degraded.
5. the platforms were right to deplatform repeat election-denial and covid-misinformation accounts in 2020 and 2021, even though no individual post was defamatory, threatening, or inciting.
6. a private employer should be allowed to fire someone for off-duty racist rants — the first amendment restrains the state, not the boss.

does this package hang together? if there's a load-bearing conflict in there, i want to know which pair it is, whether it's real or only apparent, and what the cheapest fix is — which commitment bends or gets reworded, and what that costs me everywhere else on the list.
