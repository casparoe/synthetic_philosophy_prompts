I've been working through axiomatic bargaining theory and keep running into the claim that the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution is the fair alternative to Nash bargaining. As I understand it, both solutions are Pareto efficient, symmetric, and scale-invariant; they part ways because Nash adds independence of irrelevant alternatives while Kalai–Smorodinsky adds individual monotonicity. The Kalai–Smorodinsky solution also has a striking property: it gives each player the same fraction of the maximum utility she could get while keeping the other player at least as well off as at the disagreement point, whereas Nash need not. Since individual monotonicity is what makes Kalai–Smorodinsky fairer, my question is this: in a real negotiation where the two solutions recommend different divisions, how should I use the monotonicity axiom to establish that the Kalai–Smorodinsky division is the fair one? Is the fairness located in the equal-fractions property or in the monotonicity axiom itself?
