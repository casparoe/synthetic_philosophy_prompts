I am a PhD student in moral philosophy, designing the golden-rule component of an upper-level undergraduate course on moral rules. I would like you to build an eight-question sorting quiz, with a scoring key, that classifies respondents into the main positions on whether the golden rule needs repair and, if so, which repair is best. The target positions are: (i) the negative formulation alone, which forbids only doing to others what one would not want done to oneself; (ii) the positive formulation, which adds a duty of active reciprocity; (iii) the platinum-rule repair, which defers to the other party's actual stated preferences; (iv) an objective-preference repair, on which the rule should be applied to what a rational or adequately informed person would want rather than to actual preferences; and (v) a heuristic-only view, on which the rule is a defeasible default that role-specific duties may override. The questions should force respondents to take sides on the familiar problems for the rule—divergent preferences, paternalism, incompatible wishes, and asymmetric relationships—without naming those problems or the five positions in the question text. Each question should present a concrete scenario with three answer options, and none should ask respondents which label they prefer. Use at least the following scenarios: a masochist who asks to be harmed, a competent patient who refuses a beneficial treatment, two parties whose preferences about respectful treatment conflict, and a parent deciding whether to override a child's stated wish. Build the instrument from the logical space rather than from the list of positions: first identify the diagnostic questions on which these five views genuinely come apart, state which combinations of answers those questions allow, locate each position among those combinations, and mark unoccupied combinations as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Finally, flag any pair of the five positions that the quiz cannot cleanly separate, and say whether that is a flaw in the quiz or a genuine overlap among the positions.
