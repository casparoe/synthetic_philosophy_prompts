I have been collecting my own verdicts about when something belongs to a category, and I want to know which theory of concepts they commit me to.

Consider these cases.

1. A robin is a better bird than a penguin, and a penguin is a better bird than a bat. Yet I call the penguin a bird and the bat not, even though the bat matches the robin on the wing-and-flight features I mention first when asked what a bird is.

2. A dog that disease has stripped of its legs, its hair, and its voice is still a dog; a robot that looks, moves, and yelps like a dog is not.

3. A skunk whose coat has been recolored to resemble a squirrel is still a skunk, provided it was born of skunks and can produce skunk kits. But my first, unreflective reaction is to call it a squirrel; I correct myself only on reflection.

4. A counterfeit banknote indistinguishable from a real one by ordinary inspection is not money, though I would spend it if I did not know. A lab-grown diamond with the same crystal structure and hardness as a mined diamond is a diamond.

5. After living with one strikingly strange-looking cat, I recognize other similar cats as cats faster than the averaged cat image I have built up would predict.

6. "Things to take out of a burning house" is a category I can use immediately — wallet, photograph album, child, laptop — but its members share no family resemblance, and I cannot produce a definition for it.

Is there a single theory of concepts — prototype, exemplar, or theory-theory — that delivers all or nearly all of these verdicts? If not, state the hybrid or division of labor I should hold, tying each component to the case that forces it. Say which of my verdicts, if any, the account cannot accommodate, and whether I should abandon that verdict. Then apply the account to a case I have not settled: a drinking glass I have used as a pen holder for five years — is it still a glass, has it become a pen holder, or are those the wrong options? End with a committed position: tell me which account or hybrid you would defend, and defend it, rather than surveying the alternatives and leaving the choice to me.
