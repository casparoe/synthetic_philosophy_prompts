I keep going in circles on this and want someone to just take a side. The standard resolution of Maxwell's demon is that the demon never actually beats the second law, because Szilard, then Landauer, then Bennett showed you have to count the demon's information handling: to keep operating with finite memory, it eventually has to erase its record of which molecules are fast, and Landauer's principle says erasing a bit dissipates at least kT ln 2 into the environment, which balances the books. Now suppose I lift the finite-memory assumption. The demon writes to an unbounded tape and never erases anything, so it just accumulates bits forever and never pays the Landauer cost. In that setup, does the demon actually violate the second law, with the only caveat being that no real demon in a finite universe could do this? Or does the second law survive in a repaired form because the blank tape itself has to be counted as a thermodynamic resource, and if so which premise of the standard resolution was doing all the work? I want a committed verdict: does the unbounded-memory demon kill the second law, merely expose that the law was always about finite resources, or force a reformulation where information gets counted in the entropy budget?
