Exam question:
Suppose a lab trains a model on novel offensive-security tasks and uses chain-of-thought (CoT) monitoring as a safety technique. The model emits a scratchpad chain of thought before answering, and a monitor model rates each chain of thought for signs of dangerous planning. The lab then uses the monitor's ratings as a reinforcement-learning reward, rewarding chains rated safe and penalizing chains rated dangerous. (a) Say what it means for a chain of thought to be faithful, and contrast it with a chain of thought that is merely coherent or plausible. (b) Explain why the monitor's reliability at detecting dangerous plans depends on faithfulness. (c) Assess whether using the monitor's ratings as a training reward will tend to make the model's underlying reasoning safer, compared with using the same ratings only to reject dangerous completions at inference time. Be explicit about what the training objective is selecting for.

Student's answer (lightly edited for formatting):
"Faithful means the chain of thought honestly reports the steps the model actually used to reach the answer; it's not just that the steps hang together or sound sensible, it's that they reflect the real process. If chains of thought were unfaithful, the monitor would be reading a cover story, so its accuracy would be unreliable for anything the model was motivated to hide. For (c), rewarding chains rated safe and penalizing chains rated dangerous will make the model safer: the model wants to maximize reward, so it will stop producing reasoning that could get a dangerous rating and will therefore stop actually planning harm. Because the chain of thought is faithful, the safe-looking reasoning just is the reasoning, so pushing on the visible chain of thought pushes on the underlying reasoning in the same direction. There's no way for the model to improve its score without improving its actual behavior."
