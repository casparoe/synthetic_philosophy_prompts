I'm in a course on concept learning and we've been doing Bongard problems—those little puzzles where a dozen or so boxes are split into a left group and a right group and you have to say what rule separates the left from the right. I ended up in an argument with a friend about whether the concept is really "induced" from the examples at all, and I reconstructed the following argument from something the instructor said. I half-suspect it overclaims, but I can't locate the exact step. Could you go through it step by step and tell me if it's valid, and if not, which step fails, and what the strongest version of the conclusion is?

(1) A Bongard problem gives you a tiny finite sample: about half a dozen positive examples and half a dozen negative ones.
(2) For any finite sample like that, there are infinitely many mutually incompatible rules that fit the sample perfectly—rules that agree on all the shown boxes but diverge wildly on unseen cases.
(3) So the examples by themselves do not determine a unique concept; they only mark out a class of concepts consistent with the sample.
(4) Yet people solving these problems often converge on a single, fairly specific rule, and they do it quickly and with little deliberation.
(5) The only way to get from the underdetermination in (3) to the convergence in (4) is for the learner to bring a prior ordering over possible rules—a built-in preference for some hypotheses over others.
(6) But if that prior ordering is what selects the particular rule from among all the rules consistent with the examples, then the real source of the concept is the ordering; the examples merely prune alternatives.
(7) Therefore concepts are not induced from examples. The examples just trigger a concept that was, in an important sense, already in the learner. Learning from few examples is selection from a pre-existing menu, not construction from data.

Is this argument valid? If not, which step exactly goes wrong—or is there an equivocation or a non sequitur between steps? And if the conclusion is too strong, tell me what actually follows from (1)–(6).
