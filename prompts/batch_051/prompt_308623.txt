She is unusually self-conscious about this—she once left a shopping trip rather than tell a convenient lie—and I think her decisions are being driven by a principle rather than by mood. She unapologetically maintains Santa Claus and the tooth fairy, in the full present-tense way, and says she will never treat having done so as a mistake. When the family dog was put to sleep, she told her then-four-year-old that the dog had gone to live on a farm with other dogs, and a year later she told him the truth; she still stands by that decision.

By contrast, she has refused to tell, or has openly corrected, the following: that the toy store is closed when it is open; that a vaccination won't hurt; that the police will come if the child doesn't behave; that a child's teeth will fall out by morning if they aren't brushed; that a face will freeze that way; and that the ice cream truck plays music when it is out of ice cream.

A new case has now come up. A close family friend has been hospitalized for a mental health crisis and has been away for two weeks. My six-year-old cousin has noticed the absence and begun to ask whether he did something wrong or whether she is angry with him. My aunt is thinking of telling him that the friend has a bad flu and will be back in a few weeks.
