I keep running into a pattern in the law that I can't make cohere, and I'd like you to reconstruct the view it quietly commits a society to. In the United States, paying someone for a kidney is a federal crime; the transaction itself is forbidden no matter how informed or willing the seller is. Yet it is legal to pay people for blood plasma, and clinics do so openly; it is legal to compensate egg donors, often at thousands of dollars; and it is legal to hire people for unusually dangerous work — commercial fishing, logging, roofing, test piloting — at wages that partly reflect the risk. So the same legal system treats some exchanges that put a price on the body, or on bodily risk, as ordinary commerce and others as criminal.

That gap seems to need a rationale. One might say a kidney is vital and irreplaceable while plasma replenishes and eggs are not needed for life. One might say selling a kidney is exploitative, or coerced by poverty, while wages for dangerous work are freely chosen. One might say some things are degraded by being priced and others aren't. But each of these lines wobbles under the comparisons: plasma is collected repeatedly from people who often need the money; egg donation carries real medical burdens; dangerous jobs can kill or maim people, and the wage is exactly what compensates them for that. So the distinctions can't just be about risk, bodily invasion, or poverty.

What I want is an answer that commits to a reconstruction: what view of which exchanges are properly for sale — and what makes the forbidden ones wrong — does this pattern of laws actually presuppose, as opposed to the things people say that the pattern merely tolerates? Is there one coherent principle underneath it, or does the law presuppose several principles that point in different directions? And if the principle you identify were followed through consistently, which other currently legal or illegal exchanges would it force us to reconsider? Defend the reconstruction you think best fits the pattern, and say where you are least confident.
