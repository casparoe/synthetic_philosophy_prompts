Last night a friend and I argued long into the evening about whether an activity can still be play once scores, rankings, or any reward beyond the activity itself enter the picture. I maintained that play is corrupted the moment it is pursued for an ulterior end; he maintained that a freely chosen competitive game remains play even when a league assigns it a rating. The next morning I realized that this disagreement bears directly on a provision I had never read carefully. Our neighborhood's Free Play Cooperative, to which my family belongs, is governed by a founding covenant adopted in 1975 that contains this sentence:

"For the purposes of this cooperative, an activity counts as play only when it is entered into freely and pursued for its own sake, and not for pay or any reward outside the activity itself."

The covenant was adopted before the cooperative organized any teams. This year the cooperative chartered a youth table-tennis team that practices in the hall, plays official matches in a regional league, and keeps a running ladder of player ratings. The players join freely, are not paid, and may quit at any time; they plainly enjoy the sport. But the league publishes standings and awards trophies each season, and the cooperative has begun advertising the team's improving rating as an accomplishment.

The cooperative's space-use rules permit a room to be reserved free of charge only for activities that count as play; all other uses carry a fee. Determining whether the team's practices and matches count as play therefore determines how the team's use of the hall is assessed. The covenant says nothing about organized competition, ratings, or trophies, since no such arrangements existed in 1975.

Please work through what a plain reading of the quoted sentence would decide in this case, what the evident purpose of the play definition would suggest, what the 1975 drafters would most plausibly have said had they been asked about a competitive youth team, and what an ordinary member of the cooperative today would understand the sentence to mean. When these approaches diverge, say which one ought to control and why.
