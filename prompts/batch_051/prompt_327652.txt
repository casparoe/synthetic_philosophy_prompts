I sit on a statutory advisory panel that decides, case by case, what welfare conditions attach to licences for fish and shellfish farms in our region, and our past decisions function as precedents we are expected to follow unless we can distinguish them. Over the past four years we have: approved with conditions a request to raise stocking density for Atlantic salmon in sea cages, provided dissolved oxygen stayed above 7 mg per litre and fin condition was scored monthly; rejected a proposal to hold edible crabs out of water at 4 °C for up to 48 hours before sale, requiring submerged, chilled holding and a journey cap of 12 hours; approved with conditions a plan to pump harvest-sized rainbow trout from tanks to the stun table without anaesthesia, as long as crowding time stayed under 15 minutes; rejected a proposal to introduce eyestalk ablation in Pacific whiteleg shrimp broodstock, the practice of cutting one eyestalk from females to force spawning, even though intact females were projected to spawn a third less; and required a salmon harvest station, after an inspection, to replace carbon-dioxide stunning with percussive or electrical stunning within 18 months, with daily checks that every fish is insensible before bleeding out.

The application now before us is from a European sea-bass producer who wants to harvest by submerging fish in an ice slurry until movement ceases, then bleeding them out, with no prior electrical stun. An electrical stunner for sea bass is commercially available but has not been validated at the line speeds this farm would run, and fitting and trialling it would raise processing costs by roughly 15 per cent; percussive stunning is not feasible for sea bass at this throughput, and the firm is not proposing carbon dioxide. The applicant is asking for a five-year licence to harvest using ice slurry while running validation trials on the electrical stunner in parallel, and it cites our trout-pumping decision as evidence that we permit conscious handling under bounded conditions.

I am less interested in my fellow panellists' gut reactions than in what our own record commits us to. What should we decide if we are to be consistent with ourselves; which earlier decision is the closest analogue; which candidate rules explain the precedents equally well and what each predicts in this case; and what further case, not yet before us, would do most to choose between them?
