In a seminar on the Sleeping Beauty problem, a colleague kept settling every dispute with the one-liner "credence defers to objective chance": since the coin's chance of heads is 1/2, Beauty's credence in heads must be 1/2, full stop. I suspect that sentence compresses several different principles. What could "credence defers to objective chance" be claiming, precisely? For each candidate reading, specify what kind of chance is operative, what background information is and is not admissible, and whether the claim comes out near-tautological, substantive but defensible, or false (and whether it survives cases such as crystal-ball evidence, undermining futures, or self-locating beliefs); then say which reading, if any, actually licenses the move from the coin's 1/2 chance of heads to Beauty's credence of 1/2 when she wakes, and what further assumptions that inference would need. Lay the readings out as a menu, and identify which one the colleague's argument has to be relying on.
