I am drafting a short statement of my views on the self for a workshop and would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. My current commitments are as follows.

1. The self is a center of narrative gravity: an abstract posit we introduce to unify and predict a person's conduct over time, rather than a substance, a location in the brain, or a nonphysical soul.

2. A center of gravity is real, though real in an abstract way, and I take the self to be real in the same way.

3. The self is not a causal agent. It does not literally initiate movements, decisions, or utterances.

4. People are morally responsible for their actions, and persons can be wronged.

5. Severe autobiographical memory loss, of the sort seen in advanced dementia, can destroy a self while the human animal lives on.

6. Autobiographical memory is not what the self is. Memory supplies material that the narrative works over; the self is the narrative's center, not its archive.

7. A coherent but substantially false self-narrative still supports a self; selfhood does not require accurate autobiography.

8. Narrative agency — the capacity to shape and revise one's own self-story — can be damaged, and damaging it is a way of harming a person.

I am not asking for a survey or a reading list. I want an audit of this package. Which of these commitments genuinely pull against one another, which pairs only seem to, and why? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, state what would have to give in each case, and then say which single revision would leave the overall position in the best shape. If an apparent tension dissolves on closer inspection, say exactly how. Keep the entire audit under 500 words.
