Act as a philosopher of social science who specializes in social ontology. Before you commit, quickly brainstorm candidate requirements and toss any that would fit an arbitrary 20-year band of the population just as well as they fit a generation. The question: what would a theory of generations have to get right in order to count certain cohorts (Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z, whatever sub-labels people invent next) as real social kinds rather than marketing labels with birth-year cutoffs? Give exactly five desiderata such a theory must satisfy, each in one or two sentences, tied to the case or distinction that makes it a genuine requirement, and say for each whether it is a hill to die on or a nice-to-have. No intro, no conclusion, just the numbered list. At least one desideratum should rule out treating a random 15-year band with its own slang as a real generation, and at least one should reckon with the fact that the label itself reshapes the cohort—people are sorted, marketed to, and start telling their life story through it.
