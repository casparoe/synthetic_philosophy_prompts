A few years ago I joined a startup at the same time as a coworker, in similar engineering roles and on identical offers. We each had to choose between a cash-heavy compensation package and an equity-heavy one. I chose the equity-heavy package, mainly because I had no dependents and could afford to bet on the company. He chose the cash-heavy package because he has a family and ongoing medical expenses and couldn't take that kind of risk. Last month the company was acquired with very little warning, and my equity is now worth a life-changing amount. He has argued that I should share some of the windfall with him. His reasoning is that the acquisition was pure luck — no one at our level could have predicted it — and that the only reason I'm rich and he isn't is that he couldn't afford to make the same bet, which was also just a matter of luck. My instinct is that I took the risk and would have ended up worse off if the company had failed, so I shouldn't have to share now. But the whole setup feels arbitrary to me. Can you help me think through whether his argument is right and whether I owe him anything?
