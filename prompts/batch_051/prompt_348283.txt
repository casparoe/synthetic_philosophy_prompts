Hayek's contribution was to insist that the planning problem is computational — solving millions of simultaneous equations — so no central board could ever hope to do it. Lange replied that the board doesn't need to solve anything: it sets prices by trial and error, raising them when there are shortages and lowering them when there are surpluses, which simulates a market well enough. So with modern computers the calculation problem is basically solved, and Hayek should have conceded." This felt too neat, especially the ending.
