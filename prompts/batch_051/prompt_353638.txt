I've been trying to get clear on an analogy that comes up in discussions of self-reference and self-replication, and I want the conceptual mechanics rather than the formal details. To fix ideas, suppose we define a small formal system—a simplified relative of the Typogenetics system from Hofstadter's writing—as follows. There is an alphabet of four symbols, say A, C, G, T. A string of these symbols is called a strand. A strand can be interpreted in two different ways: as inert data, and as a program. Under the program interpretation, a fixed translation table converts the strand (read in small blocks) into a sequence of operations on strands—operations such as cut, insert, delete, move left or right, and copy the currently read symbol. An interpreted strand is called an enzyme; it moves along whichever strand it is applied to, performing its operations, and thereby transforms an input strand into an output strand. The crucial collapse is that enzymes are themselves strands made of the same alphabet, so the system is closed: strands encode operations on strands, and a strand may encode an operation that is applied to other strands, or even to itself. Some strands, it turns out, encode enzymes that copy arbitrary strands—and therefore copy the very strand that encoded them, so self-replication arises without any external copying mechanism.

I understand the claim that this is "the same move" as Gödel's construction of a sentence that, via Gödel numbering, refers to its own unprovability. But I want to grasp why that is more than a loose analogy. Please explain the conceptual isomorphism between a strand that encodes an enzyme that copies strands and a formal system that can talk about its own provability. In particular: where does the data/program duality show up in the Gödel setting, and what plays the role of the copying enzyme? Why does the same diagonal structure produce a positive, constructive result in Typogenetics—a self-replicating strand—but a limitative result in Gödel—an undecidable sentence—and what does that tell us about when self-reference is harmless versus when it causes trouble? I'm less interested in the details of the enzyme table or of Gödel numbering than in the underlying idea.
