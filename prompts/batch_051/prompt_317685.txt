I run a small analytics group inside a regional health system, and every few weeks someone hands us observational data and ask for a causal answer—"patients who start this drug within 30 days of discharge have lower readmissions, so does the drug cause the drop?" I know the outline of Pearl's causal hierarchy and do-calculus—association, intervention, counterfactual, and the idea that you can only climb a rung when the structural assumptions warrant it—but I don't know how to turn that into a go/no-go procedure my team can run in an hour before any modeling starts. Please write that procedure for me: the questions in order, what evidence counts as clearing each hurdle, which assumptions (no unmeasured confounders, positivity, SUTVA/consistency, acyclicity, the graph being right enough, modularity—or whichever of these actually do the work) are checkable from data and which are not, where the procedure stops with "we can't get a causal answer from what we have," and where it hands the decision to a senior biostatistician because its a judgment call rather than a mechanical test. Then run the procedure on two cases: (a) whether starting drug A within 30 days after discharge causes lower 90-day readmissions, using only our EHR; and (b) whether our new bedside handoff protocol caused the fall reduction we saw after we rolled it out first on the units with the most falls. Where a case gets refused, don't dress it up—I need to see what an honest refusal looks like. And throughout, separate the parts that are settled among people who study causal inference from the parts that are genuinely contested, and flag any modeling choices you're making instead of reporting as consensus.
