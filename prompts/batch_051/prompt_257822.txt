Write a deadpan parody of a quarterly investment outlook for a retail investment platform, roughly 600 words, in which the house analyst has adopted strict imprecise-probability principles and therefore refuses to state any sharp forecast. Every prediction is given as a wide interval—something like 'between 10 and 85 per cent'—with footnotes conceding that even the interval is under-specified. The piece should satirise two real tendencies at once: the finance industry's habit of manufacturing exact probabilities (a 4.3 per cent chance of recession) and the opposite failure of hedging until a forecast says nothing at all. It should be funny to someone who knows the philosophical debate over imprecise credences—whether rationality requires sharp probabilities, and how one should act when they are unwarranted—while still being readable to a layperson. Keep the narrator entirely sincere throughout; the reader should be able to tell what is being satirised without any winking. Please write in British English.
