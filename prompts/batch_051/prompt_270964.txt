Lately I've been going down a Schopenhauer rabbit hole (a friend keeps briging him up to argue about whether podcasts count as art, but never mind). The claim that's stuck with me is how special he thought music was. As I understand it, he thought the other arts represent the Platonic Ideas through particular objects or scenes, but music doesn't do that; instead it's a direct copy of the will itself—the inner striving, longing, and suffering—without the detour through concepts or images. One of the consequences he seemed to draw is that music gives the quintessence of the emotions, sorrow itself or joy itself, rather than depicting this or that sorrowful event. And that's why he figured a complete philosophy of music would basically be a complete metaphysics, just done in a language that hits the emotions directly.

Here's the question I want to put to you. If Schopenhauer could somehow see today's ecosystem of algorithmic mood playlists and background music—Spotify throwing lo-fi beats at you to study or relax, endless auto-generated ambient streams designed to nudge you toward focus or calm, stores piping in music engineered to lower anxiety, music being used like an acoustic drug for managing mental states—what would he make of it? Would he see it as vindicating the idea that music is a direct copy of the will, since we're literally using it to steer the will, or would he see it as a corruption of what music is supposed to do? Where exactly do you think he'd draw the line, and why? Ground this in the details of his actual system, not just the predictable complaint that he was a grumpy pessimist.

Also, I can't get a tension in his own aesthetics straight: aesthetic contemplation is suposed to be a will-less state, a temporary escape from willing, but music is the art that directly copies the will itself. So if you have a view on how he'd resolve that when he talks about actually listening to music, work it in—it seems important for whether he'd think mood-tailored background music is the purest or the most degraded form of listening that exists.
