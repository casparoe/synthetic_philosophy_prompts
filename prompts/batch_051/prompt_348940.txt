ok so in my gov class we're doing contracts of adhesion—when a company gives you take-it-or-leave-it terms and the only question is wether you actually agreed. we got a packet of made-up rulings by the same judge, all about that, and we have to predict her next one. her past decisions are:

- game sold in a box, with a sticker on the outside saying "by opening this package you accept the license inside, including arbitration; if you don't accept, return it unopened." buyer opens it and plays. judge: agreement, formed when he opened it.
- website has terms of use only as a footer link, no popup or notice, someone reads three articles without seeing it. judge: no agreement.
- app signup shows terms in a scroll box with one button that says "I agree." user clicks without reading. judge: agreement.
- a site's home page has a big button that says "Enter" and a terms link above it, but nothing says entering counts as accepting. judge: no agreement, because entering a site is not the same as agreeing to a contract.

new case: a streaming service updates its terms and the next time a user opens the app a banner blocks the screen saying "we've updated our terms. by continuing to use the service, you accept them, including mandatory arbitration." the only way to get rid of the banner is an X to close it. the user closes it and keeps watching.

the assignment asks: if the judge wants to be consistent with her old rulings, what should she decide, and what rule would she be committed to either way? i keep switching between yes (she already counted opening a box as acceptance once you're on notice) and no (closing a banner isn't agreeing, and "continue using" is passive). what are the rules that fit everything she's said so far, which past case is the true closest analog, and what new case would finally force a choice between the rules?
