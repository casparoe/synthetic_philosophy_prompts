I'm TAing a philosophy of marriage seminar, and we're about to do a unit on whether monogamy and sexual exclusivity are moral requirements or just conventions. I want a classroom exercise that sorts students by what they actually believe, not by the labels they came in with. I keep coming back to this passage from David Hume's essay "Of Polygamy and Divorces":

"But nature, having endowed man with reason, has not so exactly regulated every article of his marriage contract, but has left him to adjust them, by his own prudence, according to his particular circumstances and situation. Municipal laws are a supply to the wisdom of each individual; and, at the same time, by restraining the natural liberty of men, make private interest submit to the interest of the public. All regulations, therefore, on this head are equally lawful, and equally conformable to the principles of nature; though they are not all equally convenient, or equally useful to society."

I'd like a decision tree or sorting quiz keyed to that passage. Let the root question be whether the ends Hume names—propagation and subsistence—exhaust the moral stakes of marriage, or whether sexual fidelity itself carries independent moral weight. Branches should test things like: whether jealousy is a natural sentiment to accommodate or a social artefact to criticize; whether legal permission of non-monogamy would undermine some social good or only redistribute power; whether exclusivity's justification is promissory/consensual or depends on a thicker natural-law claim; and whether "useful to society" is doing all the work or just papering over a prior moral commitment. Build from the logical space rather than from a list of -isms: first the questions and the combinations of answers they permit, then locate the actual positions in that space, and flag any unoccupied cells as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Each question should be answerable by someone who doesn't already know the position names. The class has about an hour, so aim for exactly five branch questions that yield something like five to seven leaves. Please give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion; each item should state one diagnostic question plus its answer-options and, where the mapping is tight, which leaf each option points toward.
