A university's promotions code contains the clause: "A research finding may be described as established only if the probability of obtaining the reported result, assuming the finding is false, is no greater than 0.05." The code was drafted with lab experiments and surveys in mind. A geologist is up for promotion, and her central claim is that a particular basin on one of Saturn's moons was formed by a single impact rather than by gradual volcanic resurfacing. She argues the clause cant apply to her case: with no repeatable experiment and no well-defined class of similar basins, "the probability of obtaining the result" has no clear frequency meaning—at best it is a committee member's subjective degree of belief, or an unobservable propensity of a unique past event. The code says nothing about single, nonrepeatable events. What does the clause require here, is her finding eligible or outside its reach, and how much turns on reading "probability" as a frequency, a credence, or a propensity? Give what the literal wording, the purpose of the clause, the drafters asked directly, and a reasonable reader would each say, and say which of those readings should win and why.
