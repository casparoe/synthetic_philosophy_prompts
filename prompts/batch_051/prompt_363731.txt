Hey — I'm a political theory PhD student working on populism, and I keep wincing whenever 'the will of the people' gets used as if it names one determinate thing. Could you give me a working definition of 'the will of the people' that actually does classificatory work? Propose the definition, run it against each case below, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdict. Then tell me which of your verdicts is least secure and why.

1. A referendum to tear down an unpopular urban motorway and restore the riverfront passes 90 percent yes on 82 percent turnout.
2. A self-selected reader poll on a news site where 84 percent vote to ban a minority group's religious symbols.
3. A refrendum passes 50.3 to 49.7 on 29 percent turnout.
4. Voters rank three options, A, B, and C: a majority prefer A over B, B over C, and C over A.
5. A policy that 58 percent oppose, where the 42 percent in favor care much more intensely and the opponents admit they would hardly notice its passage.
6. A deliberative poll where a random sample's support for a land-value tax moves from 45 percent to 78 percent after a weekend of moderated discussion.
7. A representative votes against the expressed majority of her district because she thinks the measure is unjust.
8. A constitutional court strikes down a popular mandatory-minimum sentencing law.
9. A protest movement that denounces both the government and majority opinion in polls as corrupt, and claims to speak for 'the real people.'
