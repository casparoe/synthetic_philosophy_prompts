Explain the claim and assess it.' The student wrote: 'A dialetheist is someone who holds that there are true contradictions, the standard case being the liar sentence, which is both true and false. Classical logic cannot accommodate this, because classical logic validates ex falso quodlibet, or explosion: from P and not-P one can derive any sentence whatsoever. If P and not-P were both true, explosion would let us derive every sentence, making the theory trivial. Since no dialetheist is a trivialist, the dialetheist must abandon classical logic. Paraconsistent logics are logics in which explosion fails, so a contradiction no longer entails everything; this is exactly what the dialetheist needs. Priest's LP is the best-known such system. The claim is therefore right, with two clarifications. First, dialetheism costs less than it seems: one gives up a single inference rule and can otherwise reason classically. Second, paraconsistent logic really just is the doctrine that some contradictions are true, so the second half of the claim is also correct.'
