Two people on my criminal law course got into it over a draft city ordinance that makes it an offence to 'loiter or stand about in or around any public building in a way that is unusual for a law-abiding citizen.' (1) Ana said the ordinance is void for vagueness because a reasonable person can't tell in advance what counts as unusual conduct, so there is no fair notice. (2) Ben replied that the doctrine doesn't require edge-to-edge precision — plenty of valid laws use open terms like 'dishonest' or 'reasonable force' — and this one has a clear core: someone with no lawful business hanging about and refusing to leave when asked. (3) Ana answered that the clear-core point only meets the notice worry, but vagueness doctrine also targets arbitrary and discriminatory enforcement, and this wording invites police to stop whoever looks unusual to them. (4) Ben said arbitrary enforcement is really an equal-protection problem about how the police use discretion, not a reason to void the legislation, and anyway as-applied challenges can correct abuses without striking the whole ordinance — otherwise any law with a discretionary term would fall. (5) Ana said that still leaves people self-censoring lawful conduct near the edge, which is exactly the chill the doctrine is meant to prevent. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the five moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each, then say who gets the better of it overall.
