I work on reward learning and inverse reinforcement learning, mostly in the context of trying to infer what an agent is actually optimizing from its behavior. Last night I had a long argument with a friend in philosophy of science who kept insisting that underdetermination of theory by evidence poses a deeper problem than the usual inverse RL ambiguity. His claim was not merely that many reward functions can make a policy look optimal, but that the very distinction between what the agent is optimizing and the procedure by which it searches its option space is not pinned down by any finite behavioral record. I pushed back by saying this sounds like familiar non-identifiability between objective, environment, and learner: the same trajectory can be explained by changing the reward, changing the transition model, changing the hypothesis class, or changing the search budget and implicit biases. He replied that I was already smuggling in a framework that assumes an objective plus a search space can be cleanly separated. 

I would like you to restate the underdetermination thesis as carefully as you can in my actual working vocabulary: state and action spaces, transition models, trajectory and preference data, reward hypothesis classes, policy classes, search procedures, planning horizons, regularization, priors, identifiability, partial observability, model misspecification, and experimental design. Then give me the residue report. Which parts of his thesis map cleanly onto non-identifiability in inverse reinforcement learning? Which parts get distorted in translation? Which parts have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? Finally, tell me whether the residue points to something my engineering framework is missing, or whether his worry collapses into a problem I already know how to state but not solve.
