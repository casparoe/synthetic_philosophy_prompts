I'm a software engineer serving as a first-time TA for an undergraduate "Big Questions" course, and I have to grade a short-answer assignment on the Fermi paradox. The question I gave the students was: "Explain what the Fermi paradox is, how the Drake equation is supposed to bear on it, and what the Great Filter hypothesis is. State whether the absence of detected extraterrestrial civilizations supports the Great Filter hypothesis, and explain why or why not."

A student submitted this answer:

"The Fermi paradox is the apparent contradiction between the fact that the universe is old and vast enough that intelligent life should have arisen many times, and the fact that we have no evidence of any other civilization. The Drake equation makes this quantitative: it multiplies factors like the star-formation rate, the fraction of stars with planets, the fraction of planets that develop life, and so on, and on common estimates it gives a large number of communicating civilizations that should be in our galaxy right now. The Great Filter hypothesis says that some step on the way from non-living matter to a detectable interstellar civilization is very improbable, so detected civilizations are rare. Since we exist, the filter was probably behind us rather than ahead of us. And because absence of a signal is evidence of absence, the Great Filter is almost certainly the correct explanation for the silence."

I don't want to penalize a mostly strong answer for minor slips, but I also don't want to pass through mistakes that matter. What letter grade would you give it, and what specific comments should I write? Organize your comments point by point under "conceptual accuracy," "use of the Drake equation," and "quality of inference."
