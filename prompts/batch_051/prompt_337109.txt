In my own mathematical and philosophical reasoning, I have adopted a standing policy: I will not allow a claim that depends on a completed (actual) infinity to revise my beliefs unless it can be restated in finite, constructive, or merely potential terms. For instance, when a Hilbert's hotel argument concludes that an infinite collection can be put into one-to-one correspondence with one of its proper parts, or when a diagonal argument concludes that there are more real numbers than natural numbers, I treat the result as a feature of a formal system rather than as a discovery about the world. Is this policy a defensible form of finitism, or is it a bias that protects my pre-theoretic intuitions about size and collections from being challenged? How could I tell the difference from the inside? Please respond in British English.
