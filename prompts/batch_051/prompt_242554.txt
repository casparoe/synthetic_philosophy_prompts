I'm a novelist and I need a spec before I write the middle of this book, so I dont just have scientists scoring ideological points at each other. Setup: a deep-space survey station has detected something they call the Anomaly. They can register it only through two instruments, from one angle, once. They will never get a sample, never open it, never revisit it, and never run a new experiment; the data they already have is all they will ever have, and it underdetermines whether the Anomaly is an organism, a machine, a mineral formation, or something no one on the crew has a concept for yet. Different characters each commit to one of those readings, and I want their disagreement to run on the real epistemic joints, not vibes.

So: what must an adequate theory of ontology-identification-under-inaccessible-information get right? I want the desiderata list, not the answer for this particular Anomaly. That means the cases any such theory has to handle (for instance, two incompatible ontologies that are observationally equivalent on all available evidence; an object whose behavior is changed by being observed; a signal that might be an artifact of the instrument rather than a property of the thing), the distinctions it can't collapse (evidence vs. inference, identifying a kind vs. characterizing it, "we can't know" vs. "the question is ill-posed", is vs. does), and the standing results from philosophy of science it can't contradict (underdetermination, the role of priors, the difference between information that's merely missing and information that's in principle unavailable). For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and say which single requirement does the most work against the naive readings like "the instruments just show us what it is." Then flag which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one character's camp being fussy, and whether any pair of them is jointly unsatisfiable. Skip the scorecard of real theories; I'll map my characters onto the criteria myself.

Hard cap: 700 words. Bullets or a table are fine.
