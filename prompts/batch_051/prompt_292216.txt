Before I read any more arguments about whether truth-seeking, tradition, and purity are intrinsically or merely instrumentally valuable, I want the full specification of what a theory in this area has to get right. Write the desiderata for an adequate account of when a value of this kind is genuinely non-instrumental rather than merely useful: the cases it must handle — the truth-seeker who is pained by not knowing something that will never affect any decision; the inherited practice kept up after its original function has gone; the purity reaction that tracks no real contamination — the distinctions it must not collapse — being valued for its own sake versus being valuable independently of consequences; pro tanto non-instrumental value versus an all-things-considered verdict; why a value persists versus whether it is worth having — and the empirical findings about disgust, moralisation, and cultural evolution it must remain consistent with. Give the case or consideration behind each requirement, mark it negotiable or non-negotiable, and flag any pair that cannot be jointly satisfied. Lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence: the single requirement that in your view does most to eliminate the standard positions, then set out the rest of the list with your reasons. I want a committed answer — choose the central requirement and defend it rather than surveying options — and no preamble or restating of the brief.
