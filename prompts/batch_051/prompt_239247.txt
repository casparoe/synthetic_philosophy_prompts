I am a science journalist who sits on the grant advisory board of a small private foundation whose founding statement names the advancement of knowledge for its own sake as the foundation's sole purpose, but offers no test for deciding when that purpose is outweighed by other considerations. This month the board must decide whether to award one of our four annual grants to a proposal that would establish why one genus of alpine moth begins its evening mating flight roughly forty minutes earlier than its closest relatives; the applicants explicitly frame the work as pure curiosity and disclaim any foreseeable application. The board is split between those who regard the proposal as the clearest expression of the foundation's mission and those who argue that the same sum would fully fund two projects on the early detection of pancreatic cancer, and that a commitment to understanding does not license indifference to the relief of suffering. Please draft the short recommendation memo I would circulate before the vote. It should contain a definite recommendation to fund, reject, or fund with conditions, and it should engage the strongest argument for the option you reject rather than praising curiosity in the abstract.
