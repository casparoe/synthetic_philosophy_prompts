I am a student at a sixth-form college, and I co-run our Philosophy and Politics Society, which usually draws around 20 members. We have one 75-minute session to run an evening activity, and I would like to design a structured game or negotiation exercise around an idea I keep returning to but have seldom seen taught well: that compromises between people with very different value systems can work like gains from trade. Each side concedes on a point that matters comparatively little to them in exchange for a point that matters comparatively much to them, so both end up better off by their own standards, even while each continues to regard parts of the other side's outlook as mistaken or objectionable. I want the activity to make participants feel the difference between this kind of value-trade and the cruder thought that compromise just means meeting in the middle or splitting the difference, and I also want it to force them up against the point where trading across values starts to feel like corrupting or betraying something they hold as non-negotiable.

Please write me the full session plan: the rules of the game or exercise; the materials required (nothing expensive, and no real-money payouts — an explicit points or token scheme is fine); a timed sequence for the 75 minutes, including any small-group stage, whole-group stage, and debrief; and a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity back onto the underlying ideas. The payoff structure should give different participants different internal rankings of the same issues rather than one shared scale, since much of the point is what happens when people trade across differently weighted priorities. The effect should appear in what participants actually do — the deadlocks, the package deals, the uneasy bargains — before it is named in the debrief, rather than requiring me to explain the concept in advance. Participants should be able to take part without any outside reading. Finally, please tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run the session and how to handle each failure, and be honest about which feature of the idea the activity cannot reproduce and which I will need to supply myself in the debrief.

Please write your answer in British English.
