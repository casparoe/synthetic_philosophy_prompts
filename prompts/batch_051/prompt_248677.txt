I've got a rule about when I'm allowed to consider something actually established. The other week a friend gave me a tidy argument for some supplement claim, and I rejected it not because I could spot the flaw, but because the chain of reasons never got down to something I'd directly observed or something self-evidently true — it just kept referring to studies he half-remembered and stuff "everyone knows." That's the rule: I don't accept a claim until I can trace my reasons for it all the way back to a stopping point that's either an observation or a self-evident truth, and if the chain goes on forever, loops around, or bottoms out in common sense, the claim goes in the not-yet-established pile no matter how much it would fit with the rest of my beliefs. My friend says nobody actually does this: my socalled foundations are just the places where I got tired and stopped asking for more reasons, and half my "observations" are secretly background assumptions anyway. Is my rule defensible? If it is, how do I tell a real foundation from the point where I just gave up?
