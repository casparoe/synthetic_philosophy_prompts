I am taking a graduate seminar on concepts and machine learning this term, and my final paper asks what Bongard problems show about how a concept can be induced from only a handful of examples. By a Bongard problem I mean the standard display: two panels, each containing six simple line drawings, together with an author-intended rule that every left-panel figure satisfies and no right-panel figure satisfies. The solver’s job is to recover the intended rule rather than any of the indefinitely many other rules that also separate the two panels. Before I evaluate candidate accounts one by one, I want to write down what any adequate theory of concept induction in this setting has to deliver. That is what I am asking you to produce: a list of desiderata, with each requirement tied to a case or consideration that makes it a genuine criterion rather than a preference of one camp. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are negotiable; flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied; and say which single requirement does the most filtering work against the stock candidates (similarity-based, prototype, exemplar, rule-based, Bayesian program-style, and modern few-shot learners), while keeping the emphasis on the criteria themselves rather than on a scorecard. Please do not turn this into a reading list; I want the specification, not the literature.

To make the list concrete, it would help if you worked through the following kinds of cases. One problem whose intended rule is a vague, relational property that no single local feature of the figures captures. One problem in which a salient feature separates five left figures from five right figures but fails on the sixth figure of each panel, so the adequate theory has to explain why the solver abandons that cue rather than settling for near-perfect discrimination. And one problem whose rule becomes natural only if the solver imports an antecedent concept such as inside, convex, or growing. Finally, as a check on your own list, tell me which desideratum you are least confident is a genuine adequacy condition rather than a camp-specific taste, what sort of result — an experiment on human solvers or a formal result about few-shot learners — would lead you to add, remove, or reweight a requirement, and where your reasoning about the joint satisfiability of the requirements is most likely to fail.
