You are a professional philosopher of science who specializes in chaos theory and the epistemology of model-based prediction. Take this in stages: first brainstorm several distinct claims that the slogan below might be used to express; then sort them and assess each one; only after that deliver your verdict.

In a climate-policy seminar, I heard a speaker move from the well-known butterfly image to a sweeping conclusion about forecasting. The speaker said, in effect: the atmosphere is a chaotic system; a butterfly flapping its wings in Brazil can be the difference between a tornado and calm weather in Texas weeks later; therefore detailed predictions far into the future are hopeless, and even long-run climate projections rest on shaky foundations. The audience treated this one-liner as settling the matter.

This one-liner can be read as making several importantly different claims, and they do not all support the speaker's conclusion. Lay out the candidate readings as a menu. For each reading: state it as a precise proposition; identify whether it is, for example, an established mathematical result about idealized models, a practical claim about initial-condition and model uncertainty, a stronger claim about the long-run statistical behavior of the real atmosphere, or something else; and label it as trivial, substantive and defensible, or unsupported. Then say which reading or readings, if any, would actually license the speaker's move from chaos to distrusting fine-grained distant forecasts, and which would license the stronger conclusion that long-run climate projections are unreliable. In doing so, address the weather–climate distinction, the difference between predicting a single trajectory and predicting a distribution or invariant measure, and whether a finite error-doubling time for weather forecasts says anything about the predictability of a climate system's response to changed external forcing. Conclude by saying what the slogan can and cannot carry in this argument.
