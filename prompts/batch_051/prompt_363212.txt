I've lived in a place where people would never cross against the light at 3 in the morning, and a place where nobody forms a line for the bus, and I've been trying to pin down what I actually think about how much rule-following a society should expect from people. So I've been going by gut on isolated cases:

- Crossing a completely empty road against a red light at 3am: fine.
- Jumping the queue in a busy bakery: not fine, and I'll usually say something.
- A neighbor who reports someone for putting the recycling bin out twenty minutes early: the reporter is worse than the one who did it.
- Parking in a disabled spot for two minutes, hazard lights on, to grab a package: not fine.
- A cashier who lets a customer ten cents short slide: fine. The same cashier giving free coffees to friends all week: not fine.
- A restaurant turning someone away for wearing shorts in the middle of a heatwave, when the patio is empty: I think they're within their rights but I also want to say its wrong anyway, and I can't say why.

What I want to know is whether there's one principle that gets all six, or close to it. If there isn't, which of my verdicts should I give up? And what would that principle say about the one I keep flip-flopping on, a city that fines people for selling gum to keep the streets clean, plus maybe a case of your own it would settle?
