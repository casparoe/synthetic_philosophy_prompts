W. V. O. Quine did not live to see neural machine translation, and still less the large language models that now produce fluent translations between languages after training on enormous corpora. Suppose he had been shown two systems. The first is trained on vast quantities of parallel text and translates fluently between English and Urdu. The second is trained only on monolingual corpora in each language, receives no bilingual data and no field linguist's analytical hypotheses, and nonetheless converges on a stable mapping between the two. Would Quine treat either system as a refutation of his indeterminacy of translation, as a vindication of it, or as orthogonal to it? In your reconstruction, keep apart the two claims he himself distinguished: the inscrutability of reference dramatized by gavagai, and the underdetermination of whole translation manuals by the totality of behavioral evidence. For each system, specify whether it resolves each problem, relocates it, or leaves it untouched, and say where in the pipeline a Quinean would locate the slack between the system's apparent determinacy and any fact of the matter about meaning.
