A blog post I read summarized what linguists actually think about "bad grammar" like this: "Linguists have shown there's really no such thing as bad grammar. Every dialect has its own perfectly regular rules, which is why linguists say things like African American English or Appalachian English are just as grammatical as standard English—calling them 'bad grammar' is mostly class and race prejudice dressed up as grammar. And since every native speaker has already internalized the rules of their own variety, the idea that you can make a grammar mistake in your native language is basically a myth. 'Ain't' and double negatives aren't errors; they're just different rules. So when a teacher 'corrects' your grammar, they aren't teaching you anything about how language works—they're just enforcing one arbitrary prestige dialect." I found this pretty persuasive, but a couple things about it bugged me and I can't quite articulate what. How fair is this as an account of what linguists believe? Go through it claim by claim and tell me where a descriptivist would nod, where they'd role their eyes or want to add a caveat, and what a thoughtful defender of teaching standard grammar could still say after the necessary corrections. I especially want to know what I can and can't take from it next time someone says somebody has bad grammar.
