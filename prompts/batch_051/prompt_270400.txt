I am putting together the next episode of a small seminar podcast on the foundations of AI interpretability, and the guest is a researcher who defends a position she glosses as recursive interpretability without regress. Her claim, as I understand it, is that using opaque AI systems to interpret other opaque AI systems is not an embarrassing stopgap but the only scalable route to understanding large models; the standard objection that this merely relocates the opacity one step back is, on her account, generated by a mistaken demand that every justifiable interpretation must bottom out in human-inspectable reasons. She argues instead that an interpreter need not be transparent to be trustworthy. What matters is that its reports about a target model can be independently calibrated, cross-checked against the target's behaviour and against the effects of interventions, and shown to earn their keep predictively, rather as we rely on instruments and on expert human report without inspecting their internals. I recently spent an evening arguing about this with a friend. His line was that calibration only works where one already has ground truth, that the philosophically hard cases are precisely those where the interpreter is doing the epistemic work and cannot be checked, and that the long human track record does nothing to vouch for a newly trained interpreter. He ended by pressing a dilemma I could not get out of: either the interpreter is just another black box, in which case the problem has been doubled rather than solved, or we are somehow entitled to trust it without interpreting it, in which case our standards have shifted in a way that looks suspiciously convenient. I came away more sympathetic to her position than I had expected, but I could not answer that dilemma, and I do not want the questions to be softballs.

I would like you to draft eight to ten interview questions for this guest. They should go beyond asking her to restate the view and should press where a careful sceptic would press. Please include at least one question on each of the following: whether the regress genuinely stops or only becomes slower and less visible; the circularity worry that verification requires ground truth exactly where the interpreter is needed most; the comparison with trusting human interpreters on the strength of a shared training history; the possibility that target and interpreter share systematic blind spots or a common inductive bias; and whether her criteria reduce interpretation to successful prediction or still allow for explanatory content beyond it. For each main question, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give, in the way an interviewer who has anticipated her standard moves would, so as to make those moves costlier. Please write the questions in British English, and when you answer, say how confident you are in the claims about interpretability on which the questions rely, and single out the point you are least sure of.
