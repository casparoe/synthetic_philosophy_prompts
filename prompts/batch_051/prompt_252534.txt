I've been reading Mill on individuality and I keep having this feeling that his argument for tolerating eccentrics is really just the exploration term in an explore–exploit tradeoff. He seems to be saying that society needs unconventional ways of life because they test modes of living that the majority would never try; conformity means we keep pulling the arm of custom and never learn whether other arms would pay off better. That sounds like a multi-armed bandit problem, with conformists doing pure exploitation and nonconformists doing the costly exploration that generates information for everyone else. Is this a deep structural identity or am I just pattern-matching on the word experiment? Where does the mapping hold and where does it come apart — for instance, in a bandit the payoff function is fixed, whereas Mill seems to think new ways of living can change what counts as a good life, not just reveal better means to pre-fixed ends; and the costs of exploration fall on the eccentric while the benefits mostly accrue to society. If the analogy is deep, what should transfer from the bandit framework (regret, exploration bonuses, optimism under uncertainty) to Mill's argument, and what would it predict that turns out false?
