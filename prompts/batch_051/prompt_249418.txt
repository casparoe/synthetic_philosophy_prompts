Something's been bugging me about the dirty hands debate. People talk about a politician who has to do something awful to bring about a necessary good — like authorising an operation that will kill an innocent person to stop a much worse attack — and say that although it was the right thing to do, their hands are still dirty, as if some wrong remains. But I dont see how both can be true. If the act really was right all things considered, then in what sense was it wrong? And if the wrong isn't cancelled, doesn't that just mean the act wasn't right after all? It seems like philosophers are double-counting the same wrong, or dressing up ordinary guilt as a philosophical result. Could you sort this out? Please answer in British English.
