I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on philosophy of language, focusing on Wittgenstein's Philosophical Investigations. Fourteen students, one two-hour session. I want a structured activity built around the diary example in section 258 — where Wittgenstein imagines someone who resolves to write S in a diary whenever a certain private sensation recurs, and then presses the point that the diarist has no criterion for whether S is applied correctly on a later occasion; whatever seems right to him will be right, and that means there is no room for a genuine mistake. I would like the students to run into this problem themselves before the section is named.

Design a runnable activity for the first hour, with a debrief for the second hour. The activity should give them a reason to try to set up their own private sign or private record, with some small stake in being right, so that the absence of a workable standard of correctness is felt rather than announced. Include exact rules or roles, materials, a timeline, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto the moves in section 258 and the surrounding argument. Then tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be explicit about which feature of Wittgenstein's argument the activity cannot reproduce and must be supplied by the facilitator. Do not introduce the term private language argument or name the philosophical conclusion until the debrief.
