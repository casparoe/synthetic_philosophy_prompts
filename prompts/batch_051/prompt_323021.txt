I am a retired physical chemist who has lately been reading Chinese philosophy alongside some contemporary moral epistemology, and I keep getting tangled in a dispute about Wang Yangming's doctrine of the unity of knowledge and action. I wonder if you could build me a thought experiment that would sharpen one disagreement within it.

Construct a one-paragraph, realistic scenario — set in a hospital, a university, or some other ordinary institution — satisfying the following specification. Two readings of Wang Yangming's doctrine are on the table. On the first, careful reading, genuine knowledge of a moral matter is not a separate cognitive state that then optionally causes a deed; it is already an activity of the heart, an incipient doing, so that a person who fully and sincerely recognizes a duty has already begun to act in the sense that matters, even if the overt deed is afterward blocked by something external. On the second, cruder reading, the doctrine amounts to an operationalism about moral knowledge: the publicly observable performance is the test, so if no such performance occurs, the person did not really know. The scenario should concern a person who clearly recognizes a duty, is wholehearted about it, and yet does not carry out the observable act — and the reason for the nonperformance must not be weakness of will, self-deception, divided motivation, or ignorance. It must be the sort of impediment that the cruder reading would nonetheless treat as fatal to the knowledge claim. The verdicts are fixed in advance: the careful reading must count the person as knowing, while the cruder reading must count them as not knowing.

After giving the scenario, walk through both readings and show exactly how each reaches its verdict; then identify which feature of the case is doing the work — try removing that feature and say whether the case collapses into an ordinary instance of akrasia — and finish by letting a determined defender of the cruder reading push back. What would such a defender say the careful reading has illicitly smuggled in, and what is the best reply available to Wang Yangming that does not simply redefine action into invisibility? I want this for a reading group of retired scientists, so please keep the apparatus explicit and do not lean on scholarly literature.
