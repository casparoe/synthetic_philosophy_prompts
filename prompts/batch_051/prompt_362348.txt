Here's one for the dataset: I'm trying to collect the different ways people respond to the absent-minded driver problem. Setup: a driver must decide before leaving whether to exit at the first or second highway exit; exiting at the first gives 0, exiting at the second gives 4, and missing both gives 1. He's absent-minded, so at each exit he can't tell whether it's the first or the second. The plan that maximizes expected payoff from home is to exit with probability one-third. But at an exit, if he could condition on being at the second exit, he'd obviously exit, which seems to undermine the plan.

Responses I already have:
(1) Planning view: the only coherent evaluation is ex ante, so one-third is simply correct; there's no new information at any exit, so no reoptimization.
(2) Multi-selves view: model the two exits as separate agents who share preferences but can't distinguish their positions; under the constraint that both must use the same mixed strategy, the equilibrium is again one-third, so the paradox dissolves into a coordination problem with no single time-inconsistent self.
(3) Information-set reoptimization: once at an exit, the fact that he's at an exit is itself evidence about which exit he's at, so the best exit probability conditional on being at an exit is higher than one-third; this exposes the clash between ex ante and ex interim evaluation.
(4) The second-exit intuition is not a strategy: the thought that he'd exit at the second exit is only compelling because it smuggles in the ability to distinguish exits, which the problem explicitly denies.

I want one more response that isn't any of these and isn't just a combination of them. Is there a genuinely different way to dissolve or diagnose the paradox? What does it add, and do you think it's actually better than the four on my list? Also, tell me where your own proposed response is most likely to fail.
