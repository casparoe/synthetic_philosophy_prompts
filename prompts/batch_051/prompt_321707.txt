I am drawn to the view that an alphabet is a theory of its language's sound system, and that a good orthography maps letters to phonemes as regularly as possible, ideally one-to-one. By that standard English spelling is simply defective: silent letters, inconsistent vowel spellings, and several written forms for one sound record history rather than sound. Yet I cannot shake the conviction that much of this supposed noise is doing real work — keeping the same morpheme recognizable across sign and signal, holding right, write, and rite apart, and preserving derivational links that pronunciation has obscured. Present the strongest case for each side, then say what it would take for the phonemic ideal to give way, what it would take for my intuition about English spelling to be exposed as a rationalization, and whether there is a reading of orthographic quality on which both survive. Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
