hey, i'm in a philosophy of private law class and we've been on contracts of adhesion for a couple weeks, mostly about whether clicking "i agree" on a terms of service is actually agreement. every time i try to settle it i end up going in circles on a clash, and i want someone to actually work through the options rather than just give me the standard "both sides have a point" thing.

the theory i'm drawn to: contract is basically autonomy. obligations are self-imposed, so if an adult gets a chance to look at the terms, isn't coerced, and clicks "i agree", then they've agreed, and the law should enforce it. this is a genuinely tidy view. one mechanism explains everything from a negotiated contract to a signed hospital waiver to a clickwrap box, and it's non-paternalistic: it doesn't say "we know better than you what you would have wanted."

the intuition i can't shake: when i clicked through a fourty-page terms document to use a messaging app last week, i was not agreeing to anything. i was doing whatever you have to do to get the app. it seems like a catagory error to put that click in the same box as signing a contract after you've read and maybe negotiated it. and the standard reply — that you could have read it — feels like it changes the subject, because no one actually can, and the whole design assumes no one will.

so which yields? maybe i should thin down my theory of agreement until a click counts (but then does the theory still explain why agreements bind, or is it just a ritual?). maybe i should trust the intuition and say clickwrap often isn't agreement (but then what is the law doing when it enforces these terms, and is it just pretending?). or maybe they're compatible because agreement isn't a mental state you have but a kind of status you step into by doing the right sort of thing in the right sort of setting — but then i need to know why a click can put you in that status for forty pages of terms you have no content-knowledge of. that's the part i can't get past. walk me through what each move costs, and what would justify deciding that the intuition is just confusion rather than tracking something real.
