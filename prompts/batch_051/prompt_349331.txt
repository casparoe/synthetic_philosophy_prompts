Here's an asymmetry I keep chewing on. In a courtroom, if a non-expert judge had to settle a technical question — does this chemical cause leukemia? — by listening to two hired experts argue it out, everyone would say that's a recipe for nonsense: the slicker or better-paid expert wins, and the judge has no way to fact-check either of them. Yet the 'debate' approach to AI oversight seems to run on almost exactly that dynamic: two AI's argue for opposite answers in front of a human judge who is far less capable than either debater, and the claim is that the judge can still pick the truth, even on questions the judge could never answer alone. Is this a real asymmetry — does the AI debate setup have some property the courtroom setup lacks — or is the ordinary skepticism about lay judges a problem the debate proposal just hasn't absorbed? I want exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion: (1) the asymmetry stated as sharply as possible, (2) the strongest reason to think the two cases are disanalogous, (3) the strongest reason to think they're not, (4) which way you'd bet on a debate protocol where the judge can ask questions, and (5) what would have to be true for that bet to be wrong.
