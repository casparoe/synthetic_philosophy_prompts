I'm taking a philosophy of mind class and I keep turning over a tweak on Mary's room that I can't quite resolve. You know the setup: Mary knows every physical fact about color vision but has spent her whole life in a black-and-white room, and when she finally sees red the question is whether she learns something new. Fine. But here's the version that's stuck in my head. Imagine a neural interface that can directly drive the cells normally driven by the retina, so it can produce any ordinary color experience you like. Now suppose the same interface can also produce experiences human eyes could never deliver — the forbidden colors, like a stable, unified reddish-green, not a flicker between red and green and not something someone just describes as "red and green next to each other." And then further suppose the interface can be tuned past that, to a color-like sensation no human has ever had, which isn't just a mixture of the familiar opponent axes. Call the new sensation xari. Mary reads all the physical facts about what the interface does, which cells it activates, how the opponent process normally blocks certain combinations, and so on. Then she gets the implant and has xari for the first time.

Does she learn a new fact in that moment? And does this version cut differently than the original red case? A freind told me the standard replies (the ability reply, the acquaintance reply) say she only gains new abilities or knowledge-by-acquaintance, not propositional knowledge, but I dont see why the reply should work as cleanly here. In the original case everyone can at least point to red out in the world, whereas with xari nobody has ever introspected the thing before, and there's no ordinary object that instantiates it. So maybe it makes the knowledge argument stronger. Or maybe it just relocates the question: the physical facts about the stimulation arguably just are what xari is like, so there's nothing extra left over. I'd like to know where the standard analyses hold up and where they stop applying, and what the novel quale case does to the physicalist's position compared to plain red.
