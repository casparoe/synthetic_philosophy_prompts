Consider the color phi phenomenon. A red dot is flashed at one location, and a green dot is flashed shortly afterward at a nearby location. Subjects do not report two successive dots; they report a single dot moving smoothly from the first location to the second and changing from red to green roughly halfway along its path, before the green dot was actually flashed. Two stories have been proposed. On the Orwellian story, the subject first consciously sees the beginning of the sequence veridically, and then, when the second flash arrives, the memory of the recent past is edited, so that the subject retrospectively remembers the color change as having occurred earlier than it actually could have. On the Stalinesque story, the editing happens before anything reaches consciousness: the subject actually undergoes a single, already-revised experience, and there is no earlier conscious experience that later gets rewritten. The deflationary position associated with Dennett and Kinsbourne says that this is a distinction without a difference. No introspective or neuroscientific evidence could tell the two stories apart, and insisting that one must be true presupposes a Cartesian theater, a single place and time where the events of consciousness occur in a definite order. I find that position genuinely compelling. It avoids a homunculus, explains why the two stories feel identical from the inside, and keeps the science of consciousness anchored in observable reports. But I cannot shake the intuition that there is a determinate fact about the timing and order of my own experience. Either I consciously saw the color change only after the second flash, or I consciously saw it midway through a pre-edited show. Those are different histories of what I actually experienced at a given moment, and it feels as though one must be right.

I want the strongest case for each side, and then a reasoned verdict about what should give way. First, give the strongest case for the deflationary conclusion that the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction should be abandoned rather than settled. Second, give the strongest case for the intuition that there must be a fact of the matter about the timing of experience. Then explain what would justify each resolution: dropping the intuition as an artifact of how introspection works, amending or abandoning the deflationary view, or finding a reading on which the two are compatible. Which should yield, and why? Please write your answer in British English.
