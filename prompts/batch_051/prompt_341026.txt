I'm a designer on a turn-based tactics game set during the siege of Leningrad. We added a ration system: your units and the civilians on the map share a food supply, and when it hits zero people start dying, with a little bread icon going gray. Playtesters immediately started optimizing it — they'd deliberately let the civilians starve to save the soldiers. The team is split. Some say the mechanic is honest because those choices were real. Others say we've turned mass starvation into a resource-management spreadsheet, and that there's something worse about making it playable and winnable than about showing the same thing in a film or book would be. One person wants to fix it by removing the civilians and only modeling soldier supply, since soldiers signed up for a war and civilians didn't. I dont know whether the problem is the mechanic itself, the fun around it, or what we chose to depict. Can you help me think through whether there's a defensible line between seriousness and turning real suffering into a game resource — and whether the civilian/soldier fix does any moral work?
