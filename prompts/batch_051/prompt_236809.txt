In my government class we have been discussing voter turnout, and I keep coming back to an asymmetry. Most adults I know treat voting as a civic duty. They say that someone who stays home on Election Day—even in a district whose result is a foregone conclusion—has failed to do something important. But these same people accept "one more person wouldn't make a difference" as a complete excuse almost everywhere else. Nobody is called irresponsible for skipping a park cleanup that already has two hundred volunteers, or for not adding a signature to a petition that has already reached the required number. In those cases, the negligible effect of a single contribution is treated as a reason not to contribute. In the voting booth, the negligible chance that one vote will decide the election is never treated as a reason to stay home. Is that difference defensible? The usual justifications—that voting expresses support for democracy, that nonvoters free-ride on voters, or that the practice would collapse if everyone stayed home—seem to have analogues in the other cases. I want the strongest justification you can construct for treating voting as special, tested against the closest analogy you can think of, and an honest verdict on whether we should keep the asymmetry or give it up.
