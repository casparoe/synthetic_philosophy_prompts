After a long argument with a friend about external-world skepticism, I am trying to check whether the set of epistemological commitments I found myself leaning on is jointly tenable, and I would like an audit before I present this position to my reading group. My commitments are: (1) knowledge requires truth-tracking in roughly Nozick's sense: S knows p only if, had p been false, S would not have believed p, and had p been true, S would have believed p; (2) knowledge is closed under known entailment, so that if S knows p and knows that p entails q, S can come to know q by competently deducing q from p; (3) I know a number of ordinary empirical propositions, such as that I have hands; (4) knowledge is fallible, since ordinary empirical knowledge need not rest on evidence that entails the known proposition; (5) I do not know that I am not a handless brain in a vat, because my belief-forming methods would keep producing that belief even under that false scenario. Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision that restores consistency? Please rank the tensions by severity, explain what each revision would force me to give up, and write the audit in American English.
