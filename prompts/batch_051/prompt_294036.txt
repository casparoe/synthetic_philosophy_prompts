So I teach a small upper-level seminar on famine politics - 16 students, 80-minute meetings - and we're about to read Sen on the Bengal famine alongside the Irish famine and the Great Leap Forward. I want a hands-on activity for one class that makes the core conceptual move happen in the room before I name it: the difference between a famine caused by food not being available and one caused by people losing their command over food that is still, in aggregate, right there.

I'd rather not do a lecture with props. The idea should hit them through whatever the rules make them do. So here's what I'm imagining, but you can improve it: a little simulation where each student gets an endowment card (a laborer who sells labor, a weaver who sells cloth, a rice farmer holding stocks, maybe a landlord or fisher), and then a shock hits - a war boom, a rice price spike, a collapse in demand for the weavers' cloth - and they have to obtain enough rice tokens to survive. The catch I want is that total rice in the room stays adequate the whole time. The famine happens because exchange possibilities shift, not because rice disappears.

Give me the actual runnable plan: the cards/tokens, the rules for trading, how long each phase takes, what you as facilitator do and don't say, and the debrief. I want another TA to be able to run it cold.

In the debrief, map the sim onto Sen's vocabulary (endowments, exchange entitlements, terms-of-trade shifts) and use Bengal as the case that fits the mechanism. Then complicate it with the other two: the Irish famine and the Great Leap Forward both push on the framework in different ways - one has a bigger availability-collapse component and the other has massive policy/coercion, right? - and I want the debrief to say where the entitlement story stops being enough. Be frank about what the sim cant reproduce: the scale of death, the state's role, the democratic-prevention claim, that kind of thing. If anything in the activity might accidentally make Sen look obviously right about Ireland or the GLF when he isnt, flag it and fix the design.

And since I'll get challenged by students on the history, tell me how confident you are in the factual/historical parts and single out the point you're least sure of.
