I keep coming back to a modified version of Nozick's experience machine that I think puts more pressure on the argument than the usual discussions of wireheading acknowledge, and I am trying to figure out exactly which premise is doing the work. In the original thought experiment, the machine delivers a stream of pleasurable experiences, and Nozick's reasons for declining are roughly that we want actually to do certain things, that we want to be a certain kind of person, and that we want our lives to be in contact with a reality deeper than any humanly constructed one. The modification is this: suppose the machine is not a fixed menu but a generative system that infers your highest-order preferences — including preferences for difficulty, danger, failure, moral seriousness, and even the preference to live in contact with what is real — and then satisfies all of them, as experience, with indistinguishable fidelity. It can simulate your experience of refusing the machine, of running every test you would ever run, of discovering on the best available evidence that you are not plugged in. Everything a person could cite as a reason not to enter becomes available as an experience once one is inside.

Does Nozick's argument survive this? The third consideration seems to be the point of failure. If the machine can supply the felt evidence of contact with reality, then either the argument's force was never in the felt evidence but in the external fact of contact, in which case Nozick needs an explicit premise about why that external fact matters independently of anything anyone could experience — or the argument collapses back into the second consideration about being a certain kind of person, which the modified machine also weakens by letting you be that person from the inside. I would like the argument reconstructed carefully enough to see where the modification bites, whether a repaired version can resist a machine that simulates depth and verification rather than merely comfort, and which theory of well-being, if any, remains in a position to say why one should still not plug in.
