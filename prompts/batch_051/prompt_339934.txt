Ok here's the situation. I'm a retired chemist -- spent most of my career on reaction kinetics and never thought about medical ethics -- and a few years back I got into philosophy, which is how I ended up as the token non-clinician on our hospital's research ethics board. We're stuck on a randomized trial and I want you to draft the memo I'll circulate to the full board so we can actually vote on the thing tomorow.

The trial randomizes patients with early-stage esophageal cancer between a new keyhole procedure and the standard open resection. The data safety board just gave us an interim report at roughly two-thirds of planned enrollment: the keyhole arm is showing a lower rate of serious surgical complications (about 6% vs 11%), nominally significant, but it hasn't crossed the pre-specified stopping boundary for efficacy. So the trial continues on paper.

One member of our board, a surgeon, wants to stop randomization now. Her argument is that no doctor who's seen these interim numbers can honestly tell a patient the two arms are an open question, so individual equipoise is gone, and continuing means the enrolling clinicians are either misleading patients or doing a weird soft-pedal around what they actually believe. Another member says that's exactly the wrong picture: clinical equipoise isn't about any single doctor's private uncertainty, it's about whether the relevant expert community genuinely remains divided, and the community is still divided precisely because a trend that hasn't crossed the pre-registered boundary could well be noise. He also says stopping on an early trend basically guarantees we'll overestimate the effect and leave future patients treated on weak evidence.

I've read the standard stuff on clinical equipoise and I get the usual move from individual to community uncertainty, but I've never seen a clean account of what accumulating within-trial evidence does to that community uncertainty when the formal boundary hasn't been crossed. That's the piece I need the memo to sort out.

Draft the memo, maybe 700-900 words, as if from me to the board, with a clear recommendation at the top: stop, continue as planned, or continue with some modification. Don't give me a menu of options -- take a definite position and defend it. And since I'll be sending it to people who disagree with me, make sure you state the strongest version of the argument for whichever side you don't recommend and say why it doesn't carry the day. If your recommendation depends on facts we don't currently have, say what we'd need to know and how to get it, but don't let that turn into sitting on the fence.
