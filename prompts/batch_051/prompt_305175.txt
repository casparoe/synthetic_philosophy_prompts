I want the specification before the verdict. Before I accept any answer to the question whether a language model's hallucinated answer is a lie, bullshit in Frankfurt's sense, or a mere error, I need an account of untruthful communication that is actually entitled to apply those categories to a system with no beliefs, no intentions, and no hearer in view. Write the desiderata for such an account: the cases it must classify correctly, the distinctions it must respect, and the constraints it cannot fudge, with the consideration behind each requirement.

Make the account earn its categories across the awkward range of cases. It should, for example, deliver the right verdict on: (a) the knowing liar, who says what he believes false in order to induce a false belief; (b) Frankfurt's bullshitter, who does not care whether what he says is true because his aim is to shape how others regard him rather than to report how things are; (c) the honest speaker who asserts a falsehood he has every reason to believe; (d) the spokesperson who reads a script he has not checked and does not care whether it is accurate; (e) the language model that fluently outputs a false answer to a factual question; (f) a deterministic lookup system that returns an incorrect stored string when queried; and (g) a human who takes the model's output, endorses it, and puts the falsehood into circulation. Each case should put pressure on the list in a specific way.

Among the distinctions the account must respect, pay particular attention to the ones that keep collapsing in these debates: the state of the speaker, the act performed, and the sentence produced; inducing a false belief versus merely failing to track the truth; being indifferent to truth versus not being the kind of thing that can be indifferent to anything; being deceptive versus being unreliable; and being truth-indifferent by design versus being truth-indifferent on an occasion.

Then flag which requirements are non-negotiable because dropping them makes 'lie', 'bullshit', and 'error' collapse into one another, and which are parochial commitments of one side in the dispute. Also flag any pair of requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied once non-human speakers are included, and say which one must give. I especially want to know which single requirement does the most to force the choice among lie, bullshit, and error for a language model, and whether that requirement already smuggles in the assumption that communicative vice can be borne only by agents. Close by saying briefly how the three candidate classifications—lying, bullshit, and error—fare against the list you have built.
