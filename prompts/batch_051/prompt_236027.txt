Construct a case where sphexishness and free will come apart in the direction people find hard to construct: the agent shows the classic sphex-loop — a relevant variable changes, the agent is in a position to notice it, and yet re-runs an invariant action routine from the top instead of re-entering at the appropriate step — but the action comes out free on a reasons-responsiveness account of free will (the actual mechanism that issues in the action would have done otherwise in a relevant range of nearby worlds where there was sufficient reason to do so) and unfree on a flexibility/context-sensitivity account (free action requires noticing and adjusting to just this kind of change in real time). Set the case in ordinary adult life, with no coercion, addiction, or mental disorder, and do not stipulate the verdicts; the scenario should generate them from the two accounts. Then run the check yourself: specify the goal, the routine, the changed variable, the actual mechanism that issues in the action, and the nearby worlds that matter, and explain why each account gives the verdict it does. Finish by saying which feature of the case does the work, what a stripped-down version without that feature looks like, and how a defender of the flexibility account would attack the case as misdescribed or rigged.
