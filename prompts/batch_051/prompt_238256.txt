You are a professional philosopher of medicine who has facilitated several adversarial collaborations between clinicians and theorists, and you know both the biostatistical/evolutionary naturalist accounts of disorder and the normativist objections to them. Before you settle on any wording, think carefully and brainstorm the strongest steelmanned version of each position, including the cases that are most awkward for each side.

Two members of my reading group have argued for a year about whether health and disease are natural or evaluative concepts. One is a naturalist: a condition is a disorder just when some part or process fails to perform its species-typical biological function, that judgment is in principle value-free, and health is simply the absence of such dysfunction; values enter only later, when we decide what to treat. The other is a normativist: the concept of disorder is irreducibly value-laden, because harm or undesirability is part of classification and because what counts as proper functioning cannot be specified without evaluative commitments. They have now agreed to produce an adversarial-collaboration document. Draft the joint statement they could both sign.

The statement should contain: (a) substantive claims both can endorse about the concept of disorder, about clinical classification, and about what their dispute is not about; (b) the exact residual disagreements, each phrased so its own side would sign it as written; (c) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions whose settled answers would move at least one of them; and (d) for each crux, a concrete case or observation both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about that case recorded in advance. Work in at least these cases: adult-type lactose intolerance; ADHD-like traits across environments that make very different attentional demands; the removal of homosexuality from the DSM; age-related cognitive decline; and a harmful but statistically common condition such as dental caries. If part of the disagreement turns out to be merely verbal, say exactly which part and why. Keep the document self-contained, with no citations or bibliography.

Format the deliverable as follows: exactly five numbered agreed claims, each no more than two sentences; then immediately one table with exactly five rows and these columns: Crux as a question; Agreed test case; Naturalist's prediction; Normativist's prediction. No introduction, no conclusion, no other sections.
