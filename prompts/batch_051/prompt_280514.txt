The rule: every adult member either pays full dues or, on the lower-income track, does six hours a week of house labor—cooking, cleaning, maintenance. Dana's been on the lower-income track for two years, but for the last eight months she's done almost no hours. She says she's burnt out and being made to work turns the house into a job. The house isn't in financial trouble, and a couple members genuinely like having her around, but others are building resentment because they're covering her shifts. Her actual defense is worth taking seriously: the co-op exists to meet housing needs, not to enforce a fair-share work ethic; she needs housing; and her slacking doesn't leave anyone materially worse off since the house would run the same with or without her six hours. Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the vote. Recommend a concrete course of action—revoke, probation with a hard deadline, keep her, whatever you actually think—with a time frame, give the strongest consideration on the losing side an honest run, and don't just restate the rulebook. Format it as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
