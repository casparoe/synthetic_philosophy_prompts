At a recent seminar on the interpretation of quantum mechanics, the speaker defended a psi-epistemic view—roughly, the view that the wavefunction encodes an agent's information about a system rather than a physical feature of the system itself—by pressing a single analogy. A weather forecast, he argued, assigns probabilities such as a 60 percent chance of rain tomorrow. No one treats that 60 percent as a physical property of the atmosphere, and no one treats the forecast itself as one more weather system moving alongside the cold front. The forecast is a compact summary of what an agent knows and does not know about the atmosphere, and it gets updated when the agent's information changes. The wavefunction, he continued, stands to a quantum system as the forecast stands to the atmosphere: it is a compact summary of what an agent knows and does not know about the system, it updates when information changes (including at measurement), and it is not itself a field or substance. From this he concluded that the position sometimes called wavefunction realism rests on a reification error, mistaking a state of knowledge for a thing in the world.

I would like a rigorous audit of how far this analogy holds. Begin by laying out the mapping explicitly, ideally as a correspondence table: the atmosphere, the meteorological state the forecast describes, the forecast itself, its assigned probabilities, the updating of the forecast with new data, and the eventual observed weather—what does each correspond to in the quantum case? Then identify the points at which the mapping breaks. For each substantive disanalogy, say whether it is merely incidental or whether it is load-bearing for the speaker's anti-realist conclusion, and specify which feature of the meteorological source domain the analogy is quietly importing into the quantum target. Finally, assess whether the conclusion—that treating the wavefunction as physically real is a reification error—survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and say whether there is a better analogy that does the work the weather forecast was meant to do without smuggling in those assumptions.
