I’m on the board of a small nonprofit, a secular community center, and I’m in the middle of a fight about a clause in our bylaws I never paid much attention to before. It says:

“No board member may support a motion by appealing to a sacred text, a religious authority, or a claim about divine will, unless in the same meeting the member also gives at least one reason for the motion that rests on none of those grounds.”

The clause was written in the 1990s and everyone agrees it was meant to stop people from turning board meetings into church arguments while still allowing people to speak from their whole convictions. Fine.

Now here’s the case. A board member, D, wants us to fund a particular after-school program. At the meeting she gives two secular-sounding reasons: the program is cost-effective and it fills a service gap for low-income families. But I happen to know—because she said so in a private group chat with some of us the night before—that her actual reason is that her pastor asked the congregation to support this program as part of their Lenten practice, and she sees it as a work of mercy. She also let slip that she used ChatGPT to generate the cost-effectiveness argument because she was worried the religious reason alone would violate the bylaw. I don’t think she genuinely believes the empirical claim about cost-effectiveness; she just needed a “secular” reason to attach.

So my question isn’t really about whether we should fund the program. It’s about what the clause actually governs. Does D violate it? Is the clause about what reasons may be *offered in the meeting*, or about what may *motivate* a member? If the secular reason is offered in the meeting but is not the member’s real reason, does that satisfy the clause? Does it matter that the religious reason was shared in a private side chat rather than in the meeting itself? And if the clause doesn’t reach D’s behavior, is that a failure of the clause or just a proof that the drafters didn’t foresee private digital channels and LLM-generated justifications? I’m trying to figure out whether the clause needs to be amended, and if so, whether any amended version can catch this without becoming unworkable.
