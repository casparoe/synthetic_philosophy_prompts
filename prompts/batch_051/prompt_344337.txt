My friend sent me this argument that gradual whole-brain replacement wouldn't kill you, and I can't tell if its sound or a sleight of hand. (1) You are not your biological neurons; you are the pattern of thoughts, memories, and dispositions they realize. (2) If you replace one neuron with an artificial neuron that duplicates the old neuron's input-output behavior exactly, the pattern is unchanged—so you're still there. (3) If a process consists of steps and each step preserves you, then the whole process preserves you. (4) Whole-brain replacement is just a long sequence of one-neuron replacements like that. (5) So a person whose entire brain is gradually replaced by artificial neurons would literally survive—no copy, no death, just a change of hardware. I keep feeling like (3) is pulling a sorites trick, but identity is transitive, so maybe it's fine. Tell me straight: is the argument valid? If not, which exact step fails and why? If it is valid, which premise should I actually be worried about, and why?
