I'm taking a course on the history of scientific explanation, and one of the assigned books closes its chapter on Aristotle with a brisk paragraph that I keep turning over because something about it strikes me as too neat. The passage runs roughly like this: 'Aristotle distinguished four kinds of cause, though explanation would be a better translation. The material cause is the stuff something is made of; the efficient cause is the agent that produces the change; the formal cause is the shape or defining pattern; and the final cause is the end or purpose, what the thing is for. For Aristotle, the final cause is the most important of the four, since you have not really explained a thing until you have said what it is for: an eye, for instance, is only an eye because it is for seeing. The Scientific Revolution was largely the story of science dropping the final cause. Early mechanics and chemistry replaced the sake of which with efficient causes, and stopped asking what a falling stone or a chemical reaction was for. Darwin then completed the story, because natural selection showed that biology too could be done without purposes: apparent design is just the cumulative result of blind efficient causes. What purpose-talk survives is confined to artifacts, where the maker's intention genuinely supplies the goal; everywhere else, saying something has a function is just a compact way of describing its causal role. The four causes are therefore of purely historical interest today.' I'm inclined to think the last two moves are overstatements—I've heard, but only vaguely, that Aristotle treated natural things rather than artifacts as the clearest case of teleology, and that biologists still take function-talk seriously—but the author is a professional historian of science, so I don't trust my hunches.

I'd especially like to know where the summary conflates claims that should be kept apart, or slips from a modest point to a sweeping one, and whether the concluding claim—that the four causes are only of historical interest—survives the corrections or depends on the most doubtful parts of the passage.
