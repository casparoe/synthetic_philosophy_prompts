I teach a 300-level philosophy-of-action seminar, 14 students, 75 minutes. Design an in-class game or structured exercise that makes them feel the force of Kavka's toxin puzzle before they hear the name: the payoff should depend only on their forming an intention at a certain moment to do something mildly unpleasant later, and the later act itself should be unenforced — something they will clearly have no reason to do once the payoff is in hand. They should come away seeing, in their own behavior, the gap between deciding and intending. Give me rules, materials, a timeline, a payoff scheme that doesn't use real money, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the puzzle's pieces (the condition on intending rather than doing, the missing future reason, the self-knowledge that blocks the intention). Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time, espeically a rational student trying to game it, and be honest about which feature of the real puzzle a classroom can't reproduce.
