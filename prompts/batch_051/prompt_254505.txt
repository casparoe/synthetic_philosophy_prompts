Before I evaluate the standard proposals for separating science from non-science—falsifiability, research-programme progressiveness, explanatory coherence, and institutional consensus—I want the specification against which they should be judged. Write the desiderata that any adequate demarcation criterion must satisfy. Fix the purpose explicitly: the criterion should be usable prospectively, by something like a research-funding panel, a state science standards committee, or a court deciding whether a field's claims may be introduced as scientific expert testimony. That means it must operate on features available at the time of decision, not on whether the field later succeeded; explain precisely what this rules out and why it is a substantive requirement rather than a convenience.

For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it. The kind of thing I have in mind: a criterion must not classify a false theory as non-scientific merely because it was false—phlogiston chemistry was wrong but was doing science—and it must not discriminate astrology from early astronomy only in hindsight, since the two look far more alike before astrology's record of predictive failure has accumulated. It also must say which revisions of a theory in response to apparent refutation count as legitimate development and which as ad hoc immunization; naive falsifiability says too little here because auxiliary assumptions can absorb almost any failed prediction. Distinguish which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a particular school. Then say whether any subset of the non-negotiable ones is jointly unsatisfiable; I most want to know whether prospective applicability, fidelity to the clear cases, and tolerance for young research programs can all be had at once, and what must give way if they cannot. Close with a brief score of falsificationism, research-programme accounts, and institutional accounts that treat science as whatever the relevant scientific communities certify, against the list, keeping the weight on the desiderata themselves.
