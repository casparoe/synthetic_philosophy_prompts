For a graduate seminar on non-Western metaphysics, I want you to write out the list of requirements an interpretation of Dōgen's being-time (uji) has to satisfy to count as a real metaphysical position, not just a poetic redescription of ordinary temporal experience. Give each requirement a name and say what it is supposed to capture: what phenomenon would be left unexplained, or what distinction would collapse, if we dropped it. I want this to be usable as an actual rubric, so the requirements should be specific enough to separate readings that treat uji as a kind of presentism from readings that treat it as eternalism, process metaphysics, or something else entirely. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable for anyone claiming to do justice to Dōgen and which are your own commitments as a modern reader. If some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable—maybe the demand that each dharma be an unpassing now conflicts with the demand that firewood is genuinely different before and after burning—say so rather than papering over it. That last part is the thing I most want to see.
