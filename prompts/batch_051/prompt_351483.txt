I'm a first-year law student and got into an argument with my roommate (who's a philosophy phd student) over a hypothetical our criminal law prof gave us, and I'd like someone to actually work through it instead of just gesturing at it. The jurisdiction we're studying has a sentencing statute, section 7.03 of the Criminal Code, that reads: "The severity of any sentence imposed under this Code shall be proportionate to the blameworthiness of the offender for the offence, and no sentence may be imposed whose severity exceeds what that blameworthiness will support." It was enacted in 1978 and everyone agrees the point was to put a retributivist ceiling on sentencing: the court can't tack on extra punishment for deterrence, incapacitation, or reform beyond what the person deserves.

The case: in 2021 D, then nineteen, acted as a lookout in an armed robbery. Before trial he was found to have a brain tumour, and after surgery he no longer remembers the offence and his values changed so drastically that two psychiatrists say there is no meaningful psychological continuity between the person before the surgery and the person now before the court—he is, in every sense that matters to character and personality, a different person. The prosecution is asking for seven years. The defence says section 7.03 forbids any sentence, because the only person the court can punish is the person before it now, and that person's blameworthiness for the robbery is effectively zero. The drafters plainly didn't have this sort of case in mind.

I want you to argue how 7.03 applies to this case from the text, from the purpose of the clause, from what the drafters would have said if asked about post-offence personality transformation, and from the standpoint of a reasonable reader of the code. Tell me where those approaches diverge and which should win, and say whether the clause requires, permits, or rules out punishing D. Please seperate what is genuinely settled in the theory of punishment from what is actually contested among people who work on this (for example whether desert attaches to the act or to the continuing person), and don't present a contested view as if it were the consensus.
