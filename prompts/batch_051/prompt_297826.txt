I am a graduate student serving as note-taker for our department's year-long constructive mathematics seminar. The instructor, Professor Lindqvist, uses an unusual format: rather than adopting a single formal system at the outset, she rules on proposed proof moves case by case as they arise, and I keep the running record. Her decisions so far are:

- She allows the move from A to ¬¬A, and from ¬¬¬A to ¬A.
- She allows the negative form of proof by contradiction: if assuming A yields a contradiction, one may conclude ¬A. She rejects the unrestricted positive form: from the fact that assuming ¬A yields a contradiction, she will not in general permit A.
- She allows the equivalence between ¬∃xA(x) and ∀x¬A(x), in both directions.
- She rejects, without any decidability condition, the move from ¬∀xA(x) to ∃x¬A(x).
- She accepts modus tollens: from A → B together with ¬B, one may infer ¬A. She rejects, without any decidability condition, the converse contraposition: from ¬A → ¬B one may not in general infer B → A.
- She rejects, without any decidability condition, the De Morgan step from ¬(A ∧ B) to ¬A ∨ ¬B.
- She accepted one bounded existence move: when P(n) is a decidable predicate of the natural numbers, she allowed the step from ¬¬∃n ≤ 1000 P(n) to ∃n ≤ 1000 P(n), remarking that a finite check could in principle be carried out.

Next week we face the unbounded version of that last case. A seminar member wants to argue as follows: the predicate P(n) is decidable by a fixed algorithm, and it is contradictory to suppose that every natural number n fails P; therefore some natural number n satisfies P. In symbols, the proposed move is from ¬¬∃nP(n) to ∃nP(n), with no finite bound supplied. I suspect her prior rulings underdetermine this, and I am less interested in the bare allowed-or-not than in the principle she is running. Please (a) state the two or three most plausible general principles that fit her recorded rulings, (b) say what each principle predicts for the unbounded decidable case, (c) give your best prediction, with the strongest consideration on each side, and (d) if her prior rulings do genuinely leave the matter open, specify the cleanest further test case we could put to her. Write the answer in American English.
