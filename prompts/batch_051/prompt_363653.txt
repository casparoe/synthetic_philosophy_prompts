I run a small executive-coaching practice, and over the last few years I've noticed clients keep bringing up the Stoic dichotomy of control — usually as some version of focus on what you can control and stop worrying about the rest. I've grown to dread it, becuase the two-box version keeps collapsing in the room. A redundancy scare isn't simply outside your control, since the client can update their CV, talk to their line manager, angle for more visible work; but it isn't in their control either, because someone else makes the final call. When I point that out, clients either go fatalistic — so why do anything? — or they quietly put the outcome back in the controllable box and then treat themselves as failures when it goes badly.

I know there's a more careful version underneath. Epictetus only put a narrow class of things fully eph' hemin — roughly, the judgements you make, the impulses you act on, what you set yourself to aim at — and a modern revision adds a middle box for partial control. But I've never seen that turned into something I can actually run in a fifty-minute session with a client who has never read the Stoics and isn't going to start. I see these clients weekly and the stakes are high — career, family, health — so I need it repeatable.

So please write me a decision procedure for sorting a specific concern into the right box and then assigning it an internal goal of the kind the client genuinely does control. It has to be usable by me, not by a philosopher: concrete questions in the order I should ask them, what a sufficient answer sounds like, where the procedure stops and says enough sorting, this is now a values conversation, and where the client and I have to agree the category together. Be honest about where the distinction does real work and where it quietly turns into self-blame.

Then run it on three concerns from my actual caseload: a client facing possible redundancy, a client whose daughter is waiting on A-level results, and a client caring for a parent with worsening dementia. At least one of those should produce an answer the client will not like — show me how you would deliver it.

After the procedure, give me a JSON object summarising the scheme with these fields: category, membership_test, internal_goal, failure_mode, worked_example. category is the box; membership_test is the question that puts a concern in it; internal_goal is the kind of goal that box licenses; failure_mode is the way this box goes wrong in a session; worked_example is one of the three cases. Use lower_snake_case for the keys.
