I've been participating in a perception seminar where the same disagreement keeps resurfacing, and I'd like you to design an experiment that might actually make progress on it. The dispute is over whether perception itself can be penetrated by cognition. Suppose a novice climber, just before leading a route that intimidates her, reports that the cliff edge above her looks farther away and steeper than it did a moment earlier; suppose a person with a spider phobia reports that the spider across the room is moving faster than it actually is. One side takes these reports at face value: the climber and the phobic person are having a different perceptual experience, because expectation and affect reach down and alter how the world literally looks. The other side says the percept is unchanged and only the downstream interpretation, judgment, or description changes: they see the same scene but their anxiety moves them to describe it differently.

Your task is to take one concrete case of that sort — perceived distance, size, speed, slant, brightness, or another dimension — and propose a test that could distinguish cognitive penetration of perception from post-perceptual judgment or response bias. Specify what subjects would do, what you would measure, and what pattern of results would count as evidence that the perceptual experience itself changed rather than merely the criterion or the report. Where possible, design the test so that a simple difference in verbal ratings between calm and anxious subjects would not, by itself, settle the matter. Then do the harder part: state what a positive result would still not show. In particular, address whether a successful test could locate the effect in early vision as opposed to somewhere downstream of perception, whether it could distinguish a changed experience from a changed response threshold, and whether it would say anything about whether such penetration is rational or adaptive. Finally, identify the residue: which part of the original claim do you think no experiment, however well designed, can settle, and why does it lie beyond testing?
