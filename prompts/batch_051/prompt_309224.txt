Recently, in the course of a long family conflict, I have repeatedly been told that I "have a reason" to forgive someone, to continue providing financial support, and to accept various obligations. I have developed a rule for handling such claims: unless the speaker can trace the alleged reason, through an intelligible deliberative route, back to commitments or concerns I actually hold, I treat it as no reason for me at all — at best a disguised demand, or a reason that would exist only for someone with a different psychology. I apply the same test to my own self-reproaches. Is this rule defensible, or am I merely adopting a self-serving version of internalism about reasons so as to disown any consideration I dislike? Are there legitimate external reasons that the rule would wrongly exclude, and what would a corrected version of the rule look like? How could I tell, from the inside, whether the rule is tracking the real structure of practical reasons rather than rationalizing?
