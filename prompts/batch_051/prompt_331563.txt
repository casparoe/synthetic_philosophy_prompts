Cox's theorem is widely summarized as proving that probability theory is the unique logic of plausible reasoning: any assignment of real-valued degrees of belief to Boolean propositions obeying a few seemingly minimal consistency desiderata must satisfy the probability axioms. Many Bayesians go further and hold that the theorem also fixes the dynamics of belief, showing that rational updating can only proceed by Bayesian conditionalization — upon learning E, one simply replaces P(H) with P(H | E).
