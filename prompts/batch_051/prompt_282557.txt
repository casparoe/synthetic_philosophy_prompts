I want you to translate three of those stock lessons into my vocabularly and tell me what doesn't survive. The lessons, as they usually get deployed: (1) Munich — appeasing an aggressive leader early only emboldens him, so you should demonstrate resolve now; (2) Vietnam — outside military intervention in another country's civil war tends to become a quagmire, so stay out; (3) the Thucydides trap — when a rising power approaches parity with a ruling power, war becomes likely almost independently of what either side wants. Take each one and restate it as a claim about learning or generalizing from experience: what would the state space, reward, training distribution, and generalization claim have to be? Where does the analogy distort when you force it into that shape, and where does it have no translation at all? Then do the part I actually care about: tell me whether the residue the translation can't carry is rhetorical weight my framework is right to refuse, or something real about international politics my framework is too thin to see. Don't fix the lessons up to make them translate; I want an honest inventory.
