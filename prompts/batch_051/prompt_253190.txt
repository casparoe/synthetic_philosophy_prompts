I keep meeting the same argumentative move in conversations about voting, and I would like to know whether it has an established name and, more importantly, how it should be carved up. The move runs like this. A friend concedes that no single ballot in a large election will decide the result, and that the chance of casting the pivotal vote is effectively zero. Then he immediately adds that if everyone reasoned this way, nobody would vote, turnout would collapse, and politics would be captured by the most motivated or extreme groups; or else he says that by staying home I would be free-riding on the civic conscientiousness of everyone who does vote. I find this puzzling, because when I decide whether to vote I am not choosing the rule that everyone will follow. I am choosing only my own act under the actual circumstances, where other people's turnout is whatever it is. I can sincerely hope that everyone else votes, and think their voting is a good thing, while still believing that my own journey to the polling station will change nothing. The pattern seems to be: an individual act is admitted to be causally inert, so the defense shifts to evaluating a world in which the act is generally practiced, even though that generalized world is not one of the options available to the agent and the agent would welcome others performing the act.

I have heard fragments of this discussed near the idea of asking what would happen if everyone did that, near the free-rider problem, and near rule consequentialism, but none of those quite captures the specific tension in the voting case, where one's own act remains inert under every actual scenario and the desirable generalized outcome is built out of acts one is not in a position to bring about. Is there an established concept for this exact move, namely defending an individually inefficacious act by appealing to the value of its universalization, and how exactly does it differ from the familiar universalizability test, from the free-rider problem, and from rule consequentialism? If there is no settled name, I would like you to propose one, define it, and give boundary cases: does the same move arise with recycling, boycotting a product, or voluntarily paying a tax that is hard to enforce, and where does it cease to be the same phenomenon or cease to be a legitimate justification?
