Draft the joint statement that a committed Mencian and a committed Xunzian could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on human nature. By a Mencian, I mean the view that human nature is good in the sense that people are born with incipient moral dispositions—sprouts of compassion, shame, deference, and a sense of right and wrong—which education and self-cultivation bring to maturity. By a Xunzian, I mean the view that people are born with self-regarding desires and impulses that, left to themselves, produce contention, and that virtue consists in acquired patterns of deliberate artifice, ritual, and instruction working on that recalcitrant material. Format the document as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. It should contain: (a) substantive claims about human nature, moral development, and the effects of ritual and teaching that both sides can sign without qualification; (b) the precise residual disagreement, stated so that each side would accept the wording of its own position; (c) two or three cruxes—sub-questions such that an answer would move at least one side; and (d) for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides would agree bears on it, such as spontaneous responses to a child at a well, children raised with minimal adult moral instruction, or controlled instruction in ritual, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Separate genuine disagreement from the merely verbal: if some of the dispute turns on different senses of 'good', 'nature', or 'moral', identify that part and give the reformulation that dissolves it. Take definite positions on the wording and on where the substantive disagreement ultimately lies; do not simply survey the options or conclude vaguely that both are partly right.
