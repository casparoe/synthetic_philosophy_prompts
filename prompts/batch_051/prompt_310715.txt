The same fight comes up whenever my group discusses a randomized trial: one of us holds the individual-equipoise line, the other the clinical-equipoise line. The first says a physician can enroll a patient in a trial only if that physician is herself genuinely uncertain which arm is better. The second says what matters is honest, unsettled disagreement in the expert community, so an enrolling physician may go ahead even with a personal lean, as long as the field hasn't converged. After a year of this we've agreed to produce the adversarial-collaboration document we should have written at the start. Write it for us.

Give us the document itself, not a commentary on it:
1. A numbered list of claims we can both sign — about what equipoise is supposed to protect, what our views share, and what each view commits us to in practice. Make these substantive enough that each of us flinches at least once while signing, not just 'we both oppose exploiting patients.'
2. The sharpest fair statement of each view, worded so that the other person, after reading it, says 'ok, that is my position stated at full strength, not a strawman.'
3. A short disagreements table: the issue, what individual equipoise says, what clinical equipoise says, and whether the practical upshot actually differs.
4. Two or three cruxes — sub-questions such that settling one would force at least one of us to revise. I suspect one crux is whether an enrolling physician's private hunch is a piece of suppressed evidence or an irrelevant private state, and another is what to make of a response-adaptive trial where the allocation algorithm already assigns, say, 71% of new patients to arm B but the trial hasn't crossed its stopping boundary. For each crux, give the concrete case we would both agree bears on it, and record in advance what each of us predicts about it.
5. A definite closing verdict on whether any part of our disagreement is purely verbal, and if so, which part and how you can tell.

Take definite positions and phrase thing's concretely enough that we'd know exactly what we were signing and which of us would have to cave if a crux came out the other way. I'd rather have your best shot, with a note on where you're least sure of the formulation, than a neutral menu of options.
