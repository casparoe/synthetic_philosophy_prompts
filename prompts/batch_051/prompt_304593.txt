I've been trying to get clear on everyday conventions by logging my gut verdicts on cases. Not tipping at a sit-down restaurant in the US: wrong, even if the service was just okay, because the server's pay depends on it. Trying to tip in Tokyo after a meal: also wrong, even though I meant it nicely—it embarrassed the staff and I was refusing to read the situation. But a visitor to the US who doesn't tip because nobody tips in their country is also wrong, even though that feel like the mirror image of my Tokyo case. Cutting the airport security line when your flight is boarding: fine if you ask and people wave you through, not fine if you just push past. Letting one friend join me in a queue: fine; letting four friends join: not fine. Jaywalking across a completely empty street in a German city at 3am: fine, honestly, though I wouldn't do it in front of small kids. Is there a principle that captures all of these, or nearly all? What does it say about a tourist in London who pushes ahead at the pub because "that's how it works where I'm from," and which of my verdicts, if any, have to go?
