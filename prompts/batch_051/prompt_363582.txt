I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so my terminolgy is probably off. I've been trying to write ensemble scenes properly and I've realised I keep needing a theory of how people do things together. So here's my attempt. I call it a 'first-person-plural intention'. The claim: a joint action just is a set of individual actions in which each agent's intention already has the group built into its content. When you and I carry a wardrobe upstairs, I don't intend that I lift my end; I intend that we lift it. The 'we' is inside my head, not hovering between us. And that's the whole of jointness: no promise, no common knowledge, no mutual obligation, no shared plan. Each of us just has an intention whose content is plural. I'd even say it counts as a joint action if each of us privately believed the other was being coerced into helping — so long as each still intended 'that we move it'. That seems to explain how groups act without a group mind, and it suits me as a novelist because I can write an ensemble action without needing to know what any other character really thinks; each character carries the plural around in their own skull.
