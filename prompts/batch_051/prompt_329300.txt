I'm taking a module on self-reference and formal systems, and last week we did Hofstadter's Typogenetics. Quick setup in case it's not fresh: strands are strings over A, C, G, T; enzymes are encoded by strands and carry out operations (cut, insert, copy, and so on) on other strands. You can build a strand which encodes an enzyme that, when applied to that same strand, copies it exactly. Hofstadter spins this as more than a toy: it's meant to be a so-called DNA–Gödel isomorphism, where a base-string 'says' something about itself in roughly the way a Gödel sentence does.

Please argue that the Typogenetics self-replication result is not a genuine case of Gödelian self-reference: it's really just a string that happens to encode a copier, and copying isn't a semantic relation, so calling the two isomorphic overates the analogy.
