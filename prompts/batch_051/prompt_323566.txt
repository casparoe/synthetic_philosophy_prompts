I've been reading through some Husserl on my own, and Ive ended up turning the epoché into a standing personal policy, not just a philosophical method. I want you to tell me whether this is defensible or whether I'm just dressing up my own introspection with a fancy name.

Here's the policy as it actually operates. Whenever I catch myself answering before I've really looked at the thing—an argument with my partner about whether some pattern was really there, a political disagreement, a moment when I cant tell whether I believe something or just want to—I try to perform a kind of everyday epoché. I suspend the natural attitude: I set aside the question of whether the objects of my beliefs exist independently, whether my standing theories are true, whether the causal story I tell myself about why I think what I think is accurate. I dont deny any of it; I just hold it in abeyance. Then I attend only to what is actually given in my experience: what's evident, what's merely posited, what's added by interpretation, what I can't actually make present when I try. The idea is to stop my metaphysics from settling questions about my experience before I've even had the experience.

My friend, who actually studies this stuff, says Husserl's epoché was a method for doing transcendental phenomenology and for studying the structures of consciousness—not a device for deciding whether your partner is being fair or whether your political view is just ideology. She says my version is probably a way of making my hunches feel authoritative while I ignore exactly the kind of evidence, about the world and about my own causal history, that would correct them. I see her point, but I also don't think I'm using it to settle empirical questions outright; I'm using it to check what I'm actually conscious of before I go back to ordinary reasoning.

Act as a philosopher who knows both the Husserlian epoché/reduction and ordinary epistemology, and think carefully before answering. Maybe start by separating the historical method from my homemade version so I can see the difference. Is my policy defensible as an epistemic habit? When does it help, when does it fail, and is there a restated version that keeps whatever is good in it without becoming a shield for my prior beliefs?
