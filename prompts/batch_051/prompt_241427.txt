As a journalist who covers philosophy and the behavioral sciences, I have been turning over an asymmetry in ordinary judgments about recognition and self-respect. We tend to admire the person who insists on being acknowledged—the colleague who threatens to quit unless publicly credited, the activist who demands that an institution name its debt—as someone with self-respect. We tend to describe the person who quietly accepts a subordinate role and throws themselves into the work as servile, or as lacking self-respect. Yet when the same people talk about how anyone actually becomes good at something, they sound as though the truth runs the other way: mastery comes from doing the work, from letting the craft shape you, not from demanding credit. So ordinary culture seems to locate the seat of selfhood in the demand for recognition, while simultaneously locating the real engine of self-development in patient labor that defers recognition. I want to know whether this asymmetry can be given a principled defense, or whether it is simply a tension we live with.

Hegel’s Phenomenology of Mind, §178, seems to offer a natural resource for defending the ordinary view: SELF-CONSCIOUSNESS exists in itself and for itself, in that, and by the fact that it exists for another self-consciousness; that is to say, it is only by being acknowledged or “recognized”. On its face, this says recognition is constitutive of self-consciousness, so the person who demands recognition appears to have the right of it. But the later movement of Lordship and Bondage is usually read as complicating, even reversing, that appearance: the lord receives recognition from a bondsman he does not recognize, while the bondsman, through work, comes to see his own agency in the thing he has formed and develops a more enduring kind of self-certainty. My question is whether the ordinary asymmetry—admiring the demand for recognition over the quiet labor that does without it—actually tracks Hegel’s argument, or whether the dialectic shows that the asymmetry is an inversion we would be better off dropping.
