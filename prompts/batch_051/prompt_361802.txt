I just got out of a three-hour argument with a friend about discounting the future, and I can't figure out whether our disagreement was real or just about words. My verdicts, as near as I can tell, are these. If someone offers me $100 now or $100 in a year, fully guaranteed, no inflation, and no way to invest the money, I'm taking it now and I don't think that's irrational. But if you ask me whether a death in 200 years is less bad than a death tomorrow, I want to say no, they're equally bad. I also think a benefit that goes to people born in 150 years matters just as much as the same benefit now, unless the later benefit is genuinly uncertain; if the future is risky I'm happy to discount for that, but that feels like risk, not time. Meanwhile my friend pointed out that I'd pay five bucks to get a package one day earlier this week, but I wouldn't pay five bucks to move a big project payoff from five years to four years and eleven months, even though it's the same one-day difference. I had no idea what to say. Is there a coherent principle that captures all of these, or do I just hold a messy version of pure time preference? And if there is a principle, what does it say about the one I keep going back and forth on: whether I should care less about my own far-future self's pleasures than my current ones?
