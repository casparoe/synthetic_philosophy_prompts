The setup. The republican notion of freedom as non-domination distinguishes itself from plain negative liberty with the case of the slave and the non-interfering master. Negative liberty apparently says the slave is free, since nobody is stopping him doing anything; the republican says no, he is still unfree because the master could interfere any time, without asking, and that standing itself is a loss of liberty even if the power is never used. The thought experiment seems to work because the master is a person with a will and discretion: we can imagine him deciding to make things worse, so the slave lives under an arbitrary power that negative liberty has no vocabulary for.

Now swap the master for a ranking algorithm, a credit model, or a platform feed. It has the same kind of life-shaping power but no will. It decides what jobs, flats, prices, and claims you ever get to see; it manages the menu before you choose rather than blocking any specific choice. It can wreck your prospects by never surfacing them, and there is nobody who decided against you and no one to petition. It has power over you that seems arbitrary in the relevant sense, but it has no intentions, no discretion in the human sense, and arguably it never interferes at all. Does the republican argument survive this swap?
