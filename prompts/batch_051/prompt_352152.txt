I am writing a paper contrasting two ways of formalizing what it means for an AI system to avoid side effects, and I need a thought experiment built to order. The first formalization is reachability-based: an action counts as higher-impact to the extent that it changes the set of states the agent can subsequently reach, as measured by a reasonable distance metric over states. The second is utility-preservation-based: an action counts as higher-impact to the extent that it changes the agent's ability to maximize each of a large set of auxiliary reward functions, compared with an inaction baseline. Please construct a small, deterministic, one-paragraph scenario—a household or warehouse robot, not an abstract grid-world—in which the two measures rank two available actions in opposite order: the action the reachability measure ranks lower-impact must be the one the utility-preservation measure ranks higher-impact. Both actions must accomplish the agent's assigned task, and neither may be what an ordinary observer would call a disaster. The auxiliary reward set must not be rigged: it should include at least one goal unrelated to the task that a human might incidentally care about, and the divergence should fall out of the structure of the environment rather than from tuning the metric or the rewards. The case is meant to show that the two definitions do not merely track each other, so the rankings must be generated by the scenario rather than stipulated. After presenting the scenario, (a) walk through how each measure scores each action and why, (b) identify the structural feature of the scenario that drives the disagreement, and (c) explain how a defender of the reachability measure would resist the result.
