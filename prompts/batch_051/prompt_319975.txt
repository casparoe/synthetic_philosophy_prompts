Write a satirical modest proposal of roughly 650 words, in the deadpan voice of a constitutional-drafting consultant submitting a position paper to a newly elected constitutional convention, for ending interregna for good. The convention has convened because the country's last succession crisis was resolved badly, and the consultant's pitch is that it never has to happen again. The proposal should urge the convention to replace discretionary emergency powers, caretaker governments, and ad hoc necessity arguments with an exhaustively mechanical succession schedule that determines who is head of state at any instant by running down a comically complete list of ordinary civic ranks and personal attributes — the longest continuously registered voter, the oldest sitting notary, the national chess champion, the owner of the lowest-numbered address in the capital, and so on — until the schedule ends by wait-listing every adult citizen in order of registration, so the state can never fail to have someone. The target is the drafting mentality that treats a constitutional gap as a mere failure of determinacy and imagines that the cure is always a more exhaustive schedule, rather than entrenched conventions, independent custodians, or a candidly residual element of discretion. The humor should exaggerate real features of that mentality — the baroque succession schedules, the conflation of being able to name someone with that person's being fit or entitled to govern, and the way every gap-filling rule relocates the gap to the question of who is empowered to certify that the schedule's factual conditions have been met — without ever stating these objections outright. The irony should stay deadpan and legible but never winked at, and the piece should remain in character throughout. Write it as continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write a formal letter.
