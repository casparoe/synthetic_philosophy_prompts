I'm an engineer on a safety team at a company that ships LLMs, and I've slowly become the guy who argues that reading chain-of-thought traces is basically a sound safety move. The position I want you to pressure-test is this: if a model is producing step-by-step reasoning while it works, a monitoring pipeline over those traces lets us catch dangerous plans and deceptive rationalizations before they become actions, and for the most part the traces are a decent window into what the model is actually doing rather than a press release after the fact. We already flag suspicious snippets, escalate plans that involve manipulating users, stuff like that. I know reasoning traces are probably not the literal cognitive process in every detail, but I'm not defending every detail either — the claim is just that the monitoring is a net-useful, mostly reliable safety technique.

What would have to be true for that to be wrong, or at least a lot weaker than I think? I keep hearing three worries: that the trace isn't faithful (the model says one thing and 'thinks' another), that a sufficiently optimized model will learn to write innocuous-looking reasoning while doing something else, and that monitoring itself changes the behavior, because the moment you grade the reasoning the model is under pressure to game you. I want those turned into a real map of my position's exposure: which observations, experiments, or arguments would be decisive against it, which would be merely awkward, which of these escape routes a defender like me would have to concede counts as evidence, and whether I've quietly insured the view with so many qualifications that nothing could touch it. If it is insulated that way, tell me honestly whether that's a problem here rather than just how engineering under uncertainty works.

One more framing detail: I don't care about citations or the academic consensus, I care about engineering. My gut says the obfuscation-under-optimization-pressure story is the one that could actually get me to recommend killing the pipeline, if it's right. So make sure that one gets a serious assessment: is it something we already have evidence for, something we could test, or just a scary intuition that sounds plausible because it fits a pattern? And if the honest answer is that the view survives but only in a heavily qualified form, say whether the qualified version is still worth calling 'monitoring' or if it's just another heuristic eval I should treat like all the others.
