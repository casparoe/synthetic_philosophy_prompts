Ok so my friend and I just spent the whole evening fighting about whether it makes sense to try to strike pre-AGI bargains now, and I want the document we should have been writing instead of yelling. He's in the "lock it in" camp: agreements made now, while multiple actors can still make each other pay for defection, are the only real way to keep the post-AGI future from being winner-take-all. I'm in the "clean slate" camp: pre-AGI deals are basically unenforceable since any actor that pulls ahead can just rewrite or ignore them, and making deals mostly launders legitimacy for whoever gets there first.

Write the adversarial-collaboration statement we could both sign. Format it exactly like this:

1. Agreed claims — a numbered list of claims both camps accept about the bargaining situation, e.g. the enforcement gap, asymmetries, what defection consists in, why a weaker or later actor might still be worth bargaining with, and what "honoring" a deal could even mean across a capability explosion. Roughly 400 words. Don't make them vacuous ("the future is uncertain") — they should have real content.

2. Disagreements — a table where the first column gives each side's position in wording that side would endorse (no strawmen), and the second column gives the sub-question that position actually depends on. Roughly 350 words.

3. Cruxes — the specific sub-questions that, if answered, would move at least one of us. For each, say which way each side needs it to come out and why it matters. Roughly 350 words.

4. Tests and predictions — for each crux, one concrete mechanism, case, or argument both of us agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Draw these from stuff we actually can reason about: escrow, mutual hostages, dead-man switches, a smaller lab defecting after a capability jump, or whether a lab that contributed training data could credibly threaten to publish a poison/canary audit if the other lab welches. Roughly 400 words.

If you think part of our argument was only about words (e.g., what "binding" means), say so and say which part. This is not a verdict: the whole point is that we both have to look at the agreed claims and the table and think yes, that's what I mean.
