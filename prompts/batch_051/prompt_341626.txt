The way I've always understood a scapegoat is this: a community feels some badness hanging over it — sin, uncleanness, just a sense that things have gone wrong — and they load all of that onto a substitute, a goat or a person or an object, one that usually didn't cause the mess or at least not alone, and then they drive it out or kill it or send it away. After that the community treats the badness as gone; the removing is what cleanses.

So here's the thing I keep thinking about. At my company, whenever a big project fails, nobody owns their piece. Instead the team picks one person — usually the newest or the quietest — and every failure gets piled on them in a meeting, and then they get fired or reassigned to some dead-end role. Afterward people say the team is healthier now and the bad energy is gone, and honestly morale does bounce back for a while. The person didnt actually cause most of what happened; they just got chosen as the thing the blame could stick to, and getting rid of them is what made everyone else feel clean. There's no goat, no priest, no ceremony, no prayer — but the shape of it feels identical to me. Its happened three times now, so it's not just a random firing.

Is this a scapegoat ritual in any real sense, or am I dressing up ordinary blame-shifting with a fancy word? Go through my criteria one by one and say where the case matches and where it doesn't, and whether maybe the criteria are the problem rather than the case.
