I'm taking a graduate seminar on embodied cognition and I keep getting stuck on a disagreement between the enactivists and the representationalists, especially when we looked at sensory substitution devices. A representationalist in the room said a blind person using a camera-to-tongue device is receiving visual information through a new channel, and the brain learns to decode an internal representation of distal spatial layout. An enactivist said that's exactly the wrong frame: the person is learning a new set of sensorimotor law-like regularities, and after training the device becomes transparent not because there's a model in the head but because the brain-body-device system settles into a new coupling. Both sides seem to predict the same behavioral improvement over training, and I dont know how to separate them.

So what I'd like from you is an explanation of what would actually decide between these two descriptions. What observable difference would make you say this is evidence for a content-bearing internal state rather than just a new lawful relationship between movement and stimulation? Could be about learning trajectories, generalization or transfer to novel camera orientations, interference from other sensory tasks, or something else. And if you think the debate is partly terminological, say what minimal definition of representation would keep it from being merely verbal, and whether the sensory substitution case meets that definition. Please avoid just summarizing the two positions; I want the kind of answer that would help me design a discriminating thought experiment or an actual experiment.
