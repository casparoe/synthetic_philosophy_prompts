I've been turning over a question that sits exactly at the boundary between the vocabularies I can think in precisely and the ones I can only gesture at, and I'd like a systematic translation rather than a loose set of analogies. I am a longtime reader of rationalist-adjacent material and work on reinforcement learning and control, so the concepts available to me include: objective functions and reward shaping; policies, including learned, amortized policies compiled into fast, low-deliberation mappings and open-loop controllers; value estimation; gradient-based and nongradient optimization; exploration versus exploitation; Goodharting; specification gaming; meta-level versus object-level choice; and the idea of training a system indirectly so that a desired behavior becomes the deployed policy rather than the continuing result of deliberation. The view I want translated is wu-wei, usually rendered as effortless action or acting without forcing, together with the paradox of trying not to try. On the descriptive side there is a claim: the expert performer does the right thing without holding an objective consciously in view — the swimmer, the archer, the person who has become the kind of person who acts a certain way — and deliberate self-monitoring reliably degrades that performance. On the prescriptive side there is a paradox: explicitly deciding to be spontaneous, effortless, or natural is self-defeating, so the recommendation cannot be to aim at effortlessness directly. The proposed resolution, as I understand it, is indirect self-cultivation: practice and gradually reshape the person so that the right action arises on its own, in accord with the situation, instead of being forced by an objective pursued in the moment.

What I want from you is a faithful restatement of each of those three pieces — the descriptive claim, the paradox, the proposed resolution — in the control and reinforcement learning vocabulary above, followed by the part I expect to be more interesting, the residue report. For each piece, say what translates cleanly, what can be translated only if you distort it, and what has no expression in the vocabulary at all. Keep the translation honest by anchoring it in a concrete case: a policy that has been trained until it no longer has to plan through a task at deployment. Where exactly does that picture stop being an adequate model of wu-wei, and why? Then give me the verdict I actually want. Is the residue — for example, anything about there being no separate self that does the trying, or about some actions being forced while others follow the grain of things — evidence that the control/RL framework is missing something load-bearing, or evidence that wu-wei, once its poetry is refused, collapses into automaticity plus a preference for low-interference action? I would rather be told that a piece cannot be expressed in the vocabulary than have it quietly reinterpreted until it fits.
