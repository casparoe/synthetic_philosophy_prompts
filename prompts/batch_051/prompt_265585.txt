I'm in a seminar on Borges and philosophy, and we spent a whole week on A New Refutation of Time. I keep going back and forth on wether the ending contradicts the rest of the essay or is the whole point of it. On my reading, Borges starts by admitting he has glimpsed a refutation of time that he himself doesn't believe, but that keeps returning to him with the force of an axiom. Then he actually runs the refutation: using Berkeley and Hume, he says there is no single time connecting everything, that nothing exists outside each present moment, and that the self is just a series of perceptions. But then in the last paragraph he swings the other way. He says that denying time, the self, and the universe looks like a desperate gesture but is really a secret consolation; that our fate isn't frightening because it's unreal, it's frightening because it's irreversible; that time is the stuff he is made of; and that the world is real and he, unfortunately, is Borges. Those are basically two contradictory positions sitting in one essay. I can see candidate reconciliations: maybe he never actually endorses the refutation, since he flags at the start that he doesn't believe it; maybe the ending is only about lived or personal time while the refutation is aiming at metaphysical or serial time; maybe the whole thing is dialectical and the ending is him recovering from a temptation he performed on purpose. But I'm afraid some of those are just ways of smoothing it over. I'd like a committed answer: pick the reading you think is most faithful, say which of the reconciliations is special pleading and why, and tell me whether the final paragraph undermines the refutation or is what the essay was building toward. If you genuinely think it's just a contradiction with no honest way out, defend that too, but then say what kind of contradiction it is and why Borges would have left it in.
