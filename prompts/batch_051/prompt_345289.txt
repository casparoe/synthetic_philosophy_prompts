ok so in my philosophy of social science class we've spent three weeks on methodological individualism vs holism and I'm starting to get frustrated, because the argument has completely stalled. The individualists keep saying that any social explanation has to bottom out in facts about individuals—their beliefs, desires, choices, interactions—and that anything that doesn't is incomplete or just a placeholder for a mechanism we haven't found yet. The holists keep saying that there are social-level facts (norms, institutions, network structure, segregation patterns, suicide rates) that aren't just summaries of individuals, because they constrain and partly constitute what any individual can do, and if you try to reduce them you lose the thing you wanted to explain. What's weird is that everyone seems to agree on the empirical facts in any particular case—a bank run, a norm that persists even though nobody individually wants it—and they only disagree about whether the explanation is finished.

Last week we tried to settle it with a toy case: two groups of a hundred people with identical preferences and resources but different social network structures, where one ends up with a stable cooperative norm and the other doesn't. The individualists said: same people, same preferences, so the difference has to be in how their interactions aggregated—still individuals all the way down. The holists said: the network structure is exactly the kind of social fact that constrains each person's options, and describing all the nodes and edges doesn't explain why the norm stuck. Both sides walked away more convinced.

Our prof says this debate has gone on for ages and nobodies moved much. My working theory is that it might not be an empirical dispute at all—maybe one side is using 'explanation' to mean giving a causal mechanism and the other to mean capturing a pattern or regularity, or maybe there's a hidden formal question about reduction and supervenience underneath, or maybe the word 'explanation' is just doing to much work. Can you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, engaging with what the two sides actually say? And given that diagnosis, tell me what would actually count as progress—a distinction, a formal result, a type of case, a change of question—or whether I should expect it to stay stuck forever.
