I am a philosophy professor preparing a seminar on the ethics of persuasive technology, and I would like help articulating what a run-of-the-mill design practice is philosophically committed to. I will describe the practice in detail because the details matter.

Consider a mobile video-sharing application with the following features. On first launch, notifications are enabled by default, and disabling them requires working through several nested settings screens. The home feed has no chronological option and no end; it is ranked by a machine-learning system whose sole measured objective is predicted time spent watching. When a video finishes, the next one begins after a short delay unless the user intervenes. The product team occasionally varies the length of that delay and the order of recommendations, even when the underlying candidates are of the same kind, in roughly the way a slot machine varies outcomes. Users are never asked whether they are satisfied with the time they spent, and the team's A/B tests treat a change as an improvement just in case it raises total watch time.

My question is not whether these practices are manipulative, but what theory of agency, welfare, and attention they presuppose. Do they strictly presuppose a particular view — for instance, that revealed engagement tracks welfare, that users' momentary decisions are authoritative, that attention is an extractable resource rather than a capacity for autonomous choice, or that leaving an option available is enough to make influence legitimate? Or are the practices compatible with several rival theories? I would also like to know whether the elements are mutually consistent: can a system coherently treat users' clicks as revealed preferences while also treating their attention as something to be harvested through variable rewards? And what in the design would have to change if the presupposed view were replaced by, say, the view that attention is a fiduciary resource held for the user's own ends?

Please distinguish carefully between what the practice strictly presupposes, what it merely fits well with, and where it is philosophically underdetermined. For each main claim you make, indicate how confident you are, and close by singling out the point you are least sure about.
