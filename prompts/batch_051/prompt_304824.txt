Bertrand's paradox asks for the probability that a chord taken at random in a circle is longer than a side of the inscribed equilateral triangle. The standard presentation observes that three natural procedures disagree: choosing two endpoints uniformly on the circumference gives 1/3; choosing a radius and then a point uniformly along that radius, and drawing the perpendicular chord through that point, gives 1/2; and choosing a point uniformly in the disk to serve as the chord's midpoint gives 1/4. Now change one feature of the setup: the chord is to be compared with a side of the inscribed square rather than the inscribed equilateral triangle. Work through each of the three procedures under this changed criterion, computing the three probabilities exactly. Then assess what the changed pattern of agreement and disagreement does to the usual diagnosis of Bertrand's paradox as showing that the phrase at random is under-specified and that the principle of indifference cannot by itself settle a unique answer.
