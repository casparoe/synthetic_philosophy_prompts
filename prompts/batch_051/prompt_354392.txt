Here's the thing I keep going back and forth on. Morphology textbooks treat inflection and derivation as cleanly different: inflection gives you forms of the same lexeme (walk, walks, walked), derivation makes a new lexeme (walk, walker). A linguist friend insists the boundary is psychologically real — speakers genuinely file inflected forms and derived words as different kinds of thing, and "word" has a real edge. I suspect it's a bundle of criteria that falls apart the moment you push on borderline cases. Take English -ing: in "she is walking" it looks inflectional, but in "walking is good for you" it starts acting noun-like in some respects while staying verb-like in others. How would you test the friend's claim? Design the strongest concrete test you can, say which result would pull you toward each side, and be explicit about what a clean result still wouldn't settle about where a word ends. I'd rather you commit to a design and a verdict than lay out the options.
