I am a novelist, and I am currently drafting an essay about my long-standing reluctance to describe certain kinds of play as art, even though the people who design games speak of their craft in the same language I use about fiction: structure, pacing, tone, implication, revision. My difficulty is not with any particular title or genre but with a conceptual question I cannot settle alone: whether the appreciator's exercise of agency can be part of the artwork itself. If it can, then a game in which the player's choices shape the experience might be art in a sense that a painting is not, and I need to understand what that difference is. If it cannot, then games may contain art without themselves being art, and I owe my friends who make games an account of why the interactive elements of their work stand outside the work.

I would therefore like a working definition of art, the one you would actually defend, tested against the following cases, which are the ones that trouble me:

1. A conventional, single-authored print novel. I take this to be a clear case of art, and I want the definition anchored by it.
2. A branching-path novel in which the reader selects among numbered passages; the text is fixed, but the order of events depends on those selections.
3. A tabletop role-playing campaign lasting several months, in which one participant designs scenarios and non-player characters while the others improvise the speech and decisions of their own characters; afterward, the campaign exists only in notes, dice rolls, and recollections.
4. A single-player video game with carefully authored landscapes, sound, and writing, in which the player's decisions determine which of several endings occurs.
5. A newspaper crossword puzzle.
6. A professional basketball game, watched by a spectator.
7. An elaborate building constructed by a player inside a sandbox game and opened to other players to walk through; the building tools are the developer's, but the design of the structure is the player's.
8. A composed chess problem in which White is to mate in three moves and the composer intended one particular solution.
9. A poem generated anew each time a program is run, from rules and word lists written by a poet who never sees the particular outputs.

I believe case 1 is clearly art and case 6 clearly is not, and I hold no settled view about the others. My strongest intuition is that the relevant difference has something to do with whether the work is completed by its maker or completed by the person who encounters it, but I have not been able to turn that intuition into a definition.

Please propose a definition, run it through all nine cases, and say explicitly where it delivers the answer I expect, where it delivers an answer I did not expect, and where it gives no verdict at all. If the definition produces surprising results in several cases, either revise it or defend the surprises. I am less interested in settling whether games in general are art than in arriving at a definition whose verdicts I can accept case by case. Please write your answer in American English.
