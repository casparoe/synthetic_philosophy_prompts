I cover science and ideas for a national magazine, and I serve as the community representative on my country's maritime rescue review board. We are reviewing an incident from last winter: a rescue vessel rated for 400 people was already carrying 370 survivors when it encountered a raft with 60 people aboard in deteriorating weather. The captain took 30 aboard and left the remaining 30 with a dropped life raft, supplies, and a locator beacon, judging that taking all 60 would risk swamping the vessel; the 30 left behind died before another vessel could reach them. The board is split between those who hold that a rescue crew's duty to people already in its care can justify refusing some newcomers, and those who hold that only the number of lives saved or lost matters. Draft the board's written decision: make a definite finding on the captain's conduct, recommend a concrete rule for future rescues, and engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side.
