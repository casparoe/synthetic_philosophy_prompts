I've been going down a G. E. Moore rabbit hole lately, mostly because a friend and I got into an argument about whether AI-generated paintings can be genuinely beautiful, and I realized Moore is wierdly relevant to it. For context: Moore held that beauty and personal affection (friendship, roughly) are among the few things good in themselves, and he had an organic-unity view where the value of a whole isn't just the sum of the value of the parts — a beautiful object plus someone's appreciative contemplation of it can be worth more together than each would be alone. He was writing in the early 1900s, so he never had to think about generative models, recommendation feeds, or parasocial attachment at scale.

Here's the question. If Moore were alive now, what would he most likely say about two things: (a) art made by a diffusion model that is, by any normal perceptual standard, as beautiful as good human-made art but has no human maker or expressive intention behind it; and (b) relationships with streamers or AI companions where one side feels genuine affection and shared history but the other side is either a crowd-facing performer or not a person at all. For each, give your best prediction of his view and, more importantly, the chain of reasoning from his commitments that gets you there. Then tell me which of the two predictions you're more confident in, and what additional fact about Moore's system (if any) would flip the other one.
