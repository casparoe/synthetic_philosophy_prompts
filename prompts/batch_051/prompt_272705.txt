I am a PhD candidate in economic history, and the pivotal chapter of my dissertation defends a contrastive question of roughly the form: why did the breakthrough to sustained economic growth occur in Britain rather than in China? My supervisor has pressed me to show that the question is well posed before I build anything on it, and I have collected six ways of answering the objection that such contrastive questions presuppose a shared developmental path and are therefore ill posed. Please rank these six positions from most to least defensible as a response to that objection, and give one sentence of justification for each placement. The positions are: (1) the question is unproblematic once the contrast class is made explicit, since Britain-rather-than-China is no more built into it than China-rather-than-Britain would be; (2) it should be dissolved into the question why sustained growth emerged anywhere at all; (3) it should be reposed at sub-national level, comparing specific regions such as the lower Yangzi and the English midlands rather than two reified national units; (4) it is only a hypothesis-generating heuristic, not a well-posed question with a determinate answer; (5) it is unanswerable, because answering it would require unconstrained counterfactual histories in which China industrializes; and (6) it should be replaced by a mechanism-level question about which specific impediments to growth were present in each case and where they were removed.
