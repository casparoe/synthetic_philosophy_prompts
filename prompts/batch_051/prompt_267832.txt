I've been arguing with a friend about Maxwell's demon, and he summarized the modern "information physics" resolution for me like this: "The demon shows information is physical. It sorts slow and fast molecules to make one side hot and one side cold without doing work, which looks like a violation of the second law. But Landauer and Bennett showed in the 1960s that the demon has to erase its memory to keep sorting, and erasing bits costs at least kT ln 2 of heat, so the second law survives. The real upshot is that information is just another physical resource, and you can trade it for work up to the Landauer limit." I've seen this exact story repeated on rationalist-adjacent blogs enough that I suspect there's something a little too tidy about it, but I don't have the physics to say where.

The passage I want to use as a check is from Maxwell's Theory of Heat (1871), the section near the end where he introduces the being. I'll paraphrase from memory because I don't have the page in front of me: he asks you to imagine a being whose faculties are sharp enough to follow individual molecules, standing at a small hole between two chambers A and B. It opens and closes the hole so as to let only faster molecules pass from A to B and only slower ones from B to A, with no expenditure of work, so B gets hotter and A gets colder. Maxwell says this is in contradiction to the second law, but he presents it as a limitation on the second law's range rather than as a puzzle about memory or information.

What I want is an accuracy audit of my friend's summary against that passage and against the actual physics. Go claim by claim: which parts would someone working on thermodynamics of computation accept, which parts are overstatements or anachronisms, and whether the memory-erasure resolution actually answers what Maxwell was pointing at or answers a different, later problem. I'm especially suspicious of the slide from "the demon must erase its records" to "information is physical" as a general slogan. Is the Landauer limit a real lower bound that rescues the second law, or is it one mechanism among several, and does Maxwell's original being even need to keep records in the way the modern account assumes? Also, if Maxwell framed the demon as a limit on the second law rather than as a paradox to be dissolved, does that change how fair it is to call the memory-erasure story "the resolution of Maxwell's demon"?
