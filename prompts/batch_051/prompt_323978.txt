i'm taking an environmental history course this term and we kept going in circles last week about whether things like plagues and volcanoes can be called "historical actors" without that just being a metaphor. I came away with a homemade test I've been using in my section posts, and my TA basically said it was either trivial or false and wouldn't tell me which, so I want it stress-tested.

I call it the "unabsorbed intervention" test. A non-human thing counts as an actor in history, not just a background condition, only if three things are true. First, you can point to a specific intervention in time and place — the plague arriving in Europe, a particular big eruption like Tambora — something that actually happened on a datable-ish schedule. Second, the society it hit didn't already have routines or institutions roughly prepared for that kind of event, so the response had to be improvised after the fact rather than edited from an existing playbook. Third, the thing keeps "answering back" over time: people change their practices in response to it, and those changed practices then change what it does next — quarantine reshapes an epidemic's path, disrupted trade reshapes which places are vulnerable next, that sort of loop. By this test the Black Death is an actor and the Little Ice Age isn't: the Little Ice Age wasn't a punctual intervention, and communities sort of absorbed it through existing seasonal flexibility and slower adaptation, whereas a plague behaves more like an adversary that responds to your moves.

I genuinely can't tell whether I've built a real distinction or just relabeled "big cause with a date attached" as "actor." Conditions two and three seem to be doing the work, but I might have rigged them so only fast, punctual non-human things can pass, which would make the test vibes-based and sort of circular. Could a slow climate shift ever pass my test, or should a good test allow for slow actors too? And am I basically reinventing environmental determinism with extra steps, or is there a defensible version of this that doesn't turn humans into puppets of germs and ash?
