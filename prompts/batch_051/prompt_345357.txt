A state's compulsory education code says: 'Every child between the ages of six and sixteen shall attend a public school, a licensed private school, or a home instruction program in which a parent or guardian provides instruction only to the children of that one family.' In one city, twenty families have formed a cooperative: they rent a former shopfront, hire a retired teacher to instruct their children full time, and send their children there five days a week, while each family files the annual home instruction notice and lists the hired teacher as a tutor assisting the parents. Determine whether each child is attending a licensed private school, enrolled in a home instruction program, or neither, under the words of the code, the purpose of the three permitted alternatives, and what the drafters would likely have said had they foreseen cooperatives of this kind; then say which of those approaches should control the outcome and why.
