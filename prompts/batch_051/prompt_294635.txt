I'm a PhD student in political philosophy and I've got myself stuck on sweatshops. My supervisor keeps telling me to stop arguing case by case and write down what an account of exploitation is actually for, but I'm realising that's the hard bit. The live fight is usually framed as whether a deal can be both a genuine Pareto improvement for employer and worker *and* exploitative, and papers talk past each other because they never state what they need the theory to deliver.

Before I read anything else, I want the full desiderata for an adequate theory of labour-market exploitation—especially the mutually beneficial kind, where the worker is better off than she would otherwise be and it still seems wrong. The cases it can't fudge include: (i) a worker who freely takes a factory job that's better than her rural alternative but pays less than the value of what she produces and keeps her in conditions she'd reject if the gains were shared differently; (ii) an employer who helps worsen the worker's outside options and then offers a job that's an improvement over those now-worse options; (iii) a one-off rescue where the helper acts only at a price that feels extortionate but leaves the rescued person better off; and (iv) the firm that's simply the only employer in town, with no manipulation at all. Which distinctions can't be collapsed? I'm thinking exploitation vs coercion, exploitation vs unfairness, exploitation vs mere inequality, and maybe exploitation of a person vs exploitation of their situation. Which empirical constraints—like the actual size of the factory-vs-rural wage gap—should a theory have to respect, and which should be treated as beside the point?

Then the part I actually need: which single desideratum knocks out the most popular accounts—unfair-price views, coercion views, and the view that a voluntary Pareto improvement just can't be exploitative by definition? Are there pairs of desiderata that can't plausibly be satisfied together, for instance non-paternalism about voluntary exchanges and the idea that at least some real sweatshop contracts are exploitative? Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's prejudice. I'm after the spec with the cases behind each requirement, not a reading list.
