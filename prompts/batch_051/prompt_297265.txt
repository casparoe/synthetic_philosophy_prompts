The no-trade theorem is widely taken to show that a market cannot aggregate purely private information through trade alone: if traders share a common prior, are commonly known to be rational expected-utility maximizers, and there are no noise traders, then a willingness to trade that is motivated only by informational differences should unravel, because the offer itself reveals information. Now consider a market in which most order flow comes from machine-learning systems trained on different proprietary datasets, using different model classes and retraining schedules; such traders cannot plausibly be said to share a prior or to be mutually transparent reasoners. Does the no-trade theorem survive this development, does it need repair, or does it simply fall outside its scope? Identify which premise carries the conclusion, and say whether some weakened version of the theorem still delivers its original epistemic lesson about the limits of markets as information-aggregating institutions.
