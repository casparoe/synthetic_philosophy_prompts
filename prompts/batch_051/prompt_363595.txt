I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology, and I've been voluntold to referee an adversarial collaboration in our decision-theory group between a thirder and a halfer on Sleeping Beauty, and every time I try to draft the thing myself I notice I've loaded the dice toward the thirder. Could you draft the document they'd both actually sign?

Setup, in case you want it restated: on Sunday Beauty is told the protocol and put to sleep. A fair coin is flipped. Heads means she's woken only on Monday. Tails means she's woken on Monday and Tuesday. After each waking she's given a drug that erases the memory of that waking before she's put back under, and the room looks exactly the same each time, so on waking she can't tell what day it is or whether she's woken before. Each waking she's asked for her credence that the coin landed heads. Thirders say 1/3, halfers say 1/2.

What I need is the joint statement, formatted roughly like this:

- a numbered list of claims both sides can sign — the setup, her Sunday credence, what she can and can't remember, what the self-locating evidence question even is, anything they genuinely share and that isn't vacuous;
- a disagreements table where each row names the sub-claim, then gives the thirder's wording and the halfer's wording, each phrased so its own side would sign it;
- the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions where settling one would actually move at least one of them;
- for each crux, a concrete case, argument, or variant both sides would except as bearing on it, and what each side predicts going in.

One thing to get right: the betting stuff is messier than people pretend. Some halfers will say their credence stays 1/2 but that you'd still take different betting odds in a repeated version, because Tails buys you two bets, so don't let the document treat betting as cleanly decisive unless both sides sign off on that framing. I also half-suspect part of the fight is verbal — something about what the evidence she has on waking means, or whether an uncentered proposition like the coin landed heads can get evidence from a centered fact like her being awake now — so I'd want you to flag which disagreements are genuine and which are really about words.

If the whole assignment is misbegotten — if you don't think there's a single document both sides could honestly co-sign, or the crux table would just be fake precision — tell me that flat out instead of filling it in politely. And keep it in American English.
