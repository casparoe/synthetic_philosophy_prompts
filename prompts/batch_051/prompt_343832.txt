I've been arguing with a friend who sits on a city council and has become taken with an idea she calls "lexical threshold budgeting," a municipal implementation of lexical threshold negative utilitarianism. The rule she wants to propose has three parts. First, the city must commission an annual survey identifying every resident currently experiencing suffering above a set severity threshold: severe untreated chronic pain, street homelessness during the winter, serious untreated mental illness, and comparable conditions. Second, the budget is lexically ordered. No new spending on quality-of-life amenities—parks, libraries, public art, festivals, street repairs that are merely cosmetic—may be enacted while any above-threshold suffering remains unrelieved, no matter how many residents would enjoy the amenities or how cheap they are. Third, above-threshold suffering may not be compensated with below-threshold pleasure: in the council's calculations, a single episode of severe pain cannot be outweighed by any quantity of mild enjoyment. Once every above-threshold case is either relieved or fully funded for relief, the council may revert to ordinary cost–benefit analysis for everything below the threshold.

The implementation details are left deliberately loose: the threshold is set each year by a committee of clinicians and social workers; "fully funded" is judged by the council's own analysts; and the city cannot plausibly relieve every above-threshold case in a single budget cycle, so the freeze on amenities may prove indefinite. I want you to treat this not as a moral position but as a mechanism. Walk me through how it would actually behave if enacted.

Who is incentivized to do what—residents, service providers, the clinicians who set the threshold, the council's budget analysts, local businesses, neighboring municipalities—and how would each of the key definitions be gamed? Where would the rule break first, which failure modes could be patched without abandoning the lexical structure, and which cannot? Finally, say whether the mechanism's predictable behavior would tend to confirm or undermine the philosophical idea it encodes—that severe pain is lexically prior to lesser pleasures and cannot be rendered commensurable with them—and whether the experiment would show the theory to be unworkable or merely this implementation to be naive.
