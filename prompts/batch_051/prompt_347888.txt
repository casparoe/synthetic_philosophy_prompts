Assume for the sake of the question that an omnipotent, omniscient, and perfectly good God exists, and that there is an afterlife in which each person ends in exactly one of three ways: eternal conscious torment, annihilation, or eventual reconciliation to God. I want your actual credence in universal reconciliation, understood as the proposition that the third outcome obtains for everyone and no one is everlastingly tormented or annihilated. I currently put this at about 0.35; a colleague puts it at 0.85. Do not just report a number: decompose the question into the sub-claims that drive your credence, give a rough probability and a one-sentence justification for each, and state how they combine. The decomposition should at least cover whether a finite lifetime of wrongdoing can merit an infinite or irreversible punishment; whether divine punishment is essentially retributive, remedial, deterrent, or something else; whether a free person under full knowledge can finally and permanently reject God; whether annihilation would satisfy divine justice better than unending conscious torment; and whether perfect love can coexist with anyone being lost permanently. Then tell me which single sub-claim your final credence is most sensitive to, which one most plausibly explains the gap between my 0.35 and my colleague's 0.85, and what evidence or argument would move that sub-claim most.
