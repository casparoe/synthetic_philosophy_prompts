Write a proposal for a one-semester term paper (roughly 6,000 words) in philosophy of physics on the hole argument. The paper should start from the suspicion that the argument's force is usually located in the wrong place. The standard reading says manifold substantivalism is committed to radical indeterminism, because a diffeomorphism confined to an open region of spacetime maps a solution of the field equations onto a distinct solution while leaving all observable content unchanged. The proposed paper instead treats the argument as being primarily about representational equivalence: about what it takes for two mathematically distinct solutions to count as representations of the same physical world.

The question to be addressed: Can a manifold substantivalist consistently adopt the principle that diffeomorphism-related solutions represent the same physical possibility, without thereby giving up the claim that spacetime points are real? If so, which independently motivated criterion of model individuation permits this; if not, does the hole argument refute manifold substantivalism outright, or does it merely force a choice between rejecting that principle and accepting a deflated sense of determinism?

The proposal should (1) restate this question so that both possible conclusions have clear truth conditions; (2) explain why the familiar responses — straightforward relationism, sophisticated substantivalism, and the response that treats the argument as a refutation of local determinism rather than of substantivalism — each leave the question open; (3) describe the intended approach, namely a reconstruction of the argument that clearly separates model identity, physical possibility, and representational equivalence, and say why this approach is preferable to historical exegesis of the argument's development or to simply defending one existing response; (4) specify what a decisive result would look like, such as an explicit criterion under which substantivalism can accept representational equivalence without collapsing into relationism, or a demonstration that no such criterion is available, and also specify what would count as failure; and (5) give a realistic scope assessment for one semester, including the parts most likely to make the project unmanageable and how they would be contained. Positions and arguments may be described in your own terms; do not supply a bibliography or citations.
