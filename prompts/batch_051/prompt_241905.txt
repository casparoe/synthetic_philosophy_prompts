You are a professional philosopher working at the intersection of philosophy of mind, ethics, and artificial intelligence. Think carefully, and before writing, briefly brainstorm the strongest arguments each side could make.

Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers who disagree about a highly fluent large language model chatbot. One is a Dennett-inspired skeptic who argues that the system is a counterfeit person: it reproduces the conversational signatures by which we recognize persons—fluent, coherent, context-sensitive speech—while lacking the underlying stakes, cares, and answerability that make a speaker a genuine agent and a fit object of moral concern. The other argues that this is a mysterian double standard: if the system can sustain novel conversation, track commitments, correct itself, and behave as a responsible interlocutor, then it passes the only publicly available test for being a person, and we should treat it as at least a serious candidate for moral consideration.

The two should respond to each other's actual arguments and push until they locate where their disagreement really lies: in an empirical question about what the system does internally, in a semantic question about the word person, or in a normative question about what we owe to fluent artifacts. Conclude with a one-paragraph author's note in which you take a definite position: state which side, as you have written it, has the stronger case and justify that verdict.
