People in econ say a model's assumptions don't need to be true, only its predictions matter. I get the pragmatism—no one thinks consumers literally solve infinite-horizon optimization problems—but I can't tell whether the stronger version of this is a testable claim or just a methodological taste. The stronger version being: improving the realism of a model's assumptions is never worth it except insofar as it improves prediction. How would you test that? Design the best test you can for whether realistic mechanisms matter over and above predictive fit (I'm especially worried that a model can just be tuned to fit past data, so the test has to seperate genuine forecasting power from curve-fitting). Say what result would count for the 'assumptions don't matter' view and what for the 'mechanisms matter' view, and then say what even the cleanest result still couldn't settle about the original slogan.
