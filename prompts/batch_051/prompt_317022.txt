Write an essay on the following controversy about the boundaries of sentience. Suppose someone holds a broadly computational or functionalist criterion of sentience: a physical system has morally relevant experiences just in case it implements a certain abstract computation C, where C is a specified information-processing structure—for instance, one in which information is integrated across subsystems and made globally available for behavior. Now consider the attribution problem. Any sufficiently complex physical system can be mapped onto indefinitely many computations, depending on how one individuates and groups its physical states; under one mapping the system might accurately be said to implement C, while under another mapping it might accurately be said to implement only a trivial automaton. If nature does not single out one of these mappings as privileged, then whether a system is sentient—and therefore whether it falls within the moral circle—appears to depend on a human choice of description.

Your essay should weigh the strongest responses available to a defender of a computational or functionalist criterion of sentience. Consider, in particular, the proposal that only mappings satisfying certain causal, counterfactual, or parsimony constraints count as admissible, and the competing proposal that morally relevant computations must be fixed at a more basic physical or causal level rather than by abstract mapping. Reach and justify a definite conclusion: for instance, that the attribution problem is fatal to computational criteria of sentience; that it is serious but soluble once admissible mappings are specified; or that it exposes an ambiguity in the concept of moral status rather than a defect in computationalism. Defend the verdict you judge best, and do not end by merely listing considerations on both sides.

End the essay with a final line of the form \answer{...} whose contents are a short label for the position you defended, such as \answer{fatal}, \answer{soluble}, or \answer{misplaced}.
