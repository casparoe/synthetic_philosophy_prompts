I've been thinking about AI risk on my own for a while and I've landed on a view I want someone to try to kill. The view: the profit motive — basically the engine of capitalism — is the root cause of the danger from advanced AI, not just one factor among many. The companies racing to build frontier systems are doing it for money and market position, so they cut corners on saftety, ship things before they understand them, and give systems more autonomy because that's cheaper than paying humans. If AI were built outside that logic — one group that isn't trying to beat rivals or get rich, or a society not run on profit — the dangerous dynamics would mostly disappear. I'm not saying misalignment is impossible in principle. I'm saying the version of the problem that actually threatens us is downstream of the incentives.

What I want from you is a map of what would count against this. Not the lazy "authoritarian states can be dangerous too" line, unless that line is actually stronger than I think — then defend it properly. Sort the defeaters from fatal to merely awkard, and say which ones a committed profit-motive person would have to concede are real counts against the view and which they'd dismiss. Also pull apart "root cause" from "accelerant": what would have to be true for profit to be only gasoline on a fire that would burn anyway? What should my view predict that a "profit is just one contributing factor" view wouldn't, if anything? And be honest about whether the view is set up so it can't lose — if every counterexample can be rewritten as "well, that was still about profit," is that a problem, or is that just what it means to think something is structural? I'd especially like you to address cases where very dangerous technology got built without much profit motive, like the Manhattan Project, and whether those actually cut against the view or not. Also, the view can be read a few different ways — profit as the historical origin of the race, profit as the thing keeping the race going now, profit as the reason individual engineers and executives ignore safety — so tell me which reading each defeater actually hits.
