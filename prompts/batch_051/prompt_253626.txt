Here is a way of reading Pascal on justice and force that I have been developing in my own work, and I would like an honest assessment of it. I think Pascal runs together two claims that should be pulled apart. The first — call it the enforcement claim — is that a rule or right which no power stands behind will not actually govern; justice without force behind it is inert. The second — call it the constitutive claim — is that what a community regards as just simply is the prevailing custom that has succeeded in attaching force to itself, so that there is no independent fact of justice left over. My proposal is that Pascal needs only the enforcement claim, and that the constitutive claim is an additional, much more sceptical commitment. If I am right, one can concede that unenforced justice is inert while still maintaining that a force-backed custom can be unjust — and unjust in a way that is not merely the complaint of some rival force. Assess this: reconstruct the two claims as charitably as you can, say whether the constitutive claim is genuinely independent of the enforcement claim or somehow forced by it, and give the hardest counterexample you can devise to my position, with your verdict.
