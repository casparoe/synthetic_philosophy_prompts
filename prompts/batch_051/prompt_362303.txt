I've been trying to work out, on my own, what actually makes a person incorruptible. I don't mean just refusing bribes in the narrow sense; I mean a broader kind of unbendability in judgment, where a person can't be bought, worn down, flattered, frightened, or slowly desensitized into doing what they believe is wrong. I've ended up with five candidate ingredients, and I can't tell how much of the work each one is really doing; some of them even seem to pull apart when I test them against cases.

Please rank the five ingredients below from most to least essential to counting someone as incorruptible. Then rate each one on a five-point scale for how unambiguously good it is for a person to have it to a high degree, and give a sentence or two for each explaining its place in the ranking and its rating.

The five candidate ingredients:

1. Bribe-immunity: no amount of money, and no threat to livelihood or safety, will make them knowingly do what they think is wrong.

2. Non-drift: their standards do not slide over time; small daily compromises, a corrupt surrounding culture, and the gradual redefining of what is acceptable do not move their baseline.

3. Faction-blindness: they hold friends, allies, and people they admire or depend on to the same standards as everyone else, and they do not excuse wrongdoing just because it was committed by someone on their side or someone they love.

4. Temptation-absence: they simply lack the appetites that corrupt people in the first place; the pull of money, status, or comfort is weak or absent, so refusing costs them little.

5. Cost-endurance: when their refusals bring real social costs—mockery, ostracism, being seen as self-righteous, a ruined career—they absorb those costs without revising the decision.

Two things matter to me here. First, I'm asking about essentiality: which ingredient would be hardest to remove while still calling the person incorruptible. Second, I'm asking whether an ingredient can be essential without being good—for example, whether non-drift can become rigidity, or whether temptation-absence makes the conduct less admirable because the person isn't actually resisting anything. So after the ranking and ratings, say which ingredient you think is most overrated as a mark of integrity, and why.
