hey—I'm in an Islamic philosophy class, and we're just read al-Ghazali's *Tahafut al-Falasifa*, the Seventeenth Discussion (the one refuting the necessity of the natural course). The bit that's stuck in my head is where he goes through a list of standard causal pairs—drinking and quenching thirst, eating and satiety, contact with fire and burning, the sun's appearing and light, decapitation and death, medicine and healing—and says the connection in each case is not necessary; it's only God's habit, so the effect is created directly by God while the cause is just an occassion.

I tried to sort out my own verdicts on the examples, and they're all over the place. Fire on cotton: I actually side with Ghazali. I can imagine the cotton not burning, and calling it necessary seems like I'm projecting my expectations onto the world. Drinking and thirst-quenching: also with Ghazali, no problem. Sun and light: with Ghazali, I can easily think darkness at noon. Medicine and healing: with Ghazali, in fact it's easiest here because medicine often fails.

But then decapitation and death: I don't see how I can give him this one. If the head is genuinely severed from the body, I don't know what it would be for the person to keep living; it doesn't feel like a mere coincidence that death follows. Magnet and iron: that one feels more like a real power too, though I can't say why. A match struck and flame: I lean toward necessity, but maybe just because I've seen it so often. A seed in soil becoming a plant: I lean toward necessity, though I know it depends on lots of background conditions.

My rough principle was something like: I accept Ghazali where the cause is only an external occasion or a probabilistic/biological process, but not where the cause enters into the physical constitution or mechanism of the effect. But that doesn't work, because fire burning cotton is also a mechanism and I still had no problem with Ghazali there.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or nearly all? And which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? Also, what would it say about a case I keep going back and forth on—a virus entering a cell and the cell dying? Is that more like medicine and healing or more like decapitation?
