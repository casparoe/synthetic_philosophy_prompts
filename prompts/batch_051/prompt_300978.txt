I keep going back and forth on what the word "terrorism" actually picks out, and I want a working definition I can use when I read about insurgent groups and state militaries instead of the usual UN boilerplate. Here are the cases I care about, with my gut verdicts where I have one:

- A rebel group bombs a crowded market to pressure a government into withdrawing its troops — clearly terrorism.
- The same group bombs a bridge at night to cut a supply route; they issue no statement and design the attack so nobody is anywhere nearby — I still want to lump this in with terrorism, but if terrorism is mostly about sending a message to an audience, this looks ruled out, and I dont know whether that means my instinct or the definition is wrong.
- A state air force firebombs a city with the stated aim of breaking civilian morale — I want to call this terrorism, but I know plenty of definitions carefully exclude state armed forces, and I want to know whether that exclusion is defensible or just convenient.
- A resistance movement blows up an empty police station at 3 a.m.; no casualties, but they claim it afterward as a political act — no idea whether property damage with a political message counts, or whether the fact that police are armed agents of the state changes it.
- A lone gunman shoots up a grocery store in a neighborhood he claims he is liberating; no group, no demands, just a manifesto — I think this is terrorism, but it nearly always gets prosecuted as a hate crime, and I don't know if the law is tracking a real distinction.
- An occupying army quietly kills the family of a man suspected of building bombs, as retaliation rather than as a warning — I think this is terrorism, but the condition that terrorism must be aimed at an audience beyond the immediate victims feels shakier here than in the first case.
- Protesters burn a line of police cars during a riot; no one is hurt — I don't want to call this terrorism, but it is political violence intended to intimidate, so the definition has to explain the difference.
- A group hacks a hospital's systems and cuts its power for a day, no one dies, and posts a political demand afterward — no idea.

Part of why I'm stuck is that when I call something terrorism I'm usually both describing it and blaming whoever did it, and I'm not sure those two jobs come apart the same way in every case. So if the definition splits into a descriptive one and a condemnatory one, say which of my cases change status between them. Then run your definition through every case and tell me which verdict is least secure. I don't just want the standard term for the standard view — I want the actual boundary lines drawn.

Format: exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, with no introduction or conclusion.
