In my ethics seminar we've been doing the grit/resilience thing — the original studies, the backlash, the standard replies — and the argument has totally stalled. The anti-grit people say its ideology: it locates failure in individuals so institutions never have to change, and the empirical effects are tiny anyway. The pro-grit people admit the effect sizes are small but say the critique is overblown; treating resilience as mere ideology is basically telling poor kids their effort doesn't matter. Everyone's read the same meta-analyses, so it's not that one side is missing the data. I'm starting to think they're not disagreeing about any fact at all, just using 'virtue' differently or answering different questions. Is that right? What kind of disagreement is this actually — empirical, conceptual, political, or something about what we're doing when we call someone resilient? And if it isn't empirical, would another study ever settle it, or does progress have to come from somewhere else?
