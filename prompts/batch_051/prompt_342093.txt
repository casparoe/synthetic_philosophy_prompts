Several months ago my sister began saying, in an oddly matter-of-fact way, that our mother is no longer our mother. On her account, the woman in our parents' house has the same face, voice, mannerisms, and memories, and performs the role so well that no one else would notice the difference, but the feeling of familiarity my sister used to have around her is simply gone, and the only explanation she can accept is that this person is an extremely good impostor. She will readily agree that a perfect replacement is bizarre and that she cannot explain how it could have been arranged, but saying so does not seem to make her reconsider; she treats the conclusion as something she has had to accept rather than as a hypothesis to test. Her conduct is uneven. She still calls this woman most evenings and addresses birthday cards to "Mom," but she has also told me she plans to move out because she does not want to go on living with a stranger.

She is being treated by a psychiatrist and a neurologist, so I am not asking for medical advice or a diagnosis. What I need help with is how the rest of us should understand what she is saying, because it seems to change how we should respond. Is her statement best understood as a genuine belief — the sort of state I am in when I believe ordinary facts, open to evidence and integrated with the rest of what she thinks — or is it closer to an imagining, an avowal of a changed feeling, or something she says without fully endorsing? I have read that states like hers are sometimes explained by two distinct failures: first, a changed or diminished experience, such as a loss of the felt familiarity of a face, and second, a failure to notice or reject the wild improbability of the explanation one settles on. If something like that picture is right, does it tell us which stance the family should take toward the claim — pressing her with evidence, gently disputing it, redirecting attention, playing along, or simply accepting that she holds it? For each stance, please say whether it fits better or worse with treating her state as a belief, and why. Finally, tell me how confident you are in your analysis overall, and identify the specific step in your reasoning you are least sure about.
