Conceivability is a filter, not a source

The idea that conceiving a scenario is evidence for its possibility faces what I'll call the sorting problem. Nobody holds that every act of conceiving is modal evidence: people conceive of time travel, of squaring circles in dreams, of states that could not obtain. So the conceivability theorist needs a principled criterion seperating modal-reliable conceivings from merely psychological ones. My claim is that every candidate criterion borrows the modal facts it was meant to deliver, and so conceivability can at best reveal commitments we already hold, not supply new modal knowledge.

Take negative conceivability first, understood as failing to detect a contradiction. This is too weak because contradiction-detection tracks the conceiver's logical abilities, not the modal facts. It is negatively conceivable, in this sense, that Goldbach's conjecture is false and also that it is true, but only one of those can be a genuine possibility in the relevant sense, assuming classical logic. So negative conceivability fails to discriminate between p and not-p when both are consistent with everything the reasoner knows.

Positive conceivability is meant to fix this by requiring an actually constructed scenario. But the scenarios we construct are partial: a zombie scenario fills in the behavioural and physical facts and leaves everything else schematic. To know the scenario is genuinely possible, you would need to know that the filled-in details can be completed without contradiction, and that completion task is just ordinary modal reasoning. Positive conceivability therefore does not provide independent evidence; it is the very reasoning it was supposed to underwrite.

The obvious retreat is ideal conceivability: what an idealised reasoner, immune to the limits in the first two cases, could conceive. But 'ideal' is specified either in non-modal terms—perfect logicality, unlimited memory, no biases—in which case it inherits the weakness of negative conceivability, since the claim that water is not H2O is consistent with everything a non-modally-idealised reasoner could detect; or 'ideal' is specified as already knowing the modal truths, in which case the account is circular, or at least stops being a guide and becomes a report.

If all three readings fail, what is left of conceivability as a guide to possibility? A modest diagnostic role. Trying to conceive a scenario can flush out hidden modal commitments, expose the difference between what I thought I meant and what I can actually make coherent, and reveal where I was leaning on a picture rather than an argument. That is real epistemic work, but it is work my existing modal beliefs do on themselves, not work done by conceivability as an independent source of evidence. I conclude that conceivability is a filter, not a source.

One thing I'm specifically unsure about is whether I've been unfair to the positive-conceivability camp by assuming scenarios can't be fully specified, so if you'd push back there, please say where.
