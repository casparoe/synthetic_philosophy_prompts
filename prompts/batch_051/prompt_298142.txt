I serve on my university Faculty Senate's Rules and Governance Committee. Our bylaws contain a single amendment clause: "These bylaws may be amended by a two-thirds vote at any duly convened meeting, provided written notice of the proposed amendment is circulated at least fourteen days in advance; amendments take effect immediately upon passage." Last month the Senate voted, by exactly two-thirds, to amend that very clause to lower the voting threshold for all future amendments to a simple majority and to eliminate the notice requirement. Later in the same meeting, relying on the amended clause, the Senate passed an amendment to the grading policy by a bare majority and without the previously required fourteen days' notice. A minority of senators now challenge both actions: they argue that a rule cannot confer a power that extends to amending itself, so the first amendment was invalid and the grading-policy amendment therefore lacked the two-thirds vote and notice it required. The chair replies that the clause applies to "these bylaws," which includes itself, and that treating the amendment clause as unamendable would permanently freeze the Senate's rules. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate to the committee before we rule. Recommend a concrete outcome for both amendments, engage the strongest version of the self-amendment objection rather than dismissing it as a technicality, and briefly indicate what principle the bylaws should adopt for future self-referential amendments.
