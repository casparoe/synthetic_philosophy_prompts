In my old line of work I fit data with models that assumed measurement errors were independent, and the justification was usually something physical: randomization, or at least an argument that nothing in the setup would couple them. Since retiring I've been reading number theory, and I keep noticing the same move: to estimate how many integers near x are prime, you pretend that divisibility by different primes are independent events, so the probability comes out as the product of (1 − 1/p) over primes p up to √x, even though for a fixed n the events 'divisible by 2' and 'divisible by 3' are not independent at all. I can't decide whether this is the same kind of reasoning I used in the lab, or only the same word. In the lab I could in principle check or engineer independence; in number theory its knowingly false for any fixed n, yet the heuristic seems to give the overall right shape though (if I recall) maybe not the constant. Is the number-theoretic presumption of independence a real analogue of statistical independence in science, or a different licence that happens to look similar? If it's different, what is doing the justificatory work, and are there signs that the heuristic is about to mislead?
