In a graduate seminar on consciousness, we spent three sessions on the eliminativist case against qualia, and I came away convinced that the argument between the eliminativist and the realist is stuck in a peculiar way. The test case everyone returns to is the one about the two coffee tasters. One taster's palate changes over time so that he now likes a flavor he used to dislike; the other's standards change so that he now calls acceptable what he used to call too bitter. Yet both report at the end that the coffee tastes just the same to them now as it did at the start. The eliminativist reads this as showing that there is no stable, private, introspectively infallible feel being tracked, because the subjects cannot tell whether their experience changed or their judgment changed. The realist reads it as showing only that memory and preference are fallible, which no one denies. What strikes me is that neither side contests the behavioral facts, yet neither side has moved in the literature, and each accuses the other of begging the question about what introspection actually shows.

I would like you to diagnose the structure of this impasse rather than settle who is right. Is the disagreement verbal, hinging on what qualia would have to be or on what counts as introspection? Is it a difference about what counts as admissible evidence, with one side treating first-person reports as data and the other treating them as data only when corroborated by third-person measurements? Or is there a hidden empirical question underneath—one about the actual mechanisms by which taste judgments and taste reports are produced—that would resolve the impasse if we knew the answer? My own hypothesis is that the two sides are answering different questions under one heading: one side is asking whether there is an inner felt quality distinct from any set of dispositions, and the other is asking whether a certain theoretical posit earns its keep in a cognitive model. Given your diagnosis, what would count as progress? A conceptual distinction, an empirical result, a formal argument, or is this a case where one side is right that the question is ill-posed while the other side is right that something is being left out, with no possible resolution?
