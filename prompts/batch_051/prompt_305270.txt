I keep having the same argument with a friend about Malthus, and I want you to settle whether his position is even testable. His line is that Malthus was not wrong, just early: he identified a real, standing pressure between population and the resource base, and the only reason the predicted crash hasn't arrived is two centuries of technological substitution and then the demographic transition — which, my friend says, are exactly the kind of escape valves Malthus's framework already left room for. My worry is that this is an unfalsifiable rescue. Everytime the catastrophe fails to show up on schedule, a new auxilliary hypothesis gets bolted on (the New World, then coal, then the Haber process, then falling fertility), and the theory never has to stick its neck out.

So design the strongest test you can of the claim that Malthusian pressure is real-but-latent rather than just false. Make it concrete: what data, comparison, or intervention would you look at, and what pattern of results would count against my friend's reading, not just for it? Then tell me what a positive result would and wouldn't settle. If the test favors him, have we detected the underlying pressure itself, or merely shown that the theory can be made consistent with the record one more time? And where exactly is the line between a long-run prediction that was merely early and one that has been kept alive by epicycles? I'd also like to know if there's any part of the "right but early" move that no test could reach even in principle.
