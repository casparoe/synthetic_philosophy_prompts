My friend Leah works in privacy and has very firm views about whether information about you belongs to you, but she refuses to articulate the principle—she just rules case by case, and I've been collecting her rulings. She said a company that scrapes people's public social media posts to train an advertising model owes them nothing: you put it out in public, so you don't own it downstream. She said patients own their own smartwatch heart-rate and sleep data and should be paid when a hospital sells a de-identified dataset to a drug company; stripping the name doesn't strip the ownership. She said a messaging app that reads private one-to-one chats to decide which ads to show is on the wrong side, and neither person could license the conversation for ads without the other's okay; the exchange is jointly owned. She said a supermarket can sell loyalty-card purchase histories to a data broker because a loyalty program is an explicit swap—discounts for your data—and the store isn't taking anything you didn't hand over, as long as they actually told you in plan language. And she said if a friend writes an autobiography and includes a story you told them in confidence, you should have a veto; a memory about someone doesn't become the rememberer's property.

The new case she's deciding: a maps app tracks your phone's location continuously, even when you aren't actively using it, and sells the movement data to advertisers who can work out your home address, your workplace, and your visits to a particular clinic. It's de-identified, but each person's trajectory is effectively unique, and you did press "agree" to a long terms-of-service that mentioned sharing location data somewhere on page nine. What should she say, or mostly, what would she say if she's consistent with her other rulings? Which of those earlier cases is the closest analog? What principle or principles fit all five rulings, and which of those principles would each pull her toward on the maps case? And if you had one more case to run by her to settle which rule she's actually using, what would it be?
