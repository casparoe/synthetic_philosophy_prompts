I'm trying to sort out what I actually think about when cooperation is rational in one-shot games where players can see each other's code. Take a prisoner's dilema where if you defect while the other cooperates you get 5 and she gets 0, if both cooperate you each get 3, and if both defect you each get 1. If the choices are made in private and simultaneously, I think defecting is just clearly right.

But now suppose each side submits a computer programme instead of a move, and the programmes are given each other's source as input before choosing. Here are my gut reactions.

(a) Both submit programme X: "Cooperate iff the other source is exactly X, otherwise defect." I think both cooperate, and I'd call that rational and genuinely cooperative.

(b) One side submits T: "Cooperate iff the other source has a line at the top saying I-WILL-COOPERATE, whatever the rest does." The other submits U: first line says I-WILL-COOPERATE, and after that it just defects no matter what. I think T is a sucker, and U is rational but not something I'd endorse.

(c) Both submit W: "Cooperate iff the other source contains the same 64-character secret key anywhere in it, otherwise defect." My first reaction is that this is fine, exactly as good as X. But then it nags me: if source is public, the key isn't secret, so U could just paste it into its header and defect. So maybe W is just T with a longer tag.

(d) I also think X is a bit brittle: it won't cooperate with a programme that's behaviourally identical but written differently, even though if they did cooperate both would do better. But I don't think that makes X irrational.

Is there one principle behind these verdicts, something like "a programme is and should be trusted only when passing its test already guarantees the other will reciprocate"? If so, does it force me to give up (c) or the last claim in (d)? And what does it say about Y: "Cooperate iff I can prove, before choosing, that the other programme will cooperate against me"? I keep flip-flopping on whether Y is the right generalisation of X or just a cleverer way to join a club.
