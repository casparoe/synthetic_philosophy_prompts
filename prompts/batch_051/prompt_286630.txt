In our criminal law seminar we’ve been on about the so-called blackmail paradox, and in the Theft Act 1968, s 21(1), it says: “A person is guilty of blackmail if, with a view to gain for himself or another or with intent to cause loss to another, he makes any unwarranted demand with menaces...” Fair enough. But here’s the asymmetry doing my head in. If I ask my flatmate for £100, that’s lawful. If I tell his girlfriend that he’s cheating on her, that’s also lawful. If I say “give me £100 or I’ll tell her”, suddenly it’s blackmail. Why does the word “or” turn two lawful acts into an offence? I get that the statute says the demand has to be “unwarranted”, but that seems to just push the question back: what makes it unwarranted when it’s just a conditional combination of two actions that are each fine on their own? Is there a defensible principle that makes the combination worse than the sum of its parts, or is the law just punishing the use of information as leverage?
