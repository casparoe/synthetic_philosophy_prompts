I run a weekly forecasting/risk meeting at work and I have a rule: every claim that matters has to get a probability attached. If someone says "the vendor will deliver on time," I make them put a number on it, and if they answer "there's no fact of the matter yet" or "its not true or false until it happens," I treat that as dodging and make them commit anyway. A coworker who studied philosophy says my rule is confused: not every statement is true or false—future contingents, borderline cases, presupposition failures, stuff like that—so I'm forcing fake precision onto claims that genuinely lack a truth value. Is my rule defensible as a policy? When is "neither true nor false" a real answer rather than an evasion, and how would I tell the difference in a work meeting? Is there a version of the rule that keeps people honest about committing to a forecast without assuming bivalence everywhere?
