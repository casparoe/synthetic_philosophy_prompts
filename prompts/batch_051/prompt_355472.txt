I serve on the council of a small incorporated community whose founding Charter was adopted in 1973. Article VII of the Charter reads:

"Subject to the equal basic liberties of all members, an arrangement by which some members come to hold greater shares of the community's primary goods than others is permissible only if (a) the offices and positions through which those greater shares are gained are open to all under conditions of fair equality of opportunity, and (b) the scheme of inequalities is to the greatest expected benefit of the least advantaged members, measured over the whole course of their lives and taking into account income, powers and prerogatives of office, and the social bases of self-respect."

The Charter was drafted at a convention whose delegates chose principles behind a veil of ignorance; Article VII was intended to apply a Rawlsian difference principle. The drafters imagined inequalities arising from occupations, offices, land, and inherited advantage. They did not anticipate the proposal now before the council.

A member-founded medical cooperative has developed a heritable cognitive enhancement. It proposes to sell three quarters of the places in the treatment queue at a high market price, and to allocate the remaining quarter by lottery open to every member-family. The sales revenue would endow a trust providing childcare, tutoring, healthcare, and apprenticeships for the least advantaged families. The council's own modeling shows that the least advantaged would have a greater expected lifetime package of primary goods under this proposal than under either a ban or a fully lottery-based distribution. But the resulting cognitive inequality would be written into children's bodies and would be visible before any later redistribution, and some members would enter adulthood already marked as having lost the lottery.

Does Article VII permit this arrangement? Work through (i) a plain-meaning reading of the text, (ii) a purposive reading rooted in the difference principle, (iii) a reading based on what the drafters would likely have said had they been asked to write the clause for this case, and (iv) a reading based on what a reasonable member would understand the Article to require. Then say which of these readings should govern and why, and state what, if anything, would have to be added to the Article for the answer to be clearly yes.
