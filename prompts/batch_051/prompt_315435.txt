I've spent the last few months deconstructing my own religion and fell down a cognitive-science-of-religion rabit hole, and the piece I can't shake is agent detection: the idea that humans run a hair-trigger system for seeing a someone (a mind, an intention) behind ambiguous events — rustling bushes, a sudden illness, a storm that flattens one house and not the neighbors. So try this counterfactual on for size. Take early Homo sapiens, same language, same grief, same fear of death, same tribal social instincts, same everything you need, except their agent-detection threshold is freakishly high: they essentially never infer an agent unless they can actually see or hear the thing doing the action. No invisible watchers, no ancestor spirits behind bad luck. Now run the clock forward through agriculture, villages, cities, early states. Do we still get anything like religion — gods, ancestor cults, taboos backed by watchful spirits, shaman/priest specialists — or do we get a world of non-religious explanations and rituals only? I care less about your final yes/no than the chain: which of those features collapse without invisible agents, which survive through some other route, and how confident you are in each link. What would have to be different about the rest of human life for the answer to flip?
