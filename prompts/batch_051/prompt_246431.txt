If grammatical categories such as parts of speech, case, and prepositions were natural kinds, we should expect linguists to converge on them as features they discover in languages, much as chemists converge on the elements. In practice, however, linguists disagree about whether some languages have adjectives at all, whether English has a dative case, and whether postpositions are a distinct category from prepositions. Does the fact that these categories are contested and revised across languages show that they are merely descriptive conveniences instead of natural kinds?
