I'm a retired physical chemist, and I've recently started reading philosophy of mind for fun. The thing that's been nagging me, though, comes from a puzzle habit I've had for decades. I like those little letter-string analogy problems: abc goes to abd, so what does ijk go to? The obvious answer is ijl — you change the last letter to the next letter of the alphabet. Fine. But the moment I stray from the tidy examples, the rule I thought I'd extracted stops being a rule.

Give people abc → abd and then kji, and the answers split. Some say lji: they treat kji as abc written backwards, so the letter playing the role of c is k, and k's successor is l. Others say kjj: "last letter" is last letter, so i becomes j. I can feel both answers at once and flip between them without changing my mind about anything. With xyz, "next letter" runs out after z — some people wrap around to a, some refuse the puzzle, and some quietly switch to a different operation altogether. And with strings like aaabbbccc, even "the last letter" or "the same position" seems to bend: people start grouping the letters into runs, or reading the string as a pattern rather than a list, and the operation follows whatever grouping they settle on.

So the phenomenon I'm trying to name is this. In these tiny puzzles, the concept I extract from the first pair — "last", "next", "same position" — doesn't stay fixed; it drifts into a neighbouring concept to make the analogy work, and the puzzle doesn't determine which drift is correct, even though the answers don't feel arbitrary. Its not ordinary ambiguity, where a word has two fixed senses; the concepts themselves seem to reshape under pressure. Does this have a name in the analogy or concepts literature? If it does, I'd like the carving too, not just the label: where does a legitimate reshaping end and a non-sequitur begin? And if it doesn't have a name, what would you call it, and what would count as a clear case versus a borderline one?
