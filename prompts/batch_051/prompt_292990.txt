For the last two years I have been convening a faculty reading group on the normativity of promising, and it has settled into two stable positions that now mostly argue past one another. The first position, call it the assurance view, holds that promissory obligation is ultimately a matter of expectations I knowingly create: when I promise you that I will φ, I lead you to rely on my φ-ing, and the wrong of breaking the promise is, at bottom, the wrong of disappointing an expectation I voluntarily induced. The second, call it the normative-power view, holds that promising is the exercise of a power to place myself under a new, directed obligation to you; the obligation is created by the act itself and does not consist in anyone's expectations, even if expectations ordinarily accompany it. Both sides accept that paradigm promises bind, that a bare intention does not, that the promisee can in some sense release the promisor, and that promising differs from predicting, threatening, and merely raising expectations; beyond that point their formulations slide around, and I suspect some of the disagreement is verbal while some is real.

I would like you to draft the document this group should have produced after an adversarial collaboration. It should open with a numbered list of claims both camps can sign, each formulated so that neither side would call it slanted — including what the camps jointly accept about how promises bind differently from predictions or threats, what release consists in, whether reliance matters, and where the boundaries of the disagreement lie. Then provide a disagreements table. Each row should state the precise sub-question on which the two camps divide, formulated neutrally; give each side's position in wording its own proponents would accept; identify at least one concrete case or argument that both sides agree bears on that question; and record each side's prediction about it in advance. The cases should include at least a promise to someone who cannot form expectations, a case of detrimental reliance without a promise, and a promise from which the promisee has released the promisor. The document should not summarize who said what in the literature, and it should not cite sources; it should be the text the two camps could actually put their names to, with the residual disagreement made exact and the merely verbal part of the dispute identified as such.
