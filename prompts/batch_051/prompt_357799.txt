I've been going in circles trying to figure out why I care about some things that don't have an obvious payoff. It started when my cousin said our family's New Years tradition of eating this awful pickled fish (everyone hates it, we all complain, but we still do it every year) is pointless and we should just order pizza. I defended it, but the only reasons I could come up with were "it's tradition" and "grandma would be sad." After that I noticed I do the same thing with other words like "the truth" and "pure" or "tainted," and my gut gives different answers on different cases without any clear rule.

Here are the verdicts I want someone to find the pattern behind. If my best friend said something mean about me behind my back, I'd want to know, even if it would only hurt and wouldn't change anything I'd do. But if someone offered to tell me a random true fact, like how many ceiling tiles are in the gym, I could care less. If I found out our family's story about great-grandpa being a war hero was mostly made up, I'd probably correct the record. But I'd never sit my little cousin down to tell her the truth about where her pet hamster actually went. The fish tradition I want to keep, but I suspect that's only because grandma is still here and it's one steady thing every year, not because tradition itself matters. A school tradition where seniors humiliate freshmen I'd vote to end tomorrow; saying "it's traditional" means nothing to me there. A washed, basically new shirt from someone who did something monstrous I wouldn't wear, it feels tainted. But my friend who flips out when her salad touches her pasta and calls the plate contaminated is just being fussy. And I genuinely admire people who turn down a scholarship because the money comes from a company they think is evil; that feels principled, not superstitious.

Is there one principle behind all of these, or do I need two or three? My best guess so far is that truth, tradition, and purity only matter when they're protecting something else, like a relationship, or my sense of who I am, or not being associated with something wrong. But that seems to predict I shouldn't want the truth about my friend, since it wouldn't protect anything. Do I have to drop that verdict? And what does your principle say about the case I keep flipping on: I can take a genetic test to find out whether I'll likely get a disease that runs in my family, decades from now, with no prevention available. Do I want that truth?
