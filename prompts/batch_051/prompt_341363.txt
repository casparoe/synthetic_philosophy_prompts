I’m currently taking an upper-division Kant seminar, and we’re reading the *Critique of Pure Reason* with special attention to the antinomies. In the Preface to the First Edition, Kant frames the whole problem this way:

“It thus falls into confusion and contradictions, from which it conjectures the presence of latent errors, which, however, it is unable to discover, because the principles it employs, transcending the limits of experience, cannot be tested by that criterion. The arena of these endless contests is called Metaphysic.”

The four antinomies seem to do quite different work: the first two appear aimed at ruling out the theses by showing they assume the world exists as a determinate whole, while the third and fourth seem designed to show that both sides can be saved if one distinguishes appearances from things in themselves. I want you to give me your actual credence—a number between 0 and 1—that at least one of Kant’s four antinomies succeeds in diagnosing reason’s conflict as a structural symptom of the latent error described in the passage, rather than merely exposing an ambiguity in terms like “world,” “simple,” “cause,” or “necessary.” I’m not looking for “it depends on how you read Kant”: I want a number and a decomposition.

Please decompose that number into the following subclaims, give each a rough probability, and show how they combine: (1) at least one antinomy presents a genuine conflict that cannot be dissolved by ordinary conceptual disambiguation alone; (2) that conflict is best explained by reason’s extension of a principle of experience beyond possible experience, rather than by something local to that antinomy’s concepts; (3) the resolution of the strongest antinomy commits us to transcendental idealism, not merely to a cautious epistemic modesty about unobservables; (4) the third antinomy, rather than the first, second, or fourth, is the strongest vehicle for this conclusion. Then state which subclaim your final number is most sensitive to, and say what evidence or argument would most move that number in either direction. Please answer in American English.
