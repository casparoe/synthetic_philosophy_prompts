Write a dialogue between James F. Thomson and Paul Benacerraf about Thomson's lamp. The lamp is off at t=0. The switch is flipped at t=1/2, then at t=3/4, then at t=7/8, and so on, so that infinitely many flips occur before t=1. Thomson has just given his argument, in the paper 'Tasks and Super-Tasks', that the lamp's state at t=1 is impossible: it can't be on, because every time it was turned on it was later turned off, and it can't be off, because every time it was turned off it was later turned on. Benacerraf is trying to convince him he's mistaken, not by denying the set-up but by denying that the earlier times determine the state at the limit.

Don't make either side a strawman. Thomson should press the intuition that if the lamp ends up in any state at t=1, there must have been a last flip before t=1 that left it in that state, and Benacerraf should press the underdetermination point, that the rule governing times before t=1 simply doesnt settle what happens at t=1, so there is no contradiction, just an incompletely specified function. They should respond to each other's actual points, not talk past each other, and the dialogue should get fairly technical.

After the dialogue, include a short separate paragraph saying which argument or consideration would most change your own assessment of who has the stronger position, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. Use British English throughout.
