Suppose that maybe thirty years from now, reliable backwards time travel turns out to be possible, but the universe turns out to be strictly single-timeline: you can travel to the past, but whatever you did there is what already happened. No branching timelines, no alternate worlds you can jump to, and no erasing and re-running. Obviously the very first thing everyone does is run the grandfather experiment: someone tries to kill their own grandfather before their parent is conceived. I want your actual prediction for what they observe, not just the one-liner 'they fail.' What do the failures look like? Do guns jam, do bullets miss, does the would-be killer has a heart attack, or does the attempt turn out to be impossible to even formulate? How would you tell apart the universe actively blocking contradictions from the universe simply being such that the contradictory action was never part of history in the first place — and does that distinction even have empirical content, or does it collapse into being the same experience either way? Then push it further: what does this world do to deliberation and responsibility? If your attempted murder was guaranteed to fail from before you got in the machine, are you still blameworthy for it? Which of these predictions are you confident in, which are you basically making up, and what data from the first few years of time travel would most change your mind?
