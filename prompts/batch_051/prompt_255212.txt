Im in a cognitive science course on embodied cognition, and the same argument keeps breaking out every week. The professor shows a little wheeled robot that turns toward the brightest light in the room, and half the class says it represents the direction of the light (or the location of the charging dock), while the other half says no, it's just a closed sensorimotor loop and you don't need to posit any representation. Then she shows a second robot that builds an internal map of the room and plans a path, and basically everyone agrees that one represents. The fight is about where the line between those two cases is—basically the representationalism-vs-enactivism debate in miniature.

Here's the version I want to work through. Robot B has two light sensors, no memory, and a hardwired rule: the difference between the two sensor readings directly sets the wheel speeds, so it turns toward the brighter side. In a fixed lab room with one lamp over the charging dock, it reaches the dock every time. Robot A has an internal map and a path planner, and it also reaches the dock every time. My labmate says the sentence 'Robot A represents the room; Robot B does not' is an empirical claim about the two systems, not just a choice about how we talk: if you stress both robots in the right ways, their behavior will come apart, and the difference is what representation buys you. I'm not so sure.

Design the strongest test (or a small battery of tests) you can for whether a system like B represents something—say the direction of the light, or the dock's location—rather than merely being coupled to its environment. Be concrete about the manipulations (moving the lamp mid-run, occluders, mirrors, new rooms, delaying or crossing the sensor signals, whatever you think matters), what you would measure, and what pattern of results would count for and against the claim. Then say what the tests still could not settle even in principle. For example, if B passes every behavioral test you throw at it, is the enactivist still allowed to say 'still no representation', and would that be a principled point or just moving the goalposts?
