I'm a PhD student working through Berkeley's Three Dialogues, specifically the master argument in the First Dialogue. As I understand it, the argument is roughly this: I cannot conceive of an object except as something I conceive; therefore I cannot conceive of an object existing unconceived; therefore there is something incoherent in the very idea of a mind-independent object. The objections I have collected so far from the literature and from discussion are these: (i) the act/content objection — the property of being conceived attaches to my act of conceiving, not necessarily to the object conceived; (ii) the de dicto/de re equivocation — one can consistently say, in a de dicto sense, that I conceive an object that is unconceived, without thereby attributing the property conceived to the object itself; (iii) Russell's point that a definite description may pick out an object without the object's being presented to me as conceived, so that conceiving the object no one is conceiving need not entail that the object is conceived; and (iv) the overgeneralization or parity objection — the same reasoning would prove that I cannot conceive of any object existing outside my mind, but that is just the thesis in dispute, so the argument begs the question. I have the feeling that there is a further, distinct objection I have not included, one that does not merely recombine these, perhaps one that presses on what conceive must mean for the argument to work. What would that be? Please identify what seems to you the strongest candidate for a missing objection, being careful that it is not a restatement or special case of (i)–(iv).
