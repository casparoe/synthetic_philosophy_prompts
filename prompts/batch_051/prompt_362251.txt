Hey — I'm a retired chemist and I've gotten really into political philosophy lately. I sit on my city's public health board, and we're stuck on something that I'm pretty sure is a textbook public reason problem. Last month we were debating whether to fund a harm-reduction van — needle exchange, naloxone, that kind of thing. One member argued in the meeting that scripture requires us to vote no because making drug use safer would make us cooperators in sin, and he says it would violate his conscience to set his religious convictions aside when casting a vote. Some other members wants us to censure him, or at least adopt a rule that board members may only offer reasons any reasonable citizen could accept, with religious justifications excluded. The chair asked me to draft a short policy memo for the whole board recommending what our policy should be. I've read some Rawls on public reason, but I need a document we can actually vote on next month.

Could you write the memo? Act as a professional political philosopher. Take your time to think through the strongest arguments on both sides before committing. The memo should give a clear, actionable recommendation, treat the strongest consideration on the losing side fairly, and include two or three concrete criteria the board can actually apply to tell acceptable from unacceptable arguments.
