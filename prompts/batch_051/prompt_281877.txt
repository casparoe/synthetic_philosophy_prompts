Some personal context: after spending more time than I would like comparing the career path that would be best for me with the path that seems to do the most good from the standpoint of everyone affected, I found myself going in circles. Both standpoints feel, from the inside, like full rational authority; they just recommend different futures. This led me to Sidgwick's dualism of practical reason. As I understand it, the argument runs roughly as follows. Rational prudence and universal benevolence are each, considered alone, a fully general method for deciding what one ought to do. When the two methods conflict, there is no third, higher principle that arbitrates between them, yet the intuitive demand for a single all-things-considered verdict remains. So practical reason turns out to be divided against itself, and Sidgwick took this to be a fundamental, unresolved division in reason rather than a merely difficult trade-off.

The later development I want to press against it is the apparatus of modern decision theory: incomplete preferences, vector-valued objectives, Pareto dominance, and the standard practice of either accepting partial rankings or completing them with weights that no principle selects. Under that apparatus, the situation looks straightforwardly representable as two utility functions over the same options, and a choice guided by both looks like multi-criteria optimization. My question is whether Sidgwick's argument survives this development or dissolves into it. Specifically: which premise is actually under pressure? Is there an important difference between having two utility functions that are each already complete rankings of the same acts and having multiple dimensions of a single good, and does the decision-theoretic translation quietly assume the latter? If the dualism still survives, I want it stated as something weaker but still troubling; if it dissolves, I want to know why the original worry still feels like more than ordinary incommensurability. I am interested in the fate of the argument, not in scholarship on Sidgwick.
