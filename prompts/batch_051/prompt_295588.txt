I'm a graduate student in philosophy working on the cognitive status of language model outputs, and I've been given a slot at a small interdisciplinary workshop later this term. My talk will defend the following position: the debate over whether a chain-of-thought trace faithfully reports a model's reasoning rests on an unhelpful picture in which the trace is either a transcript of an inner process or a post-hoc confabulation about one. I will argue instead that a chain-of-thought trace is a causal scaffold: once it has been generated, it enters the model's context and constrains subsequent token-by-token sampling, so that the trace partly constitutes the computation the overall system ends up performing rather than merely describing it. Faithfulness, on this view, is not a property of the trace alone; it is a property of the whole configuration — model, prompt, trace, and decoding procedure — relative to a specified counterfactual question such as whether the final answer would change in the way the trace predicts if the trace were altered. My running example is a model that reaches a correct answer to a multi-step problem after writing down one false intermediate lemma; I want to defend the counterintuitive claim that we can still ask a precise, non-vacuous question about whether the rest of the trace is doing genuine work in producing that answer.

The room will be split roughly between mechanistic interpretability researchers, who regard chain-of-thought text as at best an epiphenomenal smokescreen and want every claim to bottom out in circuits, attention patterns, or features; and NLP practitioners who build systems that read chain-of-thought as if it were transparently introspective and will want usable design guidance. There will also be one or two philosophers. The format is a twenty-minute talk followed by a fifteen-minute question period. Prepare me for the question period by giving a prep sheet: the objections each camp will actually raise, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way that audience would phrase them, each with a short reply and a note on where the reply is weakest; which objections have real force and what I should concede up front; which claims I should refuse to give up; what the one reply is that has to land for the talk to hold together; and what the question is that I should most hope nobody asks.
