im on the data team at a human rights NGO and every year i'm the one who has to decide whether to use the word genocide in our country reports. i'm not a lawyer so i've been going off Lemkin's original account in Axis Rule in Occupied Europe, the genocide chapter, rather than the 1948 convention. Lemkin describes genocide there as a synchronized attack on the different foundations of a group's life—its political institutions, its economy, its culture, its language, its religion, its social structure, its health, and its physical existence—not just mass killing, with the aim of destroying the group as a distinct collective. I turned that into a working checklist:

1. there is a coordinated plan or pattern of measures against a group, not just isolated incidents
2. the measures hit at least several different spheres of the group's life, not only physical violence
3. the aim is to destroy the group as a group, even if individual members survive or even if killing is not the main tool

Now I have a case I genuinely can't decide on. A state with a large majority community systematically dismantles a minority's economic base: it revokes their land titles, bans their traditional farming and fishing practices, and blocks them from markets unless they adopt the majority language and official names. It closes their religious schools, bans their main holiday and processions, and replaces schooling with state-run boarding schools that teach that their history and origin stories are myth. It moves children far from home for years. There is no mass killing campaign; a few dozen people are shot by security forces during evictions over a decade. The government formally says this is national integration and development. Some outside researchers call it cultural genocide or ethnocide, others say forced assimilation but not genocide. My colleague says the word genocide should be reserved for mass murder and that using it here will destroy our credibility. Another says not using it is exactly how these governments get away with destroying groups slowly.

Under my Lemkin-derived checklist, do I call this genocide? Does it matter that the mechanism is assimilation and economic displacement rather than killing? And if my criteria say yes but that feels like overreach, does that mean my criteria need fixing, or is my discomfort just the politics of naming?
