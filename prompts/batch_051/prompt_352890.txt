I have been reading around in the methodological individualism versus holism debate in the social sciences, and I keep finding the individualist side stated in a way that is either trivially true or too vague to reject. A view that says only individual persons act, or that every social fact must have some individual-level realization, does not yet tell me why I should prefer explanations framed in individual terms, or what I lose when I help myself to a macro category such as an institution, a norm, or a suicide rate. So before I read any further, I want you to write the desiderata: what a substantive, defensible version of methodological individualism has to get right if it is to function as a guide to empirical social explanation rather than as a metaphysical aside. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it — whether that is a stable macro regularity it must accommodate, a distinction it must draw rather than blur, or a reformulation that would render it empty. Then mark every requirement as either non-negotiable for any credible version of the view or as a demand that only one tradition in the debate would insist on, and say which of them pull against each other and why.

I am not asking you to adjudicate the debate or to supply a bibliography of who said what. What I want is the criteria, and the reasoning that makes each of them genuine rather than a prejudice of one camp. In particular, I want to know which single requirement does the most work to separate a real explanatory program from the near-tautology that social arrangements are, in some loose sense, made up of people doing things. The intended use is my own sorting of positions, so be explicit about which requirements you think cannot be jointly satisfied by any one theory — that is the part I most need, since it would mean the list itself is a result.
