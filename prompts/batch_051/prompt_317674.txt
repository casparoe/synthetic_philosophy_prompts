In regular systems work we ship on average-case metrics all the time -- p99 latency, aggregate error rates -- and nobody demands worst-case guarantees, because the worst case is usually some absurd input that never occurs in practice. So I don't get why AI safety people talk as if only worst-case guarantees are worth having. I tried to formalize my intuition, and the argument looks valid to me, but I have a feeling something in it is doing more work than I realize. (1) We only need an AI system to be safe in the situations it actually faces in deployment.
(2) The situations a system actually faces are, by definition, the typical or average cases drawn from its deployment distribution, not the adversarial worst cases.
(3) An average-case safety guarentee is a guarantee that the system is safe on average over that deployment distribution.
(4) So an average-case guarantee over the true deployment distribution is exactly the guarantee that step (1) asks for.
(5) Therefore worst-case guarantees are redundant for safety.

I half-suspect step (2) or step (3) is sneaking in the assumption that we have the true deployment distribution, or that "safe on average" means "safe in each actual case," but I can't make the objection precise.
