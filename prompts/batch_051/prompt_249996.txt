i'm collecting bad arguments that lean on gödel outside mathematics, where the theorem gets stretched to license something it cant actually support. so far i have:

(1) "gödel proved there are true but unprovable statements, so there are truths no one can ever know." this one drops the conditions (consistent, recursively axiomatizable, strong enough for arithmetic) and turns "unprovable inside this one formal system" into "unknowable full stop."

(2) the lucas/penrose move: "we can see that the gödel sentence of any formal system is true, but the system itself can't prove it, so the human mind isn't a computer." this assumes the mind is a single consistent formal system in the first place, and that our "seeing" the sentence is true isn't itself the kind of thing the theorem constrains.

(3) "any system complex enough to talk about itself is incomplete or inconsistent, so a constitution, a religion, or a theory of everything is too." this treats "system" metaphorically and ignores that the theorem is about formal systems containing a certain amount of arithmetic.

(4) "even mathematics can't prove everything, so there's no objective truth / truth is relative." a non sequitur from provability to truth.

these are all pretty standard by now, so i want one more that's genuinely different in structure, not just the same move aimed at another field (like "gödel shows a legal code can't be complete"). give me another bad argument that cites gödel outside math, say what it gets wrong, and whether it fails in a way the four above dont.
