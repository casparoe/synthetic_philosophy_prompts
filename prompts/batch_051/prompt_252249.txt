For a workshop on self-models and consciousness next term, I am presenting a paper that defends a Hofstadterian claim about self-awareness, and I want a question-period prep sheet. The claim: the structural feature exploited by Gödel's first incompleteness theorem — a formal system's ability to encode, refer to, and reason about its own syntax and provability relation — is the same kind of structure that, instantiated in a rich self-modeling cognitive system, explains the emergence of a unified 'I'; self-awareness should be understood as a strange loop, a self-referential representational cycle in which a symbol for the self is generated and stabilized by the system's own activity. The paper argues in three steps: Gödel's construction works by getting a formal system to say something about itself; a naturalistic self must likewise be a representational structure that refers to the whole system that produces it; and the experienced unity of the self is a fixed-point-like product of that self-referential dynamic rather than an inner observer. I will spend my opening minutes disavowing the old anti-mechanist Gödelian argument (that a mind can see the truth of an unprovable Gödel sentence and therefore cannot be a computational system), because this audience has heard that argument, rejects it, and will otherwise assume it is my target. The audience: ten or so analytic philosophers of mind and language and three cognitive scientists who study metacognition and the sense of agency. They are naturalists; they respect formal results but distrust analogies between logic and phenomenology; they take the explanatory gap seriously; and they are inclined to say that self-reference is cheap — a quine program or a thermostat with feedback is self-referential without being self-aware. Prepare me for the questions I will actually get: what will they object to, in their own vocabulary, which objections have real force, what should I concede up front, and what is the one reply that must land for the paper to survive? Order the objections by how likely and how damaging they are, phrase each one the way a person with that background would actually put it, give a two-minute reply to each, and note honestly where each reply is soft. End with the question I should be praying nobody asks.
