I want to design a study around a sharper version of "do psychopaths know what they're doing is wrong?" I think the usual question gets stuck because people use "know" lazily, so I want to split it into three levels. Level one: a mouth — the person can produce the word "wrong" for textbook cases, the way a parrot can. Level two: a tracker — the person can correctly predict that others will condemn the act, and knows the condemnation doesn't depend on there being a posted rule somewhere. Level three: a grip — the person can use the wrongness in new inferences: explain why a superficially similar act is not wrong, or say what would have to change for the act to stop being wrong other than "the rule changed." I suspect psychopaths have level two but not level three, and I want to know whether that gap is real and testable rather then just me drawing a line on vibes.

So: design the single strongest experiment you'd actually run to test whether psychopaths have level-three grip or are just sophisticated level-two trackers. Give me the concrete task participants would do, the comparison groups (and what each control is for), and the exact result pattern that would count as "they have the grip" versus "they're tracking without the grip." Then be straight with me about what even a clean result would not settle — whether the deficit is specifically moral, or just some general difficulty with novel counterfactual reasoning, or an artifact of the task. I want a committed answer: pick one design and defend why it beats the alternatives you mention, and give a real verdict about what it can and cannot show. Don't hand me a list of options and leave me to choose.
