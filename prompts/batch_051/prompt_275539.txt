A mate of mine has just come out of a long Rorty phase and she's shown me an argument that, she says, pulls the rug out from under his own position. I can't find the exit, so I'm going to lay it out for you.

Her reconstruction of the antirepresentationalist side goes like this. Language isn't in the business of getting reality right; vocabularies are tools for coping, so it's a mistake to ask which vocabulary better mirrors how things are. Different tools are just better or worse for different jobs, and no one asks whether a saw is truer than a hammer.

Fair enough. But she also wants to hang on to the liberal politics: the central task is to reduce cruelty and widen the circle of who counts as one of us, not to demonstrate that some descriptions correspond to how things really are.

So here's the argument that worries me.
(1) If vocabularies are only tools, then no vocabulary gets reality more right than any other.
(2) So the human-rights vocabulary doesn't get things more right than, say, the vocabulary of a torturer or an authoritarian.
(3) Yet we should still prefer the human-rights vocabulary, becuase it's more useful for reducing cruelty and widening solidarity.
(4) But calling it more useful for reducing cruelty seems to require comparing the two vocabularies' effects from a standpoint outside both; otherwise the comparison only works inside the vocabulary we already prefer.
(5) Antirepresentationalism denies that there is any such outside standpoint.
(6) So the position either goes quietist, with no basis for preferring liberal politics over the alternatives, or it quietly smuggles back a representational claim — that cruelty-reduction is a vocabulary-independent fact.

I most want to keep (1) and (3). Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If you think the argument is valid, tell me which premise is the least costly to give up and what's left of the position afterwards. And is there a way to read (4) where the comparison is made from inside the liberal vocabulary without the whole thing being question-begging?

Please write the answer in British English.
