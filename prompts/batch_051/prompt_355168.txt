I'm in a comparative politics seminar this term on democratic erosion — we've gone week after week on Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and now the US, and I've realized I can throw around the phrase "democratic backsliding" in discussion while having basically no clue what would falsify it. We read Levitsky and Ziblatt early on, and their talk of informal norms feels right but impossible to actually code. My prof insists its a real concept and not just a pejorative for "governments we don't like," but I'm starting to suspect the class is using it as a vibe. So I'd like a working definition of democratic backsliding, one I could code cases with, tested against these seven.

(1) Hungary after 2010: Fidesz wins a constitutional supermajority in a reasonably free election, then rewrites the electoral law to entrench itself, amends the constitution, packs the constitutional court, and fills the media regulator and state media with loyalists, while elections keep happening. (2) Poland after 2015: PiS captures the constitutional tribunal, forces judges into retirement by lowering the retirement age, and turns the public broadcaster into a government mouthpiece, but elections stay competitive and the opposition eventually wins one. (3) Turkey, especially after 2017: an executive presidency adopted by referendum after a post-2016 purge of judges, civil servants, academics, journalists, and opposition MPs, with elections still held but on a heavily tilted field. (4) A US state that passes a strict voter ID law, cuts early voting, and purges voter rolls in ways that predictably suppress poor and minority voters, without evidence of meaningful fraud. (5) A US president who loses the election, refuses to concede, pressures a state election official to manufacture the vote margin he needs, and urges state legislators to appoint alternative slates of electors. (6) An elected government that enacts a huge redistributive program through the ordinary legislative process, with courts signing off, while business groups and most newspapers denounce it as a slide into authoritarianism. (7) A long-established democracy that for decades legally disenfranchises a racial minority — something like Jim Crow, though maybe you'd prefer a cleaner example.

My gut says (1) and (3) are clearly backsliding, (2) is too but maybe a reversible case, (4) and (5) I honestly can't tell wether they're the same phenomenon or just normal party conflict, (6) is not, and (7) feels different from the others in a way I can't articulate. Propose the definition, run it through all seven, tell me where it gives a verdict you'd want to revise or defend, and then say which of my tentative judgments is least secure and wether "democratic backsliding" is one thing or a family of related things.
