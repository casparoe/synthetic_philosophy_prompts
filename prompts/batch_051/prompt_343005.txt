My friend in an aquaculture-policy reading group keeps insisting that a salmon farm is just a battery-cage egg operation dropped underwater. Stock tens of thousands of animals in a volume where they can't swim or school normally, breed for fast growth until they're structurally fragile, fight disease with chemicals, and you've reproduced the moral situation we already decided was unacceptable for caged hens. Her conclusion is that anyone who opposes battery cages but still eats farmed salmon is being inconsistent, and the only reason the inconsistency feels less obvious is that the whole thing happens out of sight underwater.

I can't tell weather she's right or just good at metaphors. Walk me through how far the analogy holds. First give the correspondence table: what maps onto the cage walls, the stocking density, beak trimming, ammonia buildup, disease pressure? Then the places it breaks. Which features of the battery-cage case does the analogy quietly import that a salmon net pen doesn't actually have, and which of those disanalogies are load-bearing for the inconsistency verdict as opposed to cosmetic? Are there disanalogies that cut the other way, where a salmon farm is worse than the hen-house picture would predict? Then say whether the conclusion survives the audit, and if the analogy is doing too much work, what analogy you'd use instead.
