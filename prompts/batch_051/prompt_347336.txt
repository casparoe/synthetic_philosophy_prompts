I'm a novelist, and I'm working on a novel where the main character gets a terminal diagnosis and has to decide wether to sign up for cryonics. I want the chapter to be smarter than the usual pro-con list, so write me a real essay on whether it's rational for him to bet on revival, and actually reach a verdict. Weigh the simple expected-value argument (any nonzero chance of revival times a huge amount of extra life makes the cost look trivially worth it) against the less obvious problems: the probability is basically an invented number that can be tuned from near zero to a few percent depending on mood, the person who wakes up might be a copy or a reconstruction rather than the continuer of the original self, the future people doing the reviving might have their own plans for him, and spending money and hope on a freezer might quietly change how he lives the life he definitely has. Don't end on 'both sides have a point.' A conditional conclusion like 'it's rational only if he can satisfy X' is fine, as long as you defend it. Also poke at whether this is a bet that ordinary expected-value reasoning even applies to, or whether it's more like a leap of faith dressed up in numbers.
