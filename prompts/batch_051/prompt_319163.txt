Question: Briefly describe the Knobe effect, explain why it was taken to be surprising, and say how it is usually explained. Keep it to a short paragraph.

Student answer: "The Knobe effect is Joshua Knobe's finding that people's moral evaluation of an outcome changes which mental states they ascribe to the agent who brings it about. In the chairman vignette, the chairman is told that a new program will raise profits and will also either harm or help the environment, and he says he cares only about profits. People tend to say that the chairman intentionally harmed the environment in the harm condition, but that he did not intentionally help it in the help condition. What makes this surprising is that the chairman's beliefs and desires are held fixed across the two conditions: in both, he foresees the side effect and says he does not care about it either way. On the standard belief-desire model of intentional action, the intentionality judgments should therefore have been the same. The usual explanation — which I think is Knobe's own — is that in the harm case our negative moral evaluation of the chairman inflates his perceived intentionality, while in the help case our positive evaluation deflates it. The asymmetry is therefore a bias, and Knobe takes it as evidence that people commit an error when blame and praise contaminate their factual judgments about what the agent did intentionally."
