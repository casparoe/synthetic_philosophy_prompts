I'm on my state's parole board. We use a structured risk instrument that scores inmates on employment history, family support, and their own account of the offense. In this case, the instrument returns "low risk of violent reoffense." But the inmate just completed a prison-run program that teaches people how to talk about their offense so the instrument reads it as genuine remorse, and the program's own materials basically say graduates score low on the instrument almost regardless of their actual history. Half the board says the score is epistemically useless: if this man were actually high risk, he'd still be telling the same coached story and would still be scored low, so the score doesn't track the real risk. The other half says that's too clever — parole decisions are made under uncertainty, not in an epistemology seminar, and the instrument is still the best validated tool we have. I need to circulate a short decision memo before next week's hearing that recommends granting parole, denying it, or continuing for more assessment, with a concrete time frame. It should engage the first group's worry seriously rather than dismissing it as philosophical navel-gazing, and it should avoid turning into jargon.
