I teach an upper-level undergrad seminar on rationality and games, about 16 students, one 75-minute session. Design an in-class activity around the Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery. I want them to feel, before hearing any terminology, how a group of equally rational people who can't communicate can undermine their own best guesses, and why mixed strategies like opting in with probability 1/n stop looking like a weird compromise once you see them across repeated trials. The game should have a basic round where exactly one person must opt in to win points (and zero or two-plus opt-ins means nobody gets anything), then a second variant roughly like a lowest-unique-positive-integer lottery. Use points, not money. No communication during choices, but you can let them debrief briefly between rounds. Give me rules, materials, a timing plan, and a debrief that maps moments in the game onto the distinction between standard game-theoretic best-response reasoning and superrational symmetry. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time, especially whether students will start trying to coordinate with winks or side-channels, and be honest about what an in-person class cannot reproduce about Platonia's no-communication setup.
