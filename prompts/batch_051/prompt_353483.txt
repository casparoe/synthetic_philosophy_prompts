I keep hearing "Scripture interprets Scripture" from my Reformed in-laws, usually right when a disagreement about a text gets interesting. Last sunday it was women preaching: my brother-in-law says the verse telling women to keep silent in the churches is "clear," so the places where the same letters discuss women praying and prophesying have to be bent to fit it. I'm a philosophy professor, not a biblical scholar, and I couldn't stop thinking: what decides which passage lands in the "clear" bucket before the rule gets applied? Doesn't "clear interprets unclear" quietly presuppose the very act of interpretation it was supposed to replace? Anyway. Can you give me a menu of the precise claims "Scripture interprets Scripture" could be making — about perspicuity, about the text being self-contained, about an underlying unity of meaning, about a decision procedure for apparent conflicts — and for each say whether its trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false? And tell me which of those readings actually does the work when someone uses the slogan to settle a conflict between two passages, and which ones just move the regress one level up.
