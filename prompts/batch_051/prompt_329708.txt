I am taking an upper-division formal epistemology course this term, and we are currently working through the accuracy-first program in epistemic utility theory. One of the course handouts summarizes the view as follows:

"According to accuracy-first epistemology, the fundamental epistemic value is truth, and beliefs, like actions, are rational only insofar as they are useful for attaining it. The view aims to derive all norms of epistemic rationality from this one idea. Degrees of belief, or credences, are scored by how close they come to the truth: a credence of 0.8 in a proposition that turns out true is more accurate than a credence of 0.7, and both are more accurate than believing the proposition false. The central result of the program is a proof that any assignment of credences that violates the probability calculus is strictly less accurate than some coherent assignment in every possible world, so anyone who values accuracy is rationally required to be probabilistically coherent. From this single result, so the handout says, the rest of standard epistemology follows—conditionalization, the Principal Principle, and the general norms governing how evidence should move belief. The main difficulty is that the proof depends on 'proper scoring rules' for measuring accuracy, and these scoring rules are themselves as much in need of justification as the norms they were supposed to derive. The program therefore looks circular: it reduces epistemic rationality to a kind of pragmatic utility-maximizing, and then quietly imports distinctively epistemic standards in order to get the maximization going."

I have a lingering sense that this is not entirely fair—especially the slide from "truth is the fundamental epistemic value" to "beliefs are rational only insofar as they are useful," and the claim that conditionalization and the Principal Principle simply follow from the coherence result. But the handout was written by someone whose command of the material I trust, so I want to check my suspicion rather than dismiss it. I would like an accuracy audit of this summary: which parts would a careful defender of the accuracy-first program accept as stated, which parts are overstatements or conflations, and which is the worst distortion? In particular, does the final charge of circularity survive once the misleading parts are corrected? I do not need references; I can supply those myself.
