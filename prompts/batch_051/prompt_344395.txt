I am setting up a research group on the social epistemology and politics of archives, and before we argue about particular practices I want the specification of what any theory in this area must get right. By archive I mean the whole apparatus through which some people, events, and forms of life get recorded and others do not: decisions about what to collect, keep, or destroy; how material is arranged, described, classified, and linked; how provenance and context are documented or lost; who may consult what, when, and on what terms; and how a surviving record is treated as standing for a past it only partially contains. I am not asking for a survey of existing positions; the criteria of adequacy are what I need.

An adequate theory would have to handle, among other things: records made by a colonial administration about a subject population and later used as evidence in a truth and reconciliation process; a private diary donated to a library on condition that it stay closed for decades; a community oral-history project that refuses to deposit its recordings in the national repository; census categories that make a group unrecordable; a departing regime deliberately destroying its files; a dataset scraped from a social platform that includes posts the authors have since deleted; and the standing fact that most people who ever lived left no record at all.

Please give me the requirements in the form of a list or table: the phenomena a theory must explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the institutional or practical constraints it cannot ignore, with the case or consideration behind each that makes it a requirement rather than a preference of one camp. For each, say whether it is non-negotiable or a legitimate site of disagreement. Then say which single requirement does the most to exclude currently popular views, and keep any scoring of particular positions to one line. The part I most want is the incompatibility question: whether some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, the demand that everything be kept and the demand that communities can refuse to be recorded, or the demand for open access and the demand that people appearing in the records not be harmed when they are re-identified. If some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, tell me which requirements conflict and what determines which gives way; if you think none conflict, say why, since that itself would be a substantive result.
