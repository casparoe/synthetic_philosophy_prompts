Ancient atomism sets matter apart from the void in terms of fullness and solidity: atoms are impenetrable bodies with no void inside them, and void is simply where no body is. The standard modern picture appears to reverse this: atoms are said to be mostly empty space, macroscopic solidity is treated as an emergent effect of electromagnetic interaction and quantum statistics rather than as an intrinsic feature of fundamental matter, and elementary particles are not hard little corpuscles. Should we conclude that impenetrability has been empirically refuted as a criterion for being material, and that the atomists' contrast between the full and the void was not a prescient insight but a mistaken extrapolation from the macroscopic to the fundamental?
