I spent forty years in a spectroscopy lab, and retirement has finally given me time to read the philosophy of science I'd always claimed I was too busy for. I've been through Kuhn, Popper, and just finished Feyerabend, and his anything-goes position bothers me the way a slightly mis-calibrated instrument does: it reads true at the bench but false in the hallway.

I've been working up a way to hold both reactions at once, and I want an honest check on it. I call it the two-storey account (my wife says call it the parliament of methods; I can't decide). Storey one: at the level of actual discovery there is no single scientific method — the only acurate summary of what scientists actually do is that whatever works is temporarily allowed. Feyerabend is basically right up there. Storey two: science does have a method, but it is not a discovery method. It is a governance method for handling apostates, a procedure for deciding which rule-breakers get promoted from crank to pioneer. Roughly: you may violate an established rule, but the violation only gets adopted once its result has been reproduced by people trained under the old rules, and the old rule is rewritten afterwards rather than shrugged off in the moment. So the first floor is anarchist, the second floor is deeply conservative, and the interesting stuff all happens in the stairwell. I think this explains why practitioners nod at Feyerabend while the institution somehow doesn't collapse, and it makes a prediction I like: most practices we call unscientific fail on the second floor, not the first.

Am I just badly reinventing Lakatos or someone like that? Give it to me straight. And can you format it as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the nearest existing idea and the real difference between it and mine; (2) the strongest objection; (3) a second strong objection; (4) an actual case from the history of science where the two-storey account distorts what happened; and (5) whether anything of it should survive.
