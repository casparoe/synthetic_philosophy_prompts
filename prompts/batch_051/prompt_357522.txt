I've been turning over a view I keep calling tranquilism in my head — I don't know if that's a real label or just one I half-remember from somewhere. The view is that the only thing that ultimately matters, positively or negatively, is the absence of craving and suffering. Pleasure, achievement, love, knowledge, beauty: all of those matter only as instruments, insofar as they reduce or increase craving and suffering. The uncomfortable corollary is that two lives with the same overall level of craving-free calm are equal in value, even if one is dense with relationships, grief, art, effort, and failure, and the other is, bluntly, blank.

Here's the conflict. I find the arguments for tranquilism weirdly compelling: if you kept subtracting every source of disturbance from a life, what's left to be bad seems to bottom out exactly at craving and suffering, and the view gives a clean, non-arbitrary measure of how a life is going — less of those two things, full stop — which dodges all the usual puzzles about how to weigh friendship against beauty against accomplishment. But I also cannot make myself believe that a person who wrote a string quartet while grieving their mother didn't have something better than a person who spent the same years in a medically induced calm, even if we stipulate that their craving/suffering totals come out equal. That intuition doesn't feel like a prejudice I half-disown; it feels like one of the most solid things I have. I don't want to be told which side wins. Walk me through what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that gets debunked and dropped, what it would take for the theory to be amended or abandoned, and whether there's a reading of "matters" on which both survive — for instance, the calm matters most but not exclusively, or the intuition is tracking meaning rather than well-being, or something else. Give me the strongest version of each side, not a strawman, and tell me which you'd give up and why. Be specific about whether the theory actually contradicts the intuition or only contradicts a philosophical gloss on it, and how we'd tell the difference.
