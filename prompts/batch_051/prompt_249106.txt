Just got back from a long argument with a friend about what makes a science a science. He kept insisting the line is simple: real science drops a theory when its predictions fail, while pseudoscience just keeps bolting on little patches so the theory can survive anything. But I kept thinking of cases where actual science did bolt on a patch after a failed prediction and the patch later turned out to be doing real work—like proposing an unseen planet to clean up a weird orbit, or adding an invisible particle to balance the books, which then got detected. So "adding patches is bad" can't be the whole story, because sometimes that's exactly how a field moves forward. Is there a name for the difference between patches that later payed off and patches that just keep a theory alive no matter what? I'm not just after the label—I want to know how you're supposed to tell the two apart at the time, not in hindsight, where the boundary gets murky, and how that fits with the usual falsificationist story about trying to kill your theory. Also, please keep straight which parts of this are settled among people who work on the topic and which parts are still genuinely contested, rather than presenting one camp as the consensus.
