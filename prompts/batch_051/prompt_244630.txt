I've been going in circles on an argument a friend and I built, and it seems to prove way too much. Heres the thing.

(1) The fundamental laws of physics are time-reversal invariant: any process that can run forward can run in reverse, and the reverse is just as lawful. (2) So the second law — entropy tends to increase toward the future — can't come from the dynamics alone. The laws don't point from past to future. (3) The usual fix is the past hypothesis: the universe started in an extraordinarily low-entropy state, and entropy has had nowhere to go but up. (4) But thats a boundary condition at one end of time, not a law. If the universe had instead been arranged to end in a low-entropy state, the exact same statistical reasoning would make entropy increase toward the past. (5) So the arrow of time is not in the laws; it's a one-off fact about how things happen to have started. (6) And that seems to prove too much. We explain a gas spreading through a room by its earlier state, not its later one; we explain events by earlier causes, never by later ones. If (5) is right, that whole asymmetry of explanation is just a projection of a contingent boundary condition, and 'causes precede effects' becomes a parochial fact about our patch of the universe rather than a deep truth about causality. (7) But that's nuts: even in a universe running down from high entropy, a later event wouldn't explain an earlier one... or would it?

Where does this go wrong, if it does? I'd most like to keep (1) and the idea that cause before effect is a deep truth, but I suspect that's just me flinching. Tell me which step you reject and what rejecting it costs, and also tell me where your own diagnosis is shakiest — what argument or consideration would make you flip the verdict.
