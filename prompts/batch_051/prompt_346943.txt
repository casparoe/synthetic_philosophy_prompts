I am a high school student taking a philosophy elective, and I have been reading parts of Hume's essay on miracles. I want to know whether his argument still holds when the evidence for a purported miracle is not human testimony but large amounts of machine-generated recordings, and whether the argument then changes again now that such recordings can be faked with deepfake tools.

As I understand it, Hume argued that we should be extremely reluctant to accept another person's report that a miracle has occurred, where a miracle is understood as a violation of a law of nature. His reason is not that the reporter is certainly lying or mistaken. It is that the uniform experience on which the law of nature rests is the strongest kind of evidence we can have, so it will always be more probable that the report is false than that the law was broken. He allowed that testimony might be credible in principle if it came from many independent witnesses who had nothing to gain and something to lose by testifying, but he thought those conditions are not met in the cases people actually cite.

Now consider a modern case. A supposed miracle is recorded by many independently owned security cameras, phones, and hospital monitors, with timestamps, locations, and raw data, and the recordings are examined by independent forensic analysts who find no signs of editing. This looks as if it gets around the part of Hume's argument that depends on the weakness of human testimony: the recordings are not excited, credulous, or motivated to confirm a miracle, and there are many of them. But it may still fall to the deeper part of the argument, because the evidence for the laws of nature is also made up of records and data, and that body of evidence is vastly larger. And then there is the further development: deepfake tools and generative models now make it cheap to produce convincing fake recordings, so machine evidence is becoming fallible in something like the way human testimony always was.

My question is whether Hume's argument survives this two-step change. At the first step, does the shift from testimony to abundant, independently corroborated recordings weaken the argument, and if so, which premise is under pressure? At the second step, does the rise of deepfakes strengthen the original argument again, or does it do something else to it? And is there a repaired version of the argument that does the work Hume wanted across both steps? I am asking about the argument itself, not about whether any particular miracle claim is true.
