You are a researcher equally at home in evolutionary biology and in the theory of optimization, and you are used to translating between the two fields. I keep hearing a colleague in machine learning argue that natural selection just is stochastic gradient descent on a fitness landscape. On his account, a population corresponds to a point in parameter space, the distribution of variants corresponds to the stochastic perturbations used to estimate gradients, fitness corresponds to negative loss, and generations correspond to iterations. From this he concludes that the two processes are near enough to interchangeable that lessons should transfer in both directions: in particular, that gradient-free evolutionary search can be expected to inherit the convergence and generalization behaviour of deep learning, and that evolution's success in producing complex organisms is evidence that noisy, heuristic search can be trusted on hard high-dimensional problems. Please audit this analogy rather than merely ruling on it. Start by writing out the full correspondence table and then brainstorm candidate disanalogies. Then identify what the analogy quietly imports into evolution from gradient descent (for example, a fixed objective function, a training/deployment distinction, smoothness or differentiability, and i.i.d. sampling), and conversely what it imports into gradient descent from evolution. Separate the disanalogies that actually matter for the colleague's stated conclusion from the ones that are only cosmetic, and end by saying clearly whether the conclusion survives the audit. If the analogy cannot carry that conclusion, say what framing of the relationship between evolution and gradient descent would be more honest.
