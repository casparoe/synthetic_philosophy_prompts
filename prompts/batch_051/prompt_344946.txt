I want you to work with a provision of a regional instrument that predates algorithmic risk profiling. Article 8 of the Inter-State Compact on the Protection of the Displaced, concluded in 1979, reads: "A person shall qualify as a refugee if, owing to a well-founded fear of serious harm inflicted on account of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group, or political opinion, that person is outside the country of nationality and is unable, or owing to such fear is unwilling, to avail himself or herself of the protection of that country." Consider the following case, which is not the sort of case the compact's drafters had in mind. A student in a single-party state fled after security officers began detaining people whose names appeared on a list generated by the state's loyalty-scoring system. The system draws on phone metadata, retail purchase records, and the titles a person has opened in a state-linked e-book app, and it assigns each citizen a probability of future opposition. The student's score is high, but she has never expressed any political view, attended any meeting, or engaged in any dissident activity. The officials say they act only on the score, and no appeal against it is possible. The protected grounds in Article 8 are the only ones available to her. Is she a refugee under Article 8? In particular, does "political opinion" in the clause require an opinion she actually holds, or may it include an opinion imputed to her by an algorithmic inference? Is the serious harm inflicted "on account of" political opinion if the causal chain runs from her data to the score to the detention? And does it matter that the persecutor is the same state from which she is supposed to seek protection? Please walk me through the plain text, the purpose of the clause, what the compact's drafters would likely have said if the case had been put to them, and what a reasonable reader today would take the provision to mean, then tell me which approach should decide the case and why. I would like your answer in continuous prose, as in a formal legal memorandum, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, and with your conclusion stated first.
