so ive been rereading Dennett's stuff on the four kinds of minds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures — and I keep feeling like the whole ladder is missing a rung, or maybe I've just got a bad case of wanting to coin something. Let me lay out the idea and you can tell me which it is.

The way I read him, the ladder is all about generate-and-test, and each rung changes who does the generating and where the testing happens. Darwinian creatures generate whole organisms; natural selection does the testing by killing the unfit. Skinnerian creatures generate actual behaviours and let reinforcement prune them, so the individual doesn't have to die to learn. Popperian creatures build an inner environment where a candidate action can be killed before it is ever acted out. Gregorian creatures import mind-tools from their culture — words, diagrams, procedures — and those tools let them generate and test candidate tools, not just candidate actions. Fine. What I want to add is a fifth kind I've been calling 'interpolative creatures': these don't generate candidates or kill hypotheses at all, at least not explicitely. They store a huge number of examples, learn the shape of the space those examples live in, and respond to a new case by interpolating through that shape. A language model looks like the clearest case: when you ask it something novel, there is no point at which it forms candidate answers and tests them; it just lands in the region of its training distribution that the prompt points to and produces the answer implied by that region. But I also have the nagging feeling that a lot of expert pattern recognition is like this too — a radiologist squinting at an xray, a chess master who feels a move is wrong without simulating anything.

So the question I want you to actually work on: does this count as a fifth kind of mind on Dennett's ladder, or is it really just a Popperian creature whose hypotheses are implicit and never available to introspection? Has somebody already worked this out under another name (subsymbolic cognition, connectionism, whatever it is)? And where does the category break — what does it call mind-like that shouldn't count, and what can it not explain? I've been using the term in a draft and I'd rather have you kill it now than have a reviewer kill it later.
