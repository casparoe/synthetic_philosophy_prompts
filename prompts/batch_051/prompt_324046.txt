So I had a long argument with a friend last night about whether it is ever actually justified to just multiply probabilities — to assume independence until you have evidence of dependence — in heuristic arguments and risk models. Her line was that independence is not a neutral default; it is the maximally informative joint distribution consistent with the marginals, so assuming it is the most overconfident thing you can do when you are ignorant about the coupling. I see the formal point, but in practice people do it all the time in reliability, epidemiology, and forecasting, and it mostly does not blow up, while the obvious alternative — robust Bayes, sets of distributions — gives intervals so wide they are useless for an actual decision. I have a half-formed idea that the presumption might have a defensible restricted form: a principle that says exactly when treating events as independent is safe and when its reckless, maybe keyed to whether the marginals are known exactly or estimated from data, or to which tail event you care about.
