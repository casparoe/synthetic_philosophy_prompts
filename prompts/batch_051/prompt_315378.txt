Take the following forced choice. Exactly one of two outcomes will occur, and some third party must decide which: one person is tortured continuously for fifty years, or an astronomically large but finite number of people each gets a single dust speck in the eye. I hold the firm conviction that the dust specks are the better outcome and that choosing the torture would be monstrous, even when the number of dust specks is astoundingly large. But the argument below seems to show that this conviction is inconsistent. I want to know whether it goes through and, if not, exactly where it breaks.

(1) A single dust speck in the eye is a harm of small but nonzero badness.
(2) Badness is additive across harms and across persons: the total badness of a state of affairs containing several harms is the sum of their individual badness, whether those harms fall on one person or are spread across many.
(3) For any fixed finite quantity, however large, some sufficiently large finite number of equal positive quantities sums to more than it.
(4) The badness of one person's being tortured for fifty years, though enormous, is a fixed finite quantity.
(5) From (1)–(4), there is some finite n such that n dust specks, one per person across n people, have greater total badness than the single torture.
(6) If one must choose which of two states of affairs will occur, one is required to choose the state with the lesser total badness.
(7) Therefore, for some finite n, one is required to choose the torture rather than arrange n dust specks.

My suspicion is that (2) or (6) carries the fault, but suspicion is not a diagnosis. Tell me whether the argument is valid. If it is invalid, locate the exact step at which it goes wrong — the premise that is false or the inference that is invalid — and give both the reading of that step under which it is true and the reading under which it is false. Then state the corrected principle that should replace it. If the argument is valid, say which premise the dust-speck conviction forces me to reject and what would be lost by rejecting it.
