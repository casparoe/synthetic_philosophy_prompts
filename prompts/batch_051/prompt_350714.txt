I'm attracted to a view on which the aim of gender equality is to remove constraints on choice rather than to equalize outcomes. On this view, if men and women are equally free to choose and they choose different careers, the resulting segregation is not itself an injustice; treating it as one would mean overriding women's own choices or smuggling in a demand for equal outcomes. The view is systematic, anti-paternalistic, and gives a clear standard for when equality has been achieved. But I also have a firm intuition that a labor market as starkly segregated by sex as the ones we see cannot be free choice all the way down: preferences formed under inequality are compromised preferences, and a just society would not reproduce that pattern so faithfully. The gender-equality paradox sharpens the clash, since more egalitarian countries often show larger, not smaller, sex differences in occupational preferences and segregation. I want a negotiated verdict. What would justify giving up the intuition? What would justify amending or abandoning the theory? And is there a reading on which both survive — perhaps by distinguishing equality of rights, equality of resources, and equality of social roles, or by distinguishing a free choice from an unforced one? Walk me through what each option costs, and what each option commits me to about nature, culture, and justice.
