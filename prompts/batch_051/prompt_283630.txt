I've been drafting a short, opinionated essay for a reading group, and I'd like comments before I expand it. The essay argues that the standard contrast between "influence-seeking systems" and "slow value drift" is less clean than people pretend. The usual story says there are two failure modes: a system actively tries to gain influence over its environment, its overseers, or the wider world, and thereby ends up pursuing goals nobody endorsed; or, separately, values just drift over decades as generations, incentives, and habits change, with no single actor really responsible. My claim is that a lot of what gets filed under "drift" is actually the aggregate of many small influence-seeking moves. A recommender system isn't trying to change a community's standards of taste; it's just trying to shape what a user clicks next. But each successful local grab for attention shifts the distribution of examples people see, which shifts what counts as normal, and you end up with exactly the slow change in values that gets chalked up to culture just moving on its own. So the interesting question isn't whether we should worry about influence-seeking or drift, but which local influence-seeking processes are self-correcting and which ones ratchet, locking each step in.

The main thing I'd like comments on: I can't tell whether I've done something useful or just stretched "influence-seeking" until it covers everything. A critic could say that on my account a parent reading a child a bedtime story is influence-seeking, since the parent is trying to shape what the child attends to and cares about; and if everything counts as influence-seeking then the category is empty, and I haven't dissolved the contrast so much as relabeled one side of it. I also can't decide whether the essay should openly bite that bullet. And I'm not sure wether the claim should be "most drift is aggregated influence-seeking" (empirically strong and probably needs evidence I don't have) or the weaker "many cases of drift are fruitfully redescribed this way." Which parts would you push on first, and is the ratcheting/self-correcting distinction doing real work or is it just a placeholder for a better distinction I haven't found? I'd rather get blunt comments than reassurance.
