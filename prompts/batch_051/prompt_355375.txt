i teach an upper-level medieval philosophy seminar and i'm writing the final. I want one essay question on divine simplicity that can't be answered by just reciting the definition (God has no parts, his essence and existence are identical, etc). I want it to force students into the problem of the attributes. Here's the crux I have in mind: if God is simple in the strong sense, and God is omniscient, omnipotent, perfectly good, just, and merciful, then either these attributes are really distinct from one another (which looks like composition) or they're really identical (which seems to collapse mercy into justice, knowledge into power, and so on). The standard medieval reply is that the attributes are distinct only in our way of conceiving them, not in God. The question should make students test whether that reply actually solves the problem or just relabels it, and whether it can make sense of apparently different divine actions like forgiving and punishing. A really strong student should also connect this to God's freedom: if God is identical with his act of will and his will is identical with his essence, then whatever God wills seems necessary, which would make creation necessary too. Please write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme that seperates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each answer must contain. The essay is worth 20 marks and students have about 60 minutes. In the marking scheme, also flag the common mistakes you'd dock marks for, like thinking simplicity only rules out physical parts, or saying the attributes are just human metaphors and leaving it at that.
