A friend and I spent a long evening arguing about a provision of our university's recently adopted Community Expression Code, and I want to see the competing readings set out more carefully than we managed at one in the morning.

Section 7.3 of the Code reads: "Any student or recognized student organization that, in a University forum or by means of University computing resources, directs at another University member, because of that member's race, religion, national origin, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, or disability, words or expressive conduct that a reasonable person would find severely demeaning or likely to incite discriminatory harassment, violates this Code."

The case that divided us: a third-year student maintains, on University-provided servers, a Discord bot he configured to answer certain prompts in the style of an insult comic. The bot autonomously generates messages that single out members of a minority student club by name, mock their appearance and accent, and urge other members to exclude them. The student says he never wrote or previewed the particular outputs, which the bot assembles from a training corpus; he did, however, point the bot at the University's public directory so that it could address students by name. A club member has filed a complaint under Section 7.3.

Please give me exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Cover, in order: what the plain wording of Section 7.3 would require in this case; what its evident purpose would require; what the drafters would most plausibly say if asked whether the provision covers automated, self-generating abuse; what a reasonable student reading the Code would take it to prohibit; and, finally, how the American free-speech framework and the European dignity-and-pluralism framework would each assess a rule like Section 7.3 as applied to this case.
