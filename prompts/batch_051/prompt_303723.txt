I keep noticing a parallel between the tight/loose distinction in cultural psychology and the bias-variance trade-off in statistical learning, and I want help deciding whether it is deep or superficial. A tight culture imposes strong, widely shared norms and punishes deviance; that looks like a low-variance strategy: it buys predictability and coordination at the cost of possibly overfitting to arrangements that happened to work in a particular historical environment. A loose culture tolerates varied behavior and weak enforcement; that looks like a high-variance strategy: it buys exploration and adaptability at the cost of noise and miscoordination. Is there a real structural isomorphism between cultural tightness and the choice of how heavily to regularize, or is the word 'variance' doing all the work? In particular, does the normative question of how much rule-following a society should demand have the same shape as the engineer's question of how strongly to regularize a model, and what would be the cultural analogue of out-of-sample error that separates adaptive norms from norms overfit to historical accidents? Please explain the mapping and where it comes apart, and separate what is settled about tight and loose cultures from what is genuinely contested among people who study them; do not present a contested reading as the field's consensus.
