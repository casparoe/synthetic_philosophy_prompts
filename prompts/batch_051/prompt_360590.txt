I recently spent a long evening arguing with a friend about whether the outbreak of the First World War was overdetermined. His position was that the standard causes—imperial rivalry, the alliance system, the Anglo-German naval race, domestic pressures on the Habsburg and Russian empires, the rigidity of mobilization timetables, and the assassination at Sarajevo—were so numerous and so mutually independent that a general European war would probably have come by the mid-1910s even if any one of them had been removed. I pushed back, but I struggled to state exactly what would make his claim true rather than merely vivid.

I would like a careful case analysis of the July Crisis and the outbreak of the war through the lens of philosophical accounts of causal overdetermination and multi-causal historical explanation. Please do the following. First, distinguish the main causal structures someone might have in mind: strict symmetric overdetermination, where two or more independent causes are each sufficient for the effect and operate simultaneously; preemption, where one cause operates and another would have sufficed had the first been absent; joint necessity, where several factors are each necessary given the others; INUS conditions; and mere background or enabling conditions. Second, assign the principal candidate causes of the war to these structures, or explain why a particular candidate resists assignment. Third, specify what counterfactual evidence would be needed to show that the war was strictly overdetermined, and assess how far the historical record can actually support those counterfactuals. Fourth, reflect on what the case teaches about the theory: whether strict overdetermination is even the right category for unique, massively conjunctive historical occurrences, or whether the term is doing something looser—expressing a judgment about the robustness of an outcome across a range of nearby possible worlds rather than picking out a precise causal structure. I am less interested in a chronological narrative than in the conceptual mapping, and I would like you to be explicit about where the theory distorts the episode and where the episode strains the theory.

Please write the answer in American English.
