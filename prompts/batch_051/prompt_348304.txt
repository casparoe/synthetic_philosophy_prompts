Suppose a country scraps its ordinary protected-area system and replaces it with a market for wilderness credits. Any landowner can register a parcel, and a government agency awards credits by a public formula: points for the number of native species found in a recent survey, for years since the land was last logged, grazed, or burned, for parcel size, and for contiguity with other credited parcels. Developers have to buy credits before they can pave or build, and the credits trade freely. Spare me the abstract pros and cons: trace how this arrangement would actually play out. What would it push landowners, surveyors, developers, and the agency itself to do? Where would the gaming and Goodhart effects show up? Does the market price track anything worth calling the value of nature—intrinsic, relational, or instrumental—or just the features the formula happens to count? And are the failures fixable by retuning the formula, or does the mechanism bake in a particular theory of what wilderness is for from the start?
