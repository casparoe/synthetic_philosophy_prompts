Our seminar has been working with eight rule-of-law criteria: a legal order does well to the extent that its rules are general, publicly promulgated, prospective, intelligible, mutually consistent, possible to comply with, relatively stable over time, and congruent with the actual conduct of officials. We then read about desuetude, the idea that a statute can lapse or lose its authority through long non-use, and I am left unsure whether desuetude is best thought of as a defect in a legal order or as a feature of one. Consider this case. A city has on its books an 1890s statute making it a misdemeanor to run a commercial establishment within two hundred feet of a church on a Sunday; the statute is in force and has never been amended. It has not been enforced in living memory: no one has been charged under it, the police do not cite it, and city attorneys, when asked, will not say it could support a prosecution. Generations of shops, restaurants, and cafés have operated openly within the prohibited zone, and their leases, permits, and insurance are all written as if the statute did not exist; entire business plans depend on its never being enforced, and no official has ever suggested otherwise. Yet the city council has refused six times to repeal it, each time resolving that the statute expresses the community's values and should remain on the books for that reason alone. Go through the rule-of-law criteria one by one and say whether this legal order satisfies them with respect to this statute. Then take up the harder question: is this desuetude best understood as a violation of the congruence criterion, because the announced rule and official practice have come apart, or as the order's quiet way of curing that very violation, because the operative rule everyone knows and officials actually apply is that the statute will not be enforced? Does the council's keeping the statute in force for its expressive value make the case a violation in a way that ordinary dead-letter desuetude would not, and if so, which criterion captures the difference? Say where the criteria themselves underdetermine the answer, and whether the case shows that one of the eight criteria needs revision or refinement.
