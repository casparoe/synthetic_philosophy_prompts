In my epistemology seminar, the instructor summed up Quine's "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" like this: "Quine proved that the analytic-synthetic distinction is a myth. A sentence like 'all bachelors are unmarried' is supposed to be true purely in virtue of meaning, but Quine showed this can't be spelled out without already assuming the notion of meaning or synonymy, so the whole idea is circular. He also argued that individual sentences don't have their own empirical content—only whole theories meet the tribunal of experience. The upshot is that there is no difference at all between analytic and synthetic truths, all truths are empirical, and even something like 'all bachelors are unmarried' could in principle be revised if the evidence pushed hard enough." I think this is basically right, but the ending feels like it slides into something Quine wouldn't actually say. Can you go through it piece by piece and tell me what's accurate, what's overstated, and whether the conclusion about all truths being empirical actually follows from the earlier parts? I'd rather know where to trust the summary than just argue with it.
