Our congregation's constitution says that "baptism shall be administered by an ordained minister of this church in a public service of divine worship, upon a credible profession of faith." When the building closed in 2020, I was asked to set up the online service, and the elders permitted a woman who had completed the membership classes to make her profession of faith during a live video call; the minister pronounced the baptismal words from his study while her husband immersed her in water at their home, with the rest of the congregation watching the stream. The elders must now decide whether she has been baptized for the purposes of membership, or whether a later in-person baptism is required. I have no formal theological training, so I want the interpretive reasoning made explicit. Apply the clause from its wording, from its apparent purpose, from what its drafters would most likely have said if asked about remote baptism, and from what a reasonable member reading it today would take it to require; then say which approach should govern and whether the answer changes if baptism is treated as a symbol only rather than as something through which God acts.
