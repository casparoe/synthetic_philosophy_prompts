I’m taking a course on consumption ethics and voluntary simplicity, and something came up in my housing co-op that I’d like help thinking through. Our co-op has a Simplicity Covenant from the 70s. Article IV says: “No member shall purchase any new garment, electronic item, or household article when a serviceable used alternative can be obtained at reasonable cost; nor shall any member replace an item when it can be repaired.” One member adheres to this for physical stuff, but he pays for 2TB of cloud storage, an AI note-taking app, and rents his phone on a yearly upgrade plan. He argues he hasn’t purchased any “garment, electronic item, or household article”: a subscription is a service and the phone is leased. The covenant writers plainly had thrift stores, repair shops, and ownership in mind, not access-over-ownership or digital goods. We’re trying to figure out whether he’s violating the rule. I’m not looking for a policy answer so much as how the text, the purpose, the founders’ likely views if we could ask them, and what a reasonable member reading the covenant would think each bear on this. Which interpretive approach should win when they pull apart? Is he exploiting a loophole, or is this just outside what the rule was ever able to say?
