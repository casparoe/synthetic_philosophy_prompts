Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Ava and Ezra, who disagree about whether possibility or probability is the more fundamental notion. Ava holds that possibility is prior: something can be genuinely possible even when no coherent probability assignment captures it. Ezra holds that probability is prior: possibility talk is a coarse, context-dependent way of reporting probability judgments, and a claimed possibility that cannot be given a coherent probability model is only a pseudo-possibility. Their argument should focus on a fair lottery over the natural numbers. Ava argues that each number could win even though no standard countably additive probability measure can assign every number the same positive chance and still sum to one—evidence that possibility outruns probability. Ezra replies that, because the lottery as described cannot be modeled coherently, its apparent possibility is borrowed from finite lotteries and is illusory. Have each respond to the other’s actual arguments: Ezra should press Ava on what a bare, model-independent possibility amounts to, and Ava should press Ezra on how his view avoids making possibility an artifact of whichever probability model one happens to choose. The dialogue need not resolve, but it should bring out the deeper disagreement about whether ways the world might be or degrees of belief come first. Write your answer in American English.
