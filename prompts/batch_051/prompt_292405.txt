I direct a small research foundation in formal epistemology, and we have set aside a two-year postdoctoral fellowship to be spent on one question within the problem of old evidence. As we understand the problem: standard conditionalization says that conditioning on a piece of evidence already assigned probability 1 cannot change any credence, so old evidence cannot confirm a hypothesis—even a hypothesis that later accounts for that evidence elegantly; yet long-known planetary regularities seemed to count strongly in favor of Newtonian mechanics, so something in the Bayesian picture appears to be missing. Draft the foundation's agenda-setting memo. It should map the main live lines of work (for example, re-describing old evidence as a newly recognized logical relation between a theory and the data, revising the updating rule itself, or separating discovery from confirmation), select the single question the postdoc should pursue, and defend that selection. State the criteria you are using—importance, tractability, how many downstream debates in formal epistemology turn on the answer, and the risk that settling the question would change little elsewhere—and say what result would most change your ranking.
