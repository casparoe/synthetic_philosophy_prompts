For a game theory seminar I am writing a short statement of my views on rational play and solution concepts, and I would like a consistency check before I circulate it. Here is the package I find myself committed to.

(1) A pure strategy is irrational exactly when it is strictly dominated, and I count domination by a mixed strategy: if some mixture of my other pure strategies gives a strictly higher expected payoff than s against every pure strategy the opponent could play, then s is inadmissible.

(2) Iterated deletion of strictly dominated strategies, understood in that mixed-strategy sense, gives the full behavioral content of common knowledge of rationality in finite two-player games — the assumption that all players are rational, know this, know that they know it, and so on. More precisely: the surviving strategies are exactly those compatible with common knowledge of rationality, so rationality alone rules out the others and nothing more.

(3) Weak dominance has a different status. A weakly dominated strategy can be a best response to a sensible belief, so it is not irrational as such; deleting weakly dominated strategies is a refinement or tie-breaking move, not a requirement of rationality.

(4) In finite perfect-information games, I take backward induction to be rationally required. A player at any decision node, including a node that would never be reached if everyone followed the backward-induction plan, should choose the backward-induction action.

(5) Finally, I hold a local view of rationality: at a given node, what a player should do depends only on their beliefs conditional on having reached that node, and if they gave that node prior probability zero, those conditional beliefs are unconstrained by their earlier beliefs.

I want an audit rather than a lecture. Which of these are in tension, and which pairs only appear to be? Rank the tensions by severity, say what would have to give in each case, and tell me the least costly revision.
