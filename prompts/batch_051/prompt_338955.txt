I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, though I followed the 2017 Catalan independence episode closely and have been trying to think about it in a structured way. The facts as I understand them are these: the Catalan government held an independence referendum on 1 October 2017 even though Spain's Constitutional Court had declared it illegal; Spanish police used force against some voters; reported turnout was around 43 per cent, with opponents mostly boycotting; the Catalan parliament then declared independence, the Spanish government invoked Article 155 to impose direct rule, and several Catalan leaders were later prosecuted; no state recognised the new republic. I'm setting these out only as a shared case to reason about, not as disputed points to investigate.

I've since learned that philosophers who work on secession divide roughly between two positions. One says a group may secede only as a remedy for serious injustice — annexation, systematic discrimination, or the denial of meaningful self-government. The other says a distinct national or territorial community can have a right to secede simply because a majority of its members chooses to, provided it is willing to protect minorities and uphold liberal institutions; a more demanding variant adds that the process must be democratic, fair, and capable of producing two viable states. I want you to analyse Catalonia 2017 through these positions. For each, spell out what it would have required the Catalan government and the Spanish state to establish or do: what facts would have counted, what a 43 per cent turnout with a boycotting opposition does to the argument, what the Spanish constitution's doctrine that sovereignty belongs to the Spanish people as a whole amounts to, and whether the absence of serious oppression cuts one way or the other. Then run the analysis in reverse: what does the episode reveal about the theories themselves? Does it support the remedial-only view, support the plebiscitary view, or expose gaps in both that a more qualified account would need to fill? I am more interested in where a theory falls silent or has to stretch than in a tidy verdict.

End by saying which position seems to you least damaged by the case, and why. If the framing I have given you is confused — for instance, if the moral question cannot be separated from the constitutional one in the way I am assuming, or if the labels I'm using collapse into one another — tell me plainly and reformulate the task rather than forcing an analysis.
