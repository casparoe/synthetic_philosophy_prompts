Several years ago I bought an internet-connected thermostat. During setup the companion app displayed a Terms of Service, non-negotiable, with a button marked "I agree," which I clicked. The Terms contained this clause: "We may amend this Agreement at any time by posting the amended terms on our website or in the app. Your continued use of the Service after the amended terms are posted constitutes acceptance of the amended Agreement." Last year the company posted revised terms adding a mandatory arbitration clause and a waiver of class actions. It sent notice only to an email address I no longer monitor. I never opened the app, never visited the website, and never clicked anything; I did not know the terms had changed. The thermostat, however, has kept contacting the company's servers continuously, as it must to operate, and the company has kept charging my card. The company now says my continued use of the Service accepted the arbitration clause and the class waiver.

I understand a court might settle this one way or another, but I want the conceptual structure underneath the dispute. On a plain reading of the text, does an unattended device's automatic connection count as "continued use" by "you"? What does the purpose of the clause suggest its authors were trying to capture, and does that purpose reach a device that uses the service without its owner's attention or awareness? What would the drafters most plausibly have said if asked about always-on devices, and should that answer, or a reasonable consumer's understanding, control? Finally, step back: if the company's reading is accepted, are we forced to say that my single click of "I agree" bound me to terms I had never seen and could not in practice refuse, or is there a principled difference between the initial click and a silent amendment? I care less about who wins in court than about what theory of agreement makes those two cases different, or collapses them into one.
