Below is a draft essay in note form that I would like comments on. It is built around a passage from Aristotle’s *Politics*, Book IV, 1294b, in the Perseus translation:

“it is thought to be democratic for the offices to be assigned by lot, for them to be elected oligarchic, and democratic for them not to have a property-qualification, oligarchic to have one”

Draft notes. The passage looks like a simple classification: lot is democratic, election is oligarchic. But Aristotle immediately complicates this when he discusses mixed constitutions. He says a well-mixed constitution can take one feature from oligarchy and one from democracy — that offices are elected, from oligarchy, and that election is not on a property-qualification, from democracy. That already implies that election can be detached from oligarchy. The same complication appears later in the *Politics*: democracies often fill offices requiring special skill, such as generalships, by election rather than lot, while using lot for offices where no special skill is needed. If election is simply oligarchic, this should be a problem; Aristotle seems to treat it as compatible with democracy. The apparent tension may be resolved by distinguishing two claims: first, that lot and election have characteristic political tendencies — lot tends to equalize, election tends to favor reputation and advantage — and second, that each device can be recombined with different property qualifications and eligibilities within a constitution. On this reading, the deeper principle is not that lot is democratic and election is oligarchic, but that democracy’s equality applies to the pool of citizens, while exceptions for expertise can be justified without abandoning democracy. The original classification would then be a report of how the devices usually function, not a definition of them.
