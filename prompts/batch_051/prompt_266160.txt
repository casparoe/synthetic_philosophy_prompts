So I got into an arguement about 'survival of the fittest.' A friend keeps saying the phrase is either meaningless or a tautology, because if 'fittest' just means 'the ones that actually survive,' then 'the fittest survive' says nothing beyond 'the survivors survive.' I mostly bought this until I was reading Darwin and hit this, from Chapter IV of the first edition of On the Origin of Species (1859): "If such do occur, can we doubt (remembering that many more individuals are born than can possibly survive) that individuals having any advantage, however slight, over others, would have the best chance of surviving and of procreating their kind?" What jumped out at me is that 'best chance' sounds like a claim about probabilities or dispositions, not a definition. A friend who does philosophy of biology then told me this is basically the tautology objection, and that the standard way out is the propensity interpretation of fitness: fitness is a probabilistic disposition to survive and reproduce, not actual survival or reproductive output. So now I'm trying to sort out what's going on. Could you lay out the different precise claims 'survival of the fittest' could be making, from trivial to radical?
