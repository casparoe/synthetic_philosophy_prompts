I'm taking this animal minds / moral status course right now and we've been talking about how sentience is probably a matter of degree rather than a yes/no thing, from thermostats up through insects and mammals. I had an idea for a concrete scheme that I think would make a good discussion, but I don't want the abstract "is it a good idea" answer — I want to know how the mechanism would actually play out, who would game it, and where it falls apart.

Here it is. A country creates a Sentience Registry. Every animal species and every deployed AI system gets assigned a single moral-weight coefficient between 0 and 1, set by an expert scientific board. Legal harms get computed as severity × coefficient × duration, and that product directly determines fines, criminal sentences, and required welfare standards. So a thermostat gets 0, a locust maybe 0.03, a chicken 0.4, a pig 0.6, a dog 0.8, a human 1.0, something like that, with numbers revised every few years as evidence comes in.

How does this actually behave once it exists? What gets contested first, and by who? I'm imagining industrial agriculture pouring money into studies showing chickens are barely above 0.1, while pet and companion-animal groups fight to push dogs and cats close to 1. Does a smooth scalar survive politically, or does it collapse into two or three tiers anyway? What second-order effects show up — like breeders selecting for animals that seem more sentient to raise their coefficient, or AI companies designing systems to score low on whatever tests the board uses, or black markets in "high-coefficient" species because owning them now counts as a greater crime? Also wether the numbers create injustices the old binary view didn't, e.g. a person with severe brain damage who scores a 0.7 being legally worth less than a healthy adult, or a honeybee colony as a whole being treated as more valuable than any individual bee. Can these failure modes be fixed without dumping the whole registry, or is the cardinal-number framing itself the part that fails?
