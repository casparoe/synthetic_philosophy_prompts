I cover science and ideas for a general-audience publication, and I am researching a feature on nuclear power's place in the energy transition, framed around the contrast between Germany's exit from nuclear power and France's continued commitment to a grid that has drawn roughly two-thirds of its electricity from fission in recent years. Before I interview sources, I want the standard of assessment fixed in advance, because this debate usually runs on whatever metric the speaker happens to favor. Please write the desiderata: the list of requirements that an adequate framework for assessing nuclear power's role in the transition must meet. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and mark it as non-negotiable or as a preference of one defensible camp. Make sure the list adjudicates, rather than merely asserts, whether the following have to be met: the counterfactual-baseline problem (what each country's path actually displaced or enabled, rather than an idealized alternative); comparison at the level of firm, dispatchable system capacity rather than nameplate capacity or levelized cost alone; how to handle catastrophic tail risk rather than folding it into per-kilowatt-hour expected harm; whether political opposition and regulatory ratcheting should be treated as external constraints or as costs the technology itself partly generates; the intergenerational distribution of waste burdens against the present distribution of the electricity's benefits; the option value of keeping a low-carbon, dispatchable capability available for a deeply electrified later grid; and whether importing nuclear-generated electricity counts as continuing to rely on a technology a country has formally rejected. Then flag any subset of those requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied, or can only be satisfied at serious cost — that is the part I most need. End with a short note, two or three sentences each, on where the German exit and the French fleet fail your list most severely, and which single requirement does the most to discipline the comparison.
