When people rotate a mental image, it turns at a constant rate through intermediate orientations, just as a physical object would; when they scan between two points on an image, reaction time rises linearly with distance. So the brain must store and manipulate spatial, picture-like representations. Pylyshyn rejected this, arguing that the pictorial effects are just the result of the experimental instructions and the participants' tacit knowledge about what it would be like to inspect real pictures. For Pylyshyn, the mind's code is a kind of language — an abstract, symbolic propositional system — and mental images are only an idle by-product, with no role in cognition. The debate was eventually settled by neuroimaging, which showed that early visual cortex is active during imagery, proving that the pictures are real."
