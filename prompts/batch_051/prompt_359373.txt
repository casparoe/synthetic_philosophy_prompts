ok so i'm teaching a small seminar on the early sections of *Being and Time* — the worldhood of the world, the hammer passage, the move from readiness-to-hand to presence-at-hand — and my students can recite the glosses but they don't feel them. they'll say the tool withdraws into the task and that breakdown reveals the referential whole, and then they'll go back to their phones. i want something to read before class that actually does the thing instead of describing it.

can you write a sonnet from the point of view of a carpenter absorbed in some task (planing, nailing, chiseling — your pick), where the octave renders absorbed coping: the tool transparent, not an object with properties; the room not a collection of things but a seamless in-order-to; everything quietly for-the-sake-of the project. the volta has to be the actual moment the tool breaks or slips — a crack, a jam, an edge giving way — and it has to be the event itself, not a comment on the event, so the reader feels the tool suddenly become conspicious and unusable and just there. then the sestet should let that failure disclose what Heidegger thinks it discloses: the whole network of nails, wood, bench, the half-finished thing, the light in the workshop that had been invisible until now.

two constraints. first, don't name the theory anywhere in the poem: no readiness-to-hand, no present-at-hand, no equipment, no Dasein, no worldhood. someone who knows Being and Time should read it and think yes, that's the hammer; someone who doesn't shouldn't need a footnote, just be a little unsettled by how a broken tool can make a world appear. second, be accurate before you be pretty — a Heidegger person who knows the passage shouldn't wince at the details of what breakdown reveals and what it doesn't (the hammer doesn't first show up as a pure object with scientific properties; it shows up as an obstruction inside a whole context, and only then could you stare at it as a thing). the volta at line nine is supposed to do the philosophical work, not just decorate it.
