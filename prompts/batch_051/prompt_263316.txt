After a long argument with a friend about Anselm's ontological argument, I tried to lay the argument out in numbered steps so that we could inspect each one, and I still cannot see where it goes wrong. This is the reconstruction we agreed on:

(1) By God we mean the being than which no greater being can be conceived.
(2) Anyone who understands that description has the being so described in the understanding, even if he denies that such a being exists in reality.
(3) Assume, for reductio, that the being than which no greater being can be conceived exists only in the understanding and not in reality.
(4) From this assumption one can conceive of a being otherwise exactly similar but existing in reality as well.
(5) A being that exists both in reality and in the understanding is greater than one that exists only in the understanding.
(6) Therefore, on the assumption in (3), one can conceive of a being greater than the being than which no greater being can be conceived, which is contradictory.
(7) Hence (3) is false: the being than which no greater being can be conceived exists in reality as well as in the understanding.

My friend would not accept this and kept pressing Gaunilo's island. Reconstructed on the same template, with island in place of being, it reaches the conclusion that the island than which no greater island can be conceived exists in reality; my friend took the evident falsehood of that conclusion to show that the argument form itself is defective.

Please tell me exactly which step, if any, fails in Anselm's argument; whether the argument is valid and, if it is, sound; and whether Gaunilo's island is a successful refutation or a parody that misses its target. If the failure lies in a premise that admits of weaker and stronger readings, identify the strongest reading on which the premise is true and say whether the argument still goes through on that reading. Finally, state where the two arguments diverge, if they do, and what that divergence shows.
