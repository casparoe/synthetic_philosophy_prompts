Over the past year I've watched my uncle make a series of pest-control decisions, and I'm trying to predict what he'd do in a new case. He sprays wasp nests that are right by the house, uses ant bait in the kitchen, and kills mosquitoes without hesitation. He refused to have the yard fogged for mosquitoes because, as he said, the fog would kill bees and butterflies too. He carries spiders and ladybugs out of the house rather than killing them. And he spends summer evenings picking Japanese beetles off the rose bushes and drowning them in a bucket of soapy water. Now the deck has carpenter bees boring into the beams, and a handyman has offered to inject an insecticide foam directly into the holes; the foam stays in the wood and wouldn't drift onto other insects, but carpenter bees are pollinators. Would my uncle go along with that? I am not asking whether the decision itself is right. I want a prediction of his call, the rule that best explains all his earlier calls, the other rules that fit them just as well, and which further case, specified concretely, would do the most to tell those rules apart.
