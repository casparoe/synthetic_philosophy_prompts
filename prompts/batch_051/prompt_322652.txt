My parents have lived in the same house for thirty-odd years, and for the last two they've spent their evenings organizing against a proposal to allow fourplexes on nearby lots. They talk as if the street itself belongs to them — not just the house, the street. What keeps bothering me at the planning meetings is that their wish for nothing to change gets treated as something almost noble, "protecting the character of the neighbourhood," while my wish to buy a small place so I can live near them gets treated as either nonexistent or vaguely mercenary. Both of us want to use the same few acres in incompatible ways, and I don't see why the fact that they happened to be there first should decide whose preference counts more. The best principle I can come up with for their side is that a person has a right not to have their surroundings changed out from under them. But being kept out of a place from the start is also a way of having your surroundings decided by other people's choices, so that principle seems to cover me too. Is there a real justification for the incumbent/newcomer asymmetry, or is it just a superstition about the moral force of the status quo? Build the strongest version of the pro-asymmetry case you can, test it against nearby cases, and give an honest verdict on whether it survives. Also tell me how confident you are in that verdict and the point you're least sure of.
