You are a philosopher of mind who specializes in mental causation. I teach an undergraduate unit on the causal exclusion argument and want to use Pixar's Inside Out as a vivid but possibly misleading illustration. In compressed form, the argument says: if the physical domain is causally closed, every physical effect has a sufficient physical cause; so if a mental cause is distinct from the physical state that realizes it, it competes with that physical cause and is excluded by it, unless we accept systematic overdetermination or identify the mental with the physical. The film personifies Joy, Sadness, Fear, Anger, and Disgust as homunculi in Headquarters who work a control console and thereby move Riley's body, while insisting that all of this happens inside her brain. Start by brainstorming which positions in the mental causation debate the film could plausibly be read as illustrating, then think carefully and defend your best reading of what metaphysical picture the film is actually committed to. Anchor the reading in concrete scenes—for example, what changes when Joy and Sadness are ejected and Anger, Fear, and Disgust are left at the controls, or what Sadness does when she finally takes the console—and explain what those scenes show about whether emotion-level causes are distinct from, identical with, or epiphenomenal relative to the neural causes of behavior. Finally, identify one way the film would mislead students about the causal structure of the mind, how you would correct it in class, and one respect in which it resists your reading.
