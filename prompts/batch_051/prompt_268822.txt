Take the familiar no-vehicles-in-the-park example, the one usually used to illustrate a rule's core of settled meaning and a penumbra of disputed application. The rule has an easy center (a delivery truck plainly counts) and a familiar fringe (bicycles, mobility scooters, electric kick-scooters, drones, wagons, parade floats), and the standard debate asks how legal interpretation should handle those fringe cases. Now change the setting from a civil regulation of park use to a criminal statute. The city enacts the following:

No person shall bring a vehicle into a public park. A violation of this section is a Class A misdemeanor, punishable by up to one year in jail and a fine of $10,000. A police officer may arrest without warrant any person the officer has probable cause to believe has brought a vehicle into a public park. The Commissioner of Parks may exempt any device from this section where the public interest so warrants.

The legislature intentionally leaves the term vehicle undefined, and the legislative history says nothing about the fringe devices listed above. The Commissioner has adopted no general standards; exemptions are granted ad hoc. The city's own enforcement data show that e-scooters and bicycles are ticketed in some parks but not others, and that drivers of expensive cars are much more likely to receive a warning than e-scooter riders.

Run the void-for-vagueness analysis on this statute rather than the ordinary interpretive analysis the example is usually made for. Address both standard inquiries: whether an ordinary person can tell what conduct is prohibited, and whether the statute supplies standards that constrain enforcement rather than inviting arbitrary or discriminatory arrests. Then confront the core-and-penumbra point directly: every rule has clear cases and unclear cases, so why should open texture become a constitutional defect just because the penalty is criminal?

Commit to a definite answer. Should the statute be struck down as void for vagueness, or upheld, and which of the two inquiries does the main work in that conclusion? Finish by saying what your answer implies about how precise a criminal law must be, as distinct from the interpretive tolerance we give civil rules, and where the boundary between ordinary open texture and unconstitutional vagueness should be drawn.
