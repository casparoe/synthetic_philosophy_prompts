I'm a PhD student in moral psychology working on character education, and I keep coming back to the old debate about whether competition builds character or corrodes it. Depending who you read, competitive sport is either a school of the virtues or a machine for producing cheats, sore winners, and kids who think there worth is their ranking. The weird thing is both sides seems to have decent evidence for different claims: kids in competitive programmes show persistence and resilience, but also more strategic fouls and lower empathy in some studies. The debate has run for over a century and nobody moves. My working theory is that the sides aren't talking about the same thing by 'competition'—one means a zero-sum scoring structure, another means a culture of ranking and comparison, and a third means an adversarial stance toward opponents—and these might have different effects on character, so the evidence only looks contradictory because it's all lumped together. Is that diagnosis right, or is the real disagreement somewhere else? And if I'm onto something, what would count as progress: a distinction, a study design, a change of question, or something else?
