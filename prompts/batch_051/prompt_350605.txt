I've got a rule for how I read testimony from amnesty hearings, and I want to know wether it's fair or just cynicism dressed up as method. Because amnesty is conditional on full disclosure, I treat every confession as at best half-true: I assume the witness has admitted just enough to qualify and is shaping the story to look like a political act rather than a private grudge or ordinary greed, unless there's independent corroboration. A friend says I'm comitting the genetic fallacy, since someone can confess for self-interested reasons and still be telling the truth, and the full-disclosure test gives at least some incentive toward accuracy rather than just a plausible story. So: is my discounting defensible? Is there a principled rate of discount short of my flat near-zero? And does the institutional setup change the ordinary rules for assessing a witness's credibility, or does an interested witness remain an interested witness however the questions are put to them? I'm mostly thinking of the South African TRC amnesty hearings, but I'd like to know how far the answer generalises.
