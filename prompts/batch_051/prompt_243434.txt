The two positions to translate. Reductionism about testimony (the Hume line): testimonial justification is never basic. When someone tells you P, your justification for P is only as good as your independent evidence that reports of that kind track the truth. The classic problem for it is circularity: how do you get the evidence that testimony is generally reliable without allready trusting testimony somewhere in the loop. Anti-reductionism (the Reid line): testimony is a basic source of justification, more like perception or memory. Absent specific defeaters, you're default entitled to believe what you're told, and a kid can be justified in trusting their parents without first building up a reliability track record.
