People doing population ethics talk past each other about how consciousness and happiness combine. To keep things concrete, here's a stripped-down setup. A possible world is just a list of beings, each with a consciousness score c and a happiness score h, both numbers from 0 to 20, where 0 is none and 20 is the max. Consider three clean views:

- additive: score a world by summing (c + h) over all beings
- multiplicative: score a world by summing (c × h) over all beings
- lexical (consciousness-first): compare total consciousness across worlds first; only if tied, compare total happiness

I need a case built to order, for a section where I'm arguing that multiplying consciousness and happiness isn't the same thing as making consciousness lexically prior, even though people run them together. Construct exactly two options, P and Q, with at most four beings between them, such that additive and lexical both prefer P, while multiplicative prefers Q. Require all beings to have c > 0 and h > 0 (no zeros), all three verdicts strict, and, this is the part I don't want to just hand you, P's total happiness has to be lower than Q's.

Then walk through the scores for the three views, and say which single feature of the numbers is doing the work to make multiplication peel off. After that, show it's really that feature: tweak one number in P, keeping everything else fixed, so multiplicative flips and all three agree, and say what that tells us. Finally, give me the best objection a multiplicative person would make against the original case. I want to know wheter the disagreement is real or just an artifact of how the scales get drawn.
