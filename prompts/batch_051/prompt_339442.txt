As the philosopher on our university's animal care and use committee, I have been asked to prepare the analysis for a proposal that has exposed a tension in our own precedents. Our past decisions run as follows. We approved a mouse protocol involving moderately painful procedures, on the ground that although mice clearly feel pain, their mental lives are too simple for that pain to carry as much weight as suffering embedded in a richer psychological life. We rejected a chimpanzee protocol, holding that the animals' self-awareness, memory, and capacity to anticipate the future placed nearly any invasive use beyond justification. We rejected, with comparable resolve, a pig protocol involving social isolation and moderate pain, because pigs' emotional lives make boredom, frustration, and isolation serious harms even if pigs are not exactly chimpanzees. We approved a zebrafish protocol with modest refinements, judging that fish are probably sentient in only a thin and transient way, and we treated an oyster protocol as effectively outside welfare review, since we saw no credible evidence of a felt point of view in bivalves.

The proposal now before us would use octopuses in invasive neuroscience, with tissue damage and persistent behavioral disruption. Octopuses display sophisticated learning, problem-solving, and individually distinctive exploratory behavior, but they are so far from us phylogenetically that our usual assumptions about vertebrates transfer uncertainly, and whether their pain-related responses are accompanied by an actually felt, unpleasant state in the sense we attribute to vertebrates is a genuinely open question. I can reconstruct the record as tracking degree of sentience or as tracking cognitive sophistication, and the two readings appear to make different predictions here; there may be other readings as well.

Should we approve the octopus protocol if we want to be consistent with our own record? Which of our earlier decisions is the closest analog, what principle about the grounds of moral status would we be adopting under each of the main readings, and what further case would most cleanly separate the surviving readings?
