In my work covering science and ideas, I keep noticing a split that I cannot easily justify. Consider two reporters covering the same clinical trial. The first changes an effect size in the published results and is fired for fabrication. The second files a scrupulously fact-checked piece that omits the limitations the authors stressed, foregrounds a technically true but unrepresentative statistic, and carries a headline that is accurate on its face yet leaves readers with the opposite impression of what the trial showed. The audiences of the two pieces walk away with the same false belief and may act on it in the same way. Yet nearly everyone inside journalism and out treats the first wrong as far graver than the second. Is there a principle that makes a fabricated claim a different kind of wrong from an engineered misimpression, or is the asymmetry a professional convenience we have mistakenly given moral weight?
