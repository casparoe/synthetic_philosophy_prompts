I've been going back and forth on anthropics for years, the way you do when you read rationalist blogs, and I keep landing in the same spot. The self-indication assumption strikes me as basically forced by Bayesianism once you start treating "I exist" as an observation: an arbitrary observer is more likely under a hypothesis with more observers, so I should update toward observer-rich theories just by finding myself alive. It's more principled than SSA because I don't have to choose a reference class, and it mostly dissolves the doomsday argument, which I'll admit is part of the pull for me. But the presumptuous philosopher feels like a reductio: two theories agree on all the non-indexical evidence, except one says the cosmos contains enormously more observers, and SIA tells me to be close to certain of the more populous theory purely because this one observer exists. I can't acutally get myself to update like that; it feels like treating one's own existence as evidence for a claim about the universe that has no empirical content beyond itself.

I don't want the standard survey. Then actually commit: tell me what would justify giving up the intuition, what would justify giving up or amending the theory, which you'd do, and whether there's a reading of probability or evidence on which both sides survive.
