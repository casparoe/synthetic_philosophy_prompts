I got into an arguement that's been sticking with me. A friend claimed Descartes would have to admit that a current LLM thinks, because the language test he gives in the Discourse on Method — no machine can arrange words to respond sensibly to whatever is said in its presence — was basically a seventeenth-century Turing test, and it has now been beaten. I think that's too quick, but the usual counter-move (Descartes would just call it an automaton and be done) also dodges how much weight Descartes put on flexible speech as a public sign of reason. So I want a serious prediction, not the stock answer. Put Descartes in a room with GPT-4 for an afternoon and reconstruct his position. Which commitments would bear the load: the Discourse's two tests for telling a real human from an imitation, the animal-machine doctrine, the Meditations' insistence that the one thing he cannot doubt away is that he is a thinking thing? And which of those would come under real strain because the machine appears to clear the language bar he said no machine could clear? Would he double down and say token-probability output is just clockwork — and can he say that without undermining his own criterion, which is supposed to be checkable from the outside? Or would flexible conversation have to stop being, for him, a sure sign?

I'm after the counterfactual history: what he'd surrender, what he'd fight over, and where the pressure would force him to re-draw the line between speech as evidence of thought and speech as organised noise. Don't just assert a conclusion — take the view's internal tensions seriously, and make clear which premise is doing the work at each step.
