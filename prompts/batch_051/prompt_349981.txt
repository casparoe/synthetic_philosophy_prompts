I'm a PhD student in a geophysics group, and we have a weeks-long standoff about a prior that none of us can settle. We are conditioning a Bayesian model of a reservoir on borehole data, and for one key parameter—mean grain diameter—the only thing we can agree on before seeing the data is that it lies between 0.1 and 1.0 mm. One lab member argues that we should put a uniform prior over diameter, since treating every diameter in that interval as equally probable is the only maximally neutral way to represent our ignorance. Another points out that the quantity that actually matters downstream is grain volume, which scales with the cube of diameter, and that a prior flat over diameter is not flat over volume; had we parameterized the unknown as volume instead, the "uniform" prior would have looked completely different, and the two versions of the model give meaningfully different posterior probabilities for, say, the total mass exceeding a threshold. A third lab member says this shows that the whole idea of a uniquely neutral prior is incoherent, and that we should just pick whichever parameterization connects to the decision we will make with the estimate. Each position sounds plausible to me in isolation, but they cannot all be right. Is there a principle that decides—or dissolves—this disagreement, and what should our group actually do?
