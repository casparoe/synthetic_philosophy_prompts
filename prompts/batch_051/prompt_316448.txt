I run sourcing for a small clothing brand — me plus two assistants, no background in any of this theory stuff — and twice a year we go through around forty factories and decide which ones get next season's orders. For years the same question has been eating at me. A lot of our factories pay wages that look terrible by our standards but are honestly better than what people could earn nearby, sometimes by a lot, and when I ask workers through a translator they mostly say they want the work. By the better than their next-best-option test, the factory comes out fine. But I don't trust that test, because it seems like it would let almost anything slide: a factory could be the best option around and still force overtime, or pay below what it could easily afford, or hire people who are in debt and stuck. I need to seperate a hard factory in a poor place from one that is taking advantage of how few options its workers have even while making them better off.

What I want is a decision procedure my team can actually run on a supplier, not general principles. For each step, tell me what to ask, what a good enough answer to keep going looks like, when the procedure says no and we walk, and when it should hand the call to me or the founder because it's a values question rather than a facts question. It has to be runnable by non-academics in about half a day per factory, using a one-day visit with a translator plus whatever paperwork we can get. Then show me how it runs on three factories. One: a factory that pays about one and a half times the going local wage and is safe, but requires mandatory overtime in peak season with no real worker say. Two: a factory that pays the going local wage and meets every local law, but that wage does not cover what people need to live on, the owner could easily pay more, and we know it is our cheapest supplier by a lot. Three: a factory that pays low by our standards but much better than the brick kilns most of its workers came from, and if we leave they will mostly drift back to the kilns. I want to see the procedure give me an answer I won't like in at least one of those, and be honest about where the procedure breaks down or where it is smuggling in a value judgement while pretending to be a checklist. I don't want a reading list or citations, I want something we can use on site.

Also write it as straight continuous prose — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just paragraphs like a long email, because that is how it will circulate to the team.
