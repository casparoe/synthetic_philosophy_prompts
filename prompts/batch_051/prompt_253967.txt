I'd like your all-things-considered credence in the following proposition: constitutive micropsychism can, at least in principle, solve the subject-combination problem. By "solve" I mean specifying, without circularity and without merely asserting a brute psychophysical correlation, a relation R such that micro-subjects standing in R compose into a genuinely unified macro-subject — not merely a system of interacting subjects. If you regard the proposition as ill-posed as stated, state the precisification you are willing to adopt and give your credence under that precisification. Give me a number, and decompose it into the sub-questions doing the work, including at least: (a) whether the apparent unity of ordinary human consciousness is real or partly an introspective artifact; (b) whether a primitive phenomenal-bonding relation is an intelligible possibility or merely presupposes the macro-unity it is meant to explain; (c) whether it is legitimate to demand an intelligible relation at all, or whether that demand smuggles in an objectionable bias against acceptable brute facts; and (d) whether neural integration and synchrony could be the physical correlate of R, rather than only the physical side of a subjective composition that remains unexplained. Assign a rough probability to each sub-question, show how they combine into the final number, state which sub-question your credence is most sensitive to, and say what evidence or argument would move that sub-question most.
