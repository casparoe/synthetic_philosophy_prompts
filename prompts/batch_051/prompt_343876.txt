I'm a phd student in information science working on digital preservation, and I keep needing a workable sense of "abandoned" as applied to software, but every time I try to pin it down it slips. I don't mean orphan work in the copyright-law sense — an unlocatable rightsholder — though its adjacent: I mean when a codebase or project gets left behind. Give me a working definition of "abandoned" that sorts these cases:

(a) a utility whose last commit was in 2017 and whose listed maintainer's email bounces;
(b) a paid app that its vendor end-of-lifed last year: the company still exists and sells a successor, but won't patch the old version;
(c) a library with no release in six years because the maintainer says it's feature-complete and he'll still fix security bugs;
(d) a repo whose maintainer is active on other projects, has marked this one unmaintained in the README, but still occasionally merges a trivial PR;
(e) a repo untouched for seven years, while a fork with a new maintainer has regular releases and thousands of users; and
(f) a source-available codebase with no license, whose creator stopped responding years ago.

Part of the trouble is I keep mixing up the visible state of the repo (commits, issues, releases) with facts about the owner (gone, reachable, intending to return), and these pull apart, especially in (c) and (d). I'm confident (a) is abandoned and (c) isn't. I flip-flop on (e): from the original repo's perspective it's abandoned, from the project's perspective it obviously isn't. I have no idea about (b) and (f), and (d) is the one that makes me doubt the word does one stable thing at all. Propose a definition, run it through all six, and tell me which verdict is least secure or where the definition has to be stretched. Also please seperate what's settled among people who actually work on this — maintainership, open-source governance, preservation, maybe law — from what's genuinely contested. I'd rather hear that there is no settled definition than have one community's convention presented as the consensus.
