I am preparing a short position statement on the moral force of the question "what if everyone did that?" and I would like an audit of whether the commitments below hang together before I circulate it. My current commitments are these:

(1) I treat "what if everyone did that?" as my default test for wrongness. It seems to me to explain, better than any appeal to actual harm, why fare evasion and small-scale tax fraud are wrong even though a single act makes no detectable difference, and why a lying promise is wrong even when the deception would never be discovered.

(2) I follow Hare in holding that universalizability is a purely formal constraint on moral judgment: in judging an act wrong I commit myself to judging any relevantly similar act wrong, but this consistency requirement by itself yields no first-order moral conclusions.

(3) I also think that once universalizability is combined with impartial concern for the interests of everyone affected, something close to preference utilitarianism follows; and I take the drowning-child case, in the form made famous by Singer, to show that I am thereby required to give much more of my income to prevent easily avoidable suffering than I currently do.

(4) At the same time, I accept at least one deontological constraint that is not merely a heuristic for maximizing good consequences. I think it is wrong to punish an innocent person in order to prevent a riot, even in a case where no one would ever find out and where doing so would leave the affected interests better served overall.

(5) I am uneasy about how the test behaves at the level of act-types. Run coarsely, it seems to condemn innocuous career choices: if everyone became a nurse, or a farmer, or a poet, the world would collapse, yet choosing any of these careers is not wrong. I do not know whether this shows that the test needs to be restricted to properly specified maxims, or whether the test itself is too coarse to carry the weight I give it in (1).

I would like a consistency audit of these five commitments. Which pairs are in genuine tension, and which only appear to be? Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. For each, say what would have to be given up on each side to restore consistency, and then identify the single revision that would do the most to make the whole set hang together.
