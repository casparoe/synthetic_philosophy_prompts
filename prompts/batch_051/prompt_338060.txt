I'd like you to act as an expert on institutional design and on the "systems change vs. direct service" debate in the effective-altruism-adjacent world. Think carefully, and start by brainstorming before you commit to the analysis; I'm more interested in the mechanisms and the failure modes than in whether the idea sounds nice.

I'm on the board of a small donor collaborative. We keep re-litigating the same argument — whether to fund marginal, legible stuff like bednets, deworming, and cash transfers, or to fund groups trying to shift laws, budgets, defaults, and procurement rules. We're considering a rule to force the question. Here's the proposal:

Every grant we make to an organization whose primary activity is direct service must be paired with an equal-sized grant to a different organization whose primary activity is changing an underlying rule (a law, regulation, budget line, default rule, industry standard, or similar). The direct-service grantee certifies the pairing: their theory of change has to name the upstream change they regard as the root cause of the problem they work on, and they must propose the partner group. The upstream group then gets their half with no delivery conditions, but the direct-service half only gets approved if the same committee approves the upstream pairing. And heres the kicker: after two years, each pair is reviewed, and if the upstream group hasn't produced a "concrete rule-change event" (a bill introduced, a regulation amended, a lawsuit filed, a procurement policy altered), the direct-service grant gets clawed back.

Before we pilot this, take me through how it would actually behave in the wild. What does it incentivize among direct-service charities, among self-described systems-change orgs, and among our own grantees and committee members? Where does it get gamed first, and what do the games do to which problems end up funded or quietly dropped? Is the clawback clause a bigger source of distortion than the pairing rule itself? And could any tweaks fix the worst failure modes without turning the whole thing back into an unconstrained giving free-for-all?
