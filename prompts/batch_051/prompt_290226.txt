I'm a trustee of a small Holocaust education charity that vets teaching packs for local secondary schools. A history teacher has sent us a proposed Year 9 scheme of work that teaches the Holocaust inside a comparative unit called 'the dark side of progress', alongside the Congo Free State and the Bengal famine, organised around railways, censuses, registration, and the division of administrative labour. She says this is just what Bauman was getting at when he argued that the Holocaust was not a relapse into barbarism but something modern bureaucratic rationality made possible, and that pupils nowadays think the Nazis were monsters, so the lesson doesn't land. Another trustee wants us to refuse endorsement: making the Holocaust one case study among others buries what was unique about an ideologically driven attempt to murder every Jew, which was not a side-effect of administration. I need a short memo for next week's board meeting recommending whether we endorse, reject, or endorse with required changes. Please give a concrete bottom line with a time frame, and make sure the memo engages the strongest point on the losing side honestly rather than strawmanning it.
