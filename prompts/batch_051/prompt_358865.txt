For a seminar on the epistemology of long-run social prediction, write the desiderata that a Malthusian-style theory must satisfy to count as a genuine empirical theory rather than a doctrine that can be indefinitely postponed with the claim that it was right but early. A theory of this kind typically predicts a long-run tendency toward resource scarcity, demographic pressure, or diminishing returns, with the predicted crisis lying beyond the theorist's lifetime and thus available for postponement as data come in. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that motivates it. Mark each as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp, and identify which single requirement does the most work in blocking the right-but-early defense. If some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable for one theory, say so and explain why. Keep the emphasis on the criteria themselves rather than on grading existing theories against them. Format the response as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
