People discussing the surprise exam paradox often treat the judge's announcement — roughly, that an examination will be given on one day next week and that the students will not know in advance which day it will be — as a single claim whose truth is the whole question, and they take the students' backward induction to show that the announcement is self-defeating. But the phrase "will not know in advance" seems to me to blur together several different requirements: not provable from the announcement alone, not predictable by any means whatsoever, not justifiably expected on the morning in question, not knowable even after being given partial information, and perhaps others. Could you separate the precise claims the announcement might be making, say for each whether it is harmless or substantive or outright false, and then say which of those readings would actually allow the students' reasoning to establish that the judge could not have made a true announcement?
