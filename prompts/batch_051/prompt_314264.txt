Consider a widespread industry practice: developers of large language models label confidently fabricated outputs as 'hallucinations', and respond to the problem chiefly by appending disclaimers that the system may produce inaccurate information. They rarely call these outputs lies, and rarely treat the model as insincere. What view of assertion, testimony, and truthfulness does this labelling practice presuppose? Does it presuppose something like Frankfurt's distinction among lying, bullshit, and error—and, if so, does 'hallucination' fall closer to bullshit than to mere error? Does the disclaimer practice implicitly commit the developers to denying that the model asserts anything at all, and is that denial consistent with the way these systems are trained and marketed to produce fluent, confident, declarative prose? Say where the practice is strictly committed to a philosophical view and where it is underdetermined, and what the developers would have to say differently if they accepted that bullshit consists in indifference to truth rather than in an intention to deceive.
