I've been going back and forth with a friend about Black's two-sphere universe, the one where all that exists is two exactly similar iron spheres some fixed distance apart and nothing else. I get the standard anti-PII move: the two spheres share every intrinsic property, and they seem to share every relational property too (each is the same distance from the other, each lives in a universe with exactly one other sphere, and so on). So if the identity of indiscernibles says distinct things must differ in some property, we'd have to collapse the two spheres into one, which is absurd; so PII either fails or has to be propped up with something like primitive thisness. That part I understand.

But here's the variation I can't sort out. Change the universe so there are three exactly similar iron spheres in a straight line, with the middle one equidistant from the two outer ones (distance d between neighbors, 2d between the two outer ones). Now the middle sphere and each outer sphere are easy to distinguish by relational properties: the middle is distance d from two spheres, while an outer sphere is distance d from one sphere and distance 2d from the other. So far PII is happy. But the two outer spheres still seem to share every property: each is distance d from the middle, distance 2d from the other outer sphere, each is an endpoint of the line, each is in a world with exactly two other spheres, and so on. So the defender of PII is, as far as I can see, in exactly the same trouble as before — just for the outer pair rather than for the whole universe.

Is she though? I'd like you to take the standard moves people make in response to Black's original case — refusing to count purely relational properties, appealing to haecceities or primitive thisness, denying that the two-sphere world is genuinely possible, saying the spheres are distinguished by their histories or by the particular bits of iron that make them up — and tell me which of those moves survive the three-in-a-line variant, which get harder, and which get easier. In particular: does the middle sphere give the PII defender any new resource for telling the two outer spheres apart? I keep wanting to say "the one to the left of the middle sphere" versus "the one to the right," but in otherwise empty space I don't know whether that names a real qualitative difference or just a feature of how I've drawn the diagram. And does it matter that the middle sphere is now discernible from every other thing, while each outer sphere is discernible from one thing but not the other? For what it's worth, I'd most like to keep the principle that distinct objects always differ in some respect, and I'd rather not pay for primitive thisness; but I'm not sure I can have both after this variant.
