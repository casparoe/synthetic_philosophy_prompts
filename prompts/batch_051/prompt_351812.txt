My housing co-op pools everyone's earnings and votes on requests from the common pot, and we keep saying we're running on the from-each-according-to-ability, to-each-according-to-need ideal. Looking back, our actual decisions are: we covered Priya's insulin and physio after her crash; we paid Sam's bus pass so he could keep his warehouse job; we refused Lena's pottery course even though she said pottery was her real self; we bought Tom a secondhand laptop when the online training for a call-center job required one and the house had no shared machine; and we refused Marco's car repair, even though he drives for work, because he could borrow his brother's van. Now Dana wants us to cover a six-month part-time coding bootcamp. She doesn't need it for her current job, but she says it'll triple her income and therefore her contributions to the pot. What should we vote if we want to be consistent with our own past selves? Which older decision is the closest analog? If our precedents genuinely underdetermine it, what principle would we be adopting with each option, and what future request would finally show which principle we'd actually been running all along?
