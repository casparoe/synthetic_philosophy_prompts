I've been rereading Nietzsche's essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life — the one where he separates monumental, antiquarian, and critical history. I'm one of those people who reads this stuff in their spare time, no training, so forgive me if I get the terms a bit off. But the part that hooked me is what he says about critical history, the way the past sometimes has to be dragged before a tribunal, interrogated, and condemned so we can go on living. I've basically convinced myself that the critical mode is the only honest one. Monumental history feels like it flatters us with fake greatness, antiquarian history feels like it wants to embalm everything, but critical history at least admits that the past was full of cruelty, stupidity, and bad verdicts, and that sometimes you have to break with it. My position, I guess, is that a person or a culture should relate to the past mainly through this critical mode, even if its painful, because the alternatives are self-deception.

What I want to know is: what would actually count against that position? Not just the obvious point that Nietzsche himself warns critical history can cut away your roots and leave you unable to build anything — I know he says something like that, and I take it as a cost worth paying. I mean, what evidence, arguments, possible cases, or consequences should move me? If a culture went fully critical, what would the world have to look like for that to be a mistake? For example, would it count against the view if critical history turned out to depend on a set of values that it can't ground — like if condemning the past presupposes a standard of judgement that itself comes from the past? Or if it turned out that the more you "convict" the past, the less you understand it, because understanding requires some sympathetic reconstruction? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I'd like to know which of them a committed defender of the critical mode would concede as real costs and which they'd dismiss as Nietzsche just being romantic about roots. Also be honest: is this position actually risky, or have people qualified it into unfalsifiability? Because I don't want to hold a view that can't be wrong.
