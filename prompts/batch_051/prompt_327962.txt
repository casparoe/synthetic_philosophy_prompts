So I just spent the walk home arguing with my friend about how much risk a city should regulate, and I still can't pin down her rule. Her positions on recent local votes: she backed twenty-mile-an-hour zones with speed humps, backed mandatory helmets for kids riding bikes, backed a patio smoking ban, backed a rule that private pool owners install isolation fences, backed a requirement that all-night corner stores have security cameras—but she opposed the big-soda cup cap and opposed banning indoor tanning for adults. She insists it's all one consistent principle, and I can see two ways to cash it out: either she wants to protect people from risks they never chose to take while leaving chosen private pleasures alone, or she never wants to ban or ration a private pleasure but is fine with add-on safety rules and relocating the risky activity. Now the council is voting on whether adults who rent kayaks or paddleboards from the city dock must wear a lifevest the whole time. What should I expect her to say, if she's being consistent? Which of her earlier positions is the closest analog? If her record underdetermines it, what rule would each answer be committing her to, and what one further proposal would cleanly settle which rule she's actually running?
