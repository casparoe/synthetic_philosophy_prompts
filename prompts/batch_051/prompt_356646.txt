In philosophy, people keep drawing a contrast between counterfactual theories of causation and interventionist theories of causation, and I want to know how much of that contrast survives being restated in my framework. Assume I know my own causal-inference vocabulary cold but have almost no philosophy-of-science background beyond the rough descriptions I give here.

Take the two families one at a time. Second, the interventionist tradition in the Woodward style: the idea that causal relations are characterized by what would happen under idealized interventions on a variable, so that manipulability and invariance play the role that bare counterfactual dependence plays for the first family. Translate each into potential-outcomes and do-calculus vocabulary as precisely as you can, and flag every place where you have to fudge the mapping. Finish with your own judgment about what the residue shows: a genuine gap in my vocabulary that I should try to patch, or evidence that the counterfactual-versus-interventionist contrast is less substantive than its rhetoric suggests.
