I recently read a blog post that summarizes the theorem like this:

"In the 1950s, John Harsanyi proved that if everyone's preferences conform to the standard axioms of rational choice under uncertainty, and society as a whole has preferences that conform to those same axioms, then society's preferences can be represented by a utility function that is a weighted average of the individuals' utility functions — provided only that society respects the Pareto principle, meaning that whenever every individual prefers one option to another, society prefers it too. Crucially, Harsanyi showed that the weights assigned to each person must be equal, because the axioms force us to adopt an impartial standpoint from which no one person's preferences count for more than anyone else's. The theorem is therefore widely regarded as a formal proof of utilitarianism: any society that is rational in the same sense its members are, and that defers to unanimous individual preferences, is committed to maximizing average utility. What remains after the proof is mostly the practical problem of measuring everyone's utility on a single common scale so that the averaging can actually be performed."
