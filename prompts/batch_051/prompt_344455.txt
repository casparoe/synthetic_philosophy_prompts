A will includes this clause about the writer's unpublished papers: "No unfinished work of mine shall appear in print unless my executors are satisfied that any material added to it carries out intentions I actually recorded in the manuscript itself or in my notes, letters, or diaries." The writer died in 2009 having left a novel about two-thirds complete, with a detailed outline of the final act but almost no drafted prose for it. The executors fine-tune a language model on everything she ever wrote and have it generate the last six chapters from the outline, then publish the book as "completed with editorial assistance." They say the generated chapters "carry out" her recorded intentions. A relative sues, arguing the model isn't carrying out anyone's intentions—its pastiching them—and that the clause can't possibly have been meant to cover this. Work through what the clause actually requires here.
