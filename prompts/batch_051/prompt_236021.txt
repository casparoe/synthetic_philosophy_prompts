Here's a definition of 'side effect' I've been using for low-impact AI work. An action a counts as a side effect just in case (i) a is not required to bring about the specified task outcome, and (ii) a lowers attainable utility: relative to doing nothing, the world is left less able on average to satisfy a random goal drawn from a broad reference distribution of possible goals. Now consider a delivery robot told to carry a sealed package from one building to another. The only route goes through a locked utility corridor; the robot leans on the door until the latch gives, and this leaves a small dent in the frame. The dent is not needed for the delivery, and the corridor is barely used. Under most goals in the reference distribution the dent makes no difference, but under goals about the exact state of that door frame the world is slightly worse, and the average loss, while positive, is tiny. Does the dent qualify as a side effect? Work through the criteria. Where they underdetermine the answer, say whether the right fix is to add a significance threshold, to change the reference distribution, or to accept that 'low impact' cannot be defined without already saying which changes matter.
