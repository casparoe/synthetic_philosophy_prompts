Yet we don't ordinarily treat such choices as transgressions at all, let alone transgressions of the same kind as fraud or assault. And since a theory that turns normal life into a constant stream of wrongdoing is more implausible than any of the premises used to defend it, philosophers standardly take the demandingness objection to have shown that act-utilitarianism has to be abandoned in favor of something like rule-utilitarianism, which judges acts by whether they follow rules whose general acceptance would produce the best outcomes — and so releases us from the duty of ceaselessly maximizing."
