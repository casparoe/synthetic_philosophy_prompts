American courts will throw out a criminal law as void for vagueness if it doesnt give ordinary people fair notice of whats illegal and if it invites cops and prosecutors to enforce it however they like. A law against annoying conduct in public has been struck down, while a law against reckless driving stands, and nobody would say reckless is precise. So what picture of language, or of what a law is, does the void-for-vagueness doctrine actually presuppose?
