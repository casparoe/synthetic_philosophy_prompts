I need a working definition of "corruption" as applied to politics—not a legal test, but the concept behind the word. Please give me a definition that correctly classifies each of the following cases, and tell me where it strains.

(a) A senator accepts a large campaign contribution from an energy company and soon after votes for a bill the company wants, while genuinely believing on the merits that the bill is good policy.

(b) A utilities regulator approves a firm's rates for years, then retires and immediately takes a salaried position with that same firm.

(c) A mayor awards a city contract to a friend's company without competitive bidding, at well above the market price.

(d) A wealthy individual funds an outside group that runs attack ads against a candidate; the funding is disclosed and the ads contain nothing false.

(e) A voter gives a hundred dollars to a candidate whose platform she already supports.

(f) A first-time candidate funds almost her entire campaign out of her own fortune.

(g) A legislator votes to keep a military installation open largely because it employs thousands of people in her district, though she also thinks the base is strategically marginal.

I am confident that (c) is corrupt and that (e) is not. I can argue (a), (b), (d), (f), and (g) both ways.

I suspect the word is doing two jobs at once: one roughly descriptive—something like breaking official rules or making an explicit deal—and one condemnatory—something like the wrong kind of power over a public decision. If you agree, give me both definitions and say which cases move between them; if not, give one definition and defend it. In any case, run all seven cases through your definition(s), state where the definition delivers no answer or a surprising one, and say which single verdict you are least secure about.

Keep the entire response to no more than 500 words. Open with your definition in one sentence, then apply it.
