I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I keep returning to one question: whether human values are genuinely many distinct things, or a single thing appearing in different guises. Before I read further, I want the specification of what a theory in this area must deliver — a requirements list rather than a survey. It should cover the cases and phenomena a theory must handle (hard choices; options that feel incomparable rather than merely differently priced; lasting regret after choosing between two good options; values that emerge or fade over a life); the distinctions it must not collapse (a value versus a preference, conflict between values versus uncertainty about which action is best, trade-offs versus reduction of one value to another); and the everyday psychological regularities it must stay consistent with, stated without relying on named studies. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that earns it its place, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp. Then briefly score monist, pluralist, and hybrid two-level views against the list, identify the single requirement that does most to separate them, and say whether any pair of requirements looks jointly unsatisfiable. Please write in British English.
