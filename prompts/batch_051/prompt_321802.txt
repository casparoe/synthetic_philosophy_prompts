In the letter-string analogy microdomain, problems have the form "S is to T as U is to ?". Consider the problem "abc is to abd as iijjkk is to ?". Two candidate answers dominate. One is iijjkl: it takes the object corresponding to c in abc to be the rightmost letter of iijjkk, namely the final k, and replaces it by its alphabet successor. The other is iijjll: it takes the corresponding object to be the rightmost group of identical letters, namely kk, and replaces each member by its successor. Here is an argument that the problem has no determinate correct answer.

(1) To solve the problem, a solver must determine whether the object in iijjkk corresponding to c in abc is the rightmost letter or the rightmost group of identical letters.
(2) The data of the problem — the two strings and the relation "is to" — are equally compatible with both correspondences.
(3) If the data are equally compatible with both correspondences, then no fact of the problem makes one correspondence more correct than the other.
(4) If no fact of the problem makes one correspondence more correct than the other, then the problem has no determinate correct answer.
(5) The dispute between iijjkl and iijjll is substantive only if the problem has a determinate correct answer.
(C) Therefore the dispute is not substantive, and the problem is at bottom ill-posed.
