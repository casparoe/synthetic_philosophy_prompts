I am compiling arguments for the conclusion that an LLM's fluent, apparently belief-like outputs — such as a confidently asserted answer that Paris is the capital of France — should not be treated as expressions of genuine belief. So far I have: (1) the training-goal argument, that the system is optimized only for statistically plausible next-token prediction, so its outputs are pattern completions rather than representations that are about the world; (2) the grounding argument, that its tokens lack the causal-perceptual ties to their referents that give genuine mental states their aboutness; (3) the standing-state argument, that the model retains no commitments across sessions, so there is no stable belief content that could outlast or be independent of a particular prompt; (4) the action-role argument, that belief is individuated partly by its function in practical reasoning and action, and the model does not act on its avowals; (5) the consciousness argument, that belief requires phenomenal understanding, which the system lacks; and (6) the truth-aim argument, that outputs are optimized for plausibility and helpfulness rather than accuracy, so they are insensitive to truth in a way incompatible with genuine belief. I take these six to be the standard objections. Give me one further objection that is genuinely distinct from all of them — not a rewording, special case, or combination — and then say honestly whether it succeeds.
