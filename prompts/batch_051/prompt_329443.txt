ok so I've been working on this idea I call the 'trade-off scale' for comparing how bad two different people's pain is. The problem is you can't feel someone else's pain, so you can't lay both pains on one ruler. My fix: don't measure the pain itself, measure what each person would give up to get rid of it — but not in dollars, cause a dollar to a rich kid and a poor kid are different things. Measure it in the person's own happiness. Like, if A would give up 15% of her overall life-satisfaction to cure her migraines, and B would only give up 5% of his to cure his depression, then the migraines are literally worse than the depression, even if I'd personally pick the migraines. The central claim is that 'percentage of your own total wellbeing you'd sacrifice' is a unit any pain can be converted into, no mind-reading needed. I think this solves the whole apples-and-oranges problem. Is this actually a new idea or did someone already work it out under another name? Be honest: is it coherent, and where exactly does the conversion break — what does it wrongly count as worse?
