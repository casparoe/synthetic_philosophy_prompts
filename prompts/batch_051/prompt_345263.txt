so in RE we spent a lesson on the golden rule and i've ended up with a set of gut reactions to cases that won't line up, and i want someone to tell me what principle, if any, i'm actually reaching for, or which of my verdicts i have to drop.

here are the cases.

1. my friend missed chemistry and i texted her my notes, because i'd want someone to text me their notes if i'd missed. this feels obviously fine.

2. for my dad's birthday i nearly bought him the sci-fi book i most want, even though he never reads fiction. i didn't, and i think it would have been wrong, not just a bad gift.

3. a kid in my class goes by a nickname i would hate to be called, but i use it because they asked. that feels right to me, and it feels like the golden rule gets this one backwards if it tells me to call them what i would want to be called.

4. my friend failed her driving test and said she wanted to be left alone; i kept messaging and turning up because i would want a friend to check on me constantly if i were crushed. looking back, i think i was wrong.

5. another friend asked whether her new haircut suited her. i would genuinely want to be told the truth, so i said it didn't. i still think that was right, but the same "i would want honesty" reasoning would not make it okay for me to correct someone in front of the whole class, even though i personally wouldn't mind that.

my current candidate is to swap my wants for the other person's wants: do to them as they would want done, not as i would want. that handles 2, 3 and 4 pretty well, but it makes 5 shaky, because she probably wanted reassurance rather than criticism. and it has a worse problem: it seems to make whatever the other person wants final, so my little sister would get to quit piano the moment she wants to, even though i'm nearly certain she'll regret it later. that seems off too.

so: is there one repaired version of the golden rule (or one small set of candidates) that gets all five cases, or close? if not, which verdict should i abandon and why? and what does your principle say about the piano case, and about a friend who asks me in advance to hold her to her own rule not to let her back out of something, then later begs to opt out? oh, and could you say how confident you are in whatever you land on, and which point you're least sure of. british english please.
