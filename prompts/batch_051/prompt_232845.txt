I'm trying to figure out what I actually believe about reduction and emergence by going off my gut calls on cases. The temperature of a gas just is the mean kinetic energy of its molecules; that one feels like full reduction and I'm not bothered by it. Water being liquid is entirely a matter of how the H2O molecules are arranged and moving, but it feels more like an explanation of liquidity than a statement of what liquidity is—though I'd still call myself a reductionist there. A traffic jam is nothing over and above the cars, their positions and speeds, yet the jam causes the delay and there's no extra jam-stuff, so again reduction. Pain is not reducible to C-fiber firing or any neural state, because there's something it's like; I won't budge on that. An enzyme catalyzing a reaction just is its amino acid sequence, the shape it folds into, and the surrounding conditions; no extra ingredient. But on 'alive' I flip-flop: a bacterium is just molecules and reactions, but 'life is nothing but chemistry' sounds wrong in a way I can't quite locate. Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? What does it say about the case I left out, whether a bridge's safety reduces to the strength of its parts and the loads on them? And which of my verdicts does the principle force me to drop?
