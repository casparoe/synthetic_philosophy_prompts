I've been having the same argument with two friends for weeks now and I'd love to see it turned into a proper dialogue. One of them is a hardcore Davidsonian about metaphor: she says when someone utters something like "my lawyer is a shark," the sentence just means, literally, that the lawyer is a cartilaginous fish, and since that's false, the whole interest of the metaphor is in what the utterance causally gets you to notice—relentlessness, circling, going for blood—not in any seperate kind of "metaphorical meaning." My other friend is into conceptual metaphor theory, Lakoff and Johnson style. He says we only get those effects because we're running an underlying conceptual mapping (PEOPLE ARE ANIMALS, or maybe LAWYERS ARE PREDATORS), and that's what does the cognitive work. His favorite move is that the systematicity is the giveaway: the same mapping lets you extend the metaphor in new ways (the lawyer smelled blood, went in for the kill, got cornered) and the extensions come out coherent rather than random, which he thinks a bare "it makes you notice things" story can't explain. The Davidsonian's reply is that "systematic" just means the associations happen to be ones we've learned to have, and calling them a mapping doesn't add explanatory power.

Could you write a dialogue of around 900 words between the two of them, maybe at a bar after a conference talk, where they actually respond to each other's actual points instead of talking past each other? I'd like the Davidsonian to grant that metaphors do cause us to notice things but push on whether we need to posit any metaphorical content in the semantics to account for that, and the conceptual metaphor theorist to push on why the noticings are so structured and inferentially productive if there's no mapping doing the work. Don't make either of them an idiot or a strawman. And please have them end by locating the real crux—maybe whether "meaning" should be cashed out as truth-conditions or as something broader like conceptual structure, or whether the seeing-as phenomenon is semantic or just causal-psychological—rather than settling it. No citations or footnotes, it's a dialogue not a term paper.
