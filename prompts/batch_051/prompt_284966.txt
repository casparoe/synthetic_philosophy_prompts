A grad student keeps pushing back that this is exactly the anachronism that keeps people from understanding Greek mathematics, since magnitudes aren't numbers, there are no variables or equations, and the construction isn't there to be solved. I'd like you to audit the analogy. Give me a correspondence table: what in Euclid is meant to play the role of a variable, an equation, an operation. Then say where the mapping breaks down, and sort the disanalogies into the ones that matter for the claim that Euclid had algebra and the ones that are just surface differences. Finally tell me whether my shorthand is harmless pedagogy or whether the student is right that it distorts more than it reveals — and if there is a better analogy, give it.
