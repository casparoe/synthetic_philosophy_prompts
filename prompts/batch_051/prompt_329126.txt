I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher, and I keep getting pulled into "fairness review" for our hiring model. Our team agrees we absolutely shouldn't feed race or sex into the model, and we also agree that something like years of relevant experience is fine. But the middle of that spectrum feels totally arbitary, and every time I push for a principle I get shrugs. I'm not asking about the exact legal list — I can google what the EEOC says — I want the underlying rule: a working definition of what makes a trait a protected characteristic (or a suspect basis for discrimination, if those aren't the same thing) that gets these cases right: race, sex, age, disability, pregnancy, religion, national origin, height, physical attractiveness, a strong regional accent, criminal record, credit score, education level, political affiliation, whether someone smokes, marital status, whether someone has young kids at home, veteran status, a genetic risk for a disease, IQ score, and personality type (say, introversion).

My own intuitions are messy. I'd say height and accent are fair game as long as they're actually relevant to the job, but using a criminal record feels wrong even when it's statistically predictive, and I can't say why it's different from, say, a bad credit score. I have no idea what to do with political affiliation, marital status, or genetic risk. So: propose the definition, run it through the cases, tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts.
