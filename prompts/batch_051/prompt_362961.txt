Write an essay on the following controversy about Heidegger's account of readiness-to-hand. In his analysis of equipment, familiar tools normally withdraw into absorbed use; when something malfunctions, the tool and the network of purposes around it allegedly become explicit. One reading treats this breakdown as a distinct, revelatory event: it is what first makes the structure of practice visible, as opposed to mere glitches within an unbroken flow of absorbed coping. Another reading holds that breakdown has been exaggerated: skilled coping already involves noticing, adjusting, and repairing, without any sharp transition to detached, present-at-hand awareness. On this second view, breakdown is simply one phase of the same ongoing activity, not a door to a different mode of intelligibility.

Your task is to reach and justify a definite conclusion in this debate. To keep the discussion from rehashing familiar examples, test the two readings against a concrete case: an automated industrial system that monitors its own equipment, detects deviations, and schedules maintenance without any human ever becoming aware of a problem. Does this count as the kind of breakdown Heidegger describes, and what does that show about whether breakdown is a distinct revelatory mode or an ordinary feature of skilled coping?

Structure your answer in four titled sections:

1. The dispute (about 100 words): state exactly what is at stake between the two readings.
2. The case for breakdown as revelatory (about 250 words): give the strongest version of the view that breakdown discloses practice in a way smooth coping cannot.
3. The case for breakdown as ordinary coping (about 250 words): give the strongest version of the view that breakdown is continuous with absorbed activity.
4. Verdict (about 200 words): defend your own conclusion, using the automated-system case as evidence.
