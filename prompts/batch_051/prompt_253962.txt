I am a novelist, and the current draft of my book turns on an argument between two characters about cultural appropriation in the visual arts. I want their exchange to be philosophically serious rather than a restaging of familiar slogans, so I am asking you to write the dialogue for me. One character is a painter who holds that the wrong of cultural appropriation is not best understood as theft of property but as a breach of a community's authority over the interpretation and public circulation of its visual forms: what is taken, on this view, is not the form itself but control over what the form is allowed to mean. The other is a curator who holds that forms are not the kind of thing that can be gated in this way; on this view, cross-cultural borrowing is how visual languages have always developed, and the only norms that should govern it are craft norms such as accuracy, credit, sensitivity, and the quality of the resulting work. Write the dialogue between them. The two should be evenly matched and genuinely responsive to one another; each should be able to restate the other's position in terms the other would accept, and each should answer the actual argument just made rather than changing the subject. Please write in continuous prose only, as an exchange of speech would naturally be written, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
