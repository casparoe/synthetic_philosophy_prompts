I spent most of my working life on the computational side of fluid dynamics, and have only recently begun reading the philosophy of determinism and prediction with any care. I keep running into an ambiguity I would like a definition to resolve. The word “chaotic” is used for at least three different things: a system whose nearby trajectories diverge exponentially, a system whose orbits are dense in some invariant set, and a system that is practically unpredictable from finite-precision data by a bounded agent. These do not always travel together, and I want a working definition of “chaotic dynamical system” that forces the distinctions into the open.

Here are the cases I would like the definition tested against: the Lorenz equations at the usual parameter values; the logistic map on the unit interval at r = 4; the logistic map at a parameter value where its attractor is a stable period-three orbit; an irrational rotation of the circle; a quasi-periodic flow on a two-torus; the map x → 2x on the real line; a cryptographically strong pseudorandom number generator regarded as a deterministic map of a large finite state space; and a strange nonchaotic attractor, by which I mean an attractor that is geometrically fractal but has no positive Lyapunov exponents.

My pretheoretic judgments are these. The Lorenz equations at those parameter values and the logistic map at r = 4 are as clearly chaotic as anything I know. The irrational rotation and the quasi-periodic flow are clearly not. The others I am genuinely unsure about, and I would like the definition either to force a verdict or to explain precisely why they are ill-posed test cases. I am not looking for a survey of existing definitions; I want one definition, or one clearly organized family of definitions, made to face these cases. Please state the definition, run it through all eight cases, say where it delivers a result that surprises you, and then revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. If you must distinguish senses, say which sense does the philosophical work in discussions of determinism and predictability, and which does not.
