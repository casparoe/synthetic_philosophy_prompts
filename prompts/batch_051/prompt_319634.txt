I've been stuck in a running argument with a few friends about whether the two American political parties are mirror images of each other when it comes to arguing in bad faith, and I had the idea of drafting the document an adversarial collaboration between the two camps would actually sign. One camp — call it the symmetry view — thinks Democrats and Republicans are near mirror images on this: both sides caricature what the other side actually believes, both sides hold their own side to looser standards and the other side to stricter ones, and both sides are convinced the other side is uniquely dishonest while their own side is just being reasonable. The other camp — the asymmetry view — thinks that's a false equivalence: it says one side has moved much further into closed, conspiracy-friendly media and anti-democratic behavior, and that saying both sides are equally bad is itself a motivated way of protecting the worse side. I'm not asking you to pick who's right. I want the constructive text the two camps could put their names to after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

I'd like it to read like a jointly signed open letter, so write in continuous prose with no headings, no bullet points, no bold, and no table — the way you'd write an email. In whatever natural order it takes, it should include: the empirical and definitional claims both camps can sign without flinching, made substantive rather than tautological; the residual disagreements, stated exactly, with each side's own view phrased so that side would say yes, that's what I mean; then two or three cruxes, the sub-questions where a settled answer would actually move at least one camp; and for each crux, the kind of study or real-world event both sides would accept as a fair test, with each side's prediction recorded ahead of time. I suspect one crux might be about double standards when identical norm violations are put in front of each side, and another might be whether the media-ecosystem asymmetry shows up in ordinary people's information diets or mostly in elite behavior, but pick the real ones, not just my guesses. Also try to catch places where the argument has been partly semantic — like what "bad faith" or "echo chamber" even means here — and seperate those from the genuine stakes.
