(1) when i'm actually reasoning i have to trust the reasoning i'm doing right now — there's no outside vantage point — and i take that trust to be rationally fine, not just pragmatically forced;
(2) an ideal reasoner should be able to inspect its own inference rules and spot a broken one, the way i can read a function i wrote and see its got an off-by-one error;
(3) i'm convinced by the löbian point that a consistent system strong enough to do arithmetic can't prove its own soundness, and i think that generalizes: no reasoner can fully verify its own reliability from the inside;
(4) i hold a reflection principle: if i think that after doing some specific further thinking (sleeping on it, talking to a few people) i'll conclude P, then i should conclude P now — deferring to future me just is updating now;
(5) i also think self-modification is real and good: i've caught flaws in my own inference rules before, and post-revision me is usually more reliable than pre-revision me, so i trust post-revision me over current me.

which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix? rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and say which single revision would clean up the most.
