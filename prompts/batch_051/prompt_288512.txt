I've been chewing on a quick argument a friend made about social construction, and I could use a second pair of eyes. It's numbered so you can point at a step.

(1) A piece of paper is money only because enough people in a society take it to be money and treat it as such. (2) If a fact holds only because people take it to hold, then it is not an objective fact. (3) Therefore, that the twenty-dollar bill in my pocket is money is not an objective fact. (4) But whether it's money is also an objective matter: a cashier can be simply wrong about it, and there's a fact of the matter about who's right. (5) Since (3) and (4) contradict, the idea that money is socially constructed is incoherent.

I suspect the argument is too quick, but I can't seperate which step is doing the sneaky work. Is the argument valid? If not, which step exactly fails, and what's the corrected version of that step that lets someone keep the social construction claim about money without the contradiction?
