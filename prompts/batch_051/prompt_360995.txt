Write a dialogue between two philosophers of fiction who disagree about whether "Sherlock Holmes" refers to anything. Call one Anne: she thinks fictional names pick out abstract artifacts, things like characters that Conan Doyle literally brought into existence by writing the stories, roughly the way a law or a national holiday exists only because people said and did certain things. Call the other Will: he thinks "Sherlock Holmes" is an empty name that refers to nothing at all, and that talk about Holmes is really shorthand for talk about what the stories prescribe imagining.

They meet to hash out a concrete puzzle. A fan publishes an unauthorized pastiche set in Conan Doyle's London in which Holmes is shown with a small scar on his left temple and a lifelong hatred of aniseed. Are these now true in the Conan Doyle fiction? If Anne says authors create characters by writing, why does only some writing count? If Will says truth in fiction is just what the authorized stories tell us to imagine, then what should he say about things never stated but universally inferred, like Holmes having two arms? And what do either of them say about stuff the stories simply dont settle, like whether Holmes's grandmother's name began with M?

Have them genuinely argue. Each should press the other's weak points, concede when the other lands a real blow, and revise their account on the fly. Dont strawman either side; give both their best tools. Try for around 1,200 to 1,800 words, and let it end unresolved rather than with a tidy verdict.
