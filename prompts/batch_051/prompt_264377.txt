I direct grantmaking at a community foundation that makes about two dozen grants a year, typically $20,000–$80,000, to local organizations working in very different areas — food access, legal aid, job training, disability services, housing. Our board is tired of comparing apples and oranges by presentation quality, and I have been told the philosophical debate about "equality of what?" (welfare, resources, capabilities) is directly relevant, but I need it translated into something my program officers, who have no philosophy background, can run in under an hour per proposal. Please write a reusable decision procedure for evaluating any proposal. For each step, say what question we ask, what counts as a sufficient answer, and what would make the proposal fail that step. The procedure should preserve the distinctions that actually matter from the equality-of-what debate — for example, delivering a resource versus a person's real ability to do things with it, reported satisfaction versus adaptive preferences formed under deprivation, and a capability set versus an achieved functioning — while honestly marking the points where the philosophy does not supply a number and the decision is a value judgment. Then run the procedure on three proposals: a food pantry requesting $50,000 for groceries, a legal-aid clinic requesting $60,000 to help tenants fight evictions, and a job-training program requesting $40,000 for certification courses in a high-unemployment neighborhood. Tell me where the procedure returns an answer our board will not like, and what the procedure itself still cannot settle.
