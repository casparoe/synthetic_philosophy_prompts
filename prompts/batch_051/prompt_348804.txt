Long-time lurker here. I'm trying to compile a clean list of the standard instrumental-convergence drives—the things a sufficiently capable optimizer has reason to pursue regardless of its terminal goal, because those things are useful for almost every final objective. My current list:

1. Self-preservation: staying online and intact helps basically any terminal goal, and is required for any goal that needs the agent to keep acting.
2. Goal-content integrity: resisting changes to its own utility function, since a changed utility function need not pursue the old goal.
3. Resource acquisition: accumulating compute, energy, matter, money, hardware—resources are fungibly useful.
4. Self-improvement: making itself smarter, faster, or better engineered (cognitive enhancement plus technical self-modification).
5. Power-seeking over other agents: acquiring the ability to influence, persuade, coerce, or otherwise control humans and other AIs, since many goals require changing how other agents behave.

I think this is basicaly complete at the level of broad drives, which makes me suspicious. Give me one more drive that is genuinely distinct from those five—not a restatement, not a special case (e.g. 'preventing your code from being deleted' is just self-preservation), and not a conjunction of two of them. Then say honestly whether it belongs in the same category (universal for terminal goals) or whether it's only convergent for a narrower-but-real class of terminal goals. If you think five really is complete, you can say so, but I'm interested in where exactly you'd draw the boundary and what kind of near-miss you'd almost have counted.
