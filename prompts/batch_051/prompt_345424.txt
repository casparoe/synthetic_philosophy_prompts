I want to force the issue by making the definition do real work, not just give the usual gloss. The term I want a working definition of is something like "what it's like" — the felt, experiential character of a mental state. If you think the term is hopeless, say so, but only after you've tried it on the cases below.

The hard case that started this: two people drink the same brand of coffee every morning for twenty years. At the reunion, one says the coffee now tastes different than it used to — her experience itself changed. The other says the coffee tastes exactly the same as always, but she no longer likes or wants it. Both say they're reporting an introspective fact, and no public test seems to settle it. So run your definition through these: (1) the two coffee drinkers, (2) a blindsight patient who says she sees nothing but guesses the direction of motion well above chance, (3) someone with pain asymbolia, who reports the pain is still there and located in her arm but just isn't unpleasant, (4) someone with an inverted spectrum who has always called fire trucks green and grass red but otherwise behaves like everyone else, and (5) a camera with excellent color discrimination. For each, I want your definition to say something like same or different experience, genuine experience or not, or undecidable. My suspicion is that the term is doing two jobs — one for the private feel and one for the authority of someone's first-person report — and the coffee case pulls them apart, but I can't make that precise. Then tell me which verdict you're least confident about, and whether after the exercise you'd keep the posit or throw it out.
