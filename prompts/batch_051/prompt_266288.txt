I'm an undergraduate in an upper-level seminar on moral psychology and the emotions—eighteen students, ninety minutes per meeting. Our current unit asks whether judgments of purity and sanctity (treating certain objects, bodies, or rituals as inviolable independently of harm) belong in ethics or should be debunked as disgust and convention dressed up as moral reasons. I'd like an in-class activity that lets students feel the force of such judgments before the issue is named: a structured role-play or deliberation with real low-stakes choices in which they discover their own resistance to defiling, selling, or even pricing something they had not antecedently called sacred—ideally so that merely entertaining the trade comes to feel like a second, distinct wrong. Please give the full setup: materials with no real money, roles, rules, a timeline, and a debrief that maps specific moments of the exercise onto the philosophical question—what work the disgust or respect response is doing, whether a harm-based account can capture it, and where the exercise cannot reproduce the gap between a felt prohibition and a justified moral principle. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it without turning the debrief into a lecture.
