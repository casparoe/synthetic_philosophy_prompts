You are a professional moral philosopher specializing in the ethics of compromise and value pluralism. I am a PhD student working on whether bargains between groups with incompatible non-negotiable commitments can be justified. Please think carefully and begin by brainstorming the strongest considerations on each side before you write. Then write an essay on the following controversy. A deontological animal-rights group holds that killing animals for food is categorically wrong; a religious community holds that eating a particular meat on a feast day is obligatory. They consider a bargain: the community roughly halves its non-festival meat consumption, and in exchange the animal-rights group suspends its public campaign against the community's feast-day slaughter. Neither side accepts any common metric of value, yet each side ranks the combined package as better than the status quo by its own lights. Can the compromise be permissible even though each party, through its own action, deliberately helps bring about a state of affairs it regards as seriously wrong—and does the fact that each side's own ledger improves turn this into a genuine moral gain? Weigh the main arguments on both sides, including the objection that moral commitments are not fungible bargaining chips and the objection that consenting to another's wrongdoing is a further wrong. Do not end non-committally; reach and justify a verdict.
