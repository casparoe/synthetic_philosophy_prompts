Could a professional philosopher who thinks about interpersonal ethics and moral psychology help me with an argument I've been turning over? I want you to think carefully and start by brainstorming the possible readings of the key terms before you commit to a verdict. My question is whether we owe it to people we're close to to be legible — not just honest on a question-by-question basis, but scrutable in a deeper way: the other person can see, roughly, what we're about and where our decisions are coming from. A concrete example: a close friend makes a decision that changes my life too, and when I ask why, they say they can't explain it and that they just need this right now. I felt shut out, and maybe wronged, but I can't tell whether that feeling tracks any actual duty.

I tried to formalize what I think is going on:

(1) When you enter a close trust-based relationship, you invite the other person to rely on your treating their reasons as reasons that carry weight for you.
(2) For someone to rely on you that way, they must be able to form a working model of how you'll respond, and forming that model requires access to your actual reasons, not merely a track record of your outward behavior.
(3) You can't consistently ask for that kind of reliance while refusing the other person such access; that would be asking them to trust you while making the basis of that trust uncheckable.
(4) So, in any trust-based close relationship, you owe the other person enough transparency for them to see at least roughly what moves you.
(5) Therefor, there is a duty of legibility to the people close to you.

I have a stubborn sense that the argument overreaches somewhere, maybe in the slide from reasons for particular actions to being legible as a whole person, or maybe in the idea that trust requires access to actual reasons rather than sustained patterns of behavior. But when I try to say which step fails and under exactly what reading, I end up just repeating that the conclusion feels demanding. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and what would the corrected version of that step be? If it is valid, what exactly does the duty require of someone whose inner life is messy even to themselves?
