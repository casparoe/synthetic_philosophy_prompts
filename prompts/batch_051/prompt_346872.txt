Here is the task I want carried out. Take the heterogeneity problem for hedonism as the central constraint: a warm bath, relief when pain stops, pride at a child's success, the pleasure a masochist takes in pain, delight in an elegant proof, schadenfreude, and an objectless good mood seem to share no felt quality, no bodily location, and no object. Produce a list of desiderata that an adequate theory of pleasure must meet, with the concrete case or consideration behind each, and mark each item as one of three kinds:

1. non-negotiable, in the sense that a theory that fails it has stopped being a theory of pleasure;
2. required only if the theory is to carry hedonism's load, such as comparing or summing quantities of pleasure across heterogeneous kinds; or
3. optional, a demand imposed by one camp that a rival could reject without absurdity.

In particular, take a position on the following candidate demands, letting the cases do the work: that pleasure is a felt quality, that pleasures have bodily locations, that pleasures have objects (being pleased that something is the case rather than simply feeling good), that pleasure and pain are opposites on a single axis, and that an account of what pleasure is must connect with the normative fact that pleasure seems to give reasons. State which pairs of these demands you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which member of each pair you would relax and why. Finish with a brief scorecard for a pure felt-quality account, an attitudinal account, and a desire-based or functionalist account, judged only against the desiderata; keep the scorecard brief relative to the work on the desiderata themselves.
