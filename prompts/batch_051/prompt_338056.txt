I've ended up in the subjectivist camp on probability — probabilities are degrees of belief, not stuff in the world. What draws me to it: it gives one clean answer to what a probability is (a coherent betting price), it doesn't force me to locate frequencies or propensities somewhere in reality, and de Finetti's representation theorem seems to hand me the math of objective chance for free. If I treat coin flips as exchangeable, my credences update exactly as if I were learning about a hidden bias, so I get the behavior of "there's an unknown objective probability" without having to put the probability out there. But I can't fully shake the contrary feeling. When I say a lump of plutonium-239 has a half-life of about 24,000 years, I don't feel like I'm just confessing my ignorance or my betting temperament; I feel like I'm attributing something to the plutonium itself. Give me both sides at their strongest and a negotiated verdict, not just a winner. What would I be giving up if I debunk the intuition as a projection of my own predictive habits? What would subjectivism have to be amended into if the intuition is tracking something real? And is there a reading of "chance" on which my subjectivism and the plutonium's half-life both survive — and what exactly does that reading cost?
