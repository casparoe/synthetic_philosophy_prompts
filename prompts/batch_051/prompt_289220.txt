Two friends of mine got into an argument a few weeks ago that I still cant fully adjudicate. Marcus, who organized his own birthday dinner, is chronically late; he arrived about half an hour late that night. Priya, who's normally punctual, got caught by a work thing and showed up about forty minutes after the time we'd set. Marcus immediately lit into her: selfish, doesn't respect anyone's time, the works. Priya shot back that he, of all people, can't lecture her about lateness — he was late to the same dinner and is late to basically everything. Marcus said that's beside the point: the judgment is about her lateness, and his own record doesn't make a true claim false. Priya said she wasn't saying it was false; she was saying he lacks standing to blame her. There's a difference, she said, between a true criticism and being in a position to deliver it as blame: a judge with a conflict of interest can reach the factually right verdict and still be doing something wrong by hearing the case. Marcus said that's a weird analogy, and anyway if only people with clean records get to object to lateness, then the people who know the harm best are exactly the ones silenced, which seems backwards. Priya said he was overreading her: she never said he can't be annoyed, or ask her to apologize; she said the finger-wagging, you're-a-bad-person speech is what he's in no position to give, because he exempts himself from the standard he's deploying against her. Marcus said that draws a line without a difference — if he can be annoyed and ask for an apology anyway, then 'standing' is just tone-policing, and talking about it is a way to dodge the actual criticism. They dragged me in to settle it, and I honestly couldn't.

Please adjudicate this move by move: for each step in their exchange, who's right about what and who's overstating; what important distinction either or both are missing; and who, if anyone, is more right overall.
