I'm putting together a teaching note for ML engineers about why our evaluation culture keeps overweighting average-case metrics in AI safety, and I want a running example I can use. Construct me a concrete AI system — ordinary supervised or RL, nothing superhuman, buildable in the next few years — which has a genuine, well-specified worst-case safety guarantee of the kind a paper could state as a theorem, but which is plainly unsafe on average once its deployed. The guarantee must be about safety itself, not an obviously irrelevant proxy, and it can't be gerrymandered: it has to cover a set of inputs the team actually cared about, and a neutral referee would agree the guarantee is substantial. After you've given me the system and setting, do the walk-through: state the safety property, the set of inputs the worst-case guarantee covers, the deployment distribution, and why the worst-case assessment says safe while the average-case assessment says unsafe. Then tell me which structural feature is doing the work, what happens if the team expands the guaranteed set to cover the whole deployment distribution and why that isn't free, and how someone defending the worst-case guarantee would push back against your verdict.
