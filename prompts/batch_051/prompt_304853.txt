Came out of a work argument, believe it or not. We've been rewriting an old service at my job piece by piece for two years; the repo name and the product name never changed, but almost none of the original code survived. A teammate keeps saying it's the same system, and I want to say it's a different one after some point, but I can't point to a line where it changed. Then I realized this is basically the Latin-into-Italian problem. My working criteria for two ways of speaking being the same language are: (1) speakers can understand each other without special study, (2) there's an unbroken chain of children learning it from their parents, and (3) speakers themselves treat it as one language. Now apply that to Latin and Italian. A modern Italian generally can't just read or hear Latin and understand it, so (1) says they're different languages. But there was never a generation in Italy that couldn't understand its parents, so (2) says Italian is just late Latin. Does Italian count as the same language as Latin under my criteria? Go criterion by criterion, tell me where they underdetermine the answer, and then say whether the criteria need fixing or whether 'same language' isn't really a fact about the language itself. Please answer in American English.
