A comparison associated with Daniel Dennett that I keep encountering holds that the self is a center of narrative gravity: the self stands to the stories told of a person as a center of gravity stands to a physical body. The work this analogy is asked to do is deflationary. Just as a center of gravity is not a material part of an object but an abstraction fixed by the distribution of mass, the self is not an inner substance but an abstraction fixed by the narratives about a person, so there is no persisting thing over and above the stories. Please audit the analogy as an argument. Give the mapping explicitly: what corresponds to the body, the mass distribution, the gravitational forces, and the point at which they are taken to act? Then identify the disanalogies, separating those that are harmless from those that undermine the deflationary inference. Finally, state whether the deflationary conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and say whether a better analogy is available for whatever remains of the self.
