I've been defending epiphenomenalism about consciousness for the better part of two decades: phenomenal experiences are real, but they have no causal powers. Pain doesn't make me say 'ouch'; the neural state it accompanies does. I got here through causal closure of the physical plus a refusal to accept systematic overdetermination, so interactionist dualism feels like a nonstarter and functionalism strikes me as changing the subject. But I want a serious map of where the view is exposed. The big cost I'm aware of is the paradox of phenomenal judgment: if epiphenomenalism is true, then my judgment that I'm conscious, and my judgment that consciousness is nonphysical and inefficacious, are themselves produced by nonconscious mechanisms, not by the experiences themselves. My convictions about qualia then lack the kind of causal connection to qualia we'd ordinarily want for justification. Ive always treated that as a bullet to bite — beliefs can be true and justified even if their causes don't track truth in the ordinary way. What I want is for you to tell me what would count against the view, sorted by how decisive it would be, including the objections a committed epiphenomenalist ought to concede have bite rather than just what critics say. Which introspective findings, neuroscientific results, evolutionary considerations, or conceptual arguments would have to obtain for me to be rationally obligated to give the view up, and which are merely awkward? Be specific about what the world would have to look like for the no-causal-work claim to be false rather than just surprising — for instance, what would a brain actually have to do for us to say that the experience itself, and not merely its neural correlate, is doing the causing? Also tell me what, if anything, epiphenomenalism predicts that interactionism or functionalism doesn't, such that a failed prediction would count as a real defeat. And judge honestly whether I've insulated the position to the point where nothing could touch it, and whether that's a real failing for a view like this or just what metaphysics is like. Don't turn this into a literature review; I already know the territory. Please write your answer in American English.
