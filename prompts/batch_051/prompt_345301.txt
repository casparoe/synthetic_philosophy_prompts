I've come around to the view that qualia are an incoherent posit — not just that talk about them is sloppy at the edges, but that the word doesn't pick out any real property. The supposedly private, intrinsic, ineffable what-it's-likeness of experience turns out under interrogation to be a bundle of reactive dispositions, judgments that things seem thus-and-so to me, and loose metaphor. The standard cases that are supposed to isolate the pure leftover never quite do it. You got the taster who says the coffee tastes exactly the same but he no longer enjoys it, and another who says she still enjoys it just as much but the taste itself has changed; the inverted spectrum with no detectable difference anywhere; the pain that's supposed to remain after every cause and effect is stripped away. In each case the alleged residue either shifts when you press on it or collapses into something dispositional we already had words for.

So tell me what would count against this. Not the usual replies — I want a careful map of what should actually move someone who already holds the eliminativist view. Which observations, introspective reports, results from the science of consciousness, or conceptual arguments would be decisive against it, and which merely awkward? Which of these do eliminativists themselves have to concede count against the view, and which do they get to wave off as aiming at the wrong target? Then give me the honest verdict on whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability, and whether that's a real problem for a metaphysical position, or just what you'd expect from this kind of view. If there's a game-over defeater, put it first and say why it can't be waved off.
