I help run a sunday evening discussion group at my community center. its mostly adults, mix of ages, maybe 20 people show up, some work at the warehouse, some are retired, a couple of young people starting out. weve been argueing for weeks about work and free time, ever since someone floated the idea of pushing the local plant to a 35-hour week. half the room thinks its obviously a right to have your time back, the other half thinks forced laziness is insulting, and they point to people in japan working themselves to death (karoshi i think its called) as proof that just capping hours doesnt really work. theres also an older guy who keeps saying no one who works hard ever felt guilty about it, the idle are the problem, which sounds to me like that old protestant work ethic thing.

I want a 90 minute activity for roughly 20 people that lets them actually live the argument instead of just taking sides again. I dont want a lecture up front, and nobody should hear the words "protestant work ethic" or karoshi until the very end.

The thing i want them to feel in their guts is this: that free time can be a kind of trap if choosing it means falling behind, that even when everyone agrees in principle people still cant take the rest theyre owed, and that the people policing the long hours hardest are often workers themselves, not some boss. i also want them to see that putting a legal cap on hours (like the 35-hour week) isnt just a restriction, it changes the whole game so people are actually free to choose the thing they already wanted.

In the past Ive run things with simple role cards and point scoring and people got into it. Can you design me a game along those lines? Something with hidden constraints so that some people are quietly cornered into volunteering for long hours, and a payoff that makes choosing 35 hours look bad for an individual even though the group would be better off if everyone did. Then halfway through let the group vote whether to impose a 35-hour cap on themselves for the rest of the game, and see who fights it and on what grounds.

Give me the exact rules (no real money, maybe points and a small prize), the role cards word for word, materials I need to print, a timeline for the 90 minutes, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the three ideas. The work ethic: why does someone who is exhausted still argue against rest? The 35-hour week: what does a collective cap buy you that individual good intentions cant? And karoshi: what makes something that looks voluntary not really voluntary? Also tell me what will probably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be honest about what a paper-and-points game cannot get across about real jobs and real bodies.

One more thing: a couple people in the group are guaranteed to say the whole thing is rigged to make work look bad. Please build in at least one role or design feature that gives the long-hours view its strongest possible case, so they cant dismiss it as a setup.
