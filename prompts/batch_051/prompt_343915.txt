Imagine Karl Popper had lived long enough to observe the contemporary debate over what has come to be called the extended evolutionary synthesis. The participants agree that organisms modify their own selective environments (niche construction), that developmental processes bias the range of phenotypic variation available to selection, and that some information is inherited through channels other than DNA. They disagree about whether these phenomena amount to a substantial revision of evolutionary theory or are simply facts that the standard neo-Darwinian framework can absorb. Please write a polished essay predicting, as faithfully as possible, how Popper would have come down on this dispute he never saw. In particular, address two questions. First, would he regard the extended synthesis as a genuine empirical advance, as a merely verbal or metaphysical redescription of standard theory, or as something in between? Second, what would he require its defenders to demonstrate in order to show that their framework has empirical content over and above the standard theory? Engage substantively with Popper's actual views on the status of natural selection—especially his early characterization of it as a metaphysical research programme and his later concession that specific Darwinian hypotheses can be tested—but do not merely summarize those views; use them to generate a verdict on a newer controversy. Answer in American English, and do not include citations or a bibliography. At the end, state how confident you are in the overall reconstruction and identify the single point of the argument about which you are least sure.
