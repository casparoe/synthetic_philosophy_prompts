Construct a one-paragraph thought experiment, set in an ordinary institutional context (a workplace, a university, a foundation, or a residential building), in which a single agent satisfies all three of the following at once: her negative liberty (freedom from actual interference by other people) remains constant over the episode; her republican liberty (freedom from dependence on the arbitrary will of another, whether or not that will is exercised) decreases; and her positive liberty (her capacity to govern herself and act on ends that are authentically her own) increases. Do not use slavery, a benevolent despot or kindly master, a hostage situation, or any change in the criminal law; the mechanism should be an ordinary private arrangement such as an alteration in her employment, tenancy, sponsorship, or institutional standing. The case is intended to test whether these three traditions measure distinct dimensions of freedom rather than one underlying thing, so the scenario must generate its verdicts from the internal logic of each tradition; it must not simply stipulate that the agent is simultaneously un-interfered-with, dominated, and self-governing. After the paragraph, show, for each tradition, exactly why it returns its specified verdict about the agent; identify the single feature of the scenario doing most of the work; state how each verdict would change if that feature were removed; and give the strongest objection a republican would raise to the case as constructed.
