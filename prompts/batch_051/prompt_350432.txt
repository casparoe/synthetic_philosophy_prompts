Okay so I've been reading through old LessWrong threads again, and I keep coming back to the pair of scenarios people use to separate updatelessness from regular evidential reasoning. Counterfactual mugging: Omega flips a fair coin, and if it lands heads you get $10k iff Omega predicted you'd pay $100 on tails; but it landed tails, so now all you can do is hand over $100 for no observable reason. XOR blackmail, as I understand it, is meant to break the naive evidentialist intuition: the blackmailer's threat is set up so that your payment and the reveal are entangled in a way that makes paying causally irrelevant, and maybe even logically irrelevant once you're inside the branch. I've noticed a lot of people, myself included at first, want to pay in the mugging because your buying a version of yourself in the heads branch, but would absolutely not pay in the blackmail. The difference between those two stances is exactly the interesting part, and I want a joke that hangs on that difference rather than just 'ha ha decision theory'. So write a joke where the punchline turns on the distinction between counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail—like, the humor should be lost on someone who doesn't understand why paying in one case is rational and in the other case is just being extorted. It can be a dialogue, a one-liner, whatever, but it should make a LessWrong reader go 'yes, that's exactly the disanalogy.'
