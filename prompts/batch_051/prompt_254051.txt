I'm a high school student and a peer tutor for my school's psychology course; one of my duties is to grade classmates' practice short-answer questions and write comments. Below is a question I wrote and a classmate's answer. I would rather not grade from first impressions, since the mistakes on this topic tend to be subtle, so I'm asking you to do the grading.

Question: A textbook says the heritability of adult height in a wealthy country is about 0.8. Explain what that number does and does not mean.

Student's answer: Heritability is the proportion of the total variation in a trait, within a given population, that is due to genetic variation. So a heritability of 0.8 for height means that 80 percent of the differences in height among people in that country are traceable to genetic differences, with the remaining 20 percent due to environmental differences. Since most of the variation is genetic, height is largely fixed by genes, so improving childhood nutrition in that country would have little effect on how tall people grow. By the same logic, if that country's average height is higher than a poorer country's average, most of the between-country difference is probably genetic as well.

Also indicate how confident a grade can be from a written answer alone. If the question itself is badly posed or too ambiguous to grade fairly, tell me that plainly rather than grading it anyway.
