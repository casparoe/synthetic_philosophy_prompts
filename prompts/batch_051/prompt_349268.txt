You are a moral philosopher who is also fluent in game theory and the formal analysis of multi-agent systems. I am a game theorist, not a philosopher, and I want you to translate a philosophical debate into my vocabulary and then report what fails to survive the translation.

The debate: some hold that everyday local conventions, such as tipping a customary percentage, forming an orderly queue, or obeying residents' jaywalking norms, generate genuine moral obligations even for visitors who never consented to them; others hold that these conventions bind only prudentially or instrumentally, and that a visitor's only moral obligations are standing ones, such as avoiding harm, deception, or exploitative free-riding; still others try to split the difference.

Please restate each of these positions as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary of coordination games, Nash and correlated equilibrium, payoff structure, common knowledge, equilibrium-selection devices, sanctions, and off-path behavior. Then give me a residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which require distortion, and which have no expression at all in this vocabulary (for example, the specifically moral force of an obligation, the standing to blame, or the difference between being obligated and its merely being prudent to conform). Finally, tell me whether the residue is a defect of the game-theoretic vocabulary or a sign that these moral positions are less precise than they look.

Please think carefully, and begin by sketching the mapping and noting where you expect it to break before writing the translation.
