I'm on my university's building-and-naming review committee. We've got a petition to take J.S. Mill's name off two things—a residence hall and a chaired professorship in political theory. The petitioners arn't doing the standard he-was-a-man-of-his-time dance; their argument is that Mill is a live case of liberalism and empire, because he spent essentially his whole working life at the East India Company, ended as one of its top officials for Indian correspondence, and helped draft the Company's 1858 petition to Parliament asking not to have its Indian territories transferred to the Crown. On top of that, in On Liberty he makes an explicit exception to his liberty principle for societies he judged not ready for self-government, allowing despotic rule there so long as it genuinely aims at bringing about their improvement. The defenders reply that he held that such rule could only ever be a temporary trust, that he supported women's suffrage and opposed slavery, that his general principles undercut the empire when applied consistently, and that he never even set foot in India, so his civilization talk is exactly the kind of parochial generalization his own method was supposed to catch. Our chair wants a draft memo for next meeting, not a seminar. Recommend one of (a) remove the name from both, (b) keep both unchanged, or (c) keep with a concrete form of contextualization, and be specific about what contextualization would look like. Don't assume the hall and the professorship have to get the same answer. Give us reasons we can actually vote on, and make the strongest case for the side you are recommending against before you reject it. Don't turn it into a general essay on cancel culture—apply it to Mill's India record.
