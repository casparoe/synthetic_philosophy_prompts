We were set to read the opening of section 2(1), which goes: "Subject to the provisions of this Act, the Crown shall be subject to all those liabilities in tort to which, if it were a private person of full age and capacity, it would be subject:— (a) in respect of torts committed by its servants or agents; (b) in respect of any breach of those duties which a person owes to his servants or agents at common law by reason of being their employer; and (c) in respect of any breach of the duties attaching at common law to the ownership, occupation, possession or control of property:" It then has a proviso saying, for paragraph (a), there has to be an act or omission by a servant or agent that would itself have been actionable in tort. I understand the basic case—a Crown driver crashes a delivery van, and the Crown is treated like any employer.
