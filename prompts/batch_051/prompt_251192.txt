I sit on my state's business filing review board, and we have to decide next week whether to accept an amended filing from a small company that wants to put an AI system on its three-person board. The AI would get a full vote and the same fiduciary duties as the human directors. Our corporation statute says a board must have one or more "persons," but our own filing manual tacks on that directors must be "natural persons." The company's lawyer argues the manual's gloss has no basis in the statute: the state has been treating corporations as legal persons for contracts, property, and lawsuits forever, and if a paper entity with no body or mind can be a person for those purposes, an AI that actually deliberates can't be disqualified merely for not being human. I'm not sold, but I also don't think "a person means a human, obviously" will survive five minutes in the meeting.

Could you draft the short opinion I'll circulate before the vote? It should recommend accepting, rejecting, or accepting with conditions, and give reasoning my colleagues could quote. Most importantly, it should honestly engage the strongest argument on the side you don't take. I keep going back and forth on whether "legal person" is just a bundle of capacities rather than a natural kind, since that seems to cut both ways, so I'd like the recommendation to rest on something sturdier then that.
