I'm a PhD student working on hedonism — specifically the heterogeneity problem, the worry that the things we call pleasures don't share any one felt quality, so its not even clear what hedonism claims has value. I've been trying to connect that to Nozick's experience machine, and I have a variant I want to walk through. In the original, the machine gives you a whole simulated life, as rich and varied as you like. In my variant, it gives you one kind of experience repeated forever with no memory of the repetition: a single uniform pleasant tone, no object, no activity, no achievement, no narrative. You can turn the intensity up or down, but every moment is that same tone, and you know this before deciding. Would you plug in? I genuinely can't tell which intuition is doing the work anymore, which is sort of the point. Here's the problem. If the heterogeneity objection is right, the original machine is a messy test of hedonism because it lets people smuggle in real-world values like achievement, love, and authenticity by making the simulated life rich in exactly those things. The uniform-tone machine strips that out and tests a purer, generic pleasantness. But then the anti-hedonist cant fall back on the usual explanations for refusing (wanting contact with reality, wanting to accomplish real things) without it looking like those reasons were riding on content the machine doesn't even have. And if people are now much less willing to plug in, that suggests the original's appeal was carried by variety and meaningful content, which is itself grist for the heterogeneity objection. So how should a hedonist handle the uniform machine, and does the variant change the dialectic or just simplify the original? I keep feeling there's something here about whether hedonic value is one dimension of intensity or necessarily takes varied objects. Help me work through it.
