I'm a novelist, and I serve on the working-conditions commitee of my screenwriters' guild. A studio has asked us to sign off on a schedule that would keep a head writer and two staff writers on a show working seven-day, 75-hour weeks for the last two months before a locked delivery date. The studio says the writers want it, and two of them co-signed a letter saying this kind of marathon is when the work finally gets good. Half the committee wants to reject the waiver: our union historically fought for the 35-hour week precisely so writing doesn't become karoshi, and approving one waiver becomes precedent for every show. The other half says writers aren't assembly-line workers and a consenting writer who treats the screenplay as a calling shouldn't be saved from her own choice by a union. I have to circulate a short decision memo before our Friday meeting, recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with reasons the studio and the writers would actually accept. Make sure the memo honestly engages the strongest version of the work-as-a-calling position, not the straw-man version.
