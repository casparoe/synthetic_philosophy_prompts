I've been trying to get clear on what separates temperance (the virtue) from plain self-denial or repression. Here's my working definition. Someone counts as temperate only if:

1. They actually have the desire they're limiting — it isn't that they simply dislike sugar or didn't care about alcohol anyway.
2. They limit or delay the desire for a reason they can articulate, one that has to do with living well, not just fear of consequences, a doctor's order, or other people's opinions.
3. Their restraint flexes when the reasons change — they can make the occasional exception without it becoming a personal crisis.
4. Their attitude toward the desire isn't disgust or self-punishment; they still think the pleasure is good, just that it shouldn't call the shots.

Now the case. My cousin is doing a 30-day "dopamine cleanse." He's given up alcohol, sugar, TV, music, podcasts, scrolling, and junk food all at once. He's clear that he doesn't think any of these are bad in themselves; he says he wants to stop using them as a reflex to fill every empty moment, and he can give a pretty articulate speech about self-respect and agency. But he also keeps a daily log where every "slip" is recorded, and any slip means he restarts the whole 30 days. He's restarted three times. When his girlfriend's birthday dinner happened to be at a brewery, he went but sat there drinking tap water and refused even the toast, wich struck me as weird rather than admirable.

Does my cousin's cleanse count as temperance on my criteria? Go through the criteria honestly; where the answer is mixed or underdetermined, say whether that's because of my criteria or because of what he's doing. And does his case show that one of the four criteria needs revision?
