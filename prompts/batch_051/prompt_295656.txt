I've been turning over a view I think Nozick floated once: utilitarianism for animals, Kantianism for people. On one floor, animals count because they can feel pleasure and pain, and we're allowed to add those up and trade them off however maximizes the total. On the other floor, people are ends in themselves, so there's no sacrificing one healthy person to save five dying patients who need organs. I like this view a lot. It feels clean, and it tracks my instincts exactly: a farm killing a pig 'humanely' barely bothers me, but a surgeon harvesting a pedestrian does.

The snag is the in-between cases. Some humans—a newborn, someone with a severe cognitive disability—have less thinking, planning and self-awareness than a healthy dog or pig. If my view puts creatures on the top floor because of those capacities, then these humans belong on the bottom floor. But if you tell me we may treat them the way we treat pigs, I recoil. Thats not a half-hearted reaction, its a firm no. If instead I keep them on the top floor, I need to say what keeps them there without letting the dog up, and every candidate I try sounds either made-up or secretly just 'human species.' I dont want to give up the two-floor view, but I also cant stomach where it seems to lead.

So walk me through the options with their actual costs. Should I debunk the recoil as a boundary intuition, something evolution gave me and I shouldn't trust? Should I amend or abandon the two-floor view, and if I amend it, what's the smallest change that doesn't collapse it into plain utilitarianism or plain Kantianism? Or is there a reading where the view and the newborn intuition are compatible? I want to know what would justify each move, not just the move you'd make.
