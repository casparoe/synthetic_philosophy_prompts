I've been doing some small-time sports betting lately, and the more I read the Kelly people the more the geometric view of rationality seems right to me. Maximize expected log wealth rather than expected wealth, because log is what tracks how a bankroll compounds, the Kelly fraction keeps you from ever going to zero, and over a long sequence of bets nothing reliably beats the growth-rate-maximizing stake. It gives one tidy answer in every situation, which I like. But there's one one-off case I can't stop chewing on. A book offers me, as a one-time promo, even money on a coin I know is biased 55/45 in my favor. Kelly says stake ten percent of my bankroll. Expected value says stake all of it. If this really is the only time I'll ever face this bet, I cant make myself believe that betting ten percent isnt just leaving rent money on the table. Is my reluctance an artifact of imagining a single payout instead of a repeated multiplicative process, or is it pointing at a place where geometric rationality overreaches when there is no long run? Give me the strongest version of both positions and tell me which should give way, and on what interpretation of one-shot they might both be right.
