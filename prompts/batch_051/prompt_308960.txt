Is there a name for this? Lately I keep seeing people turn their own ignorance into a way to win an argument, and I don't think "humblebrag" or plain false modesty captures it. On a panel I watched, the speaker opened by saying he wasn't a real expert and would mostly just listen, then every time someone objected he'd retreat to "well, I'm just a beginner here, but that sounds backwards to me" — and the audience read his announced cluelessness as a sign that he was the only honest person on the stage. At work, when a meeting gets tense, the most senior manager says "look, I know no more than anyone else in this room," and the argument ends with his view winning, not because he pulled rank but because admitting he was lost came across as virtuous. The people doing this often seem sincere, so it isn't straightforward fake self-deprecation, and they aren't advertising accomplishments, so it isn't the usual humblebrag; the thing their showing off is how uncertain they are. Does this exact thing have an established name? If it does, how does it differ from false modesty and humblebragging? If it doesn't, can you define and name it, including whether it can ever amount to genuine intellectual humility or whether it's always a status play — and where the line is?
