I'm taking an upper-level political philosophy course, and next week we're working through the debate over what egalitarians should equalize. A friend in the course sent me the following summary of the exchange between Dworkin and Sen, and I'm trying to decide how much of it I can rely on as I draft a paper. His message read:

"Here's the compressed version. The equality-of-what debate asks whether egalitarians should equalize welfare, resources, or capabilities. Dworkin rejects equality of welfare because it would have to give extra resources to people with expensive tastes, since they need more to reach the same level of welfare, and this makes people's shares depend on their ambitions, which Dworkin considers unjust. He instead proposes equality of resources: hold an initial auction in which everyone has equal purchasing power and divide the goods so that no one envies anyone else's bundle, then let the market run from there. On Dworkin's view, inequalities that later arise from people's choices are their own responsibility, and the state has no business compensating people for bad brute luck or for tastes they didn't choose. Sen's objection is that treating resources as what matters is too detached from what goods actually do for a person: a disabled person and a nondisabled person with the same bundle can do and be very different things. Sen therefore argues that the right target of equalization is capabilities, by which he means the functionings people actually achieve, and a just society should aim to equalize those."
