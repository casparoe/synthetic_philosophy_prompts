After retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have been working through philosophy at a pace my earlier schedule never allowed. Two positions I recently studied began to look to me like variants of a single argumentative move, and I cannot decide whether the resemblance is a deep identity of structure or an artifact of the fact that both literatures reach for the same loose metaphor of a causal chain. The first is Kendall Walton's claim that photographs are transparent: when I look at a photograph of someone, I do not merely see a representation of them; I literally see them, in something like the way I see through a telescope or a mirror, because the image was formed through a counterfactual-tracking process that does not require the photographer's or anyone else's beliefs to mediate between the scene and the image. The second is Saul Kripke's causal-historical account of proper names: a name refers to its bearer not because the speaker has a description that uniquely fits the bearer, but because a historical chain of uses traces back to an initial act that fixed the reference. Both positions, as I understand them, locate the relevant relation — seeing on the one side, reference on the other — in a causal-historical connection between subject and object, rather than in the descriptive content the subject brings to the encounter.

What I would like to know is whether this parallel is deep or superficial. The best readings of Walton seem to deny that a hand-made painting is transparent even when it is causally produced by the scene, because the painter's beliefs intervene; Kripke's chains, by contrast, pass through speakers' intentions to use a name with the same reference as the person from whom they acquired it, which sounds like a kind of belief-mediated step. So the two accounts may be running in opposite directions, with one excluding belief and the other merely refusing to require descriptive content. Alternatively, both might be making the narrower point that the relevant relation to the object survives radical error about it. If the parallel is genuine, what should it predict, and is there some prediction it makes that turns out false? I am asking for a precise diagnosis of where the structures match and where they come apart, not for source references.
