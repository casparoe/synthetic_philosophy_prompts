I cover math and the ideas beat, and I keep almost convincing myself of an analogy I can't tell whether its deep or just a vibe. In algebra the two square roots of −1, i and −i, are indistinguishable until you pick one and call it i: the complex numbers don't care which is which, any algebraic fact true of one is true of the other with the labels swapped, and the choice is a convention rather than a discovery. That feels exactly like a problem in my reporting: whether I call the same armed group "rebels," "a government in exile," or "a militia" is underdetermined by the facts, and once I choose, the rest of the piece quietly inherits the choice while the choice itself disappears from view. Is this really the same structure — a symmetry that has to be broken by a non-canonical choice, with downstream claims invariant (or not) under the swap — or does the reporting case come apart in a way that matters, since labels carry valence and causal effects and i and −i don't? Spell out the mapping honestly, say what would have to be true for the mathematicians distinction between canonical and non-canonical choices to transfer to word choice, and name something the analogy predicts that turns out false.
