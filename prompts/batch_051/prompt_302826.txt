I was teaching the Condorcet jury theorem and gave my students these as the conditions: a binary question with a factually right answer, each voter has a better-than-half chance of being right, and the votes are independent conditional on the truth — one person's vote tells you nothing about another's once you know the right answer. Now here's the case I couldn't settle cleanly: ten election forecasters vote yes/no on whether the incumbent wins. Each one has a genuine 60% hit rate on races with polls this strong, so the first two conditions hold. Go through the conditions one by one, say where it passes and where it fails, and wether the shared-evidence problem is really a violation of independence or a sign that 'the voter' should be redefined (like counting each independent poll rather than each forecaster). Then tell me whether majority vote among the forecasters still beats any single forecaster, and what that says about whether the theorem's conditions are too strict or just mismatched to the case.
