Before I read any more accounts of sovereignty, I want the specification first: what must an adequate theory of membership in a customs union or confederation actually deliver? By an adequate theory I mean an explanatory account of what joining such a body consists in and how it changes a member's legal and practical position, not a normative case for or against joining. Write the desiderata for such a theory: the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the legal or economic facts it must be consistent with. For each requirement give the motivating case or consideration, and mark the requirement as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular school. Pay particular attention to whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — for example, whether a theory can consistently hold that members retain undivided and freely revocable sovereignty while the body itself exercises exclusive external competences and can bind them over their objection. If you find a conflict, spell out exactly which requirements clash and what has to be given up. That conflict analysis, not the scorecard, is the part I most want.

Your answer must be a single JSON object with no surrounding prose, following this schema:

- "requirements": an array of objects, each with:
  - "id": a string such as "R1"
  - "requirement": one imperative sentence stating what the theory must do or respect
  - "motivation": the case or consideration that makes this a requirement
  - "negotiable": one of "non-negotiable" or "preference"
  - "camp": which school or camp holds the preference, or null if non-negotiable
  - "excludes": an array of the views or positions this requirement rules out or badly strains
- "jointly_unsatisfiable": an array of objects, each with:
  - "set": an array of requirement ids that cannot all be satisfied together
  - "conflict": a precise statement of why they clash and what must be given up
- "strongest_discriminator": an object with:
  - "id": the requirement that does the most disqualifying work
  - "why": why it does the most work
  - "views_disqualified": an array of the views it knocks out
- "scorecard": an array of at most five objects, each with:
  - "view": a main contender named generally (for example, an indivisible-sovereignty view, a pooled-sovereignty view, a divided-competences view, a federalist view, a post-sovereignty view)
  - "meets": an array of requirement ids the view handles
  - "fails": an array of requirement ids the view fails or strains against
  - "note": one sentence, at most

The requirement list must at least put the theory to these tests: the difference between a free trade area, where members keep their own external tariffs and need rules of origin, and a customs union, where they adopt a common external tariff; the difference between a confederation that binds only member governments and a federation whose law reaches persons directly; a participant with substantial obligations but no vote over the relevant decisions, as in Turkey's position in its customs union with the EU; partial participation, as with the non-EU states inside Schengen, the EEA states inside the single market, or Switzerland's sectoral agreements; and the distinction between losing legal authority and losing or gaining practical capacity. For each, the requirement should say what more a theory must do than merely mention the case. Do not include references or source lookups; I want the analysis itself.
