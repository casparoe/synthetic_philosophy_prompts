You are a philosopher of science who works on mechanisms, causal explanation, and the epistemology of classification. Think carefully, and start by brainstorming several candidate restatements of the rule below before you assess it.

I lead a small team at a hospital system that reviews anomaly clusters — unexpected runs of post-surgical infections, medication administration errors, equipment failures — and decides which clusters should be treated as separate problems and which should be merged and escalated together. Our manual says to merge anomalies only when they arise from the same underlying mechanism. Over several years I have developed a stricter personal rule. Whenever someone on the team says that two clusters share a mechanism, I stop the discussion and require them to state that mechanism as a specific sequence of conditions and events: what triggers it, what propagates it, and what would interrupt it. If they cannot give me such a sequence in a few minutes, I treat the sameness claim as noise and vote to keep the clusters separate.

The rule has served me well: it blocks the kind of loose pattern-matching where people lump together events that merely look alike. But I have begun to suspect that it encodes one narrow picture of what a mechanism is, and that I may be using a demand for detail as a shield against comparisons that are correct at a higher level of description. Two infection clusters might involve different organisms, different wards, and different individual errors, and yet an experienced clinician might be right that they are, in an important sense, the same kind of breakdown — even if neither of us can yet articulate it as one linear causal chain.

I realize this is partly a metaphysical question hiding inside an operational one: what does it even mean for two occurrences to be instances of one mechanism? But I need an answer I can use under time pressure, not just a definition. Is my rule rationally defensible as an epistemic policy, or is it a bias dressed up as a protocol? In particular: when is a sameness judgment justified without a step-by-step mechanism, when is my demand for such a mechanism likely to mislead, and what would a better restated rule look like — one that keeps the protection against false clustering without assuming that “same mechanism” means “identical step-by-step chain”?
