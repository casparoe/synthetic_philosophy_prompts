In his essay “Of Tragedy,” Hume proposes that tragic representations please us because the eloquence with which the poet expresses painful passions converts the pain into a predominant pleasure, especially since we remain aware that the events are merely feigned. I am paraphrasing from the essay rather than quoting it, but that is the mechanism as I understand it. My question: if Hume has already explained how we can take pleasure in fictional suffering, why do contemporary philosophers still treat our emotional responses to fiction as a paradox? Shouldn’t this account from “Of Tragedy” settle the paradox of fiction as well, or is there something about our emotions for fictional characters that his eloquence-based solution leaves untouched?
