Having spent my working life in the natural sciences and only recently taken up philosophy, I have been reading about the result in economics often called the no-trade theorem. As I understand it, the theorem shows that when agents share a common prior, differences in private information alone cannot generate trade, because each party would interpret the other's willingness to trade as evidence against the transaction. Yet I also keep encountering the claim, in discussions of the epistemology of markets, that prices aggregate widely dispersed private information. I am having difficulty seeing how both can be true. If rational agents cannot trade purely on informational differences, how does private information ever enter a price? Should I conclude that the claim about markets aggregating information is an empirical claim that the theorem shows to be impossible, or am I misunderstanding the theorem's assumptions in a way that a more careful reading would correct?
