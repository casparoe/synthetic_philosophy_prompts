I was raised being told the numbers in scripture are doing real work — seven for completeness, forty for a stretch of testing, twelve for the tribes and apostles, a hundred and fifty-three fish as some kind of sign. And I bought it. But the other day I was reading that the genealogy at the start of Matthew is arranged into three groups of fourteen, and my first instinct was "see, that has to be on purpose." Then I realized I could probably find something like that in a phone book if I tried. I counted the number of times the word "the" showed up on a page and it came out to a nice round number, and I briefly felt like that meant something, which it obviously doesnt. So now I'm stuck on where the line is between "the text is using numbers to mean something" and "I'm just pattern-hunting."

How would you actually test a claim like: the big scriptural numbers show up in ways that go beyond what you'd get from a random long text, and that overrepresentation is a sign of deliberate symbolic design? Design the strongest test you can. Say what you'd compare, what you'd measure, what result would make you say the numbers are genuinely doing something, and what result would make you say it's all us. Then tell me what even a clean result still wouldn't settle. Like, suppose seven really is overrepresented in some book — does that show the authors meant it symbolically, or could it just be that seven became culturally contagious and got used without much thought? I genuinely don't know what the actual counts would come out to, so if you're guessing at the data, say so.
