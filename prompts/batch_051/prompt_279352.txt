Researchers often describe Dennett's heterophenomenology as the neutral method for a science of consciousness: it collects subjects' first-person reports of what their experience is like, takes those reports at face value, and refrains from committing to any theory of what is really going on in the mind. I do not see how this neutrality does any work. If the reports are taken at face value as authoritative about the subjects' experiences, then heterophenomenology is just introspectionism under a new name, and all the explanatory weight falls on the very first-person authority the method was meant to bypass. If the reports are instead treated as mere speech or behavior, then the method seems to have stopped studying consciousness altogether and is studying only what people happen to say. What would I be missing?
