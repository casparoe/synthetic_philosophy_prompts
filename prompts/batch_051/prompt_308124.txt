I've ended up defending a fairly specific version of the 'psychopaths know what they're doing is wrong' line, and I want it stress-tested rather than just rehearsing the same moves with a few friends. The view I hold is not the simple one that psychopaths are just like everyone else minus empathy. I think the deficit is real, but it's motivational and affective, not epistemic. On my view, someone scoring high on Factor 1 affective/interpersonal traits typically has intact moral knowledge in the propositional sense: they can classify an act as wrong, produce the usual harm-based justifications when pressed, and they don't systematically fail the moral/conventional distinction in the way the early findings in psychopathy seemed to suggest once you look again at the later work. What they lack is the caring, the felt wrongness, and the ability to deploy that knowledge in real-time deliberation about their own behaviour. The punchline is that when philosophers say psychopaths don't 'really know' that what they do is wrong, they're smuggling in a thicker notion — knowledge-with-understanding, or knowledge-with-feeling, something closer to recognition — and then helping themselves to the conclusion that the deficit is cognitive.

So: what would count against me? I don't want the standard objections I already have replies to; I want the strongest case that the epistemic deficit is real, not merely a deficit in motivation or affect. Walk me through the evidence and the arguments — the moral/conventional task data, what psychopaths do when asked about victims rather than rules, conceptual claims about whether moral knowledge requires the relevant emotions, and anything else that bears on it — and order them from fatal to merely awkward. For each, tell me whether a defender of my sort of view should concede that it counts or may reasonably dismiss it. I'd also like an honest verdict on whether my thin/thick distinction is doing genuine work or just insulating the view against refutation, and, if it's insulating it, whether that should bother me for a view of this kind. Things that would move me personally: evidence that psychopaths fail at deploying moral concepts in ways that can't be traced to motivation, or an argument that the thin/thick distinction can't carry the load I'm putting on it. Please write your answer in American English.
