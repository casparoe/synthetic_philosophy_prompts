Our epistemology course textbook glosses Nozick's tracking theory, and something about the gloss strikes me as off, though I have not worked through the original argument and I am not sure which parts to trust. I want an accuracy audit of the gloss, not a general introduction.

Here is the gloss, roughly as it appears in the book:

On Nozick's tracking theory, knowledge is true belief that tracks the truth. S knows that p just in case: p is true; S believes that p; and, holding fixed the method by which S formed the belief, if p had been false, S would not have believed p. Nozick adds that the belief must also be produced by a process that is reliable in the actual world, since otherwise the belief's being true would be a lucky accident. Because justification is not on this list of conditions, the theory lets someone know a proposition even when they can offer no reasons for believing it, as long as the belief tracks the truth. Tracking theory is best known for rejecting closure under known entailment: I know that I have hands, and I know that having hands entails that I am not a brain in a vat, but I do not know that I am not a brain in a vat. Everyday knowledge therefore survives the skeptic's challenge, while the anti-skeptical claim does not amount to knowledge.
