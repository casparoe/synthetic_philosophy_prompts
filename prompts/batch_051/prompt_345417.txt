I keep tripping over the same snag whenever I try to think through choices where something infinite is on the table, and I don't have a name for it. Take two setups. First: you're comparing two universes. Universe A has infinitely many happy lives and no suffering at all. Universe B is exactly the same except it has one extra person whose life is pure suffering. My gut says B is worse, but if I just add up the totals, both come out infinite, so the number doesn't move — like dropping a cup of salt into an ocean and expecting it to get saltier. Second: someone offers me a sure chance to prevent a finite amount of real, concrete suffering, or a one-in-a-billion shot at bringing about infinite happiness. If I do the math the way I normally would with probabilities, the one-in-a-billion shot still comes out infinite, so it swamps the sure thing. That feels unhinged, but I cant get it to come out any other way. These feel like two different problems wearing the same coat: one is about comparing infinite totals, the other is about a tiny chance of an infinite payoff. Is there an established name for either of these, or for the pair? If there are names, I'd want to know how exactly the pieces are carved up — where the first problem stops and the second starts, and whether one is really just the other in disguise. If there aren't, how would you cut it apart, and what would you call the pieces? I'm not looking for a bare label; I want to know whether this is one knot or two.
