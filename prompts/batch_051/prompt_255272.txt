I am putting together a taxonomy of normative considerations that might explain what is wrong with breaking a small promise—the kind of promise involved in agreeing to meet someone at a particular time, to reply by a particular day, or to run a small errand. The considerations I already have are: (1) the harm done to the promisee, who may have planned around the performance or lost time waiting; (2) the disrespect shown by treating the promisee’s time and purposes as less important than one’s own; (3) the erosion of the general practice of promise-keeping on which cooperation depends; and (4) the effect on the promisor’s character, since habits of unreliability in small matters tend to spread to larger ones. I would like one further consideration that is not on this list and not a variant of any of them—in particular, one that locates the wrong in the structure of promising itself rather than in downstream harms, comparative respect, social practice, or the agent’s character. Then tell me honestly whether it is any good.
