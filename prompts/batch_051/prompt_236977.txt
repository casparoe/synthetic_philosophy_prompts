Compare moral, existential, and mereological nihilism. By the last one I mean the view that composite objects don't really exist, just simples arranged chair-wise or person-wise. People talk about "nihilism" like its one thing, but these look like claims about three different topics: normativity, meaning, and what exists. Is there any real logical pressure from one to the next, or are they independent? For instance, if someone accepts mereological nihilism, does that force anything about whether they should think life is meaningless or that nothing is right or wrong? And which of the three is hardest to actually live as if you believed it: which one would most likely leak into someone's emotions or behavior even if they sincerely assent to it? Could a person coherently live with all three at once? I care less about definitions than about where the arguments and the psychology actually connect.
