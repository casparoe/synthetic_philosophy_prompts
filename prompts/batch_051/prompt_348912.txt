Three years out of school, I'm deciding between two jobs, and a friend's objection has left me unable to settle the decision. The first job is at a large social media company, where I would work on the recommendation system that determines what content gets amplified to millions of people. The second is at a small nonprofit that builds open-source tools for local election offices; the pay is much lower, but the work is concrete and arguably more civic-minded. I have been trying to decide by thinking through the consequences of each option. My friend, who is normally the most level-headed person I know, told me I'm fooling myself. Once I start following the causal ripples of either choice—the content that gets shown, the people who are affected, the decisions they then make, the institutions that shift years later—I quickly reach chains I can't predict at all. Even the election work has second-order effects: cleaner voter rolls might, somewhere down the line, shape a close race, which then shapes policy, and so on. Since the full consequences of either job are unknowable, he argued, any attempt to rank them by their overall effects is just noise dressed up as reasoning. He thinks I should either admit the comparison is impossible and decide on some other ground, like what I would find meaningful, or fall back on a simple, visible rule such as doing the most direct good to the people in front of me.

I don't think he is wrong that I can't foresee the full causal chain, but his conclusion unsettles me. If the same reasoning applies to any major life choice—where to work, whether to have children, where to give money—then the idea of trying to make things go better starts to look empty. So: is my friend right that ignorance of total downstream consequences makes reasoning about consequences hopeless in a case like this? Or is there a coherent way to choose under exactly this kind of uncertainty, without pretending to know what is unknowable? And is the position he is pushing me toward any less arbitrary than the calculation he is criticizing? I would like the underlying issue worked out, not just a nudge about which job to take.
