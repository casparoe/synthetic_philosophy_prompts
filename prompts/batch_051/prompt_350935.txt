I'm grading final papers for a decision theory course but actually I want your credence on something I keep going back and forth on. Take the absent-minded driver: a guy on a highway with two exits needs to take the second one. Exiting at the first gives payoff 0, exiting at the second gives 4, and blowing past the second to the end of the road gives 1. The kicker is that after he passes the first exit he forgets he did, so at any exit he can't tell whether it's the first or second, and he has to use the same probability p of exiting at each exit. Ex ante, the best p is one in three, with expected payoff four-thirds. But at the action stage—sitting at an exit—he's no longer at the planning stage, and some people say the ex post optimal p is different, or that the problem shows expected utility theory isn't well-defined for imperfect recall. My proposition: the ex ante one-in-three strategy really is the rational strategy even at the action stage. Give me your actual credence in that proposition, decomposed. I want the sub-questions you're implicitly weighting—maybe how much rides on whether ex ante and ex post rationality must agree, how much on whether the driver's belief about which exit he's at should be derived from his own strategy, how much on whether a randomization can be binding against later temptation, and whatever else you think belongs. For each term a rough probability and a one-line justification, then how they combine. Then tell me which term would move your number the most and what evidence or argument would shift it. Also: if one person has credence 0.3 and another has 0.8 on this, where does the disagreement most likely live? I don't want a restatement of the ex post argument; I want the decomposition.
