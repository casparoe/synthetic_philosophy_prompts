In arguments about AI law I keep running into this move: a corporation is a legal person without being a human being — it can own property, enter contracts, sue and be sued — and nobody thinks a company is conscious. So, the argument goes, we can make an AI system a legal person for limited commercial purposes, and doing so doesn't commit us to anything about machine consciousness or rights. I want to know how far the analogy actually carries. Lay out what maps onto what: in the AI case, what plays the role of the shareholders, the directors, the employees, the company's assets? Then tell me where the mapping breaks, and which of the breaks matter for the conclusion being drawn. Once the disanalogies are on the table, does the conclusion survive — that we can create a narrowly commercial legal personhood for AI without it becoming a backdoor to robot rights or a liability shield for the people who run it — or was the analogy doing the work the whole time?
