I do a bit of programming and Ive also been going down a rabbit hole about how some sentences dont just describe the world, they change it. Like saying "I bet you five dollars" isnt a report about a bet — saying it just is the bet, and saying "I now pronounce you married" actually creates the marriage if the speaker has the right authority. The nerdy term for these is performative utterances. The thing that struck me: arent these basically state-changing function calls as opposed to queries? "The cat is on the mat" is like a pure function or a GET request — it just reads the world and returns a truth value. "I now pronounce you married" is like a POST or a setter — it changes the shared social state, and it only goes through if the request is well-formed: right words, right people, right circumstances. Those are preconditions and schemas in software; in speech they are called felicity conditions. The analogy even has something for authorization: not just anyone can call the marry() endpoint. My question is whether this is a real structural identity or just me pattern-matching on the word "state". If it is a real parallel, I would expect other software ideas to transfer. For example code has this notion of idempotency: repeating a PUT is harmless, but repeating a POST can create a new resource each time. Would that sort a list of performatives cleanly? And does the software picture predict things about performatives that are actually false — like that they should fail with a clean exception, or be reversible with a rollback when the preconditions were missing? Lay out the mapping precisely, say what would have to be true on both sides for the transfer to actually go through, and then gimme one thing the parallel predicts about some specific speech act that turns out wrong.
