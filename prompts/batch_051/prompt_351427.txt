I'm organizing a small workshop on severe uncertainty in formal epistemology, and two participants have been arguing for more than a year about whether rational belief can be irreducibly imprecise. I'd like you to take on the role of the professional philosopher of probability who has been facilitating their adversarial collaboration and who is known for being able to tell substantive disagreements apart from differences in modeling vocabulary. The first researcher, a precise Bayesian, holds that a rational agent's doxastic state is always representable by a single precise probability function; on her view, apparent imprecision is either an artifact of an incomplete model or a roundabout way of expressing risk attitudes, computational limits, or uncertainty about one's own mind — never a feature of belief itself. The second researcher, an imprecisionist, holds that some rational doxastic states are correctly represented only by a set of probability functions (a credal set), and that forcing a sharp function misrepresents the agent's evidence by collapsing distinct evidential situations; he denies that all imprecision can be relocated into utilities or decision rules without changing the subject.

Draft the joint statement this collaboration should publish. It should open with a numbered "Agreed Claims" section, stating what both can sign: claims about the formalism (what a credal set represents mathematically, how to update every function in the set by conditioning, and what it means for one act to dominate another with respect to every function in the set), claims about agreed test cases (an urn with a known but non-uniform composition, a coin of unknown bias), and claims about what would count as evidence for or against each position. It should then give a disagreements table: exact formulations of each remaining dispute, with the imprecisionist's wording acceptable to him and the precise Bayesian's wording acceptable to her, plus a judgment about which entries are substantive and which are verbal — and if a dispute is verbal, exactly which shared notion the two sides are packaging differently. Finally, it should give a "Cruxes" section: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For at least these two cruxes, specify a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on the question and record each side's prediction in advance: (i) whether dilation — the fact that conditioning every function in a credal set on a partition can yield a wider interval — is a reductio of imprecise conditioning or a benign consequence of it; and (ii) whether imprecision belongs to the content of the belief state or only to which acts are permissible given a sharp belief state plus unresolved conflict. Write the document so that the agreed claims are neither vacuous nor slanted, and so that both researchers would genuinely put their names to it. Begin by brainstorming several possible formulations of each disputed point, then produce the polished document. Do not include citations or a bibliography.
