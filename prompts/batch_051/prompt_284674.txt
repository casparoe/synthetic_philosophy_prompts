The fact that copper expands when heated is treated as a law, which licenses the claim that a particular unobserved heated wire would expand. The fact that all quarters in my pocket are copper is treated as an accident, which doesn't license the claim that some other quarter, if it were in my pocket, would be copper. Both are true universal generalizations over some domain, and the accidental one might even be simpler. Is there a principled difference between laws and accidental regularities, or is the distinction just a projection of explanatory habits? I want the strongest defense of the distinction, and then an honest verdict on whether it survives a case where the accidental pattern is just as simple, just as informative, and just as useful for prediction as the law. If it doesn't survive, say whether we should give up the laws-of-nature category or keep it as a pragmatic convenience. Please answer in American English.
