I keep going back and forth on what art actually is, and every defintion I try ends up either too broad or obviously rigged to include the things I happen to like. I'm a programmer, not a philosopher, so I'd like a working definition I can actually use -- something that gives a yes/no, or at least a clear lean, for each of the cases below rather than just saying "it's complicated".

Stuff I'm fairly sure is art: a Rembrandt portrait, a Beethoven string quartet, a well-regarded novel.

Stuff I'm fairly sure is not art: a traffic light turning red, a parking ticket, a spreadsheet of quarterly sales figures, a sunset over the ocean.

Cases I genuinely don't know how to sort:
- A urinal bolted to a wall in a public toilet.
- Duchamp's Fountain, which is the same kind of urinal, signed and put in a gallery.
- A three-year-old's crayon drawing of their family stuck to the fridge.
- A photograph taken automatically by a trail camera when a deer trips the shutter.
- An image generated by Stable Diffusion from the prompt "a sad robot in the rain".
- A procedurally generated landscape in a roguelike that no human designed, which a player later screenshots and everyone agrees is gorgeous.
- A meme: a stock photo with Impact-font text over the top.
- A nightingale's song.
- A chess game between two engines that a grandmaster later described as beautiful.

What I want from you: propose a definition first, then run it against every case I listed and tell me where it says something I'd probably disagree with. If it misfires, either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict. I'd rather you defend a weird verdict than bend the definition until it's so vague it can't say no to anything.

Please structure the response like this, with rough word counts: "The definition" (100–150 words), "Running the cases" (350–500 words, go through each case in a sentence or two), "Where it breaks" (150–250 words), "Revised or defended" (200–300 words), and "What would change my mind" (100–150 words, so I can see wich of these cases or arguments is doing the most work).

British English throughout, please.
