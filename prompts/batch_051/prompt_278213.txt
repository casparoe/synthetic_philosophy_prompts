Over the past few years I have become tentatively convinced by the case for AI safety via debate: the proposal that we can supervise systems smarter than ourselves by training two of them to argue opposite sides of a question in front of a human judge, who may interrupt, demand clarification, request sub-arguments, or press on a suspicious step rather than checking the answer directly. The specific claim I want pressure-tested is that, for a judge less capable than the debaters, the view that survives cross-examination tracks truth better than the judge's own unaided judgment, even when one debater is actively trying to construct deceptively persuasive arguments. The main threat I see comes from what are usually called obfuscated arguments: arguments that persuade the judge while being false, because the judge cannot assess the machinery behind them — one side hides a false lemma inside a long, boring derivation the judge will not unpack, or replaces a messy real argument with a clean but misleading analogy. My current view is that obfuscation is a genuine failure mode but not a fatal one: if the judge can ask for shorter explanations, direct comparisons, or the weakest premise stated outright, the obfuscation eventually has to compress into a step small enough to inspect, and the debate then exposes it.

What I would like is an honest account of what would have to be true for that view to be wrong. Which assumptions of the debate protocol are load-bearing, what would the world have to look like for each of them to fail, and which escape routes are live rather than merely logically possible? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, including the ones a careful defender of debate would themselves concede count against it rather than only the criticisms outsiders make. And I want a verdict on whether the claim that obfuscation is eventually exposed by cross-examination has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and, if it has, whether that is a serious problem for a proposal like debate, or an acceptable price for a scheme whose main alternative is trusting an unchecked model to tell the truth.
