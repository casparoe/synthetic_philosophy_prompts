I cover the ideas end of foreign policy, and I keep hearing smart people say "humanitarian intervention" is just regime change with a moral stamp — that Kosovo, Iraq, and Libya settled the matter. That sounds like a conceptual claim wearing an empirical coat, but I'm not sure it is. Suppose you had to design the strongest test you could to distinguish a genuinely humanitarian intervention from a regime-change war that borrowed its humanitarian clothes for the occasion. What would you measure — the preexisting threat to civilians, the intervener's own stakes, whether protection actually happens, how long they stay, what comes after? What pattern would count as evidence that the humanitarian rationale did real explanatory work, and what pattern would show it was mostly window dressing? And when the best possible data is in, what would it still leave open about intention, legitimacy, and whether the distinction matters in the first place?
