I have been thinking for a while about rule systems that are allowed to rewrite their own rules, and one case keeps nagging at me. Suppose a country of about four million people, emerging from a long crisis, adopts a constitution made of a single clause. The country has a single-chamber parliament, a weak executive, and an independent but ordinary judiciary. The clause says that any rule in the constitution, including the clause itself, may be amended or repealed by a two-thirds vote of the parliament. It then adds that no amendment may lower the two-thirds requirement for amending the constitution, and that this no-lowering ban applies to amendments of the ban itself. Predict how this legal order develops over the next fifty years. I want the mechanisms rather than a final verdict: which amendments actually get passed, how parliament and the courts behave when someone tests whether the ban can be undone in two steps or by repeal-and-replace, which interests learn to entrench their goals through the clause, and whether the two-thirds floor survives. Give me exactly five numbered predictions, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Each prediction should name a mechanism and its long-run effect, and say whether it is a robust effect or a guess.
