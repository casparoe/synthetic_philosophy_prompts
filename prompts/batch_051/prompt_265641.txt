I'm a novelist and I keep running into a psychological pattern with my characters, and I'm trying to figure out if there's an established name for the thing itself rather than just a literary description of it. Here's what I mean. One character has been up all night, and at six a.m. she says, out loud, that she knows she can't think straight right now—and then she quits her job with total certainty, as if the admission about her own state didn't need to touch the conclusion. Another has five friends who all read the same email he read, and all five think it was a deliberate insult; he thinks it's clearly innocent. He even says that the fact that five people see it differently is itself a reason to think he might be reading it wrong. Then he doesnt budge. He acts as if he's dealt with their disagreement just by mentioning it. The strange part is that these people aren't deceiving themselves about the state they're in. They'll warn other people not to trust them right now because their judgment is compromised. But their own belief just sits there, unchanged by the fact that they themselves hold that the belief was produced by a process they shouldn't trust. Is there a name for that specifically—believing something while also believing, in the very same breath, that one's own judgment about it is unreliable? It seems different from confirmation bias or self-deception, where the person is missing something about themselves. Here they see it plainly, and it changes nothing. If there's an established concept, I want to know its edges: does it apply only to states like exhaustion or drunkenness, or also to evidence that you're biased, or to knowing that your friends disagree? Does it require the belief to actually be false, or is the internal tension the thing? And if there isn't a good established name, I'd rather you coin one and define it against the closest neighbors. I won't be satisfied with just a label, so please do the carving. And when you answer: no preamble, no restating my question—open with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning.
