I'm a program officer at a family foundation, and I need a screening procedure for grant applications built on the importance/tractability/neglectedness framework. We get a couple hundred proposals a cycle, three cycles a year, I have about a hour per proposal before deciding what goes to the board, the grants are $50k–$250k, and the board is smart but allergic to EA jargon. I want a checklist I can actually run in that hour: what to do when importance or tractability can't be quantified, what to do when the three factors pull in opposite directions, and which steps are really judgment calls dressed up as scoring. It also has to handle the 'why is this neglected' question, because underfunded because it's unglamorous is a very different signal than underfunded because other informed funders looked at it and walked away. Then run it on three cases: a proven deworming program in a country whose government is already scaling it up; a local arts-education program for poor kids that our board is personally excited about; and a small AI-safety field-building project with no track record but a plausible case that it's important and neglected. I want at least one case where the procedure tells me to do something the board won't like, and say wether the whole checklist stops being useful.
