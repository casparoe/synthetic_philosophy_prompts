Hobbes in Leviathan has this whole account of artificial persons — roughly, a natural person owns his own words and actions, while a feigned or artificial person speaks and acts in the name of someone else, so the actions get attributed to the one who authorized them; and he calls the commonwealth itself an artificial man created by covenant. Now drop Hobbes into a 2026 hearing about whether a large language model should get legal personhood, with corporate personhood as the obvious precedent. What would he say? I don't mean write a parody speach, I mean the best faithful reconstruction of his actual position: which parts of his account line up with giving an AI legal personhood the way we give it to corporations, and which parts would make him balk. The sticking point I keep coming back to is that a corporation acts through authorized human representatives — the CEO signs, the board votes — but an LLM isn't obviously representing any natural person at all, and it can't be punished or hold assets except through its operator, which doesn't map cleanly onto his idea of who owns an action. Would he end up in favour, against, or at some weird third view like the AI is only a person insofar as a natural person authors it's outputs? And where is the corporate-person analogy actively misleading about what his view would be?
