I have recently found myself in a long argument with my younger brother, who, after a period of acute grief, underwent what he describes as a direct experience of the divine and now believes I am being unreasonable not to count it as evidence. He leans on an analogy: we trust our ordinary perceptual experience unless we have specific defeaters, so we should likewise treat a clear, noetic, transformative religious experience as prima facie justification for belief in God; withholding trust without such defeaters would be inconsistent. He points me to William James's The Varieties of Religious Experience as offering something like this case.

What complicates matters is a passage from James's preface to that work:

If, however, they will have the patience to read to the end, I believe that this unfavorable impression will disappear; for I there combine the religious impulses with other principles of common sense which serve as correctives of exaggeration, and allow the individual reader to draw as moderate conclusions as he will.

That passage is not one of the famous ones, but it has become the heart of our disagreement. James seems to say that the religious impulses are not sufficient on their own and that he joins them to common-sense correctives so the reader can draw moderate conclusions. My brother reads this as merely a presentational point—James does not want to alarm his Edinburgh audience—whereas I wonder whether it admits exactly what the analogy needs to rule out: that religious experience, unlike ordinary perception, needs external correction before it can carry epistemic weight.

I would like a careful audit of the analogy as it is actually used in this kind of argument. What corresponds to what if religious experience is being modeled on perception—the object, the faculty, the background conditions, the defeaters, the community of observers? Where does the mapping break? Which disanalogies matter for the argument that religious experience should be treated as evidence for God, and which are merely incidental? Does the moderate conclusion my brother wants survive once the disanalogies and James's own corrective are on the table, or was the analogy doing most of the work?
