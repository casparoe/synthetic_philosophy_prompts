I am a novelist, and I am drafting a novel in which a political philosopher, some years after the legalisation of same-sex marriage, is drawn into a dispute about the religious-exemption cases that followed in its wake — cases John Rawls did not live to see: a baker who will not make a wedding cake for a same-sex couple, a registrar who will not officiate at a civil partnership, a faith-based adoption agency that wants to turn away same-sex applicants. The scene is not a debate so much as an act of counterfactual interpretation: the philosopher is trying to work out, out loud, what Rawls would have made of these cases, and I want her reasoning to be faithful to the machinery Rawls actually left behind. I would like you to predict, as carefully as you can, what Rawls would have thought. Use the concepts he developed for thinking about religion and public life: the fact of reasonable pluralism, the distinction between the right and the good, the idea of public reason and the proviso, his account of reasonable comprehensive doctrines, and his insistence that constitutional essentials and matters of basic justice be settled by considerations all reasonable citizens could accept. Where his theory seems to underdetermine the answer, say so, and distinguish what the theory requires from what Rawls, given his broader commitments, would probably have said about a particular case.

I need this as a speculative reconstruction I can adapt into fictional prose, not as a scholarly essay, so please avoid citations, footnotes, and bibliographic scaffolding. I am less interested in the outcomes of any particular litigation than in the underlying structure of the problem: how a liberal committed to public reason should regard a religious citizen who asks the law to make room for conscientious refusal, and whether the answer changes if the refusal touches a matter of basic justice. Keep the focus on the counterfactual Rawls, and do not retreat into the idea that he would need more information without first saying what the available information would do.
