I have been trying to make up my mind about Wang Yangming's thesis that knowledge and action are one, and I keep ending up in the same place: the doctrine seems to me to earn its keep theoretically, and yet it collides with something I find phenomenologically undeniable. On the reading I find most compelling, the view is not the anodyne claim that people often fail to act on what they sincerely avow; it is the stronger claim that a "knowledge" of what is right unaccompanied by action is not genuine knowledge at all, but something more like assent, repetition, or unassimilated opinion. Genuine knowledge of what a situation demands is already the beginning and activity of responding to it, so the gap between knowing and doing disappears: there are not two separate faculties that need to be yoked together by an additional act of will. This is a genuinely elegant way of unifying what an akrasia-friendly picture has to leave disjointed — cognition and conduct, the recognition of a demand and the response to it. It also explains the felt difference between really seeing that a friendship is failing and merely saying so, by locating the difference in the quality of one's knowledge rather than in a mysterious failure of the will.

And yet I cannot make myself believe that akrasia is unreal. I know, for example, that the kindest thing would be to send a difficult message tonight, and I can reason through exactly why, and I still do not send it. Nothing about the experience feels like a failure of knowledge; if anything, the knowledge is precisely what makes not acting feel bad. If the unity doctrine is right, this experience must be re-described as a case in which I did not really know after all — that I only thought I knew. That re-description strikes me as suspiciously convenient, even unfalsifiable, and it threatens to turn the doctrine into an immune tautology rather than a substantive claim about agency. I would like the strongest possible versions of both sides. Should the intuition that akrasia is real be debunked and dropped? Should the doctrine be amended so that unity holds only for some special, perfected, or realized kind of knowing, leaving ordinary propositional knowledge compatibly inert? Or is there a careful reading of what Wang Yangming means by genuine knowledge on which both the doctrine and the reality of weakness of will survive?

I am not asking for a vote, and I do not want a bland "there is something to both" conclusion. I want to know what would justify each move — dropping the intuition, amending the doctrine, or accepting the compatibility reading — and what each move costs. Then tell me which argument or consideration would most change your verdict, and where in your own reasoning you think you are least secure.
