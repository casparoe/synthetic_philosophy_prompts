I'm a graduate tutor for an undergraduate philosophy of language course, and next week I'm leading a two-hour seminar on presupposition, accommodation, and the idea that conversation works like a game with a scoreboard. The students have read a little Frege, some speech act theory, and Strawson's claim that a sentence can fail to be true or false when a presupposition fails, but they have not been introduced to the common ground or to the thought that presuppositions are requirements on the conversational record rather than contents inside a speaker's head. The topic has become concrete for me because I watched it happen in a department planning meeting: a colleague said, in a perfectly matter-of-fact way, "Once the new grading scheme is in place, we'll need to rewrite the rubric," even though no scheme had been adopted. Nobody objected, and by the end of the meeting the presupposed scheme was being treated as settled. I want my students to understand how that move works and how a hearer can resist it.

Don't explain the material to me; plan how I should explain it. I need the order of ideas and the reason for that order: presumably the common ground before felicity conditions, felicity before accommodation, and then the scorekeeping metaphor, but tell me if that order is wrong and what each step depends on. Pick the single example that should carry the session, and say why it works better than the tired example of the present king of France; I worry that example trains students to hear a presupposition failure as a merely semantic curiosity rather than as a conversational move someone makes. Identify the misconceptions I should expect from students with exactly their background—at least the idea that a presupposition is what the speaker happens to believe, and the idea that accommodation is just the speaker getting away with something—and specify where in the sequence to head each one off before it sets. Give me one final exercise or pair of moves that will show me whether they can distinguish a presupposition being already satisfied, quietly accommodated, and blocked. And tell me what to leave out at this level, especially anything about presupposition projection, and how to say openly that we are leaving it out.
