I'm preparing a unit for a course on decisional incapacity, and I keep returning to a puzzle about the exact kind of authority an advance directive should carry. The familiar framing is whether an earlier self can bind a later self when the later self's values seem to have changed—for example, a person who, while competent, refuses antibiotics for advanced dementia and then, once demented, appears content and even asks for them. I want to set aside both the question of personal identity across dementia and the broad question of whether advance directives should exist at all. Instead, I want the essay to adjudicate between two models of what a directive does. On the sovereignty model, a directive issued by a competent person is closer to a standing order from a legitimate authority: it should be obeyed unless the original decision was procedurally defective—made under coercion, misunderstanding, or without relevant information—because its force comes from the fact that the earlier self did the deciding. On the evidential model, the directive is high-quality testimony from someone who knew the patient well—the patient's own earlier self—but it is still evidence to be weighed against the person's current expressions of preference, apparent well-being, and the rest of the present picture; its force comes from its reliability, not from any right to command. The models agree in easy cases and diverge exactly where it matters: when honoring the earlier self would require overriding what looks like genuine present contentment.

Write an essay defending the model—or a determinate hybrid of them—that you think best captures the authority of the earlier self. Defend your verdict against the strongest objection from the other side, and be explicit about the underlying mechanics: in virtue of what does the earlier self count, and what exactly does its entitlement permit in the hard cases where present and prior preferences conflict? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence (state the model or hybrid you defend), then give the reasoning after it, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
