I spent my career in biochemistry, and I have recently been reading Strawson’s “Freedom and Resentment.” The part that keeps nagging me is his argument that the objective attitude—viewing a person as something to be managed, treated, or causally explained—is a real standing possibility but cannot be made the whole of our relations with others, because the reactive attitudes are not optional additions to ordinary interpersonal life but constitutive of it. This looks to me structurally like the distinction in enzyme regulation between describing a protein’s behavior from outside, via a rate equation over a free-energy landscape, and the cell’s own mode of control, which works only by allosteric contact within a network of mutually constrained conformers. The rate-equation description is not false; it is just not a mode in which the system participates in itself. Is Strawson’s claim that the objective attitude is necessarily local and parasitic on the participant stance the same kind of claim, or am I pattern-matching on the word “outside”? If it is a genuine structural parallel, does anything transfer in either direction—for example, does the fact that the rate-equation description is incomplete but predictively useful bear on whether the objective attitude could be globally predictively useful, and does Strawson’s point about the inescapability of the participant stance transfer to the biochemical case?
