Suppose a small country is founded on a constitutional principle that inequalities in life prospects are legitimate only if they trace to people's choices rather than to brute luck, and a standing body called the Luck Auditorate must issue a 'clean luck certficate' for any law or market regulation likely to shift the distribution of income, health, or education. Predict, with explicit reasoning, how this plays out over fifty years: what ends up doing the work of distinguishing brute luck from option luck, where the gaming and boundary disputes concentrate, how insurance, parenting, disability support and inheritance get reshaped, and whether the principle survives contact with a market economy and an ageing population. I care less about your overall verdict than about the mechanism: which bits of the distinction hold up under institutional pressure, which get stretched beyond recognition, and what would have to be true for the whole arrangement to collapse.
