Last night I had a long, unresolved argument with a friend about the hedonistic imperative — the proposal that biotechnology should be used to eliminate suffering from sentient life. My friend defended the proposal as the straightforward extension of ordinary medicine. I kept pressing the standard objection. As I can put it most sharply, the objection is that pain, fear, frustration, and grief are not merely defects. They warn an organism of harm, mobilize effort, give achievement its stakes, and give love and loyalty much of their seriousness. Abolish these states, the objection continues, and either the organism cannot learn and act in a complex world, or it becomes something so changed that the goods the intervention was meant to protect have been hollowed out. The program therefore looks impossible or self-defeating.

My friend then asked me to grant one specific supposition. Suppose the engineering problem were solved in the way the proposal's defenders expect, at least as a first step: a reliable intervention replaces aversive states with graded signals that carry the same information and motivation but never fall below hedonic zero. A person still flinches from an injury, still works through a difficult problem late into the evening, still treats danger as urgent, but the felt tone of none of these states is ever bad. The person still distinguishes better and worse outcomes along a gradient, yet the floor of that gradient has been raised. I found I could no longer say confidently that my objection goes through.

So this is what I want you to examine: does the standard objection to the hedonistic imperative survive that development, does it need repair, or is it dead? Say precisely which premise of the objection the development bears on — the empirical premise about what a mind needs in order to function, or the normative premise about what gives goods their value. If something of the objection survives or reappears in another form, say what that repaired version still rules out. If the objection is dead, say what would still need to be argued before my friend's confidence is warranted. I am asking what happens to the argument, not for a technology assessment.
