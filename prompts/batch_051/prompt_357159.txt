I'm teaching an upper-level history of economic thought seminar and I need a final essay question on Hume's Of Money and Of the Balance of Trade. I want something that can't be answered by just summarizing the quantity theory of money. Here's the thing I want to get at. In Of the Balance of Trade Hume has that annihilation thought experiment: suppose four-fifths of the money in Britain vanished overnight, with everything else unchanged. He thinks prices would fall, foreign buyers would rush in for cheap British goods, and the lost money would flow back until Britain's prices were back in line with its neighbors. And then he says the advantage from being cheap disappears once the money has returned. But in Of Money he's also pretty clear that a gradual increase in money can be good for a nation's industry, at least for a while. Students tend to either not see any tension there or get stuck saying Hume is just inconsistent.

So please write: (1) an exam question that makes them explain what the annihilation case is supposed to show about whether money is wealth, and then reconcile it with the claim about gradual increases being beneficial. (2) a model answer of about 700 words. (3) a marking scheme with five must-hit moves, marks for each out of 20, and a one-line note on how candidates typically get each one wrong. The question should be answerable in 60 minutes. Please act as an expert on early modern philosophy and economic thought, and start by brainstorming the specific distinctions you want the question to test before you write it. The marking scheme should seperate an A from a B not just by whether they mention short-run versus long-run, but by whether they can say where the short-run benefit comes from and whether that actually dissolves the tension.
