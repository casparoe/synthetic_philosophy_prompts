I'm putting together a handout for a philosophy of biology discussion group and I want a sorting quiz on what life is, not another "here are the definitions" list. Make it a decision tree that sorts someone by their answers to four or five concrete cases rather than by which label they like. Use at least these cases: a virus, a mule, a flame, and a synthetic vesicle that actively maintains itself against its environment but cannot replicate. Each branch node should be a yes/no (or point-on-a-spectrum) question about one of those cases — or a short made-up borderline case — phrased so someone can answer it without knowing which position it pushes them toward; questions about whether the thing self-maintains, whether being part of an evolving lineage matters, whether heritable information matters, and whether "alive" is all-or-nothing are the sort of thing I mean. Leaves should include the metabolic/autopoietic view (self-maintenance is the core), the Darwinian reproduction-and-evolution view, the information-theoretic view (life is about encoded heritable information), a "life is a prototype concept, not a natural kind" view, and an eliminativist "the word is doing no work" view. Also mark any empty cells — combinations of answers no live position occupies — and say briefly whether each is incoherent or just unpopular, and flag any two leaves your questions can't seperate. Finally, tell me which single question is doing the most sorting work, and what answer or counterexample would most likely make you redraw the tree.
