I keep noticing a parallel and want to know whether it is deep or superficial. In the metaphysics of dispositions, interference cases are standardly divided into finks, masks, and mimics: a fink is a disposition removed when its stimulus occurs (a fragile glass protected by a sorcerer at the instant of striking); a mask is a disposition retained but whose manifestation is blocked by a background condition (a fragile glass wrapped in packing foam); a mimic is an object lacking the disposition but made to manifest it by outside interference. In psychometrics, the standard threats to measuring a trait look structurally parallel: measurement reactivity, where the act of measurement changes the trait being measured; socially desirable responding or demand characteristics, where a present trait is not expressed in the test setting; and faking or malingering, where a trait is simulated without being present. Is this a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion? If it is genuine, give the exact mapping from trait, manifest indicator, and test context to disposition, manifestation, and stimulus; say what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer; and identify at least one prediction the parallel makes that is false on one side or the other. In particular, does the claim that masks undermine purely counterfactual analyses of dispositions transfer to any lesson about inferring latent traits from observed test behavior, or does it come apart?
