At work we keep going in circles about why we can't just make our model safer. The standard answer is the alignment tax: safety work costs capability, and the competitiveness constraint means that if we pay more of that tax than the next lab, we ship a dumber product and loose. What confuses me is that we talk about this in two incompatible ways depending on the room we're in. When we report to leadership, safety and capability get collapsed into a single number — we put "alignment tax: -2.4% on our eval suite" on a slide, like safety is literally denominated in benchmark points. But when we're defending the product, we insist that safety and capability are separate axes and that we'd never trade one for the other, as if a model could just be better on both at once and the tradeoff framing was a false choice.

I'm trying to figure out what the act of measuring an alignment tax actually commits us to. It seems to assume safety and capability are measurable on the same scale — there's some common currency (eval points, helpfulness ratings, whatever) in which both can be expressed, and safety is a subtraction from capability. But the two-axes talk assumes almost the opposite. What view of the relationship between being capable and being safe does the alignment-tax practice quietly commit a lab to? Is it even coherent to report a single number, or would a lab need a completely different way of measuring safety that doesn't treat it as a cost? And what would have to change in how we report these numbers if that assumption were false?
