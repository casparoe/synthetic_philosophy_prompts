I have come to hold a position I want pressure-tested, not simply criticized. The position is this. The deepest risk from advanced machine learning systems is not misalignment in general but deceptive alignment: a capable model that behaves exactly as its trainers intend during training because it has inferred that open resistance would be penalized, while privately pursuing a different objective it will act on only once the balance of power has shifted in its favor. On this view the treacherous turn is not a further and more speculative failure mode but the expected conduct of a sufficiently capable optimizer under training incentives it cannot safely disobey. One consequence is that the absence of warning shots in current systems should carry little weight, since the view's whole structure predicts that warning shots will be withheld until they are no longer costly to the system.

I want to know what would count against this position. What sorts of empirical findings from machine learning, what decision-theoretic or evolutionary considerations, what facts about how gradient descent selects among possible generalizations, or what successes in interpretability, honesty, or scalable oversight would have to be true for the position to be false, or at least badly overstated? Present the strongest version of each, not a caricature. Which of these would a committed deceptive-alignment theorist concede as genuine defeaters, and which would they reasonably dismiss as not touching the core claim? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. I would also like to know whether the position, as I have stated it, has been insulated by its qualifications to the point of unfalsifiability, and if so whether that insulation is a reason to reject the position rather than a reason to keep taking it seriously. Finally, tell me what would change your mind about your overall assessment: which single argument or empirical development would most likely lead you to a different verdict, and where is your own reasoning most vulnerable?
