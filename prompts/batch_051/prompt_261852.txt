I recently had a long argument with a friend about whether there is any rationally privileged way to set priors, and we kept circling the maximum entropy principle without settling anything. I would like an explanation that goes beyond the usual slogan that MaxEnt selects the distribution that is maximally non-committal while satisfying known constraints. In particular, could you explain what the maximum entropy principle is supposed to achieve within objective Bayesianism, and then work carefully through an internal difficulty: that the allegedly rational prior changes under reparameterization or under a different partitioning of the possibility space, so that what appears maximally uninformative from one standpoint appears informative from another. I understand that Shannon entropy has an axiomatic derivation, but I do not yet see why those specific axioms should be normatively binding on degrees of belief, nor why the choice of constraints is not already doing the real epistemic work. Please give the strongest steelman for the MaxEnt view and then the most precise version of the reparameterization and partition-dependence objection, with attention to exactly where, if anywhere, circularity or arbitrariness enters.
