I've been going back and forth with a friend of mine who's in a linguistics grad program about whether a language could just not have pronouns, or not have the kind of words that point at the speaker, the listener, here, or now. She calls the pointer words indexicals, and I've been inventing fake little languages to check whether her rulings line up. So far they do, but I cant figure out the rule she's actually using.

First I gave her Moppi, which has mi and yu working exactly like I and you. She said pronouns yes, indexicals yes, no discussion. Then Teko, which only uses people's names — 'Sam hungry' for I'm hungry, 'Sam saw Aisha' — and has no real words for here, now, this, I, or you. She said no pronouns, no indexicals. Then Vo, which has no I or you, but it has tla, a kind of this/that-one word you use with a point: point at yourself and it means me, at the listener and it means you, at someone else and it means her. She said no pronouns but yes indexicals, because tla shifts with the situation even though it's a pointing-word, not a person-word. Then Hiru, which has ka and te for he and she, but for yourself and for the person you're talking to you have to use names, and it has no here, now, or this. She said pronouns yes — just third-person ones — but indexicals no, since he and she aren't anchored to the speech situation the way I, you, here, and now are.

Now I want to test the boundary with a fifth one I made up, Nemi. It has no standalone I or you at all. Instead it glues a little prefix onto the verb: na- means the speaker is the one doing it, ta- means the addressee is the one doing it. So 'go' with na is 'I go' and 'go' with ta is 'you go,' but you can't say na by itself — it only exists stuck to a verb — and if you want to say 'Aisha hit me' you have to use my name as the object. What would my friend say about Nemi: does it have pronouns, indexicals, neither, both? Which of the earlier languages is the closest precedent, what's the most likely rule she's running, and what should I make up next to tell the remaining rules apart? I care less about the yes/no than about what kind of thing she thinks counts as a pronoun and as an indexical.
