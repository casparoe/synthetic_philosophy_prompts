I'm a novelist and I keep ending up in arguments about historical fiction and biopics where everyone talks past each other. One group says you owe the dead the truth: don't invent motives, don't put words in their mouths, don't file off the ugly parts. The other says a novelist's duty isn't to fact-check the dead, its to make them live, and a drama that sticks to the archive is just a dressed-up Wikipedia entry. This fight has been going on for decades, same examples, same objections, nobody changes their mind. My current hypothesis is that it isn't really a disagreement about falsehood at all — it's a disagreement about what a dead person is to us: a fact to be preserved, a property to be protected, a raw material, a kind of ghost. I want a diagnosis of the debate, not a ruling on which side is right. Is this a substantive ethical disagreement, a verbal one, a clash of genre conventions, or a fight about who has standing? And if anything could move it, what would that look like — a distinction, a new concept, a changed question — or is it just permanently stuck? Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no introduction and no conclusion.
