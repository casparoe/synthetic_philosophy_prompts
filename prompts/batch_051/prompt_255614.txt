I live in a small town upstate, and we've spent two years fighting about a statue, but the argument is really about an old ordinance, so I want someone to work through the text with me instead of just trading slogans. The town was incorporated in 1901, and that same year the common council put up a statue of Josiah Harrow, the mill owner who basically founded the place, in the center of Founder's Square. The ordinance reads:

"That a statue of Josiah Harrow be erected in the center of Founder's Square, and there remain in perpetuity; and that it shall not be defaced, altered, removed, or relocated, nor shall any structure or improvement be raised within fifteen feet of the same, so that the square may remain open for public assembly."

What the 1901 council didn't imagine is that a hundred and twenty years later Founder's Square would also hold the town's only public wading pool and playground (built in the 1930s at the north end of the square, outside the fifteen-foot ring), and that during our recent heat waves the square doubles as an official cooling center. Two kids have gotten hurt climbing the granite base, and the stone throws off so much heat that the cooling center has had to close on the hottest days. The town engineer says the only workable fixes are either to move the statue to the edge of the square and leave the middle open, or to put up a small shaded safety fence around the base. The first looks like "relocation" under the ordinance; the second looks like a "structure . . . within fifteen feet." Nobody is really saying Harrow should be dishonored, but the people who want to keep the statue in place say the ordinance was a promise the town made, and the people who want change say a hundred-year-old promise can't hold a public square hostage when the square's use has changed this much. I can see the literal-words answer, the original-purpose answer, the "what would the 1901 council have said if you'd asked them" answer, and the "what would a reasonable person today understand" answer, and they pull in different directions. Could you lay out what each approach yields for this specific case and say which one you think should control, and why? Please answer in American English.
