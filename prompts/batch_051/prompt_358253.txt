Anti-reductionists (roughly, Reid) say testimony is a basic source of justification, like perception or memory: you're entitled to believe what people tell you unless you have a specific reason not to. Does anti-reductionism start to look reckless once the 'speaker' isn't a person with a face and a reputation? Or does reductionism collapse under its own weight, since you can't check the track record of everything you read online without leaning on still more testimony? Go into the weeds: engage with the standard moves on both sides — the charge that reductionism is circular or too demanding, the charge that anti-reductionism is just a license for gullibility, the role of defeaters, and the difference between local and global justification.
