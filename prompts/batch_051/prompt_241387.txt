I am a high school student preparing for a philosophy club debate on whether consciousness is a kind of user illusion. I want you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two positions:

Position A: Illusionism, roughly the view that phenomenal consciousness — the subjective "what it is like" aspect of experience — is not a real inner theater but an introspective confusion or simplified self-model. Proponents of this sort of view include Keith Frankish and Daniel Dennett.

Position B: Robust phenomenal realism, roughly the view that there are real subjective phenomenal properties, that these are not merely explained away by describing cognitive function, and that introspective reports sometimes refer to them. Proponents of this sort of view include David Chalmers and Thomas Nagel.

Your response should begin with the document itself, with no preface or commentary. The document should contain:

1. A numbered agreed-claims section listing claims both sides can sign without hedging about which mental capacities exist, what introspective reports are, what the apparent features of experience are, what the explanatory gap is, and what a successful theory needs to predict. Avoid vacuous claims and avoid phrasing that either side would reject as slanted.

2. A disagreements table with one row for each residual issue. For each row, give the issue, the wording of the illusionist position that both sides would accept as a fair statement of illusionism, the wording of the realist position that both sides would accept as a fair statement of realism, and what each side would count as genuine evidence or argument against its own view.

3. A cruxes section: the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one side. For each crux, give the test, argument, or case both sides agree would bear on it, and state each side's prediction in advance. Include one innocuous-seeming empirical result that both sides agree would be modest but diagnostic.

Finally, if part of the dispute is merely verbal, say exactly which part and why. If some of it is not verbal, say what kind of fact could decide it. The document should be one both camps could put their names to, with formulations that are neither slanted nor so diluted that the disagreements disappear.
