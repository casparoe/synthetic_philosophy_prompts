I'm tutoring a first-year ethics course, and we've just read the part of G. E. Moore's Principia Ethica where he argues that by far the most valuable states of consciousness we know are those involving the appreciation of beauty and personal affection. My students can repeat the slogan, but they cannot tell what commitments they are actually assenting to, so I want to build a sorting quiz for a discussion section. The quiz should sort respondents into the main interpretations one could hold of Moore's position: reductive hedonism, experiential holism, object-inclusive holism, personal-affection holism, and instrumentalism. The distinctions that should drive the branching are whether the beautiful object itself has intrinsic value; whether the value of aesthetic experience is just pleasure plus true belief or an organic whole greater than those parts; whether personal affection requires admiration of the other's actual qualities or can rest on false beliefs; and whether beauty and friendship are irreducible goods or merely useful.

Please write the instrument as a numbered list of exactly five items, no introduction or conclusion, each item at most two sentences. Items 1–4 should be diagnostic questions built around concrete cases: an unseen landscape, a forgery viewed under a false belief that it is the original, a friendship based on invented virtues, and a friendship with someone known to be vicious. Each question should give two or three answer options and be answerable by someone who has never read Moore. Item 5 should be the scoring key in one or two sentences, mapping the main answer patterns to the five positions, flagging any pairs of positions the questions cannot actually separate, and noting any unoccupied combinations with a brief diagnosis.
