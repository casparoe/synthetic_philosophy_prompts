A friend forwarded me a long blog post that tied together wild animal suffering and the beef-versus-chicken debate, and it left me with that uneasy feeling of this is too neat but I can't find the actual mistake.

The post's argument, as I understood it, runs like this. Most people assume chicken is the ethical meat because chickens are small and convert grain into flesh efficiently, but that gets things backwards. Raising chickens at the scale people actually eat them means growing enormous amounts of corn and soy to feed billions of birds, and harvesting those crops kills field mice, rabbits, and insects in huge numbers. A genuinely grass-fed steer mostly eats pasture, not harvested feed, so the animal suffering bill is basically one large animal at the end. If your aim is to minimize the number of animals killed, beef beats chicken decisively. The same logic even undercuts veganism: a bowl of lentils comes from arable fields, and that kills more animals per calorie than a cow raised on pasture does. Once you bring in wild animal suffering, crop farming looks even worse, because fields replace habitat, and less habitat means fewer wild animals enduring lives of predation, starvation, and disease—so the intuition that the salad is the kind option may be doing wild animals no favours. In the end, the post says, a thoroughgoing consequentialist should rank a grass-fed burger as causing less suffering than a grain-fed chicken sandwich, and maybe even less than a plate of lentils.

I suspect the post is sliding too easily between animals killed and suffering, and maybe treating grass-fed beef as the default when most beef is not actually grass-fed, but I don't trust my own read here. Could you go through this summary piece by piece and tell me what's accurate, what's a common oversimplification, what's subtly wrong, and whether the final grass-fed-burger-wins conclusion actually follows from the parts that survive correction?
