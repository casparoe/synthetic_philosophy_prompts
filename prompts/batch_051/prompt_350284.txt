I'm a philosophy PhD student, and in our reading group the one argument that has completely stalled is over the ethics of panpsychism. Everyone in the room grants, for the sake of argument, that fundamental physical things are subjects of some kind — that there is something it's like to be an electron, or a field, or whatever the micro-level stuff turns out to be. And then the group splits into two camps that never move. One camp says this makes the moral universe explode: if the basement of reality is full of subjects, then every physical interaction is a place where things can go well or badly, and we are embedded in an ocean of morally relevant experience — possibly suffering — that no ethical framework is built to handle. The other camp says that's a non sequitur: micro-experience, if it exists at all, would be so simple that it has no hedonic tone and no valence. It would be a bare flicker of something, not the kind of state that can hurt or go badly, so nothing ethically changes. There is also a smaller third group that says we cant settle the valence question but the mere live possibility plus a precautionary principle means we should treat micro-subjects as morally considerable anyway, and the other two camps both hate that move.

My working hypothesis is that the real disagreement isn't about panpsychism at all, but about what suffering — or moral patiency, or mattering — requires. Specifically whether valence is a seperable dimension of experience, something that only shows up with enough integration or complexity, or whether any phenomenal state whatsoever comes with at least a faint positive or negative character. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is, not a survey of panpsychism and not a verdict on which camp is right. Is it a hidden empirical question about what micro-experience would be like, which we have no access to? A conceptual question about whether the idea of a valence-free phenomenal state is even coherent? A verbal dispute about how broadly to use the word 'suffering'? Two groups answering different questions under the heading 'the ethics of panpsychism'? Or something else about the burden of proof and the precautionary principle? And given the diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, a thought experiment, a formal result, a change of question — or is the impasse permanent?
