The low-impact approach to AI safety promises a way to avoid harmful side effects without first specifying everything we value. The idea, roughly, is to penalize an agent for changing the world relative to a baseline: to what would have happened had it not acted, or to what a default policy would have brought about, or to the values of a chosen set of protected state variables. What attracts me is that this would convert the vague prohibition against doing harm into a tractable constraint, a kind of minimal ethic of non-interference that could be applied before anyone has solved the full problem of value alignment.

But I keep running into an intuition that feels firm and not merely conservative: there is no value-neutral way to choose the baseline. Whether a change counts as a side effect, and how severe it is, depends on which dimensions of the world are treated as precious and which may be treated as inert. If the baseline is what would have happened anyway, then a train running on schedule and a forest that was going to be clearcut are both part of it, but they matter very differently, and almost everyone would judge interfering with one to be a side effect and interfering with the other to be an improvement. The more the baseline is specified to avoid such counterexamples, the more it looks like a disguised specification of value rather than a substitute for one.

Give me the strongest version of each side. Then tell me which should yield. Should I debunk the intuition as a generic resistance to formalization and accept that an approximate, domain-relative impact measure is the reasonable target? Should I amend or abandon the low-impact idea, and if so, what is the least amount of value specification that could repair it? Or is there a reading on which the two are compatible, perhaps because the baseline is not meant to be neutral but rather to encode a minimal preference for leaving options open and avoiding irreversible changes? I want to know what would justify each of these moves, not just which side you would pick.
