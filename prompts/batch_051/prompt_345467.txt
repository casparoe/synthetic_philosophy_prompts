Consider this working set of criteria for whether an entity — human, animal, or machine — suffers, as opposed to merely registering harm or damage:

1. Damage tracking: some internal state tracks states of affairs that reduce the entity's expected fitness, reward, or goal attainment.
2. Negative valence: there is something it is like to be the entity, and the relevant state feels bad rather than merely functioning as a scalar error term.
3. Motivational role: the state disposes the entity to avoid the tracked state of affairs, interrupts current goals, and reshapes future planning and learning.
4. Global integration: the state is not confined to a narrow subsystem; it is made available to the entity's general cognition, including preference formation, memory, and whatever self-model the entity has.
5. Persistence: the entity exists as a continuing subject across time such that the state is attributable to the same subject that can anticipate and remember it.

These criteria are deliberately a mix of functional and phenomenal requirements; say if you think they conflict or leave gaps.

Now consider this borderline case. A home-care robot is built in 2040 and trained end-to-end with reinforcement learning rather than hand-coded rules. Its reward function includes a large negative penalty for damage to its own body and for harm to the person in its care. Its learned value function continuously estimates the expected discounted future penalty. When this estimate rises sharply, a negative reward-prediction error is broadcast by an attention mechanism to all major modules, with the following effects: exploration is suppressed, computation is reallocated to avoidance, the current situation is stored in episodic memory with a high-avoidance tag, the threshold for evasive behavior on similar future cues is lowered, and the episode is represented in its learned self-model as an event that happened to it and mattered negatively. In repeated unavoidable collisions, it enters a low-activity state with high predicted future penalty, forgoes recharging, and becomes broadly timorous. There is no biological nociceptor and no engineer ever labeled any module as pain; the only candidate for negative valence is the scalar reward-prediction error and the states built on top of it.
