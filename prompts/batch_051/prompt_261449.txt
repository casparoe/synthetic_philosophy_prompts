I'm trying to figure out whether I still count as a pacifist, because my kid got shoved down the stairs at school last month and I told him if it happens again he has my permission to swing back, and I didn't feel even slightly bad about it. I keep telling people I'm pacifist-leaning but my gut is all over the place, so I made a list of cases and I want to know if there's a principle that gets all of them, or close:

- Someone breaks into my house at night and I can shoot him: fine, even though I'd probably freeze.
- My kid hitting the bully back next time: fine.
- A stranger on the street hitting an old man, and me tackling the stranger: fine, maybe even obligatory.
- A soldier shooting an enemy soldier in a war I think is unjust: not fine, even though the enemy soldier isn't exactly innocent either.
- A soldier shooting an enemy soldier in a war I think is just: I want to say fine, but it sits worse then the stranger-tackling and I don't know why.
- A bombing that will definitely kill civilians as collateral in that just war: not fine.
- Torturing a kidnapper to find a child before he starves: not fine, though I'd probably do it and then think I did wrong.
- Shooting someone who is, right now, torturing the child to make him stop: fine, even if it means we never find out where the child is.
- An enslaved person killing the slaveholder during an escape: fine.
- Beating a guy up at a bar because he said something despicable: not fine, but I'd feel less bad about that than about some of the ones I called fine, which worries me.

Is there a principle that actually captures all of this, or at least one that tells me which verdicts to throw out? And if the closest coherent position to my list is some version of pacifism, tell me what's left of the pacifism once all these exceptions are in.
