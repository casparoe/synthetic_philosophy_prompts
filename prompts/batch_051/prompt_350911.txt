Ok here's a counterfactual. Suppose that from the very first optical Bell tests in the early 1970s onward, and through every later Bell-type experiment no matter how many loopholes get closed, the measured correlations never exceed the local-realist bound. However entangled the pairs and whatever the detector settings, the data come out as a local hidden-variable theory would predict, and the quantum-mechanical prediction is the one that gets falsified every single time. The results are clean enough that no one doubts them after the mid-1980s. Everything else in the history of physics is unchanged up to that point: Bell still publishes his theorem before the experiments, and quantum mechanics keeps passing every other test spectaculary.
