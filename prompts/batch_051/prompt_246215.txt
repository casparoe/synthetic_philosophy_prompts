I am a PhD student in philosophy of science, working on the metaphysics of laws of nature. I would like you to draft the joint statement that a committed Humean and a committed anti-Humean about laws of nature could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. By Humean I mean someone who holds that the laws are not an additional layer of modal reality over and above the particular facts, but rather are a kind of systematization of those facts—roughly, the principles of the system that best balances simplicity and strength. By anti-Humean I mean someone who holds that lawhood is, or is grounded in, an irreducibly modal relation—governing, producing, or necessitating—that is not merely a summary of what happens. The statement should not be a bland compromise or a lowest-common-denominator document; it should record substantial agreement in wording neither side would reject, and then make the remaining disagreement exact.

Begin with a numbered section of agreed claims. These should be substantive rather than vacuous, and should cover at least the following: what both sides take the target of a theory of laws to be; their shared verdicts about paradigm cases, such as the contrast between the inverse-square law of gravitation or Coulomb's law on one side and an accidentally true generalization about the coins in someone's pocket on the other; and the phenomena any account of lawhood must capture, including the support of counterfactuals and inductive inference, the law/accident distinction, and the role of laws in explanation. Each agreed claim should be worded so that neither side would read it as a disguised statement of the rival theory.

Then provide a disagreements table with at least four rows. For each row, give: the crux, understood as a well-defined sub-question such that a settled answer would move at least one side; the precise formulation of the disagreement, with separate wording for each side wherever they cannot share a formulation; the test, argument, or thought experiment that both sides would accept as genuinely bearing on that crux; and each side's prediction about the outcome, registered in advance.

The cruxes should be genuinely in the weeds. Please include at least the following, and add further rows only if you can state them with equal precision:

- whether the law/accident distinction is fully fixed by the totality of particular matters of fact, or whether two possible worlds could agree in all particular facts and yet differ in their laws;
- whether the resources needed to state the best system—in particular, the distinction between natural properties and gruesome or gerrymandered ones—can be characterized without already presupposing lawhood or nomic modality;
- whether laws explain their instances or their instances explain the laws, and whether this explanatory-direction question settles the debate or merely restates it;
- whether there is a sense in which laws govern that goes beyond the metaphor of governance, and what such governing would have to consist in to make a counterfactual difference;
- whether laws and accidental regularities differ in kind or only in degree of systematic integration.

The tests you attach to each crux should be ones a fair-minded member of both camps would accept as probative: a thought experiment whose setup does not already encode one side's theory, a case about which both sides share the pre-theoretical verdict, or an inference drawn from data both accept. Where the two sides diverge, record their different predictions rather than having each side simply restate its own position.
