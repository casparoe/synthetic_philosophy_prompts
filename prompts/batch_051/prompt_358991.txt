Last night I had a long and somewhat bruising argument with a friend about whether Nazi Germany would still have emerged if Hitler had never been born. My friend kept pressing the side of structural explanation: the postwar settlement, the fragility of Weimar institutions, the Depression, and the deep currents of nationalist and antisemitic politics made some kind of authoritarian catastrophe highly likely. He accused me of smuggling in a Great Man picture whenever I pointed to Hitler’s specific obsessions and tactical choices. I wanted to resist his wholesale structuralism, but each time I tried to state exactly how individuals mattered, I found myself retreating into qualifications that seemed to pull against my earlier claims. I am trying to write a short statement of my actual position, and I would like an audit of whether the following commitments can be held together.

Here is the set I am working with. (1) Structural conditions—economic crises, institutional failures, long-run ideological formations, and international constraints—do most of the explanatory work in large historical transformations. (2) Nevertheless, some individuals make a decisive difference to some outcomes: without the particular views and decisions of specific people, certain historical events would have been materially different. (3) In the German case, interwar conditions made some form of radical right-wing authoritarian regime highly likely, but they did not determine that the regime would become genocidally antisemitic in the way the Nazi regime did. (4) Hitler’s particular obsessions and decision-making were causally necessary for the Holocaust to develop as an industrialized campaign of racial extermination rather than some other form of persecution or state terror. (5) But counterfactual claims about figures like Hitler are generally too underdetermined to be judged straightforwardly true or false: once one removes such a person, the background conditions and nearby alternative agents are not fixed by any principled rule. (6) An explanation can be adequate at one level of description while incomplete at another; structural accounts explain macro-level outcomes, while individual-level accounts explain the particular shape and timing of events. (7) When assigning responsibility for large historical crimes, we should treat individual leaders and their choices as the primary locus, not diffuse structures or abstract social forces.

Please treat this as a consistency check rather than a lecture on each commitment separately. I want to know which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only look as though they are, how severe each tension is, and what the least costly revision would be if the whole set is not jointly tenable. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad.
