I cover automation and labor for a general-interest magazine, and I've been knocking around a policy idea that sounds tidy on paper but probably goes sideways in practice. I want someone to walk me through how it would actually behave, not whether it's fair.

Heres the mechanism. Any firm that introduces a machine or process that cuts the labor hours required to make a unit of output in one of its establishments by more than 30%, measured against a rolling six-month baseline for that establishment, must pay an Automation Transition Fee. The fee is a lump sum equal to 18 months' worth of the wages attached to the eliminated hours, paid directly to the affected workers in proportion to how many of their hours got automated, whether or not the firm actually lays them off. The firm can cut the fee in half if a majority of the affected workers vote to approve a transition plan: retraining and redeploying at least 70% of them at no less than 90% of their previous pay for 3 years. Every automation event and every worker vote goes into a public registry.

I've been trying to think about this through that old Marxian picture where machinery is fixed capital that raises productivity spectacularly but doesn't itself create value, while living labor is the source of profit—so labor-saving technology is always grinding toward a contradiction for capital. Taking that picture as roughly right, tell me how this mechanism would play out. Where do firms game it (I can already imagine them slicing a 33% productivity jump into two 29% steps, or fighting endlessly over what counts as the baseline)? How do workers actually vote on the waivers, and what splits open up inside a workplace? What does the public registry do to bargaining, poaching, and organizing? What second-order effects does the whole thing have on which technologies get adopted and on wages? Are the worst failure modes fixable, or does fixing them just move the gaming somewhere else? And does the mechanism wind up accelerating the contradiction Marx worried about, or dampening it?
