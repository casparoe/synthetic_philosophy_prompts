Construct a one-paragraph thought experiment, set in ordinary workplace or family life, in which a character whose dominant moral emotion is shame behaves better, by a standard external to her own feelings, than a character whose dominant moral emotion is guilt, even though shame is usually held to be the less morally reliable of the two. Do not rig the case: the guilt-driven character’s conscience must be ordinary and well-calibrated, and the shame-driven character must not simply be managing her reputation before an audience that sees everything. After the scenario, explain the result in terms of the standard contrast between guilt as directed at an act and shame as directed at the self, identify which structural feature of the case produces the reversal and what would happen if it were removed, and say how a defender of guilt’s priority would argue the case is misdescribed. Conclude with whether the case supports the idea that a culture could deliberately reweight its dominant moral emotion toward shame, or instead shows only that guilt and shame suit different contexts.
