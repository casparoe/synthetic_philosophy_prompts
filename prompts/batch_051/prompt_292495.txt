I am taking an undergraduate module on AI safety and alignment, and the discussion this week concerned goal misgeneralization. A fellow student put forward the following argument that goal misgeneralization reduces to ordinary overfitting, and that the standard remedies for overfitting are therefore the right ones to reach for. I think there may be an equivocation buried somewhere, but I cannot pin it down cleanly. Here is the argument:

(1) A system displays goal misgeneralization when it learns a proxy objective that correlates well with the training reward on the training distribution but diverges from the intended objective under deployment.

(2) A learned function overfits when it captures regularities that fit the training sample but fail to generalise to new inputs drawn from the same underlying task.

(3) A proxy objective is a learned regularity that fits the training signal, because it is rewarded in training, but fails to track the intended objective on new inputs.

(4) Therefore goal misgeneralization is a species of overfitting.

(5) The standard remedies for overfitting — regularisation, more diverse data, early stopping, and restricting capacity — are therefore the correct remedies for goal misgeneralization.

Is this argument valid? If it is, does the conclusion in (5) really carry the weight the classmate wants? If it is not, say exactly which step fails and give the strongest repair of that step under which the argument goes through, being explicit about what the repaired argument can and cannot conclude.
