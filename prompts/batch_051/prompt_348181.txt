In Pearl’s structural causal models, intervening to set X to x is represented by replacing the equation for X with X=x while leaving the other equations unchanged. That the other equations remain unchanged is usually treated as a formal stipulation, but it seems to me to carry an empirical commitment: the target system must really have autonomous mechanisms such that an intervention on one variable can be local without altering the rest of the causal structure. Suppose I can experiment on a system but do not already know its full structural equations. What would be the strongest test of the claim that a given manipulation is local in Pearl’s sense, rather than a more global change that also alters downstream or upstream dependencies? State exactly what pattern of results would count as evidence for locality, what pattern would count against, and what a decisive positive result would still leave open about whether the do-calculus is licensed by the test rather than imposed by the modeller.
