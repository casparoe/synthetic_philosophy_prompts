I've been trying to get clearer on what role 'dignity' actually does in human rights discourse, and I keep flipping between the Kantian story (dignity is doing real foundational work, tied to rational autonomy and the idea that persons have a worth beyond price) and the Macklin-style deflationary story (dignity is a lofty placeholder that does no work beyond respect for persons and autonomy). A friend suggested I stop treating it as an abstract fight and look at a case where the concept had to earn its keep: the drafting of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in 1947–48. The broad story, as I understand it, is that the drafting committee was deliberately pluralist, with members coming out of very different religious and political traditions, and they disagreed pretty sharply about what grounds human rights or whether any shared foundation was even possible; yet dignity ended up at the center of the text. The Preamble makes the recognition of inherent dignity the basis on which freedom, justice and peace are said to rest, and Article 1 puts dignity right alongside freedom and equality when describing what everyone is born with. One standard reading is that dignity worked precisely because it was elastic enough that everyone could sign on to it without agreeing on why.

I'd like you to analyze the UDHR drafting as a case study for the debate over whether dignity is a foundation of rights or a useless concept. Give me a careful mapping of how a Kantian and a Macklin-style deflationist would each interpret what the drafters were doing when they reached for dignity: what each view would have predicted about a pluralist committee needing a shared foundation, whether the episode confirms or complicates those predictions, and which features of the episode each lens captures cleanly versus has to ignore or redescribe. End with what the episode, honestly read, should push someone to update about dignity as a concept in rights discourse, and don't just call it a draw; if one lens buys more of what actually happened, built that case.
