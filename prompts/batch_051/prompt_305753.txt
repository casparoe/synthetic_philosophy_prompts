I've been trying to pin down why I find Dennett's deflationary treatment of original intentionality both appealing and faintly unsatisfying, and I'd like comments on the following essay sketch before I develop it further. The essay centers on this passage from Dennett's "Evolution, Error, and Intentionality":

"The doctrine of original intentionality is the claim that whereas some of our artifacts may have intentionality derived from us, we have original (or intrinsic) intentionality, utterly underived. Aristotle said that God is the Unmoved Mover, and this doctrine announces that we are Unmeant Meaners."

My working idea is that Dennett's two-bitser argument is often read as denying any important difference between a vending machine's coin validator and a human belief, but that the passage points toward a slightly different target: not the fact of aboutness, but the demand that human aboutness be utterly underived. I want to argue that the real dispute is not about whether human intentionality is causally rooted in natural selection and learning, but about whether "derived" is the right word for the kind of dependence those roots involve. A coin validator's capacity is assigned by us; a human's capacity is, in Dennett's account, maintained by an interpretive stance that is not itself assigned by any human. So the charge that Dennett merely extends derived intentionality to us may rest on an equivocation between two senses of derivation: design by another agent and explanatory dependence on a non-intentional process. My worry is that this defense makes Dennett's view too close to the very doctrine he rejects, since once "derived" is split in this way, Searle or Fodor could accept the second sense while insisting on the first. I am not sure whether the essay should then accuse Dennett of relabeling, or instead argue that the relabeling is the substantive point.

Could you comment on whether this interpretation of the passage is textually plausible, whether the argumentative move I describe is strong enough to carry an essay, and where you would push back hardest? In particular, do you think the distinction I draw between assigned by an agent and explained by a non-intentional process does any philosophical work, or does it simply redescribe the original disagreement?
