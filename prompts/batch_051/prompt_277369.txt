I write about science and ideas for a general-audience outlet, and I'm working on a piece about the new pronatalist wave—the folks who say falling birthrates are a slow-burn crisis and that states should nudge people into having more kids. Underneath the policy dispute there's this older question about whether a bigger population is actually a good thing, and my editor wants a which-philosophical-camp-are-you-actually-in quiz to make that question feel concrete. I want it to be an honest quiz though: it should sort readers by what they say about little imagined cases, not by which label sounds nice to them. So no question can use words like totalism, person-affecting, critical-level, or repugnant conclusion; they have to be choices a reader can answer by gut.

Could you build me a nine- or ten-question quiz with a scoring key? The cases should cover at least: adding an extra happy person who has no effect on anyone else; a huge population of lives barely worth living versus a small population of excellent lives; replacing an existing happy person with a different, slightly happier person; bringing into existence a child whose life will be net miserable; and whether the continued existence of humanity gives us a reason to prefer a long chain of mildly positive future lives over extinction with a smaller number of excellent ones. Two or three answer options per question, phrased so each option pulls toward a genuinely different view.

The scoring key should be able to land readers in one of these camps: total utilitarianism, average utilitarianism, critical-level utilitarianism, a narrow person-affecting view that only counts people who will exist anyway, and a stronger asymmetry/anti-natalist view. But build it from the logical space rather than from the names. First lay out the underlying distinctions—average versus total aggregation, whether there's a wellbeing threshold below which extra lives don't count, person-affecting versus impersonal and whether person-affecting is narrow or wide, whether it matters that the people are different people, and whether failing to create a happy life is neutral while creating a miserable one is a wrong. Then say which combinations of those distinctions are coherent, place the named positions in that space, and flag any combinations nobody defends with a sentence on why (incoherent, wildly unattractive, or just unexplored?). Also tell me which pairs of positions your questions cant separate, and either add a diagnostic question or say what further case would split them. I'd rather have the quiz admit when two camps answer everything the same then pretend it's sharper than it is.
