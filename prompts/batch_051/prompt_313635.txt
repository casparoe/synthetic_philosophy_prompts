I am trying to reconstruct a friend's implicit view about what makes pain bad from the verdicts she gives on particular cases. So far I have only heard her judge ordinary, non-dissociated pains, where felt intensity, unpleasantness, and the urge to escape all ran together. She called a 1/10 pinprick not bad but just a sensation, a 3/10 stubbed toe mildly bad, a 5/10 toothache bad, and an 8/10 migraine very bad; in each case the intensity and unpleasantness ratings were equal, and her spontaneous explanations emphasized how unpleasant the pain felt rather than how intense it felt in isolation. I have never heard her judge a case in which these features come apart.

Next week I will ask her about a patient with pain asymbolia. When the patient is pricked or pinched, she can identify the location and quality of the stimulus and rates its intensity at 7/10, but she reports 0/10 unpleasantness, shows no distress or aversion, and feels no impulse to withdraw; she says the stimulus is painful but does not bother her. I want to predict my friend's verdict about how bad this patient's pain is, and, more important, to recover the rule she seems to be running, the rival rules that fit her earlier verdicts just as well, and a further case that would tell those rivals apart.

Return your answer as a single JSON object with the following fields:

- "candidate_rules": an array of objects representing distinct rules consistent with all the earlier verdicts, each with "name" (a short string), "statement" (a one-sentence formulation of the rule), and "predicted_verdict_for_new_case" (a string).
- "best_guess": an object with "verdict" (your predicted verdict for the asymbolia case), "preferred_rule" (the name of the rule you think best captures her, or a string of the form "underdetermined between X and Y"), and "reasoning" (a string explaining why that verdict and rule are favored).
- "decisive_further_case": an object with "description" (a string describing a further pain case involving a different dissociation than pain asymbolia), "per_rule_predictions" (an array of objects, each with "rule" and "verdict"), and "what_it_settles" (a string naming which rules the case separates and how).
- "closest_earlier_precedent": a string identifying the earlier case that most constrains the prediction, with a one-sentence explanation of why.

Use American English spelling and punctuation throughout the JSON values.
