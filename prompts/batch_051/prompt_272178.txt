I write about military technology, and I keep encountering the phrase "meaningful human control" as if it named a settled standard; in practice it covers several quite different demands, from control over a system's design to control over the parameters of an operation to control over each individual engagement. Before I draft the piece, I want the specification itself: what would an adequate account of meaningful human control over autonomous weapons have to deliver? Lay out the desiderata — the cases it must classify correctly (a commander who merely approves targets the system has selected; an operator who defines the targeting profile weeks in advance; an autonomous drone that selects targets but cannot strike until a human authorizes it; a fully autonomous system confined to a narrow, predictable environment), the distinctions it must not collapse (being in the loop versus actually exercising control; a system's predictability versus its controllability; responsibility for a war crime versus responsibility for an accident), and the legal and moral constraints it must respect (individual criminal responsibility, the duty to take feasible precautions, the principle that a weapon must be capable of lawful use). For each desideratum, give the case or principle that forces it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are one camp's preference. Then tell me which single requirement does the most work to exclude candidate positions, and flag any pair that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Keep any scoring of familiar proposals brief; the criteria are what I want.
