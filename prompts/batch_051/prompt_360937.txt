Take the standard Bayesian analysis of two independent witnesses to an unlikely event. An event M has prior probability π (0<π<1). Two witnesses each report that M occurred. When reporting on their own, each witness says that M occurred with probability p if M really occurred and with probability r if M did not occur, where 0<r<p<1, and the two reports are standardly treated as conditionally independent given M and given not-M. Under that treatment, the posterior odds of M after two positive reports are (π/(1-π))(p/r)²: the second report multiplies the evidential support of the first by another factor of p/r.

Now break the independence in one precise way. Suppose the second witness, with probability q, simply repeats the first witness's report verbatim, while with probability 1−q she produces her own report with the same reliability parameters p and r. After the reports are given, no one—including the second witness—can tell which process produced her report. Both witnesses report that M occurred.

Set p=0.9, r=0.1, π=0.01, and q=0.2 for a concrete comparison, and then give the general formulas. Work out the exact posterior odds of M in this correlated case, and compare them with the independent-witness calculation. In particular: (i) for what value or values of q does the second report add no further support, and why? (ii) For fixed p and r, how does a small positive q affect the second report's incremental likelihood ratio, and does this effect ever reverse direction? (iii) State a general condition on the reports' conditional dependence under which the multiplication heuristic is exact, and a condition under which it is merely a good approximation. Finally, say what this variation shows about the presumption of independence in heuristic probabilistic reasoning: when is it a harmless idealization, and when is it a systematic distortion?
