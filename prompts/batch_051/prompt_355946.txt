Since retiring from research in mineralogy, I have been reading philosophy in a small group, and we have spent the last few weeks on Tolstoy’s *What Is Art?* I am stuck on a passage in chapter 5 where Tolstoy sets aside the idea that art is the expression of beauty and states what he takes to be the essence of art: a human activity in which one person deliberately transmits a feeling he has himself lived through to another by means of external signs, and the other is infected by that feeling, so that the power of the art is measured by the power of the infection. He adds that much of what his own society calls art fails this test, since it is meant only to produce pleasure, novelty, or the flattery of the tasteful. The criterion has a chemist’s clarity to me, but when I try to apply it, it collapses distinctions I am reluctant to give up.

Consider these cases: the courtship song of a lyrebird, which is clearly shaped to affect a listener but is not a human activity; a Paleolithic handaxe in which a fossil shell is centered so that the tool’s symmetry runs through it, a choice that seems to exceed practical function; a proof of a theorem that trained mathematicians call beautiful, though it seems to produce no feeling in Tolstoy’s sense; a toothpaste jingle that everyone says they detest but cannot stop humming; a technically expert but emotionally cold academic painting; a child’s first drawing of a cat, made to convey her feeling about the cat; and a forged painting that is emotionally indistinguishable from the original. I suspect the child’s drawing and the handaxe are art, the jingle and the proof are not, the bird song is not, and I have no stable view about the forgery or the academic painting.

Give me a definition of art that handles these cases—or that shows me why some of my verdicts are wrong—and then run your definition and Tolstoy’s infection criterion through all seven, indicating where each gives the wrong answer or no answer. I am not looking for a survey of aesthetic theories; I need a working definition I can actually use.
