In my law-and-AI seminar, the corporate-person analogy is doing a lot of argumentative work. The move goes roughly like this: the law already treats non-human entities — corporations — as legal persons, giving them the capacity to own property, enter contracts, sue and be sued in their own name, and bear liability separately from their shareholders. So, the argument continues, there is no conceptual barrier to treating an AI system as a legal person; we already have a ready-made template for wrapping a non-human entity in legal personhood, and we should extend it to AI systems whenever we need a party to own assets, bind itself by contract, or absorb liability. I suspect the analogy is carrying more weight than it can bear, but I cannot yet say exactly where it gives out.

Please audit it for me. Give the correspondence table first: what in the target is supposed to correspond to the corporation itself, the shareholders and their limited liability, the board and the human agents who actually act, the entity's property, and the entity's liability. Then separate the places where the mapping fails into those that are merely cosmetic and those that do real work for the argument that corporate personhood shows AI personhood is unproblematic. In particular, I want you to press on these points: whether corporate personhood is best understood as a device for aggregating the interests and agency of human beings, while an AI system may not aggregate human principals in the same way; whether limited liability's rationale — encouraging human investment by capping downside risk — carries over when there may be no shareholders bearing residual risk in the relevant sense; whether granting an AI contractual capacity genuinely parallels granting it to a corporation, given that a corporation's capacity always has human decision-makers on both ends; and whether the corporate-person move would actually solve the liability-attribution problem or merely relocate it to an assetless shell. Finally, tell me whether the intended conclusion — that corporate personhood shows AI legal personhood is straightforward — survives once the serious disanalogies are on the table, and whether there is a better analogy or model for whatever is actually salvageable in the corporate-person move.
