Shipowners can register a vessel in Panama, Liberia, or the Marshall Islands even when they have no genuine connection to the place, and suddenly that boat is governed by Panamanian labor rules, Panamanian safety rules, Panamanian taxes. The owners are basically picking which country's jurisdiction their floating property lives under, just by filing papers and paying a fee. But the same owners, sitting in their office in Rotterdam, can't opt out of Dutch law by filling out a form, and they can't stop paying Dutch income tax by declaring themselves Marshallese. What does the flag-of-convenience system presuppose about what state authority is? Does it treat state authority as something you can contract into or out of, like a service provider, or is it just a legal fiction that works because enough states agree to go along with it? Reconstruct the implicit theory of political obligation behind the practice, say wether the practice actually needs that theory or is compatible with several rival ones, and tell me what would have to change on land if we took the maritime logic seriously.
