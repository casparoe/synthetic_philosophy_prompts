I'm in a grad seminar on social ontology and I keep having the same argument with the holists in the room, and I can't tell whether I've actually cornered them or whether I'm pulling a fast one. The dispute is whether social structures—norms, institutions, market positions, roles—have causal powers of their own, or whether every causal story in the social world bottoms out in individuals. My argument goes like this:

(1) Any social structure, at any time, is realized by individual people, their properties, and their relations; a norm isn't something over and above people believing, expecting, and sanctioning certain things.
(2) For any social outcome, there is a complete individual-level cause: the beliefs, desires, decisions, and interactions of particular people are jointly sufficient for the outcome. A bank run is just many depositors each deciding to withdraw given what each believes about what the others believe.
(3) If an event has a complete cause at the individual level, and a social-structural property is realized by those individual-level facts, then the structural property cannot be an additional cause of the event—unless the structural property is identical to some individual-level configuration.
(4) But structural properties are not identical to individual-level configurations, since the same structure (a market, a legal system, a norm) can survive complete turnover of the individuals realizing it.
(5) Therefore social structures never have causal powers above and beyond the individual level, and holist causal claims are at best shorthand.

The holists say the cheating is in step 3, that I'm just asserting an exclusion principle. But I don't see how they can deny step 3 without double-counting causes. Is the argument valid? Which step, if any, fails under the strongest reading a holist could give it? And if step 3 is the culprit, what's the most defensible version of their denial—do they have to deny that the individual level is causally complete, or is their a way to accept completeness and still say structures are genuinely causes?
