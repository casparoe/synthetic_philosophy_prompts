Lately I've been reading about antimemes -- pieces of information that seem to resist being remembered or spread, for all sorts of reasons: they're to boring to bother encoding, too complex to compress, too embarrassing or dangerous to repeat, or the mind just edits them out even after it sees them. I keep thinking a chunk of epistemology is about the single-mind version of the same thing: beliefs that resist being *kept*. Global skepticism is the obvious one: if you came to believe that no belief is justified, the reasoning that got you there seems to loose its own status, so the position is hard to hold stably. Epistemic akrasia (believing something while taking your evidence not to support it) seems similar, like the mind can't hold the combination for long without snapping to one side. Even Moore-paradoxical contents (roughly, asserting a claim while also asserting you don't believe it) can't be sincerely asserted together, even though each half is fine alone.

So here's the parallel I can't decide about: antimemes are usually discussed at the population level -- the measure is whether an idea replicates across minds -- while these epistemology cases are about one mind failing to keep a content coherent over time. Are these the same phenomenon at different scales (a content whose properties clash with the persistence conditions of the system carrying it), or is hard to keep in your head just a loose metaphor once you move from memory to belief? If it's deep, does anything transfer -- would we expect self-undermining views to spread only through courier-like tricks that bypass the host's consistency checks, the way antimemes supposedly need mnemonic workarounds or go-betweens? If it's superficial, give me the cleanest disanalogy. One reason I worry it's superficial: philosophical self-undermining views seem to propagate just fine -- people keep rediscovering global skepticism over and over -- which makes them memetically successful even if each individual instance is unstable. Maybe the transmission-between-minds vs stability-inside-one-mind distinction is doing the real work, but I can't tell if that's a refutation of the analogy or just the right way to state it.
