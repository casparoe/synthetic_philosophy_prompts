Here's an asymmetry in ordinary seeing that I can't quite settle. Stare at a bright light and then look at the wall, and you'll say the colored blob isn't really on the wall — it's just in your eye or your head. But look at the wall in the normal way and you'll say the whiteness is really on the wall; telling you it's only in your head sounds like sophistry. Yet your visual system is constructing both, and you never catch yourself constructing either. Is there any principled difference between the two cases, or is our habit of sorting experience into "really out there" and "merely in my head" an inconsistency we just live with? A theorist like Metzinger says all conscious experience is transparent representation — we see through the representation and never the representing itself. Does that dissolve the asymmetry, or just give it a fancier name?
