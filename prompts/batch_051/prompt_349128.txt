I'm trying to get a clear picture of the actual state of the debate between prioritarians and telic egalitarians over the leveling-down objection, and I think an adversarial-collaboration document is the right way to force both sides to stop talking past each other. Write the joint statement that a committed prioritarian and a committed telic egalitarian could both sign after a good-faith attempt to pin the dispute down. By prioritarian I mean someone who thinks benefits matter more the worse off you are, and that equality has no independent value; by telic egalitarian I mean someone who thinks inequality itself is non-instrumentally bad, not just a proxy for unfairness or for the worse off being badly off. By the leveling-down objection I mean the charge that telic egalitarianism is stuck saying a change that helps no one and harms some (pure leveling down) is in some respect better.

The deliverable is the document itself, not commentary on the debate. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table. Each row of the table should have: how each side would state its own position in wording that side would actually accept (no caricatures), the crux of that row (the subquestion such that settling it would move at least one side), and the case, argument, or test both sides would agree bears on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If a disagreement turned out to be merely verbal, put that in the agreed claims instead. Don't ask either side to concede more than it would, and don't let either side smuggle in its conclusion through slanted vocabulary — flag terms like "envy" or "fetishizing inequalities" as exactly the kind of wording the other side would reject.

Hard cap: 800 words. If the table gets too big, cut the weakest rows rather than compress the wording.
