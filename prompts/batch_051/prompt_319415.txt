Last night I spent a long evening arguing with a friend about whether a state can be genuinely secular while still maintaining an established church. The argument eventually turned on a passage from Locke’s *A Letter Concerning Toleration*, where he writes: “For whatsoever some people boast of the antiquity of places and names, or of the pomp of their outward worship; others, of the reformation of their discipline; all, of the orthodoxy of their faith — for everyone is orthodox to himself — these things, and all others of this nature, are much rather marks of men striving for power and empire over one another than of the Church of Christ.” My friend read this as implying that what matters for secularism is not whether a state has an official religion but whether it uses coercive force or creates civil advantage for religion. I am not sure the passage can bear that weight. We kept arguing about particular cases, and I want a more precise working definition of a secular state than either of us had.

I need a definition that correctly classifies the following cases, several of which we disputed:

- A state with an established church whose bishops sit in the legislature, but which does not require attendance, does not fund the church’s clergy from general taxation, and imposes no legal disability on other religions.
- A state with no established church that operates a church tax: it collects a surcharge from registered members of recognised religious bodies and passes the money directly to those bodies, with members free to opt out by formally deregistering.
- A state with no established church that prohibits pupils from wearing conspicuous religious symbols in state schools and funds private religious schools on the same terms as other private schools.
- A state with no established church that grants property-tax exemptions to all houses of worship, opens legislative sessions with prayers led by rotating clergy, and pays military chaplains.
- A state with an official religion that makes conversion away from that religion a criminal offence but imposes no tax or fee for its upkeep.
- A state with no established church that levies a mandatory tax on all residents and distributes the proceeds proportionally to recognised religious bodies according to their share of the population.

Propose a definition, test it against each case, and say where it yields the wrong answer, no answer, or a surprising answer that you think I should nevertheless defend. Then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. Please answer in British English.
