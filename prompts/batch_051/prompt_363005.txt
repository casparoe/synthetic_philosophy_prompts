I sit on my company's automation review board—the group that signs off before any internal system gets wide permissions like unguarded write access or the ability to run long unattended jobs. Our working rule is that tool-like systems get a quick review, while agent-like systems get the heavy treatment: a human-in-the-loop kill switch, sandboxing, full logging, the works. We've got a request in front of us right now that split the board. It's a system that refactors our monorepo's dependency graph: when it finds a cycle, it doesn't just flag it—it drafts a plan, opens PRs in the owning teams' repos, waits for CI, and if CI fails it revises and retries up to three times. It never deletes anything and never touches production. Half the board says its clearly a tool: deterministic, narrow, just does the one job. The other half says it's an agent: it has a goal state (an acyclic graph), keeps running for days, and decides how to get there without asking a human at each step. I need to bring a short memo to next week's meeting: do we treat this as tool or agent for review purposes, and what controls should we attach? Give me a clear recommendation with a bottom line, but make sure you engage the strongest argument on the losing side instead of a strawman.
