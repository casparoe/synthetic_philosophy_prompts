I've been doing one of those food delivery gigs in the evenings for the last few months while I finish school, and I keep noticing that I make choices which feel fine one at a time but add up to something I'd never have picked. A friend told me this is basically a known puzzle sometimes called the self-torturer, but I haven't read the stuff on it, so I want to work out what principle, if any, captures my actual verdicts. Here they are, as honestly as I can state them.

When I first signed up, the deal was two nights a week, 7 to 9, for around $240 extra a week: no hesitation, easy yes. A month later the app offered a boost to stay on until 10 for $25 more per shift; I was already out there, and the extra hour felt clearly worth the money, so I took it. That kept happening: 10 to 10:30, 10:30 to 11, a new $20-something per shift each time, and every single increment, judged from where I already was, looked worth it. Now I work six nights a week until 1:30am, I've basically dropped my hobbies, and the total is around $950 extra a week. But if you had shown me this final schedule up front and offered me $950 a week to do it, I'd have turned it down without thinking, and I'd turn it down again today. I'd prefer my old evenings-plus-no-extra-money life to the package I drifted into, even though I prefer each later step to the one before it.

To convince myself it's not just about delivery driving: I'd also accept a psychology lab's offer of $800 to sit with my hand in a warm water bath while the temperature is raised one half-degree at a time, with $50 more per half-degree; each half-degree feels like nothing when I'm already at that temperature, so I'd say yes to each increment. But I would never sign up in a single move for the final temperature for the full payment, and if I'm honest the final temperature would have me yanking my hand out within seconds. I also think, as a general matter, that "I prefer A to B all things considered" should be transitive. So either my verdicts are inconsistent, or one of the steps was the real mistake, or all-things-considered preference doesn't work the way I assumed.

What I want is the principle behind these verdicts, or the nearest coherent one. Which of them has to go, and why? What would the principle you settle on say about the decision I'm staring at right now: the app is offering $22 to extend tonight from 1:30 to 2:00am, and my gut says take it since I'm already out and it's only half an hour, but I also know that if I keep saying yes I'll be sitting here at 3am in a month telling myself the same story. And what does it say about the next small offer after that, if I keep the rule?
