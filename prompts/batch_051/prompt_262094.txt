So I just spent like two hours arguing with a friend about epiphenomenalism, and he kept coming back to the old steam whistle picture: consciousness is like a train's whistle, its making all the noise while the engine underneath does the actual work. His point was that you can admit qualia are real while still saying they don't cause anything. But the more I think about it the more the analogy feels like its smuggling in the one thing he's trying to deny. A real whistle doesn't just accompany the work; it makes sound waves, and those waves cause things: they vibrate eardrums, warn people, make an engineer pull a brake. If a mental state were epiphenomenal in the way he wants, there'd be no comparable route for it to 'make noise,' even to the person whose state it is. So I'm trying to figure out how far this analogy actually goes. Can you lay out the mapping — what in the analogy corresponds to what — where it breaks, and which disanalogies matter for the conclusion he's using it to defend? And after all that: can the whistle be patched up into a good way to think about epiphenomenalism, or is there a better analogy — or does the fact that we can know about our own experiences at all force the whistle back onto the wheels somehow?
