I'm putting together a one-page statement of my position in the philosophy of mathematics for a reading group, and before circulating it I want the position audited for internal consistency rather than argued with from outside. Here is the package as it currently stands. (1) I am a finitist in this sense: there are no actually infinite totalities, and the only legitimate use of "infinite" is the potential infinity of an indefinitely extendible process. (2) I accept classical real analysis as true, or at least as being in perfect standing: I have no interest in a philosophy of mathematics that makes the least-upper-bound property of the reals, or the usual notion of a function on the continuum, false or merely fictional. (3) I find Cantor's diagonal argument valid and persuasive, and I accept its standard conclusion: the real numbers cannot be placed in one-to-one correspondence with the natural numbers. (4) I regard Hilbert's hotel as a legitimate and instructive thought experiment: under the supposition of an actually infinite totality, it correctly shows that such a totality can be matched one-to-one with a proper subset of itself. (5) I believe the calculus, and indeed most of classical analysis, needs only potential infinity, so that the completed infinite sets in the standard presentations are eliminable or paraphrasable without loss. I genuinely do not know which of these, if any, has to go, and I would rather you did not assume I have a preferred candidate in mind.

Please go through the commitments pairwise, and in larger clusters where that matters, and tell me which pairs conflict in substance, which pairs only appear to conflict and can be reconciled, and which pairs support one another. Order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each tension say exactly what would have to give, what the least costly revision would be, and what that revision costs elsewhere. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to restore consistency and why it is the best trade.
