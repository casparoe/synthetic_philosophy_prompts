So I had a long argument last night with a mate who reckons video games can't ever be art because they're just rule-systems you win or lose, and any 'art' in them is bolted-on story and graphics. I kept saying the interesting bit is the agency itself — the choices you make and what it's like to make them — but I couldnt make a definition of art that captures that without either counting every branching decision tree as art or ruling out stuff that's plainly art. Give me a working definition of 'art' (or 'artwork', if that's the better term) that can handle these cases, and run it against each one: a Vermeer painting; a child's crayon drawing on the fridge; a big-budget narrative game about a father and daughter surviving a plague; a block-dropping puzzle game; a two-hour game where you mostly just walk around listening to a voiceover and can't fail; a football match; a pile of bricks arranged in a gallery; a piece of music composed by an algorithm; a wedding cake; a slot machine. Some of those are clearly art to me, some clearly aren't, and a few I genuinely don't know. I especially want the definition to say something precise about agency: can the choices a player makes be an artistic property, and if so, why isn't every spreadsheet with a couple of drop-downs art? Be honest about cases where your definition gives a surprising verdict, and either revise it or defend the verdict. End with a one-sentence summary of the final definition as the last line.
