You are a professional philosopher specialising in philosophy of mind and intentionality. Please think carefully, and perhaps start by brainstorming the strongest versions of the opposing view before you commit yourself.

I'm a novelist, and I'm writing a book set in a near-future translation bureau in Swansea. One of the characters is a machine that translates between several languages by matching every input against an enormous finite table of human-translated sentences and then assembling the closest available bits, so it has no grasp of what anything means, but its output is indistinguishable from that of a fluent human translator. The people in the bureau spend much of the book arguing about whether this machine genuinely means anything by its translations or whether the meaning is entirely borrowed from the humans who built and trained the table. I've been doing a bit of reading around the Searle–Dennett dispute about original versus derived intentionality, and I keep coming back to Dennett's two-bitser: roughly, a coin-operated gadget that, when you pay it, insists that it means its words intrinsically, and whose claim to intrinsic meaning most of us find easy to dismiss. The challenge, as I understand it, is to say what extra ingredient a human brain supposedly has that the two-bitser and my translation machine both lack.

Please make the strongest possible case for the position that the distinction between original and derived intentionality is not a real difference in kind. That is, argue that human intentionality is just as derived as the machine's, because it is built up through natural selection, neural learning and cultural training rather than through some intrinsic glow of aboutness inside the mental state itself. Engage seriously with the Searle-style objection that when I think or speak, my meaning is not assigned by an outside interpreter, whereas the machine's is. If you think the strongest version of the view needs weakening or modifying to be defensible, say so, and then argue for the version you actually accept.
