Consider the following reconstruction of an argument for mandatory moral bioenhancement of citizens, of the sort found in the moral bioenhancement debate associated with Ingmar Persson and Julian Savulescu.

(1) If there is a collective duty to prevent a foreseeable moral catastrophe, and safe, effective moral bioenhancement is the only feasible means of preventing it, then there is a duty to implement moral bioenhancement.

(2) There is a collective duty of humanity to prevent foreseeable moral catastrophe, such as nuclear war or environmental collapse resulting from moral failures.

(3) Safe, effective moral bioenhancement is the only feasible means of preventing such catastrophe.

(4) Therefore, there is a duty to implement moral bioenhancement.

(5) States are the agents best placed to implement moral bioenhancement for their populations.

(6) Therefore, states would be justified in implementing mandatory moral bioenhancement for their citizens.

I want a precise verdict: is the argument valid? If not, at which step exactly does it fail, and under what interpretation of the key terms? If the argument is valid but unsound, identify which premise or premises a critic should reject and why. If you think it can be repaired, state the additional premise or modification and check whether the repaired argument still supports the conclusion. Restrict yourself to the logic of the argument and the stated premises; do not answer with general worries about enhancement or human nature.
