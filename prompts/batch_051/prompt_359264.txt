I would like a working definition of the term agent as it is used in the contrast between tool AI and agent AI, where the distinction is doing a good deal of work in current debates about the safety and governance of artificial systems. I do not mean agent in the grammatical or commercial sense; I mean the sense in which an artificial system that persistently pursues a single objective, such as manufacturing paperclips, would be called an agent, while a device that merely performs whichever operations a user selects would be called a tool. Please give me a definition that correctly classifies the following cases, which I have chosen to stress-test the boundary: a pocket calculator that evaluates the arithmetic expressions you type into it and displays the result; a thermostat that switches the heating on when the temperature falls below a set point and off when it rises above it, and does nothing else; a chess engine that, for any position it is given, returns only the move it evaluates as strongest and performs no other action; a passenger jet's autopilot that, once engaged, maintains heading and altitude until the pilot takes the controls, at which point it disengages immediately; a large language model that normally answers a query in one pass but which, when asked something that requires calculation, may write and run a short program as an intermediate step before producing its final answer; a robot controlled by a vision-language model that is told to clear the table and then forms a sequence of subgoals, re-plans when something goes wrong, and chooses among available implements such as a cloth and a bin; and a large corporation that pursues quarterly profit through the coordinated actions of many employees and can do things that none of its members individually decided to do. I am confident that the calculator and the thermostat are tools and that the robot and the corporation are agents, though I could not state the principle behind these verdicts. Of the remaining three I have no settled view, though I lean towards counting the chess engine as a tool and the code-writing language model as an agent. Propose the definition, run it through all seven cases, and tell me where it surprises you, where it yields the wrong answer or no determinate answer, and either revise the definition or defend any surprising verdicts. Please write your answer in British English, and please write in continuous prose only, as in an email or a letter, with no headings, bullet points, numbered lists, bold text, or tables.
