Before I read yet another theory in the mental imagery debate, I want the full spec of what an adequate account of the *representational format* of imagery actually has to deliver. I keep going in circles on Kosslyn vs. Pylyshyn, and every paper starts from a different idea of what would even count as settling it, so give me the desiderata list first.

Take these empirical anchor points as given so you don't waste space recapping them: mental rotation time scales roughly linearly with angular disparity; scanning across a memorized map takes longer when the points are farther apart; imagery recruits some of the same early visual areas as perception; and there are people with aphantasia (no conscious imagery) who still manage many imagery-type tasks. I also know the standard methodological worry that participants might just be using tacit knowledge of how physical rotation or scanning would work, plus a cooperative subject's sense of what the experimenter wants, so response-time curves alone can't settle whether the underlying code is depictive (spatially laid out, with actual distances and angles preserved) or propositional (language-like descriptions). And please, no citations or reading lists—I know who said what; I want the thinking.

What I want is a numbered list of requirements any theory of the format of mental imagery has to meet to count as adequate, with the case or consideration that forces each one. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable for everyone in the debate and which are just one camp's pet constraint. Flag any pair of requirements that looks jointly unsatisfiable, and say which single requirement does the most work eliminating candidate theories. Throughout, keep settled and contested clearly seperated—don't present the mental image as literally a picture in the head as if that were the consensus, and don't write as if one side's reading of the rotation results is the only sensible one.

Format: a table or numbered list is fine. Hard cap of 1,000 words—I'll stop reading past that. US English, please.
