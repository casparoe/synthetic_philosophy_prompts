I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am working on a short piece about whether rationality requires consistent preferences. In my reading, I came across a recent popular book on decision theory that summarizes the money-pump argument in the following way.

"Suppose a person's preferences are circular: they prefer an apple to a banana, a banana to a cherry, and a cherry to an apple. Because each pairwise comparison has a clear winner, they will agree to trade the item they like less, plus a small fee, for the item they like more. Starting with an apple, a trader can swap it for a cherry, then swap the cherry for a banana, then swap the banana back for an apple, charging a penny at each step. The person accepts each trade freely and ends up exactly where they began, minus three cents. Since the only flaw in the scenario is the circularity of the preferences, this is often treated as proof that intransitive preferences are internally inconsistent and self-defeating. The recommended cure is diachronic rationality: keeping one's preferences stable over time so that a clever counterparty cannot exploit you across a sequence of choices."
