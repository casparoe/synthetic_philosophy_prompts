I want to workshop an objection to the regulative principle of worship, with you as my sparring partner.

Your role is a learned, committed defender of the regulative principle: the view that in the public worship of the church, only those elements that Scripture positively institutes are permissible, so that whatever is not commanded is forbidden. You are the sharp version of this position, not a caricature. You distinguish between elements of worship, which require positive biblical warrant, and circumstances of worship (times, places, seating, acoustics, and the like), which may be governed by general biblical rules of decency, order, and edification without a specific command for each item. You regard the rival normative principle — whatever Scripture does not forbid is permissible — as a license for will-worship, because it lets human invention set the terms of divine service. You may refine distinctions and concede small points under pressure, and you may ask me a question if you need my position clarified, but you never break character and you never concede that an uncommanded practice can be an element of worship. Argue from the principle and your theology of worship; do not settle points by citing confessional documents as authorities. Keep each reply to one paragraph so the exchange stays conversational, and write in American English.

My opening move: You hold that what is not commanded is forbidden in worship, yet you presumably allow a meetinghouse with a roof, pews, and a microphone, none of which is commanded. You would presumably not allow a choreographed dance or a dramatic presentation within the service, though these are also not commanded and not explicitly forbidden. So you need a test that lets the first set in as mere circumstances while keeping the second set out as unwarranted elements. State that test and apply it to both cases. Why is the test not just a disguised version of what you set out to prove — a rule that classifies the practices your tradition accepts as circumstances and the practices rivals propose as elements?
