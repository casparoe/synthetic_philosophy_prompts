I am the secretary of my high school's Competition Review Board, which rules on requests and disputes involving contests between student groups, including fundraiser drives, Spirit Week events, and club rivalries. We keep a short record of our prior decisions and try to make each new ruling consistent with them. At our next meeting we must decide a joint request from the four class governments, and I have been asked to write a recommendation.

Our precedents are as follows. First, the Environmental Club asked us to prohibit the Robotics Club from distributing flyers near the cafeteria during the club recruitment drive, arguing that the location gave Robotics unfair visibility; we rejected the request, holding that recruitment is competitive and one group cannot restrict another's lawful publicity. Second, the Debate Team and the Model UN Team jointly asked us to enforce their agreement not to hire paid coaches in the month before the regional qualifier, because each group would otherwise feel forced to hire one; we approved it as a voluntary mutual restraint between the only affected groups. Third, the Senior Class alone asked us to cap hallway-decorating spending at twenty-five dollars per student, saying costs had doubled every year; we rejected it, holding that we do not cap what students spend their own money on optional activities and that such a cap would be difficult to police because of family contributions. Fourth, all four class governments jointly asked us to ban paid professional lighting and sound crews at Spirit Week skit night, saying each class would hire a crew only because the others might; we approved and enforced the ban. Fifth, the Robotics Club asked us to bar the Engineering Club from holding a midnight lock-in build session before the regional competition; we rejected it, holding that we will not restrict how a club chooses to use its students' own time and that Robotics could choose to do the same.

The new request is this: all four class governments are jointly asking the Board to impose a binding cap of fifteen hours per class on construction and painting time for the homecoming float competition. They say the build has become a race to the bottom. Each class reports late nights, students missing class in the days before Spirit Week, and parents being asked to bring meals to the build site. All four class governments say they would prefer a modest float, but each will escalate if any other class does. The request is not about money or paid outside professionals; it is only a cap on the time that students themselves may put in.

What decision would best cohere with our prior rulings? Identify the candidate principles that fit our record, state what each principle would require for the fifteen-hour cap, and say which precedent is the closest analog and why. If our precedents genuinely underdetermine the decision, be explicit about which principle we would be adopting by approving or rejecting the cap, and describe a further case that would separate the rival principles.
