I work at a regional auction house, and we just got a consignment that's forcing me to guess what my colleague Mara will do, because she has an almost unerring track record on originals versus fakes and she hates when we ask her to write out a policy. It's a small bronze, cast in the 1950s from an 1880s sculptor's original plaster mold, by a collector who'd ended up with the mold; the estate never authorized the cast, but the collector also never faked a signature or passed it off as a lifetime cast, and its been in his family since. Mara is the one who signs off on whether we take this kind of thing, and I honestly can't call it.

That's because her past calls don't line up under any single slogan. When a painting long billed as Rembrandt was reattributed to a capable pupil, she dropped the estimate hard but said it was still a fine seventeenth-century painting, not worthless. When someone brought in a modern copy of a Cézanne that was indistinguishable to the naked eye, she said it had essentially none of the Cézanne's value, decorative use at most. When that famous Vermeer forgery was unmasked, she said the thing was worthless as a Vermeer but genuinely interesting as a forgery, and she'd go to an exhibition of it. When a Rembrandt that had survived a fire came in with seventy percent of the surface repainted by conservators, she said it was still the same painting, damaged but not a different work. When a bronze was cast from a sculptor's own plaster after the sculptor's death, under the estate's authorization, she called it a real work by the artist, just a later cast. And when someone tried to consign a 3D-scanned, CNC-milled reproduction of a sculpture, she refused to list it as the artist's work at all.

So here's the new case: the unauthorized 1950s cast from the genuine original plaster, no deception, no estate blessing. Does Mara treat it as a real work by the artist, like the authorized posthumous cast; as a fake with only curiosity value, like the Vermeer; or as a mere reproduction, like the CNC piece? I'm less interested in the auction outcome than in the rule she's actually running, the other rules that fit her track record just as well, and where they split on this bronze. Give me a committed prediction of Mara's call, with the principle behind it, and name one further case you'd want to run by her to separate whichever rules are still in the running.
