My friends and I run a small sci-fi and fantasy club, and we have a written constitution. Rule 2 says, roughly: "Any rule of this constitution, including this rule, may be amended or repealed by a two-thirds vote of members present at a properly called meeting." Last Tuesday we had a quorum and someone moved to amend Rule 2 to say: "Any rule of this constitution may be amended or repealed only by unanimous vote." It passed with about three-quarters in favor — comfortably more then two-thirds, but obviously not unanimous. Now one of the members who voted no is insisting the whole vote was invalid, because under the newly amended Rule 2 the amendment would have needed unanimous approval. The people who voted yes say that's backwards: the vote happened under the Rule 2 that was in force at that moment, so it met the requirements and the new Rule 2 is now in effect. We can't even agree on which version of Rule 2 currently governs. There's no higher rule in the constitution for what to do when an amendment changes the amendment-rule itself, and no outside authority to ask. Which side is right? And how should we go about fixing this, given that any next vote seems to presuppose an answer to that question? I'm less interested in parliamentary-procedure trivia then in getting the underlying logic straight.
