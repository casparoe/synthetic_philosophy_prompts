I am a novelist. Clause 12 of the contract for my first novel reads: “The Author grants to the Publisher, throughout the world and for the full term of copyright, the exclusive right to reproduce, distribute, adapt, dramatize, and otherwise exploit the Work, in whole or in part, in all languages and in every medium, form, or channel of communication now existing or hereafter developed.” At signing, everyone understood this to cover paperback, translation, serial, and film rights. No one foresaw the following case. A company now proposes to license from the publisher the complete text of the novel as training data for a generative model that would produce new narrative prose using my characters, setting, and narrative voice, sold as new stories from the world of the novel. The output would not reprint my sentences. The publisher says the use falls within “adapt” and “otherwise exploit” in a medium “hereafter developed.” Does the clause, properly construed, reach this? Argue it from (a) the words, (b) the purpose the clause served in the original transaction, (c) what the parties would have said had the use been put to them, and (d) what a reasonable reader at the time of signing would have understood. Where these approaches diverge, say which should control and why; and address whether it matters that the system would imitate my style and story-world without copying my wording.
