Write an essay on whether the golden rule can actually be repaired. I'm doing this for my moral philosophy module, so don't just rehearse the usual slogans. Get into the difference between the positive version (treat others as you'd like to be treated) and the negative version (don't do to others what you wouldn't want done to yourself), and the standard objection that the rule turns the agent's own quirks into the moral standard, like the masochist who reckons he ought to harm people because he'd enjoy being on the receiving end. The thing I'm stuck on is this: if you patch the rule by making the other person's actual desires the test, the so-called platinum rule, have you fixed the golden rule or just swaped it for something that isn't really a version of it anymore? Weigh the main ways people try to save the rule, then commit to a verdict. Defend whichever side you land on rather than sitting on the fence.
