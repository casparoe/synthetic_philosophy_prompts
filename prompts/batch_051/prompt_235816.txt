I've been stewing on something in the law and I want you to take a real position on it, not just survey the options. Suppose I know an embarrassing truth about you, and suppose it's the kind of truth I'm legally free to publish. If I just tell everyone, that's lawful — mean, maybe, but lawful. If I just ask you for a thousand dollars, no threat attached, that's also lawful. But if I put the two together — "pay me a thousand dollars or I'll tell everyone" — now it's blackmail, a crime. The law seems to say each ingredient is fine on it's own, but combine them and a new wrong appears out of nowhere. I can't see what the "new" part is. Is there a real difference between the pair and the combination, or are we just giving the combo a nastier name? Build the best case you can for the asymmetry, and test it against this: I calmly tell you "I'd like a thousand dollars, I also know this secret, and I'll publish it unless you pay." No deception, no violence, just a conditional statement of what I'll do. If you think the *threat* is what does the work, say exactly what makes a threat different from a plain announcement of my intentions. Then tell me whether we should keep the asymmetry, make blackmail legal, or criminalize more of the ingredients. And whatever verdict you land on, tell me what would change your mind — which argument or counterexample would flip you, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
