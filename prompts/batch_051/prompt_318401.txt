I'm in a metaphysics reading group and got into it about dispositions last week, and I've accidently built an argument that seems to prove nothing is fragile, which obviously cant be right. But I can't see the flaw, and the group just talked past each other. We started from the idea that to say "this wine glass is fragile" is to say the glass would break if struck in the right circumstances. Then someone brought up finks and masks. A fink is something ready to remove the disposition the instant before the stimulus arrives (a meddling sorcerer or a device wired to the glass), and a mask is something that stops the manifestation without removing the disposition (like packing the glass in foam so it stays fragile but doesn't break when struck). The problem is that these seem to screw up any attempt to say what the "right circumstances" are. That pushed me into the following argument:

(1) A disposition ascription is true only if the manifestation would occur in some suitably specified circumstances.
(2) For any proposed specification of the suitable circumstances that just lists an ordinary physical setup — struck with such-and-such force on a hard floor and so on — there is a possible case that meets the specification but with an added fink or mask, and in that case the glass does not break.
(3) The only way to rule those cases out is a blanket clause like "in the absence of anything that would prevent the manifestation" — but spelling out what counts as preventing the manifestation seems to require referring back to the disposition itself (a fink of fragility, a mask of breaking).
(4) So the analysis is either circular or it leaves exceptions it can't rule out.
(5) Either way, the conditional analysis of fragility fails, and since that was the only account on the table, we should conclude nothing is really fragile.
(6) But that's absurd: wine glasses are fragile.

I'd much rather keep (6) than any of the others, but I want to know exactly what I'm paying. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost if I want to keep fragility as an intrinsic property of the glass?
