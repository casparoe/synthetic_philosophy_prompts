In The Fixation of Belief, Peirce writes: “Kepler undertook to draw a curve through the places of Mars; and to state the times occupied by the planet in describing the different parts of that curve; but perhaps his greatest service to science was in impressing on men's minds that this was the thing to be done if they wished to improve astronomy; that they were not to content themselves with inquiring whether one system of epicycles was better than another but that they were to sit down to the figures and find out what the curve, in truth, was.”

I am a high-school student working on a paper about Peirce’s account of inquiry, and this passage has left me stuck. I find Peirce’s fallibilist theory compelling: he seems to say that the method of science is superior to tenacity, authority, and a priori reason because it does not ask which belief feels most satisfying or which belief is most widely shared, but instead asks how our beliefs must be formed if they are eventually to be determined by something outside ourselves. But the passage also presents Kepler’s greatest service as mainly a matter of impressing on people that they should sit down with the data and find out what the curve truly was, while the surrounding text describes Kepler blundering through twenty-two irrational hypotheses and only hitting on the right orbit by the mere exhaustion of his invention. I have a firm intuition that scientific rationality must involve more structure than that: otherwise the method of science seems indistinguishable from stubborn persistence plus luck, and the difference between a good inquirer and a crank would be hard to state. Which should yield—Peirce’s theory that the method of science is genuinely self-correcting, or the intuition that a scientific method must constrain and guide inquiry in a more definite way than Peirce’s historical account suggests? I do not want only a verdict. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for Peirce’s view to be amended or abandoned, and whether there is a reading on which the two are compatible.
