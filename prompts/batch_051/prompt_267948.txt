This is for an upper-level philosophy of mind and cognitive science course. The question below was on the midterm, and a student wrote the answer that follows. Please grade the answer the way an instructor or TA would.

The exam question was:

Explain the idea often called competence without comprehension, as it shows up in Darwin's strange inversion of reasoning and Turing's strange inversion of reasoning. For each inversion, say what exactly gets turned upside down, and then discuss how the two inversions are related. Finally, say what revision, if any, these inversions force in our ordinary assumptions about where design and understanding come from.

Student's answer:

Darwin's inversion is the idea that you can get design without a designer. Before Darwin, people tended to reason from the adapted complexity of organisms to an intelligent mind that made them. Darwin reversed this: mindless processes—differential survival and reproduction acting on heritable variation—can build up adaptations over many generations. So biological complexity is explained from the bottom up rather than the top down. Thats competence without comprehension: the process produces hugely functional results without anybody, including the process itself, understanding what it is doing.

Turing's inversion makes the same kind of move for computation and intelligence. Turing showed that any effective procedure can be carried out by a machine that just follows a finite set of mechanical rules and manipulates symbols without knowing what they mean. So a system can be extremely competent—doing arithmetic or playing chess, say—without comprehending the content of what it is doing. This inverts the commonsense idea that understanding has to come first and that competent performance is what flows from understanding.

The two inversions are basically the same move in different domains: competence is explained as emerging from the blind operations of simpler uncomprehending parts, and comprehension is demoted from being the source of competence to being something that has to be built up out of such mechanisms. Darwin's version concerns design in the biological world; Turing's version concerns intelligence and meaning.

One consequence is that purpose and function are not real features of the world. If you can get design without a designer, then saying a heart is for pumping blood is only a useful metaphor we project onto organisms, not something true of them.

Another consequence is that, since any effective procedure can be run by a non-understanding machine, there is no deep difference between a system that merely follows rules and one that actually understands what it is doing; the difference is just where the rules came from.
