I keep running into the slogan "philosophy never makes progress" — most recently from a senior colleague, who used it to dismiss a job candidate's suggestion that recent work on causation has improved on Hume. He seemed to be sliding between several distinct claims: that philosophical questions never get settled, that philosophy produces no technology or predictions, that any real progress eventually gets hived off into the sciences, that there is no accumulating body of agreed results. Could someone seperate out the precise claims hiding in that one line, say which of them are true-but-trivial, which are substantive and defensible, and which are actually false, and then say which reading does the work when someone invokes it against a specific example of alleged philosophical progress?
