Im a novelist, not a psychologist, but I need a working map of the introspection/rumination terrain for the book I'm drafting. My protagonist spends a huge amount of time in her own head, and I want to be able to sort her episodes of self-examination into types, because the plot turns on her sliding from honest, useful self-examination into something that is slowly wrecking her. Could you write me a decision tree (or a taxonomy, if a strict tree forces too many false choices) that classifies an episode of self-examination? Make it diagnostic: each branch should be a question I could answer about the scene from the outside, without already knowing the jargon — like "is she asking a question she can actually settle?" or "does the episode have a stopping condition?" rather than "is this rumination or reflection?". I'd like around six or seven leaves, enough to be useful but not so many that it turns into a parody of a personality quiz.

The angle that matters for my purposes is mostly three distinctions. (1) Whether the episode is directional or loop-shaped: does it build toward a decision, a new thought, or a change in feeling, or does it cycle over the same material? (2) What kind of question drives it: "why" questions aimed at causes and flaws versus "what" or "how" questions aimed at what happened and what to do next. (3) Where the attention sits: looking at a problem from a little distance, or staring at the self as the object. If you can, also work in whether the episode is chosen or whether it pounces on her. Build the tree from the logical space of those questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then locate the actual named positions inside it — rumination, reflection, worry, counterfactual dwelling, self-distanced analysis, whatever really deserves a leaf. Each leaf should have a name and one sentence on whether that mode tends to be harmful to her, and why or when it flips. For combinations of answers where nobody lives, say so and take a guess at why: incoherent, too unpleasant, or just unexplored. Also flag any two positions the tree can't actually tell apart, so I dont overtrust it when I map scenes onto it.

I've got test scenes in mind: her lying awake at 2 a.m. re-running an argument from dinner; her taking a long walk and actually deciding to end a friendship; her standing in the shower and suddenly doubting whether she's ever been a good person. The tree should be able to place those three without me knowing ahead of time which leaf each lands in.

And at the end, tell me how confident you are in the whole scheme, and single out the one claim or branch you're least sure of. I'd rather have an honest, uncertain sorting tool than a clean fake one.
