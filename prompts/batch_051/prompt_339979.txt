I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, so when I had to decide whether to leave my secure job for a startup offer, I treated the decision as a modelling problem. I wrote down the possible outcomes, assigned utilities, and then noticed that I needed a probability for the event "I will accept the offer." It soon felt like a recursive function that passes its own output back as an input: the probability I was trying to pin down concerns the very decision I was in the middle of making. That led me to the argument below, which I cannot assess on my own. (1) To decide by expected value, I need a probability p for the proposition that I will accept the offer. (2) For p to be rational, it must be the probability supported by my evidence. (3) My evidence includes my preferences, my habits, how I have handled similar choices, and the reasons before me now. (4) But while I am still deliberating, whether I accept is not yet settled; it is the output of the process of weighing those reasons. (5) If I adopt a particular value for p, that adoption itself becomes new evidence about my choice and can shift the very value it was meant to measure; I see no guarantee of convergence on a stable fixed point. (6) So any p I adopt is either arbitrary or unstable, and neither an arbitrary nor an unstable number is a rational input. (7) Therefore expected-value reasoning about my own choice needs an input it cannot have. (8) Hence I cannot rationally use expected-value reasoning to decide what to do while I am still deciding.

I have a nagging feeling that one of the early steps mischaracterises what an expected-value calculation must contain, and I also wonder whether this is just a special case of a more general problem: can I assign any probability to my own choices at all while I am still deliberating? Please go through the argument step by step, state whether it is valid, and if not, identify exactly which step fails. For the step that fails, give the strongest reading on which it is true, say what the correct version of it would be, and explain whether the final conclusion still follows in some weakened form. It would also help to hear what this implies for how one ought to deliberate about a large personal decision. Please answer in British English.
