I'm a retired chemist who's only recently got into philosophy, mostly logic and metaphysics. I keep going back and fourth on an argument I half-remember, and I can't tell whether it's a real problem for the doctrine of the Trinity or just a word trick. It runs like this:

(1) The Father is God, the Son is God, and the Holy Spirit is God.
(2) If a is God and b is God, then a is identical with b. (If two things are both God, there's just one God between them.)
(3) So the Father is identical with the Son, the Son is identical with the Spirit, and the Father is identical with the Spirit.
(4) But the Father is not identical with the Son; the persons are really distinct.
(5) Contradiction. Therefore the Trinity is logically incoherent.

I once read that identity might be sortal-relative: nothing is just 'the same', only the same person or the same God or the same lump of matter. On that story the Father and the Son could be the same God without being the same person. But I was trained on classical logic, where identity is absolute and obeys Leibniz's law, so I don't know whether that move is a genuine fix or a dodge.
