I spent my career designing experiments, so when people in the autonomous-weapons debate say a human has to keep "meaningful control" over the system, my first move is to ask how you'd test for it. Here's the setup I keep coming back to: a loitering munition scans a grid, its software flags candidate targets, and an operator has to press "authorize" before it fires. In version A the operator gets a clear feed and ten minutes per target; in version B they get a grainy image and two seconds; in version C they're ten hours into a shift and mostly clicking through a queue. All three involve the same button press, so a purely behavioral measure can't tell them apart. What would you measure — or what experiment would you run — to decide wether the control in each version is meaningful rather than nominal? What result would count for and against, what part of "meaningful" do you think no test can reach, and if a test came out positive, would it show there's a real property there or just that we've agreed to call something measurable "meaningful control"?
