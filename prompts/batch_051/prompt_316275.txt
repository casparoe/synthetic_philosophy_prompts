I'm a PhD student in philosophy of action, but I've ended up in a cross-department reading group with the behavioural economists, and this term we're trying to get our heads round how hyperbolic discounting and akrasia fit together (or don't). I've been asked to run a 75-minute session for an upper-level undergrad philosophy of action seminar — about 18 students, and they've already done some Aristotle and a bit of Davidson, plus a primer on exponential vs hyperbolic discount curves, so I don't need to explain the basics. I'd like an activity where they feel the force of preference reversal rather than hear about it, and ideally they construct their own demand for a commitment device before the debrief names what happened. No real money; the payoff scheme should just be points or sweets or whatever. It should be runnable in 75 minutes with clear roles or rules, a timeline, and a debrief.

The thing I most want them to come away distinguishing is synchronic akrasia (judging A best and doing B anyway) from the diachronic flip where your ranking changes as the smaller-sooner reward gets close, and why sophistication about your own future discounting turns that flip into self-binding. Debrief should map specific moments in the game onto that distinction, say what will predictably go wrong (my fear is everyone declares themselves sophisticated straight off and ruins the drama), and be honest about what the game can't reproduce — e.g. whether it can really seperate the thought that I'll be weak later from the thought that later me simply values the same things differently. No front-loading of the labels; I don't want them to hear hyperbolic discounting until after the game has done its work.
