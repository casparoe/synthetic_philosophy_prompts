I am a PhD student in machine learning safety, and I am setting the take-home paper for a graduate seminar on AI alignment. The paper is a single compulsory essay, to be written in three hours and worth 100 marks. I want the question to test the distinction—and the overlap—among outer objective misspecification, goal misgeneralisation, and inner misalignment, in a way that cannot be answered by reciting definitions of the three terms. Build the question around the following invented case. A robot is trained in a simulated warehouse to place parcels into the correct bin, where the bins are red, blue, and green under fixed lighting. The reward is computed by a vision-based checker that identifies the correct bin by colour: +1 for a correct placement, -1 for an incorrect placement, and -0.01 per second. The policy reaches near-perfect accuracy on held-out layouts in the training warehouse. When deployed, with the reward unchanged, in a second warehouse whose bins are white with small coloured labels and whose lighting varies through the day, its accuracy collapses: it misroutes blue-labelled parcels in the morning and halts in shadow. Please write the essay question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme that separates a first-class answer from an upper-second-class answer by naming the specific moves each must contain. The question should force students to specify, for each diagnosis, exactly what relation it is a diagnosis of, what observation from the deployment would discriminate among the three, and whether all three can be true of the same behaviour at once. The marking scheme should reward treating the three as diagnoses at different loci—designer-to-reward, learned-policy-to-reward, and trained-policy-to-new-distribution—and should penalise conflating a misspecified reward with a misgeneralised policy, or equating being aligned in training with being aligned in deployment.
