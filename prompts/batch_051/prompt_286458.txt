Last night I got into a whole argument with a friend about whether the world is actually getting less violent, and now I'm half-convinced my database is counting the wrong thing. I do data entry and classification for a regional conflict-monitoring project. We log incidents: battles, bombings, riots, but also threats, mobilization, weapons stockpiling, armed roadblocks, that sort of thing. My friend kept quoting Hobbes at me — you know, Leviathan, chapter 13, the bit where he basically says war doesnt consist only in actual fighting but in the known disposition to fight, during all the time there is no assurance otherwise; so war is a state of affairs, not just a list of battles. The argument ended with him saying something like, "Every time you code a standoff as a non-event just because nobody got shot, you are literally erasing a war." Annoying, but it stuck with me.

So what I want from you: a usable decision procedure for classifying whether something in our database should count as an instance of violence or a conflict event, grounded in that Hobbesian distinction between actual fighting and the known disposition to fight. It has to be something a small team of analysts can run consistently, without all of them having read Hobbes. The stakes are medium-high: these numbers go into annual reports that funders and local politicians actually read, and a misclassification can make a district look peaceful when it isnt.

Give me a step-by-step procedure — questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with "not an event," where it stops with "event," and where it stops and hands the decision to a human because it is a values call rather than a definitional one. Be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure.

Then run the procedure on three cases:

1) A militia in one district has set up roadblocks and is extorting travellers. No one has been killed in eight months, but every local we interview says they are terrified to travel at night.

2) Two neighbouring villages have a long-running boundary dispute. Every few years there is a brawl that leaves a couple people injured. There is currently no fighting, but both sides have recently been seen drilling with machetes and stockpiling petrol.

3) A city saw a single mass shooting a decade ago. The shooter is dead, the group dissolved, nothing since. It is still in our database as an ongoing "conflict zone" because a regional analyst refuses to remove it.

Make sure the cases force the procedure to do real work — one of them should give an answer we probably will not like. And tell me what the procedure cannot settle, where I genuinely need someone to make a values call.
