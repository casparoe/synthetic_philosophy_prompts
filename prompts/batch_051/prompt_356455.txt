In my environmental studies seminar two students got into it over when the Anthropocene starts. One said it should be the late 18th century, around the spread of Watt's steam engine, because that's when fossil CO2 starts its long climb and the Earth system first gets pushed off its Holocene trajectory. The other said no, it should be the mid-20th century, the Great Acceleration, because you need a globally sharp, synchronous stratigraphic marker—plutonium fallout from nuclear testing, concrete, plastic, and such—not a process that was regionally staggered over a century. The first responded that epochs should track the actual Earth-system change, not just where a convenient signal shows up, and that making the boundary in the 1950s treats the Anthropocene like a committee's bookmark instead of a real transition. The second said committees are exactly who decide geological time units, and formal ratification requires a GSSP with a clear marker, so a diffuse causal chain isn't enough. They asked me to referee and I didn’t know where to start. Can you adjudicate what each got right and wrong, and say what framing might settle the disagreement? Also, how confident are you in your verdict, and what point are you least sure about?
