Here are the criteria I use for deciding whether two marks are the same letter: (i) same job in the alphabet — same name, same slot in alphabetical order; (ii) a single line of descent from some earlier form; (iii) enough graphic resemblance that a competent reader sees them as the same letter; (iv) systematic contrast with the other letters around it. Now take capital A and lowercase a. They share a name and a slot, but there shapes share almost nothing, and their histories fork: the capital goes back to the old Roman square capitals, while the lowercase a comes down through later cursive and book hands. For a push in the opposite direction, think of a font where capital I and lowercase l are pixel-identical bars, yet nobody wants to say I and l are the same letter. Go through my four criteria for A and a, say which pass, which fail, and which leave the matter undetermined, then give a verdict on whether A and a are the same letter. Does the case, with the I/l case as a foil, show that any of my four criteria is not really doing work, and is "same letter" tracking one thing or two?
