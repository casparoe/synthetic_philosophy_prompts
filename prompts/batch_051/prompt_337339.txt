A health economist I know insists QALYs are just a measurement: they record the rates at which people actually trade length of life against quality of life, with no moral content. I think that's only true is you've already accepted some pretty loaded assumptions — that quality-of-life weights are the same across people, that a year of life saved at 20 and at 80 counts the same, and that you can just add QALYs up across strangers. I'd like to know whether that disagreement is testable. Design the strongest test you can — say, variants of the time trade-off, or a design where people choose between helping an identified patient and an aggregate of unidentified ones — and tell me what result would favor each side. Then say what even the best-designed test could never settle about whether QALYs 'measure' value or just impose a scheme.
