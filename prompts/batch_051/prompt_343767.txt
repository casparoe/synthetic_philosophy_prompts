Consider the thesis that markets are a school of character: that repeated, voluntary exchange among strangers trains people in promise-keeping, toleration of difference, and a kind of impersonal fairness, and does more than merely select or incentivize people who already possess these traits. The opposing view is that markets corrupt character by training people to treat others instrumentally and by crowding out intrinsic honesty and benevolence, so that observed market trustworthiness is only prudence in disguise. Design the strongest feasible test you can of whether market participation changes character in one of these directions rather than merely revealing or rewarding preexisting dispositions. Specify the population or setting, the treatment or comparison, the outcome measures, and the pattern of results that would favor each interpretation. Conclude by saying what even a favorable result would fail to show, in particular whether the acquired dispositions are morally admirable as opposed to merely useful, and whether they would persist outside the market setting.
