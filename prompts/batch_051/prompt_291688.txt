My brother and I keep having this argument, and it got reheated at a family dinner last week. He says asking whether there would have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler is basically a non-question: the powder keg was already there, someone just had to light it. Versailles, the Depression, the weakness of the Republic, the already-present currents of antisemitism and authoritarianism — on his read that makes something like the Third Reich overdetermined, and Hitler was just the particular spark who happened to catch. I want to audit that analogy properly. What maps onto what in the spark/powder-keg picture, where does the mapping break, and what does it quietly import about how interchangable individual leaders are? Then the actual question: does the conclusion he uses it to support — that without Hitler things would have gone roughly the same way — survive the disanalogies, or was the analogy doing all the work? If it fails, is there a better analogy that gets the structure right?
