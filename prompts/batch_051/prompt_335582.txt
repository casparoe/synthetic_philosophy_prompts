A friend who works on AI alignment has been trying to convince me of something he calls the Lobian obstacle, and the argument he gave me feels airtight in a way that makes me suspicious of it, because its conclusion is more or less that self-trust is impossible, and that can't be the whole story. He's normally the careful one, so I can't just dismiss him. I'll reconstruct it as he laid it out. (1) To be rationally entitled to rely on the outputs of your own reasoning, you need to be able to establish, from your own resources, that your reasoning is reliable: for any conclusion C you derive, the step from "I have derived C" to "C" needs to be warranted. (2) Now think of a formal reasoner S that can do arithmetic and can reason about what S itself proves, the standard Godel-Lob setup. Lob's theorem says that for any sentence P, if S proves "if P is provable in S, then P," then S already proves P outright. (3) In particular, a consistent S cannot prove the instance where P is a contradiction, because "if a contradiction is provable in S, then a contradiction" is just the statement that S is consistent, and by Lob, proving that would make S prove a contradiction itself. So S cannot prove even that one safety check, let alone a general proof that everything S proves is true. (4) Therefore no reasoner of this kind can establish the reliability of it's own reasoning from inside that reasoning. (5) But any AI we would actually build, and arguably any human mind on a computational picture, is, or is well modeled as, a reasoner of exactly this kind. (6) Therefore such systems cannot be rationally entitled to trust their own conclusions, and on the computational picture, neither can we. Yet we do trust ourselves, and it seems right to; so one of the steps has to go. I'd most like to keep (1), because it sounds like a minimal definition of what rational self-trust is, but I can't find where the argument slips. Wich step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? And is this really a problem about formal proof, or have we smuggled in a confused picture of what it is to trust your own reasoning?
