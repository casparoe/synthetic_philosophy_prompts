I'm a retired population ecologist, and over the last few years I've gotten into the path dependence and historical contingency stuff you see in economics and philosophy of history — QWERTY keyboards, increasing returns, alternative stable states, that sort of thing. It overlaps in a satisfying way with the hysteresis and multiple-equilibria systems I used to model. I'm putting together a short piece for a reading group, and I'd like a consistency check on the cluster of things I find myself believing before I commit anything to writing.

Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) At bottom I'm a determinist about history: rerun the tape with exactly the same initial conditions and you get exactly the same outcome, human choices included.

(2) But I'm also convinced by the strong path-dependence stories: tiny early accidents compound into locked-in outcomes that can be worse than the alternatives they beat, and the lock-in can be extremely durable.

(3) At the same time, I think there are deep structural constraints — geography, climate, energy flows, disease pools — that make the broad pattern of history robust. Agriculture, cities, industrialization were going to happen somewhere in some recognizably similar form; particular people and particular events are mostly surface noise.

(4) I also trust selection: genuinely bad arrangements tend to get weeded out by competitive pressure over time, so I find a lot of the doom-and-gloom about locked-in inferior standards overstated. If something has survived for a long time, my default is that its good enough, not that we're stuck in a bad accident.

(5) And I accept ordinary counterfactuals: if the assassin had missed, the twentieth century would have gone differently in all sorts of detail, even though I doubt the big patterns would of been avoided.

The question is whether (1) through (5) actually hang together, or whether I'm quietly helping myself to an inconsistency. Go through the pairs that are genuinely in tension, rank them by how severe the tension is rather than saying everything is equally bad, flag any apparent tension that dissolves on closer inspection, and then tell me what the cheapest revision is and what it costs. Be specific about which commitment would have to go.

Oh, and please write the answer in American English.
