Me and a friend in reading group have been going back and forth about divine impassibility for weeks, and we decided to write an adversarial-collaboration statement, using Anselm of Canterbury's Proslogion chapter 8 as our shared text. We can't agree on what he's actually showing. The passage is the one where he ask how God can be both merciful and impassible, since being merciful seems to require being pained by another's suffering. His answer, roughly, is that God is called merciful because of what he does for the wretched, not because he feels their pain; the help flows to us, but the feeling doesn't arise in him. One of us thinks this is a genuine way to say God is merciful without being passible, and maybe the best a perfect being can offer; the other thinks it just redefines mercy into beneficence and loses the thing that matters about love.

Draft the document we could both sign. Don't referee between us, just make the residual disagreement exact and show whether any part is merely verbal.
