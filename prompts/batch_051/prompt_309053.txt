I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am preparing a feature on how people induce concepts from a handful of positive and negative examples, a problem made vivid by Mikhail Bongard's two-column puzzles. For the piece I would like you to work through a small, self-contained case assembled from a colleague's lab notebook. A researcher has sorted six simple panels into "in" and "out" using a single rule she has not disclosed. Each panel contains exactly two shapes, one filled (solid) and one outlined (hollow); each shape is either large or small and is a circle, a square, or a triangle.

Her labelled panels are:

- In: a large filled circle to the left of a small outlined circle.
- In: a large filled square to the left of a small outlined square.
- In: a large filled triangle to the left of a small outlined triangle.
- Out: a small filled circle to the right of a large outlined square.
- Out: a small filled square to the right of a large outlined triangle.
- Out: a small filled triangle to the right of a large outlined circle.

I would like to know how she would label the new panel: a large filled circle to the left of a small outlined square. Several different rules are consistent with her six labels; identify the most natural candidate rules, say what each predicts for the new panel, give your best guess about her label and the rule she is actually running, and then name the single most informative further panel you would ask her to label in order to settle whatever remains open.

You are a cognitive scientist who works on concept learning and Bongard-style problems. Think carefully: begin by brainstorming candidate rules, and analyse each one against all six labelled panels before committing to your predictions. Please write the answer in American English.

Please organise the answer into four named sections:

- Candidate rules (roughly 150 words): the distinct rules that fit all six labelled panels, and why each fits.
- Predictions for the new panel (roughly 120 words): what each candidate rule implies for the new panel, stated separately.
- Best guess and reasoning (roughly 120 words): your predicted label, the rule you regard as most likely, and your grounds for preferring it.
- Discriminating further case (roughly 100 words): the next panel you would request, with an explanation of what each possible label would confirm or eliminate.
