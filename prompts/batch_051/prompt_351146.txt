Two members of my department — a prioritarian and a telic egalitarian — have been arguing for nearly a term about the leveling-down objection, and they have agreed, with some scepticism, to write up their exchange as an adversarial collaboration rather than as duelling papers. Write the document they should have produced after a good-faith attempt at collaboration. The prioritarian holds that the value of a distribution of welfare is an increasing function of each person's welfare, weighted so that gains to the worse-off count for more; on that view equality itself has no non-instrumental value and matters only insofar as it tracks how badly off individuals are. The telic egalitarian holds that inequality is intrinsically bad, over and above whatever welfare effects it has, and therefore accepts that leveling down — lowering the better-off to the level of the worse-off while benefiting no one — can make an outcome better in at least one respect, though this egalitarian is a pluralist and need not say leveling down is better all things considered. They want a document both could sign without watering down their positions: the claims that survive joint scrutiny, including exactly what each accepts about the canonical leveling-down case; the precise statements of where they still differ, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; the cruxes such that settling them would move at least one of them; and, for each crux, a case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. The document should not be a verdict: both parties must be able to sign it, including the parts that record their residual disagreement.

I suspect one crux concerns whether the one-respect improvement the egalitarian sees in leveling down is a genuine reason or only a façon de parler, and another concerns whether distributive judgments should track individuals' absolute positions or their comparative positions. Format the response in four sections, in this order: Agreed claims (numbered, roughly 600 words); Statement of disagreement (roughly 400 words, beginning with a jointly acceptable prose framing of the divergence and then a two-column table giving each side's own formulation of the disagreement); Cruxes (roughly 300 words); and Agreed tests and advance predictions (a table, roughly 200 words).
