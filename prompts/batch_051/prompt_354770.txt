I keep noticing an asymmetry in myself and I can't tell if its principled or just laziness wearing a philosopher's hat. I treat "be frugal" as a genuine requirement on me: I eat leftovers, I mend clothes, I take the train instead of flying, and I'd feel guilty if I stopped. But "promote frugality" doesn't feel like a requirement at all. My brother-in-law flies for fun every other weekend, replaces his phone every year, and throws out half the food he buys; I grumble about it, but I don't feel like I'm failing at anything by not trying to change him. Yet if waste is bad, then waste is bad regardless of whose it is, and one serious sit-down with him would plausibly save more than a year of my leftovers and trains. So I've got "be frugal" as a duty and "make the world more frugal" as a hobby I can take or leave. Is there an actual principle that generates both, or is that just a bias toward duties that happen not to bother other people? Give me the strongest case for the asymmetry, test it against a nearby value where I'd probably refuse the same move, and tell me honestly whether I should keep it or give one side up.
