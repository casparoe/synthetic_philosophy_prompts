Habermas's ideal speech situation is usually treated as a regulative ideal for argument, not as a blueprint for an actual institution. Suppose a city tries to build one anyway: a standing citizens' assembly, randomly selected each quarter, that deliberates anonymously in writing on a moderated platform. Participants may not reveal their occupation, education, age, or group memberships; the interface suppresses threats, bargains, flattery, and any appeal to authority or majority opinion; everyone receives the same daily character allowance; and the assembly's recommendation becomes city policy only if it is unanimous, with questions falling back to the city council after two weeks. Analyze this as a mechanism rather than as a piece of political theory. What would it incentivize in deliberators, in the moderators, and in the council that created it, and where would it be gamed first? Most importantly, where does the design most sharply expose the gap between Habermas's ideal and any workable institution, and which of those failure modes could be fixed without abandoning the ideal?
