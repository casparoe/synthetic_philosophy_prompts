I am trying to pin down what 'confirms' should mean when the hypothesis under test is a universal generalisation. By 'confirms' I mean the ordinary scientific notion of lending at least a little support to a generalisation — raising its credibility above where it was before — rather than proving it; by 'disconfirms' I mean lowering its credibility. The word is used confidently, but its definition is contested, and I keep running into difficulty with the familiar raven paradox: the generalisation that all ravens are black is logically equivalent to the generalisation that all non-black things are non-ravens, which makes it seem that a white shoe supports a claim about ravens just as much as a black raven does. That feels wrong to many people, and I want a definition that says clearly what is going on.

My suspicion is that part of the trouble comes from 'confirms' sometimes tracking the content of the observation itself and sometimes tracking the procedure that produced it. I want a working definition that makes that interaction explicit rather than simply hedging.

Imagine a large, well-mixed warehouse of ordinary objects and birds, with no prior inventory. The warehouse has been divided in advance into two sections, one containing only black items and one containing only non-black items. There is also a cage known to contain only ravens, whose colours are unknown to the researchers before each draw. A 'random draw' means a researcher takes one item without looking and examines it fully afterwards. Throughout, the only generalisation under test is this: all ravens are black.

Here are the cases:

(a) A researcher makes a random draw from the raven cage and finds a black raven.
(b) A researcher makes a random draw from the raven cage and finds a white raven.
(c) A researcher makes a random draw from the whole warehouse and finds a white shoe.
(d) A researcher makes a random draw from the non-black section of the warehouse and finds a white shoe.
(e) A researcher makes a random draw from the black section of the warehouse and finds a raven.
(f) A researcher deliberately ignores every non-black item in the warehouse, searches only among the black items, and after some time finds a raven and records it as black.
(g) A researcher dreamt the previous night of a white shoe.
(h) At dusk a researcher records a distant bird as a black raven; the next day the bird is caught and identified as a crow, and I want this later identification treated as authoritative.

Some of these cases have a clear intuitive verdict and others I am genuinely pulled in different directions about; I want the definition to settle all of them rather than simply respect my hunches. What I want is not a label for a theory, but a definition that yields the right pattern of verdicts, run case by case and revised where it fails.
