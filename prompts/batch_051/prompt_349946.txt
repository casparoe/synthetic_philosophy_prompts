I'm working on a novel where the world has been running stratospheric aerosol injection for about twenty years, and I want a villanelle to open the chapter where the chief engineer finally gets that the program is a trap. Write it from that engineer's point of view. The two refrains should be, roughly, a line about the sulfur having to keep going up and a line about the heat coming back all at once if it stops — and let the villanelle's structure carry the idea: the refrains keep returning the way the problem keeps returning, no matter what the engineer tries to argue. Dont let it slide into a generic climate-change poem. The poem should show, not announce, the termination-shock logic that you cant turn the thing off, and the moral hazard should sit inside it too — spraying as a substitute for cutting emissions, the carbon debt accruing the whole time and coming due the moment the pumps quit.
