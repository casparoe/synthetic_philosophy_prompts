You are a professional philosopher of language with a background in classical Chinese philosophy, especially the School of Names. Before writing, take a moment to sketch privately each character's core semantic commitments and identify the strongest and weakest move available to each side. Then write a dialogue of exactly five numbered exchanges between two equally sophisticated philosophers debating Gongsun Long's claim that a white horse is not a horse. Call the characters the Separator and the Unifier. The Separator holds that the term 'white horse' involves the notion of whiteness together with the notion of horseness, whereas the term 'horse' involves only horseness, so 'a white horse is a horse' is not the trivial truth it is taken to be. The Unifier holds that this confuses what a term means with what it applies to: a white horse still falls under the kind horse, and the modifier 'white' merely restricts the extension of 'horse' without replacing it. Have the two respond directly to each other's points and press each other on whether 'is' here marks predication or identity, whether a modifier can change a sortal, and what the difference between seeking a horse and seeking a white horse actually shows. Number the exchanges 1 through 5; in each exchange each character may speak for at most two sentences; give no introduction or conclusion.
