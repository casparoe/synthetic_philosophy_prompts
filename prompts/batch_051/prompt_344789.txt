I have no academic background in philosophy; I am a self-taught software developer whose main practical obsession is digitising and cataloguing a large music collection. In doing that work I keep running into what I gather is called the ontology of musical works, though I have never read the academic work on it. The problem shows up as a set of surprisingly hard questions about identity: when is a live performance, a studio recording, a remaster, a cover version, or an edited set of takes the same piece of music? Some people seem to hold that a piece is an abstract thing standing behind all its performances and recordings; others seem to hold that only performances and recordings really exist; and even the first group disagree about how much a performance may differ, in instrumentation, tempo, or even wrong notes, before it stops being a performance of that piece. Studio recordings are especially strange because they may never have existed as one continuous performance at all.

I would like you to restate this debate in the vocabulary I actually think in most fluently: version control, source trees, commits, branches, forks, tags, builds, release artefacts and checksums. Treat a piece as something like a repository or source tree, a score or MIDI file as source, live performances as builds, studio recordings as release artefacts assembled from multiple takes, covers and arrangements as forks or ports, remasters as rebuilt artefacts, and then push the translation as far as it can honestly go. Please keep the translation honest: do not let the version-control vocabulary quietly settle a philosophical question by assuming, for instance, that there must be a canonical main branch or a well-defined upstream.

Then give me the residue report: which distinctions and disagreements translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in that vocabulary at all. For each item of residue, tell me whether it indicates that my vocabulary is missing something real or that part of the original dispute is less substantive than it looked.

Please separate what is genuinely settled among people who work on this from what is actively contested, and do not present a contested position as if it were the consensus. Please write in British English.
