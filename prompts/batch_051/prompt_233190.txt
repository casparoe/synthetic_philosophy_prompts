For a grad seminar I'm teaching on the epistemology of markets, I'd like a classification instrument—a decision tree or a short quiz with a scoring key, your choice—that sorts people by what they actually believe the no-trade theorems show about real markets, as apposed to which camp they'd tick on a survey. The starting tension is the Milgrom–Stokey result: rational expected-utility traders who share a common prior and have common knowledge of each other's rationality shouldn't trade on information differences alone, yet real markets have enormous volume. The branch questions should be concrete and answerable by someone who's never read the paper—for example, after a public news release, do they expect the jump in volume to mostly be rational agents extracting different things from the same signal; would they predict any trading in a market of fully rational bots that share their source code; do they treat the fact that people keep disagreeing as evidence someone is being irrational or as an ordinary fact about people. None of the questions should just ask which label the taker likes. I want the leaves to include at least these: (i) no-trade as benchmark (most real volume is hedging, liquidity, lifecycle rebalancing, not speculation); (ii) no-trade as evidence against common priors (disagreement is real, priors differ); (iii) no-trade as a reductio of the common-prior idealization itself; (iv) no-trade as evidence that much volume is noise, overconfidence, or bounded rationality; (v) no-trade as scope-limited (true, but actual markets aren't the pure information markets the theorem describes). Don't let it sprawl past six leaves or so. Flag any leaves the questions can't separate and say whether that's because they're the same view underneath or because the instrument is too coarse, and note unoccupied cells in the logical space with a quick diagnosis of why nobody is there (incoherent, unattractive, unexplored). I'd rather you commit: give me the finished tree or quiz plus scoring key, with your chosen questions and your reasons for each split, not four candidate designs for me to pick from.
