I'm a retired geologist and only started reading philosophy last year, so when two friends in our Kant group got into it about the formula of universal law, I couldn't referee them. The argument went roughly like this.

Dana said take the maxim: "when I'm running late on the motorway, I'll drive in the emergency lane to get ahead, and I'm fine with everyone else doing the same when they're late." She said it fails the universalizability test. Marcus said it actually passes, which shows the test is to weak to catch selfishness.

Dana: Universalize it and the emergency lane just becomes a fourth congested lane. The entire point of the move is to get ahead of people who are stuck, so in the universalized world the action cannot achieve its purpose. The maxim undermines itself; that's exactly Kant's test.

Marcus: That's not a contradiction in conception. I can conceive that world perfectly well; it's just a world with worse traffic. Contradiction in conception would require something like the practice being impossible, not the action ceasing to benefit me. And since I explicitly will that others do the same, there's no contradiction in the will either. If the formula condemns a maxim just because universalizing it makes everyone worse off, it's a consequentialist principle in disguise.

Dana: The clause "I'm fine with others doing the same" is idle. You're only fine with it because you assume it won't happen. Someone who genuinely willed the universalized world couldn't will it, because using the lane to get ahead is parasitic on most people not doing it. Same structure as the lying promise: promising only works because people generally keep promises.

Marcus: That just means the honest statement of my maxim is "use the lane only while others don't," and that one fails the test. But then the formula condemns putting inconsistent content into a maxim, not using the lane. The act itself, with the acceptance clause, passes. The verdict is hostage to how I phrase my own maxim.

Dana: But that admission is the point. The formula forced you to state what you were really willing: you wanted to make an exception of yourself. A test that exposes a hidden "while others don't" is doing its job, not failing.

Marcus: You've just conceded that the formula never ruled out lane-using as such; it only ruled out your reconstruction of my maxim. You've had to rewrite my maxim to get the result you wanted.

So, go through the exchange move by move and tell me who's right about what, where each is overreaching, and what the whole thing really turns on.
