In a discussion group I watch, two people got into it about whether psychopaths know that what they do is wrong. Aziz said they plainly do: they can state the rules, they hide their crimes, and they'll call the same act wrong when it's done to them — they just don't care. Elena said that's exactly what's at issue. A colorblind person can memorize that the light is red without knowing red, and maybe the psychopath uses the word "wrong" fluently while lacking the felt recognition that a victim matters. Aziz replied that she was confusing knowledge with motivation — you can know smoking is bad and still smoke, so caring isn't part of knowing. Elena said moral wrongness isn't a property like color: feeling the force of a reason is partly what knowing wrongness consists in, so being motivationally dead to it is a defect in the knowing, not a separate indifference. Aziz said by that standard a severely depressed person who can't feel anything doesn't know right from wrong, which is absurd. Elena said depression is a temporary occlusion of a present capacity, whereas psychopathy is the absence of the capacity itself. Can you adjudicate this move by move — who's right about which subclaims, what are both missing, and which empirical questions would need settling before a final verdict?
