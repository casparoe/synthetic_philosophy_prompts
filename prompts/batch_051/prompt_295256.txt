In preparing a graduate seminar on metaethics, I am organizing the section on evolutionary debunking arguments. I already count the following as the standard realist replies to the Darwinian dilemma, and I do not want restatements of them: (1) the truth-tracking reply — that moral judgments conferred fitness precisely because they approximated independent moral facts; (2) the third-factor or common-cause reply — that a single underlying state of affairs made certain moral judgments both adaptive and true; (3) the wide-target reply — that the selective explanation reaches only a broad evaluative capacity or a few proto-moral dispositions, not the specific content of our moral judgments, so the debunker has overstated what evolution explains; (4) the proves-too-much reply — that parallel reasoning would equally debunk perception or scientific inference; (5) the underdetermination reply — that any adaptive story can be tailored to whatever moral content we happen to hold, so the debunker has not genuinely explained the target; and (6) the origins-are-irrelevant reply — that tracing a belief's causal origin does not by itself defeat its justification or show it false. Give me one further reply that is structurally different from all six — not a restatement, combination, special case, or relabeling. It can be something you construct yourself; indeed I prefer that to another familiar move I have overlooked, but if you believe I have missed a standard reply, say so briefly and then go beyond it. End by saying honestly whether the reply succeeds and which commitment of the debunking argument it puts under the most pressure.
