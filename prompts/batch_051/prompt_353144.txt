I am a sixth-form pupil preparing a handout for my school philosophy society on cultural relativism. We have been reading Montaigne's essay 'Of Cannibals', and I would like the handout to take the form of a decision tree rather than a discursive essay. The passage that interests me is this, from Montaigne's 'Of Cannibals' (in the Cotton translation):

"I am afraid our eyes are bigger than our bellies, and that we have more curiosity than capacity; for we grasp at all, but catch nothing but wind."

The tree should sort someone into a position on cultural relativism by asking them what they make of that passage and of two or three concrete practices, including the Brazilian cannibalism Montaigne discusses. I do not want it to sort people by labels; the questions must be answerable without already knowing the names of the positions. Please include as leaves at least these positions: descriptive relativism, normative relativism, universalism, and ethnocentrism. If the tree's branching logic produces unoccupied leaves, say which combinations are empty and briefly diagnose whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Each branch should be labelled by the claim that splits it, and each leaf should carry the position's name and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Seven or eight nodes is about right for a sixth-form handout. Please write in British English. End with a one-sentence summary of the decision tree as the final line, beginning 'Summary:'.
