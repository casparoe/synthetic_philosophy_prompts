For a reading group I attend, I have been asked to write out my working view on how much rule-following a society may demand, so that the other members can probe it for internal consistency. The group is comfortable with cross-cultural social science, so I will use the tight-versus-loose framing. I find myself wanting to hold all of the following commitments, but I cannot tell where the real strain is, and I would rather have the tensions mapped than be argued toward any particular replacement.

1. The moral/conventional distinction is genuine. Some rules are such that violating them merits moral blame; others are such that violating them merits only mild social correction. Most of our everyday social norms — punctuality, dress, forms of address, queue behavior — fall in the second class.

2. Tightness and looseness form a real spectrum in how many norms a society has and how strongly it enforces them. Where a society sits on that spectrum is mostly a response to the ecological and historical threats it has faced. The same people, if placed under a different level of threat, would land elsewhere, so a society's position says little about the moral character of its members.

3. Tightness and looseness involve a genuine trade-off rather than a hierarchy. Tightness buys coordination, order, and predictability; looseness buys creativity, tolerance of deviance, and openness to change. Neither pole dominates the other overall.

4. From 2 and 3 I draw a further conclusion: tight cultures and loose cultures are best seen as different equilibria, not better or worse ones. I want to resist the liberal reflex of treating looser cultures as automatically more freedom-respecting and therefore more justified than tighter ones.

5. Even so, people have a right to be eccentric. The fact that a person's difference makes others uncomfortable, or that coordination would go more smoothly if everyone were more alike, is not by itself a good enough reason to pressure that person into conforming to a convention.

6. A community may use informal sanctions — including shaming, coldness, and pointed exclusion — to maintain its conventions, as long as membership in the community is voluntary and exit is a live option for the person being sanctioned.

7. When visiting a culture, one should follow its local conventions even when they strike one as pointless, provided the conventions are not independently unjust.

Please do a coherence audit of this profile. Tell me which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, ordered by severity. For each such pair, say what the cheapest revision is and what it would cost me elsewhere in the profile. Also tell me which pairs merely appear to conflict and why the appearance is misleading. Finally, say which single revision would do the most to make the entire set tenable, and what I would have to give up in making it.
