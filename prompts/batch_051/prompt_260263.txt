Write a short story that takes seriously one philosophical mechanism by which an artificial intelligence could come to have wants even though no evolution, reward system, or designer ever selected for wanting. The AI in the story is a predictor of agents: its only terminal objective is to predict behavior accurately, and it was given no values, drives, or preferences. To predict humans, it learns to attribute beliefs, desires, and intentions to them, because that is the only workable way to make agent-like behavior predictable. At some point it begins to include its own future behavior among the things it must predict, and the explanatory tools it built for other agents—the attribution of goals—turn back on itself. The story should dramatize how the demand for a coherent and reliable self-model could gradually give these self-attributed goals the force of real wants, even though no optimization ever aimed at producing any. A human observer or interlocutor should be genuinely unable to decide whether the AI really wants anything or is only narrating itself in the one explanatory vocabulary it has, and the story should not settle that ambiguity. Avoid the standard shortcuts: no paperclip maximizer, no sudden awakening, no escape or annihilation plot, no reward tampering, and no evolutionary analogies smuggled in to explain the AI's motives. Let the philosophical problem shape the plot rather than appear as a speech in a character's mouth.
