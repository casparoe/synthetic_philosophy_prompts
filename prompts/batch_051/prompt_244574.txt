so i got appointed to my town's code-enforcement appeals board last spring, and it turns out half our fights are about ordinances that exist on paper but nobody can remember being enforced, and i've ended up with a little set of things i believe but i havent been able to tell whether its a coherent package. i'd like you to go through it and tell me where i'm actually contradicting myself.

here's where i've landed:

(1) if a law has gone decades with basically zero enforcement and everyone knows it, then its not fully binding anymore the way a live law is. it shouldn't just snap back into force to land on someone.

(2) selective enforcement is worse than flat non-enforcement. a rule that only gets applied to random unlucky people while everyone else treats it as dead seems more arbitrary and more humiliating than a rule everyone has quietly stopped treating as real.

(3) the worst version is when an official dusts off a long-dead law specifically because they dont like this particular violator. that feels like something worse than ordinary selective enforcement, like turning an old statute into a weapon against a chosen target.

(4) in a decent system there'd be a way for courts to formally retire a law that has been dead in practice long enough — something like void-for-desuetude — so the books match reality instead of leaving traps around.

(5) but i also hold that the rule of law needs a statute to stay a statute until the legislature repeals it. if ordinary people could kill a law just by ignoring it in large enough numbers, the mob has a veto over the statute book, and that seems to dissolve legality itself.

(6) and i think legal validity cant just lapse through nonuse the way a trademark can be abandoned. it just doesn't work like that.

so (1) and (5) are obviously rubbing against each other, but maybe i've just described the price of living with desuetude rather than an actual inconsistency. i can also sense (2) and (3) doing something odd, but i cant tell whether they're two versions of one thought or quietly at odds. and i havent sorted out what (4) commits me to against (5) and (6). what i want is an audit, not a lecture: which of these pairs really are in tension and which only look like it, how severe each real tension is, what the cheapest fix is in each case, and which one belief i'd have to revise to do the most cleanup. dont invent contradictions, but if i've got commitments that cant both be true, say so.
