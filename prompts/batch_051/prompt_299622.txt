The employee handbook at my company contains two provisions that keep striking me as contrary. Section 9.4 (Corporate Responsibility) says: "The company as a body accepts responsibility for the aggregate outcomes of its systems and processes, including outcomes that no single employee intended or could have prevented alone."

The immediate context is an internal investigation on my own team. Over three years, we built and maintained a pricing model that gradually learned to charge customers in certain postal codes consistently more for the same service. The biased pricing was never specified by anyone; each of several dozen changes passed code review and looked defensible in isolation, and a data-pipeline change that supplied the relevant proxy feature had been made earlier for an unrelated reason. No individual engineer intended the discriminatory result, and no single engineer could have prevented it alone, because no one saw the entire dataset across the whole period.

Read Section 2.1 and Section 9.4 alongside each other in this case. Do they actually conflict?
