I keep noticing a structural resemblance between two distinctions that are usually discussed in different rooms, and I want to know whether it is deep or only verbal. In the deliberately narrow definition of the AI alignment problem, "intent alignment" means that a system is robustly trying to do what its designers intended it to do—satisfying the intended meaning of a specification, not merely its literal form—while "value alignment" means that the system is pursuing the values that those designers, or humans generally, would reflectively endorse. The two come apart when a designer's specification encodes a mistake, a short-term instruction, or an objective that fails to capture what the designer ultimately cares about. A similar-looking distinction shows up in fiduciary and medical decision-making: an agent may be assessed by whether it followed the principal's expressed instructions, or by whether it acted in the principal's best interests, and these part ways when a principal's stated wishes are ill-formed, underinformed, or in tension with that principal's own settled values.
