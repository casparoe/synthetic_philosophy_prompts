I've been writing up how I actually think philosophical method works, and I'm now suspicious that the list is too tidy. Before I show it to anyone, I want a coherence audit. Here are the commitments I keep finding myself endorsing:

1. Wide reflective equilibrium is the right method for moral and political philosophy: you go back and forth between considered judgments, principles, and background theories (from psychology, sociology, economics, whatever seems relevant) until the whole package is as coherent as you can get it. And reaching that state can genuinely justify a belief, not just make it hang together with the rest of my head.

2. There are no fixed points. Every considered judgment is in principle revisable, even the ones I find nearly impossible to imagine giving up, like the judgment that pain is bad or that it's wrong to punish a person known to be innocent. In practice I treat those as very expensive to move, but not unrevisable.

3. The method needs an input filter that gets specified before you start: a judgment only counts as "considered" if it was formed calmly, with the relevant information, without obvious self-interest, and away from known distorting influences like disgust, panic, or framing effects. Otherwise equilibrium is just laundering whatever biases you walked in with.

4. That filter is itself up for grabs inside the equilibrium. The standards for what counts as a considered judgment are normative, and they can be revised by the same back and forth; otherwise I'd be smuggling in a foundation through the back door.

5. The method should be at least moderately convergent across people. If two sincere, well-informed inquirers end up in persistently different equilibria, I treat that as evidence that the method (or its application) has gone wrong, not as two different but equally fine endpoints.

6. There is no completely judgment-independent check available to the method. Even the empirical background theories enter through observation and interpretation, which are themselves shot through with judgment, so I'm not leaning on "the facts" as a neutral referee.

Does this hang together, or are some of these pulling against each other? I genuinely can't tell where the problem is, which is why I want the audit. Go through them, tell me which pairs are in real tension and which only look like they are, rank the real ones by how severe they are, and for each say what would have to give to fix it and roughly what that revision would cost me.
