I was chewing on something from a logic seminar last week and I still can't settle it, so I'm hoping you'll work through it with me. It's a variation on Lewis Carroll's old Achilles-and-the-tortoise dialogue. In the original, the tortoise is happy to accept that things equal to the same are equal to each other, and that the two sides of a given triangle are equal to the same thing, but it won't accept that the two sides are equal to each other. When Achilles says the move is forced, the tortoise says fine, it would accept the conclusion if it were also written down that those two premises, being true, force the conclusion. Achilles writes that down. The tortoise now has all three statements and still doesn't accept the conclusion; it wants a further statement saying the three statements together force the conclusion. And so on, endlessly.

Here's the variation I argued about afterward: suppose instead of one obstinate tortoise, you have an infinite line of perfectly rational, perfectly cooperative tortoises. Tortoise 1 is given the two original premises and says it won't accept the conclusion until it's handed the first connecting principle. Once that's supplied, Tortoise 1 passes the notebook to Tortoise 2, explaining that it still won't accept the conclusion unless it also gets the next connecting principle, the one saying the three claims now in the notebook together force the conclusion. Tortoise 2 accepts everything in the notebook so far, asks for exactly one more connecting principle, and passes it along. Each tortoise does the same: accept everything its been given, demand one further conditional, pass the notebook down the line. Nobody ever actually accepts the conclusion.

Does the standard diagnosis of the original puzzle — that the regress is an artifact of treating the rule of inference as if it were just another premise — carry over to this many-tortoise setup, or does spreading the demand across infinitely many reasoners change whether the regress is vicious? Is there any real sense in which the chain is making progress, or does the conclusion still never get reached, and does the fact that no individual tortoise is being as unreasonable as the original tortoise make a philosophical difference?
