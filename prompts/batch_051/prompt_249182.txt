ok so in bio we had to memorize the seven characteristics of life (metabolism, reproduction, homeostasis, growth, response, cells, all that), and then right after we finish, the teacher says viruses are not technically alive because they dont have their own metabolism and can't reproduce on their own. everyone just nodded and wrote it down like its settled. But then someone asks about a mule, which also can't reproduce, and the teacher said well thats different and moved on, and I have not recovered. Me and my friend kept adding cases after class: fire metabolizes-ish and grows and reproduces, crystals grow and copy their structure, a frozen tardigrade is basically doing nothing until it thaws, stars literally have so-called life cycles. It started to seem like the checklist was reverse-engineered from the obvious cases and then patched up every time a weird one appears.

for my project, write me a satirical piece about this, specifically a deadpan fake government memo from the Department of Vital Registration (or Office of Biological Status, whichever sounds funnier) that processes applications from borderline entities trying to get themselves classified as alive. A virus, fire, a mule, a prion, maybe a crystal each submit evidence under the usual seven criteria and get rejected for exactly the criteria they fail, except the office keeps inventing ad hoc exemptions so the mule doesn't get deported, so to speak. I want it around 600 words, fully in a flat bureaucratic voice with subparagraphs and footnotes.

The target should be the idea that there's a single checklist that cleanly divides living from nonliving, not that biologists are dumb, so don't let it tip into "scientists are silly." And the irony should stay legible without the memo ever saying the whole system is arbitrary — the reader should feel it through the decisions and the increasingly strained footnotes.
