I'm in a study group on democratic theory and we've been going around in circles on a made-up scenario, so I'd like someone to actually work through it instead of giving us the standard talking points. Suppose a country's constitution contains this clause:

"Every citizen who has attained the age of eighteen years shall be entitled to one vote in any election for public office. All ballots shall be of equal weight, and no law shall discriminate among voters in the exercise of the franchise on any ground whatsoever."

Now a state in that country enacts the Competence-Maximizing Suffrage Act. No one is turned away from the polls: every registered adult still casts a ballot. But before voting, each person may take a 25-question quiz on the offices being contested and the candidates' actual platforms. Your quiz score is converted into a ballot weight on a continuous scale from 0.2 to 1.8; someone who skips the quiz gets a weight of 0.4. The weights are not assigned by a legislator writing down which traits matter. Instead, a regression model was trained on historical voting and outcome data to choose the mapping from quiz answers to weights that maximizes the expected "competence" of the electorate's aggregate choices. The act's defenders say this cannot be what the clause prohibits: everyone still votes, no group is named, and no category like income or schooling is used — only answers to neutral factual questions, weighted by an algorithm. Its critics say it makes a nonsense of one person one vote.

Does the clause, read literally, bar the scheme? Does it bar the scheme read in light of its purpose, where the purpose is something like equal political standing rather than merely equal ballot-paper? What would the clause's drafters most likely have said if shown the scheme, and would they have drawn the line at the algorithm part or at any weighting at all? What would a reasonable reader today take "no law shall discriminate" to mean when the discriminator is a fitted model rather than a list of forbidden grounds? I dont want a canned "it depends" — tell me which interpretive approach should win here and why, and then say which argument or consideration would most likely change your mind, or where your reasoning is shakiest.
