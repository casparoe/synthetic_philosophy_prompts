I want a working definition of a repugnant exchange — a market transaction that is objectionable in kind, not merely because it has bad downstream effects or because its terms are unfair in the ordinary way. The definition should classify the following cases correctly:

- selling a kidney to a stranger;
- buying a vote;
- a hotel tripling its rates the night of an evacuation;
- a ride-share app's surge pricing during the same storm;
- selling blood plasma for cash;
- paying a surrogate to carry a pregnancy;
- paying someone to stand in line for you at a passport office;
- reselling a concert ticket above face value outside the venue;
- a town selling the naming rights to its public park;
- paying a child for each book she reads.

I am fairly confident that the kidney sale, the vote purchase, and the hotel's rate hike are repugnant in kind, and that the plasma sale is not. I also cannot shake the sense that the hotel's rate hike and the surge price should come apart, even though both are just demand-sensitive prices; if your definition says they do not come apart, tell me why I am wrong. I have no settled view on the middle cases.

I would like the definition to do four things. First, separate objections that target the exchange itself from objections that target the background conditions in which it occurs — desperation, inequality, asymmetric information — and say which of the above cases move between those two categories. Second, state what feature, if any, unites the repugnant ones: that the good should not be for sale at all, that the exchange corrupts the meaning of the good or the relationship between the parties, that it degrades buyer or seller, or something else. Third, explain how two structurally identical exchanges can come apart, as the hotel and the ride-share app are supposed to. Fourth, flag the classification you are least sure about, and say how confident you are in the definition overall and in each of your verdicts.
