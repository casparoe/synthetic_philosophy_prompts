Write a short story of 1,200–1,800 words that functions as a philosophical thought experiment about whether self-deception is really possible. The story is set around a near-future device, the attestation screen, which displays a person's implicit commitments — the beliefs that best predict their choices, attention, and emotional responses — regardless of what the person says they believe. Center the story on one protagonist whose displayed commitments sharply contradict their explicit avowals, and trace the psychological and social consequences of living with that contradiction.

Do not resolve the question by making the protagonist a conscious liar, and do not stipulate that the device is infallible. Instead, dramatize at least three rival interpretations of the protagonist: (i) the protagonist genuinely holds two conflicting beliefs, one explicit and one implicit; (ii) the protagonist has managed not to form the troubling belief, and what the screen displays is only a pattern of motivated avoidance and selective attention; and (iii) the protagonist's avowals are a social performance sustained for an audience that includes the protagonist, without the corresponding belief. The ending may remain open, but each of the three interpretations should be visibly motivated by concrete details in the story, not merely announced in narration.
