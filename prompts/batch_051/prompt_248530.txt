For a workshop on temporal aspects of consciousness, I have been asked to circulate a short statement of my current views on the color phi phenomenon, and before sending it out I would like a consistency audit of the statement. I have been working close to this material long enough that I suspect I can no longer reliably see where my commitments pull against one another.

The setting, as I understand it. In the standard color phi display, a brief red dot is flashed at one location and then, after a short interval, a brief green dot is flashed at a separated location. Normal observers report not two successive dots but a single dot in apparent motion that changes from red to green roughly halfway along its path. Two standard descriptions of what is going on are usually contrasted. On the Orwellian description, the subject first has some other conscious experience, perhaps of a moving red dot, and the later arrival of the green flash causes the memory of that experience to be revised; the report of a mid-path color change is then a report of a rewritten record rather than of the original experience. On the Stalinesque description, conscious experience is held back until after the green flash has arrived, and the whole trajectory is then presented at once, with the color change inserted at the appropriate place; the subject never has a conscious experience that lacks the mid-path change.

My commitments, for purposes of the audit, are these.

1. I take the color phi report at face value: the subject genuinely has a single continuous experience of motion, and the experienced moving dot changes from red to green around the midpoint of its apparent path.
2. I regard the Orwellian description as false. The mid-path color change is present in the experience itself; it is not a later edit to the memory of an experience that originally lacked it.
3. I also regard the Stalinesque description as false. The visual system does not hold the experience back until the green flash and then deliver a back-filled stream; the subject's consciousness of the trajectory is not delayed in that way.
4. I hold that, for perceptual contents at least, the time at which a content is conscious coincides with the time the content presents itself as occupying. If the experience presents the color change as happening at the midpoint between the two flashes, then the conscious experience of that change occurs at that midpoint, not only later, after the second flash.
5. I hold that the content of a conscious episode at a time t is fixed by neural activity up to t; there is no retrocausal influence by which a later stimulus determines the content of an episode that is already conscious.
6. I also take it as a plain empirical constraint that the green flash causally contributes to the experienced color at the midpoint. Without the second flash, or with the second flash in a different color, the mid-path color would not be green.

I ask for the following. Identify which of these six commitments actually conflict, which merely appear to conflict and dissolve on reflection, and which are jointly fine. For each genuine tension, rank it by severity, say exactly which commitments are involved, and state the least costly revision that would remove it, including what that revision would cost me elsewhere. Then tell me which single revision would remove the largest share of the problem. I suspect the trouble lies somewhere among these claims, but I do not want to bias the diagnosis.
