I'm a journalist working on a long piece about the ethics of sex and the way courts have started treating deception — stealthing, lying about being married, lying about fertility — as something that can cancel consent. I interviewd a philosopher who works on this and she gave me an argument which she says shows the standard story about deception and consent is unstable. I can't find the hole in it, and I'd like help working out which step to reject and what that rejection costs.

Here's the argument as I understood it:

(1) You consent only to the act as you understand it. If you think you're agreeing to sex with someone who is unmarried, and they're not, then what you consented to isn't what actually happened.

(2) So when your understanding of the act diverges from the act as it actualy is, your consent doesn't cover the act that happens.

(3) Deception matters because it's a way of making your understanding diverge from the actual act. That's what makes consent invalid in the stealthing or lying-about-being-married cases.

(4) But your understanding can diverge from the actual act even without any deception. Suppose you go home with someone and have sex believing they're unmarried; they are married, but they never said a word about it either way, and you'd never have slept with them if you'd known. By steps (1) and (2), your consent doesn't cover the sex that actually happened either.

(5) And since nobody's understanding is ever perfectly accurate — everyone has at least one false belief about the person they're going to bed with — almost no sexual consent would be valid.

(6) But that's absurd: we don't want to say nearly every sexual encounter is non-consensual, and we especially don't want to say consent can be invalidated by mere luck about facts nobody knew.

So something in (1)–(5) has to give. I'd rather not give up the idea that deception about something material can invalidate consent, but I can't see how to keep that while avoiding the conclusion that every encounter is non-consensual. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
