As an exercise in the ethics of persuasive technology, write a job advertisement for the position of Senior Persuasive Designer at a fictional, late-stage startup whose flagship product is a digital wellbeing app that promises to restore its users' attention. Adopt the earnest, mission-driven voice of a startup recruiting page; keep the tone deadpan and the advert roughly 650 words. The advert must never say in so many words that the app captures attention rather than freeing it, but a careful reader should leave able to state the criticism. The humor should come from the actual machinery of persuasive design and the attention economy, not from exaggeration alone: variable-ratio notification schedules described as delightful surprises, streaks and the guilt of breaking them, friction added to offboarding while removed from engagement, confirm-shaming decline buttons, defaults and dark patterns, A/B tests of engagement loops, and the slide from a time-well-spent metric back into plain time-on-device. Make the piece fair enough that a designer working in the field would recognize each practice, and legible enough that someone with no background still understands what the company is really hiring for. Structure the advert as a job posting with an About the mission paragraph, a Responsibilities list, a Requirements list, and a closing line about compensation that itself performs the problem (unlimited vacation, always-on notifications, or equity pegged to engagement metrics). End with a final line beginning Summary: that states in one plain sentence what the advertisement satirizes.
