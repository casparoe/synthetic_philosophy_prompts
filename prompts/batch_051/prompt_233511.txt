For a philosophy of art seminar on creativity, write a decision tree that sorts views on whether there is a real difference between creative variation and merely derivative copying. Use Douglas Hofstadter's knob-twiddling picture as the framing device: much of what is called creativity can be described as altering the parameters of an existing style or pattern—tempo, line, palette, plot structure, perspective, word order, and so on—and the dispute is whether creativity is anything over and above a canny or surprising choice of parameter settings. The tree should not be an exercise in Hofstadter exegesis; the picture is only the starting point, and anyone should be able to follow the tree without prior familiarity with his work.

Build the tree from the logical space of answers to the following four questions, posed through the cases below rather than as abstract labels. No node should ask respondents to identify with a labelled position; every node should be answerable from the cases by someone who has never heard of the positions.

1. Is the difference between a creative work and a routine genre piece one of kind or only of degree within a parameter space?
2. Can an unintending process—a generative model, a happy accident, a child's scribble—yield a creative work when the result is valued?
3. Does creative status depend solely on the work's internal features, or also on facts about its making, such as which parameters the maker deliberately altered and why?
4. Is uptake by an audience, a field, or a later tradition ever constitutive of creativity, or is it merely evidence for it?

Use these cases as the sorting material, adding at most two of your own: (a) an improvising musician varies a standard's phrasing, harmony, and tempo in ways a competent listener finds distinctive; (b) a student pastiches an author so exactly that no parameter leaves the author's observed range; (c) a generative model, trained on a corpus, produces a sonnet and a human selects one output from many; (d) a painter attempting to copy a Vermeer produces, through a chemical accident, an effect critics later call a new technique; (e) a composer mechanically permutes the parameters of a Bach chorale and keeps the setting that sounds best.

The leaves should be located in this logical space, not imported as labels first; they should include at least a strong knob-space monism, a two-tier view that treats some parameter changes as deeper than others, a process-intentionalist view, and a receptionist view. Keep the tree to no more than seven leaves. Where the branching implies combinations that no leaf occupies, note whether the gap is there because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, indicating which further case would separate them.

Throughout, separate what is settled—for instance, that many creative acts can be partially described as parameter variation—from what is genuinely contested, such as whether that description captures creativity itself, and do not present a contested camp as the consensus. Write in British English.
