I'm applying for a one-person, six-month "ideas lab" fellowship and need a one-page research proposal. My half-formed idea is that outside-view forecasting, at least for questions like whether the American or Chinese state survives to 2100, has a hole in the middle: everyone says to pick a reference class of similar past states and use its base rate, but almost nobody says how to pick the class, and the conclusion changes completely depending on the choice. The claim that all large premodern empires eventually declined or transformed puts the base rate of collapse near one; nuclear-armed states with modern institutions, mass education, and global food systems has so few members you basically have no base rate; and in between there are a dozen plausible classes, each with its own number. My suspicion is that people choose the class, consciously or not, to launder the inside-view forecast they started with, which would make the outside view less a corrective than a rhetorical amplifier. I want the proposal to frame the open question as whether there is a non-arbitrary procedure for selecting and weighting reference classes when the target system is structurally novel, and to sketch a method for testing candidate procedures against a coded set of historical state-level outcomes over the last two hundred years (including explicitly how collapse gets coded, since that may be doing a lot of hidden work). It should explain why the existing inside-view-vs-outside-view debate doesn't settle this, what I'd actually do month by month, what a useful result would look like, and what counts as the project failing. Keep the scope honest for one person and six months. No citations; I'll add them after the structure is in place.
