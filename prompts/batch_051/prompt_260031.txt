I am putting together a teaching module on the butterfly passage in the Zhuangzi, and I just had a long argument with a friend who insists it should be read as an early piece of global dream skepticism. He claims that, since Zhuangzi cannot tell whether he is Zhou dreaming he is a butterfly or a butterfly dreaming it is Zhou, the passage shows that no subjective mark distinguishes waking from dreaming, and therefore ordinary perceptual confidence is unjustified. I resisted this, but I want to test my resistance properly.

Please act as an expert in early Chinese philosophy and comparative epistemology. I will give you six candidate readings of the passage. Before you rank them, brainstorm the strongest objection to each one. Then evaluate them as exegesis of the passage and as philosophical positions: which readings capture what the passage is doing, and which are imposed on it.

The six readings:
A. Proto-Cartesian skepticism: the passage denies that there is any introspectively available criterion of waking, so all sense-based belief is undermined.
B. Transformation of perspectives: the passage dissolves the fixed boundary between Zhou and butterfly, self and other, showing that both are temporary transformations of the same larger process.
C. Conventional naming: the exchange between Zhou and butterfly is meant to unsettle the distinction, but the point is that names and categories are provisional, not that we lack all knowledge.
D. Therapeutic awakening: the passage is not an epistemological proof but a prompt to stop trying to settle the question and instead inhabit the transformation without anxiety.
E. Introspective warrant: the passage actually shows that waking is known from within; the uncertainty arises only when Zhou tries to view himself from outside the dream.
F. Equipollence and suspension: the passage presents two opposed appearances of equal strength, inviting suspension of judgment rather than either skepticism or metaphysical flux.

Please return a JSON object with these fields:
- "preliminary_objections": an object whose keys are the reading labels and whose values are one-sentence strongest objections to that reading.
- "rankings": an array of objects, each with fields "label", "score" (0–10), "tier" (1–4, where 1 is most defensible), and "justification" (one sentence).
- "decisive_contrast": a string naming the contrast that does most to separate the best readings from the worst.
- "bottom_line": a string of two to three sentences saying how I should describe the passage to my students.

Do not include citations or bibliography; I want the reasoning, not references.
