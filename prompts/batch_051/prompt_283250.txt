I keep running into a situation I don't have a word for. A few months back we shipped a change that overcharged users for weeks. The postmortem kept saying the organization was negligent, but when you traced the actual decisions, no single person looked negligent: the PM wrote a vague acceptance criterion, the engineer implemented exactly what the ticket and its tests specified, the reviewer approved it because it matched the ticket, and support triaged complaints with a rubric that happened to hide the pattern. Each local move was defensible in isolation; the harm only showed up in how the moves composed. Still, blaming the company as a thing didnt feel totally crazy. Is there an established name for treating a company itself as blameworthy, as opposed to just holding it financially liable or hunting for an individual to fire? More than a label, I want the carve: how is that different from scapegoating a group because pinning it on one person is inconvenient, and what are the edge cases? For example, would a company be more or less blameworthy if its process was deliberately designed to fragment knowledge so no one sees the whole thing, and where is the line between a real phenomenon and a legal fiction?
