I am a PhD student in history and philosophy of science, working on the fate of teleological explanation after the seventeenth century. I want to work through a counterfactual with you, and I want a committed, argued prediction rather than a survey of possibilities.

Set-up. In Physics II.8, Aristotle argues by analogy between art and nature. If the craft of shipbuilding were present in the timber itself, he suggests, it would act in the way nature does; and if art could be present in things that are by nature, they would act as they do by nature. The analogy is meant to show that final causes operate in natural processes without deliberation, not only in human artifacts. But the analogy cuts both ways, and I want to know how much load it can bear under a restricted teleology.

Counterfactual. Imagine that around 1650 a compromise position wins. Inanimate nature is fully mechanized: heat, projectile motion, the movement of the heavens, and chemical processes are explained by matter in motion alone, with no final causes. But in the domain of living things, final causes are retained as indispensable: the development of the embryo, the maintenance of the organism, the cooperation of organs, and the adaptation of structure to function all require end-directed explanation. This is not a mere heuristic; it is accepted that some natural bodies act for ends, because living bodies are organized in a way no machine built from outside can be.

Predict how the next two centuries of natural philosophy and the emerging life sciences would differ from what actually happened. I want the chain of reasoning visible. In particular:

(1) Is the compromise stable, or does teleology in biology tend to spill back into inanimate nature through arguments about the generation of life from matter, the unity of nature, or the origin of life?

(2) Does the design argument in natural theology become stronger, because final causes are now found only in life and thus point more sharply to a designer, or weaker, because the inanimate world is no longer available as evidence?

(3) How do the explanatory practices of comparative anatomy, physiology, and embryology change when practitioners can appeal to ends openly rather than hiding them in mechanical metaphors?

(4) Would anything like natural selection arise earlier, later, or in a different form, if the question to which it is an answer—how can organs appear to be for ends without a designer—is posed by this compromise?

Please begin by identifying the three or four mechanisms you think would do the most work in the divergence, then give your best definite prediction of the net outcome with explicit confidence at each step. Where you are guessing, say so and tell me what assumption would most likely make your prediction wrong.
