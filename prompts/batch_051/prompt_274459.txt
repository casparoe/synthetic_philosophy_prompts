I'm working on a piece about how streaming changed what we count as "a song," and I keep tripping over a distinction — or maybe its three distinctions — that I can't name. People say a cover is "the same song" in one breath and "a different track" in the next, and neither feels wrong. A remaster gets called "the same recording," even though the actual audio has been altered in thousands of tiny ways. A live album is somehow both "that song" and "that performance," and then the CD of it is a third thing, and a file ripped from the CD a fourth. It gets stranger. A track assembled in software from samples and MIDI may never have been performed all the way through by anyone, but we still call it "a recording of a song." And a cover that keeps the melody and lyrics but rewrites the arrangement entirely: is that the same song, a new song, or something in between that we don't have a word for? I'm not just after a label — I want to know the joints here, how many distinct things I'm actually pointing at, whether there is a settled way of carving this up (in philosophy, I'd guess?), and where that carving starts to fail or get controversial. If the standard framework has a name, I'd love that too, but the name matters less to me then knowing exactly what it does and doesn't cover.
