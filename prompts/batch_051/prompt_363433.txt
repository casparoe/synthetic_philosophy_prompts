we spent a whole seminar on how much risk a society should tolerate and i keep getting stuck on the same asymmetry: a country will accept tens of thousands of road deaths every year as basically the price of driving, but a new technology projected to kill even a fraction of that would be blocked before anyone did a proper cost-benefit analysis. its not just the numbers either—after one fatal crash we ground an entire fleet or shut down a whole industry, while the background toll of the old technology keeps climbing and nobody calls it an emergency. is there a real principle here that makes familiar, diffuse, roughly chosen risks acceptable and novel, concentrated, imposed ones unacceptable, or is it mostly status quo bias dressed up as safety? give me the strongest justification you can find, then tell me honestly whether it survives the close case where a long-accepted technology causes one big concentrated disaster a year and gets treated as normal while a new technology with the same yearly body count would be banned.
