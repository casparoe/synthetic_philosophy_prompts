I'm giving a lunchtime talk next month to the phase III trials unit at a teaching hospital — mostly senior clinician-trialists and biostatisticians, plus one or two bioethicists who staff the IRB; they treat randomization as the methodological gold standard and clinical equipoise as its ethical precondition. My thesis is that clinical equipoise is doing far less moral work than they think: it is neither necessary nor sufficient to justify randomizing a patient, and the real justification is carried by a package of (i) each arm falling within the range of care a competent physician could defensibly offer off-trial, (ii) genuine informed consent to the randomization itself, and (iii) an independent monitoring committee that stops or modifies the trial once the evidence between arms stops being genuinely open. I expect the room to treat this as both philosophically naive and a practical threat to the trials they run.
