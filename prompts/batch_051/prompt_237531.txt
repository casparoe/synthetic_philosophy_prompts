ok so I'm working on Moores paradox for a grad seminar and I've ended up with a bunch of commitments that I'm not sure all hang together. Here's where I've landed:

(1) The sentence — It's raining but I don't believe it is — is perfectly consistent. There are possible worlds where it's true of me: the weather is wet and I'm just wrong about my own state of mind. The weirdness attaches to asserting it (and maybe hearing it), not to the content itself.

(2) Assertion is governed by a knowledge norm: you shouldn't assert that p unless you know that p.

(3) Knowledge entails belief.

(4) I think the unassertability of Moorean conjunctions is completely explained by (2) and (3), together with the factivity of knowledge. The conjunction p and I don't believe that p cannot be known: knowing the first conjunct would require that I believe p, while knowing the second conjunct would require that I don't believe p, since knowledge is factive. So asserting it always violates the norm.

(5) But the same kind of absurdity shows up in silent thought. If I just judge to myself, with no audience and no intention to assert, that it's raining but I don't believe it is, the incoherence doesn't go away. It feels rationally defective, not merely conversationally awkward.

Do these hang together? I want an audit rather than a lecture on Moore's paradox in general: go through the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension, which only look like they're in tension, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each real tension, say what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
