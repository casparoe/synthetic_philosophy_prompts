So last night me and my friend got into it for hours about whether two people who deeply disagree about what matters morally can still make deals that each of them honestly thinks makes the world better, and now I can't tell if the exact thing we kept circling has a name or if we just invented it.

Here's the setup. She cares a lot about climate change and only somewhat about animal suffering; I'm the reverse, I care a lot about animals and only somewhat about climate. We imagined that I agree to stop flying, and in exchange she goes vegetarian for a year. And here's the part that struck us both: I still think animal suffering obviously outweighs emissions, and she still thinks emissions obviously outweigh animal suffering, but we each look at the package and think it beats doing nothing by our own lights. For me, her year of not eating meat is worth way more than the flights I gave up; for her, my not flying is worth way more than the meat she gave up. Neither of us changed our mind, and neither of us felt like we were being bought off.

Is there an established term for this? It seems adjacent to ordinary compromise, but it isn't the usual grudging "meet me halfway so we can get along" thing, because each person looks at the world after the deal and thinks, by their own separate standards, that it's genuinely better than before. It also seems different from just bargaining over interests, because the whole point is that the two sides are still using incompatible moral scales. If there is a name, what exactly distinguishes it from compromise, from interest-bargaining, and from the kind of trade where someone pays to keep doing something bad? And when does it stop working: does it require each side to have at least roughly accurate beliefs about the other's values, or can it survive self-deception? If there is no name for it, what would you call it and where are the edges?
