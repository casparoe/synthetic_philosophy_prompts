Before I read any more chain-of-thought monitoring papers, I want the requirements list instead. I'm a PhD student in AI safety/interpretability, and the thing I keep noticing is that papers evaluate monitors on toy cases and never actually state what 'faithful' is meant to pick out once the model is doing something stranger than lying. So write me the desiderata for a technique that treats a model's chain of thought as a safety-relevant signal.

Include:
- the cases it has to handle: a model that skips steps in its written CoT because they're obvious; a model that writes a plausible but false rationale for a decision actually driven by something else; a model that reconstructs a justification after the fact rather than reporting the computation; a model whose stated reasoning is real but causally inert (the answer is overdetermined by features it never mentions); and a model that starts compressing or encoding information in word choice because the moniter is part of the training signal.
- the distinctions it can't collapse: faithful vs legible (a CoT can be a true report and still unreadable to a human); omission vs obfuscation; explaining the answer vs reporting the process that produced it; and monitoring at eval time vs monitoring as a loss signal that feeds back into training.
- the pressures/results it can't contradict: Goodhart-type optimisation pressure on the CoT to satisfy the monitor, and the abscence of any ground-truth internal report to compare against.

For each requirement say why it is one, and mark whether it's non-negotiable or just a camp preference. Flag any pair that look jointly unsatisfiable. And tell me which single requirement does the most damage to the popular proposals (recitation monitors, process reward models, linear probes on the residual stream, paraphrasers). A table is fine for the requirements part. No citations, I just want the spec.
