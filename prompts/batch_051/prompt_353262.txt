It concludes we can't represent things our distant ancestors never met, which is clearly nuts, but each step feels forced. Heres the argument:

(2) A representation-type can be selected to indicate F only if responding to F conferred a fitness advantage on the organism's ancestors.

(3) So, by (1) and (2), a mental representation can have content F only if F is something such that responding to F benefited our ancestors.

(4) Bitcoin, Wi-Fi signals, and climate tipping points never figured in our ancestors' environments, so no representation-type was selected to indicate them.

(5) Therefore, on teleosemantics, we cannot represent bitcoin, Wi-Fi signals, or climate tipping points.
