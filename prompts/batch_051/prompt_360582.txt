I recently became a permanent resident in a country where I still cannot vote, and my 16-year-old now works and will soon pay income tax; the gap between what she contributes and her lack of any vote has made the grounds of the franchise suddenly concrete for me. The criteria I keep returning to are these: a person should have the vote insofar as they are subject to the state's coercive enforcement, their interests are substantially affected by its decisions, they possess enough practical and political judgment to vote meaningfully, and they hold a long-term stake in the state's future. Consider three borderline cases: a 15-year-old who works, pays taxes, and follows election coverage closely; a lawful permanent resident of ten years with property and school-age children in the country; and an adult citizen who has lived abroad for thirty years and does not intend to return. Work through each case criterion by criterion and say which of the three qualify. Where the criteria pull in different directions, explain how you resolve the conflict, and say whether the exercise shows that one criterion is doing all the real work, that some criterion acquires its force only by presupposing another, or that the list itself needs revision. Finally, if the criteria do justify the vote, do they also make it a duty to cast one, or is compulsory voting a further step that these criteria cannot reach?
