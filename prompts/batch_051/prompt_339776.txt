I'm a PhD student in philosophy of medicine, and I want a forced comparative evaluation of accounts of disease rather than a survey. Below are five accounts and four cases. For each account, score it from 0 to 10 on each case, where 10 means it classifies the case plausibly and explains why without ad hoc machinery. Then rank the five accounts from best to worst overall and say in one sentence why each account lands where it does.

Accounts:

A. Biostatistical naturalism: a disease is a state that lowers a part's function below species-typical efficiency for the appropriate reference class, with reference classes fixed by biological considerations alone.
B. Normativism: a disease is any bodily or mental condition that a society disvalues and marks as warranting medical attention, treatment, or exemption.
C. Hybrid dysfunction-and-harm: a disease is a dysfunction that also harms the person, where harm is assessed from the person's perspective rather than the society's.
D. Eliminativism: 'disease' is too coarse a category to serve explanatory or practical purposes and should be replaced by narrower notions such as lesion, infection, and functional impairment.
E. Contextual pluralism: 'disease' has no single context-independent extension; clinical care, public health, and disability adjudication each legitimately draw the line differently.

Cases:

1. A Deaf adult who signs natively, is employed, and does not regard the absence of hearing as something that diminishes them.
2. Asymptomatic stage-1 hypertension in a 55-year-old with no other risk factors.
3. Nicotine dependence in a person who has repeatedly tried to stop and cannot.
4. Debilitating grief six weeks after the death of a spouse.

In your final answer, include the full score matrix, the overall ranking, and the one-sentence justification for each rank.
