A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about prostitution law, and I came away with a list of gut verdicts I cannot stitch into one view. We kept going around the usual camps — Nordic model, something like Germany or the Netherlands, something like New Zealand — and every time I tried to plant my flag in one, some case came up that pulled me the other way. My friend ended up somewhere near criminalize the buyer, and got annoyed when I said that just moves the penalty from one person to another without telling us what is actually wrong with the transaction.

So I wrote down my verdicts on the specific situations that actually came up. (1) A woman who sees clients in her own flat, screens them herself, and keeps all the money shouldnt be committing any crime, and (2) neither should her adult, uncoerced clients. (3) A man who pressures his girlfriend into street work and takes most of what she makes is committing a serious crime. (4) A brothel operator who provides rooms, security, and condoms, and takes a 40 percent cut while the workers set their own hours and can leave any time: I want to say thats legal, but it leaves me uneasy in a way I cant explain. (5) A landlord who knowingly rents a flat at market rate to an independent sex worker is just running a normal business, and I feel the same about a website that runs escort ads alongside ordinary classifieds. (6) But a driver who never threatens anyone, never sets anyones schedule, and simply charges a flat fee per ride and waits outside — after enough months this starts to feel like profiting off someone elses prostitution in a way that should be illegal, even though I cant point to any coercion. (7) Buying sex from someone who is very obviously being controlled by another person should be a serious crime even if the buyer used no violence, and turning a blind eye to clear signs isnt an excuse; but if there was genuinely no sign at all, I dont think the buyer is a criminal. (8) A 17-year-old selling sex online shouldnt be prosecuted as a sex worker; she should be handled as a child-protection case, and buying from her should be a crime no matter what.

The problem is I cant see the principle that makes the brothel operator different from the driver, or the landlord different from the driver, or that lets me treat buying-from-a-coerced-person as a crime while treating buying-from-an-independent-person as fine. Is there one principle that captures all or most of these? What does it say about cases I havent listed — a co-op of several workers sharing a flat and splitting rent with no profit, a retired neighbor who rents a spare room to a sex worker for a small fixed sum, or a customer who pays extra for hours of non-sexual company? And which of my verdicts does it force me to drop?
