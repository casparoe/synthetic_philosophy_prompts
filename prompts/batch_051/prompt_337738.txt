What is your credence that Sidgwick's dualism of practical reason—the apparent impossibility of rationally settling how the claims of my own good weigh against the claims of good conceived impartially—admits of an in-principle philosophical resolution? By resolution I mean a sound argument that either shows one of the two standpoints not to be a source of rational requirements, shows that the two cannot genuinely diverge for real agents, shows that their conflict is a case where practical reason is incomplete or indeterminate rather than self-contradictory, or otherwise dissolves the appearance that reason speaks with two equally authoritative voices. In assigning your number, decompose the question into the sub-questions on which the overall credence depends—for instance, whether the all-things-considered ought is always well defined; whether self-interest generates requirements as opposed to permissions or non-rational ends; whether impartial beneficence generates requirements in the same sense; and whether there can be a further rational standard for comparing such standpoints. Give a rough probability for each sub-question, show how they combine to determine your overall credence, and identify which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to and what finding or argument would move it most.
