I work as a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I have been trying to understand the position I think is called eliminative materialism about beliefs and desires. As I understand it, the claim is that our everyday mental vocabulary — beliefs, desires, intentions, and the practice of explaining people's actions by attributing such states — is not a neutral description of the mind but a theory, sometimes called folk psychology, and that a mature neuroscience could show this theory to be so deeply wrong that its central categories would be eliminated rather than reduced to neural states. The category itself gets dropped, not mapped onto something more basic.

This sounds a lot to me like a problem I have actually worked on. A legacy domain model — entities like Customer, Account, and Cart that the application logic depends on everywhere — has stopped corresponding to how the system actually stores and manipulates data after a migration from normalized relational tables to an event-sourced backend, where the source of truth is an append-only log and current state is computed by replaying events. The old entities still work because a translation layer keeps them alive as a compatibility fiction, and they remain convenient for reasoning about the product. But there is no longer any single place where a Customer or an Account is stored; each is now a derived view over a stream of events. The engineering options seem to line up almost one-to-one with the philosophical positions I have read about: keep the legacy entities as useful fictions and pay the translation cost, which sounds like instrumentalism about belief-talk; recover the old entities as compositions of the new primitives so they end up genuinely reducible, which sounds like reductive materialism; or declare the legacy model a bad abstraction and rewrite everything directly against the event log, which sounds like eliminative materialism.

I cannot tell whether this is a deep structural analogy or just a tempting metaphor. I can already see at least one asymmetry: in the engineering case, someone can inspect both the old model and the new substrate and verify that the legacy entities are not really down there as entities; in the mind case, the whole question is whether the neural substrate fails to contain anything belief-like, and there is no equivalent outside vantage point from which to check. Analyze the parallel without leaning on the mere fact that the words line up. Concretely: (a) state the term-by-term mapping between the two sides; (b) say what would have to be true of both sides for the engineering considerations to transfer to the philosophy of mind; and (c) name at least one thing the analogy predicts about folk psychology or eliminative materialism that turns out false or misleading.
