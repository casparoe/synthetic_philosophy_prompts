Many people learn their first words by having objects pointed out to them, yet the most authoritative public statement of what words mean — the dictionary — has abandoned pointing almost entirely. The entry for 'mare' does not show a horse; it explains the word as an adult female horse, and each word in that explanation is in turn explained by further words, so that the dictionary forms a closed network of verbal equivalences, contrasts, and permitted inferences that is never anchored in the world outside the language. What theory of meaning does this practice presuppose? In particular, does dictionary-making commit us to the view that a word's meaning is its place in a network of inferences and equivalences among words, or is the practice equally compatible with the view that words mean by standing for things? Reconstruct the implicit semantics, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely tolerates, and say what a dictionary built on a pure reference-first theory of meaning would have to do differently.
