I am a PhD student in linguistics working on the syntax–lexicon interface, and I keep finding that a category my undergraduates treat as settled—preposition—is doing more analytical work than any definition I know can support. I need a working definition that gets the clear cases right: it should count “under” in “under the table” and should not count “quickly” in “she left quickly.” It should also give a definite verdict, one way or the other, on each of the following: “ago” in “three days ago”; “worth” in “the trip was worth the effort”; “notwithstanding” in “notwithstanding the delay”; “except” in “everyone except Kim”; “before” in “the day before the war” and in “before she left”; “concerning” in “a memo concerning the proposal”; and “of” in “the destruction of the city.” I lean toward thinking the schoolbook formula—a word that relates a following noun or pronoun to the rest of the sentence—is either too narrow or too broad, but I cannot yet say what should replace it. Please propose the definition, run it through all ten cases, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts.
