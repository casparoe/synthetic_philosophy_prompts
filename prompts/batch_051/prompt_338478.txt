For the past couple of years I've followed a policy about changing my mind. Whenever I feel myself starting to revise a view on something I care about — a political commitment, a judgment about a person, a philosophical position — I do not let myself conclude the revision on the spot. Instead I impose a cooling-off period during which I reconstruct the exact argument or piece of evidence that was supposed to have moved me, and then I spend real time asking how much of the movement is coming from the argument itself and how much from the circumstances: fatigue, deference to the person I was talking to, a desire to finally close the question, or relief at not having to defend the old view. I only finalize the change once the argument still strikes me as compelling after I re-read it cold, imagine it coming from someone I distrust, and try to think of the strongest thing someone who held my old view would say about it.

The reason I'm writing is that a friend has started calling this rumination with a method. Her point is that the "checking" is itself just more thinking of the same biased kind, that introspection is not a neutral instrument but another source of self-report that can be wrong, and that closely watching myself form a judgment may alter the judgment in ways I can't correct for — like trying to observe an argument while participating in it. She thinks the policy doesn't reduce bias but repackages whatever conclusion I already wanted, with a longer paper trail. I concede the mechanism: my checks have no guarantee of being less biased than my first-order thinking. But the policy also catches real mistakes, and I can point to times it did. I'm trying to figure out whether the habit is defensible as a form of metacognitive caution, or whether there's some reason to think thinking about your own thinking has diminishing or even negative returns once you push past, say, one round of review. Is there a principled way to tell how much second-order scrutiny is helping rather than flattering myself into thinking I've been rigorous?
