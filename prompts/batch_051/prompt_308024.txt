My city's policy office is full of people who think concepts are tools we should fix when they stop working, not things to just analyze as they are. So they've set up a public concept registry for official statistics and eligibility rules. Every term like unemployed, disability, and chronically homeless gets a version number. Before an agency can change a definition it has to file a concept-change request saying what purpose the change serves, which people would be newly included or excluded, and a forecast of how the headline numbers would move. The old definition stays queryable for five years, and any resident can demand that reports show the statistic under both definitions side by side. Treat this as a mechanism: who lobbies for which concept changes and why; how do agencies game it; does it make concept-fixing more accountable or just bureaucratize it; and what does having two official numbers for the same measure do to public trust in the statistics? Where does it break first, and can the failure be fixed without gutting the registry?
