I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A for a graduate working group on technology and political economy, and the speaker is a researcher who defends a strong version of the claim that the profit motive is the root cause of existential risk from artificial intelligence. Their position is not merely that incentives matter among many factors. On their account, the decisive mechanisms are capitalism's specific features: private control over frontier AI development, pressure on labs to race for market share and investor capital, the treatment of safety as an unowned public good, and the tendency of regulation to be captured or diluted because it threatens the very returns the system rewards. In earlier talks, they have brushed aside reformist proposals such as licensing, pre-deployment audits, and liability regimes by saying these leave the underlying competitive structure intact. They also anticipate the obvious counterexample of state-run or state-backed programs by arguing that those are themselves embedded in interstate military-economic competition rather than standing outside it.

I want the Q&A to push this position at its actual pressure points rather than letting it coast on its broadest formulation. Please write me eight to ten questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the reply the speaker is most likely to give. Include at least one question on each of the following: (1) whether the view can distinguish capitalism from other competitive selection pressures, such as arms races, status competition, bureaucratic rivalry, or even evolution itself, and whether the causal claim is really about competition rather than capitalism specifically; (2) what the view predicts about safety-motivated labs, public-benefit corporations, and nonprofits, and whether such actors are a genuine test of the thesis or just exceptions the account can absorb after the fact; (3) how much work the phrase root cause is doing once one concedes that technical alignment difficulty, hardware overhang, and multipolar deployment could be dangerous under many institutional arrangements; and (4) what the non-capitalist alternative concretely involves, such as centralized planning, open science, an internationally enforced moratorium on scaling, or something else, and which failure mode of that alternative should worry us most. No generic opening questions, and make sure each follow-up would still land if the speaker gives the evasive but sophisticated version of their expected answer.

One ground rule: if I have mischaracterized the speaker's position, or if the framing of the Q&A rests on a mistaken premise about what it would mean to call capitalism the root cause, tell me plainly and repair the setup rather than politely writing questions against a strawman.

Please deliver the result as a single JSON object with the following schema: "framing_check" (a string noting any mischaracterization or mistaken premise in my setup, or "none" if the setup is sound), "position_summary" (a two-to-three-sentence statement of the view the questions will assume), and "questions" (an array of objects, each with "question" for the interview question, "target" for the specific part of the speaker's view it pressures, "expected_reply" for the answer the speaker is most likely to give, and "follow_up" for the follow-up keyed to that expected reply).
