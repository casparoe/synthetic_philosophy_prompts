I am a PhD student in criminal law theory, and my dissertation asks whether strict liability offenses can be defended without abandoning the principle that the state may not punish people in the absence of fault. The project keeps stalling on a threshold question: what counts as punishment at all? In the strict-liability literature, "punishment" is made to cover, at different times, any legally imposed deprivation, only those impositions that express condemnation, or any response to a legal wrong — and these choices quietly determine the substantive conclusions. I need a working definition I can use consistently, rather than another survey of what others have said.

Here are the cases I want the definition tested against. The first two are anchors; the rest are the hard cases I actually care about.

1. A one-year prison term imposed after conviction for a deliberate assault.
2. A general income tax.
3. Punitive damages awarded against a manufacturer in a products-liability suit, imposed in part to deter the defendant and others.
4. A fixed statutory fine levied on a company that sold adulterated food despite maintaining an exemplary compliance program — strict liability in the strong sense, with no proof of knowledge, negligence, or recklessness.
5. A parking ticket issued regardless of the driver's knowledge or intent.
6. Civil forfeiture of a car used to transport drugs, where the owner had no involvement in or knowledge of the offense.
7. Deportation of a lawful permanent resident following a criminal conviction.
8. Indefinite quarantine of a person with active, contagious tuberculosis who refuses treatment.
9. Revocation of a professional license after a drunk-driving conviction.
10. A contempt-of-court fine that terminates as soon as the person complies with the court's order.

Please propose a definition of punishment, run it through every case, and say explicitly which verdicts are forced on you and which are least secure. Given my purpose, I especially need you to say whether a strict-liability regulatory fine of the kind in case 4 counts as punishment under your definition, and what, if anything, distinguishes it from punitive damages. If your definition pushes some legally imposed burdens outside the category of punishment, say what further feature — condemnation, desert, purpose, process, or something else — is doing the work, and whether the boundary is sharp or scalar. I would rather have a single, argued-for definition, uncomfortable consequences included, than a menu of options, so please commit to one.
