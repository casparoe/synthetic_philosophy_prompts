Ok, here's the assignment. In the early part of Sor Juana's Respuesta a Sor Filotea de la Cruz, she describes learning to read as a small child by tagging along with an older sister to an amiga school, and then, after hearing that there was a university in Mexico City, begging her mother to cut her hair and send her disguised as a boy so she could attend. Work with that episode as I've paraphrased it, not with any particular wording. I want you to compare two readings of this moment. On one reading, she's making an arguement from natural intellectual appetite: the girl's urge to learn shows up on its own, so excluding women from formal study is fighting nature. On the other reading, the episode is a social-epistemic complaint: her self-teaching is less a celebration of autodidacticism than a symptom of educational institutions being closed to women. Which reading fits the rest of the Respuesta better? You need to weigh her list of learned women (Paula, Eustochium, Nicostrata, etc.) and her repeated claim that she studied not to teach but to know. Four pages, and don't just summarize her biography.
