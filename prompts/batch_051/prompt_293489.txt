Been going back and forth with some friends about whether "Millennial" or "Gen Z" are real things or just buckets, and I keep noticing a pattern I dont have a word for. Someone will say Boomers are optimistic because they grew up in postwar prosperity, or Gen Z is anxious because of smartphones — the trait gets read off the label. But the label was originally just a range of birth years, and the ranges dont even stay put: one chart starts Gen Z in the mid-90s, another starts it a couple years later, so two siblings born two years apart can land in different "generations," as if a line on a chart did something to them. Half the time the label is applied retrospectively, long after the people in it are already adults, but then people talk as if the label picked out something that was already there. And if the predicted trait doesnt fit someone, the category usually isnt dropped; people just adjust what that generation supposedly is like.

On top of that, when a survey says one generation is more anxious or more optimistic, I cant tell whether that's about being born in a certain window, about being a certain age when you answer, or about living through the same events — those three are all tangled together. It feels like I'm watching a handy sorting label slowly harden into a supposedly real, trait-bearing group whose boundaries keep sliding. Does that pattern have an established name in philosophy or social science? I'm not just after stereotype or overgeneralization. I'd like to know the closest concepts, whether any one of them exactly matches what I'm describing, and how you'd draw the line between treating generations as something real and treating them as purely conventional groupings. If there's no established name, what would you call it and how would you define it?
