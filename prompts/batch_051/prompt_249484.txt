I am one of the maintainers of a small online archive of political and religious essays, hosted on a shared platform with users in many countries. Until recently the platform applied only the law of the country where it is incorporated, which has strong free-expression protections. Last year, a country with considerably stricter speech laws opened an investigation into a different site on the same platform, and the platform reacted by adopting the stricter country's standards for every user in every country, including users in the host country, rather than blocking access from that country or filtering by region. A second, much smaller country has since enacted even stricter rules, and the platform has folded those into its global default as well, even though users there are a negligible share of its traffic. Geoblocking would be technically feasible but leaky, and the platform's stated rationale is that one global standard is cheaper and safer than maintaining a patchwork.

Colleagues have described this in different ways. One calls it extraterritorial jurisdiction, because the strictest country's law is effectively governing speech far outside its borders. Another calls it a chilling effect, because people are deleting or self-censoring material under legal uncertainty. A third calls it a race to the bottom, because the most restrictive law ends up winning. None of these seems to capture the exact mechanism, since no court has ordered the platform to adopt this approach, and the operative actor is a private intermediary standardizing to the strictest rule as a matter of risk and cost.

What I need is conceptual clarification. Does this configuration have an established name? Is it best classified as a species of extraterritorial jurisdiction, as a structural chilling effect in which the intermediary rather than the speaker does the self-censoring, as something closer to the phenomenon in which one jurisdiction's standards become the de facto global standard through the compliance choices of private firms, or as none of these? I am less interested in the label itself than in the boundaries. In particular, where does this phenomenon end and ordinary extraterritorial enforcement begin? How does it differ from a classic chilling effect, given that the platform is not the speaker and may be overcautious rather than accurately frightened? And does it differ from a race to the bottom because the pressure is not on states to weaken their own laws but on a platform to universalize a single strict one? If nothing established fits exactly, I would welcome a precise term and definition, together with edge cases that separate it from its neighbors. I do not need a reading list or citations; I need the carving.

Please begin with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
