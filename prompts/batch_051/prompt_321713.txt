I am drafting a novel whose protagonist undergoes a persistent but publicly unverifiable change in how the world appears to him, and I want the book's philosophical substratum to be exact rather than impressionistic. Before I settle on how to render his inner life, I need a specification of what any account of qualia must get right to be adequate, particularly if it is to survive Dennett's Chase and Sanborn challenges. Write the desiderata list for a theory of qualia: the cases it must handle, the distinctions it may not collapse, and the results from empirical psychology it must respect, with the consideration behind each requirement. In particular, separate qualitative character from judgments about that character, from reports of it, and from its functional role. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely preferences of a particular camp. Then identify the single requirement that most sharply separates eliminativist, representationalist, and naive realist theories, and say whether any subset of the list looks jointly unsatisfiable. That impasse is precisely what I want the novel to inhabit.
