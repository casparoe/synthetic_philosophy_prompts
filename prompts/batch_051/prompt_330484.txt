So me and my friend just had an argument that went on way to long about whether human values are basically one thing or genuinely many. His line was that every apparent value — truth, beauty, loyalty, equality, whatever — only matters because and insofar as it makes lives go better, where "better" means something like more pleasant experience or more satisfied preferences. So they're all really one kind of thing in disguise: conduits to well-being. My line was that this flattens stuff. Loyalty can matter to you even when it makes things worse for you on any well-being measure; a beautiful proof or a fair-but-painful decision can be worth having for its own sake; and when these values conflict there's no common scale that says how to trade them, you just have to judge. We agreed on a bunch of the factual stuff and on the examples, but after hours neither of us moved.

What bugs me is I can't tell what kind of disagreement we were actually having. Sometimes it felt verbal — like he meant "explains why we care" and I meant "justifies what's worth caring about," and the slide between those two is doing a lot of work. Sometimes it felt like we just had different bedrock pictures of what a reason is, or different views about whether a value that systematically made everyone miserable would still be a value. My working theory is that the real fight is about what "reduce" or "in disguise" is even supposed to mean here — explanatory reduction, justificatory reduction, commensurability on a single scale, or something else — and that once you pull those apart we might not disagree much at all. But I might just be flattering myself.
