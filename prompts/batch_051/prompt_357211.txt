I am taking a course on the philosophy of play and leisure, and I have ended up with five commitments that I cannot tell hold together. I would like an audit rather than a defense of them.

(1) I think of play in something like Huizinga's way: it is entered voluntarily, stands apart from ordinary practical life, is governed by rules, and is done for its own sake rather than for some external payoff.

(2) I think professional football is play. The players are paid, under contract, and the game sits inside a large commercial apparatus, but I see no reason to deny that a match is still play.

(3) I hold a welfarist view of value: an activity is valuable only insofar as it makes the lives of sentient beings go better, and its value is exhausted by such contributions.

(4) I accept a Pieper-like contrast between leisure and idleness. Leisure is not the same as recovery from work; watching a formulaic television series can count as leisure, while compulsively scrolling through a feed merely to pass the time cannot.

(5) I think children's pretend play matters for social-cognitive development, but I do not think that developmental payoff is what makes it valuable; its value seems to me to lie in the playing itself.

Do these commitments hang together? Identify the tensions, order them by how serious they are, and for each say which commitment would have to give and at what cost. Also flag any apparent conflicts that dissolve on closer inspection, since I do not want to revise something I did not need to.
