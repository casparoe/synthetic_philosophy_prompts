This one's been bugging me and I can't tell whether it's too slick. It's about sola scriptura — the view that Scripture is the sole infallible authority for Christian faith and practice, so that Scripture alone decides what you're bound to believe and do. A friend laid out this argument against it:

(1) Sola scriptura says any binding belief or practice has to come from Scripture, since Scripture alone is the ultimate authority.
(2) Nothing comes from Scripture without interpreting it.
(3) Interpreting it requires picking hermeneutical principles: how to handle genre, apparent contradictions, cultural context, whether something is literal, all that.
(4) Scripture doesn't itself settle all those principles; you bring them in from tradition, philosophy, common sense, your community, whatever.
(5) So if extra-biblical principles partly determine what Scripture means, then extra-biblical sources are exercising real authority over what you end up believing and doing.
(6) Therefore sola scriptura either collapses into 'Scripture plus whatever interpretive framework you happened to absorb,' or it undermines itself.

I half-suspect the move from (4) to (5) is where it's sneaky, becuase using a dictionary to read a text isn't the same as handing the dictionary doctrinal authority. But honestly those hermeneutical principles are doing way more than a dictionary does. Walk me through the argument step by step: is it valid, and if it isn't, exactly which step fails and why? If (5) is the culprit, give your strongest true restatement of (5) and check whether the conclusion still follows from it. Please answer in American English.
