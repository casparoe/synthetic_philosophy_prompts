So my friend and I spent like two hours last night arguing about whether you can owe someone respect in your own head rather than just in how you treat them, and afterwards I wrote down where I landed so I could sanity check it before I commit to any of it. Here's what I hold, roughly.

1. Respect is mainly an attitude or a way of regarding someone, not a set of behaviors. Two people can do all the same overt things and only one of them be acting respectfully, becuase the difference is in what they are responding to in the other person.

2. For an attitude to count as respectful, it has to track the right features of the person: their standing, their claims, their judgment. A warm feeling that happens to be directed at them is not respect.

3. Some of what we owe people is therefore in our private deliberation, not just our observable action. If a manager never talks over a junior colleague but privately discounts every point she makes the moment it matters, he is failing at something she could properly hold him to account for.

4. But I also think ought implies can. And a lot of these attitudes, like finding someone credible or taking their objections seriously, are not things you can just choose to have at will.

5. I also think we can be responsible for attitudes the way we are responsible for things we cultivate indirectly: you can't flip a switch, but you can notice the pattern, catch the automatic discounting, sometimes lean the other way, and that is enough control.

6. Basic recognition is owed equally to every person just in virtue of being a person. You do not earn it, and no one deserves more of it than anyone else.

7. At the same time, recognition is a response to something particular in the individual. If I treat everyone in a fully generic way without attending to who this person actually is, what she knows, what she is claiming, I feel like I have missed the point. Generic politeness is not recognition.

8. Disrespect can live in structures or practices even when no individual in them has a disrespectful attitude. A hiring process can predictably fail to register certain people's contributions, and I would call that a recognition failure even though everyone involved is well-meaning.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look that way, and how bad each real tension is. For the real ones, what is the cheapest fix and what would it cost me? Id rather you rank them by severity than tell me they all sort of pull.
