In my philosophy class we did the standard thing where the claim is you couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg. I've been stuck on a version where its not that simple. Suppose the exact same sperm fertilizes the exact same egg nucleus, but before fertilization that nucleus was moved into the cytoplasm of a different egg (like the mitochondrial replacement / three-parent IVF procedure). Same nuclear DNA, same sperm, but all the mitochondria and most of the cell machinery come from a different egg. Is that still "the same egg" for the purposes of the original claim? If the view is that your origin is that particular egg, which part of the egg is doing the work — the nucleus, the cytoplasm, or the particular physical object? Does the answer change if the donor cytoplasm comes from a different woman entirely? And does the original intuition that you couldn't have come from a different egg survive once you have to answer this, or does it collapse into something looser like "you had to start from roughly this DNA"? Mostly I want to know whether the sperm-and-egg claim has a determinate answer here, or whether it just stops being useful once the egg gets decomposed into parts.
