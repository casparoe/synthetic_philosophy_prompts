I write fiction, and I have a long-standing policy of discounting, almost to zero, any interpretation of my own work that conflicts with what I intended while writing it. I reason that I have privileged access to which details were deliberate, which were accidents, and what a scene was attempting to do. I carry the same policy into revision: if a trusted reader tells me a passage could be read as expressing something I did not intend, I do not treat that as a reason to change it. But I realize this makes my intention the sole standard for my text's meaning, and a critic would call it the intentional fallacy. Is my policy defensible, or am I systematically shielding myself from genuine meanings I did not put there but which a careful reader might be better placed to see? If the policy is partly sound, give the version of it that keeps the benefits while admitting evidence from unintended readings, and say where even that version fails.
