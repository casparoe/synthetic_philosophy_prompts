I'm running a one-off seminar for AI policy people — mostly engineers, a bit of economics, nobody's read any international relations — on Fearon's rationalist explanations for war (the usual trio: private information with incentives to misrepresent, commitment problems, indivisibility) and what they get us when applied to AI races between labs or states. I don't want the explanation itself; I want a plan for the explanation. The thing I want them to leave with is that a race can be a bargaining failure rather than a personality defect or a culture problem. Work out the order the ideas have to go in and what depends on what (presumably the first move is getting them to feel the puzzle: both sides would rather the safe coordinated outcome, yet the race still happens). Then give me the single carrying example to build the whole thing around, set in frontier AI labs negotiating some kind of slowdown or safety deal, and say what it must have and must lack — no cartoon villain, and I suspect not a flat prisoner's dilemma, since that just collapses the bargaining story. Then give me the misconceptions in the order they'll actually come up: racing is just recklessness, if they both want the deal they'll take it, more transparency would fix it, a treaty with teeth fixes it, the prize is indivisible so no deal was ever possible. Say where in the sequence to head each one off and with what move, including the ugly bit where the transparency a lab needs to verify the other side's compliance is itself capability-relevant, so the obvious fix eats itself. Then give me one end-of-session check that shows they've got the bargaining-failure machinery and not just the anecdotes. And say what I should explicitly refuse to cover at this level. Keep it British English, and format the reply as a numbered list with exactly five items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion.
