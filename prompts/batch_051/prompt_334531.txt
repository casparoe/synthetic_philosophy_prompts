Last night a friend and I spent the better part of an evening arguing about whether transformative AI has already occurred. He said it plainly has: systems that draft code, pass professional exams, summarize documents, and predict protein structures are already changing how millions of people do intellectual work, and that is transformation enough. I said that is a threshold problem — transformative should be reserved for a change on the scale of agriculture or the Industrial Revolution, a durable reorganization of society that alters the economy's growth path rather than merely making familiar tasks cheaper. We never agreed on what would settle the matter, because we could not agree on what the right evidence would even be.

You are a philosopher of technology with a methodological bent. Please think carefully, and begin by brainstorming possible operationalizations before settling on the strongest. Then design the strongest concrete test of the claim that transformative AI has already happened: which observable indicators you would track, what ranges of results would count for and against the claim, and whether any observation could be decisive. Then say exactly what the test would and would not settle — which part of the dispute is a stipulation about where to draw a line, and what your operationalization leaves out. If you think current evidence already points one way under your own operationalization, say so and say how strongly.
