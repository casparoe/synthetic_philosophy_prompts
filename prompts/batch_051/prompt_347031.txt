I'm putting together a small zine of academic parodies for a history reading group, and I want one piece from you: a deadpan parody of an author style guide for a made-up journal, The Journal of Unrealized Histories. Numbered rules, around 700 words, written in that awful cheerful-bureaucratic voice journals use in their instructions for authors.

The joke should be that the field's actual methodological pathologies get codified as editorial policy, and the humor has to come from the real debates rather than from inventing silly ones. Work in, without lecturing: the minimal-rewrite rule — the idea that the only respectable counterfactual changes the smallest thing possible and then runs history forward — so there should be rules about how short the pulled thread has to be and what happens if you pull two; the split between wonder counterfactuals (Nazi victory, Napoleon wins Waterloo) and plausible-world counterfactuals, with the former quarantined in a Cabinet of Wonders section; the overdetermination fight, where a paper whose counterfactual still lands on the same outcome has to either claim the event was inevitable or file for a Robustness Exemption; and the hindsight problem — authors must certify that no actor in the alternate world knows anything they couldn't have known at the time. If you can, fold in the deeper joke that the minimal-rewrite rule quietly sneaks in a prior judgment about which changes count as small, which is exactly the contingency-versus-inevitability question the method is supposed to be settling.

Deadpan throughout, no winking, and no real historians' names. It should be funny to someone who knows the counterfactual-history debates and still land for a smart person who doesn't.
