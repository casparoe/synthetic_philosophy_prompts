I run a small business that imports dried herbs and food supplements. Last year one of our shipments passed independent lab testing and was cleared for sale, but a later government inspection found traces of a banned stimulant in a different sample from the same batch, and we now face a substantial civil penalty. The agency accepts that we did not know about the contaminant and could not realistically have detected it; they maintain that under this area of regulation, liability does not depend on what we knew or intended, only on the fact that an unsafe product reached consumers. They add that if liability required proof of knowledge or carelessness, importers would have an incentive not to test their products at all. I have always assumed that punishment, even in a regulatory context, requires some kind of fault—knowledge, intent, or at least negligence. Is the agency's position morally defensible, or does penalizing me here make a principled mistake? I am not seeking legal advice. I want to understand the strongest arguments on both sides: whether a system that penalizes people without fault can be justified, and what exactly such a justification would have to accomplish.
