A friend in my metaphysics seminar backed me into a corner with this, and I can't find the exit. His argument, as best I remember it:

(1) I am the same person who sat down at the start of class and the same person who will leave at the end.
(2) If I persist through changes like sitting and standing, I must do it either by being wholly present at each moment I exist (endurance) or by being a four-dimensional thing with distinct temporal parts at each moment (perdurance). There's no third option.
(3) If I endure, then the same whole object is sitting at t1 and standing at t2, so the very same thing has incompatible properties. That looks like a straight contradiction.
(4) If I perdure, then one temporal part of me sits and another stands. But then nothing really changes: two different things (my parts) just have different properties, like a road being straight at one end and curved at the other.
(5) So either persistence through change lands me in a contradiction, or change isn't what I thought it was.
(6) But I plainly do persist and do change, so something has gone badly wrong.

He says the real moral is that ordinary change is a useful fiction, and identity over time isn't the thing we pre-philosophically take it to be. I won't accept the conclusion, but I can't see which step fails. Tell me which step you'd reject, and what rejecting it commits me to.
