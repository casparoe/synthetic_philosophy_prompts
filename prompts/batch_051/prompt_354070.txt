I'm the president of my school's student council and I keep noticing something annoying. We meet every Monday and vote on stuff like where to hold the winter dance, how to spend the leftover club fair money, whether to approve a new spirit week proposal, whatever. People give their opinions going around the table in the same order, and it's obvious that whatever the first couple people say basically decides the whole vote. Later people who obviously know more (like the treasurer actually knows whether we can afford the thing) just kind of go along with the room, and once a few heads nod it's basically over. I brought this up and our faculty advisor said something like "thats just groupthink, dont be a sheep," which is not exactly a procedure I can use on Monday. I read a little about information cascades and rational herding, and it sounds exactly like whats happening in these meetings, but all the examples I found are about restaurants or stock markets or people watching other people's choices on a street, and I don't know how to translate that into running a student council meeting.

What I actually need is a reusable procedure, grounded in what the research on information cascades and rational herding actually shows, for me as the person running the meeting to detect when our discussion is turning into a cascade (as opposed to people just genuinely agreeing because the first argument was strong) and to decide what to do about it in the moment without making everyone hate me for rigging the vote. We have about 15 minutes per agenda item, nobody else on the council has read any of this theory, and big spending still has to be approved by the advisor afterwards, so if I do something weird it has to be something I can explain in plain language later. Give me a checklist or set of tests I can run in order, and work through two or three realistic council decisions with it. Make at least one of them a case where your procedure tells me to push back on a decision I personally like or that the popular kids are excited about, because I want to know how honest the procedure actually is. And be clear about what the procedure cannot do, like cases where the group genuinely is stumped and no trick with speaking order, secret ballots, or forcing people to justify their votes will save us.
