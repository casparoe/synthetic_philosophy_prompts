I got conscripted into a medieval philosophy reading group this fall mostly because my roommate needed warm bodies, but the foreknowledge problem has somehow gotten its hooks into me. We've now spent three sessions stuck on Boethius's solution and nobody has moved an inch. The weird part is we all agree on what the text says. Boethius's move is that God doesn't have *fore*knowledge in the bad sense, because God isn't before our actions at all: he's outside time, so every moment is present to him at once. Just like my watching you sit over there doesn't force you to keep sitting, God's eternal seeing of my future action doesn't force it. One side thinks that basically dissolves the problem. The other side says it just relabels it: if God's eternal present already includes my action tomorrow, then tomorrow is fixed, and calling it present to God rather than future doesn't loosen anything. The side defending the text leans on his distinction between simple necessity and conditional necessity — a future act is only necessary *on the condition that* God knows it, not necessary in itself — and the other side says thats exactly the problem, since the condition is always already satisfied, so the act ends up necessary in every sense that matters.

I've started to suspect the real disagreement isn't about Boethius at all, and that we're talking past each other because the two camps mean different things by free: one means something like not caused by anything outside me, the other means the ability to do otherwise even while the act is known with certainty. Or maybe the hidden fight is over whether certainty quietly slides into necessity, or over whether a relation to something outside time can even be called knowledge in the same sense as ours. But I honestly don't know if that's the right diagnosis — it could just be that one side treats the fixedness of the past as the paradigm of necessity and the other treats seeing-without-interfering as the paradigm, and neither can argue for its starting point. So, concretely: what kind of disagreement is this, and what would actually count as progress?
