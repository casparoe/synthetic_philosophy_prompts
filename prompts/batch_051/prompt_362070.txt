Suppose a country is about to legalize whole-brain emulation, and the government needs a rule for deciding which emulations count as legal continuations of the scanned person (entitled to that person's rights, property, and job) versus which are just software the estate can delete. It creates a Fidelity Board with the following system. Before anyone gets scanned, they can file a "continuity packet": a few hundred autobiographical questions answered on video, plus some memory details known only to close family. After the destructive scan, the emulation is run for three months in a sandbox with no write-access to its own code, and two Board assessors interview it daily. At the end, the Board assigns a fidelity score from 0 to 100, based on how well the emulation matches the continuity packet and how it handles surprise follow-up questions. The legal consequences are graded. A score of 90 or above means the emulation is treated as the very same person: it gets the property, the debts, the job, and cannot be edited, copied, or deleted without its consent. A score from 60 to 89 makes it a "dependent emulation" with basic welfare protections but no property and no protection against being rented out as cheap labor. Below 60, it's just a program and the estate can delete it. The emulation can appeal its score, but each appeal costs the estate money and re-runs the Board with fresh assesors.

How does this actually play out? I want an incentive analysis, not a verdict on whether the scheme is good in the abstract. What would people do differently before they get scanned? How would emulations strategize in the three-month window? How would entrepreneurs design businesses around the thresholds, and how would the assessors be gamed or kept honest? What second-order effects would show up after a decade or two? And does the Board end up tracking whether an emulation is the same mind, or tracking something more boring like being good at interviews? Are the failure modes fixable by changing the thresholds, the packet, or the appeals process?
