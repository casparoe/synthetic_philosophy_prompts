Begin from the section of Keith Frankish's 2016 paper Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness where he distinguishes weak illusionism from strong illusionism and introduces the term quasi-phenomenal. The passage's proposal is this, in paraphrase: weak illusionism holds that introspection misrepresents the character of conscious states, whereas strong illusionism holds that introspection misrepresents conscious states as having phenomenal character at all; quasi-phenomenal properties are the non-phenomenal features that introspection tracks when it seems to reveal phenomenal properties.

Suppose you are advising a postdoctoral researcher in philosophy of mind who finds strong illusionism plausible and has roughly three years to produce one sustained piece of work. Which open conceptual problems should they prioritize, and in what order? For each proposed problem, explain the reasoning behind its place in the ranking: whether it is upstream of other illusionist commitments, how tractable it is using tools currently available in philosophy and cognitive science, and what conceptual or empirical results in the next several years would most raise or lower its priority. Make sure the agenda deals with at least the following issues: whether strong illusionism can specify quasi-phenomenal properties without collapsing back into realism about phenomenal consciousness; how to distinguish a system that has quasi-phenomenal states from one that also falls for the introspective illusion; and what strong illusionism implies about the semantics of phenomenal concepts and the accuracy conditions of introspective reports. The deliverable should be a defended agenda, not a survey or reading list.
