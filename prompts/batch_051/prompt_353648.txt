The author says: Anselm's ontological argument starts by defining God as the greatest conceivable being. Since even the Fool can understand this phrase, God exists in the understanding. But a being that exists in reality as well as in the understanding is greater than one that exists only in the understanding. So if God existed only in the understanding, he wouldn't be the greatest conceivable being. Therefore God exists in reality. The author also says Gaunilo's lost island shows the template proves any perfect thing, and that modern modal versions by people like Plantinga and Gödel are just a more formal way of smuggling necessary existence in as a positive property, so they inherit the same problem.
