I keep going around in circles on whether omissions are causes. Here’s the argument as I have it, with the part I suspect is a trick flagged. Walk through each numbered step: say whether the step-to-step inferences are valid, whether the premises are true, and if something fails, point at the exact step and give the strongest repair you can.

(1) A cause has to be an event — something that happens in the world.
(2) An omission, like my not watering the plant, isn’t an event; it’s just the absence of one.
(3) So omissions can’t be causes.
(4) But the plant’s death was caused, and if anything caused it, my not watering it did; that’s also what we’d cite in holding me responsible.
(5) So at least one of (1)–(3) must be wrong, or the “caused” in (4) has to be loose talk.

I’m not sure the move from (1) and (2) to (3) is as innocent as it looks — someone told me “cause” might be doing different work there than in (4). Is the argument valid? If it is, which premise do I reject and what do I have to give up?
