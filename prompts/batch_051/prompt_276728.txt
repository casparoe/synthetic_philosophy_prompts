I'm a journalist who covers cognitive science and philosophy, and I've been puzzled for years by a debate that seems to absorb new findings without ever moving: the rationalist–empiricist dispute over whether any knowledge is innate. Developmental research now shows that infants have surprisingly rich expectations about objects, number, and agents; work in connectionist and Bayesian modeling shows how much structure can be acquired from statistical regularities in input; yet both camps cite the same studies as confirming their side. I also notice that no one appears to mean quite the same thing by 'innate'—some mean a representation present at birth, others a constraint on learning, others merely 'not acquired by the mechanism in question.' My working hypothesis is that this is not one stalled debate but several disagreements bundled under one heading: a verbal dispute over 'innate,' a dispute about what counts as decisive evidence, and a dispute about what a cognitive explanation must do. Diagnose the structure of the impasse. Is it verbal, factual, evidential, methodological, or are the parties answering different questions under one name? Given your diagnosis, what would count as progress, if anything? Please write your answer in American English.
