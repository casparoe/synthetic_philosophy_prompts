I have a rule I picked up after reading Clark and Chalmers: I treat my notes app as literally part of my memory. I put appointments, names, directions, half-formed thoughts into it, and when my brain and the app disagree I side with the app, on the grounds that it's just another store in my cognitive system and I shouldn't privilege neurons over silicon. Lately this has gotten harder. The app ocasionally pulls up a stale version of a note—something I know I edited later—and I can't tell from the inside whether I'm looking at an old sync or the current one. I still side with the app when it conflicts with my memory, even though I know the app has a failure mode of its own. But my memory also confidently remembers things wrong, so I can't tell whether I'm holding the app to a higher standard than I hold my brain. Is this policy defensible as an epistemic rule, or have I turned a parity argument into an excuse to trust a gadget over my own recollection? If it's weaker now than it was, where exactly did it go wrong—the unreliability, the opacity about which state I'm consulting, or something about treating a cloud-synced artifact as mine in the first place? And is there a tightened version of the rule I'd be entitled to keep?
