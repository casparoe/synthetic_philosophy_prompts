I've been kicking around a distinction I call semantic linkage vs. mechanical linkage for the derivative-work question in copyleft, and I can't tell if it's a real conceptual tool or just vibes. The claim: whether program B is a derivative of copyleft program A shouldn't be settled by how they're wired at runtime—shared library, fork/exec, reading a socket, whatever—but by whether B's behavior is only intelligible against the background of A. A plugin whose whole point is to extend A is semantically linked to A even if it runs in a separate process, while a proprietary app that calls a GPL routine only to pad a string to an even length is mechanically linked but not semantically. This seems to cut against both sides of the usual bright-line debates. Is the distinction coherent? Does it already exist under another name in copyright or licensing theory? And where does it break—what does it wrongly count as derivative, and what does it wrongly let off the hook?
