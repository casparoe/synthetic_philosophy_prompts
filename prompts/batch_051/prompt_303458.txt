I'm presenting a seminar paper to a reading group in formal philosophy of science, mostly committed Bayesian confirmation theorists who take synchronic, logically omniscient agents as the norm, plus two people working on bounded rationality. My thesis is that the problem of old evidence dissolves once scientists are modeled as logically non-omniscient: if a scientist can hold non-extreme credence in the proposition that a theory entails an already-known datum, then discovering that entailment can shift credence in the theory, and Bayesian confirmation theory need not fall back on counterfactual priors that pretend the evidence was not known. In the paper I run this through a toy case and argue that it handles the case of general relativity and the already-known anomaly in Mercury's orbit while preserving a normative role for Bayesianism.
