I'm preparing a handout for a graduate philosophy-of-cognitive-science reading group, and I would like you to write a sorting quiz titled Which View of Language and Thought Do You Actually Hold? The quiz should classify readers by what they say about concrete cases, not by which label they like, and it should stand alone without any citations or further-reading list.

The audience is graduate students in philosophy with no formal linguistics background. Please make it eight to ten questions, each presenting a concrete case or task with two or three forced-choice answer options.

The angle I care about is where language is supposed to bite and how deep the effect runs. Build the quiz from the following fault lines, and let the doctrinal positions fall out of the combinations of answers rather than beginning from a list of named views:

1. In cross-linguistic experiments, do differences in behavior reflect genuine differences in thinking, or can they be fully explained as artifacts of translation, instructions, or response strategies that recruit language during the task?
2. If there is a genuine effect, does it occur only while language is being used, or does it also appear in offline tasks and habitual thought?
3. Does language ever affect low-level perception or early categorization, or only higher-order processes such as memory, similarity judgment, and reasoning?
4. Are the effects transient and easily shifted by context, or do they amount to durable differences in how people habitually parse the world?
5. Is the answer domain-specific, so that someone could reasonably hold a strong view for number but not for color?

Make sure the questions cover at least the following four cases, and add an additional one or two only if they separate answer patterns the first four cannot:

- a color memory task in which verbal interference helps or hurts, for speakers of a language that obligatorily marks a distinction absent from the comparison language;
- a task in which speakers of a language with grammatical gender rate inanimate objects on properties aligned with the noun classes;
- a spatial memory task contrasting speakers of a language that favors absolute spatial terms with speakers of one that favors relative terms;
- a quantity task contrasting speakers of a language with an exact number lexicon and one without.

For the scoring key, map the answer patterns onto at least these leaves: strong linguistic determinism, a moderate language-shapes-thought view, an online-only (thinking-for-speaking) view, a performance-only universalism on which apparent differences are artifacts of the task and translation, and a lab-effect-only skepticism on which effects are real but small and not shown to matter outside the laboratory. Walk through the relevant answer patterns rather than only the five named views; for combinations that no live view occupies, note them and give one sentence diagnosing whether the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely underexplored. If two named views cannot be separated by the included cases, say so explicitly and identify the further case that would separate them. Please do not include a bibliography, citations, or recommended readings.
