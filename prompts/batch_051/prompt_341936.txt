In the causal-inference course I'm taking, we were given three conditions a causal Bayes net is supposed to satisfy for a distribution: (1) the Causal Markov Condition, that each variable is independent of its non-descendants conditional on its parents; (2) minimality, that no proper subgraph of the graph satisfies the Causal Markov Condition for the same distribution; and (3) faithfulness, that the distribution has no independences, conditional or unconditional, beyond those entailed by the graph. Now consider a linear Gaussian system over three variables X, Y, and Z in which X, u, and v are mutually independent standard normals, with Y = aX + u and Z = bY + v − abX, where a and b are nonzero. The direct effect of X on Z (−ab) exactly cancels the indirect effect through Y (ab), so after substitution Z = bu + v; thus X is independent of Z, while X and Z are each associated with Y. The true causal graph has edges X→Y, Y→Z, and X→Z. Go through the three conditions one at a time for the true graph: does it satisfy the Markov condition, is it minimal, and is it faithful? If the three conditions together select a different graph, identify that graph, and then say whether the case shows a gap in the criteria themselves or merely a background assumption about parameter values that the minimal-faithful-Bayes-net rule depends on.
