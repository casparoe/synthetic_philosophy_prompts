Write a Petrarchan sonnet spoken by Abraham on Mount Moriah in which the octave gives the voice of the knight of infinite resignation and the sestet gives the voice of the knight of faith from Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling. The volta should mark the double movement: in the octave Abraham has already renounced Isaac and speaks with the calm of one who has given everything up, while in the sestet—after the angel stays his hand—he speaks with the confidence that he will get Isaac back by virtue of the absurd. Let the rhyme scheme bind the two halves into a single continuous argument rather than two separate speakers, because for Kierkegaard the knight of faith takes the movement of resignation first and then takes back what he renounced, rather than skipping over it. A reader who knows the text should locate the teleological suspension of the ethical in the pressure between octave and sestet rather than in any stated doctrine, and a reader who does not should still find it a good poem about a father who almost loses a son.
