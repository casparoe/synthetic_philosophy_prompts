I'm a software engineer, never took a philosophy class, but something has been bugging me and I'd like someone else to take a real pass at formalizing it.

Take the claim that all ravens are black. I've got a list of gut verdicts about what does and doesn't count as evidence for it, and they refuse to add up to a clean rule. Here they are as honestly as I can state them:

- A black raven on my walk this morning. Clearly confirms the claim; I'd raise my confidence.
- An albino raven at the zoo. Kills the claim outright — as close to refutation as I ever get.
- A white sneaker on my porch when I woke up. Does not confirm the claim at all. Not even a hair. It feels like a category error to treat it as evidence.
- A green apple in the fridge. Same, zero.
- A black leather dress shoe. Also zero, maybe even more clearly irrelevant than the sneaker.
- I deliberately go looking for a counterexample: since all ravens are black, every non-black thing ought to be a non-raven, so I check a few non-black things. The first one I look at is a red mailbox on the corner, and once I confirm it isn't a raven, I feel like my confidence in the claim went up by a tiny amount. Not zero, but small enough that I keep wanting to round it down.

The only rule I can state cleanly is the obvious one: a case of A and B is evidence for all As are Bs, a case of A and not-B is evidence against, and everything else is noise. That rule fits most of my list except the mailbox, and it also breaks somewhere else. The claim all ravens are black is logically equivalent to its contrapositive, all non-black things are non-ravens — that's just the contrapositive, and the implication really is the same. So shouldn't the white sneaker confirm the second claim, and therefore the first? But my gut says the sneaker is zero and the mailbox is nonzero, and I can't tell wich of those is the bug.

Is there a coherent principle behind all my verdicts, or a clean principle that forces me to drop one or two? Feel free to test the A/B rule against my list and patch it, replace it, or tell me which verdicts go. If it forces me to drop some, which ones are cheapest to give up and why? Also, what does the principle you land on say about two cases I keep going back and forth on: a black crow, and a white shoe I found specifically because I was hunting for a white shoe to settle a bar bet, not because I was checking non-black things? Do either of those confirm the claim, and by how much?
