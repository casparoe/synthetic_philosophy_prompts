"Consider the counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail scenarios. Explain how a causal decision theorist (CDT), an evidential decision theorist (EDT), and a functional decision theorist (FDT) would act in each scenario, and say what the main normative difference is."

The student wrote:

"CDT maximizes expected utility over the causal consequences of your act. EDT maximizes expected utility over what your act would be evidence for. FDT asks what decision function would do best across relevant counterfactual worlds.

In XOR blackmail, Omega sends the message iff you have termites xor you pay $1000. CDT refuses because paying doesn't cause the termites to disappear. EDT pays, because conditional on receiving the message, paying makes it certain that the message was sent because you paid rather than because you have termites, so paying buys evidence that you avoid the $1M termite damage. FDT refuses for basically the same reason as CDT: paying is only correlated with avoiding termites, not causally connected to them.

In counterfactual mugging, Omega flips a coin; if heads, it asks you for $100, and if tails, it gives you $10,000 iff you would have paid on heads. CDT refuses because the $100 doesn't cause the $10,000. EDT also refuses: paying would be evidence that the coin came heads, and heads is the branch where you lose $100. FDT pays because it evaluates the policy of paying-if-asked across both coin outcomes: it loses $100 on heads but gains $10,000 on tails, for an expected value of $4,850. The main difference is that EDT conditions on the actual observation, while FDT evaluates the same decision function across counterfactual branches."
