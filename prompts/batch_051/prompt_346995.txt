I'd describe myself as a maximizing consequentialist: the right act is the one with the highest expected moral value. I also think cluelessness means I'm not obligated to buy the fair-trade coffee rather than the cheap stuff, because the long-run market effects are untraceable — both options come out permitted. Finally I hold that consequentialism should be action-guiding; a theory that says, for nearly every choice, just do whatever, has something wrong with it. Do these hang together? Tell me which pairs are in tension, which only look tense, rank the tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest fix would be for the worst problem.
