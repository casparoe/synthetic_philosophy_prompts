You are an experienced teacher of medieval philosophy. I am a TA, and next week I have to teach Peter Abelard's claim that sin consists in consent to an illicit intention—not in the outward act and not in the prior desire or the taking of pleasure in it—to first-year philosophy students in one fifty-minute section. Do not explain the doctrine to me; plan the section. In your plan, specify: what distinction has to be in place first (for instance, how Abelard separates suggestion, pleasure, and consent) and what depends on it; which single example or case should carry the whole hour, and what it must and must not include; the misconceptions students will predictably form—I expect at least "so the deed doesn't matter at all" and "so feeling tempted is already a sin"—and exactly where in the sequence each should be headed off; what I should deliberately leave out at this level; and one final question that would show whether they really understand consent as a stopping point of the will rather than as a feeling or as a plan to act. Before drafting the plan, brainstorm the dependency structure among these ideas and the strongest student objection you will need to absorb.
