Write a decision tree for an advanced undergraduate seminar on competence without comprehension, Dennett's idea that a system can be extremely good at something while having no understanding of what it is doing. The tree should start from a single commitment—whether the respondent accepts Darwin's strange inversion, that apparent design in nature can be fully explained by uncomprehending natural selection—and then route them through yes/no questions to a named position on what, if anything, comprehension adds to competence. Both of Dennett's inversions should structure the branches: Darwin's, in which nature produces design without a designer, and Turing's, in which a rule-following machine can behave intelligently without understanding. Phrase every node as a concrete, label-free question—for instance, whether a sunflower tracking the sun, a chess engine, or someone following Turing's paper machine through a proof counts as competent at its task; whether the biological and machine cases differ in degree or in kind; whether comprehension is real; and whether comprehension explains competent behavior or is itself a product of it. Build the tree from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of positions, and make sure the leaves include at least these: a view that rejects both inversions; a view that accepts Darwin's but not Turing's; an emergentist view on which comprehension is real, generated by uncomprehending processes, and does explanatory work; an interpretationist or user-illusion view on which comprehension is real enough but not what does the explaining; and an eliminativist view that advises dropping comprehension talk. For each combination of answers that has no named occupant, say in one sentence whether it is empty because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Finally, flag any two leaves the tree cannot currently separate and state the further question that would separate them.
