In my class on ritual and social order we've been reading about sacrifice, and I keep noticing a pattern I cant find the right label for. It isn't exactly sacrifice; it's something that happens just next to it. There's an ancient Israelite rite where, once a year, the priest puts both hands on a goat's head, recites the community's offenses over it, and the goat is led out into the desert to carry those offenses away. The odd part is that afterwards everyone acts as if a real transaction happened. Feuds go quiet, ordinary cooperation comes back, people describe themselves as light again. But if you pressed any of them on how a goat ends up carrying a wrong, they'd point at the laying on of hands and change the subject.

The same shape showed up at my summer job, which is what got me thinking it isn't just ancient religion. Our company had a food-safety scare last year, and management fired two low-level supervisors who weren't even on shift when the relevant decisions got made. They weren't innocent, to be fair, but nobody actually thought they caused it. And still it worked: inspectors backed off, customers acted like the matter was closed, and employees talked about the company as if a debt had been paid. The root problem stayed in place, same as the goat never became a sinner. So the thing seems to work as social machinery while being built on a transparent fiction. I'm not sure it's the same as a mob that sincerely believes its victim is guilty, because here everyone half-knows the person or animal didn't do the deed, and they do it anyway.

I want to know whether this exact dynamic has a name. If it does, how do the ritual case and the corporate case relate to each other, is one parasitic on the other, and where are the edges: too obviously innocent, too powerful to push out, too clearly guilty? I wont be satisfied with a bare label. I need to know what the mechanism actually is, what it is not, and where it stops being this and turns into ordinary punishment.
