There's an argument that has completely taken over my utilitarian reading group, and the strange thing is that nobody is disputing any new fact. One side says pleasure and pain are fully commensurable: they're opposite directions on a single scale, so any finite amount of pain, however severe, can in principle be outweighted by enough pleasure, even if that means a city's worth of pleasant afternoons to offset one torture. The other side says that's exactly the mistake. Below some threshold of intensity, mild pleasures and mild pains trade off in the ordinary way, but above it—the way they put it, pain that breaks a person's agency—the pain is lexically prior: no amount of ordinary pleasantness justifies a single instance of it. The first side replies that the second can't say where the threshold is, and that every attempt to do so either sneaks a comparison back in or turns the rule into a mere heuristic for risk aversion.

Both sides grant that the pain is awful, the pleasure is genuinely good, and these are real experiences had by real people, so they're not disagreeing about any psychology or neuroscience I can identify. And every session still ends where it started. I've started to suspect its a disguised disagreement about the word 'outweigh'—one side means something like 'rationally permissible to choose,' the other something like 'morally permissible to bring about.' But I don't trust my own diagnosis.
