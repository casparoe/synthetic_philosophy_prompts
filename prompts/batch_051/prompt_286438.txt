I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and after sitting through too many arguments about whether taxation is just legalized theft, I got curious what a city would look like if it actually tried to make every tax payment feel like a purchase. So here's a mechanism I want you to walk me through. A city replaces its residential property tax with a voluntary civic subscription. Each household recieves an itemized annual bill for the services it might use: road maintenance for streets within half a mile, fire response capacity for their district, school places per child, library hours, park upkeep, emergency medical response, and so on. Households can pay the full suggested amount, pay for only some line items, or pay extra into a general fund. Nothing is legally compulsory, but households that underpay relative to the city's own calculation lose reciprocity privileges: their 911 calls get lower priority, their permit applications go to the back of the queue, and their kids lose priority in school lotteries. Walk me through how this would actually play out. What gets funded and defunded first, and how do people game the line items? Do rich and poor households end up buying different versions of the city? Does the whole thing settle into a market, an extortion racket, or a strangely honest tax, and can its failure modes be fixed without simply reintroducing a compulsory tax?
