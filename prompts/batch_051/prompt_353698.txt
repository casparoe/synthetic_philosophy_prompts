I keep running into the same loop-de-loop in alignment-adjacent discussions: one person says we don't need models to be understandable as long as they're right, another says uninterpretable competence is a loaded gun, and a third asks "OK, so what would 'legible' even require?" and the thread dies. Before I read another interpretability paper or transparency post, I want the spec nailed down. Give me the desiderata that a theory of making an AI's outputs (and/or the model itself) legible to humans has to satisfy. Not "transparent" as a vibe, but an actual list, with the case or consideration behind each item.

For each requirement say whether it's non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. Also flag any pair you think cant be fully satisfied at once, and say why. Then give me the single requirement that does the most work eliminating existing proposals — I suspect there's one that quietly rules out most of the stuff people gesture at. Keep the scorecard at the end short (say, mechanistic interpretability / circuits / SAEs, chain-of-thought / process supervision, "just ask the model," attention maps, debate) because the criteria are the part I actually want.

Some things I already suspect have to be in there, in case it helps: the difference between an explanation that tracks the real computation and one that merely rationalizes the output afterward; the question of who the legibility is for (expert, user, auditor); what to do when correct behavior isn't compressible into anything a human can hold in their head; and why a legibility standard can't just pass the hard part to a even smarter model. Push back on any of these you think is wrong or redundant.
