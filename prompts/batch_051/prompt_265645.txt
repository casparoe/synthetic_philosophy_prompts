Ok so here's my working definition of a promise, four conditions. (1) The utterance has to be addressed to someone who could accept or reject it. (2) It has to present the speaker as committing to a future act, not just predicting one. (3) The speaker has to intend the addressee to take it as committing, and has to take herself to be able to perform the act. (4) By saying it, the speaker undertakes an obligation or at least a responsibility she wouldn't otherwise have. Now a bank replaces its phone support with a chatbot. The bot is a language model fine-tuned on policy docs, has no persistent memory, and generates each turn fresh. A customer disputes a charge and the bot says, "I promise I'll reverse this charge by the end of the day." Its fine-tuning does route such disputes to a same-day reversal queue, so the bot only says this in cases that are about to be reversed. But the bot has no awareness of the queue, no self that persists to the end of the day, and it would say the same thing to anyone in the same state. Does the bot promise, or does the bank promise through the bot, or neither? Go condition by condition. Where my conditions force a verdict you dont like, say whether the conditions are wrong or the verdict is, and whether treating the bank as the promiser fixes it.
