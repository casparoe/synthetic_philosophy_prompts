Write a dialogue between two people at a development policy shop who are deciding whether a rural health program should make its funding conditional on recipients not giving gifts to local officials. One is a compliance officer who thinks the answer is obvious: clientelism is corruption, and "it's our culture" is the excuse local elites always trot out to protect their patronage networks. The other is an anthropologist who did fieldwork in the region and thinks the Western anti-corruption standard is a neocolonial import — there, giving an envelope of cash or a share of the harvest to the official who helped you isn't a bribe, it's how trust and reciprocal obligation work, and a blanket ban would wreck the relationships through which clinics get built. Write them as equally competent and good faith — no strawmen — and have them respond to each other's actual points rather than talking past each other. They should get into the weeds about whether it matters if the gift is given before or after the decision, whether an apparently voluntary exchange can still be coercive, whether impartiality toward strangers is itself a culturally specific value that's been mistaken for a universal one, and whether a practice can be both genuinely local and still lock poorer people out of public goods. Dont have them reach a neat resolution.
