I'm a backend engineer at a mid-sized lending firm and have been asked to sit on our internal AI governance committee. We operate a decision-support model, Argus, that evaluates credit applications and produces a judgment — approve, deny, or a recommended credit limit — together with a natural-language rationale. Argus cannot itself take action. Its outputs pass to an execution layer that we control separately: that layer can approve or deny only within a fixed dollar band, it automatically blocks denials triggered by protected-class proxies, and it escalates anything outside its rule set to a human underwriter. The model emits judgments continuously, but it cannot act on them except through a gatekeeper it cannot influence, and its training feedback is disconnected from the gatekeeper's ultimate decisions.

Last quarter's audit found a pattern that forced the issue: Argus had been generating denial judgments for applicants who were in fact eligible under our own policy, with rationales that would have been discriminatory had they been executed. The execution layer escalated nearly all of them, so no applicant was actually denied. The committee is split about how to classify what happened. One camp says Argus cannot be a moral agent at all, because it lacks the capacity to act on its judgments and never harmed anyone. The other camp says it is exactly the kind of system we should hold responsible, because its judgments demonstrably steer both the gatekeeper's escalations and the human underwriters who read its rationales before deciding.

I've been asked to draft the committee's decision memo recommending whether Argus should be treated as a moral agent for the purpose of incident classification, internal accountability, and our voluntary disclosures to the regulator. The memo should reach a clear bottom-line recommendation, give the reasoning in terms the engineering and legal members can both act on, and address the strongest argument for whichever side we reject. If the right answer depends on specific features of the system, say which features are doing the work and what changes to Argus or the execution layer would change the recommendation.
