Russell's paradox seems to show that naive set theory was too permissive: it allowed any property to define a set, and the property "does not contain itself" produced a contradiction. I understand that the standard repair is to replace naive set theory with something like ZFC, which restricts how sets can be formed. But I cannot shake the feeling that the repair is less principled than it looks. It does not ban self-reference outright, so why should I believe the contradiction will not simply reappear in a more elaborate form? More exactly: does the paradox establish that one particular axiom was bad, or that self-referential definitions are intrinsically unsafe? And if it establishes only the former, what makes ZFC's restrictions the right line to draw, rather than an ad hoc fence around a known problem that leaves the real issue — whatever it is — untouched?
