I work on AI systems, and I have come to think there is a relation we increasingly depend on that has no adequate name. Two cases keep recurring. In the first, a large model is trained on material that another model writes, selects, and orders: the second model decides what the first is exposed to next, and the human team checks only a thin sample, if anything. In the second, a system learns to act in a complex environment because another system scores its attempts and shapes the reward signal, so the standards it internalizes are inherited from another system rather than specified by hand. In both cases the shaping system is not a tool in any ordinary sense, since it supplies the examples, corrections, and criteria of success on which the other will later act unprompted. It is not a creator, since it does not fix the learner's architecture. It is not quite a supervisor, since after the shaping period the learner operates in situations the shaper never sees and cannot correct. The closest human analogy appears to be parenthood or guardianship: a temporary, asymmetric relation in which one party forms another's habits and standards before the second becomes independent. My question is whether this configuration already has an established name in philosophy, artificial intelligence, or a neighboring field. But I am less interested in the label than in the concept: if such a term exists, what precisely does it pick out, which neighboring relations (tool, teacher, peer, creator, guardian) does it resemble, and where exactly does it part company with each? If no adequate term exists, I would want a definition and clear boundary conditions — for instance, whether the relation requires the shaping system to adapt to the learner's current state, what it adds over simply calling the process training or optimization, and what would mark the difference between genuinely raising another system and merely filtering or curating its inputs.
