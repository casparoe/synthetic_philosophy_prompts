A friend in an AI policy group laid out an argument that seems to prove too much, and I haven't been able to locate the mistake. (1) A feature of a system is a root cause of a risk only if removing that feature while holding the rest of the system fixed would remove the risk. (2) Take the profit motive in AI development: remove it but keep national-security rivalry, scientific prestige, and engineers' drive to build, and the race to powerful systems would continue. (3) What holds for the profit motive holds for any candidate single feature: remove any one of them while holding the others fixed and the race remains. (4) So no single feature of the present system — not the profit motive, not anything else — is a root cause of AI risk. (5) But the same reasoning would show that no single feature of any complex system is ever a root cause of anything, since serious risks are usually held in place by several features at once. (6) That cannot be right. I would like to keep the claim that the profit motive is a root cause of AI risk, but this argument seems to force me to give it up. Tell me where the argument goes wrong; if it does not, tell me which premise is least costly to reject and what rejecting it commits me to.
