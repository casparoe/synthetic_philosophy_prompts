For a senior seminar I'm running on Madhyamaka, I want to hand students a rubric before they read five or six different interpretations of Nāgārjuna, so they can evaluate the interpretations instead of just absorbing whichever one they read first. Write me that rubric as a desiderata list: what does an account of emptiness have to deliver to count as a serious Madhyamaka position and not just dressed-up nihilism or a back door to some permanent ground. For each requirement, give the case, example, or argument that motivates it. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are only the preferences of particular camps (for instance, the camp that says a Madhyamaka thinker can't assert any thesis of their own versus the one that allows provisional commitments at the conventional level). Make sure the list covers the emptiness/nothingness distinction, how the two truths are allowed to relate, what the four-cornered pattern (the tetralemma: true, false, both, neither) is supposed to rule out, what the view does with dependent arising, and whether the account has to explain why emptiness itself doesn't become a new essence. Then flag any two or three requirements that you think can't all be satisfied at once, and say which single requirement does the most work to eliminate the popular misreadings. A table is fine. Please write in American English.
