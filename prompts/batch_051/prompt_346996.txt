I'm on the academic integrity board at a small college, and I've been asked to draft the decision memo for a case that's split us in a weirdly philosophical way. Our student conduct code says that "the student responsible for the unauthorized leak of an examination will be expelled." The facts are basically agreed: two students planned it together over text, one photographed the exam and the other uploaded the file, with each acting as lookout and cheerleader for the other, and under the code's own definition both count as responsible for the leak. There's no single student who is the responsible one and another who merely helped; it was a two-person operation. Half the commitee thinks the definite article is just boilerplate and the clause means whoever is responsible goes, so if two students are responsible, two students get expelled. The other half says "the student" carries a uniqueness condition—the code only authorizes expulsion when exactly one student is responsible, and with two we'd be inventing a punishment rule the code never states. The chair wants a short written opinion recommending a concrete decision: expel both under the clause, expel neither under it (they'd likely get probation under a separate general-conduct clause), or something else. Please draft the memo. It has to actually take the strongest version of the loosing side seriously rather than just restating our side, and it should end with a bottom line we can vote on.
