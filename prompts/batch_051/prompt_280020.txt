our film club is doing a session on Amadeus (the 1984 film) and i want to frame it around a question that keeps bugging me about biopics: what do we owe the real dead when we turn them into characters. the movie invents a huge amount about Salieri — makes him mozart's envious, scheming would-be murderer and has him confess the whole thing to a priest — which almost no historian accepts, so the film is itself a dramatization of a man telling an unreliable, self-aggrandizing story about someone else's life, which is exactly the thing the film does to Salieri. heres what i want: a reading of the film through that lens, not a fact-check. does the film, on its own terms, treat salieri as wronged by being turned into this character, or does it treat the dead as fair game for a good yarn? i want you to commit to one reading — a definite thesis with reasons, not a menu of interpretations. lead with the thesis in the first sentence and keep the whole thing under 500 words. and point me to a scene or two, or a structural choice, that supports your reading, plus one place where the film resists it.
