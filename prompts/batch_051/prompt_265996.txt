Hey, Im a journalist who covers science and ideas, and on the side I host a small podcast where I talk to philosophers about decision theory and that kind of thing. Next week's guest defends risk-weighted expected utility — the account associated with Lara Buchak, on which a risk-averse person is not just someone with a concave utility function, but someone whose weight on an outcome depends on whether worse outcomes were also on the table, through something called a risk function. He has heard every intro objection, so I don't want questions that let him restate the Allais paradox and sound clever. Could you draft me eight to ten interview questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the reply he is most likely to give, and write the whole thing in continuous prose like an email — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just paragraphs.

Here is what I want covered. One question should press on the worry that the risk function is too flexible, so that almost any choice pattern can be rationalized by finding some function that fits it after the fact; he will probably say that the function is meant to reflect an agent's real attitudes rather than to be fitted to data, and the follow-up should ask how a third party could ever tell the difference between a risk function that explains choices and one that is just a post-hoc excuse. Another should press on what gets lost when you give up the independence axiom: he tends to argue that independence assumes exactly the separability that risk-sensitive agents reject, because the overall shape of a gamble matters and a common branch is not really irrelevant. The follow-up should ask whether independence comes back as a consistency demand once choices are made over time, and whether he can avoid a money pump without going resolute. A third should push on rank-dependent utility and non-additive probabilities: if his risk-weighting can be rewritten as expected utility with unusual beliefs, has he really kept risk attitudes separate from beliefs or just relocated the weirdness into the probability side? If he says the two are mathematically equivalent but normatively different, the follow-up should ask how choice behavior alone is supposed to reveal which normative reading is correct. I'd also like a question about whether the risk function can shift with the size of the stakes: someone who is mildly risk-averse about retirement savings but will still buy the occasional lottery ticket looks like they need one function for small stakes and another for large stakes, so does the view allow that, and if not, how does he explain the stake-sensitivity of real Allais-style choices? And I want a question about the long run: he usually says a repeated gamble should be evaluated as one big gamble and the risk function applies to the whole bundle, so the follow-up should ask whether a risk-weighted agent who turns down a favorable gamble one at a time is then rationally forced to accept it when enough copies are bundled together.

After those, add two or three more questions in the same spirit, each with a follow-up tied to a specific likely answer, and end with a question about what evidence he would actually count against the view — not just inconvenient for it, but reason to abandon it. And remember: continuous prose, no formatting.
