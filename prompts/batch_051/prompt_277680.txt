I've been teaching Galileo's Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina this week, and I keep coming back to the way he uses a line from Augustine. He quotes Augustine as saying, "Now keeping always our respect for moderation in grave piety, we ought not to believe anything inadvisedly on a dubious point, lest in favor to our error we conceive a prejudice against something that truth hereafter may reveal to be not contrary in any way to the sacred books of either the Old or the New Testament." I'm less interested in the historical polemics than in the principle here. What are the most important implications of this principle for how a thoughtful person today should handle conflicts between contemporary scientific findings and a sacred text or authoritative doctrine they hold? I don't mean the answer "science wins" or "scripture wins"—I want the implications worked out. Does the principle require different responses when scientific evidence is genuinely demonstrative versus merely probable? Does it demand that a person who lacks the training to evaluate a demonstration refrain from condemning either side? What does it imply about wether an apparent conflict should be treated as a reason to suspend judgment, reinterpret the text, or revisit the science?
