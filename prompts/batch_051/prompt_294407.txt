Me and my fiancée have been having the same fight for over a year now, and I honestly can't tell if we're disagreeing about morality or about what even gets to count as a reason. Her family kept all these food and body rules and defended them with words like 'sacred' and 'unclean'—she's not religious anymore, but the instincts are deep. I was raised to think a thing is only a moral matter if it involves harm, coercion, or rights. So when she says eating a dog we raised and loved would be a kind of desecration even if the dog died of old age and no one was hurt, I hear 'that's just conditioning talking,' and she hears me as dismissing something real. The weird part: we agree on all the facts, and on most of our other moral views, but the argument always bottoms out at me saying disgust and violated sacredness are exactly the feelings to distrust until they can be translated into harm, and her saying those feelings are themselves a form of moral perception, not a bug. It's stopped being academic because we're getting married in the fall and the question of what rules we'd raise kids with keeps re-opening it. My working theory is that the disagreement isn't about which values matter more, but about what's allowed into the moral conversation as data at all—like we have different admissibility rules before any weighing starts. Is that the right diagnosis? If so, is this a substantive disagreement or are we just speaking different languages, and is there anything—a distinction, a better question, some line of arguement—that could actually move one of us, or should I accept it's bedrock?
