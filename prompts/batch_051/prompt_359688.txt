I'm in sixth form and I run a lunchtime RE/philosophy club, and we've been going round and round about hell for weeks. Some people are annihilationists, some are universalists, one lad is a proper fire-and-brimstone type, and everyone reckons they're the consistent one. I want to make a quiz for the club called 'what do you actually believe about hell?' — it should sort people by their answers to cases, not by which label they fancy.

The theme should be the proportionality of infinite punishment, cos thats the bit we keep getting stuck on. So build the questions around things like: whether punishment is meant to pay someone back, to fix them, or to respect their choice; whether a finite life of sin can ever deserve never-ending torment; whether it's more merciful to annihilate someone than to keep them alive and suffering forever; and whether God's love or his justice gets the last word.

Make it eight to ten questions, each a short concrete case — a defiant petty thief, a mass murderer who dies unrepentant, a remorseful person who dies before they can apologise, someone who would never repent no matter how long they were given, that sort of thing — with three answer options each. Then give me a scoring key that maps the answer patterns onto at least these positions: eternal conscious torment / traditional hell, annihilationism / conditional immortality, universal salvation, and hopeful universalism. If some patterns end up empty or tied, just say so and explain what the awkward commitment would be.

Dont just ask 'do you think hell is forever?' — I want it to catch what people actually think about cases. Also the club has a few people who aren't religious, so phrase everything so an atheist can answer about what they'd find just or fair, not just about what scripture says.
