I keep trying to pin down what it actually means to care about somebody, as opposed to just doing caring-looking things. After a lot of thinking I've come up with four things real care seems to need. (1) It's tuned to the particular person — you notice what she specifically needs, not just what "a person in that situation" needs. (2) You're emotionally caught up in it: if things go badly you'll actually be upset, not just think "too bad." (3) You do it for her sake, not because you promised, not because you'd look bad, not because you'd feel guilty if you didn't. And (4) it keeps going even in the stretches where it's boring or thankless.

Here's the case I can't settle. A guy looks after his mother, who has dementia, for four years. He learns her routines, tracks her meds, figures out which radio station calms her down when she gets restless in the evenings, all of it. But he has never felt love for her; they weren't close, and he's still angry about parts of his childhood. He says he does it because nobody else will and because he couldnt live with himself if he put her in a home. He describes his own manner as "more orderly than warm." When someone asks whether he cares about his mother, he says: "I take care of her. I don't know whether I care about her." Another relative puts it like this: "She's being cared for, but not by someone who cares about her."

Does this guy's relationship to his mother qualify as caring? Go through my four criteria one at a time and give a clear yes/no overall — don't just lay out options. Where something doesn't fit, tell me whether the problem is in my criteria, in the case, or both, and whether the case shows my criteria need changing. Also say whether the son himself should care about the answer, or whether the whole "taking care of vs. caring about" distinction is beside the point here. Commit to a definite verdict, even if it means biting a bullet.
