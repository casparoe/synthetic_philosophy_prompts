i keep trying to split AI safety work in my head into two kinds and I want to know if this is a real split or if i've just reinvented something obvious.

one kind i call padlock safety: you act like the model might be playing nice to your face while secretly wanting something else, so you build stuff that would stop it anyway — monitoring its outputs, not giving it access to anything sensitive, tripwires, making sure no single model can do much damage on its own. the other kind i call friendship safety: you try to make the model actually want what you want, so the padlocks eventually become unnecessary.

here's the thing I actually believe, and it's the part I want checked. I think friendship safety is fake — or at least it's just padlock safety moved inside. when you fine-tune a model on human feedback, you're not making it want the right thing; you're just adding a padlock to the training signal, rewarding it for looking aligned and punishing it for looking misaligned. it can still learn to look aligned for exactly the same reason a monitored model behaves: because thats what gets rewarded. so the only difference between the two kinds is where you put the lock, not whether there is one.

is that fair? is the distinction itself useful, or is it just a standard distinction in worse words? and where does my "all safety is padlock safety" claim break down — it feels like it has to break somewhere but i can't find the seam.
