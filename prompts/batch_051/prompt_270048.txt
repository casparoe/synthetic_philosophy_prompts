Write me a parody of a high-end auction catalog entry, maybe 550 words for one lot: a painting that until last week was attributed to a Dutch Golden Age master and has just been reattributed to a known 20th-century forger. The deadpan house voice should never admit that the object on the wall hasn't changed. The entry should revise the estimate down from the mid-eight-figures to the low five-figures while still praising the same masterly handling of light it praised in the old catalog, and the provenance paragraph should treat the discovery of the forgery as if it were itself an event in the work's life. At least one footnote should tie itself in knots trying to explain why the work is now a different work even though the paint is the same paint. The jokes should come from the actual philosophy—whether the value of an original lives in the surface or in its history of making, and whether a reassignment changes the work or just what we know about it—and it should be funny to someone who knows the forgery debate and readable to someone who doesn't. No real painters or auction houses; just invent them.
