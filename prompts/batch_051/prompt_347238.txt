I sit on the grants committee of a small international health NGO that funds maternal health programs in a country I'd rather not name. One of our best-performing partners is a community group run by a local councillor. The problem is that he runs the project partly as a political machine. At the end of each prenatal workshop, every woman gets a bar of soap and a transport voucher, but only after one of his party workers gives a five-minute speech about how the councillor secured the grant and how much more he'll do for the district if he is re-elected. The workshops still happen, attendance is high, and our health outcomes in that district are the best in the region. Separately, our local program officer, who is from that country, received a goat from the councillor when her father died. She disclosed it herself and says refusing it would have been a serious insult.

Compliance wants to suspend the grant, claw back the portion of funds spent on party-linked events, and refer the councillor to the national anti-corruption commission, with reference to our policy against gifts and partisan use of funds. I think that is too quick, but I cannot yet say why without sounding like I am apologising for corruption. The committee meets on Thursday.

Could you draft the short written opinion I will circulate? It should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame, and it should honestly engage the strongest argument on the other side. Compliance's strongest point is that we should not romanticize clientelism just because it is woven into local politics; I want the memo to take that seriously rather than dismiss the policy as Western moralising. Most of all, I want the memo to spell out a workable standard that distinguishes: the goat, the soap-and-voucher distribution, the councillor using the project motorcycle for party rallies on weekends (which he also does), and the hypothetical of him simply taking twenty percent of the grant as cash. If we are going to call something corruption, I want to know exactly what property of the act makes it corruption, not just that it violates a clause in our policy.
