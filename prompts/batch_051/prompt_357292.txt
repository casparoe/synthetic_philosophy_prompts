As I understand the passage, he says something like: people only praise justice because they're weak and scared, and if you gave a just person and an unjust person the same power to avoid detection, their choices would be indistinguishable. Then he tells the story of the ancestor of Gyges who finds a ring that makes him invisible, and uses it to seduce the queen, kill the king, and take over the kingdom. The conclusion is supposed to be that no one is just willingly; justice is just a compromise we accept because we can't get away with injustice.

I've been defending a modernized version of this view in my reading group, and I'd like you to stress-test it for me. My version is something like: moral norms are ultimately reputation-management and sanction-avoidance, plus a little bit of internalized habit. If you could make someone genuinely impossible to detect, with no reputational cost, no punishment, and no public record of what they did, then the question "should I do this?" collapses into "can I get away with this?" I'm not saying everyone would do horrific things, just that the remaining reasons not to would be non-moral: they'd be aesthetic, or neurotic, or self-image stuff, or fear of some unknown.
