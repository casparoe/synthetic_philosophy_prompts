In The Subjection of Women, John Stuart Mill argues that when people invoke the “nature” of women to justify their subordinate position, they are usually treating what is customary as if it were natural. Yet he also maintains that we cannot know what women’s nature truly is until women have been allowed the same freedom of development as men. Is Mill contradicting himself by appealing to a truer nature that would emerge only under equality? Or does his critique of appeals to nature leave no room for any notion of a knowable human nature—and if so, what does his equal-liberty argument actually show?
