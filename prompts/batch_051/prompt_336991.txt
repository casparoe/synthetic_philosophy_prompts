A third student objected that this was a rule of thumb about consequences, not Kant at all: Kant's universalizability test, she insisted, is about whether the agent's maxim can be conceived or willed as a universal law without contradiction, not about what would actually happen if everyone copied the behaviour. The dispute then stalled over what "what if everyone did that?" is even claiming, and I now suspect the sentence hides several different tests that we were all running together.

I would like you to act as a specialist in Kant's practical philosophy and in modern readings of the universalizability tests. For each reading, tell me whether it would actually support the conclusion that one ought to vote, and which of the three people in the seminar, if any, it vindicates. I also want one or two concrete non-moral test cases in which these readings come apart, so my group can see which version each of us was actually using without the election dispute muddying the water.
