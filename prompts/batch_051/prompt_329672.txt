I have been turning over a parallel between reinforcement learning and a question in the theory of well-being, and I want to know whether it is a genuine structural point or a loose analogy.

On the philosophical side, consider whether variety or novelty is a component of well-being. One view treats novelty as purely instrumental: it matters only because it prevents hedonic adaptation, reveals what you like, trains capacities, or opens future options. On this view, an ideally informed and stable person choosing between a uniform life and an otherwise equally pleasant but more varied life has no well-being reason to prefer the varied one. A rival view treats variety as a final good, so that the varied life is better in itself even when the uniform life would contain the same total enjoyment and no additional information would be gained by switching.

On the engineering side, reinforcement-learning agents are usually built to maximize a reward signal that defines the task. Because naive exploitation gets stuck, many algorithms add a novelty or exploration bonus to reward visits to unfamiliar states, but the bonus is understood as an auxiliary device outside the true objective, a way to improve search rather than part of what the task is about. One could instead build novelty directly into the task's reward function, in which case an agent that already knows the environment perfectly would still prefer varied trajectories, much as the rival theory of well-being says an informed agent would prefer a varied life.

The structural claim I want checked is this: reward function is to well-being as exploration bonus is to variety-as-instrumental, and policy is to a life. If the parallel is deep, then arguments for or against intrinsic exploration bonuses in reinforcement learning ought to transfer to debates about whether novelty is a component of human welfare; if it is superficial, something in the mapping gives way, perhaps because a scalar reward is a poor model of well-being, or because exploration bonuses presuppose uncertainty about the environment while the ethical question remains live under full information. Could you work out exactly where the mapping holds and where it breaks, say what would have to be true on both sides for a result to transfer, and identify one thing the parallel seems to predict that turns out false? Please also indicate how confident you are in what you claim and which point you are least sure of.
