so our school has this award, the Compass Award for Ethical Conduct, that the student-faculty character committee (I'm a junior rep on it) votes on every spring. The guidelines say it's for a student "whose actions in a genuinely difficult situation show integrity and moral character, not just outward good behavior." This year there's a senior, call her Dana, who found a wallet with $180, a debit card, and a phone in the gym locker room after a home meet. No one saw her find it, and she turned it in at the front office within the hour. By itself that's exactly the sort of thing the award exists for. But when the committee talked to people, three details came out: she told a friend later that she mostly returned it because the hallway right outside has a camera and she wasn't about to get caught over a wallet; she accepted the $50 reward the family offered and spent it that same weekend; and she asked her guidance counselor to log the return as service hours for college apps, calling it a smart thing for her file. Nobody's disputing she did the right thing with someone else's stuff.

Our commitee is split. One side says the award is for conduct, and Dana's conduct was excellent—motives are private and messy, and if we start trying to read souls we'll end up penalizing people for being honest out loud about what everyone privately thinks. The other side says the guidelines are about character, and doing the right thing because you ran a cost-benefit analysis (camera + reward + service hours) is not the same as doing it for the right reasons; giving her the award for that would turn "moral character" into "good risk management." A smaller group wants to deny the Compass Award but send her a formal letter of commendation for the act itself, so we're really deciding among three options.

I keep going back and forth because the guidelines sound like they're about the difference between doing the right thing and doing it for the right reasons, and I'm not sure a committee can ever know that. I have to vote anyway, and I want a draft memo to work from. Write the committee's decision memo to the principal with these sections in order: "Summary" (two sentences, one states the decision), "What the award criteria commit us to" (one short paragraph), "The case for giving her the award" (one solid paragraph), "The case against" (one solid paragraph, including the strongest point from the side you don't pick), and "Recommendation" (short paragraph with a clear yes/no/commendation-only verdict plus any conditions). Keep it 500–700 words.
