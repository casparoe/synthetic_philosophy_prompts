"Bostrom's work on infinite ethics shows that if the universe is infinite and contains infinitely many people with lives worth living and infinitely many people with lives not worth living, then the total value of the universe is an infinite sum of positive and negative numbers, and that sum is undefined. Even if we charitably supposed the universe contained only infinitely many positive lives, the total would just be positive infinity; any finite action — curing one disease, torturing one innocent person — adds or subtracts only a finite amount, and infinity plus or minus a finite amount is still infinity. So total utilitarianism is forced to say that saving a life is exactly as good as doing nothing, and exactly as good as torturing one person. Since that is absurd, consequentialism in general — not merely classical utilitarianism — is refuted unless the universe turns out to be finite. The only live options are to abandon consequentialism or to insist on a finite universe, which is just wishful thinking."
