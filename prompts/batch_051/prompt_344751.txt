So I just got back from a two-hour argument with a friend about coincidences, and I couldnt sell the point in a way that stuck. Her thing is that a pile of weird stuff keeps happening to her—meeting a stranger on vacation who went to her tiny high school, hearing the same obscure song twice in one day—and she reads it as evidence that something is going on. I kept coming back to the birthday paradox: in a room of 23 people the odds of some pair sharing a birthday are better than even, even though any particular pair feels like 1/365. Her response was basically "cool, now do my actual coincidences." And I realized the part I couldn't get across wasn't the math; it was that the number of unnoticed opportunities is what turns a "no way" coincidence into something expected, and that the anecdote itself is usually the survivor of a giant pile of non-coincidences nobody tells you about. That plus the fact that the coincidence is only described after it happens, so the telling gets to pick out exactly the features that make it look impossible.

I run a college "Reasoning about Evidence" workshop, about 25 students, one 75-minute session. I want a classroom activity for this that actually makes people feel that point in their gut, not just nod at it. What they should leave seeing: (1) their gut estimate of "what are the odds" is way off because they don't count the opportunities, and (2) a vivid coincidence story is mostly a story about selection—the teller and the room are seeing the one that hit, not all the ones that didn't.

Design me a runnable session: rules, materials, something with points or stakes that doesn't need real money but still stings a little when you lose, a timeline for the 75 minutes, and a debrief that maps the moments in the game onto the ideas. I want at least one moment where a participant tells a genuinely goosebump-y coincidence and the room feels the pull to treat it as evidence, and then the group has to rebuild the size of the search space that produced it—the lesson should come out of that, not from me explaining it. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how I should handle it on the spot. Be honest about which feature of real-life coincidence the activity can't reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself in the wrap-up.
