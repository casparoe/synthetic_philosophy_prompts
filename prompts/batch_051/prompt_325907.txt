So my buddy and I have been going back and forth about this for like two days and I'm still not sure who has the better argument. I do design work for a social app (not saying which) and there's a clause in our partner policy I'm stuck on. Here's the actual wording:

"Partners must not design or deploy interfaces, prompts, or features that trick or pressure users into taking actions they would not otherwise choose, or that exploit habitual, automatic, or emotional responses in order to hold users' attention longer than they would otherwise give it."

The feature we shipped is a little digital plant next to each of your conversations. Every chat gets one, and the plant visibly wilts unless someone in that chat sends a message at least once every 48 hours. That's the whole mechanic. There's no deception — the rules are shown up front, the plant is fully transparent about what it's doing, and users can see exactly how it works. But the retention numbers are great: people come back daily because they hate watching the thing die, and a lot of users say they feel genuinely obligated to "water" their chats even though they know the plant is just pixels on a server. My friend used to work in policy and says its obvious the clause was written after all those dark-pattern scandals — pre-checked boxes, disguised ads, fake countdown timers — and the drafters meant hidden tricks. He says a plant you can ignore with zero real-world consequence can't count as tricking or pressuring anyone. I say the second half of the clause, the "habitual, automatic, or emotional responses" part, is doing independent work, and if a feature that exploits loss aversion to keep people in the app longer than they planned doesn't trigger it then nothing does.

So my question is: does the plant breach this clause? Walk through the main readings — the plain words, the purpose behind the clause, what the drafters probably would have said if someone had shown them the feature before the policy shipped, and what a reasonable partner reading the policy for the first time would expect it to ban. Which reading should control here, and does it matter that the users know they're being worked on? Also, I can't shake the feeling that if the clause reaches the plant it might have to reach the red notification badge and the like button too, and that can't be what anyone meant. Is there a coherent line between them, or does that show the clause was only ever able to ban hidden tricks and we should just admit the plant is fine?
