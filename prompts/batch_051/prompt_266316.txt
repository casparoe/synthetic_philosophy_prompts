I'm giving a paper next month at a political philosophy workshop, and I want a Q&A prep sheet. My thesis is that the standard conditions for valid actual consent—voluntariness, adequate information, and the power to rescind—make consent accounts of political obligation collapse into either philosophical anarchism or an unacknowledged natural-duty view, but that the same conditions, applied to a democratic legal order, still license a defeasible right of civil disobedience when lawmaking predictably excludes a class of affected persons. The audience will be mainstream normative political philosophers and constitutional theorists with liberal-democratic commitments; no one will defend anarchism, but several will worry that I have quietly become one. I have been trying to apply the rationalist habit of hunting for post hoc rationalization to my own position, and I keep coming back to this passage from Hume's Of the Original Contract (Essays, Moral, Political, and Literary, Part II, Essay XII):

"The one party, by tracing up government to the DEITY, endeavour to render it so sacred and inviolate, that it must be little less than sacrilege, however tyrannical it may become, to touch or invade it, in the smallest article. The other party, by founding government altogether on the consent of the PEOPLE, suppose that there is a kind of original contract, by which the subjects have tacitly reserved the power of resisting their sovereign, whenever they find themselves aggrieved by that authority, with which they have, for certain purposes, voluntarily entrusted him."

Draft my prep sheet: the eight or so objections I will actually get from this audience, ordered by likely damage, each phrased the way a mainstream liberal legal theorist would put it. For each, give a two-minute reply that does not retreat to anarchism, and note the place where the reply is softest. Use the Hume passage explicitly to show where an objection is doing what Hume describes—rearing up a speculative principle to protect a prior practical loyalty—and where my own reply risks doing the same. End with the question I am hoping nobody asks.
