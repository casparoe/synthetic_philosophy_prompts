I run a monthly ethics discussion group, and next month we're doing immigration. The thing I keep noticing is that the usual labels are doing almost no work: two people will both call themselves "pro-open-borders" and then completely disagree about whether a country may cap long-term work visas, whether a child born to unauthorized migrants should get citizenship, or whether an investor visa is fine if the money funds schools and clinics. I'd like a "which position on the right to exclude do you actually hold?" quiz to hand out before the session: eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions. The audience is undergrads and a few grad students, so the questions shouldn't use academic jargon, and no question should just ask "do you identify as an open-borders person?"—sort by what people say about cases, not by which label they like.

Please build the quiz from the logical space rather than from a list of labels. The distinctions I especially want it to track are: (1) whether the right to exclude is grounded in collective association/self-determination or in jurisdiction over a territory, and whether that difference shows up in policy; (2) whether entry and settlement come apart (so someone could favor fairly open temporary movement but tight limits on permanent membership); (3) whether actual states' territorial claims are tainted enough by conquest or theft that no existing state has a clean right to exclude; (4) whether a border regime can be legitimate even if its enforcement is discriminatory or routinely brutal; and (5) whether coercive exclusion is only permissible when the excluded person has a decent alternative elsewhere. Concretely, I want cases like: climate-displaced people from low-lying islands; a guest-worker program with renewable visas but no path to citizenship; daily cross-border commuting; investor citizenship; children born to unauthorized migrants; and a pandemic border closure. Leaves should include at least: a strict sovereignty view; a liberal nationalist view with strong duties to refugees; a global egalitarian/open borders view; a no-border or abolitionist view; a "right to stay" view (exclusion of someone already present is much harder to justify than refusal of entry); a guest-worker cosmopolitan view (open movement of labor but not settlement); and a "tainted title" view. Where a combination of answers has no one defending it, say so and briefly diagnose whether it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored, and flag any two leaves your questions can't seperate because the real disagreement sits at the level of theory. Use the concrete cases above (you can add one or two if you need to), and end the response with a line beginning "Summary:" giving a one-sentence statement of the deepest split the quiz is actually tracking.
