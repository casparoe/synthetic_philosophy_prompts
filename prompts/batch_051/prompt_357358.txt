Assume the following working criteria for a belief's being self-locating. A belief is self-locating to the degree that:

1. Indexical content. Its truth can differ at two locations within a single possible world — two times, two places, or two individuals — because the belief includes at least one de se or de nunc element such as I, here, or now.

2. Centered evidence sensitivity. It is the kind of belief whose rational probability is sensitive to evidence that rules out no possible world, only some centers within each world (for example, evidence of the form, "of the two waking episodes in this protocol, this is the first").

3. Disagreement without worldly disagreement. Two agents, or a single agent at two times, can rationally assign it different probabilities while assigning identical probabilities to every uncentered proposition — that is, while agreeing on how the world is, independently of where each of them is in it.

Now take the Sleeping Beauty case. A subject is put to sleep on Sunday. A fair coin is tossed. If it lands Heads, she will be woken on Monday only; if Tails, she will be woken on Monday and again on Tuesday. The drug protocol makes each waking subjectively indistinguishable from the others, and she knows all this in advance. During each waking she is asked for her credence in the proposition that the coin landed Heads; call it H.

H is a natural borderline case for the criteria above. Its surface form contains no I, here, or now, and it appears to be true or false once and for all within a world, depending only on the coin toss. Yet the Sleeping Beauty puzzle is often introduced as a puzzle about self-locating belief, and the debate over Beauty's credence in H turns entirely on how to handle the indexical information that her present experience is compatible with more than one waking episode.

Work through H against the three criteria. Does H count as self-locating? Which criteria does it clearly meet, which does it clearly fail, and where do the criteria leave the question underdetermined? If H fails most criteria, say whether we should conclude that the Sleeping Beauty case is not fundamentally about self-locating belief but about self-locating evidence and its bearing on a non-self-locating proposition; or whether the criteria themselves need a fourth condition capturing beliefs that are not indexical in content but whose rational evaluation is tangled with self-locating possibilities. End with a verdict on whether the three criteria should be revised, and if so how.
