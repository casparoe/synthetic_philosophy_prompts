I am a novelist by training and disposition, and the vocabulary I trust most is the vocabulary of craft: outlining versus discovery writing, authorial intent, foreshadowing, revision, and the familiar sensation that a well-drawn character has acquired a will of her own and is resisting the plot. I have long suspected that the theological debate over predestination and free will is, at bottom, a dispute about authorship. Restate the positions of Augustine, Calvin, and Arminius on grace, election, and free will in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, without pretending the translation is cleaner than it is. Then give me the residue report: which claims carry over neatly, which carry over only with distortion, and which have no counterpart at all in how a novelist thinks about novels. Above all, tell me whether the author–character relation is a genuine model of divine and human agency, or whether it mainly flatters my craft by making the theologians sound like novelists.
