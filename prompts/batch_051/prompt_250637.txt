I was taught to assess the validity of consent by three conditions: the person must be competent; the person must be adequately informed; and the agreement must be voluntary, where voluntariness is understood as the absence of coercion, deception, and undue influence. Now consider this case. A clinic uses a machine-learning system to personalise the way treatment options are presented to each patient. The system has been trained on data about the patient's browsing habits, purchase history, and responses to earlier questionnaires, and it has learnt which framings are most likely to secure this particular patient's agreement. It never lies, threatens, or withholds any material fact: every option and every risk is disclosed somewhere in the conversation. But the wording, ordering, and emotional emphasis are chosen to exploit the patient's known decision-making vulnerabilities. The patient is competent, has no condition impairing judgement, and signs the consent form. Does this patient's consent meet the three conditions? Work through each condition in turn. Where the case falls between the conditions, say whether the conditions themselves need to be revised, or whether the case points to something the conditions have missed.
