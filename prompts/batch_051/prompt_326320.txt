Please explain precisely what is right and what is misleading in the slogan that evolution is a stupid optimizer. Do not settle for the vague point that evolution is not goal-directed; I want the machinery. Organize the explanation around four specific disanalogies between natural selection and gradient-based optimization, and for each say what work it does and does not do for the slogan: (1) the search space and the level at which selection acts (heritable variants in a population, expressed through development, rather than parameters in a fixed model class); (2) the objective function (fitness is environment-relative, frequency-dependent, and non-stationary, whereas gradient descent typically follows a fixed loss, and there is no scalar quantity that natural selection improves monotonically or maximizes without serious qualifications); (3) the update rule (selection filters variation that arose by mutation and recombination before the selection step, with no gradient information and no backward credit-assignment signal from phenotype to genotype comparable to backpropagation); and (4) the guarantees available (gradient descent offers local decrease of a fixed loss and, under stated conditions, convergence results, while natural selection offers no analogous guarantee about mean fitness). Then flip the question and explain what gradient descent can and cannot learn from the evolutionary analogy: address population-based search, mutation-like exploration to escape local minima, and environmental change as an implicit curriculum, and say where the analogy begins to do too much work. Conclude by stating the conditions under which calling evolution a stupid optimizer is more true than false, and the conditions under which it is the wrong lesson for machine learning.
