Since a close family member came out of a long surgery and described the lost hours as not being like anything, yet also reported a lingering sense that she had somehow been "there," I have found myself returning to a view I used to dismiss: illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. The specific claim I now want to know how to assess is this: there is no phenomenal property — no "what-it-is-likeness" over and above a state's causal and functional role plus the system's disposition to judge and report that such a property is present. The impression of being directly acquainted with a private, qualitative show is a systematic introspective misrepresentation, not a reliable report of something real. Design the best concrete test or measurement you can of that claim. Spell out the experiment, intervention, or set of observations you would use, state exactly what result would count as evidence for illusionism and what result would count as evidence against it (that is, for realism about phenomenal properties), and then say what the test could not settle even if the result came out as cleanly as one could hope.
