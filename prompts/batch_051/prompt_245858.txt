Question: Explain the distinction between positive and negative retributivism. Does negative retributivism give a positive reason to punish?

Student's answer: Positive retributivism says that an offender's desert is itself a positive reason to punish: we should punish because, and to the degree that, the offender deserves it. Negative retributivism says desert only constrains punishment: we may punish only the guilty, and only in proportion to what they deserve. But since negative retributivism permits punishing the guilty, it still supplies a defeasible positive reason to punish, because a permission entails a reason. That's why most negative retributivists think the state should punish even when no good consequences would come of it. So the real difference is just that negative retributivism also adds a consequentialist justification.
