The debate I keep coming back to is whether sexual exclusivity in a committed relationship is a moral requirement or just a convention. My old labmate insists it's basically a coordination convention, like which side of the road you drive on, and I want to say no, something changes once people make promises — but whenever I try to say why, I reach for commitment strategies and equilibrium selection, and I suspect I might be changing the subject.

What I'd like is an honest, systematic translation. Restate the main positions in that debate — at least the "mere convention" view and the "promissory obligation" view, plus any other major positions you think matter — in the vocabulary of behavioural ecology and evolutionary game theory, the way I'd actually have used it. For each one, say what carries over without strain, what has to be bent to fit, and what has no counterpart at all in that vocabulary. Then give me the residue report: are the untranslatable pieces where the real moral content sits, or are they places where the philosophical debate is running on fuzzy words? In particular, I'd like to know whether "convention" in the philosopher's sense lines up with any well-defined thing in game theory — an arbitrary coordination equilibrium, a Nash equilibrium, a norm kept in place by punishment — and whether promissory obligation can be restated as a kind of commitment device, or whether that just talks about something else.

One more wrinkle: in the philosophy I've been reading, people seperate a norm that's merely enforced by third-party punishment from a rule that's genuinely moral. From inside my old toolkit those can look like the same equilibrium with different enforcement costs. I want the translation to tell me whether that collapse is illuminating or whether it's exactly the kind of thing the framework loses.
