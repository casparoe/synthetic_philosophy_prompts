Design a small voting example that exposes a problem for the claim that "the will of the people" is a single latent fact that elections merely reveal. Fix one electorate, three alternatives (X, Y, Z), and complete strict-ranked ballots from every voter. Apply three aggregation rules to the same ballots: plurality (the alternative ranked first by most voters wins), Borda count (first place earns 2 points, second earns 1, third earns 0; highest total wins), and pairwise majority rule (an alternative is the Condorcet winner only if a majority ranks it above each rival). Construct the ballot distribution so that the three rules yield three different verdicts: plurality picks one alternative, Borda picks a second, and pairwise majority picks no single winner because the majority preference ordering cycles. Use at most twelve voters, no abstentions, no strategic voting, no tied first-place counts, no tied Borda totals, and no tied pairwise majorities. After presenting the ballots, show that each specification is met by computing all three verdicts on the same data. Then identify the structural feature of the case that does the work, say what would happen to the example if that feature were removed, and address this objection: a defender of the populist idea might insist that exactly one aggregation method (or a deliberative refinement of majority rule) tracks the people's true will. Give the strongest version of that reply and assess whether the example disarms it.
