"Camus starts from the idea that the universe gives us no reasons, values, or purposes; it's silent and indifferent. But we can't help demanding reasons and meaning anyway. The absurd is just that clash between our demand and the universe's refusal. Once you notice it, Camus says there are three options: suicide, a leap of faith (what he calls philosophical suicide), or revolt. Suicide is wrong because it deletes one side of the tension instead of facing it; the leap of faith is wrong because it pretends the universe answers us after all. His preferred option, revolt, means living without appeal: you give up hoping for cosmic meaning and instead give your life whatever meaning you choose. So for Camus meaning is subjective and invented. If stamp collecting feels meaningful to you, then your life is exactly as meaningful as a doctor's, because there's no fact of the matter about which projects matter. The only thing he insists on is that you experience your chosen project as meaningful and keep it up in a spirit of defiance."
