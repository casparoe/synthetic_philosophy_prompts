I recently joined the governing board of the small congregation my family has belonged to for the past two years, and almost immediately I was placed on the committee that plans the order of service. The committee has now been deadlocked for three meetings on a single question, and I have been asked to write the recommendation the whole board will vote on. For the past several months the service has occasionally ended with a member coming forward, outside the printed order, to give a short account of something they believe God has done in their life that season. A number of people want to make this a fixed practice: a quarterly report of providence before the final hymn, open to any member who asks. One group on the committee holds that corporate worship may contain only what Scripture commands, and because such a report is nowhere commanded, making it a standing part of the service would be unlawful. The other group holds that worship is lawful whenever it is not forbidden by Scripture, and Scripture does not forbid a member from briefly testifying to God's work; on their view the placement and time limit of the report are merely incidental arrangements of how worship is ordered, like the hour of the service or the choice of a tune, which every congregation settles by good judgment. Both sides agree that whatever we decide will set a precedent for every later proposal to add something to the service. I cannot tell whether we are really disagreeing about the practice itself, about how the practice should be classified, or about the underlying rule.

Please work through the competing rules as they apply to this case and give me a definite recommendation: should we adopt the quarterly report as a standing practice, reject it, or adopt some third policy? Since I will have to defend the recommendation before the board, please also say explicitly which argument or consideration, if it turned out differently, would change your verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
