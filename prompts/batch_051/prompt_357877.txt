A friend who works in AI keeps ending disagreements about machine consciousness with a line he treats as decisive: "Gödel showed that no machine can be self-aware." His compressed argument runs as follows: any machine is a formal system; Gödel showed that any sufficiently rich formal system contains truths it cannot prove and cannot prove its own consistency; a machine therefore cannot fully survey or justify the very reasoning by which it produces its outputs; and genuine self-awareness requires being able to do just that. I suspect the clean mathematical theorem cannot carry that conclusion by itself, and I want a sharper account of where the chain weakens. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims this slogan might express — about what counts as a machine, about what a Gödel sentence actually demonstrates, about what self-awareness would require — and for each say whether it is true, false, or merely an analogy being asked to do too much work, and which reading, if any, would actually deliver his conclusion?
