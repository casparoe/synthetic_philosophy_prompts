I'm writing a novel where the protagonist moves between a handful of cities, and mid-draft I noticed I keep having her do whatever the locals around her do — wait at the light, tip, queue — and I realized I should figure out whether that's because *I* think it's what she ought to do. So I wrote down where I've landed, and I'd like to know whether the whole package hangs together:

(1) I think everyday conventions can be genuine moral obligations, not just manners. Skipping a queue in Dublin is wrong, not merely annoying, and a tourist in New York who stiffs the server on the tip because the whole practice feels silly to them is doing something wrong too.

(2) But I also think the reason these things bind is that they solve a real problem — the queue allocates a scarce good fairly, and the tip props up a wage system the restaurant has already priced into the cost of the meal. That's why I don't think purely arbitrary conventions, like which fork you hold, create any obligation at all.

(3) And yet I think a visitor has to follow the local convention even if they're only in town for a week, get no long-run benefit from the scheme, and will never see any of these people again. The local practice is the one in force, full stop.

(4) I also think genuine ignorance excuses, at least the first time: if a tourist really has no idea that tipping works a particular way where they are, I can't call them blameworthy for there first undertip.

(5) But I also think that by the time you're standing in front of a queue, you can't really claim not to know what it's for — the fact that people are waiting their turn is right there in front of you — so the ignorance excuse I just granted barely applies to queue-jumping.

(6) And I think it's fine to cross against the light in a city where everyone does it, while in a city where locals wait I'd wait even at 3am with no cars, because what matters is the living local practice, not what the sign says. That one feels consistent to me, but I realize it can look like I'm saying the same act is wrong on one street and fine on another.

So: is this a consistent set? If it isn't, tell me which commitments pull against which, ordered by how bad the tension is, and what the cheapest fix would be in each case.
