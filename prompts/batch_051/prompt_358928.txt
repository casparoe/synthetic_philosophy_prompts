I've landed on a deflationary reading of the cogito: all it really establishes is that some thinking is going on right now — it doesn't, by itself, get you to a thinker who persists through time, a mental substance, or even a unified self that owns the thought. I defend this reading pretty hard. Treat it as my position and tell me what would count against it.

What I want isn't just the standard back-and-forth. I want a map of the view's exposure. Which arguments, thought experiments, or results in the philosophy of mind and language should actually move me? For each, say whether it's fatal, seriously damaging, or merely awkward, and whether a committed deflationist would grant that it counts as a defeater or dismiss it as missing the point. Be sure to hit the obvious stress points: whether "I think" is better read as an inference or as a performance; whether a thought needs a thinker in any stronger sense than the thought itself; whether the "I" in "I think" can be swapped for "there is thinking" without losing anything; and whether saying "this thought exists" or "my thinking exists" already gives away more than the deflationist should allow. Also say what the deflationist has to do about the fact that Descartes plainly thought he'd gotten a continuing self out of the argument — is that just his mistake, or does pointing that out count against the deflationary reading itself? Then rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me honestly whether the position has been so hedged that nothing could realistically count against it, and if so whether that's actually a problem for a view of this kind.
