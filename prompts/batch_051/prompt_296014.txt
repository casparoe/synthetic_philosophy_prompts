I'm on a psychiatry rotation and my supervisor has to indicate, for each patient with a delusion, whether she thinks the idea is held as a belief or is something else—a vivid imagining, a felt urge, a way of talking. She's got a broadly two-factor way of thinking about delusions: she always seperates the raw experience (the feeling of dread, the sense that a face is weirdly unfamiliar) from the step where the person turns that experience into a fixed claim about the world, and she says the second step is where the trouble mostly is. But she's never told me her rule for when a fixed claim counts as a belief.

Her calls so far:
- A man who thinks his coworkers are plotting against him, reads threats into routine emails, eventually quits and files a complaint: belief. "It runs his life, so he believes it."
- A woman who thinks her internal organs have been replaced with plastic and refuses to eat because a plastic stomach can't digest: belief. "Bizarre content doesn't stop it being believed; look at what she does."
- A man who says most days he thinks his neighbor wants to poison his plants but other days says it's probably just his anxiety: not a belief. "He can step outside it."
- A woman who says she has an unshakable feeling that her food is poisoned but adds "I don't actually believe that, I just can't shake the feeling": not a belief.

Later this week we're seeing a man with Capgras-ish symptoms who, in interviews, defends at length—with what he takes to be evidence—the claim that his son has been replaced by an impostor (the face is slightly wrong, the mannerisms are off). But every evening he plays cards with this "impostor," writes him birthday cards, and lends him money. When a nurse points out the inconsistency he doesn't blink; he says the impostor still needs to be kept close. Given her earlier calls, should I expect her to mark this one as a belief or not? If her record genuinly underdetermines it, say so; either way, tell me the rule she'd be running under each answer, which earlier case is the closest analog, and what further case would settle it.
