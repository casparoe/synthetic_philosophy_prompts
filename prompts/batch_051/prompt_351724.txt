Lately I've been struck by what looks like the same structural debate in two fields that rarely talk to each other. The first is the debate in animal and AI ethics about whether sentience is a threshold or a gradient. On the threshold view, moral patienthood is all-or-nothing: once a creature clears the relevant bar, it is inside the circle of moral patients, so a cow and an insect are both inside even if they differ enormously in interests, while a thermostat is simply outside. On the gradient view, sentience itself comes in degrees, and there is an ordered continuum from a thermostat, through an insect, to a human, with moral status—or at least the weight we give to experiential states—tracking position on that continuum. The second is the debate between classical two-valued logic and fuzzy logic. Classical logic assigns every proposition either 0 or 1; fuzzy logic allows degrees of truth between 0 and 1 and treats vague predicates like 'heap' or 'red' as picking out fuzzy sets with graded membership. The parallel seems close: 'sentient' functions like a vague predicate, 'degree of sentience' functions like degree of membership in a fuzzy set, and the threshold view corresponds to insisting that moral patienthood, unlike redness, is bivalent even where the exact threshold is hard to locate.

I want to know whether this parallel is deep or superficial. If it is deep, the standard difficulties for fuzzy logic should reappear in an ethics of graded moral status: where do the membership functions come from, is there higher-order vagueness about the degrees themselves, and do degrees of sentience compose truth-functionally when we aggregate harms and interests? If it is superficial, I want an exact account of where the mapping breaks. My suspicion is that the weak point is the step from degrees of sentience to degrees of moral status, because moral weight may not be a truth-like quantity at all—it may be irreducibly normative in a way that membership in a fuzzy set is not. But I am not sure whether that is a real disanalogy or just a failure of imagination on my part. Please give the mapping, state what would have to be true on both sides for any result to transfer, and identify at least one prediction the parallel makes that turns out false—for instance, by working through what it would say about a being located exactly halfway up the sentience scale.
