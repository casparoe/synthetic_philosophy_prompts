I keep running into a pattern in coordination problems and I don't have a word for it. Take the old story of two generals who need to attack a city at the same time: if one attacks alone he loses everything, so neither will move unless he's sure the other got the message. But any message can get lost, and each general knows that, so the first one needs confirmation that his message arrived, and then confirmation of the confirmation, and so on forever. No finite number of messages ever does the job. I've noticed the same logic in less military situations: friends trying to meet without phone service, a bank run where each depositor is almost sure the bank is fine but isn't sure the others are sure, a market where everyone is pretty sure a stock is overpriced but no one sells because they all think everyone else will keep buying a bit longer.

What interests me is a specific feature of these cases. Suppose the messages become extremely reliable—say a 99.9% chance of arrival—and the generals exchange ten confirmations. Each of them is now, individually, basically certain of the plan. But the argument still says they don't attack, and in fact making the channel 99.9999% reliable does not seem to change the conclusion at all; coordination stays stuck at zero until the doubt is removed completely, not gradually. There's a discontinuity there: tiny uncertainty at every layer of "I know that he knows that I know..." produces total failure, even though each individual's uncertainty is tiny.

I gather the infinite stack itself has a name, something like common knowledge. But does the specific failure pattern have its own name—the fact that almost-common-knowledge doesnt smoothly approximate common knowledge for purposes of coordinated action? And is there a name for the related game-theoretic setup where two people exchanging arbitrarily many messages that are almost sure to arrive still rationally refuse to act? If these don't have established names, how should I carve this up so I can tell apart the stack, the failure, and the discontinuity, and how does it relate to the difference between everyone being sure and the group being able to act?
