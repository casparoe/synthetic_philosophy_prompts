I have recently been appointed rapporteur for an adversarial collaboration between two camps in my department who have been arguing for years about whether the quantum state is ontic or epistemic. The psi-ontic side holds that the wavefunction represents a feature of the world itself, and currently leans toward a psi-supplemented hidden-variable reading; the psi-epistemic side holds that the wavefunction encodes an agent's information about an underlying ontic state, and currently leans toward the program of building explicit psi-epistemic models in the spirit of Spekkens' toy theory. They have asked me to produce the single document both parties can sign, and this is the first formal statement I have had to draft for them. It should open with a numbered list of agreed claims about the formalism: what both sides accept about the Born rule, unitarity, and the constraints Bell's theorem and Kochen–Specker place on any underlying ontic story; and about exactly what the Harrigan–Spekkers framework does and does not classify, including the fact that some anti-psi-ontic positions, such as QBism, fit poorly within its psi-epistemic category because they decline to posit an ontic state at all. It should also state precisely what the no-go results — Bell, Kochen–Specker, Colbeck–Renner, and especially Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph — actually require and rule out, and which of their premises each side may reasonably reject. Then it should move to a disagreements table: each row states the disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own position. Finally, it should identify the genuine cruxes. I suspect the main ones are whether the Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph preparation-independence assumption is a premise an epistemic view may reject without special pleading; whether a field or nomological structure on 3N-dimensional configuration space is an acceptable ontic posit or a reductio of psi-ontic realism; and whether psi-epistemic views genuinely dissolve the measurement problem or merely relocate it to the dynamics of the ontic variables. For each crux, set out the argument or concrete case both sides would agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about it.
