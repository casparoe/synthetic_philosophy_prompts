I'm trying to settle a dispute inside our seafood certification scheme and I think it's a genuinely hard interpretive question. Our welfare clause, Standard 4.2, reads: "A certified farm shall handle and slaughter all animals under its care so as to avoid unnecessary pain, distress or suffering, and any procedure likely to cause more than momentary pain shall be carried out with anaesthesia or sedation sufficient to prevent it." It was drafted in 1994, when the scheme covered only cattle, pigs and poultry. In 2021 we extended the label to farmed shrimp, and nobody thought to revisit 4.2.

Now we're auditing a shrimp broodstock hatchery, and they ablate one eyestalk from each female shrimp without anaesthesia, to drive her back into spawning condition. That's standard practice in the industry, and the farm says that without it their yields drop enough that the operation isn't viable. The auditors disagree about whether 4.2 applies to shrimp at all, and if it does, whether eyestalk ablation is exactly the sort of "unnecessary pain" the clause was meant to stop, or just a necessary production step with a bad look.

Walk me through the text, the purpose of the clause, what the 1994 drafter's would have said if someone had asked them about shrimp, and what a reasonable farmer reading the 2021 version of the standard would take it to mean. Then tell me which approach should control, and what the auditor should actually write on the certificate.
