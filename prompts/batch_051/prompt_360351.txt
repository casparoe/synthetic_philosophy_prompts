I am doing an independent study in philosophy of mind at my high school, and I have been trying to test a naturalistic account of representation against a real biological case. The account says that a state R represents a condition C when three requirements are met: first, R's biological function is to indicate C, where "function" means the effect for which R was selected or is maintained by natural selection; second, there is some downstream consumer system whose use of R helps explain why the mechanisms producing R were selected, so content is fixed by what R is used to do rather than by whatever merely happens to cause R; and third, R must be able to misrepresent, meaning it can be tokened when C does not obtain, since this is what distinguishes representing C from merely being correlated with C.

The case I want to think through comes from an article on plant communication. In dry scrubland, a shrub whose leaves are being chewed by caterpillars releases a volatile compound into the air. A different plant species growing downwind absorbs the compound, and once absorbed it activates a chemical pathway that causes the second plant to produce defensive toxins, which reduces the damage it later suffers from caterpillars. The same pathway can also be switched on by unrelated compounds in the environment, so the plant sometimes mounts this defensive response when no herbivore ever arrives. The article stressed that the evolutionary story is unresolved: the volatile release may simply be a wound byproduct that the second plant exploits, in which case the emitter was never selected to warn anyone, and the second plant's toxin pathway may be a general stress response that this chemical happens to trigger rather than a mechanism selected specifically to detect herbivore presence.

I want to apply the three requirements to both states: the emitter's release of the volatile and the receiver's activation of the toxin pathway. Does either state represent "herbivore attack nearby," or do they represent only the presence of that particular chemical, or do they fail to represent anything at all? If the receiver's state comes out as a representation under the criteria, should that worry the account, since it would then assign content to a plant's chemical signaling cascade in the same sense in which the account assigns content to an animal's beliefs? If it comes out as mere physiology, I would like to know which requirement does the excluding and whether that requirement is doing principled work or has been adjusted in advance to keep plants outside the domain of mind.
