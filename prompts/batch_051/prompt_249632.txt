I keep coming back to a structural resemblance and I cannot decide whether it is deep or superficial. On one side is Good's theorem in Bayesian decision theory: for a single expected-utility maximizer who updates by conditionalization, the expected value of cost-free information is never negative; in expectation, seeing more evidence cannot worsen the agent's decisions, because the agent could always decide to ignore the evidence. On the other side is a familiar political principle about transparency: that making more information public before institutions or electorates decide cannot make their decisions worse and will typically improve them — the idea often compressed into the slogan about sunshine and disinfectants.

The two claims look like the same shape at different scales: an individual mind on the one hand, a public or institution on the other. But I suspect the transfer is not free, because a public is not a single Bayesian with a coherent utility function, and transparency changes the strategic situation rather than merely informing a fixed decision problem.
