Exam question: Explain Dennett's four kinds of creatures — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian — saying for each what new capacity it adds over the previous one. Then say whether the ladder is best read as an evolutionary chronology, a logical ordering of design options, or something else, and justify your answer.

Student answer: Darwinian creatures are the baseline case: their behavior is hard-wired and fixed by natural selection across generations. Skinnerian creatures add learning within a single lifetime through operant conditioning — they produce behavior blindly and keep whatever gets reinforced, so selection now acts on an individual's behavioral repertoire rather than only on the species. Popperian creatures add an inner environment: instead of trying options out in the world and paying the real-world cost, they test candidate actions offline and let the bad ones die in the simulation instead. Gregorian creatures are Popperian creatures with a much more powerful inner environment — they can run longer and more abstract simulations, and that is what makes human foresight special. As for the ladder, I think its best read as an evolutionary chronology: each kind evolved after the one before it, and each later kind replaces the earlier one as the superior design.
