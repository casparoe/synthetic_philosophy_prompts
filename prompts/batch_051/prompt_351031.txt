I am a condensed-matter physicist with no formal background in social theory. The debate between methodological individualism and holism concerns whether social facts—norms, institutions, prices, linguistic regularities—are ultimately nothing over and above the actions and mental states of individual people, or whether social-level structures have genuine explanatory autonomy and can exert downward influence on individuals. Restate each side, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually work with: microstates and macrostates, coarse-graining, order parameters, phase transitions, universality, effective field theories, and the renormalization group. Where the mapping is natural, say so; where you have to stretch my concepts, say exactly what breaks. Then give the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only under distortion, and which find no counterpart at all in statistical physics. In particular, tell me whether the holist's 'downward causation' is anything more than the way a macrovariable constrains the set of microstates compatible with it, and whether 'explanatory autonomy' is the same thing as the autonomy of an effective theory from its microscopic details—or whether the residue shows that the social-science debate is about something physics has no handle on.
