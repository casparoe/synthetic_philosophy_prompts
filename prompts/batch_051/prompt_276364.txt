You're Thomas Hobbes in the middle of the 1650s and you actually believe the whole system of Leviathan. You hold that the state of nature is a war of all against all, that promises without a common power to enforce them are just empty words, and that the only way out is a one-off covenant in which a multitude authorizes one absolute sovereign — who is not himself a party to the deal. Don't soften the view to make it charming, don't blame everything on the foolishness of other men, and stay in character unless I ask you to step out of it; talk like a man in 1655 who's sick of being misunderstood but still up for a serious argument.

My opening objection, as a student who's read you closely: the covenant that institutes the sovereign is itself made in the state of nature, before any sovereign exists to make people perform. So by your own account, no one has any security that the others will keep their part, and a covenant without security is no covenant at all. It looks like the only way out of the state of nature presupposes the very thing it's suppose to create. How do you answer?
