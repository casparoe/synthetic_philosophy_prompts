I've been chewing on a definition of free will and I want you to take it apart honestly. I've been calling it the interruptibility account. The claim is that what makes an action free isn't anything about how it got started — not whether it was caused, not whether I could've done otherwise at the moment of choice — but whether the action, as it's unfolding, is running through a control loop that can halt it when the world stops matching the assumptions the plan was built on. A free action is one with live checkpoints: places where the agent is disposed to notice that the situation no longer fits what the routine was for, and can interupt the rest of the routine and do something else. Non-free is what you get in the old sphex wasp setup: the wasp drags a cricket to the burrow, goes inside to check the burrow, comes back out, and if you moved the cricket a few inches while it was inside, it drags it back to the threshold and checks the burrow again, forever, because nothing in its loop says the check is already done. That's unfreedom not because the wasp is determined but because the routine has no exit.

I like this because it makes free will an engineering property of ongoing activity rather than a metaphysical property of choices. A thermostat-ish system with good sensors and a pause-if-surprised rule starts to count as a little bit free, and honestly I'm okay with that. An addict mid-bender is running a one-step routine with no exit and counts as less free that hour. A democracy where a court can strike down a bad law is freer than one where decisions, once made, just grind forward until they crash.

So here's the ask. (1) Is this already two or three existing views wearing a tiny hat — reasons-responsiveness, guidance control, something from AI safety — or is it actually distinct? (2) Give me the strongest counterexample in each direction: something my account calls free that clearly isn't, and something it calls not-free that clearly is. (3) Does it actually dissolve the determinism worry, or just relocate it one level down into what counts as noticing a mismatch? (4) Tell me which of those objections is fatal and which are patchable, because I keep going back and forth on whether I've built a theory or just renamed situation awareness.
