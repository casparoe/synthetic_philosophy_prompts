I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar in the philosophy of social science and I am setting the take-home final. I want one essay question on the claim that the world is becoming less violent, of the sort associated with Steven Pinker's The Better Angels of Our Nature. The question must be unanswerable by reciting Pinker's charts or the usual objections to them. It should force students to say what would have to be settled before the claim has a determinate truth value: (i) the difference between per capita rates and absolute counts, including a small invented scenario in which the two pull in opposite directions as a population grows; (ii) how the phrase violence gets operationalized — combat deaths, all conflict-related deaths, homicide, state repression, structural harm — and how each choice can reverse the verdict; and (iii) the choice of time window and the problem of cherry-picking endpoints. The question should include a brief constructed dataset or scenario, not a historical citation, that students must interpret under two competing definitions of violence.

Please write the question, a model answer of roughly 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 built around five must-hit points, with the marks each is worth and a one-line note on how candidates typically get each wrong. Every loss of marks should be tied to an identifiable misunderstanding — for instance, treating violence as a natural kind with one obvious measure, or sliding from a falling rate to a falling absolute count. The crux should be whether there is a fact of the matter about the trend once the measure is fixed, or whether the choice of measure itself imports a value-laden framing that no amount of data can settle.
