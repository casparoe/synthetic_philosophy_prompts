You are an expert in formal epistemology and in the interpretation of administrative rules and professional codes. Please think slowly, and begin by distinguishing the possible readings of the provision before you settle on any conclusion.

The Compliance Manual of the Corvane State Gaming Commission (fictional) contains the following provision, Section 9.1:

"Where the Commission has certified the chance of a draw outcome, the certified chance shall be the exclusive measure of the likelihood of that outcome for all internal purposes, including post-draw reports, reserve certification, and prize-liability reviews, until the Commission issues a revised chance. No officer shall, in any internal report or assessment, record or rely upon a personal probability that differs from the certified chance."

Now consider this case. At 18:00, the Commission certified a chance of 1 in 10,000,000 that ticket 88B-0551 would win the 19:00 draw of the State Daily Lottery. The drawing is conducted by a mechanical ball machine, and an integrity officer, Ms. Ash, was assigned to observe the draw from inside the chamber. The machine selects the balls at 19:00, and Ash watches them fall, then verifies on a high-definition video replay that the winning number corresponds to ticket 88B-0551. The winning number is not released to the public until the 20:00 announcement. At 19:15, within the blackout period, Ash must sign a section of the Commission's routine prize-liability report, and the form asks her to enter the probability that ticket 88B-0551 is the winning ticket of the 19:00 draw. Section 9.1 appears to require her to enter 1 in 10,000,000, because no revised chance has been issued. The Manual contains no other provision that addresses the blackout period or an officer's personal observation of a completed draw; when Section 9.1 was adopted, no officer could observe a completed drawing before the public announcement, so Ash's situation was not one the drafters contemplated.

My questions: Does Section 9.1 actually require Ash to record 1 in 10,000,000 rather than a probability at or near 1? Work through at least four approaches: the plain wording, the evident purpose of the provision, what the drafters would likely have said if they had been asked about a blackout-period observer, and what a reasonable officer in Ash's position would take the rule to mean. Where these readings diverge, explain which should control and why. Then reflect on what this case shows about the principle that a rational agent who knows the objective chance of an event should set her degree of belief exactly to that chance, and how a well-drafted rule would need to change to handle this kind of case without readmitting the personal guesses the rule was meant to exclude.
