I sit on my department's awards committee, which must recommend to the faculty a voting procedure for choosing one prize recipient from five nominees each year. Last night I argued for hours with a friend about this decision. He pressed the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem: with three or more candidates, any ranked rule that is not dictatorial and can in principle select any candidate will, under some pattern of votes, allow a voter to gain by misreporting their ranking. He concluded that strategy-proofness is therefore unavailable, so we should either adopt a dictatorial rule (one designated voter's ranking always decides) and stop pretending, or pick a familiar method and stop worrying about manipulation. A colleague counters that approval voting escapes the theorem's assumptions, but my friend says that only relocates the strategic voting problem. I am not convinced the theorem forces his dilemma, but I need help stating what it does imply for a committee like ours. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before our vote: it should recommend a concrete ballot procedure (or a procedure for choosing one), give the reasoning, and engage seriously with the strongest version of my friend's argument against the recommendation.
