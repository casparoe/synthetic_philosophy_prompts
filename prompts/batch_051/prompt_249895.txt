Here's something I keep going back and forth on. People sometimes say deliberation crowds out prediction: while you're genuinely deciding, you cant also assign probabilities to your own options, because doing so either short-circuits the deciding or turns you into a spectator of yourself. But I want to see how far that slogan actually goes, so I came up with a test, and now I have a borderline case I'm not sure about.

Let me stipulate that an agent's credence Cr(I do A) = p, held while she's still deliberating, is "deliberation-safe" just in case it meets three conditions.

First, source asymmetry: the evidence that grounds p doesn't include any first-person information about what she's currently leaning toward. So p can come from base rates, from a predictive model of her, from her past behavior — but not from introspecting that A feels tempting.

Second, separability: when she uses p in an expected-value calculation, the event "I do A" and the act she eventually chooses stay distinct variables. She doesn't let the fact that she predicted A count as a reason for A, and she doesn't double-count her own choice as part of the world's state.

Third, fixed-point stability: p has to be a fixed point of her actual decision procedure. That is, if she runs the procedure with p already in hand, the procedure outputs A with probability p, so merely learning the procedure's output wouldn't force her to revise p.

Now the case I want you to think about. A PhD student has until Friday to sign her dissertation committee paperwork or decline the slot and take a good industry job with a master's. Her department's retention model — built from fifteen years of admission data, trained only on application and coursework variables, with no access to her private feelings — puts the probability that students with her profile master out at 0.8. She's read the report, so the number is already in her head. On Tuesday she sits down to deliberate and writes "Cr(I master out) = .8" at the top of her decision matrix. Is she entitled to do that? Walk through my three conditions and say where her 0.8 passes and fails. If it passes the conditions but still feels wrong, tell me whether the conditions or the prediction should be revised.
