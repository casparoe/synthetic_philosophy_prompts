I’m reading Adam Smith’s Wealth of Nations for a seminar on the political economy of war, and I came across this passage at the start of Book V, Chapter 1, “Of the Expense of Defence”:

“The first duty of the sovereign, that of protecting the society from the violence and invasion of other independent societies, can be performed only by means of a military force. But the expense both of preparing this military force in time of peace, and of employing it in time of war, is very different in the different states of society, in the different periods of improvement.”

A study guide I’m using then summarises Smith’s overall argument in the chapter like this: Smith thinks that as societies become commercially advanced, the division of labour makes ordinary citizens physically and mentally softened, so they can no longer defend themselves. He therefore favours professional standing armies over citizen militias, because only full-time soldiers can cultivate the martial spirit. But he also warns that a standing army is the most dangerous public institution for liberty, since the men in it have no attachment to the civilian population and will obey their officers’ orders to fire on their fellow citizens. Smith’s solution is therefore to recruit the army’s rank and file from the poorest classes, because people without property have the least interest in challenging the government.

Something about this feels too tidy given the opening passage, and I’m not sure where the summary stops reporting Smith and starts smuggling in later worries. Could you go through the summary sentence by sentence and tell me what is accurate, what is subtly wrong, and what the chapter actually maintains? Where the guide draws conclusions from the quoted passage, please say whether the passage supports those conclusions. I’d like to know what Smith explicitly says, what is a standard compression, and what has been inserted by later interpreters.
