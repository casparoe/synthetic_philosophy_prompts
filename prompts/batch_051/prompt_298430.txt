Over the past few months I've been reading about the species problem, mostly the mess around microbes and horizontal gene transfer, and I've been trying to formulate a species concept that doesn't secretly assume sexual reproduction or a single branching tree. What I've arrived at, working title the "basin species concept," goes like this. Lay out all actual and possible genotypes in a high-dimensional sequence space, and in any specified environment assign each genotype a fitness. A species is then a connected region of this space whose members tend, under mutation and selection, to move toward the same local fitness peak, or toward a set of peaks connected by high-fitness ridges, and that is separated from other such regions by low-fitness valleys across which gene flow is rare. Membership is thus fixed not by interbreeding or by a segment of a phylogeny but by which basin of attraction a genotype sits in on the fitness landscape. Horizontal gene transfer is within-species when it moves genes within a basin and simply reshuffles a species; it becomes speciation-relevant when it carries a genotype across a valley into another basin. The view seems to explain why species show up as more or less discrete clusters even in organisms that exchange genes promiscuously, and why "species" remains a usable notion in asexual lineages.

I know about the biological, phylogenetic, and ecological species concepts, and this is obviously close to the genotypic cluster idea, so I'm not claiming complete novelty. But I want an honest, charitable assessment rather than encouragement. Is this a real concept or just a dynamical-systems metaphor doing no work? Has anyone already defended substantially this view under another name, and if so, what did they handle that I'm missing? Where exactly does it break — for example, when the fitness landscape is frequency-dependent and shifts as the population evolves, when environments change, when recombination is so free that the basins blur, or when we have to specify "the" environment for a generalist? Give me the two or three strongest objections, say whether any is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable, and then tell me which consideration would most change your overall verdict about the view.
