I've been running a little giving club with some friends and we keep having the same fight. Everyone nods along when someone says a cause should be important, tractable, and neglected, but then the minute we look at an actual choice — a malaria charity with great numbers versus some long-run thing with enormous numbers and basically no evidence — we split into the same camps. I want to turn this into one of those 'which character are you' style quizzes, except instead of asking people which label they like, it should sort them by what they'd actually do in concrete cases.

Here's what I'm picturing. Eight to ten questions, each one a short made-up choice between two or three causes or donations. The questions should force people to put their thumb on the scale between big importance with weak tractability, strong evidence with modest scale, and high neglectedness. Please seperate these cases out somewhere: (1) a charity with a solid track record of saving lives now versus a speculative project with a 1% chance of averting something enormous; (2) two charities with the same expected benefit, but one works in a crowded field and the other is the only group working on its problem; (3) a cause where all three factors are so shaky that reasonable people with different estimates would flip to opposite decisions; (4) an option that helps people alive now versus one that mostly helps possible people far in the future. And leave room for whether someone changes their mind when they find out a cause has become trendy.

The scoring key is where I need the real work. Map the answer patterns onto a small set of positions that actually sit in the logical space, something like: a pure expected-value maximizer (who thinks neglectedness only matters as evidence about the other two factors), a robustness-first person who discounts whatever can't be measured, a neglectedness-for-its-own-sake person who thinks being ignored is itself a reason to take a cause on, and a person-affecting view that caps how much future or speculative benefit gets to dominate present suffering. If some combinations of answers are empty because they'd be incoherent, or just because nobody is likely to hold them, say so and say which. Also flag any pair of positions the quiz still can't tell apart, and say what question would fix that. Oh, and it's for people who have never heard of the importance-tractability-neglectedness framework, so the options should be plain-language and shouldn't use any of those three words. After the scoring key, put a last line that starts 'Verdict:' with a one-sentence summary of what the quiz actually ends up sorting people by.
