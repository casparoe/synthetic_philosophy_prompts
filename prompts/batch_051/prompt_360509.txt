I am a member of a worker-owned cooperative that runs a small chain of neighborhood cafés. We currently handle almost everything through decentralized arrangements: shift swaps, a weekly internal market in which members bid for the less popular hours, informal notes about regulars' preferences, and ordering adjustments that people make on the spot. One member has proposed scrapping all of that in favor of a single planning algorithm we would run ourselves. The algorithm would draw on everyone's stated preferences, hourly sales data, supplier prices, weather forecasts, and customer feedback, and it could also message any member for a local judgment at any time. She argues that this would cut waste and schedule mismatches far better than our patchwork of deals. Another member objects that much of what actually keeps the cafés running was never entered into any system and might not be the sort of thing anyone can report on demand: the fridge that runs a few degrees warm, the lunch rush that starts early when a nearby school lets out early, a supplier whose delivery times have been quietly slipping, the hunch that a menu item is about to catch on. The first member replies that, if it matters, someone can always type it into the algorithm; the only obstacle is that we have not bothered. We vote next month. Which argument is stronger, and what should we actually decide? Please answer in exactly five numbered considerations, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
