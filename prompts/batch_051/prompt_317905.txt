After a long thread on the company Slack about Pascal's mugging, someone told me to actually write down my positions instead of arguing case by case. Heres the list, and I'd like an honest consistency check:

1. The rational action is the one with the highest expected value, full stop.
2. Utility is unbounded: for any amount of value you can name, a larger amount is possible, so I won't cap it to dodge the mugging.
3. I don't set a lower bound on probabilities. I can't say where "too unlikely to matter" starts, so I can't justify dismissing the mugger's 1-in-10^30 claim just because it's tiny.
4. In practice I wouldn't pay a Pascal's mugger, and I'd endorse a standing policy of refusing mugging-style demands, decided ahead of time.
5. I think that policy is genuinely rational, not just a hack: a rational agent picks standing policies that survive reflection; it doesn't recompute each offer's expected value from scratch.
6. Yet I think it's fine to donate to charity on the basis of low-probability, high-payoff mechanisms, where any given dollar probably does nothing but has a small chance of doing a lot of good.

Are these compatible? Go through the pairs and tell me which are actually in tension and which only look like it, rank the real tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest single revision would be to make the whole thing hang together.
