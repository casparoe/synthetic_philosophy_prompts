Okay so I've recently started doing motion graphics for a little indie game, and I got completely obsessed with the color phi illusion after one of our artists showed me a demo. I'm supposed to present my philosophical take on it at our studio's nerd lunch next week, and I want someone to check whether my view-set actually hangs together before I embarrass myself in front of everyone.

In case you haven't seen it: a red dot flashes on the left of a screen, there's a short blank gap, then a green dot flashes a bit to the right. What you actually see is a single dot gliding from left to right and changing from red to green around halfway across — which is before the green dot ever physically appears. So the experience seems to include the color change before the event that should cause it has even happened. I've tried it a bunch of times and it's rock solid.

Here's where I've landed:

1. I'm a physicalist — conscious experience is something the brain does.
2. But I reject the Cartesian theater. There's no little me inside watching a screen where finished experiences get projected; no single place and time where the show all comes together for an inner audience.
3. Conscious experience runs in real time, or alot close to it. What I'm aware of at a given moment tracks what's happening in the world right around then, and my brain can't go back and rewrite an experience after I've already had it.
4. In the color phi case, I really do see the single moving dot change color before the green dot is there, and I'm prepared to trust my introspective report about the content, order, and timing of what I saw. For a simple, repeatable display like this, introspection is pretty trustworthy.
5. I think one of two things has to be going on. Either my consciousness is held up until the green dot arrives, and only then is the moving-dot percept assembled with the color change backdated into the middle — so what I report as an experience from an earlier moment wasn't actually being experienced by anyone at that earlier moment (the Stalinesque story). Or I consciously see the two dots in real time, and then my memory immediately gets revised into the moving-dot story, so my honest final report is about a doctored memory, not an experience I actually had (the Orwellian story).
6. But I also can't shake the feeling that the Stalinesque/Orwellian difference shouldn't be a real difference, because from the inside, once the dust settles, the two cases are indistinguishable. Not sure if that's deep or just lazy.

So that's the package. Go through it and tell me: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, how severe each tension is, and what single revision would clean up the most. Rank the tensions rather than listing them all as equally bad.
