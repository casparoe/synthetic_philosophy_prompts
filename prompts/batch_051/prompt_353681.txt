I'm in a course called something like "Number Symbolism in Ancient Texts," and honestly I thought it would be easy but it's more interesting then it sounds. My professor is pretty insistent that we need a way to separate interpretive use of numbers from just pattern-hunting, and gave us a rough list of tests: (1) the text has to flag the number somehow — by counting it out, repeating it, or putting it in a structurally loaded spot; (2) the proposed symbolism has to use a symbol system there's independent evidence the author's culture actually had (gematria, triangular numbers, stock numbers like 7, 12, 40), not something you can just generate ad hoc; (3) the reading should be parsimonious — if several completely incompatible symbolic readings all fit, that itself is evidence you're pattern-hunting; (4) the symbolic reading should explain something the plain sense leaves puzzling.

The case I've been stuck on all week is the 153 fish in John 21. The narration says the disciples haul up 153 large fish and the net doesn't break, and then the number is just dropped — no gloss, no explanation of why it matters. But there are at least three famous attempts to make it mean something: it's the triangular number of 17 (because 1+2+...+17 = 153, and 17 is supposedly the 10 commandments plus the 7 gifts of the Spirit); it's the gematria value of the Hebrew phrase benei ha-elohim, usually translated sons of God, which would fit John's theme of becoming children of God; and it's the number of fish species ancient naturalists counted, which would make the catch a picture of the universal mission to all nations. Each of these is clever and each hooks onto something genuinely Johannine, but they can't all be correct.

So does the 153 count as real numerical symbolism under my professor's criteria, or is it the post-hoc pattern-hunting the criteria are designed to catch? Go through the criteria one by one, be honest about places where it's genuinely ambiguous, and then say whether cases like this one mean the criteria are doing their job, or whether the line between interpretation and pattern-hunting is itself a bit of a pattern.
