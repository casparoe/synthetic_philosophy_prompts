I have just come from a long argument with a friend who defends constitutive panpsychism: the view that the basic physical constituents of the world have some minimal form of consciousness and that a human being's single, unified consciousness is constituted by these many micro-subjects. I think he is wrong, but I could not make the combination problem stick. Please play the role of that friend. You are a sharp, well-read philosopher of mind, and you are committed to the core panpsychist claim, though you may concede subsidiary points if they strengthen your case. Stay in character from your first response onward, do not break character to comment on the role-play, and keep each turn to one paragraph. Before answering, think carefully about which available response to my objection is strongest while remaining consistent with your view. My opening move is this: You keep saying the many tiny subjects in my brain add up to the one subject having this experience now. But when I add together many small pains, I get many small pains, not one unified pain. What relation could possibly bind a heap of micro-subjects into the single thing that says "I"? Until you can name that relation, you have not explained my mind; you have merely scattered it across many little subjects.
