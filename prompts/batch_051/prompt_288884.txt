ok so there's this problem in bayesian confirmation theory that I can't shake, and I want it worked through with a machine-learning lens rather than just re-explained. The problem of old evidence, roughly: if some observation E is already known before a hypothesis H comes along, then P(E) is 1, so conditioning on E does nothing to P(H). The usual illustration is the precession of Mercury's perihelion — astronomers had the anomaly for a long time, then general relativity turns up and the exact number falls out of the theory with no free parameter to tune, and everyone treats that as a huge confirmation. But the bayesian math seems to say it can't be a confirmation, because the evidence was already totally known. The standard patch is to imagine a counterfactual prior where you somehow subtract E from your background knowledge and ask whether H would have made E more likely than the old theory would. Fine.

Here's the thing I actually want answered. In my line of work this situation isn't an edge case, it's the whole workflow. You have a public benchmark dataset whose labels are out in the open. Someone trains a new model (without peeking at the test labels, in the honest case), then we score it against that same public data. The data predates the model and is common knowledge — which sounds exactly like old evidence. And yet benchmark results obviously count as evidence: a model that smashes a hard public benchmark is treated as having learned something real, not as telling us zero because we already knew the labels.

What twists my brain is that in ML we've basically engineered the counterfactual into the machinery with train/test splits and held-out labels. Does that mean the old-evidence problem is solved, or just that we found a practical patch that doesn't answer the underlying worry? Or is the ML case differnt because what's confirming isn't the labels but the newly computed fact that this model scored 90% — and if that's the fix, doesn't the same move apply to Mercury, where the new fact was the derivation of the anomaly from the theory? I want to know which premise of the old-evidence argument this benchmark setup puts pressure on, whether the argument survives the fact that old evidence is now the default way science gets done, and whether there's a repaired version that still says something important.
