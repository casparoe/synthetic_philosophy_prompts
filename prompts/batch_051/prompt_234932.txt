I write a column on ideas for a general-audience science magazine, and I've been drafting a piece about Goodhart's law — the way a metric stops being informative once its made a target, because people game it, teach to the test, hit the KPI and let the underlying thing rot. Fine. But then I fell down a rabbit hole reading about Fitch's paradox of knowability in modal epistemic logic, and I can't unsee what looks like the same shape.

The Fitch result, as I understand it: suppose you hold the verificationist-sounding principle that every truth is knowable — for any truth p, it's possible for someone to know p. Then take a truth of the form "p, and no one ever knows p" (true for any p that is true but never known). If someone came to know that conjunction, knowledge distributes over the conjuncts, so they'd know p and they'd know that no one ever knows p; but since knowledge is factive, the second conjunct would be true, so nobody ever knows p — including them, which contradicts their knowing p. So the conjunction is a true proposition that cannot possibly be known. In other words, from the existence of a single unknown truth you get an unknowable one, and the knowability principle collapses into the absurd claim that every truth is already known. The act of coming to know that high-order fact would destroy part of what it says.

Now, Goodhart's law says, roughly, that once a measure is made a target, agents adjust their behavior to the target, and the metric stops measuring the thing it was supposed to measure. In both cases the instrument of representation — the knower in Fitch, the published metric in Goodhart — is part of the system it tries to represent, so the attempt to represent the system accurately changes the system. But in Fitch the failure is a proof from factivity and distributivity, while in Goodhart it's an empirical regularity about incentives and adaptation. Am I looking at a real structural identity — a fixed-point constraint on systems that contain their own representation — or is this just a verbal pun on "knowing changes what's known"? Does anything transfer from the logic to the sociology of metrics, or the other way? Is Fitch's paradox just what Goodhart's law would be if the thing being gamed were truth itself?
