I am working on a feature about how institutions try to discipline the optimism of experts, and one proposal keeps recurring in my interviews. The policy is, in effect, an attempt to institutionalize the outside view — anchoring decisions in what similar cases actually deliver — while preserving a narrow channel for a well-specified inside view. Suppose a research-intensive country of roughly the size of the Netherlands adopts the following rule for all public research grants above two million euros. Every application must include two forecasts of what the project will deliver. The first is an applicant forecast, written by the investigators in any terms they choose. The second is a reference-class forecast produced by an independent statistical unit that sees only the field, proposed duration, team size, and budget band — never the specific hypothesis, the methods, or the investigators' identities. Review panels may approve a project only when either the applicant forecast is no more optimistic than the reference-class forecast, or the panel states in writing the specific mechanism by which this particular project is expected to outperform its reference class; the investigators' past success, the reviewers' enthusiasm, and appeals to novelty may not be cited as grounds. No symmetric requirement constrains panels that reject proposals the reference class would have favored.

I want to think through the next thirty years with the reasoning made visible. Predict how this policy reshapes the research system: which fields, methods, and career stages gain or lose relative funding; how proposal-writing and panel behavior adapt; which biases are suppressed and which new ones emerge; what happens to exploratory, interdisciplinary, and paradigm-breaking work; and whether the scientific community becomes more or less genuinely epistemically modest in practice. For each major prediction, say whether you consider it robust, a plausible but fragile extrapolation, or a guess, and identify the unstated assumption whose failure would most change it.
