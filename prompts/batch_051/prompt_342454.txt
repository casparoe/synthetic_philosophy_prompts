Last week in a seminar on philosophy of history we got stuck on overdetermination, and I came out more confused than I went in. The running example was the start of the First World War. Everyone agreed the assassination in Sarajevo had *something* to do with it, but everyone also agreed that the alliance system and the mobilization schedules had made a major war close to unavoidable. A friend of mine defended the following argument, and I've been turning it over since:

(1) The outbreak of the war was massively overdetermined: if Gavrilo Princip's shot had missed, some other spark would very likely have set off a great-power war within a few years, given the alliance blocks, the arms race, and the mobilization timetables.

(2) So for any single factor we might name — the assassination, say, but also the alliance system, the naval race, the war plans — the war would still have happened without it.

(3) A factor explains an event only if it made a difference: if the event would have come about anyway had the factor been absent, the factor did not make a difference.

(4) Therefore none of those overdetermining factors actually explains the war.

(5) But historians do cite exactly those factors as explanations, and they treat them as better than just-so stories.

(6) So either historians' explanatory practice is systematically confused, or historical explanation is not about difference-making — but then what makes something a genuine explanation rather than a story that merely fits?

I keep feeling the argument is too slick, but I can't locate the load-bearing step. Is it valid? If not, which numbered step (or which move between two steps) fails, and on what reading of the terms does it fail most clearly? Could the argument be made sound by restricting 'explains' in some way, and what would that restriction force us to give up? Also, tell me what would change your mind: which consideration about how historians actually answer contrastive questions (or about event individuation) would flip your diagnosis from 'the argument has a real bug at step X' to 'it is basically sound and the conclusion is just unwelcome' — or the other way round.
