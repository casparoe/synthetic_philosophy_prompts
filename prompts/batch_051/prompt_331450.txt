Write a dialogue between a natural rights theorist and a Benthamite arguing about whether a newly drafted bill of rights should open by declaring that people have certain natural rights. The Benthamite should press the two halves of Bentham's "nonsense upon stilts" objection seperately instead of blurring them: first, that a right with no lawgiver to define and enforce it is vacuous, and second, that even read charitably, rights-talk functions rhetorically to dress up controversial policy preferences as self-evident trump cards. The rights theorist should hold up their end of the argument, for instance by replying that the whole point of such a clause is to give citizens a standard outside the law for criticizing the law, and that the rhetorical force of rights-talk doesn't settle wether the claims behind it are true. Make the two characters respond to each other's actual arguments rather than talk past each other, let each side make at least one concession that costs them something, and don't let either side win cleanly.
