Two people in my study group got into it after our seminar on time travel, and I couldnt entirely follow the whole thing. One of them, Priya, said the grandfather paradox shows backwards time travel just cant be possible. Her line: if you go back and kill your grandfather before he meets your grandmother, you're never born, so nobody comes back to kill him. So you'd have an act that both happens and never gets initiated. A world with a fixed past plus ordinary abilities to kill people is contradictory. The other one, Sam, said she was conflating two things: you can go back and affect the past without changing it. In a single fixed timeline, the fact that you're standing there already entails you dont succeed — the gun jams, you miss, he gets rescued. Only self-consistent histories obtain. So the paradox doesn't refute time travel; it just tells you certain acts, like successfully killing your own grandfather, won't happen. Priya said that's a cosmic conspiracy: the gun has no mechanical reason to jam, so the self-consistency story invokes an unexplained global restriction to save the fixed past, and it ends up saying quite ordinary actions are 'impossible' without any local mechanism. Sam said she was smuggling in branching timelines — the only way the gun actually fires without contradiction is if you've already split off onto a parallel history where that grandfather isn't really the one whose non-existence would erase you, and that isn't visiting your own past, it's moving to a new one. Priya replied that either way, at best we've got a constraint with no mechanism, and if you're allowed those you might as well admit the whole setup is doing something metaphysically odd. Sam came back with compossibility: the question isnt whether pulling a trigger is possible, it's whether 'I pull the trigger' and 'I exist to have travelled back' are compossible in one history. They never settled it.

Adjudicate: who's right about which specific points, and what are they both missing?
