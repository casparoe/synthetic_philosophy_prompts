I've taught the liar paradox enough times to know exactly where the seminar stalls: not on the simple liar, but on revenge. The contextualist says revenge sentences only show we extended the language after solving the orginal paradox; the Tarskian says they show the hierarchy never tops out; the dialetheist shrugs that of course some sentences are true and false; and the hard-liner says an account that can't state its own semantics without contradiction hasn't solved anything. My working theory is that this isn't a technical disagreement about fixed points or levels at all, but a disagreement about what a solution to the liar is meant to do -- model natural language as it is, replace it with something cleaner, or something else again. I want a diagnosis of the dispute, not a survey of positions. What kind of disagreement is this, really, and what would actually move it: a theorem, a distinction, a rephrasing of the constraint -- or is a permanent stalemate the honest conclusion?
