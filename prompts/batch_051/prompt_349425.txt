A friend of mine who is a hospital administrator (not a clinician and not a philosopher) read a long piece on how death is defined and summarized it to me like this:

"The law says someone is dead when either their heart and breathing stop irreversibly, or their entire brain, including the brainstem, stops functioning irreversibly. But the actual test for brain death is just that the patient is in a deep coma, has no brainstem reflexes, and doesn't breathe on their own when the ventilator is removed. The only reason the brainstem is included is that your brain is what makes you a person, so once it's gone there's nothing left worth keeping alive. And donation after circulatory death shows the whole definition is a legal fiction: doctors wait only five or ten minutes after the heart stops before declaring death, even though the law says the stop has to be irreversible — and a stopped heart is often perfectly restartable with CPR. They just choose not to restart it. So really the line is drawn wherever transplant medicine needs it, not given by biology."

I found the final point intuitively plausible, but I've also heard that brain death and circulatory death are genuinely different things and that the word "irreversible" is doing real work. I can't tell where his summary is accurate and where he's overstating or running things together.
