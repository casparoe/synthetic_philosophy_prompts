I keep noticing what looks like a structural parallel between Locke's labour theory of acquisition and proof-of-work issuance in blockchains, and I can't tell whether it is doing real work or just trading on the word labour. On the Lockean side, a person acquires an unowned resource by mixing their labour with it, under a proviso that what remains for others must be sufficient and no worse than what was taken. On the crypto side, a miner acquires newly issued coins by expending computational work, and the protocol's open verification plus difficulty adjustment are usually defended as keeping issuance fair and resistant to capture rather than as literally preserving a commons for everyone. Both say something like: expenditure of effort turns an unowned thing into an owned thing, within limits meant to protect everyone else's ability to acquire. I'd like a committed verdict on whether this is deep or superficial. State the mapping precisely, and say what would have to be true on each side for results to transfer. In particular, is Lockean labour, which is supposed to improve what it gets mixed with, the same kind of thing as hashing, which is deliberately pure expenditure? Does difficulty adjustment play the role of the Lockean proviso, or is it doing something closer to managing a shared resource? If the parallel is mostly superficial, name a prediction it makes that turns out false.
