I have a rule for arguments about whether something is real or just a construct/convention/useful label: before I put effort in, each side has to give me one concrete downstream difference — something one side expects to observe or would do differently. If none show up, I call it verbal and move on. I ran this on a debate about whether personality traits are real or just convenient summaries of behaviour. One side predicted a lot of cross-situational consistency, the other predicted close to zero once you control for the situation — so by my rule the dispute was substantive. But after the predictions were settled I had a strong sense the leftover question was still purely verbal, something a better criterion should have caught. Is my rule actually biased toward counting every dispute as substantive, since a creative person can always invent some expected difference? Or is my sense that there's a verbal residue left over just confused, because once all expected differences are settled theres nothing left to disagree about? How should I restate the rule, and is it defensible as a shortcut given that I can't sit through every realism debate?
