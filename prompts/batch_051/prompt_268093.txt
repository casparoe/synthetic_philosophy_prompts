You are a metaphysician specializing in dispositions, powers, and counterfactual analysis. Think through the problem step by step, beginning by stating the target view as precisely as you can before criticizing it.

The simple conditional analysis of dispositions says: an object x is disposed to manifest M in response to stimulus S just in case, were x to undergo S, x would manifest M. A fragile glass, for example, is disposed to shatter when struck just in case, were it struck, it would shatter.

This analysis faces two standard kinds of counterexample. In a finkish case, the occurrence of the stimulus (or something it triggers) removes or reverses the disposition itself, so the manifestation does not occur, and the conditional comes out false even though the object genuinely had the disposition before the stimulus. In a masking case (also called an antidote case, especially for active powers like a poison's power to kill), the disposition remains fully present, but an independent factor blocks the manifestation when the stimulus occurs, so the conditional again comes out false. Give a concrete example of each kind of case, and explain with precision exactly where each refutes the simple analysis as stated.

Now consider the obvious repair: adding a proviso such as "in the absence of interfering conditions" or "other things being equal." Explain why this repair is usually found inadequate. In particular, address the question whether the proviso can be spelled out without circularity — that is, without tacitly meaning something like "unless the object fails to manifest M." Does the fink/mask distinction give us any leverage here? Is the difference between a fink and a mask a difference in the causal structure of the world, or is it merely two descriptions of the same underlying phenomenon, namely a disposition ascription paired with a false counterfactual? If it is a real difference, what exactly must a correct analysis say in order to respect it? You may also consider the converse problem of mimics — objects lacking the disposition that would nevertheless manifest M upon S — if it bears on your assessment of the proviso.

Finally, draw out one broader lesson: if finks and masks show that disposition ascriptions cannot be reduced to bare counterfactual conditionals, what does that tell us, and what does it not tell us, about whether dispositions are fundamental features of reality, and about how scientific predicates like "soluble," "fragile," "elastic," and "flammable" should be understood?
