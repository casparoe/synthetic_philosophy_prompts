me and my friend have this running argument about whether games can be art. she says no becuase art is something you look at or listen to, and a game is just rules plus a score — the visuals and story can be pretty but that's decoration, not the game itself. i keep saying she's wrong: the part of a game that actually is art is the decisions it hands you, the little space of choices the rules carve out. some choice-spaces feel alive and some feel like filling out a form, and you can switch between them by changing one rule while the board and pieces look exactly the same. so i think the art in a game is something you feel while you're deciding, not something on the screen or in the soundtrack.

anyway i run the games club at school, maybe 15 kids show up, mostly grades 9-11, and we get about an hour after school on fridays. i want to run a session that gets them to feel this instead of just hearing me argue it. design me the activity. we have paper, pens, dice, and some old board game pieces, no screens or computers. i want them to play or build tiny rule systems and notice their own experience change when a rule changes, even though nothing about the game looks different. if it's possible they should suggest the rule changes themselves, so by the end they're saying my point in their own words before i ever name it. then give me a debrief for afterwards: map the moments in the activity onto the idea, tell me what will predictably go wrong when i run it and how to handle that in the room, and be straight about wich part of the games-as-art idea the activity actually can't show — the part where i have to step in and just say it myself.
