A friend of mine who does bioethics keeps insisting that the therapy/enhancement distinction is doing real work: therapy restores someone to normal species-typical functioning and enhancement moves them beyond it, and that is supposed to be a difference in kind, not degree. I think 'normal functioning' is just the bell curve dressed up as a natural kind, and its mostly a social convention about which bodies count as the baseline. How would you actually test who's right? Take a concrete intervention — growth hormone, say — and design the strongest test (or thought experiment, if the real version is unethical) that would distinguish 'restoring a baseline' from 'moving past a baseline' without just relabeling where the person started on the distribution. Tell me what pattern of results would make you think the distinction is biological rather than conventional, what pattern would make you think it is only a line we draw, and then say what even a clean positive result could never settle — for example whether a real biological difference would automatically deserve moral weight.
