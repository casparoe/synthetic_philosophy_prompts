ok so i've been thinking about language a lot lately, specifically about how I actually know that the past tense of "go" is "went." I ended up with a list of things I believe and I can't tell if they all fit together. can you check them for me and rank the actual conflicts, skipping the ones that only look like conflicts?

where I've landed:

1. knowing a language is knowing its rules. if it was just a memorized pile of sentences you could never understand new ones, but you can, so there have to be rules.

2. a rule has to cover more than one case. if something is true of just one word and you have to learn it word by word, I don't want to call that a rule. its a fact about that word, an exception.

3. the past tense rule in english is add -ed (or -d/-t depending on the last sound). walked, kicked, hunted. new verbs follow it, like "i googled it."

4. went is an exception to that rule. the past of go just is went, and you can't get there from go, so it has to be memorized as a one-off.

5. but the exceptions kind of run in packs. go/went, be/is/was/were, good/better/best. the really common everyday words are the weird ones, and a lot of them seem to get their forms from completely different words (I read that went actually started as the past tense of wend). so there's a pattern across exceptions, and sometimes i think that pattern is its own kind of rule.

6. also, lots of irregular pasts look like they used to follow old rules. foot/feet, goose/geese, mouse/mice are all the same vowel change, and i read that used to be a regular thing back in old english. so an exception can be a fossil of an old rule.

7. because of all that, I'm tempted to say the "add -ed" rule isn't really the past tense rule at all. its more like the default for words that don't already have a past form. the actual rule is whatever form a native speaker pulls up for that verb, and went is just as much part of that as walked.

what i keep going back and forth on is whether 7 conflicts with 2 and 3, and whether 5 and 6 are really compatible with calling went an exception in 4. and i cant tell if 1 is doing too much work here. which of these are actually in tension, which just look like it, and what would be the smallest change to make the whole thing hang together?
