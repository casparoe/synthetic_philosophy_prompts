I am a novelist writing a book about a historian of the ancient world who drifts toward pseudoarchaeology, and I want to ensure my characters' arguments are not straw men. As research for the book, rank the following three doctrines from most to least defensible as historiography — that is, as candidate accounts of the actual human past, judged by the ordinary epistemic standards of professional history: source criticism, fit with independently established archaeological and textual evidence, chronological consilience, and openness to correction.

- Atlantis as a real lost civilization whose traces survive in later traditions.
- Ancient-astronaut accounts, on which some ancient structures or texts are best explained by contact with technologically advanced non-human visitors.
- The New Chronology associated with Anatoly Fomenko, on which the conventional timeline of ancient and medieval history is largely a later fabrication and much of ancient history is a phantom duplicate of medieval events.

For each doctrine, give a 0–10 score on five sub-dimensions: internal coherence; fit with independently established evidence; resistance to disconfirmation, understood as how difficult it would be for new evidence to overturn the doctrine; methodological kinship with professional historiography; and explanatory economy, understood as how much it explains relative to the number of auxiliary assumptions it needs.

Please be explicit about what is settled and what is genuinely contested among people who work on these doctrines and on historical method. Do not present a contested view — for example, that the three doctrines are equally irrational, that the boundary between history and pseudohistory is a solved problem, or that all their appeal reduces to error — as settled expert consensus. Where historians would reasonably disagree about how much weight a criterion deserves, say so.

Return the result as a JSON object with exactly the following fields:

- "ranking": an array of the three labels, ordered from most to least defensible, using "Atlantis", "Ancient astronauts", and "New chronology".
- "doctrines": an array of three objects, one per doctrine, each with: "label" (string), "overall_score" (number, 0–10), "scores" (an object with keys "internal_coherence", "fit_with_evidence", "resistance_to_disconfirmation", "methodological_kinship", "explanatory_economy", each a number from 0–10), and "rationale" (a short string giving the single most important reason for its placement).
- "settled": a string summarizing what a consensus of relevant scholars could, in your assessment, sign onto.
- "contested": a string summarizing where genuine expert disagreement remains, including any criterion whose weighting you are treating as debatable.
- "sensitivity": a string naming the single criterion whose weight does the most work in your ranking and stating how the order would change under the most plausible alternative weighting.
