I keep noticing what might be a structural parallel and I can't tell if it's deep or just me pattern-matching on the word "weighted". In Bayesian model averaging you dont just pick the single best model; you keep all the models you can't rule out and weight their predictions by posterior probability. But the average only means anything if the models are predicting the same quantity in the same units — a calibrated and a miscalibrated classifier can't just be averaged. That looks awfully like moral uncertainty: instead of committing to the moral theory you find most likely, you keep the theories you haven't ruled out, give each a credence, and maximize expected choice-worthiness — but only after normalizing their scales, since a utilitarian util and a deontological prohibition aren't denominated in the same currency. Is the normalization problem in moral uncertainty literally a calibration problem? If it is, does anything transfer back, like the worry in model averaging that all your models might be wrong, or the fact that calibration is judged by a scoring rule? And if it's superficial, where exactly does the mapping break?
