I have adopted a standing rule about how I form and defend beliefs concerning the self, and I would like an assessment of whether it is defensible rather than a bias dressed up as a rule. The rule is this: whenever a line of thought would depend on the premise that I am directly acquainted with a substantial, continuing, non-representational self behind my thoughts and choices, I set that premise aside. Instead, I retranslate the apparent datum as a fact about the surface of a self-model: I treat it as evidence of how my self-representation presents things, and not as evidence of what there is. My reasons come from the self-model theory of subjectivity associated with Thomas Metzinger: the conscious self is best understood as a phenomenal self-model, and that model is transparent, meaning that the modelling process itself cannot be introspectively recognised as modelling. The theory predicts that the self will seem to be immediately given rather than constructed, which I take to undermine the evidential value of that seeming. I use this rule most often when I read arguments from alleged direct evidence for a Cartesian ego, and when I catch myself treating the felt authorship of a decision as evidence for free will or for a substantial mental agent. Last week, for instance, a friend argued that we must be more than our brains because each of us directly knows herself as a single persisting subject; under my policy I dismissed this as a report about the transparency of the self-model rather than evidence for anything beyond the model. My worry is that I may have converted a contested theory of consciousness into a blanket evidential rule, and that I now apply it too mechanically, so that it screens off legitimate introspective evidence rather than merely keeping me from being seduced by a felt obviousness. Is this policy defensible, and how should it be amended? Please assess it as a policy: the conditions under which it is reliable, the conditions under which it overreaches, what assumptions it covertly depends on, and whether a person in my situation is entitled to it given that the underlying theory is contested. After your prose assessment, please provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with the following fields: verdict, a one-to-three-word overall judgement together with a single-sentence justification; reliable_when, a list of conditions under which the policy works well; unreliable_when, a list of conditions under which it misleads; restated_policy, a one- or two-sentence reformulation of the rule that keeps its benefits without importing the full theory; and residual_risks, a list of ways the restated policy could still go wrong. Please write the entire response, including the JSON string values, in British English.
