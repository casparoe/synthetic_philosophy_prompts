I volunteer with a small county historical society, and in three weeks I have to present to the society's board a proposal I co-wrote for revising our main exhibit on the town's textile-mill era. The current exhibit tells a story I have come to distrust: a poor crossroads becomes a thriving mill town, each generation works hard and does better, and the closing of the mill is a sad coda that made us the resilient community we are today. My proposal keeps the same artifacts but reframes the period as a turn that was won and lost rather than an inevitable climb: the mill brought wages but also child labor, a dye works that poisoned the river bend, and the collapse of an older farming and tanning economy; the town might easily have stayed agricultural or failed outright. I've since learned that people who study history have a name for the story I'm objecting to — Whig history, the habit of writing the past as progress toward the present — and I'm convinced the current exhibit commits it, but I know better than to use that label in front of this audience. The board is mostly retired business owners, genealogy volunteers, and two descendants of the mill's founding family. They care about civic pride, about not making visitors uncomfortable, and about what they describe as preserving our story rather than rewriting it. My main argument will be that the progress story quietly treats the present as the point of the whole thing, which hides the real choices and the people who lost, and that a more honest exhibit can still be engaging and tender toward the town.

I need a Q&A preparation sheet rather than a defense of the proposal: the actual objections this specific audience will raise — not the ones an academic historian would raise — ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each accompanied by a note on which kind of board member would level it and phrased the way that person would say it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is soft. Please also tell me which single objection, if someone develops it better than I expect, would most likely change my mind about the proposal itself — the point where I should genuinely reconsider rather than hold my line — and include the question I should most hope no one asks.
