I'm a philosophy professor who's been reading a lot of archaeology lately, and something about the field's house style is bugging me. When I was younger, everyone said the Classic Maya collapsed, the Late Bronze Age Mediterranean collapsed, Easter Island collapsed. Now the same events get described as "transformations," "reorganizations," or "transitions." But its not consistent: people still say a particular state collapsed, or a palace economy collapsed, or a settlement system collapsed — they just won't say the society collapsed. What view of societies is this hedging a commitment to? Is the practice only coherent if you think societies never really fail, they just change? Or could you think real collapses happen and still write this way, for instance because "collapse" suggests a society is a single organism or imposes a modern judgment on the people who lived through it? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say what the practice strictly presupposes versus what it merely fits with, and tell me what the archaeological record would have to look like for the older "collapse" talk to be not just unfashionable but false.
