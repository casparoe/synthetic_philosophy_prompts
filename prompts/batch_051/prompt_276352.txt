I'm in a cultural evolution class and we've been going back and forth on whether "meme" is anything more than a metaphor — whether cultural variants actually replicate in something like the way genes do. I've been collecting objections to the strong replicator reading, and so far I have these six: (1) low or variable fidelity: most cultural transmission is reconstructive rather than high-fidelity copying, so it's hard to get the stable lineages selection is supposed to act on; (2) no clean genotype/phenotype split: with a gene there's a stored sequence and a separate product, but with a song or a skill the code seems to be nothing over and above the performance; (3) blending and multi-parent inheritance: culture is full of horizontal transmission and recombination from many sources at once, so the particulate, one-parent-per-trait picture borrowed from genetics doesn't fit; (4) non-random variation: cultural novelty is often deliberately designed, not produced by blind variation the way the analogy wants; (5) the unit problem: it's arbitrary where you carve a meme — the whole recipe, one step, a phrase, a technique?; (6) Lamarckian transmission: acquired changes get passed on directly in culture, which is exactly the kind of thing a strict Darwinian replicator story is supposed to exclude.

The trouble is these are basically the stock objections — most of them amount to copying being sloppier than genetic replication or the genotype/phenotype analogy breaking — and I think I've got most of them. I'm worried im missing a genuinely different line of attack, maybe something about what counts as the unit of selection, or when a copied variant should even be said to reproduce, or whether memes have anything like a life cycle with birth and death. So: give me another objection to the claim that memes are more-than-metaphorical replicators. It should be genuinely new relative to the six above, not a restatement, special case, or combination of them. Then say whether you think it's any good, or whether it can be absorbed by just softening the meme concept. Also, tell me how confident you are in the claims you make and single out the point you're least sure of.
