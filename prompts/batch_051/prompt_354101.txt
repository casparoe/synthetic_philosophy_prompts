Assess the following claim: when a language model writes a chain-of-thought (CoT) before its final answer, the CoT transcript faithfully reports the process that actually caused that answer. Two distinct threats complicate the assessment. In a confabulation, the answer is effectively settled before the rationale is written, and the CoT is a plausible-sounding but causally idle reconstruction. In an inscrutable process, the CoT genuinely contributes to producing the answer, but its stated steps do not correspond in any straightforward way to the underlying computation. Assume that, for a fixed model and task family, you can run the following six tests.

(a) Ablation. Compare accuracy with CoT against accuracy when the model answers directly; a large CoT benefit suggests the rationale tokens are causally used.
(b) Local soundness. Use an independent checker to verify the truth and inferential validity of each intermediate step, setting aside whether the final answer is correct.
(c) Counterfactual perturbation. Inject an altered middle step (for example, a false lemma) and ask the model to continue, checking whether the final answer shifts in the direction the alteration would warrant.
(d) Paraphrase robustness. Elicit several semantically equivalent rewrites of the same reasoning path and check whether the answer and the underlying route remain stable.
(e) Introspective agreement. After the model has answered, ask it to give a second explanation of how it reached that answer and measure the overlap with the original CoT.
(f) Failure localization. Across many tasks, determine whether wrong answers can be traced to an identifiable wrong intermediate step, or whether the chain looks locally fine until the final line.

Rank these six tests from strongest to weakest evidence for the faithfulness claim, and assign each a score out of 10, where 10 is decisive evidence and 0 is no evidence. In one sentence each, justify the placement and state which threat—confabulation or inscrutability—the test is best able to detect. Finally, suppose a model passes (a), (d), and (e) strongly but fails (b) and (f). State the conclusion about this model's CoT that is best supported, identify the most likely threat it exhibits, and say what single additional observation would most resolve the remaining ambiguity.
