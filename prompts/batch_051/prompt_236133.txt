In my philosophy of mind course, we have been working through Ryle's The Concept of Mind. Ryle characterizes a category mistake as representing a fact as if it belonged to one logical type or category when it actually belongs to another. His central example is the visitor to Oxford or Cambridge who is shown colleges, libraries, playing fields, and administrative offices, and then asks where the University is. Now consider a present-day large language model that can answer counterfactual questions, adjust its responses to a conversational partner's apparent beliefs, and describe its own reasoning process. Is it a category mistake, in Ryle's sense, to say that such a system understands language, holds beliefs, or reasons? Work through Ryle's criteria honestly: does the claim that the model believes something confuse logical types, or does it attribute a dispositional property of the sort Ryle himself regards as legitimate? Does the case expose a problem in Ryle's category boundary, or does it confirm that his anti-Cartesian point still applies to computational systems? I am less interested in a yes or no answer than in whether this borderline case forces a revision of the criteria Ryle uses.
