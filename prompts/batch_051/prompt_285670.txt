I'm taking a political theory seminar on Habermas, and the professor keeps distinguishing between actual communicative action and strategic action that just has the trappings of discussion. She's been giving verdicts on case after case all term, and I'm trying to reverse-engineer the exact line she's drawing. Her calls so far include:

- A company town hall about a restructuring where all employees are invited and technically free to speak, but everyone knows that people who pushed back in previous rounds were quietly demoted or managed out. She said this doesn't count: people were monitoring their own speech, so the give and take of reasons was broken.
- A city consultation on siting a waste facility where only homeowners were invited, not renters, even though the renters live closest to the proposed site. The meeting itself was polite and open, but she said the discussion wasn't legitimate because the people most affected didn't have standing to participate.
- A citizens' jury on a transit plan, with a random sample of residents, paid time off so nobody loses money by attending, translation, and a facilitator who stopped interruptions. A few members changed their minds after hearing reasons they hadn't considered. She held that up as about as close to the ideal speech situation as real life gets.

Now there's a new case. A town council holds a hearing on a zoning change and all affected groups are there — homeowners, renters, local businesses. Nobody is threatened or pressured, and the facilitator makes sure everyone gets to speak. But the case for the change is delivered by a planning consultant in very technical language, and the lay residents say afterward that they couldn't really follow the argument; they assented because he seemed credible and his charts were polished. The consultant's argument was, in fact, sound.

I can't tell whether her principle is something like no one is coerced or excluded, or something stronger like everyone has to be in a position to actually assess and challenge the reasons. I also can't tell if the translation detail in the jury case mattered to her or was just a nice extra. What would she most likely say about the zoning hearing if she's applying one consistant principle? Lay out the rules she could be running, what each predicts about this case, your best guess about her verdict, and which earlier case is the closest analog. And give me the extra case that would settle which rule she is really using — especially something that isolates whether being allowed to talk is enough for her or whether you also have to be able to follow.
