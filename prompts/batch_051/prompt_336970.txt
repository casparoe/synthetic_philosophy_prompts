In my former life I worked on dynamical systems and on the limits of what can be inferred from time series, and since retiring I have been reading philosophy of mind with the intensity I used to reserve for data. A philosopher friend has been pressing on me the claim that the space of possible experiences open to a finite mind is inexhaustible: not merely that a person will not in fact run through every experience, but that no finite inventory can be completed even in principle, because for any list of experiences there is always a genuinely new one available. I cannot tell whether this is a deep point about the mind or a definitional artifact. If "experience" is individuated by the organism's discriminable internal states, and those states are finite, then the space of experiences is finite and exhaustible in principle; if "experience" is individuated phenomenologically or semantically, then the same physical state may count as many experiences, and enumeration looks open-ended by stipulation. I would like, in the spirit of an operationalizing natural scientist, a way to bring evidence to bear on the claim rather than merely adopting one reading or the other.

Take my friend's claim at its strongest: for any finite mind and any finite history of experiences, there is a genuinely novel experience it could have. Could one design an experiment or observational protocol that would test this? My half-formed idea is that one would need three things: a generative procedure that produces candidate experiences, such as a combinatorial grammar over elementary auditory or visual features or a systematic morphing of textures and tones along many dimensions; a measure of subjective novelty that distinguishes "this particular instance is new" from "this type of experience is new"; and a criterion for saturation, namely whether the rate at which subjects report type-novel experiences declines asymptotically toward zero, or remains bounded away from zero, as the number of sampled candidates grows. I would like you to lay out the strongest version of such a test, being specific about what population, stimuli, novelty measure, and stopping rule you would use, and then say exactly what a positive result would and would not show, and exactly what a negative result would and would not show. In particular, I want to know whether a positive result would establish that the mind's experience space is inexhaustible, or only that the generator itself is open-ended in just the way the mind was claimed to be; and whether a negative result would establish exhaustibility, or only that the operationalization of novelty was too coarse. What part of the original claim could no test of this kind settle, even in principle?
