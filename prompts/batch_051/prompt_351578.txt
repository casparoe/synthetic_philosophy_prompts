It keeps bugging me that the whole argument might turn on whether that feeling is a genuine signal that something is missing or more like a broken alarm. So here's the experiment I'd run if I could, and I'd like your best reasoned prediction instead.

It doesn't create happiness or contentment, and it doesn't blunt sadness, fear, grief, or joy. It just deletes the gnawing-void feeling; stop taking it and the feeling returns within days. A country of about fifty million makes MV-0 free but entirely optional, and within a few years roughly a third of adults take it regularly.

I want your reasoned prediction of where that country stands socially and psychologically by 2055. I care less about your overall verdict than about the mechanisms. Give me exactly five numbered predictions, each a sentence or two, each naming one specific downstream effect, the causal chain that would produce it, and your rough confidence (high/medium/low). For at least two of the five, also name the observation that would make you give the prediction up. No intro paragraph, no conclusion. For each item, seperate what you're treating as settled from what's genuinely contested among people who study this stuff, and if the prediction depends on one side of the signal-versus-glitch debate, say which side; don't present a contested reading as the consensus. What I'd really like are predictions that survive under either interpretation of the feeling, and predictions that cleanly separate the two.
