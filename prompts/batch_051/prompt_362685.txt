Constraints: the three options must be choices a normal high school student or adult could actually face; no exotic technology, no huge populations, no time-bending; none of the options may be strictly dominated by another; and each of the three pairwise judgments must be driven by a different concrete consideration—for example, one comparison turns on autonomy, another on comfort, another on long-term achievement, or pick three that fit your case. After presenting the scenario in one paragraph, walk through the reasoning for each pairwise judgment, say exactly which structural feature of the case breaks transitivity rather than merely reflecting vagueness or inconsistent preferences, and then give the strongest reply a defender of transitivity could make against your constructed case. End by stating what would happen to the cycle if that structural feature were removed: would the case collapse back into a standard spectrum argument where only one value is varying? Keep the whole response under 600 words.
