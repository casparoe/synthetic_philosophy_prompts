I've basically come around to Wang Yangming's view that knowing and acting are one thing: when someone says they know smoking is bad but still smoke, the right diagnosis isn't a gap between knowledge and behavior, it's that they never knew it in the way that counts, because real knowledge is already action-shaped. I like this view for principled reasons—it makes akrasia less mysterious, it treats knowing as an achieved state rather than stored information, and it doesn't let people off the hook with a shrug about weakness of will. But I can't shake the intuition that I can know fully and occurrently that I should exercise today, with no relevant uncertainty or self-deception, and simply not do it, and that this is a real failure rather than proof that I never knew. I'm not looking to be told which side wins. Give me the strongest version of each: what debunking story would have to be true for the intuition to be the thing I drop, what modification would let the theory survive the intuition instead, and is there a sense of 'know' on which both the unity of knowledge and action and the reality of akrasia can be kept?
