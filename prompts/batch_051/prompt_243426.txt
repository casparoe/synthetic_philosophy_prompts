I've been rereading the Quine passage where a field linguist hears gavagai every time a rabbit races by, and I got stuck on how little the linguist is ever in a position to decide among rival translations — whole rabbit, undetached rabbit part, temporal rabbit stage, rabbithood, the scattered fusion of all rabbits, and so on. The standard line is that pointing and collecting assent/dissent with the rabbit in view can't break the tie among the first bunch, because the same occasion that presents a whole rabbit also presents its undetached parts and its momentary stages. But I keep suspecting this is partly an artifact of the linguist being stuck with whatever stimulations happen to wander past in the field.

So here's the variation I'd like to run. Give the linguist a little lab and the chance to elicit assent/dissent in three cleaned-up situations: (S1) a whole live rabbit sitting still in a cage; (S2) a single severed rabbit's foot shown on its own; (S3) a rabbit seen only in 50-millisecond strobe flashes at unpredictable moments throughout its life, so there's no perceived temporal continuity from one presentation to the next. For each situation, and for each candidate rendering of the term — rabbit, undetached rabbit part, rabbit stage, rabbithood, scattered fusion of all rabbits — work out whether the native should assent or dissent if that rendering were correct, and say which candidate(s) each situation knocks out. Then tell me which candidates still make exactly the same predictions across the whole enlarged set of observations, and which pairs the lab actually succeeds in separating. The hard part I keep circling around is this: for the pairs that survive the lab, is the leftover slack merely ordinary underdetermination of theory by evidence (the sort of thing more or better observations could settle), or is it Quine's stronger point that no conceivable observation could settle reference — and could a 'same gavagai?' identity-question break the tie without just relocating the indeterminacy into the word for 'same'?

As you go, keep track of what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this material. If a step depends on a contested reading of Quine — e.g., whether the leftover indeterminacy is merely epistemic or properly ontological — flag that rather than presenting one side as the consensus.
