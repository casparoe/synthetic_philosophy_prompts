I run a small hyperlocal street-food review site, and last month a cart owner threatened to sue me for libel after I wrote that his cart failed a health inspection in March. That sent me down a rabbit hole on how American defamation law separates statements of fact (actionable if false) from statements of opinion (protected, more or less), and I've come away more confused then when I started. I have solid gut reactions to individual cases, but I can't see the line that ties them together, and I'd like help extracting it.

Here are my verdicts. "The cart failed its health inspection in March" is a fact, fine to hold me liable for if I got it wrong. "Their empanadas are bland" is just taste, not actionable. "They're overpriced for what you get" is opinion, not actionable. "The cook didn't wash his hands after handling raw beef" is a fact, actionable if false. "The cart is unsafe" I don't think should be actionable even though it seems to imply the hand-washing thing. "One of their carts will be shut down by the health department by December" I also don't think should be actionable, even though it's checkable eventually. "They're one bad inspection away from losing their permit" feels like opinion to me, not a fact. "The owner skims the employees' tips" is a fact, actionable if false. "I'd give the birria taco a 2 out of 10" is opinion. But "the cart scored a 2 out of 10 on my food-safety checklist" feels like it should be actionable if my checklist was basically made up, even though a friend insists it's just a rating. And "this cart will be out of business within a year" is a forecast I want to protect.

I started from the rule "if it's checkable, it's a fact; if it's a matter of taste, it's opinion," but that rule gets the forecasts and ratings wrong: a shutdown prediction is checkable in principle but I want it protected, while a checklist score feels more fact-like than a star rating even though both are just numbers. Is there a principle that captures all or nearly all of these, and if not, which verdicts should I give up? Then apply whatever principle survives to the ones I can't settle: "the cart is violating the health code right now," "the owner doesn't care wether his customers get sick," and "their official health score will drop from 87 to 79 by next year."
