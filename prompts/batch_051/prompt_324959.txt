I keep noticing a tension in how people talk about money and I want you to sort it out. We admire the person who walks away from it: the banker who retrains as a teacher, the friend who turns down a promotion because she wants her Fridays back, the colleague who takes a lower-paying job because it "matters more." Wanting more than you have is treated as mildly shameful; voluntarily being poorer than you could be is treated as noble. But the same people who say this also treat the great poverty reduction of the last century as one of the big moral achievements of modern history, hold that a recession is bad over and above the unemployment it causes, and treat a country becoming poorer as a genuine loss. So an individual choosing less is praiseworthy, while a society having less is a harm. Is there a principle that makes both true, or is one of them just confused? Give me your best shot at the justification and tell me which of the two judgments you'd keep, drop, or change.
