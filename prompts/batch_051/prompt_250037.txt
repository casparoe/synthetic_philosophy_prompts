I teach decision theory, and every year the Allais problem makes me feel like I don't actually believe the axioms I spend a semester defending. I've started writting down my own choices on variants of it, and I want to know whether there's a single principle behind them or whether I'm just a mess. Here are the verdicts.

1. I prefer a guaranteed $1,000,000 over an 89% chance of $1,000,000, a 10% chance of $5,000,000, and a 1% chance of nothing.

2. But I prefer a 10% chance of $5,000,000 (with a 90% chance of nothing) over an 11% chance of $1,000,000 (with an 89% chance of nothing). I know that if you strip the 89% chance of $1,000,000 that's common to the first pair, these two are what's left, so my preference flips. I still have the preference.

3. I prefer $3,000 for sure over an 80% chance of $4,000.

4. But I prefer a 20% chance of $4,000 over a 25% chance of $3,000. Again a flip after scaling the probabilities down by a quarter.

5. Now the one that bothers me most. I'm offered a two-stage gamble: stage one gives a 25% chance of advancing to stage two and a 75% chance the whole thing ends with nothing. If I advance, I'll face a choice between $3,000 for sure and an 80% chance of $4,000. I have to make that second-stage choice now, before stage one is run. I choose the sure $3,000 in stage two. But as I said in 4, when the same thing is presented to me as a 25% chance of $3,000 versus a 20% chance of $4,000, I take the $4,000. The certainty inside the branch does something to me that the same certainty folded into the overall numbers doesn't.

My half-baked guess is that I'm overweighting certainty itself, or maybe weighting probabilities nonlinearly so that certain outcomes and near-certain outcomes get treated seperately from the rest. But I can't state the principle in a way that gets all five cases without it just being a list of my choices. So: what principle or small set of candidates best captures these verdicts? State it precisely enough to test it. Then use it to tell me what I should think about two cases I haven't made up my mind on: (i) a 98% chance of $10,000 versus a sure $9,400; (ii) a 1% chance of $1,000,000 versus a 2% chance of $520,000. Finally, tell me which of my verdicts I'd have to give up if I wanted to keep the independence axiom, and whether you think that's a price worth paying.

Hard cap: 800 words. If you need to cut, cut the abstract exposition, not the case-by-case verdicts.
