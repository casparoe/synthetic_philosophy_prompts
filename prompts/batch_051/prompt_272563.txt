I just started on the policy review committee at work and we've been going in circles over two sentences in our employee handbook that really do look like they're in tension. Section 4.1, "Equal Treatment," says: "Employment decisions shall be based solely on the individual merits, qualifications, and performance of each candidate or employee; no characteristic of an employee or applicant, including race, sex, religion, disability, age, or sexual orientation, shall be considered in making those decisions." Section 4.7, "Inclusive Hiring," says: "When two or more candidates are equally qualified, the organization shall give preference to a candidate from a group that has been historically underrepresented in our industry, as part of our commitment to remedying the continuing effects of past discrimination." I can't tell whether the second clause is an exception to the first, a clarification of it, or just a contradiction nobody noticed.

The rest of the committee keeps splitting into camps: one says 4.1 states the principle and 4.7 is a narrow, remedy-like exception that doesn't count as discrimination; another says if you can use group membership to break a tie, then you've already admitted group membership is relevant to how you value candidates, and 4.1 collapses. We have to write guidance explaining what field managers are actually allowed to do. Can the two clauses be reconciled? Give me the strongest reconciliations you can (different stages of the hiring process, different senses of "qualifications" or "merit," formal versus substantive equality, a distinction between prohibited animus and permissible remedy, etc.), and then say which reading is the most honest rather than special pleading. Also, I'd like you to use this to answer the underlying question: what actually makes a ground of discrimination suspect in the first place—is it immutability, a history of subordination, irrelevance to the job, or something else—and does accepting the second clause force us to treat some protected grounds as less suspect than others?
