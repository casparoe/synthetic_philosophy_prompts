Rationalist commentary on markets often treats Aristotle's objection to usury in Book I of the Politics as an elementary mistake: money lent at interest does return more than the principal, and both parties consent, so the unnaturalness of money breeding money is just a failure to understand compounding. That seems right to me, and I want to be able to state the error in rationalist terms: where does the compounding argument bite hardest against Aristotle's supposed teleology?
