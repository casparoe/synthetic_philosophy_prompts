I am working through a statute in a seminar on mental privacy and cognitive liberty, and I keep changing my mind about how it reaches a contemporary practice. Section 9(1) of the model Neural Data Protection Act provides: "'Neural data' means information about the activity of an individual's brain or nervous system that is obtained by means of a neural device, and includes information derived from such information. No person may collect, store, process, sell, or otherwise use an individual's neural data without the individual's specific, informed, and freely given consent." The provision was drafted when the main concerns were consumer EEG headsets and implantable brain–computer interfaces, and its preamble states that the Act exists to protect mental privacy and cognitive liberty.

Consider a workplace-analytics company, FocusSight, whose software runs on ordinary office laptops and never employs a neural device. A webcam records small colour changes in a worker's face, blink rate, gaze direction, and pupil dilation, while the laptop logs typing rhythm, cursor movement, and mouse pressure. A machine-learning model processes these signals and produces continuous scores of the worker's attention, cognitive load, stress, and emotional valence; the company sells these scores to employers, who use them in decisions about promotion and retention. A worker objects that the platform is inferring what is happening in her mind without her consent. FocusSight replies that it collects only video images and keyboard telemetry from the surface of the body, and that no neural data is ever obtained. Could you set out, for each of the following approaches, what it would imply in this case: the plain wording; the evident purpose of the statute; what the drafters would likely have said if asked; and what a reasonable reader would take the provision to mean? Which of these approaches should govern, and what does the fact that this is a genuinely disputable case reveal about whether the concept of neural data tracks a measurement, an organ, or a protected zone of mental life?
