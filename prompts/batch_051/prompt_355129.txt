I'm taking a course this term on collective responsibility and business ethics, and the seminar keeps returning to a question that seems to have stalled both in the room and, from what the instructor says, in the wider literature: can a company be blamed as a company, or is blame only ever properly directed at individual people? One side takes an individualist line. A corporation has no mind, no conscience, and no capacity to suffer or feel remorse; so a sentence like "the company is to blame" is really shorthand for saying that some person or group inside the company is to blame, or at least that such people should be identified and held responsible. The other side replies that companies have decision procedures, reporting lines, and policies that make organizational acts come apart from any single person's intentions: a firm can do things that no individual decided, it persists through changes in personnel, and it is typically the entity, not merely the people within it, that we fine, sanction, or condemn. Each side can predict the other's moves by now, and neither has budged.

What I would like is not a verdict but a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is. Is it a substantive moral disagreement about what blame is for — say, whether blame must reach a subject who can feel it, or whether it can function as a community's forward-looking way of expressing condemnation and reshaping an organization's incentives? Is it a metaphysical disagreement about whether group intentions are real or merely a useful fiction? Is it partly verbal, with the two sides using "blame" in different senses? Or is the debate stuck because the question is ill-posed until we specify what we are blaming the company for — an accident, a policy, a culture — and in what register: criminal, civil, or purely moral? Please diagnose the structure of the impasse, and say what would count as progress given that diagnosis. I would also like you to be explicit about your own confidence: which argument or consideration would lead you to a different diagnosis, and where is your reasoning most likely to fail?
