I'm giving a seminar paper next month defending the claim that manipulation is not distinguished from legitimate persuasion by the means of influence — deception, emotional pressure, even good arguments can figure in both — but by the relationship the influencer takes up toward the other's will: manipulation is the attempt to determine what someone does or believes while presenting that determination as the person's own self-governance, so that the influencer's authority is exercised but disavowed, whereas legitimate persuasion leaves the other as the author of the change and acknowledges her standing to accept or reject what is offered. I defend this largely by cases: the same guilt-inducing appeal can be manipulative in one relationship and not in another, and a formally impeccable argument can be deployed manipulatively. The audience is a mixed department: two Kantians inclined to locate the wrong of manipulation in the failure to respect rational agency, a political philosopher who suspects 'manipulation' is simply influence we disapprove of for independent moral reasons, and two empirically minded psychologists who think the folk concept is too inconsistent to sustain a principled distinction. Give me a Q&A prep sheet, not a defense of my view: the eight or so objections this specific audience will actually raise, ordered by how damaging they are and phrased as those people would put them, a two-minute reply to each, a note on where each reply is soft, what I should concede up front, and the question I'm hoping nobody asks. Write it in continuous prose, as an email or letter — no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
