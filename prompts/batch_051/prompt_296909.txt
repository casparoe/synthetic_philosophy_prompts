A friend who read a popular introduction to Bayesian epistemology summarized Jeffrey conditionalization for me like this: "Jeffrey's rule is the fallback you use when your evidence is too imprecise to count as learning a proposition, so ordinary Bayesian conditionalization is unavailable. You take the possible observations you might have made, give each one a probability representing how strongly that observation seems to you to have occurred, and average the opinions you would have reached by conditionalizing on each, using those probabilities as weights. Because the result is an average of the possible updates, uncertain evidence can only ever move your probabilities less than the corresponding crisp observation would have. So Jeffrey conditionalization is basically a cautious, approximate substitute for ordinary conditionalization; whenever you can point to a crisp proposition you actually learned, you should just conditionalize on that proposition instead."
