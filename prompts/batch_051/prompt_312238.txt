I am preparing an essay for my course on Berkeley’s economic writings, and I want to test whether my reading of The Querist is internally coherent before I present it. I have settled on the following claims.

1. Money is not itself wealth; it is best understood as a ticket or counter that allows wealth, understood as the produce of labour and industry, to circulate.
2. What Ireland chiefly lacks is not gold or silver but an adequate domestic circulating medium, which a national bank should supply by issuing paper notes.
3. Those notes need not be redeemable in gold or silver, because their value depends on public confidence and on being accepted for public taxes.
4. Nevertheless, the notes should be secured by land, and their issue should be limited to what domestic circulation requires.
5. The export of necessaries and the import of foreign luxuries are genuinely harmful, since they deplete the country’s real wealth.

I can see that (1) might conflict with (5), and that (3) might conflict with (4), but I cannot tell which of these conflicts are substantive and which are only apparent, and there may be tensions I have not noticed. Please rank the tensions by severity, say which commitments would have to be revised to resolve them, and indicate the least costly way to make the set hang together.
