I keep noticing people defend competition—in schools, sports, hiring, startups—by reaching for an evolutionary picture: pressure selects the fittest, the survivors come out stronger, and so the whole group improves. i cant tell whether this is a genuine structural parallel or just a vibe, and i want to know whether the builds-character conclusion actually needs it. What maps onto what? In natural selection you need variation, differential reproduction, heritability, and the environment doing the selecting; the process has no foresight, only optimizes fitness relative to a local environment, and can walk itself into a dead end. In a competitive market or sports league, the losers are not dead—they are still around, can learn, form a new team, switch fields, or just try again—and the very fact of having lost changes them. So: is the parallel between social competition and natural selection deep or superficial? Tell me what would have to be true of the social side for the builds-character inference to transfer over from biology, and name the strongest thing the parallel predicts that comes out false.
