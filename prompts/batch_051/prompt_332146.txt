I am drafting the penultimate chapter of a novel set in an intentional farming community, the Coombe Fellowship, founded in 1971. Disputes in the novel are settled by reference to the community's founding charter, and the clause around which the chapter turns reads as follows:

'Article IV. A member may withdraw by delivering written notice to the steward. A member who, without such notice, leaves the land and does not return for a year shall be deemed to have withdrawn, and shall forfeit all claim upon the land, the labor, and the common store; thereafter the Fellowship shall owe no further duty of support to that member or to that member's dependents.'

The case in question is one the drafters plainly did not anticipate. Elspeth, a founder-member, has not left the land: she still occupies the northwest cottage at the edge of the property. But for six years she has attended no assembly, done no share of the work, eaten no common meal, contributed nothing to the community's upkeep, and refused every offer of help. She has severed each practical tie while remaining in place — a 'going without leaving,' as another character puts it.

The charter contains no provision for expulsion and no procedure for declaring a non-participant no longer a member, so Article IV is the only text the characters can invoke. The chapter's dispute is whether Article IV deems Elspeth to have withdrawn, such that the Fellowship may lawfully reclaim the cottage and owes her no further support.

I would be glad of an analysis of what the main approaches to interpretation would yield here: the plain words of the clause; its evident purpose; what the original drafters would most likely have said if asked about a member who cuts every practical tie while staying on the land; and what a reasonable member of the Fellowship, reading the charter in the circumstances, would have understood it to require. And because the chapter is also where the novel tries to say something about what belonging consists in, I would like to know what you think the remaining members stand to lose if they read the clause in the way they are tempted to read it.
