Retired chemist here, new to epistemology, and I keep getting stuck on this "bootstrapping" business. A friend pressed this argument on me.

(1) The liquid in this beaker is blue—I can see that it is.
(2) It visually appears blue to me.
(3) So on this occasion my color vision got the answer right.
(4) I've done this same check on hundreds of colored samples, and my color vision has come out right nearly every time.
(5) So my color vision is a reliable detector of color.

As somebody who used to calibrate instruments, this smells like using a spectrophotometer's own readings to certify the spectrophotometer. But I can't locate the bad step. So: is the reasoning legitimate? If not, which step exactly fails—and is the failure a logical invalidity, a bad inductive leap, or something epistemic like circularity or question-begging? If it is legitimate, then I seem able to know my color vision is reliable just by using it, and I don't see why a person with a wonky "spirit detector" couldn't run the same argument and conclude their detector is reliable. Is that a reductio of the argument, or should I just swallow the easy knowledge?
