I keep running into "Chesterton's fence" in arguments with a friend who sits with me on our housing association's repairs committee. His version of the principle is that you must not tear a fence down until you know why it was put up, and he recently used it to stop a proposal I made for changing how we triage and schedule maintenance. He told me that unless I could state the original rationale for the present system, nobody was entitled to touch it. I am not an academic, but I have been reading around the topic on my own — some Burke as well as Chesterton — and I can tell that several different thoughts are tangled up in that one sentence, but I cannot separate them cleanly. Could you lay out, as a menu, the distinct precise claims "don't tear down a fence until you know why it was put up" could be making? In particular, I can see that "why it was put up" might mean the builder's original intention, the job the arrangement does now, or the problem it once solved that everyone has since forgotten — and these come apart whenever a system has been repurposed over time. I can also see that the principle might be about knowledge, about whose judgment deserves deference, or only about where the burden of proof sits; and it bothers me that, as I understand the original, even Chesterton leaves room for removing the fence once you see what it is for, which makes my friend's version sound stricter than the source. For each reading you identify, tell me (a) whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or something false; (b) whether it captures the Burkean thought that established arrangements carry accumulated experience that no single person possesses; and (c) whether it actually supports my friend's conclusion that I had to supply the original rationale before the change could even be discussed. I suspect the principle is being used as a conversation-stopper, but I also want to know whether there is a stronger reading I ought to accept, especially in the case where an arrangement is doing active harm and no one alive knows why it exists.
