I am a novelist, and the vocabulary I actually work in when I think about how events unfold is the vocabulary of craft: plot and story, cause and consequence, setup and payoff, suspense, narrative inevitability, and the difference between an outline and a finished draft. I keep hearing it claimed that a process can be fully deterministic and yet unpredictable, because chaos theory shows that arbitrarily small differences in initial conditions can grow until they swamp any finite measurement; the philosophical upshot is supposed to be that determinism is a thesis about how the world is, while predictability is a thesis about what an observer can know, so unpredictability does not by itself indicate indeterminism or chance. Restate that position, with its distinctions among determinism, predictability, and chance, in my novelistic vocabulary as faithfully as you can, piece by piece, without pretending the mapping is cleaner than it is. Then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which you had to distort to fit, and which have no expression at all in how I think about narrative. In particular, tell me whether my craft vocabulary is missing something real, or whether the distinction between determinism and predictability is doing less work than it appears once it is forced into my terms. I have a working intuition that a finished draft is deterministic in something like this sense, and that a reader's inability to foresee the ending is something like a prediction horizon; I want to know how much of that intuition survives. Please say how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the point you are least sure of.
