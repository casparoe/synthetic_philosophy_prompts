Build me a case where a Wang Yangming-style unity-of-knowledge-and-action theorist is forced to say a person doesn't know a moral principle she can articulate fluently, while a seperatist about knowledge and action is forced to say she plainly does know it. Quick background in case you need it: on the unity view, real knowledge of the good just is the knowing that shows up in action, and a principle you can quote but never live by is only a semblance of knowledge; the separatist treats knowledge as something you can have fully while failing to act on it, as in garden-variety akrasia. I'm using this in a class to test whether the unity view has real content or just quietly redefines 'knows,' so the verdicts have to be generated by the two views, not stipulated by you.

Make the person strong on everything we normally count as knowledge: she can state the principle, justify it, teach it, and apply it correctly to unfamiliar cases — including the very type of situation she keeps failing in, when that situation is described abstractly or as happening to someone else. She also keeps endorsing the principle before and after the failure; she doesn't change her mind afterward. Honesty-when-flattery-would-be-easier seems a good candidate virtue, but use whatever gives the cleanest case. Then build one recurring, mundane situation — family or workplace, no addiction, no coercion, no mental illness, no tight time pressure — where she reliably fails to act on the principle. Arrange the details so the unity theorist can't just fall back on 'she was overcome,' while the separatist can look at the same details and call it weakness of will, which presupposes the knowledge it fails to enact.

Then do the check: state the belief, the recurring failure, and the evidence she knows the principle. Give each side's verdict, and say exactly what 'knowledge' has to mean on the unity side for their verdict to follow without being a semantic trick. Then identify the single feature of the case that's doing the real work, and say what the unity view is left with if you remove that feature. Finish with the strongest attack on the case from a defender of the unity view: how do they stop it from looking like they've just redefined 'knows' out from under an obviously competent person? Keep the scenario under about 350 words, and no perfect predictors, demons, or sci-fi.
