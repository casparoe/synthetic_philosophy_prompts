(a) I still basically buy virtue ethics: the main question in ethics is what kind of person to be, and claims about right action are derivative from that — right, roughly, is what the person with practical wisdom would do.
(b) I'm also convinced by the situationist line of evidence: whether people help, lie, or cave to pressure depends heavily on small features of the situation (being in a hurry, ambient mood, whether there's a deadline), and broad traits like honesty or compassion are rare and much more situation-bound than ordinary moral talk assumes.
(c) So at the practical level — at work, or in public policy — I think we should mostly change defaults, remove frictions, and design situations rather than try to improve people's character. If the goal is more helping, the move is to stop people from being in such a hurry, not to appeal to their compassion.
