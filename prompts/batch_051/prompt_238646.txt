I am a novelist working on a book that turns on a modified version of Nozick's experience machine, and I need help working out the philosophical structure before I draft the scene. In my variation, the machine offers no menu of experiences; its only adjustable parameter is the entrant's emotional life, chosen in advance and then forgotten. Setting A reproduces the Stoic ideal of the well-ordered psyche: the machine blocks grief, fear, jealousy, and the other passions, leaving only calm evaluative states. Setting B reproduces a Romantic ideal: it guarantees a life of intense, varied passions—elation, longing, devastation—directed at events and people the entrant will believe are real, though they are simulated. Nozick's standard verdict against the original machine is that we care not merely about how things feel from the inside but about actually being in contact with reality and actually being a certain kind of person. Does my variation change that verdict? Work through what the Stoic thesis that the passions are noise in the good life and the Romantic thesis that passionate feeling partly constitutes the good life each imply about the choice between the two settings, and say precisely where the two views part company—over whether to enter at all, over which setting to choose, or only over the reasons.
