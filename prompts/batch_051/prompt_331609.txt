Give me a working definiton of "the same language" — the relation that's supposed to hold between two people, two texts, or the same community at two times when we say they speak or write one language rather than two, or a language versus a dialect of it. I want a definition that gets these cases right:

- A Roman letter from around 100 BC and a modern Italian newspaper: clearly not the same language.
- Dante's Commedia and a 1960s RAI news broadcast: clearly the same language.
- Venetian and standard Italian: I have no idea — linguists apparently treat Venetian as a separate Romance language, but in practice everyone calls it a dialect of Italian.
- Serbian and Croatian: mutually intelligible, officially two languages.
- Hindi and Urdu: basically the same spoken language, different scripts and national identities.
- Scots and English: I lean dialect, but I'm not happy about it.
- Cantonese and Mandarin: not mutually intelligible, but both get called Chinese.
- Norwegian Bokmål and Danish: I can read Denmarks newspapers if I know Norwegian, but they're two languages.
- Old English and modern English: "same language" only in a historical sense; zero mutual intelligibility.

I keep going back and forth on whether "same language" carves nature at the joints or is mostly a sorites-style label we inherited from states and schools. The old line about a language being a dialect with an army and a navy keeps feeling right even though I want a purely linguistic answer, and mutual intelligibility seems to do most of the work until it gives obviously wrong answers for Serbian/Croatian and just can't be applied to dead speakers we can't test. If you agree the term is doing two jobs at once, give me the two definitions, run every case through both, and tell me where the pair together still gives the wrong answer or no answer. And if you can say anything about the over-time cases: I'm especially interested in whether Latin-becoming-Italian and Old-English-becoming-Modern-English are the same kind of thing as the Serbian/Croatian case, or whether "same language over time" is basically just a name we keep reusing while the thing itself changes underneath.
