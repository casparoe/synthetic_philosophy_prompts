I keep going back and forth on this and want someone to actually run the criteria instead of waving at them. People say a climate projection is robust when (1) it shows up across structurally different models rather than one code base, (2) the errors in the models are plausibly uncorrelated, and (3) it survives when you tweak parameters instead of just being tuned to the same calibration targets. Now here's the case. A single modeling center builds forty versions of the same atmosphere–ocean general circulation model. They all share the same code and the same subgrid parameterization schemes; the only thing that varies is twenty-odd cloud-related parameters, moved around within expert ranges, and every member gets nudged toward the same reanalysis during the historical period. The resulting ensemble gives a really tight range for regional rainfall change under four degrees of warming, much tighter than the multi-model spread. Is that robust in the usual sense? Go criterion by criterion, then tell me whether the case shows the criteria are smuggling in structural diversity, and whether someone at a water utility should treat the tight range as decision-relevant uncertainty or as an artifact.
