I'm writing up my views on effective altruism for a discussion and keep putting off checking whether they actually hang together, so here goes. I'm on board with the EA starting point: most of the good I can do is through giving, and I should allocate donations using evidence and impartial cost-effectiveness reasoning, not warm feelings. I also think earning to give is underated and probably beats working at a nonprofit for someone with my skills. But I've also come to believe the institutional critique: EA systematically neglects structural, political causes of suffering because those are hard to measure, and this isn't a minor blind spot but gets baked into its rankings. Relatedly, I think measurability bias does a lot of work in cause prioritization, pulling toward things like bednets and deworming and away from deeper causes. And yet in practice I still think the right move for me right now is to follow GiveWell-type recommendations rather than try to fix political systems. I honestly cant tell whether there's a real conflict here or whether I'm just describing different levels—individual versus collective, epistemic versus practical. Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest revision that would make the set cohere? Rank the tensions by severity and say what each would cost to resolve.
