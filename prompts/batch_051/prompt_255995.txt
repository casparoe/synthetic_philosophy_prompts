Write a short dialogue between two characters: one who defends an al-Ghazālī-style occasionalism about causation (God is the only true cause, and created things are just occasions), and one who defends the view that created things have genuine secondary causal powers of their own (fire really does burn cotton, through a power God gave it and sustains, even though God remains the ultimate source of everything). Both characters should be sharp and informed, and neither should be a strawman. They should grant the other's strong points where they can, and respond to what the other actually said, not to a weaker version of it.

Make the debate turn on this specific crux: whether the regular pairing of cotton touching fire and burning is enough to underpin explanation, prediction, and ordinary causal talk, or whether you need a real connection beyond the regularity. The occasionalist should push the idea that any necessity we read into nature is something we are projecting from habit or custom, and that God's steady custom does all the actual work. The defender should explain how a created power can be real without making God redundant, and should press the occasionalist on what a law of nature could even be on their view. The dialogue should have real movement: by the end at least one of them should make a substantive concession or refine their position. Dont let it collapse into them agreeing that the dispute is merely verbal — that would be the cheap way out.

Keep the whole thing under 700 words.
