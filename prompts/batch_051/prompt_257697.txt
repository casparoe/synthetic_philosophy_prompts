I keep seeing deplatforming talked about as punishment by other means, but I've never actually seen anyone run the comparison properly, they just assert it and move on. I want you to do it for the August 2018 bans of Alex Jones and Infowars. Roughly what happened: Apple pulled his podcasts from iTunes, then Facebook removed his pages, YouTube took down his channel, Spotify removed some episodes, and Twitter suspended him a few days later, all citing violations of their policies on hate speech or harassment. Treat that cluster of events as a case study in whether social sanctions really are punishments. Take the standard rationales of legal punishment — retribution, specific and general deterrence, incapacitation, rehabilitation, and the expressive/denunciatory view — and map each one onto the episode as carefully as you can. For each, I want to know who the punishing agent is, what the offence is, what the sentence is, who the victim is, and what proportionality or desert would even come to. Then do the reverse: say where the analogy between platform bans and legal punishment breaks down — private actors, no due process, vaguely specified norms, commercial incentives, the lack of any formal 'release' — and how much those disanalogies matter for each rationale. At the end, tell me whether the episode shows deplatforming is best understood as punishment, as refusal to do business, as something else entirely, or as a hybrid, and whether the fact that the punishment theories half-fit says anything interesting about the theories themselves.
