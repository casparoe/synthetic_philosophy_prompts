I sit on a script committee that devises writing systems for previously unwritten languages. We have now accumulated several decisions that we treat as precedent, and I would like a written recommendation about a new case.

Our first precedent involved a language in which tone distinguishes otherwise identical words, and in which a word's tone remains constant when morphemes are added. We decided to write the tone on every syllable, even where a reader could usually recover it from the surrounding grammar or lexicon.

Our second precedent involved a language in which voiceless stops are aspirated at the beginning of a word and unaspirated after /s/, with aspiration never distinguishing one word from another. We decided not to write aspiration: one letter represents each stop in all environments.

Our third precedent involved a language with a single nasal phoneme /n/. Its pronunciation is [m] before labial consonants, [ŋ] before velar consonants, and [n] elsewhere, so a frequent prefix sounds different across words. We decided to write that prefix as ⟨in⟩ everywhere, rather than writing the nasal that is actually pronounced.

Our fourth precedent involved a language in which vowels are predictably long in open, stressed syllables and short elsewhere, and the difference never distinguishes words. We decided not to write vowel length.

The new proposal concerns a language with regular vowel harmony in a productive suffix. The suffix is pronounced [e] after stems with front vowels and [a] after stems with back vowels. In root syllables, [e] and [a] contrast and distinguish many words, so they are plainly independent phonemes; only inside this suffix is the vowel entirely determined by the stem. Two spellings have been proposed. One would write the suffix as it is pronounced on each word, yielding two written variants. The other would choose a single written form for the suffix and use it in every environment.

If we want to be consistent with our own precedents, what should we decide? Which earlier decision is the closest analog, and does it determine the new case or only constrain it? Set out the competing principles that fit what we have done so far, say which spelling each principle would require, and give a best recommendation with reasons. Finally, describe one further case whose structure would separate these principles even more sharply than the present case.
