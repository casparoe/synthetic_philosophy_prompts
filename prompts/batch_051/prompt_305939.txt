Two people I was with got into an argument about whether you can say the U.S. is "due" for a fall because of how long past great powers lasted. (1) Dan said the outside view is clear: of the historical great powers people usually list, most lasted only a couple centuries, and the U.S. is right at that age, so the base rate alone should raise your estimate of serious decline well above what most of us feel. (2) Mira said that's reference-class gerrymandering. Why "great powers" rather than "constitutional democracies," "countries in the top five percent of GDP for a century," or "nuclear-armed states"? Different classes give different base rates, so his number isn't a neutral fact, but a conclusion smuggled into the choice of comparison class. (3) Dan replied that every empire's elites thought their case was different too; "we're special" is the inside view's favorite move, and the whole point of the outside view is not to let your own case talk you out of the distribution. The class doesn't have to be perfect to shift your prior. (4) Mira said the outside view only gets that authority when the class is causally relevant and homogeneous with many independent cases. Great powers are few, and lumping Rome with the Soviet Union isn't a natural kind, so what's doing the real work is a hidden theory about states, not a statistical base rate. Could you go through this move by move: say who's right about what at each step, rate each move if you can, then say who's more right overall and what they're both missing?
