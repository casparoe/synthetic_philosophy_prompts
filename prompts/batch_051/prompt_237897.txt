I'm an ML engineer, not a philosophy person, so if I'm mangling this please say so. We've been arguing at work about whether to use process-based or outcome-based supervision for training our models: outcome-based gives reward only when the final answer is right, process-based scores each step of the reasoning along the way. We're mostly deciding on cost and data-labeling grounds, but a month ago I went down a rabbit hole reading about luck egalitarianism, and now I can't unsee a parallel. Outcome-based supervision judges a model by how the run turned out, which feels like judging a person by results that could be partly brute luck. Process-based supervision judges each step on its own merits, which feels like judging a person by what they did given what they controlled at each point. So would switching our reward model to process-based basically make our training luck-egalitarian? Is that even a coherent thing to ask, or am I just patern matching on the word luck? And if there is something to it, do the standard moves in luck egalitarianism (option luck vs brute luck, for example) transfer over and tell us when process supervision is actually fairer rather then just more granular?
