Quine was willing to make epistemology continuous with natural science, but he treated moral evaluation as having no comparable empirical footing: on his view, first-order moral claims lack the kind of observational checkpoints that let scientific inquiry rationally force convergence among competent inquirers. Call this the Quinean asymmetry: mature scientific disagreements could in principle be settled by empirical evidence in a way that first-order moral disagreements could not. I want your credence that the Quinean asymmetry holds, decomposed rather than asserted as a bare number. Assign rough probabilities to at least the following sub-claims and give one line of justification for each: (a) ordinary first-order moral statements have genuine cognitive content; (b) to the extent they have empirical content, that content underdetermines central moral verdicts far more severely than it underdetermines scientific theory choice; (c) the apparent rational intractability of moral disagreement is better explained by absent observational checkpoints than by the practical, stance-like role of moral language; and (d) if the asymmetry holds, it is better explained by a non-cognitivist or expressivist treatment of moral language than by realism about evidentially inaccessible moral facts. Show how you combine these into a single credence, say which sub-claim your final number is most sensitive to, and explain what evidence or argument would move that sub-claim most. Commit to definite numbers and a definite view rather than surveying options. Write in American English, and keep the response under 400 words.
