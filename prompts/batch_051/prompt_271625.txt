Analyze the International Military Tribunal at Nuremberg as a case study in the principle of non-retroactivity in criminal law, and then test the analysis briefly against the Tokyo tribunal.

The familiar worry is that the Nuremberg defendants were convicted of crimes against peace and crimes against humanity, categories that were only given precise legal content in the charter drawn up by the Allied powers after the war, and that the proceedings therefore violated the maxim nullum crimen sine lege. I know that standard story. What I want is a slower, two-directional analysis rather than a verdict for or against the tribunal.

Start by treating the tribunal's architects and judges as facing a genuine conflict between two legal ideals: the demand that grave wrongdoing be prosecuted, and the demand that no one be punished under a rule created after the fact. Lay out how a strict legal positivist and a natural-law or inner-morality-of-law critic would each describe that conflict and what each would say the tribunal should have done — apply only pre-existing law, ground the convictions in principles superior to enacted law, pass retrospective legislation openly, or decline certain charges. Then identify which features of the trials this framework explains well and which it misses or distorts. Give particular weight to the fact that the tribunal was created, staffed, and bounded by the victors, who did not put their own wartime conduct under the same standard: is that fact an objection to the retroactivity analysis, a separate victors'-justice objection, or both?

Finally, run the episode back against the theory. Does Nuremberg show that the principle of non-retroactivity has a principled exception, or that the tribunal was a political act dressed in legal form? Does the fact that several judges at the Tokyo counterpart, most famously the Indian justice Radhabinod Pal, dissented on precisely these grounds strengthen the case for skepticism about the Nuremberg precedent, or does it reveal the limits of the retroactivity objection itself? Please write the answer in British English.
