I am a PhD student in cultural evolution, working mostly with the toolkit of the cognitive science of religion: I treat religions as populations of loosely bundled representations whose differential spread is to be explained by features of the representations themselves (minimal counterintuitiveness, inferential potential), by the credibility of the people who display and transmit them, and sometimes by group-level selection. Within that framework I find I cannot say, except by hand-waving, what Freud was claiming when he argued in The Future of an Illusion that religious ideas are illusions, that they owe their power to wishes rather than to evidence, that the god-idea is modeled on the child's relationship to a protective father, that religion runs on mechanisms kin to obsessional neurosis, and that it will dissolve as scientific habits of mind spread.

I would like you to restate Freud's view of religion, as faithfully as possible, in the vocabulary I actually use: cultural variants and their transmission, cognitive attractors, credibility-enhancing displays, byproduct versus adaptive explanations, and group-level functions. Then give me the residue report. For each component of Freud's position, tell me what translates cleanly, what had to be distorted or supplemented with assumptions my framework does not already contain, and what has no expression at all. I am especially interested in two things. First, whether the epistemic content of his charge, that religion is an illusion precisely because it is wish-generated, can be represented in a framework that is deliberately neutral about the truth of the beliefs whose spread it studies, and whether that neutrality is a strength or a place where my framework is simply silent. Second, whether Freud's individual-level story about infantile helplessness, the father, and neurotic mechanisms is a genuine rival to cultural-evolutionary explanations, or whether it can be absorbed as a hypothesis about early-life social learning and the psychological content of religious attractors. I want the translation to be honest: do not smuggle assumptions from my framework into Freud's view and then declare that they were already there.
