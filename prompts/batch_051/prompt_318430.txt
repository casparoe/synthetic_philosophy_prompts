Here's a mechanism I want someone to actually run through. A forecasting platform (the sort of thing someone would pitch on here) introduces two payout tiers: if your prediction pays off, you get the full payout only if the pre-registered reasoning you attached to the bet is judged by a panel of other forecasters to genuinely support the call; if you were right but your reasoning was missing, vauge, or bad, you collect a small consolation fraction. The pitch is that this makes the market pay for knowledge rather than mere true belief — Meno's actual problem, why the justified thing is worth more than a lucky guess. I'm not asking whether the goal is good; I'm asking how the machine behaves. What sort of trader does this select for and drive out, and what does it do to liquidity and calibration? How does it get gamed — reasoning vague enough to read as supporting whichever outcome materializes, copying strong arguments and backdating pre-registration, socially gaming the judges, paying people to write justifications? Is judge-based verification secretly just a reputation system with more paperwork? Does it track anything like justification, or mostly prose skill and social calibration? And does any fix for one failure mode — anonymous reasoning, judge pay, decoy justifications — open up a bigger one?
