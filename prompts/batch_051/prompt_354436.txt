I've sort of come round to the view that no finite sequence is random in its own right: randomness attaches to the generating process, and all talk of a particular finite string being random is relative-to-something — a reference machine, a null model, some level of statistical significance. People who say a hundred-bit string is random are, I think, either saying it was produced by a fair coin or saying it incompressible enough given a description language, and those are quite different claims. What would actually count against this? I don't just mean the standard line that Kolmogorov complexity is defined only up to a constant; I want the formal results, cryptographic practice, or thought experiments that should move me. Are there live notions of finite randomness that are not derivative in the way I've claimed? Is my view falsifiable rather than just a decision about words, and does that matter? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about whether I've insulated the position. Write your answer in British English.
