I've been working through R. M. Hare's universal prescriptivism and Marcus Singer's generalization argument, and I'm stuck on a genuine clash. The theory is attractive to me in a way few things in ethics are: it says there's a deep symmetry requirement built into moral language, that I can't prescribe an action for myself that I wouldn't prescribe for everyone in my situation. That single idea seems to explain, all at once, why free-riding is wrong, why special pleading feels like cheating, why 'I may break this promise but you must keep yours' is incoherent. And it gives me something like a decision procedure — before acting, ask whether I could will that everyone act on the same maxim. That's a lot of explanatory power for one principle, and I don't want to give it up.

But I have a firm intuition about a kind of case that the view appears to condemn. Suppose my maxim is: spend my life making music rather than doing socially necessary work. A world where everyone did that would be unworkable — no one would grow food, run hospitals, fix the grid. So the universalization test seems to say my choice is wrong. Yet it feels obviously permissible for some people to be musicians, and a world where no one could choose anything that failed the everyone-does-it test would be absurd. When I press the view on this, the standard reply is that I've described the maxim badly: the real maxim is something like 'pursue a career suited to my talents' or 'contribute to society in my own way,' which universalizes fine. But then I start to suspect the maxim description is doing all the work, and that with enough cleverness any act can be re-described into a universalizable maxim. If the procedure only gives results after I've already decided how to describe the act, im not sure what its giving me.

So: which should yield — the theory or the intuition? Or is there a reading on which they're compatible that isn't just ad hoc maxim re-description? I want a committed answer, not a survey of options. Take a definite position and defend it, and also say what would have to be true for you to switch to each of the other resolutions.
