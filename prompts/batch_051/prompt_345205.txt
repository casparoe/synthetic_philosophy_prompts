A popular comparative politics book I read summarizes Tocqueville's contrast between American and European attitudes toward inequality roughly like this: Americans care more about equality of conditions than about political liberty, so they tolerate centralization as long as it treats everyone the same, while Europeans accept inherited rank but fiercely defend local liberties; the European attitude is therefore more defensible because it acknowledges real human differences and resists soft despotism, whereas the American attitude is self-undermining because its envy of distinction produces conformity. I suspect this is partly a fair reading of Tocqueville and partly the author's own argument being projected onto him, but I don't know how to separate the two. Please give me an accuracy audit: distinguish what Tocqueville actually claimed from what is a common simplification or an interpolation, and say whether the conclusion that the European attitude is more defensible follows from the accurate parts once the misrepresentations are corrected.
