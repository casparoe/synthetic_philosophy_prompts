I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mathematics, working on structuralism and the individuation of mathematical objects. I have an argument that seems to show that the two roots of x² + 1 over ℝ are literally the same object, and I take that to be a reductio of one of my premises, but I can't determine which premise deserves the blame. Each has looked compulsory to me at some point, so I'd be grateful for a diagnosis.

Write i and −i for the two complex roots. Consider:

(1) Complex conjugation is an automorphism of ℂ that fixes ℝ pointwise.

(2) Therefore any first-order statement expressible in the language of fields, even with constants for the real numbers, that holds of i holds equally of −i; no such statement discriminates between the two roots.

(3) If no expressible condition tells two objects apart, then they are not two objects at all: a difference that nothing expressible can mark is no difference.

(4) Hence i = −i. But then 2i = 0, so i = 0, and i² = 0, contradicting i² = −1.

So at least one step must go. Rejecting (3) seems to abandon the idea that mathematical objects are individuated by their structural or definable roles, while keeping it seems to collapse two roots into one. I would like you to say which premise or step you reject, exactly what rejecting it commits me to, and what the best version of the surviving position looks like. In particular, if a structuralist can consistently say there are two roots here, what individuates them on that picture? Beyond the local point, I would also like to know what this case shows about when a choice of label, representative, or generator is well-defined even though the structure is symmetric under the swap.

Please also say which argument or consideration would most change your diagnosis, or where you regard your own reasoning as most likely to fail. Please write in American English.
