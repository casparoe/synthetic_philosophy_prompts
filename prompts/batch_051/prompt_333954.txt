In a debate tournament last month, the resolution was that prospective parents should be required to obtain a license before having children. The proposition's opening speaker built the case almost entirely on an analogy with driver licensing. The premises were that driving can seriously harm others and requires competence, so we license it; parenting can seriously harm others and requires competence; so we should license parenting. The speaker added that the point of the license would not be to impose any particular childrearing style, only to protect each child's chance to reach adulthood with meaningful life options still open, just as traffic rules protect everyone's chance to use the roads.

I found myself accepting the factual premises but suspecting the analogy was carrying more than it could deliver. Please audit the analogy. I would like: (1) an explicit table of correspondences between driving and parenting — the driver, the examiner, the test, the license, traffic rules, revocation, and what the unlicensed driver maps onto; (2) a separation of the disanalogies that matter for the licensing conclusion from those that are merely superficial; (3) an account of what values and background facts the driving analogy quietly imports into the case for parental licensing, including whether the open-future rationale is actually supported by the analogy or bolted on separately; and (4) a verdict on whether the conclusion — that prospective parents should be licensed — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, plus any better analogy you would substitute. Keep the entire response under 700 words.
