I run an upper-level undergraduate seminar on the ethics of belief, with 15 students, each session 90 minutes. Design a runnable classroom activity, with no lecture up front, that makes students feel the force of the position Clifford is defending in this passage from William Kingdon Clifford, "The Ethics of Belief" (1877):

> The question of right or wrong has to do with the origin of his belief, not the matter of it; not what it was, but how he got it; not whether it turned out to be true or false, but whether he had a right to believe on such evidence as was before him.

The activity should get students to render judgments about people forming beliefs under uncertainty before they hear the abstract principle or the name "Clifford." It must include roles or rules, materials, a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the distinction between the origin of a belief and the matter of a belief, and onto the idea that sincere belief is not enough for moral innocence. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be frank about which feature of Clifford's view the activity cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply. The answer should be in American English.
