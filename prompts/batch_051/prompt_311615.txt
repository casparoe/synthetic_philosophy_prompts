I was arguing with a friend who's been on a pragmatist kick, and he kept telling me the Peirce–James dispute about truth only looks intractable because people ask the wrong question. Instead of asking whether truth is whatever inquiry would ultimately converge on in Peirce's sense, or whatever it's useful for us to believe in James's sense, he said think of a true belief as a tool or a banknote. A hammer isn't good because it matches hammerness somewhere out there; you call it good because it keeps driving nails, and you'd stop calling it good the day it just bent them. A twenty-dollar bill isn't valuable because of the paper, it's valuable because people keep honoring it. He used this to argue James basically had it right: truth is just what works, and Peirce's long-run convergence is a red herring since there's no independent thing out there for belief to get right. I kept feeling the analogy was doing more work then he admitted, but I couldn't say exactly where it went wrong.

I want an actual audit of the analogy, not just vibes. Give me the correspondence table, and do both versions separately if they come apart: for the tool, what corresponds to the tool itself, the user, the job it's put to, the material it's used on, and the point where you'd declare it broken; for the banknote, what corresponds to the bill, the paper, the issuer, the people accepting it, and a shop that suddenly refuses it or an inflation crisis. Then lay out the disanalogies that actually matter for the James-vs-Peirce use in particular, especially the ways a tool gets its purpose from a user's prior goal, and a banknote gets its value from convention and mutual acceptance, whereas truth claims are supposed to be answerable to something that isn't just a matter of what we happen to want or accept. I also suspect the two analogies aren't doing the same work (one is about fitting an individual's purpose, the other about social acceptance) and that James slides between them when it suits him. Tell me where the analogy quietly imports James's conclusion into the premises, and whether the Jamesian conclusion survives once those disanalogies are on the table. And if the analogy turns out to be rigged in James's favor, give me the fairest analogy you can for Peirce's side and run it through the same kind of audit.
