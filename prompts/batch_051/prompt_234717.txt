so I've been put on the safety/strategy committee at my company this month and got assigned to write a one-pager on why AI races happen and what might stop them. I've been reading Fearon on why rational states fight wars and trying to map it onto AI races. I wrote down my current positions and I want a consistency check before I circulate them to people who will absolutely tear me appart. Here's the list, roughly in order of confidence:

1. Fearon's rationalist framework is basically right and applies to AI races.
2. The AI race is primarily a commitment problem: even if every major lab agreed nobody should train past some threshold, no one could credibly commit to pause, because each knows a rival might secretly race ahead.
3. The actual public disagreements about AI risk are mostly honest differences in beliefs about timelines and takeoff speed, not strategic misrepresentation. So the information-and-bluffing part of Fearon does less work for AI races than commitment does.
4. Issue indivisibilities don't really apply, because compute, capability, and profit are continuous and the stakes are in principle divisible.
5. But I also want to say the race is winner-take-all: being first to AGI isn't a divisible good. I'd call that a status/positioning thing, not an issue-indivisibility in Fearon's sense.
6. Transparency (evals, compute reporting) probably won't fix the race, because the core problem is committment, not missing information.

Do these hang together? Go through the commitments and tell me where the genuine tensions are, rank them by severity, flag any that only look like tensions, and say what the cheapest single revision would be. Keep it under 500 words, and American English please.
