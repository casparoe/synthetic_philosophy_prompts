Someone in my reading group keeps leaning on the old base/superstructure picture: the economic base — roughly the relations of production — is the foundation, and law, politics, religion and art are a building raised on top of it. He uses it to argue that when you want to explain any of the upstairs stuff you should look first at the relations of production underneath, because a foundation determines what can stand above it; change the base and the superstructure has to shift eventually, like a house resettling. I want to know how far this actually holds. Give me the correspondence table — what in society plays the role of the foundation, the load-bearing members, the soil, the planning permission, the occupants — then the places where it breaks down. Which features of a building does the metaphor quietly import that a society simply lacks? And does the conclusion he wrings out of it (roughly, that the economic base explains the main part of the superstructure) survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the building doing all the argumentative work?
