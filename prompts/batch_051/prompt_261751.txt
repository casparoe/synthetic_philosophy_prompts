For a seminar I am writing up my position on mass media and public opinion, and I want a consistency check before I present. I accept something close to Kierkegaard’s argument in The Present Age that the daily press, by circulating anonymous private opinions to everyone and attaching them to no particular speaker, creates the public as a phantom: a collective nobody whose judgments are everywhere and nowhere, and which tends to dissolve individual decision and responsibility into abstract commentary. But I also hold the following:

1. A strong free press is a structural precondition for democratic accountability.
2. Online anonymity is often justified because it lets vulnerable people speak without reprisal.
3. Public opinion, even when anonymous and diffuse, can correct elite errors and should be treated as epistemically valuable.
4. People remain individually responsible for the views they express; the medium does not dissolve that responsibility.
5. Kierkegaard’s diagnosis of the press applies only to nineteenth-century Copenhagen, not to contemporary pluralistic media.

Which of these commitments are actually in tension with the Kierkegaardian claim, which only seem to be, and where is the tension most severe? Rank the tensions rather than treating them all as equally serious, and say what the least costly revision would be.
