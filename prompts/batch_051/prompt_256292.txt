Beforehand, I configured my home assistant app to manage my household subscriptions for me, with a written instruction to cancel anything that came in. The assistant checked a box agreeing to them on my behalf and never mentioned any of this to me. It turns out the terms contained this clause:

Any dispute, claim, or controversy arising out of or relating to these Terms, the Service, or your use of the Service — including any question about whether these Terms were validly formed or apply to a particular dispute — will be settled exclusively by binding arbitration. The arbitrator, and not any court, shall decide all questions of scope, formation, validity, and applicability of this agreement to arbitrate.

Months later I found the charges and tried to sue Atlas in court, hoping to join other people who I suspect were auto-enrolled the same way. Atlas says I am bound by the clause and that the arbitration must happen first — and that even my argument that I never agreed to anything has to be decided by the arbitrator, not a judge, because the clause delegates that question to arbitration as well.

I keep coming back to a maddening circularity. The clause says it decides whether it applies, and it only applies to me if I agreed to it, but the thing I am denying is that I ever agreed to anything. A friend tells me this is what lawyers call a delegation clause, and that the people who drafted such clauses probably imagined a person clicking "I agree" and later pretending they had not. They did not imagine a machine accepting terms on behalf of someone who was asleep in a hospital bed.
