I've got a standing rule: whenever I learn that some category is socially constructed, I stop treating it as real and file it under 'made up.' Its served me well with race — once I stopped thinking of racial categories as biological facts and started treating them as social labels, I finally felt like I was thinking clearly about them. I do a milder version of the same thing with gender, and I've seen people run the same move on IQ, mental illness, and even adolescence. But now a conservation biologist tells me species boundaries are partly a matter of convention (lumping and splitting populations depends on history, funding, and institutional inertia), and that 'biodiversity' is itself a socially constructed concept shaped by donor priorities. My rule would force me to file species and biodiversity under 'made up' too, and that feels wrong. Yet I can't say why, because money is socially constructed and I treat money as perfectly real — invented, but real and causally powerful. So: is my rule defensible? Is there a repair along the lines of 'socially constructed means not natural, not non-existent'? Or is the rule just a debunking reflex I aim at categories I already wanted to undermine?
