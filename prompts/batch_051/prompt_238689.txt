So I've been thinking about the argument people make about climate change and eating meat where they say one person buying a burger doesn't actually change anything, the harm would happen anyway. I wanted a real answer to that, so I came up with something I call the Watershed Lottery Principle. The idea is: for any big collective harm there is some number of contributions that tips it over — like maybe the millionth chicken purchase is what makes a grocery chain order one extra crate from a supplier. That tipping number is the watershed. You basically never know which contribution is the watershed one, so when you act you should treat your contribution as a lottery ticket. If the watershed is the W-th contribution out of N total contributions, then your one contribution has a W/N chance of being the exact one that pushes the total over the line. So the expected harm of your burger is (W/N) times the full harm of the extra crate, which works out to your average share of the total harm. That seems like it just straight-up refutes the inefficacy objection: your individual act isn't harmless, it's a lottery ticket with a real expected cost, even though it will almost never actually make a difference in hindsight.

I have two examples I like. One is voting: my vote almost never flips an election, but if I treat myself as having a 1/N chance of being the decisive vote, then voting has expected value equal to the whole election outcome divided by the number of voters, which isn't nothing. Two is flying: my flight has some tiny chance of being the one that pushes an airline over some emissions threshold, so its expected harm is real.

The part I cant figure out is this: if the watershed point has already been passed before I act — the grocery chain already ordered the crate — then my ticket seems worthless, and the principle starts depending on whether I act early or late. That feels super weird ethically, like my moral status depends on timing I can't control. Also if the harm is continuous instead of stepwise, my whole watershed picture breaks. So I want an honest assessment: is this principle coherent, does it already exist under a different name, what does it get right, and where does it actually fall apart? Give me the strongest objections and tell me if any of them kill it or if theyre just uncomfortable.
