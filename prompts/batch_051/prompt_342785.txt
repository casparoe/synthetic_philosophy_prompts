Been roped into writing a short history of our parish primary school for its centenary, and I've just read Butterfield on the Whig interpretation of history — you know, treating the past as a slow crawl toward us and judging earlier people by whether they were 'ahead of their time'. Now I keep catching myself doing exactly that: calling the Victorian reformers ahead of their time, treating the 1944 Education Act as the moment it all started going right, and writing the whole thing as if the point of a nineteenth-century classroom was to eventually turn into our Ofsted-rated one. I do want to avoid Whig history. But here's what I can't get straight: the school did improve. Compulsory attendance, smaller classes, the leaving age going up — those are just facts, arent they? So how do you write the history of something that genuinely got better without it being Whiggish? Is the only honest alternative to pretend nothing ever improves, or to refuse to say the past was worse? Because that feels like it's own kind of distortion.
