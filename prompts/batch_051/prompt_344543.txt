I need a working definition of a protected characteristic: a trait such that treating people differently on the basis of it should be presumptively impermissible and should require a substantially stronger justification than ordinary policy considerations. I am drafting the equal-treatment rules for a new institution's automated hiring and allocation systems, and I want a principled criterion rather than a copy of any jurisdiction's statutory list.

Here are the cases I want the definition to classify. Some I regard as clear; most I am unsure about.

Clear protected cases:
1. Race or ethnic origin.
2. Sex.
3. Religion, including membership in a stigmatized minority religion.
4. Physical disability.

Unclear cases, where I need the definition to do real work:
5. Age, especially a minimum-age eligibility rule for a role with a genuine physical component.
6. Body weight, in an employment context where the employer bears health insurance costs.
7. A genetic predisposition to a late-onset condition that is actuarially relevant to those same costs.
8. A completed criminal sentence, where the job does not involve working with vulnerable people.
9. Citizenship, as distinct from national origin, where the line is drawn between citizens and long-term noncitizen residents.
10. Current membership in a political party, in a role with no political function.
11. A regional accent or first language that an employer treats as evidence of poor communication skills.
12. Marital or parental status, in decisions about promotion to a role requiring frequent travel.

Clear non-protected cases:
13. Natural eye color.
14. Month of birth.

I want more than a sorting rule. The definition should say what unifies the protected cases and excludes the unprotected ones, and it should handle the possibility that a trait is protected only conditionally — for example, when it functions as a proxy for some other protected trait. Then run your definition through all fourteen cases, say where it yields a verdict you did not expect before you started, and identify the single verdict you are least confident in and what further fact or argument would most plausibly change it.
