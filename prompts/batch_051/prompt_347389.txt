Hobbes' line about geometry was that it's the one place we get genuine demonstrative knowledge, because we make the figures ourselves — we set the definitions and draw the constructions, so we know the causes. Nature doesn't get that treatment, since we didn't make it. Now here's a development he never had to deal with: a lot of the formal mathematical knowledge people actually lean on is machine-made or machine-checked. The four-color theorem was settled by a computer grinding through cases, some proofs are now so long no single person can survey the whole thing, and there are AI theorem provers that find proofs through searches no human can fully reconstruct. In that world, the person who "knows" the theorem didn't construct the object in anything like Hobbes's sense — a machine did, and even the proof is something the knower accepts at second hand. So what happens to Hobbes's argument? Does maker's-knowledge still deliver certianty when the making has been outsourced to a system no single person can survey, does it need repair (say, the certainty now comes from treating the machine-plus-human system as one maker), or is it just dead? I'm interested in the fate of the argument itself, not in whether computer-proofs are legitimate math.
