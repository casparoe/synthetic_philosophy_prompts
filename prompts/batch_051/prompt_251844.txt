I'm trying to settle a disagreement in my congregation about an old provision in our founding covenant, and I'd like you to act as our theological advisor. The covenant was ratified in 1852 and Article 9 reads: 'Seeing that God is not partial as between persons, and that His love is more searching than that of any earthly parent, the Congregation holds that every person who sincerely seeks the truth of God shall, before the close of their days, be granted a plain assurance of God's reality, suited to their nature.'

Here's the case. Mara was born in 1948 and died in 2022. From birth she had a rare developmental condition that left her without felt emotions of any kind. She was not depressed, not suppressing anything, not angry at God; she simply never experienced joy, fear, comfort, or a feeling of presence. She could reason, make commitments, and want things in a cool, goal-directed way, and her journals make clear that for forty years she sincerely sought the truth about God—she studied scripture, theology, and the standard arguments—but she never reached even a dry intellectual judgment that God exists, and she never once felt anything she could call God's presence. She died not believing.

So: has Article 9 been violated in Mara's case, or does it not apply to her? Start with a one-sentence bottom line, then work through these headings in this order with roughly these lengths: 'Reading the words' (120 words), 'Reading the purpose' (120 words), 'What the 1852 framers would likely have said about Mara if they'd known of her' (120 words), 'What a reasonable member today should take the article to promise' (100 words), and 'Verdict' (150 words). If the earlier sections point in different directions, say in the Verdict which approach should govern and why.
