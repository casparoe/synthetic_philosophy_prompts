I spent my career as a geologist and only came to philosophy of mind a few years ago, so forgive me if this is old ground. There's a clash I cant get past. On one side: the brain is a finite physical system, so at any fixed grain it has only finitely many distinguishable internal states. If experience is determined by those states, then there are only finitely many possible experiences I can have, and a life is just a walk through a very large but already fixed catalogue. On the other side: experience feels bottomless from the inside. I don't mean I have endless time or energy; I mean the next conversation, the next hillside, the next unheard piece of music always seems able to deliver something genuinely new, not just another instance of a kind I've already met. I'm not asking you to settle the quarrel. I want to know what I'd be committing to if I wrote the intuition off as a horizon illusion produced by my inability to survey a huge finite space, and what I'd be committing to if I amended the theory instead — say, by holding that experiences are individuated by what they mean or by their relations to other things, not by internal states, so a finite machine could still live an inexhaustible life. Is there a reading where both survive, or does one have to go? Could the answer be in British English, please.
