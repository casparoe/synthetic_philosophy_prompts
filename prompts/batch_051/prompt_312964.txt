I've been working with a rough set of criteria for what makes something a proper name: (1) it refers to a particular without picking it out via a description; (2) it gets assigned by a naming act rather than applying in virtue of shared features; (3) its reference is stable across counterfactual situations in a way descriptions of the same individual aren't; and (4) it doesn't take plurals, articles, or quantifiers the way common nouns do. Now I'm stuck on family names. Imagine a society where nobody has inherited surnames. Instead, each person's surname is generated from their mother's given name plus a sex-marked suffix. So if Ana has a daughter and a son, their surnames are Anadottir and Anasson. These names clearly carry descriptive content about whose child you are, they change every generation, and they're shared only within sibling groups. Are they proper names by my criteria, or something more like relational titles? Does the fact that ordinary family names scare-quote-ably do weird things with plurals ("the Smiths") mean criterion (4) was already dubious? I'm trying to decide whether the criteria need fixing or whether family names are just their own kind of name.
