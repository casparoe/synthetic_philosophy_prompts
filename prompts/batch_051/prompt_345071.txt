I need three thought experiments built to order, about what emotions are. Here are the three theories I want to pit against each other. (1) Feeling theory: being afraid just is the felt bodily state; the racing heart and the tight chest are the fear, not side effects of it. (2) Judgment theory: an emotion is an evaluative belief or judgment—being angry is judging that you've been wronged. (3) Perceptual theory: an emotion is a perception of a value, roughly the way seeing red is a perception of color; fear is perceiving danger, even if you dont believe the danger is real.

I want three ordinary-life scenarios, no philosophy-department armchairs and no weird brain injuries. In Scenario A, the feeling theory and the perceptual theory should say the person is in an emotion, but the judgment theory should say they aren't. In Scenario B, the feeling theory and the judgment theory should say yes, but the perceptual theory should say no. In Scenario C, the judgment theory and the perceptual theory should say yes, but the feeling theory should say no. Each scenario should be one short paragraph, and the verdicts have to fall out of details you've stipulated about what the person feels, what they judge, and what they perceive—not from a character announcing the answer.

After each scenario, do the check: state the relevant felt state if any, the evaluative judgment if any, and the perceptual presentation if any; then say what each theory predicts and why. Finish with a paragraph on which feature of each scenario is actually doing the work, and how the odd-theory-out would attack the case—for instance, what would a judgment theorist say to stop Scenario A from counting as an emotion, or what would a feeling theorist say to dismiss Scenario C—and what one small tweak would pull them back. The point of the set is to see wether these three theories genuinely make different predictions or just re-describe the same verdicts in different vocabularies, so keep the cases clean and dont load them with extra quirks.
