ok so in philosophy club we've been reading Dennett on qualia, specifically his two coffee tasters Chase and Sanborn. Chase says the coffee tastes different to him than it used to (his taste qualia changed), Sanborn says it tastes exactly the same but he just stopped liking it (same qualia, different attitude). Dennett says even the tasters themselves can't really tell which thing happened, so positing qualia as private objects is unfalsifiable and does no work.

anyway heres the variation i cant stop thinking about. suppose there's a third taster, Troy. Troy reports that both changes happened at the exact same time: the coffee now tastes different AND she likes it less, and she insists it wasn't that the taste-change caused the liking-change, it's just that both flipped together overnight. Then, a month later, both flip back, and she's certain they returned to the original state. But here's the thing: when she says "it's back to normal," she's comparing to a past she can only describe in words ("it tasted sharper and I disliked that taste") not to an actual stored sample of the old taste.

question: does this variation break Dennett's argument, or just make it stronger? like, does the fact that Troy can separate 'taste' and 'liking' and report them changing independently give the qualia realist something real, or would Dennett just say the whole story is made of current judgments plus dispositions and memory reports, and the 'two things flipping together' is another unfalsifiable posit? and in the reversal, if Troy cant retrieve the old qualia itself, only a label, what could possibly make it true that the new state is the same as the original rather than a third state she's decided to call the same? idk which side this cuts.
