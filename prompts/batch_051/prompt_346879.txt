Your task is to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers who have long argued past one another about the demandingness objection to consequentialism. One party is a committed act-consequentialist who holds that an act is right just in case it produces the best consequences from an impartial point of view. The other is a defender of commonsense morality who holds that any acceptable moral theory must include a substantial agent-centred prerogative, meaning a standing permission to give one's own interests and projects greater weight than impartial maximisation allows. The statement is the document these two parties produce after a good-faith process in which each has had to state the other's view in terms the other accepts.

The statement must contain the following.

1. A numbered agreed-claims section covering: what both parties accept about what act-consequentialism requires in a world of large, cheaply preventable suffering; the precise structure of the demandingness objection and the conditions under which it succeeds; which responses each side regards as question-begging, ad hoc, or otherwise off the table; and any part of the familiar dispute the parties now judge to be merely verbal.

2. A disagreements table. Each row should give: the question stated neutrally; the consequentialist's answer, worded so the consequentialist accepts it; the prerogative theorist's answer, worded so the prerogative theorist accepts it; and the crux, that is, the sub-question the answer to which would lead at least one side to revise its answer to that row's main question.

3. A tests-and-predictions section. For every crux, give the case, argument, or empirical consideration that both parties agree bears on it, and state in advance what each side predicts that consideration, if properly developed, would show. If a side can specify no consideration that would move it, the statement must say so.

Throughout, the parties should keep separate three claims that are often run together: that consequentialism requires extreme sacrifice in the actual world; that an agent who falls short of those requirements is blameworthy; and that a theory too demanding to be internalised without alienation cannot be the correct account of what we ought to do. The parties may sign different combinations of these, and the statement should record which.

Format the statement as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by the disagreements table, then the tests-and-predictions section. Use British English. Do not cite texts or include references; the statement must stand on its own. The entire statement must not exceed 850 words.
