A few months ago I moved from a small city where people wait for crossing lights, queue without being told, and report neighbours' violations to the housing office, to a much larger city where the same norms are treated as invitations to negotiate. A colleague in sociology described the difference as one of cultural tightness and looseness: tight cultures, she said, have strong norms and low tolerance for deviance, while loose cultures have weak norms and high tolerance. That sounded like a claim about a real property of a society rather than just a label for my own comparisons, but I do not know how I would establish it. Could you lay out how one would test the claim that my former city is tighter, in this sense, than my current one? I would like concrete measures or observations, a statement of what result would favour each reading, and an honest account of what the tests would and would not settle—including whether any descriptive finding could underwrite a view about how much rule-following a society should demand. In your answer, please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who study this topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
