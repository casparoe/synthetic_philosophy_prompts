I'm in a moral psychology course and we've been going over Aristotle's distinction between the enkratic (continent) person and the temperate one: the enkratic person has the bad appetite and overrides it, while the temperate person's appetites are already aligned with their better judgment, so there isn't much to override. I keep finding myself pulled toward a view that basically collapses that distinction. On this view temperance is just successful diachronic prudence: the temperate person knows they'll cave at 11pm, so they don't buy the ice cream at 4pm, they set up commitment devices, treat their future self as a predictably irrational agent who needs managing, that sort of thing. Its appealing because it's parsimonious, doesn't invoke any mysterious transformation of desire, makes temperance continuous with ordinary willpower, and it fits the empirical stuff on hyperbolic discounting and precommitment. It also feels anti-moralistic — it doesn't rank wanting-the-cake-and-resisting as worse than not-wanting-the-cake, only the long-run behavior matters.

But then I picture my housemate, who has blocked the delivery apps, padlocked the liquor cabinet, and still thinks about it all day and resents the padlock. He never actually caves. Calling him temperate feels wrong — he looks like someone managing an addiction with scaffolding, maybe even repressing something, rather than someone with the virtue. Meanwhile my gran, who would genuinely rather go for a walk than eat half a cake and is never white-knuckling anything, seems obviously temperate even though she never exercises self-control because she never has to. So my intuition is that temperance is a condition of the desires themselves, not a technique for managing them, and the self-control view confuses a cure with a good leash.

What I want is a negotiated verdict, not a side. Should I drop the intuition, amend the theory, or is there a reading where they're compatible? What would justify each move? If I debunk the intuition, am I committed to saying the white-knuckle housemate is exactly as temperate as my gran, and that the felt ease does no normative work? If I amend the theory — say, temperance is prudent self-management plus identifying with one's abstinent desires, or plus some requirement about how the aligned desires were acquired — does that just smuggle the original distinction back in? And is there a plausible story on which precommitment and desire-management are how you become temperate, while the virtue itself is the end state where the cravings have actually changed? I want to see what each option commits me to, not just be told which one is right.
