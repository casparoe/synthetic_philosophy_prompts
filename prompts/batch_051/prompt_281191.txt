I have recently taken over the grading for an undergraduate course on genocide and historical explanation, and I am preparing a rubric for the question on the Browning–Goldhagen debate. Here is the exam question:

Compare the explanations that Christopher Browning and Daniel Jonah Goldhagen give for why ordinary German men participated in the mass shooting of Jews. State each author's central claim, and then identify the specific issue on which their explanations most fundamentally diverge.

Here is the student's answer:

Browning, in Ordinary Men, studies Reserve Police Battalion 101 and argues that these men were ordinary, middle-aged men from Hamburg, neither specially selected for ideological commitment nor pathologically cruel. At their first major action, at Józefów, their commander, Major Trapp, told them that anyone who did not feel up to the killing could step out; most still participated. Browning explains this through situational mechanisms: deference to authority, fear of letting their comrades down, career concerns, and the numbing routine of repeated killing, and he therefore denies that antisemitism played any significant role in the killings. Goldhagen, in Hitler's Willing Executioners, replies that this misses the genuine cause. On his account, the men were not ordinary at all but bearers of a specifically German and uniquely eliminationist antisemitism, a culturally shared image of the Jews as a demonized, lethal enemy whose annihilation was necessary and just. The killers were thus willing executioners, not browbeaten conscripts. The deepest disagreement, then, is whether the men were ordinary people transformed by the situation, as Browning claims, or ideologically exceptional executioners, as Goldhagen claims.

Grade this answer out of 10, and provide the point-by-point comments that justify the grade. Identify what the student gets right, flag each error or imprecision, and explain how the answer should be corrected. The comments should be specific enough that the student could use them to revise, not a general impression.
