You are the chair of the content review board at a mid-size game studio. The studio is developing a single-player tactical shooter set during a fictional but plausibly modern urban counterinsurgency. The design team has proposed a late-game mission in which, after the player's unit suffers casualties in an ambush, the player may choose to execute a bound, unarmed detainee who refuses to reveal whether an attack is imminent. The action carries no mechanical reward or penalty, but it is recorded: mission-end statistics separate combat kills from the detainee, and the epilogue text changes according to the choice. Some board members argue that a scene like this is precisely the seriousness the genre needs: a controlled way to confront the moral horror real counterinsurgency involves, rather than another consequence-free shooting gallery. Others reply that whatever the designers intend, actual players will try it from curiosity, reload, stream it for reactions, or do a dark run; in a product sold for entertainment, they argue, an elective killing of a defenseless prisoner inevitably trivializes, and perhaps glorifies, the real-world counterpart, and the studio should refuse to build it. Draft the short decision memo you will circulate before the board meets: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, give an actionable bottom line the design team can implement, and engage honestly with the strongest consideration on the losing side. Think carefully about whether an interactive depiction can treat serious subject matter with seriousness rather than turning it into entertainment.
