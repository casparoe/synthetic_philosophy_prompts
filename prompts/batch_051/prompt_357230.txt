ive been reading about proposals to use AI debate as a safety tool, where two models argue and a judge decides, and the hope is that truth wins out even if nobody is fully honest. the worry that stuck with me is that a smart but wrong model could win not by having the better argument but by putting out a pile of plausible arguments that each have a subtle flaw, so the judge just gets snowed under. i realized i have a personal version of this rule: when someones argument has a lot of moving parts and i cant check every step myself, i dont update at all unless they can state the core reason in one plain sentence i can actually evaluate. if they say the full argument only works if i read the whole thing carefully, i treat that as a point against the view, not as a reason to go read it. i know this might just be me protecting my current beliefs or favoring simple arguments over complex ones. is this policy actually defensible? what would a better version of it look like, and under what conditions is it a real defense against obfuscation rather than a bias against complexity? give me a committed answer, not a menu of options.
