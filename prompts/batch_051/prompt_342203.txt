Every couple of months someone in a group chat I'm in resurrects the dust-specks-versus-torture thought experiment, and it always unfolds the same way, which is starting to feel like information about the question rather than about the people arguing. The setup: press one button and each of, say, 10^25 people gets a single barely noticeable speck of dust in the eye; press the other and one person gets fifty years of severe pain. Nobody disputes the setup, nobody thinks a dust speck is free or that torture is fine, and the arithmetic isn't the issue. The first faction still says you obviously press the speck button, because a small harm multiplied by a sufficiently large population has to outweigh a finite horror. The second faction still says you obviously press the torture button, because no pile of negligible discomforts can license doing that to a person. And a third, smaller faction says the case is rigged because it quietly assumes a cardinal, interpersonally comparable scale of pain and then just adds it up across people.

A few of us once tried to break the stalemate by building a ladder of intermediate cases—one speck versus a pinprick, a pinprick versus a headache, a headache versus a broken arm—to find where the switch happens. Instead of converging, each side redrew the border wherever they already wanted it and then accused the other side of having no principled border at all. So I've stopped asking who's right. I'm now less interested in who's right then in what kind of disagreement this is, structurally. Is there a real question at the bottom of it, about the correct shape of aggregation or about when harms become morally negligible? Or are the two big factions just using one word, 'worse,' for two different rankings—say, which outcome is impersonally better versus which choice you could defend to the person you'd be torturing? My hunch is the latter, but I can't make it precise, and it doesn't explain the third faction's refusal to answer at all. Given whatever diagnosis you land on, what would count as progress: a distinction, a formal result, a different series of cases, or just accepting that the argument is a stable feature of our moral concepts?
