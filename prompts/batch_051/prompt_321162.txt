I need a working definition of the term epistemic backstop in the deepfake era. The term gets used loosely for everything from courtroom records to viral clips, and I want to pin down what actually earns the label. Give me a definition that classifies the following ten items correctly, where each item is offered as the thing that should settle a dispute about whether some event happened or what was said.

1. An anonymous cellphone video of a police use-of-force incident, uploaded with no metadata and no chain of custody.
2. A body-camera recording of the same incident, held in the department's own evidence-management system with an access log, released only under court order.
3. A recording made by a camera that cryptographically signs its files at the moment of capture, so anyone can later verify which device produced the file and when, but the camera belongs to one of the parties to the dispute.
4. A witness testifying live under oath, subject to cross-examination.
5. A seismograph record written automatically by an unattended instrument in a sealed, offline monitoring station, with the time stamped by the instrument itself.
6. A screenshot of a tweet.
7. A surreptitious audio recording of a private meeting, made by one participant without the others' knowledge or consent.
8. A paper ballot sealed in a box and moved under a chain-of-custody log signed by observers from the competing parties.
9. A blockchain timestamp showing only that a particular file hash existed at a particular time.
10. Three hundred photographs of the same public rally, uploaded independently by people at different vantage points, with no evident coordination among them.

My starting guesses: 6 is clearly not a backstop, 5 and 8 clearly are, and I have no settled view about 2, 4, 7, or 9. I also suspect 3 is a backstop for the claim that the file came from that camera at that time, but not for the claim that the recording depicts what actually happened. Propose the definition, run it through all ten cases, say where it surprises you, and identify which of your verdicts is least secure. If the term does two jobs — one describing what in practice settles disputes and one prescribing what we ought to treat as authoritative going forward — give both definitions and say which cases move between them.
