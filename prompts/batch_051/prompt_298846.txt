In the philosophy of language course I'm taking this term, we have been working through a Brandom-style inferentialism: the view that the meaning of an expression is its inferential role, understood as the place it occupies in a social practice of giving and asking for reasons. To make the view precise, I want to adopt four criteria for an expression's being meaningful.

First, conditions of appropriate use: there are settled circumstances under which a speaker is entitled to infer a sentence containing the expression. Second, consequences of use: there are settled commitments one acquires by asserting such a sentence. Third, difference-making: inserting the expression into a sentence changes the inferential commitments and entitlements attaching to that sentence, so it interacts with the roles of other expressions. Fourth, public mastery: competent speakers can practically track the commitments and entitlements involving the expression and can hold one another responsible for doing so.

Now consider a borderline case. Suppose a community introduces a new binary sentence-forming connective, written '⊗' and called 'blip', and fixes its whole inferential role with exactly two rules. Blip-in: from any sentence P, one may infer P ⊗ Q, for any Q. Blip-out: from P ⊗ Q, one may infer Q. These two rules are all there is to know about the connective, and the community treats them as authoritative.

Notice what follows. By applying Blip-in and then Blip-out, one can derive any sentence from any other. Every claim entails every other claim, and the language loses its ability to mark any inference as a bad one.

My question is: by the four criteria above, is 'blip' meaningful? Please work through the criteria one at a time and give me a committed verdict rather than surveying the options. Where a criterion does not settle the matter, say whether that is a gap in the criteria or a defect in the example. Finally, address the methodological point: does this case show that the inferentialist criteria need an added constraint — something like harmony between introduction and elimination rules, conservativeness, or the preservation of a nontrivial space of material incompatibilities — and would such a constraint be internal to the inferentialist picture or an import from outside it?
