I work on the procedural audio team at a game studio. We generate adaptive music for gameplay, and I’ve been trying to wrap my head around the part of Schopenhauer’s The World as Will and Representation, volume 1, where he sets music apart from the other arts and says it does not copy the world as representation, or the ideas in it, but is somehow a direct copy or objectification of the will itself. I have no formal philosophy background, so I can’t confidently turn that into something my team can use.

I want a reusable procedure we can run on any AI-generated or procedural track, or any candidate generator, to decide whether it does the thing Schopenhauer thinks music does, not just whether it sounds musical. It has to be runnable in a one-hour review by two engineers and a producer who have never read philosophy. The procedure should have the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to keep going, where it stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a human because the question is aesthetic or metaphysical rather than technical. Be explicit about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as checks.

Then run the procedure on two candidates: a recursive neural network trained to continue a corpus of classical piano pieces, and a rule-based generator that picks chord progressions from a fixed Markov table with no learned model. Also say where the whole translation breaks down, for example whether Schopenhauer would even accept that you can detect will-objectification from features of the sound rather than from what it is doing in the listener.
