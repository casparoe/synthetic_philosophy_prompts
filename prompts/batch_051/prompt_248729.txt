Ok here's a setup I want you to write up as a document. Two camps in the sweatshop debate, who've mostly been talking past each other, decide to do an adversarial collaboration. Camp A, the mutual-benefit camp, says a sweatshop job is a Pareto improvement over the worker's actual alternatives, the worker chooses it, and outside interference—boycotts, bans, minimum wages that bite—usually hurts the people it's meant to help. Camp B, the exploitation camp, says a transaction can be wrongfully exploitative even when both parties gain: the background is unjust, bargaining power is wildly asymmetric, and the firm is taking an unfair share of the value the worker creates.

To make it concrete, anchor the statement on this case: a factory offers $2.50 a day for a ten-hour shift; the worker's next-best option is $1.80 a day as a day laborer; the firm could pay $3.10 and still make a healthy profit. The worker takes the job.

Draft the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want the document itself, not a dialogue and not a verdict on who's right. Structure it as: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims, tight enough that neither side would call them weaselly; (2) a disagreements table, where each row gives the exact point of disagreement, the mutual-benefit camp's own wording of its view, and the exploitation camp's own wording of its view; (3) a list of cruxes—sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a concrete test, argument, or case both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Pay special attention to seperating verbal disagreements from substantive ones. For example, both camps might sign that the worker's consent is less than fully voluntary because her alternatives are bad, while still disagreeing about whether that fact, by itself, makes the transaction impermissible. If part of their dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part and why. Candidate cruxes you could use, sharpen, or replace: whether the wrongness of exploitation is exhausted by harm or welfare effects; whether background injustice makes an otherwise-permissible exchange impermissible, or just gives the state reasons to fix the background; whether the firm has a duty to pay a fair wage above the market-clearing wage, and if so what determines fairness; whether constrained consent waives the worker's complaint. For the tests, think about cases like withdrawing the offer (would the worker protest?), a clean-background version where the worker has real alternatives, or a monopsony version where the firm is the only employer. Each camp has to commit in advance to what it predicts about each case.

Don't cite anybody and don't give a bibliography. Just write the joint statement.
