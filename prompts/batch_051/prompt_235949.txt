I'm putting together a course module on modal epistemology, and I've noticed that every theory of how conceivability bears on possibility seems to be motivated by one telling case: one person starts from the conceivability of philosophical zombies, another from the fact that it is conceivable that water is not H2O, a third from the fact that an ordinary reasoner can conceive both Goldbach's conjecture and its negation. Before I present any positive view to my students, I want the requirements on the table: what would an adequate account of the conceivability–possibility link, good enough for assessing conceivability arguments in metaphysics, have to get right?

Please write the desiderata. For each, give the case or consideration that forces it, the distinction it must not collapse (for example, the distinction between working through a scenario in detail and merely failing to notice a contradiction), and a mark of whether it is non-negotiable or a commitment only of a particular camp such as rationalist, modal-skeptical, or two-dimensionalist. Also tell me whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, and which single requirement does the most to rule out the familiar simple positions. Skip the scorecard of existing theories; I'll do that myself.

Format: give me exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Items 1–4 are the desiderata. Item 5 states which of them most excludes popular theories and whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable; it then says how confident you are in the list as a whole and which desideratum you are least sure of.
