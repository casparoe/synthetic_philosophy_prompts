I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I have been following a methodological quarrel between two research camps that study the same phenomenon. One camp charges that the other is doing ad hoc science: each time a predicted effect fails to appear, the other camp adds a new boundary condition, an auxiliary mechanism, or a free parameter that makes the original account compatible with the failed result. The second camp replies that this is simply how productive fields adjust, and that the real question is whether the revised account generates new, risky predictions. I know there is a tradition in the philosophy of science, associated with Imre Lakatos, that frames this as the difference between progressive and degenerating research programmes, and I would like to turn that distinction into something a working scientist could actually apply.

I need a working definition of "ad hoc hypothesis" — or, if that is cleaner, "ad hoc modification of a theory" — that correctly classifies the following cases. (a) After an experimental result comes in, a researcher adjusts a free parameter so that the model fits that very result, and the revised model predicts no new observations. (b) Before any search is run, a theorist adds an unobserved entity because the mathematics requires it, and that entity is later detected. (c) A theory is modified to accommodate an anomaly that was already known, but the modification then generates a surprising new prediction that is later confirmed. (d) A psychologist explains away a failed replication by adding a post hoc assumption about the participants, and that assumption happens to account for an unrelated finding as well. (e) A group pre-registers a specific functional form and reports the outcome exactly as planned, whether or not it is confirmed. (f) A framework is so broadly or flexibly stated that it could be reconciled with almost any set of observed results. I regard (a) as clearly ad hoc and (e) as clearly not; I suspect (f) is objectionably hollow without being ad hoc in the narrow sense; and I honestly cannot classify (b), (c), and (d).

Propose a definition that draws the line where you think it should be drawn, run it through all six cases, say where it yields a surprising or uncomfortable verdict, and then either revise it or defend those verdicts. The definition should be precise enough that a reader could see whether the two camps in my story are genuinely disagreeing or simply using the word ad hoc to mean different things.
