The sphex wasp, as it appears in the usual philosophical example, exhibits a stark form of mechanical rigidity. Having stung and paralyzed a cricket, it drags the cricket to the mouth of its burrow, leaves it there, goes inside to inspect the burrow, comes back out, and then pulls the cricket in. If an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches away while the wasp is inside, the wasp drags the cricket back to the mouth, leaves it, and inspects the burrow again — and it will repeat this cycle apparently indefinitely rather than simply carrying the cricket in. The wasp seems to be running a fixed check-then-deliver routine, unable to use the fact that it has just inspected the burrow or that the cricket's displacement was caused by an outside interference.

Writers on free will use this example to mark a contrast between mechanical rigidity and flexible agency: the wasp, they say, is moved by a trigger rather than a reason, whereas a flexible agent can notice that a routine has been disrupted and revise its plan accordingly. That contrast hides a partly empirical claim: that there is a difference in kind between behavior generated by a repertoire of fixed, environment-triggered routines and behavior guided by representational uptake of the situation, and that this difference is what matters for whether behavior counts as free rather than merely complicated.

How would one test that claim? Design a concrete experimental paradigm — with nonhuman animals, human participants, or artificial agents, whichever you judge best — that could separate an agent whose flexibility comes from many richly interlocking but essentially sphex-like routines from an agent that represents an interruption as a reason to change what it does next. Be precise about the manipulation, the behavioral signature, and what result would count as evidence for each kind of architecture. Then say what the test would not settle: which parts of the distinction between blind routine and acting for a reason would remain standing under each possible result, and whether any purely behavioral experiment could show that the flexibility at issue is incompatible with a complete mechanistic account of the agent.
