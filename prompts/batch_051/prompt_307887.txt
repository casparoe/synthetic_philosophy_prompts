I referee for a mathematics journal where I repeatedly face papers that offer a proof of a conjecture and then respond to apparent counterexamples by revising definitions or auxiliary lemmas. I want a reusable decision procedure, grounded in Lakatos's Proofs and Refutations and its treatment of mathematics as quasi-empirical, for deciding when such revisions are legitimate proof-generated concept formation and when they are ad hoc monster-barring or exception-barring that merely protects a false conjecture. The procedure must be usable by a referee without philosophical training in under an hour, and it should distinguish at least these moves: incorporating a counterexample by extending the concept, ruling the counterexample out as not a genuine instance of the concept, and restricting the theorem's domain. Apply the procedure to two worked cases: one where successive lemma-incorporations yield a deeper theorem, and one where repeated exception-barring only shields the original claim. Flag the steps where judgment cannot be eliminated and state explicitly what the procedure cannot settle. Keep the response under 600 words.
