I've been reading about modal versions of the ontological argument — arguments that try to reach God's existence from the claim that God's existence is at least possible — and I tried to write out the simplest version I could make rigorous. The conclusion follows so easily that I keep thinking a trick must be hidden somewhere, but I can't locate it with confidence. Here is my reconstruction:

(1) Stipulate that "God" names a being that, if it exists at all, exists necessarily — that is, it exists in every possible world, and there is no possible world in which it fails to exist. This is only a definition of the concept, not yet a claim that anything satisfies it.

(2) It is at least possible that God, so defined, exists. I can form the concept without finding any contradiction, and what is conceivable without contradiction is possible.

(3) Because God's existence is possible, there is at least one possible world in which God exists.

(4) By (1), if God exists in any possible world, God exists in every possible world.

(5) The actual world is a possible world. Therefore God exists in the actual world.

I half-suspect the word "possible" changes meaning somewhere between steps (2) and (3), but I can't make that worry precise, and I'm not sure whether the real problem is a formal fallacy or just an unsupported premise.
