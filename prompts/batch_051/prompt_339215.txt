I've been thinking about how I use the word "intentionally" when people bring about side effects, and my judgments feel weirdly inconsistent when I line them up. Take an executive who approves a cost-cutting plan that will dump waste into a river. She knows this and says she doesn't care about the river, she just wants to save money. I'm strongly inclined to say she intentionally dumped waste into the river. Now take the mirror case: the plan will clean a stretch of river as a side effect, and she knows this and doesn't care, she just wants to save money. I'm much less inclined to say she intentionally cleaned the river. And here's the one that bothers me most: a single plan that will pollute one river and clean another, both side effects equally foreseen, and she's indifferent to both. I still want to say she intentionally polluted the one and that she did not intentionally clean the other, even though nothing about her knowledge, control, or indifference differs between the two.

A few more data points. A surgeon amputates a patient's leg to save her life, knowing the patient will be left with chronic phantom pain. I would not say the surgeon intentionally caused the chronic pain. A saboteur deletes a company's database to ruin the company, knowing this will also expose a fraud that helps the investors, and I don't think she intentionally helped the investors. And for a side effect with no moral charge at all — an office manager's new ventilation setting makes the room smell faintly of cedar, which nobody cares about — I'm genuinely unsure whether I'd call the smell intentional.

Is there a single principle that captures all of these verdicts? I don't know if it's a matter of how I use the word, or a moral lens distorting my read of the facts. I'd especially like to know which of my verdicts it forces me to give up, and what it predicts about variants I haven't settled: an agent who regrets a bad side effect and tries to minimize it, an agent who secretly hopes for the good side effect, and a side effect the agent never consciously thought about but should have been obvious. And could you write the answer as continuous prose, no headings, bullets, bold, or tables.
