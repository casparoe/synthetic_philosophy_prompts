I have no training in economics, but I've been reading around the socialist calculation debate on my own, and a particular distortion keeps bothering me. Popular discussion tends to reduce the anti-planning side to the claim that a central planner simply cannot process enough data. As I understand it, the stronger point was never computational. Mises argued that without real market prices for capital goods there is no common unit in which to compare alternative uses of a machine, a tonne of steel, or an hour of labour, so "rational" allocation becomes a metaphor rather than a calculation. Hayek added that the most important economic knowledge is dispersed, local, tacit and often unarticulated — why this farmer, in this wet week, on this field, would not plant now — and so cannot be collected and handed to a bureau, however clever the staff. The Lange-style reply was that a Central Planning Board could approximate a market by announcing provisional prices and adjusting them by trial and error whenever shortages or surpluses appeared.

A friend who works in tech recently told me, in complete earnestness, that a large language model trained on enough invoices, sensors and customer chatter would soon "solve central planning." That is the flattening I mean. I want you to write a deadpan satirical piece in the form of a seed-funding pitch deck for a fictional startup founded by someone like my friend: an AI central planner that claims to have dissolved the entire problem. The pitch should be around 700 words, laid out as slide headings with one to three bullet points each, and it should read like genuine startup prose — earnest, metrics-obsessed, given to strategic humble-bragging. It should be funny to someone who knows the Mises–Hayek side and the Lange response, because every innovation the startup boasts about is visibly an attempt to reinvent a market price while rebranding it as a prediction. It should also be legible, and ideally funny, to someone who has never heard of the debate. Keep the irony deadpan throughout — no winking or authorial asides — and let the reductio emerge from the startup's own materials: a "trial-and-error repricing engine" that is simply a market run by the company, tacit knowledge scraped from delivery-driver complaints, a pricing model's hallucinated shadow price hailed as a planning breakthrough. The target is the modern claim that the calculation problem was only ever a data-processing bottleneck, and the style of venture-backed solutionism that goes with it, not any named person.

Please take this in two stages. First, act as a satirist who genuinely understands the debate and write a short working note brainstorming the real positions you intend to exaggerate, so the parody stays fair to the underlying ideas rather than beating a straw man. Then write the pitch deck itself as a polished piece. Think carefully about the exact point of the original arguments so the jokes cut at the right place. Please write in British English throughout.
