The kalam argument runs on two claims—whatever begins to exist has a cause, and the universe began to exist—and for a while it felt like Big Bang cosmology had locked in the second one. But now cosmologists take seriously models with no absolute beginning: cyclic universes, eternal inflation, proposals where time just isn't fundamental near the start so "before the Big Bang" is the wrong question. Suppose those models turn out right and the universe did not begin. What actually happens to the kalam argument then? Which premise takes the hit, and does the damage stay there or does it leak into the first premise too? Im mostly curious whether a contingency-style argument still does the work in a universe with no beginning, and whether it can do that without just secretly relying on the same intuition that made kalam feel obvious. Is it a real alternative, or kalam with a longer fuse?
