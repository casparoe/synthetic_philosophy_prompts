I write about science and ideas for a Sunday paper, and for a piece I'm working on I've been following a woman named Lise who sits on a serious-incident review panel. The panel looks at accidents and deaths where the interesting question isn't what someone did, but what they didn't do. Lise is maddeningly terse about method: she'll give a verdict on each case, but she won't stand back and say weather it's a duty rule, a normal-practice rule, or something she's making up as she goes. I've seen enough of her cases now that I'm starting to see a pattern, but I'd like a second read before I file.

Here's her record, as far as I've seen it:
- A night nurse skips the scheduled 2 a.m. check on an elderly patient whose breathing tube has a history of slipping; the tube slips and the patient dies. Lise: the omission was a cause.
- A father leaves his two-year-old in the bath to answer the front door; the child drowns. Lise: a cause.
- A signalman nips out of his signal box for a cigarette at just the moment he should be changing the points; two trains collide. Lise: a cause.
- A man out walking his dog sees a child face-down in the shallows of a reservoir, with a lifebelt on a post beside him; he doesn't throw it or wade in, and the child dies. Lise: not a cause.
- A shopper sees an old man collapse in a supermarket aisle and doesn't alert a member of staff; the man dies. Lise: not a cause.

Now there's a fresh one, and the rest of the panel is split. Sadie, a next-door neighbour, has for the past eighteen months driven Edna to her weekly dialysis appointment every Wednesday morning. It started as a one-off favour after Edna's husband died, then just carried on. Edna has no car, no family nearby, and plainly relies on the lift by now, which Sadie knows. Last Wednesday Sadie didn't turn up — she'd been at a wedding the night before, overslept, and didn't phone. Edna waited for hours, missed dialysis, and by Friday she was in intensive care with fluid on her lungs. The panel has to say whether Sadie's not turning up was a cause of Edna's deterioration.

So: what do you predict Lise will say, and which rule is she actually running? Spell out the candidate rules that could still fit her track record, and say what each of them would do with Sadie. I keep going back and forth between a duty-based story, a normal-practice story, and a but-for story, though I don't see how the but-for one survives the reservoir and supermarket cases unless she's being inconsistent. Give me your best prediction, plus one more case I could put to her that would cleanly separate the rules.
