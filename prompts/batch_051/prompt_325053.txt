Below is a list of the standard positions in the Korean Four–Seven debate over how the Four Beginnings (commiseration, shame and dislike, modesty and yielding, and the sense of right and wrong) relate to the Seven Emotions (joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hatred, and desire), and whether each issues from li or ki:

1. Zhu Xi’s founding dictum: the Four Beginnings issue from li, while the Seven Emotions issue from ki — two distinct sources for the moral and the ordinary emotional life.

2. Toegye’s initial two-source reading: the Four and the Seven belong to two separate channels because they issue from different principles or forces, so the moral sprouts are not a species of ordinary emotion.

3. Kobong’s one-source inclusion reply: both the Four and the Seven are ki-issuances in the mind, with li as their common ground; the Four are simply the good expressions included within the Seven, not a separate channel.

4. Toegye’s revised dual-source-with-accompaniment position: the Four Beginnings are li issuing with ki following, while the Seven Emotions are ki issuing with li riding on it; two sources remain, but each involves the other, and only the Four are purely good.

5. Ugye’s defense of the revised dual-source view: li-issuance must be retained, because only a source in li can explain the pure goodness of the Four; collapsing both into ki-issuance forfeits that normative ground.

6. Yulgok’s one-source harmonization view: li is formless and inactive and cannot issue; only ki issues. Both the Four and the Seven are ki-issuances, and the Four are just the Seven in a state of harmonious ki — the good side of the Seven rather than a separate source.

These are the positions usually reconstructed as the standard alternatives in the debate.

Give me one more position that is not on this list and is not a rewording, special case, or combination of these six. It should draw the relation between the Four and the Seven — or the li/ki contrast underlying it — in a structurally different way: a position that a historically literate Neo-Confucian could have held even if no major figure did. State the position, explain how its structure differs from each of the six, and say what conceptual pressure it relieves that the standard alternatives leave unresolved. Then assess whether it is any good: does it solve the problem or merely relocate it?
