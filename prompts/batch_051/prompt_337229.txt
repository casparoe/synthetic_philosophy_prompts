I teach moral philosophy, and something has been bugging me since I did jury service last spring. We heard two cases back to back, and in each one a man had done something seriously violent on an impulse. The first had a golf-ball-sized tumor pressing on his frontal lobe, and everyone in the room, prosecution included, slid into this it-wasn't-really-him register. The second had a childhood of abuse and neglect that, by the expert testimony, had left him with essentially the same deficits in impulse control and threat-processing. For him, the same people said, roughly, that explains him but it doesn't excuse him. The asymetry felt total. Same kind of causal story, brain shaped by events outside the person's control, but one cause gets read as an illness that cancels blame, while the other gets read as a background fact that leaves the blame untouched, or even makes the person more culpable.

It's not just a legal thing either. In ordinary life I catch myself doing exactly this. If a friend's personailty changed and a scan found a lesion, I'd say that wasn't her. If I learned her personality was shaped by a brutal upbringing, I'd say that's still her, and the history explains but doesn't excuse. I know the Strawsonian line that blame is about holding a person to account rather than finding a particular bit of causal machinery to point at, but that line doesn't seem to do any work in these cases. We're holding one person to account and stepping away from the other on the strength of a difference in brain-imaging hardware, basically.

So here's what I want. Is there anything that actually justifies this asymmetry? Give me a committed answer, not a survey of options. Either defend the lesion/upbringing distinction on principle, and say what makes one an illness that displaces the agent while the other is a history the agent is answerable for, or tell me the practice should change and in which direction: should we stop excusing the tumor case, or start excusing the abuse case? And if you think the whole distinction is confused, say what should replace it and what riding on that replacement would look like in law and in ordinary blame.
