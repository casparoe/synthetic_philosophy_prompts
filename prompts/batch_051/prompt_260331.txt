I'm a junior and a member of my high school's after-school logic club, which mostly works through paradoxes and probability puzzles. A few weeks ago, a senior introduced an argument about assigning probabilities to ordinary English conditionals; we wrote it out as carefully as we could, and the conclusion it reaches is so implausible that something must have gone wrong, but no one in the club can agree about which step to blame. I'll write A→C for the indicative conditional "If A, then C" — the ordinary everyday conditional, not the truth-functional material conditional.

The argument runs as follows. (1) The probability of an indicative conditional should equal the probability of its consequent given its antecedent: P(A→C) = P(C | A). This seems to capture what it is to be confident in "If A, then C." (2) The same principle should hold when we have background information E: P(A→C | E) = P(C | A ∧ E), whenever the relevant conditional probabilities are defined. (3) Take E to be C; then P(A→C | C) = P(C | A ∧ C) = 1. Take E to be ¬C; then P(A→C | ¬C) = P(C | A ∧ ¬C) = 0. (4) By the law of total probability, P(A→C) = P(A→C | C)·P(C) + P(A→C | ¬C)·P(¬C). Substituting from (3), P(A→C) = 1·P(C) + 0·P(¬C) = P(C). (5) Combining (1) and (4), P(C | A) = P(C) for any A and C such that 0 < P(C) < 1 and both A ∧ C and A ∧ ¬C have positive probability. (6) But that cannot be right. In a standard 52-card deck, let A be "the card is red" and C be "the card is a diamond." Then P(C | A) = 1/2 while P(C) = 1/4, and the condition in (5) is met.

That means either step (1), step (2), or something in the background assumptions about how conditionals interact with probability has to be false, or step (4) is being misapplied. I would most like to keep (1), because it seems like the only principled way to assign a number to the probability of a conditional at all, but I can no longer see how to keep it without walking into the conclusion. Where does this argument actually go wrong? If the reasoning is valid, which premise is it least costly to reject, and what would rejecting it commit us to?
