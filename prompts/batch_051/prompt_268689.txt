My speaker is a philosopher who defends what she calls lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism. The rough version of the view she holds: preventing and reducing suffering always has priority, wellbeing is not symmetrical, and above some threshold of intensity, no amount of positive wellbeing anywhere can justify creating or allowing extreme suffering. Even below that threshold, preventing a given amount of suffering still counts for more than producing an equal or even somewhat larger amount of positive wellbeing.

I need 8–10 questions that force her past restating the view. I want at least one question on each of the following, and for each one a follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give:

- the classic world-exploder objection — painlessly and secretly ending all sentient life to prevent future suffering — where her likely reply is that the threshold version blocks it and she isn't committed to murdering happy people;
- the antinatalism charge — the view seems to give no reason to bring new happy lives into existence — where her likely reply is that creating happy people is permitted but not required and she treats that as a feature, not a bug;
- the threshold itself — how she identifies extreme suffering without a unit of measurement — where her likely reply is that a boundary can be vague without being unreal;
- wild animal suffering — whether the view commits her to large-scale intervention in nature — where her likely reply is that any intervention has to pass an expected-net-suffering-reduction test.

Also work in at least one question on the criterion-versus-decision-procedure move (the view might be self-defeating if people follow it too literally in daily life), and end with a question about what specific evidence, cases, or thought experiments would actually make her revise the view.
