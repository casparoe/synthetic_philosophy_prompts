I'm a junior on my school's academic honesty board, and I have to write the opinion our board will vote on Thursday. A teacher just found out that a senior — he graduates in three weeks and already got into a good college — copied big chunks of a history paper back when he was a freshman; the only reason it's coming out now is that she recovered an old file by accident, not because he did anything new. Our handbook gives the full penalty for academic dishonesty (an F for the course and a note on the transcript) but says nothing about weather an old offense counts the same as one from this year, and this is the first old case we've ever had to decide. Half the board thinks we should impose the full thing, even if his college rescends the offer, and half thinks pushing him now for something from 9th grade is just vindictive, especially since his record's been clean since, he admitted it when the teacher asked, and he keeps saying he's a different person now than the 14-year-old who did it. I need a short written recommendation saying exactly what we should do — full penalty, reduced, or nothing — and it has to take the strongest reason on the losing side seriously, not just knock down a straw man.
