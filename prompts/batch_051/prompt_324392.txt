My school's student council is considering this rule for next year's activities budget, even though nobody knows whether clubs will run in person, online, or get hit by a midyear district funding cut. I'm supposed to vote on it next week and I don't actually see what it would do. The rule: council members list every future they can think of (in person, online, hybrid, spring funding cut, whatever), then for each proposed budget everyone rates how bad that budget would make each future on a 0–10 scale. You throw out any budget whose worst-case score is worse than some other budget's worst-case score. Among the survivors you pick the one with the lowest maximum regret, where regret means how much worse that budget does in a given future than the best budget for that same future does. Ties go to a student vote. Walk me through what this actually does — what budgets people start proposing, how people game the ratings, which futures conveniently never make it onto the list, and wether the regret step quietly undoes the maximin part. Does it end up Rawlsian or Harsanyi-ish, or just a fight over what counts as a possible future? And if I've set this up in a confused way, tell me that instead of going along with it.
