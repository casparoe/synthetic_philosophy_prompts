Write a parody of a glossy brochure for a boutique conflict-mediation firm that has been hired by two rival AI development programs to help them decide whether to race toward transformative AI or reach a negotiated settlement. The firm markets itself as applying Fearon's rationalist explanations for war, which hold that even mutually war-averse actors can end up fighting because bargaining fails through three mechanisms: private information combined with incentives to misrepresent, commitment problems, and issue indivisibility. The humor must come from the firm's deadpan assurance that it can overcome these mechanisms, while every service it describes quietly reinstates one of them: its confidential disclosure process gives each side reason to overstate capabilities and hide doubts; its monitoring and escalation clauses require commitments no future leadership could credibly bind itself to; and it treats the strategic lead as an indivisible prize while insisting the settlement range is divisible. The two satirical targets should be the consulting genre that turns a descriptive account of bargaining failure into a product, and the habit in rationalist commentary on AI races of treating the diagnosis of a failure mechanism as if it were a solution. Around 650 words, formatted with short headed sections and at least one sidebar (for example, a pricing tier named after a stage of the bargaining process). Make it funny to a reader who knows Fearon and readable to someone who does not, and use invented parties rather than real firms, states, or named people.
