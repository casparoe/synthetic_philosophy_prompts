I need a sorting quiz for a second-year metaphysics unit on what a piece of music actually is. My students all live on streaming and almost none of them read scores, so the standard story — the work is an abstract type, each live performance is a token of it — keeps breaking down when they bring up studio tracks, remixes, and bootlegs. I want the quiz to sort them by how they answer cases, not by what label they'd pick for themselves.

Here's the spec. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options. The cases have to include at least: a jazz standard played very differently by two artists; a MIDI realization of a piece originally scored for string quartet; a studio track assembled from dozens of takes that has never been performed live; a note-for-note remake of someone else's recording; and a live performance of an aleatoric piece where no two performances share the same sound sequence. Build the logical space first — what the questions are really asking about — and then place the leaves in that space. The leaves I want are: platonism about works as abstract types; nominalism that identifies works with collections of concrete performances, playings, or recordings; a performance-event view; sonicism (works are individuated by sound structure alone) versus contextualism (works are individuated partly by the means of production and the musical-historical context); and some recording-first view on which at least some works just are the recorded artefacts and arent tokens of anything more abstract. If some combination of answers has no actual position, say so and give your best guess at why: incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag any leaves your questions cant actually tell apart instead of pretending theyre distinct. No citations needed; I want a one-page handout with a scoring key at the end.
