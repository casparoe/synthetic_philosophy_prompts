Consider the classic format debate over mental imagery. Pylyshyn argued that the effects taken to show picture-like imagery—mental rotation, image scanning, and related results—can be explained by participants' tacit knowledge of how objects and scenes look and move, so there is no need to posit a depictive representational format; on this view the experienced image is not doing explanatory work. Suppose we accept the later findings that visual imagery activates retinotopically organized early visual cortex in a perception-like way, and that temporarily disrupting those visual areas impairs performance on imagery tasks. Does Pylyshyn's tacit-knowledge argument survive this development—does it still go through, need repair, or is it dead? Explain which premise or stage of the argument the neuroimaging and interference findings bear on, whether the argument can be restated to accommodate them without collapsing into Kosslyn-style depictivism, and what, if anything, is given up in that restatement.
