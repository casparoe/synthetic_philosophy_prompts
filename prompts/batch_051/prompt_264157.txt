Act as a philosopher of political violence. I have been trying to bring my verdicts on particular cases into line with an explicit definition of terrorism, and they will not cohere. Here are the cases and my verdicts.

(1) A separatist group detonates a bomb in a crowded market in order to force the government to negotiate. I call that terrorism.

(2) An occupying power bombs a city's water-treatment plant, foreseeing that the resulting disease and dehydration will fall mostly on civilians, in order to break the population's will to resist. I call that terrorism too, even though the actor is a state and the immediate target is infrastructure.

(3) A revolutionary group assassinates the dictator who has run the secret prisons and ordered the disappearances. I do not call that terrorism; I call it tyrannicide, or political assassination.

(4) A hacker collective locks a hospital's records and threatens to release patient data unless the government withdraws a surveillance bill. No one is physically hurt. I still find myself calling it cyberterrorism.

(5) A protester sets fire to unoccupied police cars during a riot, aiming to destroy equipment and attract attention. I do not call that terrorism; I call it arson.

(6) A militia in a civil war shells a neighborhood inhabited mainly by the other side's civilian supporters in order to make them flee. I call that terrorism.

(7) A government's security forces disappear suspected dissidents and then quietly publicize the disappearances so that others will be afraid to organize. I call that state terrorism.

(8) A gunman livestreams an attack on a grocery store in order to ignite an ethnic war. I call that terrorism.

The principle I keep reaching for is that terrorism is political violence that intentionally or foreseeably kills, wounds, or physically endangers noncombatants. But that principle implies the hospital hack is not terrorism, which contradicts my verdict, and it threatens to imply that assassinating the dictator is terrorism if the dictator counts as a noncombatant, which I resisted. It is also silent about attacks that damage only property.

Please repair or replace this principle. Begin by sketching two or three candidate principles and showing why the best one wins. Then state it precisely enough to yield verdicts on new cases, indicate which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate and whether those are the verdicts I should overturn, apply it to the case I keep going back and forth on, and provide one further case of your own with the verdict your principle dictates. The case I cannot settle: a group bombs an empty legislative building at night, having warned the press and the police so that no one is hurt, as a purely symbolic protest against a fraudulent election.
