My philosophy of mind prof is some flavor of illusionist about consciousness and she's made us read Daniel Dennett three times. Her verdicts so far this term: the inverted spectrum with no behavioral difference, she says there's no fact of the matter about who's seeing the "real" red; blindsight, she says the blind field has no phenomenal conciousness at all; the color-phi case where a red dot seems to turn green mid-flight, she says there was no real intermediate sensation and the subject's report is just a retrospective story; a wine-tasting machine that discriminates a thousand wines, she says attributing qualia to it explains nothing and the question whether it "really" has them is a mistake. She keeps quoting one bit from Dennett's "Quining Qualia": "I want to shift the burden of proof, so that anyone who wants to appeal to private, subjective properties has to prove first that in so doing they are not making a mistake. This status of guilty until proven innocent is neither unprecedented nor indefensible (so long as we restrict ourselves to concepts)."

Next week's case is an ordinary adult with normal color vision seeing a ripe red tomato in good light. Predict her verdict: does she have phenomenal consciousness, i.e., is there something it's like for her? Give the two or three rules that fit her past verdicts, say what each predicts about the tomato case, then give your best guess and which further case would settle which rule she's actually running.
