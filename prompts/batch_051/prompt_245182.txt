I'm a software engineer, and when I read philosophy I keep reaching for the same tool: a spec. Before I take any theory seriously, I want its requirements stated up front, the way I'd want acceptance criteria for a component I'm about to build. I've been trying to make sense of a puzzle about intention, and I'd like you to write that spec for me.

The puzzle is Kavka's toxin puzzle. As I understand it: a billionaire offers you a large sum of money if, at midnight tonight, you intend to drink a vial of toxin tomorrow afternoon. The toxin will make you ill for a day but has no lasting effects. The money will be in your account tomorrow morning, before the time you'd have to drink, and you don't have to drink the toxin to get paid — you only need the intention at midnight. Because you know all this, you also know that tomorrow you won't drink: by then you'll already have the money, and drinking would only make you sick. The puzzle is whether you can form the intention at midnight, and whether you should. It looks as though the best outcome is the one where you form the intention, but it also looks as though you can't intend to do something you know you won't do, and you can't just switch an intention on the way you can raise your hand.

What I want is a list of desiderata — requirements, acceptance criteria — for any adequate theory of what it is to intend to do something, or of how intention fits into rational action if that's the better framing. The toxin puzzle should be the central stress test, but the list should cover the surrounding terrain too: what a theory has to classify correctly before it earns the right to be taken seriously, which differences it has to preserve rather than paper over, and which awkward questions it has to answer rather than dodge. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it — why a theory that fails it has missed something real. Then tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of particular camps, and mark any place where two requirements pull in opposite directions and can't both be met at full strength. If the toxin puzzle shows that two things everyone wants can't both be had, say so explicitly; that's the part I most want to see. I don't need a scorecard of existing theories — I'll do that myself once the criteria are clear.

Please structure the response like this:

1. "The target" — restate the puzzle in your own words and say exactly what a theory in this area has to be a theory of. (100–150 words)
2. "The desiderata" — a numbered list. Each item: the requirement, then the case or consideration behind it. Mark jointly unsatisfiable pairs inline as you go. (600–800 words)
3. "Negotiable and non-negotiable" — which requirements are load-bearing for any adequate theory, and which are optional or specific to one camp. (150–250 words)
4. "Tensions" — pull together the places where requirements can't be jointly satisfied, or can only be satisfied by weakening one of them; where the toxin puzzle is what forces the trade-off, say so. (150–250 words)

Prefer over-explaining the motivation for a requirement over name-dropping a theory I'm supposed to recognize. No reading list and no citations — I want the reasoning, not the references.
