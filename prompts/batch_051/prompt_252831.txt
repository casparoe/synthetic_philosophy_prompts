I'm a software engineer who maintains simulation infrastructure, and I've been asked to run a ninety-minute session for about twenty working engineers moving into climate-adjacent projects. Most of them are comfortable fitting models and doing parameter sweeps, but none has worked with the kind of multi-model projections used in climate work, and I don't have a philosophy background, so I want the session to stay concrete and hands-on. I'd like an activity, not a lecture, in which small groups each run a deliberately simplified climate model (or a spreadsheet version) for a single fictional coastal city, with slight variations in parameters and model structure, and produce a 30-year projection for peak flood height or flood probability. The groups must then make one shared decision—say, how much to spend on a sea wall—where their projections disagree, the cost of under-building is severe and asymmetric, and the cost of over-building is modest but real. I want the setup to push them toward the moves people actually make: averaging the projections, weighting by how detailed the model is, discarding outliers, or treating the spread of runs as a probability distribution over futures; and I want them to discover for themselves that adding more runs can raise confidence without improving accuracy, and that the only uncertainties worth resolving are the ones that would change the decision.

What I need is a runnable design. Please specify rules and timings for the groups, the materials I should prepare (simple code or spreadsheets rather than real climate model specifications), an explicit payoff or scoring scheme that doesn't require real money, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto specific ideas—why an ensemble average is not automatically the best estimate, why agreement across models can be manufactured by shared assumptions, and why decision-relevant uncertainty is not the same as the variance of the model outputs. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle that, and be clear about which feature of real climate modeling the exercise cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply myself in the debrief.
