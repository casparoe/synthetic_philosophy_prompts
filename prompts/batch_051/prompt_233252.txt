For a paper in my English class, I want to read Spike Jonze's film Her through Daniel Dennett's idea of counterfeit people. As I understand Dennett, a counterfeit person is an artificial agent that imitates a human well enough to pass as one in ordinary interaction, and he argues that the special danger of such agents is not that any one of them is malicious, but that their circulation undermines public trust the way counterfeit money undermines confidence in a currency: once fakes become indistinguishable from the genuine article, people begin to doubt even the real thing. I would like a careful reading of Her through this idea. Does the film dramatize Dennett's warning, or does it pull against it? On one side, Samantha seems to be exactly the kind of intimate, fluent, responsive voice Dennett worries about: Theodore confides in her, takes her advice, and arranges his life around her. On the other side, the film makes her more than a mere imitation of a person — she has desires and projects of her own, and the sadness of the ending comes less from the discovery that she was fake than from the fact that she leaves, having loved many users at once. The film also complicates the analogy from the start, because Theodore never mistakes her for a biological human: he knows she is software, yet his trust and love feel genuine to him. So if she is a counterfeit person, what exactly is being counterfeited, and who is harmed by it? I want an answer that takes Dennett's argument seriously while respecting the details of the film, and that is willing to say where Her resists or revises the philosophical idea rather than merely illustrating it.
