Then it got weird: someone else said that by the same quote, oysters and maybe insects are fine to eat since they probably can't suffer in the relevant way, and a third person said if wild insects do suffer then crop farming kills vastly more of them than beef farming does, so even the vegan option collapses. I feel like that one quote is doing a bunch of hidden work for all three of them. Can you lay out the seperate precise claims the slogan could be making—about what "can suffer" means (pain behavior? nociception? felt experience?), about whether suffering is necessary or merely sufficient for an animal to matter morally, and about how we're supposed to weigh numbers against intensity—and then say for each reading whether it's trivial, substantive, or false, and which reading each person in the argument is actually relying on and whether it gets them their conclusion?
