A decision theory discussion group is constructing a diagnostic instrument to sort participants by their implicit commitments about the Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery. The instrument should be a decision tree, or a short quiz of eight to ten items with a scoring key, that takes a person from their answers in concrete cases to a named decision-theoretic position. The instrument should be usable by people who have never read the original Hofstadter discussion; all scenarios must be self-contained.

The positions that must appear as terminal leaves are: game-theoretic rationalist or causal decision theorist; evidential decision theorist; Hofstadter-style superrationalist; functional decision theorist; and a deflationist position holding that the dilemma is too sensitive to framing or lacks a well-defined probability distribution over the other players' choices. You may add leaves for a mixed-strategy superrationalist and for an asymmetry-aware superrationalist if the tree naturally requires them, but do not force them in.

Each branch node should be a concrete case or claim, not a question about which label the person prefers. Base the nodes on variations of the dilemma, such as: the original version in which a prize is awarded to a participant only if exactly one of twenty anonymous participants responds within forty-eight hours, and nothing is awarded if zero or multiple respond; the two-player prisoner's dilemma cash version with symmetric payoffs; a version where unilateral defection yields an extremely large reward, so that the superrational strategy becomes probabilistic rather than pure cooperation; a version where one player can make a public commitment before the others choose; and a version where the participants are produced by a generator that runs the same algorithm on identical inputs. The first node should be the question that actually separates views about whether a decision can be evidence about what sufficiently similar agents in the same situation will do, even when there is no causal channel between them.

The answer should state the mapping from answer patterns to leaves, should identify which combinations of answers have no leaf, and should briefly diagnose each unoccupied combination as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. It should also flag any pair of positions that the tree cannot separate on the given cases and explain why.
