My aunt recently came into a substantial sum from a maturing endowment policy, and she asked me to help her think through some options that a financial adviser had laid out for her. Before drawing up an actual proposal, the adviser wanted to gauge her appetite for risk, so he presented her with a series of hypothetical payout profiles in pairs. In the first pair, she had to choose between Profile A, a guaranteed payment of £1 million, and Profile B, which offered an 89 per cent chance of £1 million, a 10 per cent chance of £5 million, and a 1 per cent chance of receiving nothing. In the second pair, she had to choose between Profile C, an 11 per cent chance of £1 million and otherwise nothing, and Profile D, a 10 per cent chance of £5 million and otherwise nothing. She chose Profile A from the first pair and Profile D from the second.

I mentioned her choices to a friend of mine who read economics at university, and he was dismissive. He argues that the two pairs are, beneath the surface, the same decision: the 89 per cent chance of £1 million that Profiles A and B have in common, and the 89 per cent chance of receiving nothing that Profiles C and D have in common, should both be irrelevant to the comparison, so that anyone who prefers A to B ought also to prefer C to D. He therefore maintains that my aunt's combination of choices is inconsistent. I cannot shake the feeling that he is mistaken. In the first pair, the certainty of a million pounds seems worth protecting even at the cost of forgoing a genuine chance at five million; in the second pair, the slightly greater risk in moving from C to D seems a fair price to pay for a prize five times as large.

I am less interested in which profile would be the better investment in practice than in understanding whether my friend's claim about consistency is sound. Is he right that this pattern of choices cannot be rationally defended together, and should I advise my aunt to reconsider one of them? If so, which one, and on what grounds? Please answer in British English.
