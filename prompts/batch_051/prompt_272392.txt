Sorry, this is still half-formed. In my political philosophy class we've been doing the moral emotions, and there's a distinction I keep trying to make in conversation but can't find a name for. Take two reactions to the same billionaire. One friend sees her yacht and just wants that life—that gets called envy. A union organiser looks at the same person and says the fortune depends on people being paid below a living wage, and that she resents being told to be grateful for the crumbs. Commentators call that envy too, but it seems like a different kind of thing: it isn't about wanting the advantage, it's about the advantage being unjust and an injury to the people it came at the expense of. Is there an established name for that second attitude, and is it usefully treated as a separate concept from envy as a vice? I'm also curious whether genuine envy—actually wanting what they have—can ever be the appropriate response rather than a character flaw, as some seem to think when inequality is extreme. I'm after the precise boundaries between these, not just a word.
