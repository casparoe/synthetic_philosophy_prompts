I have been working through the Judy Benjamin problem and have ended up with a principle that strikes me as either the obvious solution or a mistake I cannot yet locate. Suppose an agent has a prior over events A and B and then learns a single piece of information: the conditional probability of B given A is q. Nothing else is learned. The difficulty is that this constraint underdetermines the posterior; it fixes the ratio of B-mass to A-mass, but it says nothing about how much total mass should sit in A, or how mass should be distributed across the cells of the not-A region. The principle I have been calling antecedent quarantine says that a bare conditional constraint should do no work on the probability of its own antecedent: the posterior probability of A must equal the prior probability of A. All the belief change should occur inside the A-branch, moving credence between B and the rest of A until the conditional probability reaches q; the probability of not-A and the internal distribution of the not-A branch should remain exactly as they were. In a medical example, if I have assumed a test's sensitivity—the probability of a positive result given disease—is 0.6, and a reliable report tells me it is actually 0.9, antecedent quarantine says my credence that I have the disease should not move. The report has told me about the relationship between disease and test result, not about the presence of disease.

I take this to be a general principle about the force of conditional information: conditional constraints are relationship-level evidence, not instantiation-level evidence. Any rule that lets P(A) move is, on my view, confusing the probability of the conditional with the probability of the antecedent given the conditional. But I can feel the pull in the other direction. If I had given A high credence and B given A low credence, then learning that B given A is high will require taking away a large block of A-and-not-B mass; I could take it from not-A instead, and nothing in the constraint alone tells me that the sacrifice should not fall partly on A itself. A rule that fixes P(A) is an extra assumption, not an absence of one. The cases where my intuition flips are precisely the ones I cannot characterize—cases where the conditional is an indexical fact about my own location, or where the report could have been produced as easily whether or not A held. What I want checked: Is antecedent quarantine a coherent update rule, or is it just a familiar proposal I have reinvented? Does it produce Dutch books, fail iterativity, or mishandle cases where the source of the conditional matters? And what feature separates the medical case from the paratrooper case, where many people feel the antecedent should move?
