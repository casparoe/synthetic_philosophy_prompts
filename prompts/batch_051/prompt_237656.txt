I'm in a grad seminar on free will and we just spent a week on Frankfurt-style cases. The version we worked through is the usual one: Black wants Jones to decide to do A, where A is something actually shady (say, skimming money from the treasury of a club he manages). Black has secretly installed a gizmo that will detect the neural precursor of Jones deciding not-A and, if it goes off, will force Jones to decide to do A instead. In the actual course of events the precursor never appears, the device stays idle, and Jones decides on his own to do A. The prof's takeaway was the standard line: Jones is responsible for deciding A even though he couldn't have decided otherwise, so the principle of alternative possibilities is in trouble.

But I cant stop thinking about a variant we didn't cover. Suppose the gizmo is only probabilistically effective: if the precursor fires, the device successfully overrides Jones 80% of the time, and 20% of the time it shorts out and does nothing (assume the failure is a genuinely random hardware glitch, not something Jones can influence or foresee). In the actual run, once again, the precursor never fires and Jones decides A on his own. Now here's what I can't settle: does Jones still lack alternative possibilities in the sense the original case needs? There is a possible continuation where he's about to decide not-A, the device glitches, and he ends up deciding not-A; but whether that continuation happens isn't up to him. Does that count as him having been able to do otherwise for the purposes of PAP? And does the case still support the Frankfurt conclusion, or does the 20% failure rate give the PAP defender room to say the thought experiment no longer severs responsibility from alternative possibilities? I'd rather you weigh the strongest version of each side than get the textbook answer. And if your view is that 80% reliability is enough to kill alternative possibilities, I want to know what you make of the threshold question: is 51% enough? Is any nonzero chance of failure enough? That's the part I find genuinely confusing.
