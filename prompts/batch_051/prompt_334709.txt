I'm on the board of a small housing cooperative, and we're stuck on a disciplinary case. A resident installed a heat-pump bracket on the outside of her unit last fall. It turns out the co-op has a rule that any alteration to the exterior needs pre-approval, but here's the thing: that rule was adopted four years ago at a meeting that was announced only by a flyer in the laundry room, and it was never added to the resident handbook or the co-op website. The only official record is the minutes, which sit in a binder in the maintenance office. She says she had no idea the rule existed and that the previous board president had verbally told her the bracket would be fine. Two current board members admit they didn't know about the rule either. Some of us think the rule is still the rule and we have to enforce it, or everyone will start claiming ignorance; others think it's unfair to bind someone to a rule they couldn't reasonably have found out about. I need to circulate a short written decision memo before Thursday's meeting: should we require her to remove the bracket, and should we impose the fines the policy calls for, waive them, or do something in between? Please give a clear recommendation and engage the strongest argument for enforcing anyway, including what it would mean for precedent if a rule can be set aside because someone didn't happen to read the minutes.
