I'm taking a seminar on formal epistemology and I've gotten myself tangled up in a argument about objective Bayesianism and the maximum entropy principle. It started when a physicist friend claimed that as long as two people share the same evidence, there's exactly one rational prior for them to adopt—the one that maximizes Shannon entropy subject to whatever constraints the evidence imposes. I wanted to push back, but the argument he gave me is annoyingly smooth. I've reconstructed it like this:

(1) A rational prior is a probability distribution that reflects exactly the evidence you have and nothing more than that.
(2) If two distributions are both compatible with the evidence but differ, then at least one of them is smuggling in information that goes beyond the evidence.
(3) The distribution compatible with the evidence that carries the least extra information is, almost by definition, the one with the maximum Shannon entropy subject to the evidence's constraints.
(4) So for any body of evidence, there is exactly one rational prior: the maximum-entropy distribution under those constraints.
(5) Therefore two rational agents with the same evidence will have the same priors; any downstream disagreement in their posteriors must come from differing evidence or a mistake.

The conclusion feels way too strong, but every time I try to attack one of the steps I end up just rephrasing it and nodding along. My nagging suspicion is that step (3) is hiding a choice—something about how the possibilities are carved up being itself a prior, or how Shannon entropy is only defined relative to a background measure, so the "least information" is only least relative to some measure you already had to pick. But I can't make the objection precise, and maybe I'm just resisting a conclusion I don't like.

I'm not after a literature review or citations; I want to know whether this argument works and, if not, exactly where it breaks. If a step fails, tell me what the correct version of that step would be; if it doesn't fail, tell me why the conclusion is actually fine.
