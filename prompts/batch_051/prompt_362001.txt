The atomists' core bet was that at bottom there are only atoms and the void. Atoms were indivisible, completely full, solid, and impenetrable; the void was exactly nothing—what isn't. Now suppose Democritus gets a genuine modern-physics briefing: ordinary solidity emerges from electromagnetic interactions, atoms are mostly empty space, the fundamental picture is pointlike particles and fields, and the quantum-field vacuum isn't the pure nothing his void was meant to be. What would he say? Specifically: (a) does anything he'd still call the void exist anymore, or has physics in effect filled the void, and (b) would he count an electron—pointlike, no interior, but two of them can't share a state—as an atom in his sense? And would he end up forced to admit a third kind of thing, neither atom nor void? Don't answer as a generic ancient gawking at modern science: reason from his actual principles, and be plain about which parts of the package he has to abandon, which parts survive, and where he gets stuck.
