I keep seeing biologists and philosophers argue about biological individuality and toss around the word organism like it has a clear extension, but whenever I try to apply it myself I hit cases that break my intuition. Give me a working definition of organism that gets these cases right, or at least gives a defensible verdict on each:

- a single human
- a single bacterium
- a human heart (my "definitely not" control)
- a Portuguese man o' war, made of specialized, genetically identical zooids that can't survive on their own
- a honeybee colony, the kind people sometimes describe with workers as somatic cells and the queen as germline
- a lichen, which is a fungus and a photosynthetic partner so tangled they get named as one species
- a coral polyp plus the zooxanthellae living inside it
- an aspen grove that's all one connected clone
- a Dictyostelium slime mold when the normally free-living amoebas aggregate into a slug and some cells get stuck forming the stalk
- HeLa cells growing in a lab, descended from one person and still replicating decades later

I think the heart is clearly not an organism and I lean toward counting the be colony as one, but I flip-flop on the man o' war, the lichen, and the HeLa cells. Propose the definition, run it through all ten, and tell me where it gives you a surprising verdict or where you think the word organism is quietly doing more than one job.
