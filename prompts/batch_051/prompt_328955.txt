Analyze the United Kingdom's 2022 animal-welfare legislation, which recognized decapod crustaceans and cephalopods as sentient animals while excluding insects, through the lens of the scale argument about insect sentience. Reconstruct the argument first: because the number of insects is astronomically large, even a low probability that a typical insect is sentient yields an enormous expected number of sentient individuals and correspondingly large expected welfare at stake, so the threshold for taking insect interests seriously should be correspondingly low. Then give a disciplined mapping of the legislative line-drawing: what the argument, taken at face value, would have licensed; which features of the actual decision it explains and which it misses; and what the evidentiary difference between cephalopods and decapods on one side and insects on the other, the choice between precaution and expected-value reasoning, and treating insects as a single class do to the argument. Finally, reverse the analysis: does the exclusion of insects count as an anomaly for the scale argument, a confirmation of it, or a reminder that the argument was never meant for legal line-drawing? Be explicit about the point at which the argument's force gives out.
