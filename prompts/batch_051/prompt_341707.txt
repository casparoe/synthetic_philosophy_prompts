I keep returning to a parallel between Maxwell's demon and the trader who earns abnormal returns from private information in a financial market, and I cannot decide whether it is a genuine structural identity or merely a loose metaphor organized around the idea of a free lunch. I want the mapping checked, not just embellished.

Begin by setting each side out carefully. On the physics side: a barrier divides a box of gas into two chambers, and a small door in the barrier is operated by a demon who knows the velocities of the individual molecules. By opening the door only for fast molecules moving one way and slow molecules moving the other, the demon can sort the gas into hot and cold sides without performing work, which looks like the creation of usable energy from nothing. The standard resolution is that the apparent gain is paid for in information: the demon must obtain and store knowledge of the molecules, and if information is treated as physical, erasing those records has a positive thermodynamic minimum cost — Landauer's principle — so the second law is preserved once the demon's memory is counted as part of the system.

On the economics side: a trader with private information about an asset's fundamental value can buy or sell before the public price adjusts, earning an abnormal return without producing any real good or service. In a fully informationally efficient market that return should not exist, because prices would already reflect all information; but if prices already reflected all information, no one would be compensated for acquiring information in the first place, so the market could not be fully efficient. Here too, an apparent free lunch built on an informational asymmetry seems to cancel itself once information is priced rather than treated as free.

The alleged mapping is this: the demon's local knowledge of molecular velocities corresponds to the trader's private information about value; the sorting of fast and slow molecules corresponds to the sequence of trades that exploits the price discrepancy; the extracted work corresponds to the trading profit; and the information-erasure cost that saves the second law corresponds to whatever information cost or equilibrium force restores market efficiency. So both cases appear to have the same moral — information is not free, and once you account for its cost, the free lunch disappears.

Is this a deep isomorphism, a partial analogy, or a breakdown waiting to happen? I want you to go further than sorting the superficial features; check whether the no-free-lunch result has the same logical status on each side. Specifically, I want your judgment on these points: whether the thermodynamic bound is a universal physical floor while market efficiency is an equilibrium outcome of strategic interaction; whether the demon's information is exogenous and costless by construction while the trader's information is endogenous and costly to produce; and whether any result actually transfers in either direction — for example, whether a Landauer-style lower bound on information processing would impose a bound on what market participants would pay for a trading signal, or whether the economics side has no exact analogue of entropy.
