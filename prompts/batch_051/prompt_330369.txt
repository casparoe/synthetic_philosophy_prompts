I'm in a reading group that spent the term on the big-history debates — Harari's Sapiens and Diamond's Guns, Germs, and Steel on one side, Graeber and Wengrow's The Dawn of Everything on the other — and I've started using a phrase I can't decide is clever or just annoying: "agency of the gaps." The thought is this: whenever a big-picture deterministic story (geography, crops, energy flows, whatever) leaves a hole, the critics rarely just say we don't know why that happened. They say something like people exercised political self-consciousness, or a community chose to arrange itself differently. And because "agency" and "choice" sound like explanations, the hole gets papered over instead of filled. You see it in the usual attacks on Diamond: he's supposed to be unable to explain some case because the ecology underdetermines the outcome, so the correction is that the people experimented politically — but why those people made that choice while a similar group under similar constraints didn't is left hanging, and the choosing itself is doing all the work.

I genuinely can't tell whether I've found a real recurring move in these debates or just dressed up "god of the gaps" in social-theory clothes. So: is "agency of the gaps" a fair diagnosis, or an uncharitable reading of critics like Graeber and Wengrow? Has someone already named and worked through this, either in the grand-narrative debates or in the older structure-versus-agency literature? And where does the idea break down — what does it wrongly write off as an explanatory cheat when its actually a legitimate explanation? Please separate what's settled about these debates from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and don't present a contested reading as the consensus.
