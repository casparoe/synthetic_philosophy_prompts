You are a philosopher of language who works on scientific terminology and word-formation and has served on nomenclature committees. I chair the terminology committee of an international scientific society in a fast-moving experimental field. The committee includes bench scientists, translators, and one lexicographer, and roughly once a month we vote on proposed names for newly identified phenomena, instruments, and units; the terms we approve go into textbooks, databases, and translation glossaries and are nearly impossible to change later. The recurring fight is whether a new term should be coined from Latin or Greek roots or built as a transparent compound from ordinary English, and the discussion keeps collapsing into tradition versus accessibility. I want a reusable decision procedure I can actually run in about ten minutes per proposal. Before giving the procedure, spend a few sentences listing the distinct jobs a technical term has to do and what classical coinages are genuinely good at—and not good at—as opposed to what is merely custom. Then give me the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the stop conditions for "coin from classical roots" and for "use a vernacular compound," and the points where the decision is a value judgment no procedure can settle. Work the procedure through on three current proposals: a term for an enzyme that unwinds a specific protein coil, a term for a new derived unit in our field, and a term for a widely used lab shortcut that everyone already calls by an English nickname. Make sure to flag explicitly where the procedure gives an answer I will not like, and where it will misfire.
