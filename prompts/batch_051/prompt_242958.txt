I got into an argument with a friend about whether prosperity has any moral worth beyond the stuff it buys. Like, imagine two countries with equally good health, education, safety, and so on, but one is much richer and spends the surplus on bigger houses and fancier weddings — is the richer country in any way better off, morally speaking? He ended it with 'money can't buy happiness,' as if that settled the question. I'm pretty sure that slogan is smuggling in a bunch of different claims. Can you seperate out the precise theses 'money can't buy happiness' might be asserting — different things 'happiness' could mean (momentary mood, life satisfaction, something more eudaimonic), different things 'can't buy' could mean (no effect at all, no effect above a threshold, only short-lived effects, only no effect from relative gains), and whether it's a claim about individuals or whole populations — and then say for each reading whether it's actually true, trivial, or false, and which ones would actually support his conclusion that a richer society isn't better in itself?
