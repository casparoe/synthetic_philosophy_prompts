Here's a thing I can't get to add up. Ordinary people are completely fine running a costs-and-benefits calculation with animals: the cheap chicken is worth more than a miserable chicken life, so into the basket it goes and nobody blinks. But propose the same calculation where the costs fall on a human — even a newborn with almost no brain, or someone whose mind will stay at the level of a three-year-old — and the whole thing turns monstrous, like there's a rule that overrides the totals. So the actual position people seem to hold is: utilitarian arithmetic for animals, Kantian rules for people. Is there a real principle that draws the line exaclty there, or is it just that the animals are them and we're us? Give me the strongest such principle you can, then tell me honestly whether it survives the newborn and the three-year-old-minded adult.
