I'm a retired chemist, and since I stopped running my lab I've somehow ended up moderating a small online forum for a regional naturalist society—roughly four hundred people arguing about land management, invasive species, prescribed burns, reintroductions, that sort of thing. Lately I've been reading social epistemology, and the distinction between epistemic bubbles and echo chambers has finally clicked for me in a rough way: a bubble is mostly about not hearing the relevant outside views in the first place, whereas an echo chamber is about having been trained to distrust the people or institutions that would bring those views in. My problem is that I can't turn the distinction into anything I can actually do. A flagged post lands in my queue and I find myself seperating people into the two categories rather than making a moderation decision.

I'd like a working protocol, not a lecture. Please take on the role of a professional philosopher who works on social epistemology and has advised online communities on moderation policy. Think carefully, and start by brainstorming the ways a moderator can misapply the bubble/chamber distinction before you settle on the steps. The protocol should list the questions to ask in order, with what counts as a sufficient answer to each; say where the procedure stops with "do nothing" and where it stops with "intervene"; separate factual checks from judgment calls; and mark where the decision has to be handed back to a human because it's a question of values. I get about twenty minutes per case, I only see the threads that get flagged, two or three cases arrive a week, and while I'm the one who pushes the buttons a small admin committee reviews my removals monthly, so the steps need to leave an audit trail. I suspect that treating a chamber as if it were a bubble makes things worse, and I want the protocol to respect that. Then run it on three cases: (1) a member posts a careful, well-sourced argument for reintroducing a predator and the thread just ignores it while continuing to trade local anecdotes; (2) a small group systematically answers outside expertise on prescribed burning by saying the agencies always lie because their funding depends on fire suppression; (3) a subthread for people harmed by a past dam project that treats any government hydrology report as untrustworthy, and where posters remind each other not to engage with agency scientists. Include at least one case where the procedure gives an answer I won't like, and tell me what the procedure cannot detect from post history alone.
