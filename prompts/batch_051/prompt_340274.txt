Section 6.4 of the Oakridge Community Association's covenants reads: "Any owner who begins construction of an improvement shall carry it through to completion. No owner shall leave an excavation or foundation in place for more than ninety days unless the board certifies in writing that completion has become impossible." An owner excavated and poured a concrete foundation for a swimming pool. A county-required geotechnical study then found the fill under the site too unstable for a pool without about $70,000 in remediation, roughly twice the value the pool would add to the house. Building the pool is not physically impossible, but the owner now wants to remove the foundation and regrade the lawn. The board insists he must finish, on the ground that completion is not impossible and that, with the foundation in place and $28,000 already spent, he should carry the project through. Explain how Section 6.4 applies to this owner, arguing from the text, from the purpose of the clause, and from what the drafters would most plausibly have said had they foreseen the case. Is the board's insistence that the owner finish because money is already spent an instance of the sunk cost fallacy, or can it be rationally defended as protecting neighbors' reliance and the community's interest in preventing abandoned work? Which approach should decide the case, and must the owner build the pool?
