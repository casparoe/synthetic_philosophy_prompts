ok so i've been trying to get my own position on performance legitimacy nailed down before I write the actual post, and i'd like a consistency audit on the list of commitments i've ended up with. roughly:

(1) i think a regime that delivers growth, order, and competant administration over decades acquires at least some real legitimacy — not mere stability, and not just a cost-benefit reason for outsiders to tolerate it.

(2) but i also think legitimacy and justification are different things: a government can be stable and deliver real benefits while still being illegitimate, so the fact that it works can't be the whole story.

(3) i give real weight to hypothetical consent: if reasonable people behind a thin-ish veil of ignorance would trade some voice for the goods a regime provides, that counts toward its legitimacy.

(4) i also give weight to actual consent, and i think actual consent is hard to measure under autocracy: a system that suppresses exit and voice can't point to its own longevity or low unrest as evidence of consent.

(5) and yet singapore keeps bugging me. it looks like a case where an undemocratic system produced shared prosperity, and i'd feel sqeamish saying it's simply illegitimate.

(6) i hold that preferences adapt to repression, so reported satisfaction (and absence of revolt) inside a closed system is contaminated evidence; i'd apply this to a lot of the rosier survey data out of china.

(7) i also hold a cruder view: near subsistence, goods usually beat voice; putting voice first is a bit of a luxury for people who are already secure.

do these hang together? i want the pairs in real tension ordered by severity, with the cheapest fix for each and what it costs. also flag the ones that only look like tensions and dissolve on inspection. then tell me which single revision of the seven buys the most consistency. i don't want a lecture on the literature unless it's directly needed for the audit.
