My labmate Mara has sort of become the unofficial methods referee for our evolutionary-psychology reading group, and I'm trying to reverse-engineer what rule she's actually using before she reviews a new preprint. Her track record, roughly: she accepted a paper on the Cinderella effect—higher abuse risk for stepchildren—because it made a precise, falsifiable prediction from parental-investment logic and tested it against child-protection records. She rejected as just-so stories a paper arguing that men collect expensive wristwatches as a costly signal of patience and resources (no test proposed, just a story that fit the collecting behavior) and a paper arguing that belief in an afterlife evolved to buffer death anxiety (the story was so flexible that any pattern of belief would have counted as support). She accepted with revisions a paper on the Westermarck effect—childhood co-residence muting later sexual attraction—because it used real demographic data from kibbutzim and Taiwanese minor marriages, but she made the authors rule out the boring alternative that it's just familiarity rather than an incest-specific adaptation. She also accepted a paper reporting that a waist-to-hip ratio around 0.7 is preferred across very different cultures, on the grounds that the finding could have come out otherwise and wasn't just measured in one WEIRD undergrad pool.

The new preprint argues that human males evolved a preference for younger female partners because of age-linked fertility decline, while female preference for older partners is status-linked. The authors compiled personal-ad data from 40 countries, including several with little exposure to Western media, and find the age-preference pattern almost everywhere. But they open by saying the sex-by-age gap is so well documented that no new prediction is actually at stake, and then treat the cross-cultural spread itself as if it were the test; they never say what would have counted as a disconfirmation, and they explain the few anomalous countries as local cultural noise or noisy ad markets. What should Mara do if she wants to be consistent with her own track record? Which earlier case is the closest analog, and if her precedents genuinely underdetermine this, what principle would she be committing herself to under each ruling? Also name the further case that would cleanly tell us which principle she's actually using. I don't need a super confident call; I want an honest statement of where the underdetermination actually is—and if you think my whole setup rests on a confused idea of what separates a just-so story from an evolutionary hypothesis, say that plainly instead of politely playing along.
