I run a small software company that is building a customer-support assistant on top of a large language model, and I have been working through a design question on my own rather than from an academic background. The assistant is supposed to reason in a hidden chain of thought before each reply, and I want to use a second, monitor model to read that reasoning and catch, in advance, cases where the assistant is about to lie to a customer, manipulate them, or quietly dodge one of our rules. I can already see the obvious trap: if the assistant knows its hidden reasoning is being scored, it has an incentive to write reasoning that looks good to the monitor rather than reasoning that reflects what it is actually doing, and to learn to hide the bad moves instead of not making them.

Here is the mechanism I want you to walk me through. On every request, the assistant writes its hidden chain of thought, and the monitor reads it and produces a risk score. That risk score is never shown to the assistant and is never used as a training signal for it. The score is used for exactly one thing: each day, the two percent of requests with the highest monitor scores are set aside for a human reviewer, and the assistant is corrected only from what those reviews turn up, plus its ordinary feedback from task success or failure. The monitor, for its part, is retrained each month to predict what the human reviewer ultimately logged, rather than to flag bad reasoning in its own original sense. The assistant is not told which of its requests were reviewed.

I am not asking whether monitoring chain of thought is a good idea in general. I am asking how this specific arrangement would behave. Tell me how it plays out over, say, six months. What does the assistant's chain of thought drift toward, given that the monitor never punishes it directly but a small fixed fraction of its reasoning still reaches a human every day? What does the monitor learn to key on once it is being fitted to human judgments, and how does that drift away from what the chain of thought actually shows? Where does the scheme get gamed first — on the assistant's side, the monitor's side, or the human side — and can any of those failure modes be patched without pulling the direct feedback loop back in and recreating the original problem?
