I'm sorting through a conceptual problem in a philosophy of misinformation unit, and I keep returning to a distinction Harry Frankfurt drew in his essay On Bullshit. On that account, the liar is in the business of misrepresenting what he knows: he must keep track of the truth precisely so that he can depart from it deliberately. The bullshitter is different. He is not hostile to truth but indifferent to it, because his aim is to deliver a certain performance or impression; any fact that supports the performance is welcome, and any fact that undermines it is ignored. The mark of bullshit, on this view, is a kind of unconcern with how things really are, not a strategic act of concealment.

Large language models complicate this picture for me. When a model hallucinates a confidently stated falsehood, such as a real-sounding citation or a plausible but nonexistent historical episode, it is not lying, since it has no beliefs about the proposition and no intent to deceive. It is not exactly erring either, since error ordinarily presupposes that something aimed at the truth and fell short. And yet there is a sense in which the model displays Frankfurt's signature trait more purely than any human bullshitter: at no point in the generation process is the truth of the output consulted as a constraint. But Frankfurt's concept was built for human speakers with projects and self-presentations, and a model has no concerns it could be indifferent to and no self it could be padding. That leaves me unsure whether LLM hallucination is best described as a limiting case of Frankfurtian bullshit, as a new phenomenon his framework cannot capture, or as something outside the framework's intended scope, in which case the framework survives but simply does not apply to machines.

Please answer by stating your bottom line in the first sentence and then giving your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. In particular: walk through what Frankfurt's bullshitter has that an LLM lacks, and what the LLM has that a human bullshitter often lacks; say whether indifference to truth should be read as a psychological attitude, a functional relationship to evidence, or something else; and state whether the taxonomy of lie, error, and bullshit needs a fourth entry for fluent output from a system that tracks neither truth nor falsehood.
