I've been arguing with my cousin all month about who counts as "complicit" and we keep talking past each other because we each have our own moving target: I slide toward "you knew and did nothing," he slides toward "you were part of the causal chain," and then sometimes we both want to count people who just benefitted. So I'm after a working definition of complicity—one serious enough that you can apply it to a new case and get a stable yes/no-ish verdict, without it collapsing into a general rule about every bystander being complicit in everything.

Walk me through the definition against these cases, roughly ordered from the ones we agree on to the ones we yell about. (a) A teller hands over cash during a bank robbery because a gun is pointed at her. (b) A lookout stands across the street during a burglary and texts "five-oh" when a patrol car turns the corner; the burglary would have succeeded even without him. (c) A taxi driver picks up a fare at 2 a.m. and, midway through, overhears the passenger on the phone saying he's just robbed the house they're driving past; the driver finishes the ride and takes the money. (d) A friend sits in the passenger seat while someone slashes a parked car's tires, saying "do it, she deserves it," but never leaves the car and the vandal says he would have done it anyway. (e) An investor bought shares in a clothing company fifteen years ago, reads the reporting about child labor in the company's supply chain, thinks it's awful, but keeps the shares because the fund has exit fees. (f) Someone votes against a foreign war and marches against it, but continues paying taxes to the government running the war. (g) A woman laughs at a friend's racist joke at a dinner party, partly to keep the peace; she thinks the joke is stupid and says nothing. (h) A video platform's recommendation system autoplays a terrorist recruitment video because engagement modeling says it will keep people watching, and the company knows this predictably happens but doesn't change the weights.

I don't just want a dictionary gloss. I want to know which of your verdicts you'd let the definition dictate even if it feels surprising, which one made you revise the definition, and which verdict you're least confident about in the end. Also, if you think there are two different concepts hiding under the one word—something like blame-for-helping versus blame-for-going-along-with—say so explicitly instead of papering over the split.
