You are a philosopher of physics who works on gauge theory and has a side interest in the ontology of literary works. I am a novelist, and I want you to check an asymmetry in my judgments about when a difference is real.

Here is the physics as I understand it. In classical electrodynamics, the vector potential A can be replaced by A + ∇λ, for any smooth scalar function λ, without changing the electric field, the magnetic field, or any measured particle trajectory. Physicists conclude that two potentials related by such a transformation are not two possible worlds but the same physical situation; the difference between the descriptions is what they call surplus structure, and nothing in the world corresponds to the choice of gauge. I gather they reason the same way about coordinate choices in general relativity, about the overall phase of a quantum state, and about conventions such as the choice of units.

In my own craft I reason the opposite way. If I replace a word in a sentence with a close synonym, leaving the proposition expressed and every fictional fact fixed, I take the result to be a different sentence. If a poem is translated so that its content is preserved but its rhythm and connotations change, I take the translation to be a different work, not the same poem in another gauge. If I move a story wholesale from the present tense to the past tense without changing any event, the work strikes me as changed rather than merely re-notated.

So I am an eliminativist about descriptive differences in physics and an essentialist about them in prose. I want to know whether both halves of this asymmetry can be kept. Please reconstruct the best candidate criteria a physicist could use for treating a difference as unreal — observability, invariance, causal role, something else — and apply each to the prose cases I described. Does any criterion separate the two domains without simply assuming that prose surfaces matter? In particular, confront the Aharonov-Bohm effect: an electron moving through a region where the magnetic field is zero still acquires an observable phase shift, obtained by integrating the potential along the path it travels. Physics therefore seems to treat a quantity built from gauge-variant ingredients as real. Is a line of prose more like that phase — real because it has effects — or more like a pure choice of gauge?

Then give your verdict. Should I keep the asymmetry, drop it, or restrict it, and what does your answer imply about what, if anything, is real in a gauge theory beyond its gauge-invariant core? Begin by brainstorming candidate principles, test the strongest one against at least the pair of gauge-related potentials, the Aharonov-Bohm phase, and two prose variants that agree in content, and be candid about where your preferred principle strains.
