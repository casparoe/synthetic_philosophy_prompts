I have, on theoretical grounds, become convinced that practical reasoning should maximize expected value: an outcome's improbability matters only through being multiplied by its value, and any further discounting of small probabilities would require drawing an arbitrary line. The rule has real virtues. It is unified, it handles ordinary risk without ad hoc thresholds, and it explains why a tiny chance of a sufficiently large harm can justify serious precautions.

But I also have an intuition that I cannot bring myself to give up. If a stranger asks me for my wallet and promises that, if I hand it over, he will prevent an astronomically large amount of suffering, and that if I refuse, the suffering will occur, I judge that I should not hand over the wallet, even though I cannot assign the promise literally zero probability. The reasoning on the other side is plain: any nonzero probability, times an astronomically large payoff, yields an astronomically large expected value. I could invoke a bounded utility function or a probability threshold, but both look as though they are being introduced just to stop the mugger. At the same time, I already accept the same evidential form when it comes to low-probability, high-stakes matters, such as buying disaster insurance or supporting work on low-probability catastrophic risks. The only difference I can articulate is that the mugger's claim is self-certifying and adversarial: nothing in the world, beyond the claim itself, supports the probability I am being invited to assign. But I suspect that distinction is doing the work of bounded utility without saying so, and I do not know whether that work is legitimate.

Should the theory yield, or the intuition? More specifically: is it defensible to debunk the intuition and simply pay such muggings whenever the arithmetic says to? Or should the expected-value rule be amended, for example by bounding utility, by requiring independent evidence rather than bare assigned probabilities, or by some other constraint, and can that be done without undermining the low-probability, high-stakes commitments I want to keep? Or is there a reading on which the rule and the intuition are already compatible? What would justify each of these moves? Please keep your answer to at most 500 words.
