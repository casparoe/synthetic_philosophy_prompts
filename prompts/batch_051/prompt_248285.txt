I'm taking a course on rational-choice theories of conflict and the causes of war, and I've been trying to work out whether my commitments about the AI race form a coherent package. I would be grateful for a consistency audit before I write my term paper.

Here is where I have landed.

1. I accept the rationalist puzzle associated with Fearon: because fighting is costly and risky, there should normally be some peaceful settlement that both sides would prefer to war, so war calls for a special explanation in terms of private information combined with incentives to misrepresent, commitment problems, or issue indivisibility.

2. I think that, in the interstate war literature, commitment problems do most of the explanatory work, while private information and indivisibility matter less often than is sometimes claimed.

3. I think the current race toward advanced AI is best understood as a commitment problem. The leading labs and states would, by and large, prefer a slower and safer shared trajectory, but no actor can credibly commit not to exploit a lead once it appears, so defecting from cooperative slowdowns is rational and racing is the equilibrium.

4. I also think private information is doing genuine work in the AI case. Labs have private information about their own capabilities and safety margins, and strong incentives to misrepresent both to rivals and regulators, and this prevents the parties from locating the efficient bargain in the first place.

5. But I also think the analogy to war is strained in one important respect. In an AI race, many of the costs of conflict — above all the risk of an unsafe or misaligned system — fall on third parties or on everyone collectively, while the racing parties themselves capture much of the benefit of winning.

6. Nevertheless, I still hold that the rationalist framework applies to AI races and that all three mechanisms are in principle available, even if the inefficiency is partly externalized.

I cannot tell whether this is a coherent package or whether I am helping myself to claims the framework cannot support. Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in tension, which only appear to be in tension, and how severe each genuine tension is, ranked from most to least severe. For each genuine tension, say what would have to be revised and what the cheapest fix would be, and then tell me which single revision would make the overall position most defensible.
