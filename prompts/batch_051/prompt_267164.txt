I serve on my city's Planning Commission. At our next hearing we must decide whether to rezone a parcel now limited to single-family houses so that a developer can build a five-story, ninety-unit apartment building—twenty percent of the units reserved as below-market—two blocks from a light-rail station. Our Housing Element, adopted two years ago, commits the city to adding six thousand units within eight years and identifies single-family zoning as a central cause of the regional housing shortage and of racial and economic exclusion. Our General Plan separately commits the city to preserving the character of established single-family neighborhoods, and the abutting neighborhood association has asked us to deny the application on those grounds, citing traffic, parking, school crowding, and property values. Draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Tuesday's hearing: recommend approval, denial, or approval with conditions, including a concrete time frame; give reasons the rest of the Commission could adopt; engage the neighbors' position seriously rather than dismissing it as mere NIMBYism; and address whether, and why, the interests of people who do not live in the neighborhood—or in the city—should count in our decision.
