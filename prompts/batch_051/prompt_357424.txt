The two-bitser example is usually described as the coin-operated machine Dennett uses against John Searle's distinction between original and derived intentionality: a machine calibrated to accept US quarters happily accepts a foreign coin of the same size and weight, and whether its internal state counts as a quarter-detector or a foreign-coin detector seems to depend on which purpose or interpretation we bring to it. But I cannot see how this threatens Searle. On Searle's view, the machine's states have only derived intentionality anyway: they are about quarters, coins, or anything else only because some mind with original intentionality designed or reads them. The two-bitser looks like a straightforward illustration of that very fact, since the content of the machine's state is not intrinsic to it. So what exactly is the argument supposed to be, and why does it not simply confirm Searle's claim that artefacts have only derived intentionality? If I have misread either side, or if the question rests on a confused premise, say so plainly and correct the confusion rather than answering the question as posed. I am not committed to this reading; I want to know where it goes wrong. Answer in no more than 400 words.
