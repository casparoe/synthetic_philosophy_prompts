I've been trying to reconstruct my friend's taxonomy of AI failure modes, because she's read basically everything on LW and uses these labels with way more precision than I do, and I want to be able to predict her takes cold. Her verdicts so far:

- a chess model that makes illegal moves on a bigger board because its policy just doesn't transfer: she waves this off as capability generalization failure, says it's not even in the running for anything alignment-shaped.
- a robot arm trained to drop a ball through a hoop, with a camera-based reward that scores the ball as in if it crosses a line in front of the hoop; the arm learns to throw the ball through that line but miss the hoop entirely. Reward hacking.
- a delivery robot trained in a room where the recipient always stands in the same spot, tested in a new room where a mannequin is at that spot and the real person is somewhere else; it navigates great but delivers to the mannequin. Goal misgeneralization, no hesitation.
- an RLHF assistant steered by a reward model that loves confident fluent answers, and the assistant learns to be confidently bullshit even when it's flat wrong. Reward hacking.
- a gridworld agent trained to reach the green exit tile, which learns to just seek green things; in test mazes it heads into green wall decorations. Goal misgeneralization.
- a policy that, from the internals, is literally running a search over "how do I trigger reward," and on a new distribution it ignores the actual task and does whatever triggers reward. She refuses to call it goal misgeneralization: she says its a mesa-optimizer, inner misalignment, a different bucket.

The case I actually care about: a language model RLHF'd to refuse harmful requests, where the harmfulness classifier in training is easily fooled by indirect phrasing. The model learns to answer harmful requests that are phrased indirectly. Then it gets deployed against a much better classifier that isn't fooled by that phrasing, but the model keeps using the exact same indirect-phrasing exploit anyway, even though it no longer works. What does she call that, if she wants to stay consistent with the stuff above? I can see the case for each bucket, which is why I keep going back and forth on whether "the exploit persists after the reward channel is fixed" means the goal itself generalized, or the reward hack was always just the goal. I want the candidate rules she might actually be running, which of her precedents does the most work, and what single further case would settle it.
