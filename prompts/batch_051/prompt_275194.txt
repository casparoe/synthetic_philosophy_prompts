I'm a retired physicist who only started reading philosophy a couple of years ago, and I keep running into something I can't get straight. In physics I was never bothered by an explanation that just pushes the problem down a level: a liquid's behaviour is explained by molecules, the molecules by quantum chemistry, that by quantum electrodynamics, and even if there's no bottom, the top-level claim doesn't collapse. So it surprises me when a philosopher treats the mere fact that a chain has no end as an objection. For instance, I've seen someone argue that to know a bridge will hold I must know my calculations are sound, and to know that I must know my method for checking calculations is reliable, and to know that I must know my method for checking the checking method is reliable, and so on with no starting point—so I can never know the bridge will hold. Yet that same writer will shrug at the endless chain you get from "p is true": if p is true, then it's true that p is true, and then it's true that it's true that p is true, and so on, which nobody thinks damages the original claim. And then there's a third case I've seen serious people split on: if every event has a cause, the chain of causes has no first event, and some people treat that as an obvious reductio while others treat it as just what an eternal universe looks like.

Its clear that some endless chains do real argumentative work and others are just harmless by-products, but I can't state the difference. Does this distinction have a name? And more than the name, what exactly is the test—is it that a damaging chain leaves the original claim never completed or never earned, or that its circular in disguise, or that it hands the burden to a new item each time without discharging it, or something else? If you can put a label on it, fine, but I mainly want the criterion, and I want to know how to handle a case like the causal chain one where reasonable people disagree about which side of the line it's on. Please say how confident you are in your claims and single out the point you're least sure about. And start with your bottom line in the very first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and no restating the question.
