I am a retired physicist — my working life was spent in statistical mechanics, with a later turn toward relativity — and I have only recently begun reading philosophy seriously. I have just worked through Book XI of Augustine's Confessions and would like help bringing it into contact with the conceptual vocabulary I actually think in.

You are to act as a professional philosopher who is also at home in physics. First, think through the two frameworks side by side and give a careful, brief reconstruction of what Augustine is doing in Book XI: his claims that the past no longer exists and the future does not yet exist, that the present alone is but has no extension, that past and future nonetheless exist in the mind through memory and expectation, and that time is measured in the mind and is what he calls a distension of the mind. Then restate that position, point by point, in my vocabulary: spacetime, worldlines, simultaneity, entropy, records, and measurement.

Finally, give me the residue report. Tell me which of Augustine's claims translate cleanly into that vocabulary, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart at all — I suspect the felt passage or flow of time and the special status of the present instant are the difficult cases, but do not assume my suspicion is right. Then judge whether the untranslatable residue is a defect or gap in the physicist's vocabulary, a sign that some of Augustine's claims were doing less clear work than they appear to, or some of each. Be honest about the fudges; do not smuggle eternalism into the translation at the outset and then declare that Augustine simply contradicts physics. Think carefully and answer in a structured way. I am not asking for references or scholarly apparatus, only for a close and fair translation attempt.
