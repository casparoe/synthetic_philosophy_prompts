I work in local-government transparency programs, mostly in small municipalities, and I keep tripping over a pattern I dont have a word for. Its not the envelope-of-cash bribe. A city councilor gets his nephew hired at the water utility and everyone involved is fine with it, including most constituents, because, as he sees it, a good uncle gives family a job; he would be insulted if you called it theft or bribery, and he's refused actual cash more than once. Then an international donor, or our own staff from the capital, flags it as corruption, and the councilor's side says we're forcing a Western standard and that back in our countries it's just networking when a board member's kid gets an internship. I've seen the same with a vendor giving a lavish wedding gift to a procurement officer's daughter: not hidden, almost performed, and treated by the family as generosity, even a mild obligation. The strange part to me is that the two sides agree on the facts but disagree about which standard of fairness is the right yardstick, and the word corrupt gets used as if it settles that argument, rather than naming its conclusion. Im looking for the term for that move or structure—not the practice, which I know is somewhere near clientelism or gift exchange, but the way the disagreement keeps being presented as a factual finding. I've come across essentially contested concept, but that seems to be about concepts that stay permanently disputable; here the accusation itself does the work of choosing a standard in advance. Does this have its own name? And what are the boundary features that would make even the local standard count a gift as a bribe?
