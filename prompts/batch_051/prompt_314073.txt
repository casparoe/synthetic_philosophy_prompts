I help run a fortnightly seminar for civil servants, journalists, and a few local politicians under the title 'Why people don't do what the rational-actor model predicts'. The next session is a forty-five-minute introduction to the paradox of voting: the fact that a narrowly instrumental calculation seems to say that the expected benefit of casting a ballot is vanishingly small relative to the cost of doing so, so the act of voting itself becomes a puzzle. The audience is bright and comfortably numerate, but most of them have never met expected utility, collective action, or public choice before, and several arrive with the stubborn intuition that their vote matters because elections are occasionally decided by a handful of votes. My aim is not to persuade them that voting is stupid, but to make them feel the force of the problem and then think clearly about what might dissolve it.

Please plan the session for me rather than writing the lecture. I want the order of ideas specified, with a note on what depends on what, so that the paradox seems to grow inescapably out of the audience's own assumptions rather than being imposed by a definition. What single worked example should carry the weight — a particular kind of election, a committee vote, a jury, something else — and what features must it have and lack? Which misconceptions should I expect, and where in the sequence should each be defused? I am thinking of things like the composition fallacy that because democracy would collapse if everyone abstained, my individual vote keeps democracy alive; the mistaken idea that one's vote only matters if the election would otherwise be tied; and the suspicion that 'voting is irrational' means 'voting is shameful, and turnout is a mistake'. Are there subtle but crucial distinctions I should build in, such as the difference between the rationality of voting at all and the rationality of becoming well-informed before voting, or between instrumental rationality and reasons of a different kind? What should I openly refuse to cover at this level, and what check for understanding would show me that the distinction has actually landed? Finally, please tell me how confident you are in the overall plan and in the specific dependency claims you make, and single out the point you are least sure of.
