I keep seeing hyperbolic discounting treated, in rationalist-adjacent writing, as if it were close to the same thing as weakness of will: you resolve at t1 not to stay up late, but at t2 the near-term reward of one more video looms disproportionately large, and you act against your earlier resolution. I want to get clear on what the model is actually claiming, because I suspect the classic notion of akrasia and the model are not picking out the same phenomenon.

As I understand it, classical akrasia is a synchronic failure: at the very moment of acting, the agent judges that some alternative would be better all things considered, and nevertheless does the worse thing. Hyperbolic discounting — in its standard quasi-hyperbolic (β-δ) form, with present-biased preferences that differ across time — seems to generate a diachronic conflict instead: the agent's preference ordering at t1 ranks one action highest, the ordering at t2 ranks another highest, and at each moment the agent simply does what they then most prefer. That looks less like acting against one's own better judgment and more like being a succession of time-slices whose judgments change.

Please explain, as carefully as you can, whether hyperbolic discounting should be understood as (a) a model of weakness of will, (b) a rival account that dissolves weakness of will into time-inconsistent preference reversal, (c) a redescription of the phenomenon that leaves the original explanatory question unanswered, or something else. In particular, spell out what would have to be true of an agent — about the relation between judgment and choice at a single time, about whether they endorse their later preferences, and about whether they anticipate the reversal (the sophisticated/naive distinction) — for the hyperbolic-discounting story to count as a genuine explanation of akrasia rather than a change of subject. And say what would settle, even in principle, whether the cake-eater at the moment of eating is acting against a current all-things-considered judgment or merely has a current preference that an earlier self would disown.
