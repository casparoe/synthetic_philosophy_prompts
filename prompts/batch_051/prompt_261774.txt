I don't have any real background in philosophy or physics, so maybe this is stupid, but I've been turning over an idea and I want somebody to check it honestly. A while back I saw one of those popular science videos about quantum mechanics, and it said that two electrons are not merely identical like two phones off the same assembly line. There's supposed to be no fact at all about which electron is which, even in principle. If you swap them, the world is exactly the same. That messed with my head, because in everyday life, what makes something "the same one" is mostly that you can keep track of it: you can watch it, point at it, put a sticker on it, follow the story of it. So I started thinking that whoever says quantum particles have identity is using a different meaning of "same" than I am.

Here is the distinction I made up. I call it trackable sameness versus checklist sameness. Trackable sameness is when there's a continuous thread connecting the thing now to the thing later, or a tag that would stay attached to it. Checklist sameness is when every feature on your list matches: same charge, same mass, same spin, same position range, whatever you can measure. In normal life these two usually go together, but you can separate them. If I come home and see two identical black umbrellas by the door, and I genuinely cant tell which one is mine, they're checklist-same but I've lost the thread. Now I think quantum particles are a built-in version of that. I've been calling it "anonymous identity": things that are checklist-same, with no possible thread, but we still talk about "the two electrons" as if there are two individual things. My worry is that this isnt really identity at all, just a count plus a list of properties.

Be straight with me: is this a real distinction or just a fancy way of saying I cant keep track? Does it already exist under some other name I wouldn't know? And where does it break? For instance, if an electron could somehow be tagged, would anonymous identity just collapse into the everyday kind, or would the physics have to be different in a deep way? I also suspect I'm smuggling in a billiard-ball picture of little round things, and the whole problem comes from that. Am I? Basically, I want to know whether "anonymous identity" earns its keep or whether it just renames confusion.
