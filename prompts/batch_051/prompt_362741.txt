Critics of natural selection have long pressed a circularity charge: fitness is characterized in terms of which organisms actually survive and reproduce, yet differences in survival and reproduction are then explained by differences in fitness. The standard modern reply, associated with the propensity interpretation of fitness, is that fitness is not actual reproductive success but a probabilistic disposition—a propensity to survive and reproduce in a specified range of environments. As a journalist who covers science and ideas, I have never quite understood why this counts as a solution rather than a postponement. If a propensity is merely a compact way of summarizing the survival and reproductive frequencies we observe, then the original circle is left intact; but if it is posited as something over and above those frequencies, then fitness ascriptions seem to become empirically untestable claims about a hidden quantity. Is there a genuine third possibility here, or does the propensity interpretation simply trade a tautology for an unfalsifiable construct?
