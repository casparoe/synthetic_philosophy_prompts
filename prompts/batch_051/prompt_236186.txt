Everyone runs Nozick's experience machine as a private, scripted fantasy: you specify the experiences, the machine feeds them to you, and the whole thing is curated to make you happy. I want to know whether his argument still goes through if the machine is changed in one specific way.

In this version, you don't enter alone. Several thousand other real people are plugged into the same run, and together you inhabit a single shared, causally self-contained world. The operators don't write scripts and can't touch anything after the run starts; they draw initial conditions at random, and then the world unfolds by the same fundamental laws as ours. Once inside, you have no memory of having plugged in, and you can't edit the world or yourself just by wanting to. The only guarantee is that the world won't be uniformly miserable; otherwise things can go badly, and the other people inside can help or disapoint you exactly as in ordinary life. Your actions in there have the same kind of causal effects on other real minds that actions have out here, and vice versa.

Nozick's original reasons for refusing the machine were something like: we want to actually do things, not merely have the experience of doing them; we want to actually be a certain kind of person; and we want to be in touch with a deeper, non-man-made reality. For this new machine, run through what hedonism, desire-satisfaction theory (read as actual-state: desires must really be fulfilled, not just felt to be), and objective-list theory each say about whether to plug in. Then say which of Nozick's three reasons still has force and which ones falter. Does this variant still divide the theories the way the original does, or does it push some of them into agreement with hedonism? If a non-hedonist now says to plug in, is she revising her theory or just finding that this new case falls on the other side of her principle? And if the rational answer is now 'plug in', would that mean experience was all that mattered after all, or is there a leftover part of well-being that even this machine cannot supply?

Also, please write the answer in American English.
