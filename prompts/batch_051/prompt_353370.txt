Hey — I'm a PhD student in global priorities research, and a small foundation I consult for is floating a grant-scoring change I'd like help stress-testing. Right now they score cause areas the usual way: importance × tractability × neglectedness, each 0–10, multiply. The proposed tweak is to keep importance and tractability as expert scores but make neglectedness purely mechanical: neglectedness = 10 − log10(1 + D), where D is total dollars per year already flowing to that cause area, capped so it stays in 0–10. So $0/year scores 10, $1M/year scores 4, $1B/year scores 1, and every extra 10× of funding costs a point. There argument is that the neglectedness score is the easiest one to game — every applicant just asserts their cause is neglected — so they want to remove the judgement call and make it a pure function of the funding ledger.

What I want to work through is how this actually plays out. Who games it and how, given the score is a pure function of a ledger? Do people start carving out artificially narrow cause areas? Refuse or return co-funding because it would tank there own neglectedness score? Does the portfolio just whipsaw between whatever is momentarily unfunded, round after round? And can you patch the obvious failure modes — caps, floors, reporting lags, an externally fixed taxonomy of cause areas, audits of the ledger — without quietly ending up back at subjective neglectedness estimates? I'd like a walk through the incentive distortions and which fixes are real.
