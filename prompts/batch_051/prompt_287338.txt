A friend of mine has spent the last month trying to get me and a few of our coworkers to sign something he calls the Pre-AGI Covenant. He's convinced AGI shows up within the next fifteen years and will either make everyone rich or leave most of us unemployable, and he says the only way to protect ourselves is to lock promises in now, while their cheap. The scheme, as he pitches it: you sign a contract saying that if an AGI is certified before 2040 (under a benchmark he is still arguing about), then (1) you won't accept any job, grant, investment, or licensing deal from the AGI's operator unless that operator makes the same offer available to every other covenant member in good standing, and (2) you'll pay ten percent of any AGI windfall you get in the first five years after certification into a shared pot, divided equally among members whose income hasn't risen above its pre-AGI level. To make it binding, each member posts a bond worth two percent of their current liquid assets, held by a board of trustees drawn from the members themselves, and that board also decides who counts as being in good standing. The bond is refunded with modest interest in 2040 if no AGI is certified, and it's forfeited to the pot if the board finds you breached the terms. The part that actually gave me pause: the contract also says that once an AGI exists, the AGI itself will be invited to audit compliance and act as the final judge in disputes about whether someone broke the rules, because the organizers want to show good faith to the thing they expect to be negotiating with.

I haven't signed. My friend says I'm being paranoid and that this is basically a union plus an insurance policy. I think it's stranger than that. Walk me through what the thing would actually do: who joins and who stays out, what it incentivizes among members, non-members, trustees, and the AGI operator, and where it gets gamed first. Are the failure modes fixable without turning it into a different institution? Please answer in American English.
