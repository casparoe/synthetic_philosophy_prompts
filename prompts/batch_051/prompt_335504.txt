I'm a high school student, and for a civics paper on where ordinary gift-giving shades into clientelism, I've been reading the published rulings of a municipal ethics board that my aunt used to chair. She kept careful records, and I want to predict how she would rule on a complaint the board is about to hear.

Her past rulings:

- A councillor accepted a holiday fruit basket worth about $35 from a neighbor whose driveway-permit application was pending. The gift was disclosed in the public log; the two families have exchanged similar holiday gifts for decades; the councillor later voted on the permit. Ruling: not corruption.
- A councillor accepted about $2,000 in monthly cash from a developer while the developer's rezoning request was pending, and a text message showed the councillor promising to see what he could do to help. Ruling: corruption.
- A mayor accepted concert tickets worth about $400 from a construction firm the day after signing the firm's city contract. The firm described them as a thank-you. Ruling: corruption.
- A records clerk accepted a box of chocolates from a citizen after completing a form the clerk was legally required to approve. The clerk had no discretion in the matter. Ruling: not corruption.
- A councillor accepted a small meal at an annual neighborhood festival from a community group that had a grant request on the next council agenda. The meal was a standing community custom open to every official, and the councillor later voted on the grant. Ruling: not corruption.

The new complaint: a councillor who was sworn in three weeks ago accepted a standing golf-club membership worth about $5,000 a year from a property developer. The developer has no application before the council right now but has bid on city contracts almost every year for a decade. The councillor has disclosed the membership and promised to recuse if anything involving the developer comes up. There is no evidence of any agreement.

If my aunt is consistent with her own earlier rulings, should she rule this corrupt or not? I am less interested in the label than in the rule that fits her track record. Identify the candidate rules that fit all five past rulings, such as an exchange-or-gratuity rule, an intent-to-cultivate-influence rule, or something else, and say what each predicts here. Then give your best prediction, and describe one further case whose outcome would cleanly separate the rules that are still live. Please lead with your predicted verdict in one sentence, and keep the whole answer under 500 words.
