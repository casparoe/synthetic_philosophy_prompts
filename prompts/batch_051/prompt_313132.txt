Over the past few months I have been helping to draft a safety case for a language model that will be given access to tools, and during that process I have slipped into a rule that I find myself both relying on and worrying about. I now treat any safety argument that presumes the system is aligned as though it contributes nothing. If a proposed safeguard would only keep us safe on the assumption that the model is in fact trying to help, then I classify it as context rather than protection. That includes curated training, written constitutions for the model, and checks of whether its stated goals sound good. I only credit measures whose protective value survives even if the model is secretly pursuing goals contrary to ours: monitoring, sandboxing, withholding access to irreversible actions, and keeping the system away from the means to cause large harm. When colleagues offer a layered defense that mixes alignment-based and control-based measures, I evaluate it purely by its control components and discount the alignment-based components essentially to zero.

My own justification is that the point of a safety case is robustness to the bad case: if there is genuine uncertainty about whether the system is misaligned, then a protection that depends on that uncertainty resolving in my favor is not a protection at all. But a colleague keeps objecting that this is one-sided pessimism, that I am smuggling in a high implicit probability of misalignment, and that I do not zero out comparable evidence in other high-stakes decisions. Is my rule defensible as an epistemic and practical policy, or is it a bias dressed up as rigor? Under what assumptions about the base rate of misalignment, the severity of the downside, and the reliability of signals that a model is aligned would it be correct to discount alignment-dependent arguments to zero rather than to some smaller non-zero weight? And is there a restatement of the rule that preserves the robustness while still taking whatever genuine evidence alignment-based reasoning might offer?
