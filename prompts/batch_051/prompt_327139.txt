I would like you to reason through a counterfactual policy regime. Beginning on January 1, 2026, the United States abolishes its estate and gift taxes and replaces them with the following arrangement. Every person may receive up to $500,000 (indexed to inflation) in unearned wealth transfers over a lifetime, counted across all donors combined — inheritances, gifts, trust distributions, direct payment of another person's major expenses, and transfers made to minors or to accounts for their benefit. Any transfer a person receives beyond that lifetime cap is taxed at 100%, so no individual can ever receive more than roughly half a million dollars in unearned transfers, no matter how many relatives or friends try to split the giving. Spousal transfers and transfers to registered charities are exempt, and earned income and investment returns on assets a person already owns are unaffected. Enforcement is assumed to be essentially perfect: no offshore vehicles, shell recipients, or in-kind evasions get through. The resulting revenue is returned to every legal adult resident as an equal, non-borrowable monthly cash dividend.

Give me your best argued prediction of how the United States looks in 2075, two generations in. I want a mechanism-based chain of reasoning, not a story. Address at least four things: (1) the concentration of wealth and the intergenerational persistence of income and education; (2) the behavior of wealthy families — saving, consumption, investment in operating businesses, family firms, and succession; (3) philanthropy, universities, and the nonprofit sector; and (4) the political equilibrium — how much of the regime survives and through which coalitions. For each, mark which predictions you are most confident in and which are fragile guesses. State explicitly which background assumptions about parental investment, human capital, social mobility, capital markets, and political behavior your predictions depend on, and what would have to be different for the policy to fail to equalize opportunity even roughly.
