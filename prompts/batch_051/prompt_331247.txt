For a class on personal identity, I want you to construct a case to spec. I need a scenario where Locke's memory criterion makes personal identity come out intransitive: stage A is the same person as stage B, B is the same person as C, but A is not the same person as C. At the same time, a neo-Lockean psychological continuity view that allows overlapping chains of memory has to say all three stages are the same person. Constraints: no brave-officer military stuff, no amnesia from injury, no sci-fi memory erasure, no teletransporters. The memory failures should be the kind of ordinary forgetting a real person could have—maybe context-dependent or state-dependent recall. After you give the scenario, do the check: identify the stages, lay out the actual memory links from each stage to earlier stages, show exactly why Locke's criterion gives the three pairwise verdicts it does, and why the continuity view says all three are the same. Then say which feature of the case is doing the work, what would happen if you removed it, and how a Lockean could attack the case without just denying that fogetting ever happens.
