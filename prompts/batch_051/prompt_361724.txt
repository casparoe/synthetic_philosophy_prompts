Genesis says, "And God saw everything that he had made, and behold, it was very good." Romans says "sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin." Here's the thing I keep tripping on: the standard evolutionary story has hundreds of millions of years of animals killing, starving, and dying of disease before any human existed, so before any sin was possible on the usual doctrine. Did the people who wrote those sentences even have a case like this in view? Walk me through the different readings: the plain words, the pint of calling creation good, what the authors would have said if somebody handed them the fossil record, and what a reasonable reader after Darwin should think. Is the real choice between "very good included predation and parasites" and "death wasn't supposed to be there and animal suffering is somehow our fault," or is there a third reading that actually holds?
