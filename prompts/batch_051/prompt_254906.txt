I find myself torn between a theory about probability and a standing intuition, and I want the conflict adjudicated rather than settled by fiat.

The theory is a form of frequentism about single-case probabilities. When I say that this patient has a 70 percent chance of surviving the operation, the claim ought to be made true by the frequency of survival in some reference class of relevantly similar cases. That is what gives probability-talk its objectivity: the claim is answerable to how things actually go in the world. The subjectivist alternative — on which the probability is at bottom a degree of belief — seems to surrender the idea that such claims are made true by the world at all.

The intuition is that the world does not itself select a reference class. The patient belongs to the class of people with this condition, the class of people with this condition at this age, the class treated at this hospital, the class matched on a dozen further variables, and so on; these classes yield different frequencies, and no further empirical fact appears to pick one of them out. The maximally specific class — people exactly like this patient — either contains only the patient or is empty, so it cannot underwrite an informative probability. The objectivity promised by the theory thus seems to depend on a choice of reference class that the facts alone do not fix.

Give me the strongest version of each side. On one side, show that the intuition is a demand for more determinacy than probability-talk needs: perhaps a probability claim is always elliptical for a specified reference class, and its objectivity consists in the frequency relation once the class is fixed, while the choice of class is a pragmatic or decision-theoretic matter rather than a defect in the metaphysics. On the other side, show that the intuition tracks a genuine defect: frequentism explains why the frequency in a chosen class is objective, but not why the single-case probability is, because the choice that does the real work is ungrounded. Then tell me which should yield — the intuition, debunked as a craving for a fact the world does not owe us; the theory, amended or abandoned; or neither, because there is a reading on which the two are compatible. I want to know what would justify each move and what each move costs.
