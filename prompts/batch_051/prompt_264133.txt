Imagine that starting tomorrow, every human on Earth develops something like pain asymbolia. The injury signals still get through: touch a hot pan and your hand still pulls back, your pulse jumps, and you can point to the spot and say "that's sharp, that's burning, its intense, it's in my left palm." But the feeling has no badness at all. It doesn't hurt, it isn't unpleasant, and it doesn't give you any urge to stop it or get away from it. You still know, from memory and from ordinary reasoning, that a burned hand is a damaged hand and that damaged bodies are bad for you. Before the change, a lot of timely medicine ran on pain being aversive enough to make people act; after the change, the information is all still there but nobody minds it. Nothing else changes. Whether people still feel fear about injury is itself something you should reason through, not assume.

Predict how the world actually shakes out over the next thirty years. I want reasoning about mechanisms, incentives, and constraints, not a story. Cover at least: medicine (when do people seek a doctor, what gets missed, what happens to chronic pain clinics), everyday risk and injury (do people start taking alot more risks, and does it matter that reflexes and deliberate reasoning still work?), how children learn to avoid dangerous things, law and punishment (what happens to pain-based punishment, torture, and damages for pain and suffering?), and how the language of pain ("I'm in pain," "this hurts," "I can't stand it") drifts over time. For each thread, say which predictions you're confident in and which are guesses, and be explicit about what assumption the shaky ones would flip on. End by taking a position on what this tells us about whether the badness of pain was in the tissue damage itself or in the felt unpleasantness.
