Origin essentialism, roughly, is the thesis that an organism could not have had a different origin: had a different sperm and egg fused, the resulting organism would not have been the same one, however similar in other respects. The thesis is usually defended by appeal to the intuition one feels when running that counterfactual. I am suspicious of this move: the intuition may be tracking nothing more than contingent facts about how reproduction actually works, may be an artifact of how the vignette is presented, or may be the surface symptom of a real, unified modal principle about identity. Without pretending we can inspect alternative possible worlds, how would you test the source and robustness of this intuition? Set out the strongest empirical investigation you can — including the cases or vignettes you would contrast, the manipulations you would introduce, and the measures you would take — and specify which results would count in favor of treating the intuition as a genuine, unified principle about origins, which would count in favor of debunking it, and exactly what such a study could never settle about origin essentialism itself.
