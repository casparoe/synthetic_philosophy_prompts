In the seminar I teach on population axiology, I keep noticing the same dialectical slide: we introduce the repugnant conclusion and the students recoil, then we move on to average and person-affecting views as though abandoning totalism had settled the matter. I have come to suspect this is too quick. I would like the strongest available defense of the claim that the repugnant conclusion is not an optional consequence of one controversial theory but a structural pressure internal to any axiology that permits even modest trade-offs between the quality and the number of lives. Make the argument as follows. Grant only that, somewhere in the chain, a sufficiently large increase in the number of lives can compensate for a small loss in average well-being. That single concession looks innocuous, but when applied iteratively it generates a series of worlds running from a small, excellent population down to a vast population of lives barely worth living, and the repugnance cannot be localized to any particular step: each step shares the very feature that made its predecessor acceptable. Then show why the familiar escapes fail to localize the mistake. A lexical threshold below which additional lives count for nothing, or nearly nothing, rejects the conclusion only by positing a discontinuity in value that the underlying trade-off gives us no reason to accept. An average view does not remove the trade-off; it reclassifies the addition of lives whose welfare is positive but below the existing average as a loss, thereby masking rather than answering the question why more positive lives should make a world worse. A person-affecting restriction either leaves itself unable to compare worlds at all, or ends up valuing possible people in a way that quietly reinstates the same trade-off. Please make the case in your own terms, without scaffolding it on named theorems, texts, or attributions. Close with an honest reckoning: say where in the argument you are least confident, and identify the consideration that would most likely change your verdict.
