'Epistemic closure is the principle that knowledge is closed under entailment: if S knows that P, and P entails Q, then S knows that Q. Nozick's tracking theory says knowledge is true belief that tracks the truth: S knows that P just in case (i) if P were false, S would not believe that P, and (ii) if P were true, S would believe that P. The conflict shows up with sceptical hypotheses. Let P be "I have hands" and Q be "I am not a brain in a vat." P entails Q, so closure says that since I know P, I should also know Q. But Nozick's two counterfactual conditions both fail for Q: if I were a brain in a vat being fed experiences of hands, I would still believe I was not a brain in a vat. The conditions both hold for P, so I know I have hands but not the entailed Q, which is the closure violation. Nozick bites the bullet and denies closure. He treats this as a feature rather than a bug, because it lets him keep ordinary perceptual knowledge while blocking the sceptic's inference from "you don't know you're not envatted" to "you don't know you have hands." I think the price is too high. Deduction is one of the most basic ways we extend knowledge, and if closure fails I could know the premises of a valid argument and know that the argument is valid, yet still be unable to know the conclusion, which seems absurd. Nozick doesn't really have an answer to that.'
