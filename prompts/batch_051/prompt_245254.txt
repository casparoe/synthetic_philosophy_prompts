I keep seeing "align AI with human values" tossed around like it's a solved spec, but the part nobody writes down is the aggregation layer: when the system acts for more than one person, whose values count and how do you combine them. I'm a backend engineer, not a philosopher, so I want the requirements before anyone writes code. Write the desiderata that a theory of value aggregation for aligned AI has to meet: the cases it has to handle, the distinctions it can't collapse, the results it can't contradict, with the consideration behind each one. Then mark which are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one camp.

The cases I mean include the obvious ones — people whose values conflict, people who'd be affected by the system but never asked for anything, people who don't exist yet — and also people who object to being aggregated at all, like their value is precisely that no single function should be run over everyone. The part I most want to see is whether some subset of the non-negotiables is jointly unsatisfiable — if Arrow's theorem or something like it means no aggregation function can hit all of them, say that straight out. I keep hearing "liberal neutrality" listed as a requirement too: is that a hard constraint or a contested add-on? And which single requirement does the most work ruling out the answers people actually build (majority vote, revealed preferences, "just ask the user")? Please seperate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who know this stuff, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
