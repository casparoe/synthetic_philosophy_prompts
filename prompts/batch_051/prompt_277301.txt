I have been trying to pin down exactly what Descartes's cogito establishes, and the following reconstruction is the best I can produce, but I keep finding that it either stops too early or runs too far. The argument runs:

(1) Doubting that I exist is an episode of thinking.
(2) I cannot be mistaken about whether I am thinking while I am thinking, because the thinking is present to me as the very act I am performing.
(3) Necessarily, if an episode of thinking occurs, then there is a subject that thinks.
(4) Therefore, in the very act of doubting whether I exist, I can be certain that there is a subject that thinks.
(5) That subject is me, since the episode in question is my doubting.
(6) So I can be certain that I exist as the subject of my own thinking.
(7) But the argument gives me no information about this subject except that it thinks.
(8) To be a subject of thought just is to be a thinking substance.
(9) Therefore I can be certain that I am a thinking substance.
(10) A thinking substance is a substance whose whole essence or principal attribute is thought, whereas a body is a substance whose essence is extension.
(11) One substance cannot have both thought and extension as its principal attribute.
(12) Therefore the cogito, carried through, shows that I am a substance really distinct from any body and that I could exist without a body.

I do not believe the argument really entitles anyone to (12), but I cannot say which step is the first to fail, or whether some step is equivocating. Is the argument valid? If not, exactly which step fails, and under what precise reading does it fail? And if the correct version stops earlier, which step is the strongest conclusion the cogito actually supports?
