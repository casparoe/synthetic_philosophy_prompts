I sit on the discipline committee of our state psychological association. A member in his mid-70s, retired and well regarded, admitted in an oral-history interview last month that he fabricated the control-group data for a 1987 article on test anxiety; the article was widely cited and shaped some school screening practices. Our bylaws require complaints to be brought within ten years of the conduct, but the committee may waive that limit in 'extraordinary circumstances,' and since he only confessed now the waiver question is basicaly the whole ballgame. Some members think four decades of decent professional life is itself a reason to let it go; others say time only matters because evidence decays and punishment loses its point. I need a short written opinion to circulate before Thursday's vote: recommend concretely whether to waive the time limit and whether to expel him or impose a lesser sanction, and engage the strongest argument on the other side seriously. End with a sentence or two on how confident you are in the recommendation and the point you're least sure about.
