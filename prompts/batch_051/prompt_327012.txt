im putting together a small reading group on the Zhuangzi and i want a pantoum for wu-wei. the form should do the work: a pantoum recycles its lines on a fixed schedule, each line coming back in a new spot with a slightly different force, which is kind of exactly the paradox. the trap is that you cant make yourself effortless, and worse, you cant even try not to try, because the trying-not-to is itself a trying. i want the poem to build through that regress: first trying to be natural, then trying to stop trying, then noticing the noticing, and maybe at the point where it gets most effortful the language should loosen up a little, so the syntax itself slips. use the line-repetition to flip meanings: the line that first appears as a command to let go should return sounding like a diagnosis of how it went wrong, or the other way around. if you can work in Zhuangzi's butcher without naming the story too clunkily, do it—his skill gets easy only when hes not performing ease. end in the pantoum's final recirculation without resolving it: the first and third lines come back at the end, and they should land like the problem restarting, not like a moral. no 'and then he let go.' It has to be good enough that someone who works on wu-wei would recognize the landscape, and someone who doesnt would still feel the loop.
