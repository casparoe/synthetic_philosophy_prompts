I am a PhD student in the philosophy of cognitive science, working on the relationship between mechanistic physiology and the ascription of consciousness to non-human animals. In T. H. Huxley’s essay “On the Hypothesis that Animals are Automata” (in *Science and Culture and Other Essays*), Huxley writes:

> But, in the seventeenth century, the idea that the physical processes of life are capable of being explained in the same way as other physical phenomena, and, therefore, that the living body is a mechanism, was proved to be true for certain classes of vital actions; and, having thus taken firm root in irrefragable fact, this conception has not only successfully repelled every assault which has been made upon it, but has steadily grown in force and extent of application, until it is now the expressed or implied fundamental proposition of the whole doctrine of scientific Physiology.

I keep returning to this passage because it captures something I find both compelling and slippery. I can see a reading on which it contains only a methodological commitment: physiology ought to explain the body’s operations as mechanisms, while remaining silent about whether any of those mechanical processes are accompanied by consciousness. But I can also see a stronger reading on which it commits one to the view that consciousness, if it exists at all, is a causally inert accompaniment of certain kinds of neural machinery—and that the distribution of such an accompaniment across species is a separate empirical question with no sharp boundary. The title of Huxley’s essay, of course, points toward the stronger reading, but I want to know whether the passage itself, taken seriously, actually forces it.

Write an essay on whether accepting Huxley’s claim as a methodological starting point should make us expand or contract the set of animals to which we attribute consciousness. Weigh the main arguments on both sides, but do not end on a non-committal note. Defend a verdict about which animals, if any, should be counted as conscious, and explain whether Huxley’s mechanistic starting point genuinely supports that verdict or merely leaves it open.
