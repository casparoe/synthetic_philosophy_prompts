One is a hard Peircean about future contingents: she holds that a proposition about a contingent future event (think Aristotle's sea battle from De Interpretatione) is neither true nor false until the world is, as she puts it, 'settled' enough to make it true. The other is an Ockhamist: he holds that 'there will be a sea battle tomorrow' is already true or already false now, even if we can't know which, and that giving up bivalence for future contingents is a confusion.
