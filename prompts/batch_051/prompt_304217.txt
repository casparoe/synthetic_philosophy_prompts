I got roped into chairing my condo board's annual meeting and we're stuck. It's a tiny building, so the board is just three owners: Alice, Bob, and me. We need to decide whether to go ahead with replacing the roof this year. The bylaws say we have to vote separately on two preliminary questions before the main vote: (1) whether the roof actually needs replacing soon, and (2) whether we can afford the work without a special assessment. Then we vote on the main question, the replacement itself.

On the first question, it was 2-1 that the roof does need replacing. On the second, it was 2-1 that we can afford it. But then the main vote, go ahead with the replacement, lost 2-1. Nobody was being shifty, and each of us is perfectly consistent. Alice voted no because she thinks the roof has a few years left, though she agrees we could afford it. Bob voted no because he thinks we can't afford it, though he agrees the roof is shot. I'm the only one who thinks both premises are true, and I was the lone yes vote on the replacement.

So a majority of the board accepted each premise, and a majority still rejected the conclusion that seems to follow. Our old president keeps saying the building has 'decided' not to replace the roof, but I honestly can't tell what the building's view even is. Is there a right way to run a vote like this? Should the main question just be settled by whatever the premises imply, or should we have voted on the replacement and never separated out the reasons? What's actually going on here, and what should I do at next months meeting?
