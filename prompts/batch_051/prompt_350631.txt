Here's a variant on the ring of Gyges I've been chewing on, and I'm trying to figure out wether Glaucon's challenge still works. In the usual setup from the Republic, the ring makes you invisible, so you can do whatever you want and nobody can pin it on you, and Glaucon's claim is that a just person and an unjust person with rings would act exactly the same, because being just is only worth having for the reputation and rewards it gets you.

Now change one thing. The ring still makes you invisible, but every time you put it on, a bell tolls that cant be faked, and it tolls again when you take it off. Everyone in the city knows what the tolls mean: someone was invisible during that stretch. The tolls don't say who it was, and nobody knows you have the ring unless you've told them. You can still do any particular deed without being seen, but now the whole city knows that invisible people exist and that one is active whenever you act.

What does this do to Glaucon's challenge? Specifically: would the just person and the unjust person still end up doing the same things? Does the later comparison — the completely just person who everyone thinks is unjust, versus the completely unjust person who everyone thinks is just — get easier or harder to set up? And does the conclusion survive, that justice is only valued for its consequences, or does the bell change what the thought experiment is even testing?
