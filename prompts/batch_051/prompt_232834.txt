Javert behaves like a pretty pure retributivist: wrongdoing incurs a debt that has to be paid, the law's demand is categorical, and mercy is basically a form of corruption or injustice. Then the bishop's kindness, Valjean letting Javert go, him dragging Marius out of the sewers, and so on, seem to appeal to something that is neither retribution nor deterrence. And Javert's suicide feels like the test case for whether either theory can actually handle what the novel is pointing at.
