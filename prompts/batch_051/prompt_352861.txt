I am a PhD student in philosophy of biology, working on the epistemology of welfare measurement in nonhuman animals, and I keep noticing what looks like the same methodological problem in two otherwise unrelated literatures. In psychometrics, construct validity treats attributes such as depression or anxiety as latent variables: they are not directly observed, and candidate indicators (questionnaire items, behavioral tasks, physiological readings) are validated by showing that they fit a nomological network, with convergent validity among independent measures and discriminant validity against putatively different constructs. In animal welfare science, welfare is similarly treated as a target state that cannot be read off any single measure, and investigators must validate candidate indicators (cortisol levels, gill ventilation, avoidance learning, preference tests, conditioned place avoidance) without a gold standard, especially in farmed fish and decapod crustaceans, where self-report is impossible and species differences are extreme.

At first the two problems looked structurally identical to me: in both, there is a target construct that is underdetermined by any single indicator, a set of proxies each carrying construct-irrelevant variance, and a validation procedure that seems circular because the construct is partly fixed by the very indicators being validated. But I am unsure whether this is a genuine identity of structure or only a loose analogy resting on the word latent.

Is the parallel deep or superficial? If it is deep, what exactly is the shared structure, and what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer? For instance, would psychometric tools such as multi-trait multi-method matrices, factor-analytic tests of measurement invariance, or the distinction between content, response-process, and consequence validity transfer to the aquaculture case, or do they presuppose features that fish and crustaceans cannot supply, such as stable verbal self-report or a shared human response process? And is there something this parallel predicts about welfare assessment that seems false—for example, that one could estimate discriminant validity between pain and fear in a fish from behavioral indicators alone, or that a welfare indicator could retain validity across species the way a depression scale can retain validity across cultures? I would like a structured appraisal of where the mapping holds and where it comes apart.
