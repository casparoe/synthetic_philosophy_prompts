I've been turning over something dumb and basic: our calendar. The year before 1 AD is 1 BC, wich means there's no year zero anywhere in there. The count just jumps from -1 straight to +1, like a ruler with a centimetre missing that nobody minds. For any other measurement we'd say that scale is broken, but for time we've all quietly agreed to it. And the switch from counting backward to counting forward is pinned to a birth that's probly misdated anyway, and most of us only keep it because we inherited it. What I keep wanting to know is whether anything actually justifies that setup. Time is the same smooth thing on both sides of the epoch, so why do we flip its sign there and skip zero? And if we could pick a year zero from scratch, what should it be — the Big Bang, the first writing, just relabeling 1 BC as 0 so the arithmetic stops lying, or something else? I'm not interested in the practical disruption answer as the first move. I want to know whether the current arrangement is defensible on the merits, and if not, what the right zero point would be.
