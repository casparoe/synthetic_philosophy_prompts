Taking a linguistics course on sign languages right now and I keep tripping over the same thing. When we read definitions of "language" or lists of properties that all human languages supposedly share, there's usually an item about the sound/speech channel — produced by the voice, heard by the ear, or the whole system being "vocal" — and sign languages clearly don't have that, yet nobody concludes sign languages aren't languages. I've watched people handle this by saying that vocal item is just typical, not essential, but then later they sort or explain other phenomena using that very item, so its doing real work while officially not existing. It doesn't feel identical to the medical view that Deaf people need fixing, or to the educational approach that makes deaf kids speak, but it's adjacent. Is there an established term for this exact tendency, making speech definitional of language while denying that's what you're doing? If there's only a near miss, say which neighboring terms fit least badly and where the boundary between them is — I care about the distinction being drawn sharply more than about the label.
