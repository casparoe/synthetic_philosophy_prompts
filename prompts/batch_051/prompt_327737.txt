My team just got forced to audit a hiring screener we built, and I've been going in circles about it. It struck me the other day that the legal distinction between disparate treatment and disparate impact is pretty much the same distinction as the one between killing and letting die. If someone literally hard-codes a penalty for applicants from certain zip codes into the model, the model is doing the harming, actively discriminating. But if we instead train it on ten years of promotion data that's already biased and it picks the pattern up on its own, that's more like letting harm happen: nobody wrote the rule, we just stood back and let the old pattern reproduce itself. I can't tell if this is a real identity of structure or just me pattern-matching on the word bias. The parallel feels like it should tell us which fixes are obligatory. For instance, if letting someone die can be exactly as bad as killing them when you have a special duty to keep them alive, then maybe letting your training data discriminate can be exactly as bad as hard-coding the discrimination in when you have a duty to applicants. But then again, choosing the training data and deploying the model are both things we do, so maybe the omission is only hiding somewhere upstream and I'm fooling myself. Is there anything deep here, or does the analogy fall apart on inspection? And if there is something to it, what would the parallel predict that turns out false?
