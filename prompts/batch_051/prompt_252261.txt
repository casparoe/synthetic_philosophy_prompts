I've got a development econ job talk next month and I want to prep the Q&A rather then script the talk. My claim is that the institutions-vs-geography/culture debate is underidentified: the settler-mortality and reversal-of-fortune results are reduced-form effects of a whole bundle of colonial conditions, and reading them as evidence for the institutions channel specifically just assumes the exclusion restriction that's at issue. My three moves: (1) the reduced form is consistent with disease-ecology and human-capital channels; (2) the complier group is weird and the external validity to today's postcolonial states is shaky; (3) the mechanism from colonial constraints on the executive to current productivity is left so vague that institutions ends up doing too much work. The audience is mixed: applied econometricians who live or die by identification, economic historians who think this natural-experiment business is ahistorical, and a couple geographers annoyed that geography only ever shows up as a control. Give me a Q&A prep sheet: the objections ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way these people actually talk, with a two-minute reply for each, what I should concede, what I should hold, where each reply is softest, and the question I should be praying nobody asks.
