Me and my friend got into a long argument last night about what an emotion actually is, and near the end I realized she's got this apperently very consistent track record of calls on border cases. From what I can remember:

- Fear when she came across a bear on a trail: emotion.
- Anger when she found out a close friend had lied about something important: emotion.
- Pride watching her kid graduate: emotion.
- The jolt of fear when something jumps out in a horror movie even though you know full well it's fake: emotion, she says.
- A startle reflex when someone claps right behind your head: not an emotion, just a reflex.
- An itch on your arm: not an emotion.
- Stubbing your toe and the sharp pain that follows: not an emotion, just a sensation.

Then she hit me with a case she said she'd never ruled on. A woman was badly betrayed years ago, but by now she's completely cooled off: no racing heart, no heat in her chest, no felt bodily agitation at all when she thinks about it. She'll tell you — and clearly believes — that she was wronged, and it still structures how she treats the guy: she's cold to him, avoids him, makes little digs. But she insists she doesn't 'feel' anything about it anymore. Would my friend call that an emotion?

I'm less interested in the bare yes/no than in the rule she's running. Would you put her down as a judgment/appraisal person, a feeling/James-Lange-ish person, or a perception person (emotions as fast perceptions of value, not necessarily explicit judgments)? Which of those are still live after her track record, what does each one predict about the resentful-but-numb case, and what single extra case would cleanly split whatever's left?
