Consider a simplified, text-rendered Bongard problem. An annotator is sorting small panels into two boxes, Left and Right. Each panel is an ordered triple of symbols from C, T, S, D, where C is a circle, T is a triangle, S is a square, and D is a diamond; so the string C T S means circle first, triangle second, square third.

You are given the annotator's rulings on eight training panels.

Left:
C T S
C D T
T C D
T S C

Right:
T T D
S D S
D D D
T S T

Now predict how the annotator will sort five new panels:
(a) C C T
(b) T S D
(c) C S D
(d) S S T
(e) C S T

Your response should do the following.

First, state as many plausible, reasonably simple rules as you can find that classify all eight training panels exactly. Use the sort of vocabulary a human shape-puzzle solver would use: presence or absence of a symbol, repetition of a symbol, the number of a symbol, or position. Avoid gerrymandered rules that merely enumerate the individual panels.

Second, for each candidate rule, state the label it predicts for each of panels (a) through (e).

Third, give your best-guess label for each of the five new panels. If you think the training data genuinely underdetermine which rule the annotator is using among two or more equally simple candidates, say so explicitly, explain what is being assumed under each way of breaking the tie, and then commit to a best guess with reasons. Include a note on whether the five new panels, once labeled, would distinguish the candidate rules from one another.

Fourth, propose one additional panel not already used that would do the most to settle which rule the annotator is running. Say which candidate rules remain live under each of its two possible labels, and indicate what further panel would then settle the matter.

Finally, in at most three sentences, draw the lesson for the broader problem of inducing a concept from a few examples: why eight labels can still fail to fix the concept, and what kind of bias (simplicity, salience, naturalness, or something else) must be supplied to break the tie.

End the response with exactly one final line of the form
\answer{XXXXX}
where the five characters are your best-guess labels, L or R, for panels (a) through (e) in order, with no spaces.
