I am a high school student writing a long essay on the philosophy of perception, and I have read enough to feel stuck rather than settled. On the one hand, disjunctivism seems to me the most compelling view because it takes perception at face value. When I see the cup on my desk, what I am aware of is the cup itself, not an inner image or sense-datum that could survive the cup's sudden removal. Seeing puts me in direct contact with the world, and that both avoids the familiar slide toward skepticism and explains how perception can give me knowledge of the very things in front of me. On the other hand, I cannot shake the intuition that drives the argument from illusion. A stick half in water looks bent; a person hallucinating a dagger can have an experience that seems, from the inside, exactly as a real dagger would seem. These bad cases can be subjectively indistinguishable from the corresponding good cases. And my intuition is that if two mental episodes are indistinguishable to the subject, they must be states of the same fundamental kind, with the same phenomenal character. Disjunctivism denies exactly that: it holds that the good case and the bad case are different kinds of state even when the subject cannot tell them apart.

I do not want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that is given up, what it would take for disjunctivism to be given up or amended, and whether there is a reading of "same kind" on which both survive. Specifically: is the move from "indiscriminable by the subject" to "same fundamental kind" a genuine inference or an unargued assumption? Does it matter that indistinguishability is a relation between two cases rather than a property that belongs to a single experience on its own? And could the dispute be at least partly dissolved by distinguishing senses of "same kind" — functional, neurophysiological, representational, fundamental — with disjunctivism conceding some and insisting only on the last?

If my way of setting up the problem is confused, tell me so plainly rather than complying anyway. Where you are uncertain or where the literature is divided, mark it as such; I would rather see the unsettled parts labelled unsettled than receive a falsely confident answer.
