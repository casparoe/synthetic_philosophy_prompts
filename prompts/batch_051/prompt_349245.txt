I keep running into the same kind of thing in my work and I've got no word for it. Example one: a model that screens clinical trial protocols and gives moral verdicts — "this consent form is exploitative because the compensation is coercive" — with a real explanation behind it, but it has no way to do anything about the trial. It can't reject the protocol, can't act on its own initiative; it just emits a judgment if asked. Example two: a hiring-model auditor that can say a decision rule is unfair and explain why, but its only output channel is text, and in some deployments no human ever reads it. I keep wanting to call these systems moral agents because the judgment part looks genuine, but that feels wrong because they can't act, can't omit, can't even decide to speak or stay silent. And "moral patient" isn't the right word either, since I'm not asking about what can be done to them. Is there a name for this in-between kind — a system that judges but cannot act? If there is, what exactly seperates it from a genuine moral agent and from a mere moral calculator, and if there isn't a settled name, what would you call it and where would you draw the lines? I don't want just the label; I want to know what has to be added or subtracted to move one of these across each boundary.
