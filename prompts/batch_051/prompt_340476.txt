The slogan was doing heavy work: it was meant to show both that aid is a defective kind of help and that rich countries could meet whatever they owe by trading instead. I cannot tell what precise claim I am being asked to accept. Give me exactly five distinct candidate readings of the slogan trade, not aid, ordered from mildest to most radical, and be careful to separate readings about development outcomes from readings about what wealthy states owe as a matter of justice. For each reading, state the precise thesis in your first sentence and classify it as empirical, conceptual, or normative; in your second sentence, assess how defensible that thesis is and say whether, if accepted, it would actually license my classmate's dismissal of the aid-obligation argument. Exactly five numbered items; each item one or two sentences; no introduction and no conclusion.
