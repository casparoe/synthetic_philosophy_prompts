I am a senior software engineer at a company that runs a large content-sharing platform. I have no formal background in philosophy or evolutionary biology, and I have been asked to produce the one document my colleagues should have produced after a year of arguing past each other. The engineers who build our recommendation system and the analysts who model how content spreads disagree about whether 'meme' names something real enough to build models on, or is a metaphor that does more rhetorical than predictive work. The first camp — call it the replicator camp — treats cultural variants such as caption formats, remix templates, challenge structures, and joke framings as literal replicators: they get copied with variation, compete for attention, and form lineages that evolutionary models can track. The second camp — call it the remix camp — holds that this is metaphor doing illicit work: every re-use edits, blends, and recontextualizes the item, so nothing is copied with the fidelity replication requires, and selection talk imports premises that do not hold for culture. I am not asking you to decide which camp is right; I am asking for the document that makes their disagreement exact. Please draft the joint statement both camps could sign. It should include: a numbered list of claims both camps accept, phrased so neither side would call them vacuous or slanted; a table of residual disagreements, each formulated so each camp would own the wording of its own position; a section identifying the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp — where each crux comes with a concrete case or dataset both camps agree would bear on it and each camp's prediction is recorded in advance; and a closing section stating plainly any part of the dispute you judge to be merely verbal, with rewritten formulations both camps could accept. In particular, pin down what each side means by replication, whether a lineage can survive low-fidelity copying and blending, what each side would need to see before abandoning its position, and whether any single result exists that both camps agree would actually move the debate.
