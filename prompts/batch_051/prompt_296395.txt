Compare Herodotus and Thucydides as two rival epistemologies of accounts about the past rather than just two historians. Herodotus often lays out conflicting reports side by side and occasionally says which he finds more believable, but often leaves them standing; Thucydides tends to reduce the conflicting reports to one cleaned-up narrative and presents that as what happened. Trace which elements of modern source criticism descend from each of them, and where modern practice parts company with both; I'm especially interested in what each would count as a good reason to believe a report.
