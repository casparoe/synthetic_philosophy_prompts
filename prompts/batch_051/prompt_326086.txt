I've been going in circles on the Orwellian vs. Stalinesque debate about the color phi illusion, and it has the shape of one of those arguments where both sides know each other's moves and nobody updates. Quick version: a red dot flashes, then a green dot flashes a bit to the side, and people report a single moving dot that changes color about halfway, which is strange because the green flash that would seem to cause the change hasn't happened yet. The Orwellian story is that conscious experience runs red the whole way and is then overwritten in memory once the green dot lands; the Stalinesque story is that the brain delays the whole show and presents an already edited version to consciousness. My null hypothesis is that this is mostly verbal, because "conscious experience" is doing two jobs at once—a first-person feel and a stage in an information-processing stream—and the real hidden disagreement is whether there is a single moment when something first "reaches consciousness" at all. Is that diagnosis right? If not, what kind of disagreement is it—substantive, verbal, different questions, differing standards of evidence—and what would count as progress, or is dissolving the question the only sane endpoint?
