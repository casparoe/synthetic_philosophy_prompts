I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, so I'll describe this in my own terms. I keep running into Kuhn's incommensurability thesis: after a scientific revolution, the old and new paradigms cannot be fully compared from a neutral standpoint, because core terms change meaning, observations are partly shaped by the paradigm, and there is no theory-independent observation language to act as an umpire. A strong reading says there is no shared standard of comparison at all; a weak reading says only that translation across paradigms is partial or lossy.

The development I'd like you to test this against is computational. In my field, distributional semantics learns vector representations of terms from their co-occurrence patterns in large text corpora, and alignment methods can recover correspondences between independently trained embedding spaces with little or no supervision, by exploiting shared structural regularities. Suppose I take the scientific literature from two adjacent paradigms -- classical mechanics and early relativity, for example -- build embeddings for each corpus, and let an alignment algorithm reconstruct correspondences between their vocabularies, including the observational terms that the two paradigms classify differently. The algorithm has no prior semantic knowledge and no bilingual glossary; it derives the mapping from distributional patterns alone.

Does the existence of this kind of algorithmic, corpus-driven alignment undercut Kuhn's incommensurability thesis? Or does it show only something weaker -- that a retrospective model can reconstruct a mapping that was not available to the scientists during the dispute, and that the algorithm may merely be smuggling in a common ground through its training objective or through shared structure that persists across the shift? I'd like you to identify which premise or version of the thesis this development bears on, say what the argument needs to be true in this setting, and give a verdict: does the incommensurability argument survive intact, need repair, or largely dissolve?
