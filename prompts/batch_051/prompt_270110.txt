Hey, I'm in this class on religion as a natural phenomenon and we just read William James's The Varieties of Religious Experience, specifically the part in Lecture I where he attacks something he calls medical materialism. I don't have the exact wording in front of me, but the gist of the paragraph is: if a religious state can be traced to a nervous condition, a brain lesion, or some physiological quirk, medical materialism thinks it has thereby discredited the state. His example is Saint Paul's vision on the road to Damascus being explained by an epileptic discharge in his occipital cortex. James's move is that origins dont settle value; you have to judge the experience by its fruits, not its causes.

But here's what's bugging me. In the rest of the course we've been looking at how the cognitive science of religion actually studies religious experience today. Researchers treat reports of mystical experiences as data to be explained by natural causes like hyperactive agency detection, temporal-lobe activity, or group bonding mechanisms. And they usually say theyre not in the business of judging whether the experiences are true or not, theyre just doing science. The course keeps insisting this is just methodological naturalism, not ontological naturalism, and that it presupposes nothing about whether God exists.

Ok but James's whole argument seems to be that you can explain the origin of a religious experience naturalistically and still be completely silent about its truth or value. Meanwhile the scientific practice seems to need the truth question to be irrelevant for its explanations to get off the ground. So what philosophical commitments does this practice actually presuppose? Does it just presuppose that religious experience has natural causes, or does it sneak in the stronger claim that religious experience is nothing but those natural causes? And if James is right about medical materialism, is there a way to do the science without smuggling in the verdict that the experience is false or worthless? Push back at me if I'm misreading James or if this whole question is a false dilemma.
