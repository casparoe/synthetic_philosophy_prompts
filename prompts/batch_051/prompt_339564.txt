You are a philosopher of computer science with a solid working knowledge of complexity theory—P and NP, NP-completeness, polynomial-time reducibility, worst-case versus average-case hardness, decision versus search versus approximation. I am asking you because I have become suspicious of a pattern I keep seeing in philosophy. It runs like this: some target competence or project is modelled as a computational problem; the problem is shown to be NP-hard; and it is concluded that the competence is impossible for bounded agents, or that a whole research programme (rational choice theory, belief updating, mechanism design, causal discovery, natural-language understanding) is in principle unworkable. Before I read one more argument of that shape, I want to know what an adequate account of the philosophical significance of complexity theory would actually have to get right.

Write me the desiderata. For each requirement, give the underlying technical or methodological consideration and say whether it is non-negotiable or merely the preference of a particular camp. In particular, make the account respect—and not collapse—these distinctions: decidability versus mere feasibility; the complexity of a problem versus the complexity of its typical instances; worst-case versus average-case hardness; exact versus approximate versus heuristic solution; asymptotic behavior versus what actual finite agents can do; and the hardness of some step in a procedure versus the claim that a bounded creature cannot be rational. I also want you to confront the fact that SAT is NP-complete while industrial SAT solvers handle huge instances, whereas some polynomial-time algorithms are hopeless in practice. I do not know whether this is a local curiosity or a deep problem for the whole inference from worst-case complexity to philosophical impossibility, and I want the desiderata to force that issue. Flag any subset of the requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Then name the single requirement you would use as a diagnostic when one of these complexity-based philosophical arguments shows up, and explain why it best separates genuine results from technical dressing-up of a nontechnical intuition. Take your time: first brainstorm the full list of candidate requirements, then whittle it down and present only the ones that survive. I do not want a literature review; I want your own specification.
