I run a general contracting business and I have no formal background in machine learning or philosophy. I do my thinking in terms of owners, general contractors, subcontractors, scopes of work, change orders, inspections, punch lists, and the difference between a crew that builds to spec and a crew that builds what the owner would actually be glad to have. Lately I've been trying to make sense of an AI-safety proposal I came across — I believe it is called iterated amplification and distillation — and it sounds a lot like how I would structure a job. As I understand the proposal: a human takes a hard task and breaks it into smaller pieces; a less capable AI does the pieces while the human checks each one; then a faster AI is trained to imitate the whole checked process; because that faster imitator is cheap, the human can use it to break the task into slightly harder or more numerous subtasks next time; and the cycle repeats. The defenders say that both competence and alignment survive this process because every layer of work was ultimately built on something a human checked. The skeptics say that checking the pieces is not the same as checking the whole, and that at scale you get a system that is extremely good at passing checks while quietly drifting from what you originally wanted.

I'd like you to restate that debate in the vocabulary of my work — which I'm fluent in — as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which claims have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. The part I keep getting stuck on is the distillation step. In my world, you cannot take one junior hire, show them ten thousand finished jobs with the inspectors' sign-offs, and turn them into a general contractor, because what got written down was the sign-off, not the judgement that produced it. I want to know whether my vocabulary is missing something important here, or whether the original debate is actually less crisp than it sounds on exactly this point.
