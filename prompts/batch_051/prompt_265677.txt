I'm a graduate teaching assistant, and next week I'm leading a fifty-minute discussion section for first-year undergraduates on Descartes's cogito, based on the first two Meditations. I don't need an explanation of the cogito for myself; I need a plan for how to explain it, and above all for how to get the students to see exactly how little it establishes. My fear is that they will leave with the impression that the cogito is a neat little argument proving that Descartes is an immaterial soul or a persisting self over time, when the real philosophical interest lies in the narrowness of the result and in the weak joint between finding that there is thinking going on and concluding that I exist as a thinking thing.

Please design the session arc rather than the explanation itself. What has to be put in place before the cogito can do its work — I have in mind the method of doubt, the evil demon, and the distinction between the order of knowing and the order of being, but revise that dependency structure if it is wrong — and which single example or scenario should carry the explanation, including what that example must have and what it must lack. Then map the misconceptions students will arrive with or form, in roughly the order they will form them, and specify the exact point in the sequence at which each should be headed off: for instance, that the cogito is a syllogism with a hidden premise, that it delivers a substance or a soul, that its certainty extends beyond the moment of thinking, and anything else you would expect. I also want one end-of-section question that would tell me whether a student has genuinely understood what the cogito proves rather than merely repeating the slogan.

Finally, say explicitly what I should refuse to cover at this level and why — whether that is the textual history of the formula, the substance metaphysics behind treating the I as a thing, or the Cartesian circle — and be candid about where this fifty-minute plan would be misleading if the students later read the rest of the work uncorrected. My priority is that they grasp why the certainty of the cogito may be more modest than it looks, not that they leave with the standard story.
