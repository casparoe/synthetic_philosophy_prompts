Here are the criteria I was taught for the kind of judicial review that raises the democratic objection. (1) The reviewers are judges: legally trained, appointed rather than elected, and protected by security of tenure. (2) They review legislation against a written constitution that is superior to ordinary statutes. (3) They act only in concrete disputes between adverse parties with a stake in the outcome, not by their own initiative or by abstract referral. (4) Their invalidation of a statute is final for ordinary political purposes: the legislature cannot overcome it by enacting the same law through ordinary procedures, and it persists until the constitution is amended or the court changes its mind.

Now consider a country that establishes the following institution, which I will call the Suspensive Citizens' Jury. The country has a written constitution, ratified by referendum, containing a bill of rights. Each year, sixty citizens are chosen by sortition to serve on the Jury; they need not be lawyers, and they may not be removed during their one-year term. Within one year of a statute's enactment, the statute may be referred to the Jury by a petition signed by two percent of the electorate or by any party represented in the legislature. The Jury must decide whether the statute violates the bill of rights. It receives written and oral submissions, deliberates, and may invalidate the statute by a three-fifths vote. If it does, the statute has no legal effect for four years. During those four years the legislature may re-enact the same statutory text by a fifty-five percent majority, in which case the re-enactment is immune from Jury review for ten years. If the legislature does not re-enact it, the invalidation becomes permanent after four years. Jurors are paid, serve one year, and are barred from outside communication about pending referrals.

Is the Suspensive Citizens' Jury a form of judicial review in the sense my four criteria are trying to track? Go through the criteria one by one, and be candid where the case is close or where the criteria underdetermine the answer. Then say whether the case exposes any of my criteria as less central than I took them to be. In particular, address this: is the democratic objection to unelected judges overruling legislatures really an objection to who does the overruling, or to the finality and insulation of the decision? Conclude by judging whether this institution deserves the same democratic suspicion as a conventional constitutional court, and whether it would be more or less legitimate from a democratic point of view.
