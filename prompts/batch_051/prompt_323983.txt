I'm not a politics person, just someone who fell down a youtube rabbit hole about first-past-the-post versus proportional representation and now ends up argueing with my brother in law about it at family dinners. We keep going in circles because we never pinned down what "fair representation" even means. He thinks first-past-the-post is fair because every district picks its own member and whoever ends up with the most seats won fair and square. I think it can't be right that a party gets 40% of the votes and 55% of the seats. But honestly I'm not sure why some vote-seat mismatches bother me and others don't.

So here's what I want: give me a working definition of "fair representation" in a national legislature, then run it through all these cases and tell me what it says about each one.

1. A party wins 40% of the vote and 40% of the seats. (I take this as clearly fair, or at least the obvious baseline.)
2. A party wins 40% of the vote and 55% of the seats under first-past-the-post. (I think this is clearly not fair.)
3. A party wins 6% of the vote nationwide and gets 0 seats because its voters are spread thinly across districts, while a different party with 6% concentrated in one region wins a seat or two. Is the first one somehow less fair than the second? I honestly don't know.
4. A party wins 0.4% of the vote and gets 0 seats under a list system with a 5% threshold. Should that count as unfair, or is 0.4% just not enough support for a seat anyway?
5. A party wins 20% of the vote and 20% of the seats under a proportional system, but only half of eligible adults voted at all, so it has about 10% support among the actual public. Should fairness be judged against the people who voted, or against everyone who could have?
6. National seat shares match national vote shares almost perfectly, but every member comes from a party list and nobody represents a particular local area. Is that fully fair representation, or is "representation" about more than matching numbers?
7. In a single-member district, the winner gets 38%, the runner-up gets 37%, and the rest split among smaller parties. The winner "represents" a district where 62% voted against them. Is the district-level result fair even if the national outcome ends up skewed?
8. The party seat totals match the party vote totals perfectly, but the legislature is 90% men in a country that's almost exactly half men and half women. Is that a failure of "fair representation" too, or is that a different meaning of "representation" that my definition should openly refuse to cover?

After you run the definition through them, tell me which case it handles least securely, and whether you'd push back on any of my own starting verdicts.
