I am caught between a commitment I want to keep and a case I cannot dismiss. The commitment is that rational preference is transitive, so a sequence of choices cannot be rationally licensed if it yields preferences that cycle back on themselves. The case is of the self-torturer kind. A dial runs through many settings, from a painless low setting to an agonizing high one. Each one-step advance pays the agent a modest, certain sum and adds only an imperceptible increment of pain. For every adjacent pair of settings, taking the next step seems to me rational: the payment is real and the added pain is below any threshold at which it could be a reason to refuse. I also cannot locate a sharp cutoff where one more step stops being rational, since any such cutoff would sit between two subjectively indistinguishable pain states. Yet after several hundred steps the agent is plainly worse off than at the start and would pay a large share of the accumulated money to return to the first setting. So I sincerely hold both that each step is rational and that the sequence as a whole is a mistake. I do not want to be told which side wins; I want to know what each possible resolution would require. Respond in exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Item 1 should state what it would take for the stepwise-rationality intuition to be debunked and dropped, and what that move would cost. Item 2 should state what it would take for the transitivity commitment to be amended or abandoned—for example by permitting rationally intransitive preferences or distinguishing local from global rationality—and what that move would cost. Item 3 should state what it would take for there to be a reading on which both convictions survive, for example one that treats the cutoff as vague or as present but unknowable, and where such a reading would locate the mistake, if any. Item 4 should identify the consideration or argument that would most powerfully discriminate among those three resolutions. Item 5 should give your own verdict on which resolution you would defend, and in one sentence why.
