I have been turning over a generalized version of the causal exclusion argument, and it seems to prove that mental events never cause anything—and then, by parity, that baseballs and prices never cause anything either. I can't locate the slide.

(1) Causal closure of the physical: every physical event has a complete physical cause.

(2) Non-reductionism: mental properties are not identical to physical properties. Non-reductive physicalists accept this, and dualists accept a stronger version of it.

(3) Exclusion: if a physical cause is already causally sufficient for an effect, then no property distinct from that cause is also a cause of the same effect, except by overdetermination.

(4) Anti-overdetermination: systematic overdetermination of every case of apparent mental causation is unacceptable; it would make mental properties causally redundant and their apparent efficacy a standing coincidence.

(5) Therefore mental properties never cause physical events. My decision to raise my arm does not cause my arm to rise.

(6) But the argument cannot stop at the mental. The window's shattering has a complete microphysical cause, and the baseball's being a baseball—its macroscopic features—is distinct from that microphysical cause. So by (3) and (4), the baseball does not shatter the window. Likewise, the action potential does not cause neurotransmitter release, and the rise in price does not cause the fall in demand.

(7) That conclusion is absurd. So the argument seems to prove far too much.
