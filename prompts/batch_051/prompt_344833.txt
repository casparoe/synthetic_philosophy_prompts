Suppose, while AGI doesn't exist yet, a group sets up a registry for pre-AGI bargains. To join, you pay a €2,000 fee and file a covenant: if you ever gain majority control of an AGI (the contract defines the trigger), you must assign 10% of the resulting benefits and board seats to a common pool, shared pro rata among all other registrants. In return you get the same claim over every other member. Shares in the pool are tradable, members can buy out their covenant early for a 12% fee, and an escrow deposit is forfeited if a member tries to dodge the assignment after a trigger has been called. I don't want to know whether this is a nice idea; I want to know how it would play out in practice: who signs up and who stays out, what happens to membership as AGI begins to look imminent, what the secondary market does to the scheme's original point, what gaming and legal exit routes appear, and which of those failure modes are fixable. And write the whole thing in British English.
