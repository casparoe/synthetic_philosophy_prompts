Ok, I've been going back and forth about weather hope is an epistemic vice or a moral virtue, and I keep ending up with an argument that it's both. That can't be right, but I can't locate the bad step. Here it is:

(1) Hope systematically steers the hopeful person's attention toward ways things could go well and away from ways things could go badly.

(2) An attitude that systematically steers attention away from possible bad outcomes will, over time, inflate the person's judgments of how likely the good outcome is.

(3) A disposition that tends to inflate probability judgments is an epistemic vice.

(4) So hope is an epistemic vice.

(5) But in the same cases, hope is what keeps the person from despair and sustains their efforts toward something that still has a real, small chance of coming about.

(6) A disposition that reliably sustains agency in the face of a real chance of good is a moral virtue.

(7) So hope is a moral virtue.

(8) Therefore hope is both an epistemic vice and a moral virtue.

But that's the conclusion I keep getting, and it feels like a trick. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails? And if it is valid, what should I do with the intuition that one disposition can't be both a vice and a virtue — is that suppressed premise the real culprit, or is that intuition just wrong?
