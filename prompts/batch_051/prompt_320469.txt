Okay here's a thing I keep running into. People in my linguistics/cogsci circle argue all the time about whether something is "innate," but I've started suspecting the word is doing different work each time, and its not clear we're arguing about the same property. Somebody says face perception is innate and means one thing, then someone says reading isn't innate and means another. Give me a working definition of innateness that gets these cases right:

- a newborn rooting for a nipple
- learning to walk upright
- recognizing faces
- being able to learn a human language at all
- learning to read
- being afraid of spiders and snakes
- the taboo against sex with a sibling you grew up with (even if you didn't know you were related)
- having a basic number sense for small quantities
- believing in gods or spirits

I think the rooting reflex and the language capacity are clearly innate, reading clearly isn't, and the rest I'm genuinely unsure about. My hunch is that "innate" is tangling together at least present-at-birth, genetically specified, and "not acquirable by general-purpose learning," and I'd like the definition to say which of these, if any, does the real work. Propose the definition, run it through all nine, and tell me where it forces a surprising verdict or where you'd need to split "innate" into two or three notions. And can you say whether the definition you end up with still supports the usual poverty-of-the-stimulus point about language, or whether that argument really only needs a weaker claim than the word "innate" suggests?
