Write an essay on the question of whether there is a rational course of action in the Death in Damascus problem. Set up the case: Death, who has never predicted incorrectly, tells the agent that it has already determined where the agent will be at midnight tomorrow, and that it will be waiting in that city to kill the agent; the agent knows all of this and can either stay in Damascus or flee to Aleppo. Weigh the main positions on what the agent should do: that the agent should simply choose one city, since the choice cannot causally affect where Death has gone; that neither pure act is ratifiable, because once the agent chooses one city that choice is evidence that Death is there, and the other city would then look better; and that the only response that escapes this instability is randomization or some related mixed strategy. Do not merely survey these positions. Defend a specific conclusion about what rationality requires here, and make explicit which premise your verdict depends on, such as what a rational decision procedure is supposed to deliver or what Death can condition its prediction on.
