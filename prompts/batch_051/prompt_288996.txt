Right, I've been trying to collect all the distinctive arguments for Kelly betting — the claim that when you face a long series of favourable bets with known edge, you should maximise expected log wealth (bet the Kelly fraction) rather than expected wealth, because your wealth compounds multiplicatively. So far I have four:

(1) The ruin/overbetting point: maximising expected wealth says to put the whole bankroll on any positive-edge bet, since the objective is linear in the fraction; but for a lot of such bets that prescribes a negative log-growth rate and drives wealth to zero almost surely, so plain expected value is the wrong target.
(2) The growth-rate theorem: for i.i.d. bets, (1/n) log W_n converges almost surely to E[log(1 + f X)], so the fraction that maximises expected log wealth just is the one that maximises the long-run exponential growth rate.
(3) Asymptotic dominance: any strategy with a strictly smaller log-growth rate than Kelly's ends up with wealth below the Kelly bettor's almost surely in the long run, so Kelly wins in a with-probability-one, eventually sense.
(4) The median/typical-path argument: for multiplicative wealth the arithmetic mean of W_n is inflated by a few astronomically lucky histories, and Kelly maximises the median, i.e., the typical realised path, which is the right centre for something that compounds.

I've a nagging feeling I'm missing a fifth argument that isn't just a rephrasing of one of these. Give me one more argument for the same conclusion that's genuinely different — not a rewording, special case, or combination of the four above. Then say honestly whether it's any good, in particular whether it actually gets you all the way to full Kelly or only to something weaker like 'don't overbet' or 'bet some positive fraction'.
