Please grade the following student answer to an undergraduate metaphysics exam question. The question on the exam was:

"Reid's 'brave officer' objection is often read as a problem about transitivity for Locke's memory criterion of personal identity. Reconstruct the objection in your own words, identify the formal feature of identity on which it turns, and evaluate whether the objection refutes the memory criterion as such or only a simple formulation of it."

The student answered as follows:

"Locke's criterion says that a later person is the same person as an earlier person just in case the later person can remember at least some of the earlier person's experiences. In Reid's example there are three stages: the boy who is flogged for stealing apples; the young officer who remembers the flogging; and the old general who remembers capturing the standard as an officer but cannot remember the flogging. According to the criterion, the officer is the same person as the boy, and the general is the same person as the officer. Since identity is transitive, the general must be the same person as the boy. But the criterion says they are not the same person, because the general does not remember the flogging. So the criterion conflicts with the transitivity of identity.

The objection turns on the fact that identity is an equivalence relation and therefore transitive, whereas memory is not transitive: the general remembers an episode in the officer's life, and the officer's remembering that episode coincided with his remembering the boy's flogging, but the general need not, and does not, remember the flogging itself. Since a non-transitive relation cannot serve as the criterion of a transitive relation, memory cannot be what personal identity consists in. I think the objection refutes the memory criterion as such, not merely a simple formulation of it. The only available repair would be to define personal identity through overlapping chains of memory, so that the general is linked to the boy through the officer. But that repair is circular: the links in the chain are episodes in an individual's mental life, and joining two episodes into a single chain already presupposes which person those episodes belong to."
