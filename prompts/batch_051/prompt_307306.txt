I'm a PhD student working on diachronic rationality, and I've ended up with a set of commitments that I keep reaching for in different bits of the thesis without ever actually checking whether they hang togehter. It'd be useful to get an audit before my supervisor notices. Roughly stated, they're:

(1) I think the money-pump argument against cyclic preferences is basically sound: if your pairwise preferences form a cycle, that's a defect in the preferences themselves, not just evidence that you'd be bad at dealing with bookies.

(2) But I also think the usual reply has something to it: a canny agent who can see the whole sequence coming is rationally allowed to refuse the trade that would close the loop, so the literal pump rarely goes through against someone with full information. I don't think that's just a technicality.

(3) I think intransitive indifference is sometimes rationally fine. If A is indistinguishable from B, and B from C, but A and C are discriminable, then an agent who is indifferent between A and B, indifferent between B and C, and strictly prefers A to C doesn't strike me as irrational for that alone. I don't think the money-pump argument should be allowed to rule those cases out by fiat.

(4) I think the right general response to a foreseeable change in your own preferences is sophisticated choice: treat your future self's predictable preferences as constraints within which to optimise, rather than optimistically assuming you'll remain the person you are now.

(5) But I also think that at least sometimes it's rational to be resolute — to stick with a present plan when you later prefer to deviate, even when this means not acting as a sophisticated chooser would — and I don't think that's just sophistication in disguise.

(6) And I think a future self whose values you currently regard as mistaken has no automatic authority over your present choices. The fact that it's 'you' later doesn't by itself give that self a claim on what you do now.

Are these six jointly tenable? Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, how bad is each tension, and what's the cheapest fix? I'd like a ranking by severity rather than a flat list, and I'd rather not be told that everything's fine or that none of it is.
