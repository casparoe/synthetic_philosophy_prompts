I am a high-school student in my school's effective-altruism club. Our group keeps getting stuck on how to apply the importance, tractability, and neglectedness (ITN) framework when the three factors point in different directions, and I want a single worked case that tests whether we actually let tractability reverse a ranking when importance and neglectedness point the other way. I would like you to construct that case. It must involve two realistic candidate causes, A and B, in a community, health, or education setting. A must be clearly more important and more neglected than B, while B must be more tractable than A, with the tractability gap large enough that a careful ITN analysis ranks B above A. The low tractability of A must not come from A's being obviously hopeless, corrupt, or badly managed; it should come from a subtler structural feature, such as steeply diminishing returns, a bottleneck, or a feedback problem. The verdict should be generated by the three factors rather than stipulated. Act as a professional cause-prioritization researcher: before settling on the final scenario, brainstorm at least two candidate mechanisms, and check that the reversal follows from the framework itself. Write your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the scenario; (2) what importance and neglectedness say and why they favor A; (3) what tractability says and why it reverses the ranking in favor of B; (4) the one feature of the case doing the real work, and what changes if that feature is removed; (5) the strongest objection a defender of A would raise against your tractability analysis, and the reply to it.
