I am a novelist, and I am drafting a book built around a master weaver whose relationship to her loom I want to render from the inside. In background reading I keep circling the phenomenology of skill — the idea of the lived body, Merleau-Ponty's notion of motor intentionality, the thought that a craftsperson's hands know the work in a way her deliberate mind does not — and I want the novel to use this idea precisely rather than as mood. So I have tried to reconstruct the position as a compact argument, and I would like a professional philosopher to tell me where, if anywhere, it goes wrong.

The argument runs: (1) A skilled weaver's hands respond to snags, uneven tension, and slight variations in the thread faster than any step-by-step conscious plan could revise itself. (2) If her movements were guided by representations of each adjustment, those representations would need to keep pace with this fast, fine-grained correction. (3) No representation is formed and revised fast enough to keep pace at the limit; the corrections outrun representation altogether. (4) Yet the weaver's corrections are not reflexes or blind mechanisms, because they are directed toward the finished cloth — and that directedness is what I understand Merleau-Ponty to mean by motor intentionality. (5) Therefore motor intentionality is a genuine form of goal-directedness that does not proceed by representational guidance, and any theory of action that treats representation as the only source of goal-directedness is false.

I find the conclusion congenial, which is exactly why I distrust it. Please answer as a philosopher of mind with sympathy for phenomenology; before you commit, work through the strongest representationalist reply rather than the weakest. For each of the five steps, say in a sentence or two whether that step is true or the inference is valid, and where a step fails say what the corrected version would assert. Give the entire answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, one item per step, with no introduction and no closing paragraph. For each item, also indicate whether what you say is settled or genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested reading as if it were the consensus.
