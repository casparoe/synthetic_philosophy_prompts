I'm a retired chemist, got into philopsophy a few years ago mostly via philosophy of mind and Freud's psychology, and I recently read a short piece (a blog or maybe an editor's afterword) that summarized Freud's view of religion like this: "Freud thought religion is basically a collective neurosis: religious ideas, like the obsessions of his patients, come from wishes rather than from reasoning about evidence. The wish at the bottom of religion is the longing for a protective father; as children we are helpless in the face of a dangerous world, and we project that infantile dependence onto a cosmic father—God—who we imagine will watch over us and put things right. Religious ritual is a kind of obsessive-compulsive behavior for fending off anxiety. And because psychoanalysis can show that the belief is caused by the wish rather than by evidence, it can also show that religious claims are almost certainly false; once scientific education spreads and people stop being so childlike, religion will be outgrown and disappear." I get the broad strokes, but the leap from 'religion comes from wishes' to 'religion is false' strikes me as possibly a genetic fallacy, and I doubt Freud was that confident predicting religion's demise. Could you go through it claim by claim and tell me which parts are a fair rendering of Freud's actual position, which he'd push back on, and whether the final conclusion survives once you separate the accurate parts from the sloppy ones?
