I work as an interpretive planner at an archaeology museum that is rewriting its permanent galleries on three regions: the eastern Mediterranean Late Bronze Age, the Classic Maya lowlands, and Rapa Nui. My drafts have repeatedly described these societies as having "collapsed," and the label review committee — which includes descendant community representatives and historians who are not archaeologists — has pushed back sharply. Some members argue that "collapse" smuggles in the assumption that a civilization fails when its palaces, writing, or monumental construction cease, and that what happened in many of these cases is better described as reorganization: populations moved, elites lost power, and lifeways changed, but communities continued. Others reply that refusing the word is euphemistic and erases real, abrupt, and destructive episodes. Several dozen labels are now in dispute, I have roughly twenty minutes per label to make a defensible decision, and the committee must sign off on every word. I cannot hand reviewers a bibliography, and most of them will not read one anyway.

What I need is a reusable procedure — a checklist or an ordered set of tests — for deciding whether a given case in a museum label should be called a "collapse," a "reorganization" or "transformation," or something qualified such as "partial collapse" or "collapse of the palace system." The procedure should be usable by someone who has not followed the scholarly collapse literature, and it should make plain which distinctions from the debate are doing the real work — for example, demographic continuity or decline, administrative simplification, loss of literacy, abruptness in the archaeological record, and whether ordinary people's lives got materially worse — while marking the points where judgment cannot be eliminated. Please apply the procedure to the three galleries: the end of the Mycenaean palace system, the Classic Maya political collapse, and Rapa Nui. Include at least one application that gives an answer I will not like, such as requiring a gallery I wanted to frame around "collapse" to be framed around transformation instead. Finally, say plainly what the procedure cannot capture: which parts of the collapse debate would remain unsettled no matter how careful the checklist is.
