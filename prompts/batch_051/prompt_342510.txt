I am a retired analytical chemist, and I have recently begun reading epistemology. What some epistemologists call the bootstrapping problem now troubles me, because it seems to imply that the way I trusted my instruments for forty years was circular: I used the instrument to check itself and then treated the resulting record as independent support for the instrument. I have tried to reconstruct my actual practice as an explicit argument, and I cannot find the flaw.

(1) A certified buffer has a known pH that is fixed independently of the particular meter under test, through a chain of certification that does not involve that meter.
(2) This morning my pH meter, placed in a buffer certified as 7.00, reads 7.01.
(3) Since the certified value is known independently of this meter and the reading lies within the stated tolerance, I know that the meter reported correctly on this occasion.
(4) I know that the meter has reported correctly on thousands of such buffer checks over the years.
(5) Therefore I am justified in believing that the meter is reliable.
(6) If I am justified in believing that an instrument is reliable, and the instrument reports a value for an unknown sample, then I am justified in believing that the sample has that value, within the stated uncertainty.
(7) The meter reports that an unknown sample has pH 7.24.
(8) Therefore I am justified in believing that the sample has pH 7.24, and I need no independent measurement of the sample.

I suspect an epistemologist will object to (1): the independence is only local, because the certified buffer's value ultimately rests on other people's measurement practices of the very kind the argument is meant to underwrite. But local independence may be all that a test of one instrument requires. Is the argument valid? If it is not, identify the first step that fails, and state the corrected version that would go through. If it is valid and sound, then explain why it is not the vicious circle the bootstrapping problem describes, and say whether the local/global distinction does the work or whether the argument should be rejected on some other ground.
