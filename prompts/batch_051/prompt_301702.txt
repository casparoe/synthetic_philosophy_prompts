Take the Mary's room setup and change just one thing: Mary isn't raised with no color experience at all. From birth she sees exactly two chromatic surfaces, a pure red one and a pure yellow one, always under the same neutral light. She has all the physical knowledge the original thought experiment gives her—photoreceptors, reflectances, neural coding, how color constancy works, the lot—and she knows as one of those physical facts that orange is a binary hue, the kind observers report as looking reddish and yellowish at once. But she has never seen orange, and she has no way to generate it in her room.

On release day she is handed a ripe orange under that same neutral light and looks at it. Does she learn something new, in the sense the knowledge argument cares about?

Work through both sides. What should a proponent of the knowledge argument say: does this show there is still a non-physical fact she acquires, even though she already has red and yellow qualia on board? And what should an ability or acquaintance physicalist say: is this version an easier out for them than standard black-and-white Mary, because Mary already has the ingredients, or is it actually harder because the new experience is so close to what she already has? Finally say what the variant reveals about whether the standard argument secretly depends on Mary having zero color qualia rather than merely missing a specific one. I keep flip flopping on this and want a genuine worked-through analysis, not a survey.
