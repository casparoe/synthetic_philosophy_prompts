Me and my roommate keep having the same argument about why "try harder" is great advice for some things and the worst possible advice for others, and I'm starting to think I don't actually know what I mean by "trying." I made a list of cases I can't unify:

- Lifting a suitcase that's too heavy for me: I try, and the trying is what gets it off the ground.
- Holding my breath underwater: same thing, pure trying, no paradox.
- Blinking while I type this: clearly not trying, it just happens.
- Trying to fall asleep: I try to relax and slow down, and the trying itself seems to keep me awake.
- Trying to remember a name on the tip of my tongue: the harder I strain, the more it retreats, then it surfaces when I give up.
- Trying to be charming at a party: the effort shows and it backfires; the people who actually are charming don't seem to be trying.
- Trying not to think about something upsetting: the thought comes back every time I push at it.
- Trying to float during a swim lesson: the kids who tried hardest to relax into it sank; the limp ones floated.
- Trying to be spontaneous on a weekend trip: if I plan it in advance it stops being spontaneous, and I'm not even sure this one counts as trying.

What I want is a working definition of "trying" that handles all of these—one that explains why the same word covers the suitcase kind and the sleep/charm/float kind, and what's different about the cases where trying backfires. Is there a single definition, or are we using one word for two different mental operations? Run your definition through the list, tell me which case you're least confident about, and, if you can, say what the old Daoist idea of wu-wei (acting without forcing) amounts to on your account—is it just not trying, or something else?
