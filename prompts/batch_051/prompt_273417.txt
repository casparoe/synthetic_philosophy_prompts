I've got to write a short statement of my position for a discussion group on whether some values are really intrinsic or just useful, and I'd rather have the cracks pointed out to me now than in the room. Here's roughly where I've landed.

1. I think truth is worth having for its own sake. A world where people hold more true beliefs and fewer false ones is, other things equal, a better world, even if the difference never shows up in anyone's happiness, plans, or behaviour.

2. I think belief ought to answer to evidence. If the evidence supports p, you should believe p; if it supports not-p, you should give p up, regardless of whether believing p would make you more effective or more content.

3. But I don't think we have a standing duty to seek every available truth. Someone who decides not to read a partner's messages, or not to take a predictive genetic test for a disease they can't do anything about, needn't be irrational or cowardly.

4. I think that a practice's having been kept alive across generations is itself a reason to continue it, over and above whatever good the practice does in the present. If a festival has been held in the same way for three centuries, I take that continuity to count in its favour, not just as nostalgia or risk-aversion.

5. But when I'm actually deciding whether to keep a communal practice going, I want a cost-benefit analysis of its present effects and the alternatives, and I find myself treating 'we've always done it' as the kind of thing people say when they don't want to do the sums. Its age feels important to me, and yet in the meeting I can't see where it enters the spreadsheet.

6. I also trust, at least weakly, my sense of contamination and desecration. When I feel that some object or place or act has been spoiled by its history in a way no amount of current usefulness could undo, I take that feeling to be pointing at a real feature of the world, not just at my own stomach.

7. But I also accept that disgust is partly an evolved hygiene response that misfires in moral contexts, and that it has been used to stigmatise harmless people. So I shouldn't give it much weight; I just find I don't want to give it none.

I recognise the list is a bit of a jumble. Could you go through these as a set and audit them, rather than defend them? I'd like to know which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which pairs only look as if they conflict, and for the real tensions what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole thing cohere. Please write in American English, and no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — continuous prose only, like a letter.
