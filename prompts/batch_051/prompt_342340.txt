Quick setup so we're on the same page. AI safety via debate is the idea that you have two AI systems argue opposite sides of a question in front of a human judge, and the judge only has to check the individual moves rather than understand the whole case, a bit like a tutor who can mark each line of a proof even if they couldn't have written the proof themselves. By an obfuscated argument I mean one where the argument as a whole is too tangled for the judge to verify directly, but each individual step looks fine.

Here's the argument.

(1) In a debate, the judge can directly assess only individual steps: whether a premise is true, whether one claim follows from another, whether an objection is relevant, and so on. The judge never directly assesses whether the whole argument is sound.

(2) For any question complex enough that we'd want debate in the first place, there will be an obfuscated argument for the true answer. And for at least some false answers, there will be an obfuscated argument that passes the same individual checks, because the false argument can copy most of the true one and hide its error in a single step chosen to look just like the corresponding true step.

(3) Since the judge's only evidence is which individual checks get passed, and since the true and false arguments pass the same individual checks, the judge has exactly the same evidence whether the conclusion is true or false.

(4) If a judge has exactly the same evidence for p and for not-p, then the judge's verdict cannot be tracking the truth; it's no better than a guess.

(5) Therefore, in obfuscated cases, debate cannot turn step-by-step checking into knowledge of the conclusion.

(6) But the obfuscated cases are exactly the hard cases that debate was introduced to handle.

(7) So AI safety via debate has a fundamental limit: it can't extend human oversight to the questions where the arguments are too complex for a judge to verify whole.

I keep thinking the trouble is somewhere around (3), but maybe it's that (2) is smuggling in an empirical claim that false answers will always have barely-obfuscated counterparts. Or maybe the argument is definately sound and I should just accept the conclusion. If it is sound, tell me what it actually commits me to and whether that's really fatal to debate. If a step fails, tell me which one, what the wrong move in it is called (equivocation, scope shift, local-to-global, whatever), and what the corrected step would say.
