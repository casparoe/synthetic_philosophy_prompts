I'm in a course on well-being, and I keep tripping over a pattern I don't have a word for. When I try to say what all pleasures have in common, I come up empty. The relief of finally remembering a name you'd forgotten feels nothing like the first bite of food when you're starving, and neither feels like finishing a hard run or the swell when your team scores. Yet I call all of them "pleasures" without hesitating. The only things that seem to unite them are that I want to keep them going and that I happen to call them pleasant—and that second one feels circular. Does this problem have a recognized name in the literature on pleasure, or is it just treated as a generic objection to the view that pleasure is one kind of thing? And please don't just hand me a label if there is one: I want to know exactly what the phenomenon is supposed to show, whether it actually threatens the idea that pleasure is a single felt quality, and how someone who thinks pleasure is a single kind of thing should reply. Commit to an answer—either "yes, it's called X, and here's why the objection does or doesn't work" or "no, there's no settled name, and here's what I'd call it and why"—rather than laying out three options and leaving me to pick.
