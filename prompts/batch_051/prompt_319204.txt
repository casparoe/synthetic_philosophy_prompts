Please compare two rival answers to the question of what a musical work is. On the first view, a work is an abstract sound-structure — typically the structure fixed by a score — which performances instantiate, with recordings treated as records of such instantiations. On the second view, for much of the music of the recording era, the primary work is the finished studio artifact itself, so that a cover, live version, or remix is not another instance of the same work but a distinct work, or a transformation of and commentary on it. Examine where the two views genuinely conflict and where they are better understood as accounts of different musical practices rather than rival ontologies of one thing. Consider what each view says about identity conditions (what makes two performances, or two recordings, instances of the same work), and what each view handles poorly — for example, improvisatory traditions, live realizations of studio-built works, edits and remasters, and works that exist only as recordings. End with a final line beginning "Summary:" followed by a single sentence that captures the comparison.
