I have spent the past year trying to settle a question that keeps surfacing in my work on randomness testing, and I have come to suspect the field has never actually settled it: can a finite sequence be random, or is randomness a property that only infinite sequences, generating processes, or decision procedures can have? The question became personally pressing when I was asked to evaluate a fifty-bit seed produced by a hardware random-number generator; I consulted three colleagues who work in adjacent fields, and they gave me entirely different criteria. One held that no finite string can be random in any strict sense because the notion of withstanding arbitrary place-selection rules makes sense only in the limit; a finite string can at most look random, and what looks random depends on which tests one chooses. Another held that a finite string can be more or less random in a well-defined sense through algorithmic complexity: if the shortest program that outputs the string is roughly as long as the string itself, then the string is incompressible and therefore random enough, so the real question is what threshold of deficiency one accepts. The third insisted that randomness is not a property of the string at all but of the source that produced it; the same fifty bits could be random from one device and not from another, because what matters is the process, not the output.

I tried to referee their disagreement by asking what would count as a test of one answer against the other, and the conversation stalled: each camp could restate its own criterion, but no one could say what observation or formal result would force them to change their mind. That makes me wonder whether the debate is really about a hidden factual question, about what the word random ought to pick out, or about which practical problem, cryptographic unpredictability, statistical inference, or mathematical definability, gets to define the term. My working hypothesis is that the three positions are not disagreeing about the formal results; they are answering different questions under one heading and, in particular, disagreeing about whether the object of assessment should be a string, a sequence, or a process. I would like a diagnosis rather than a survey: what kind of disagreement is this, and is there any distinction, experiment, or formal result that could genuinely end it, or should we expect no progress because the question is ill-posed as long as the word random remains underspecified?
