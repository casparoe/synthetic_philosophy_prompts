A state-court judge has ruled on six motions to dismiss in defamation suits involving evaluative language, ratings, and forecasts. Her rulings to date, condensed:

1. A restaurant critic's review calling a dish "bland" and the service "sneering," capped by a one-star rating: non-actionable as opinion.
2. A consumer magazine's claim, drawn from owner surveys, that a particular model's air-conditioning units "failed at 3.1 times the average rate in the first two years": actionable as a statement of fact if false.
3. A brokerage analyst's note saying a company "is undervalued relative to its peers; we rate it Buy": non-actionable as opinion.
4. A sports columnist's prediction that a trade "will prove a disaster for the franchise": non-actionable as opinion.
5. A licensed appraiser's statement of a house's market value as of the inspection date: actionable as a statement of fact if false.
6. A consumer website's customer-service score of 62 out of 100 for an airline, generated from a survey of 900 readers: actionable as a statement of fact if false.

A seventh motion is now before her. An investment letter states: "Our model projects that within twelve months the stock will trade at $48.50, a 27% gain from today; we rate it Strong Buy." The publisher argues this is non-actionable opinion and forward-looking commentary; the plaintiff argues it is a precise, checkable numerical assertion, no different in principle from the survey-based failure rate or the customer-service score.
