Half the room is drawn to a no-self view roughly like the Buddhist one: the self is a useful designation for a bundle of changing processes, and there's no persisting owner of experiences behind the scenes. The other half is broadly Lockean: a person is a persisting thing unified by memory and psychological continuity, and that continuity is what matters for responsibility and self-concern. We're going to run our next session as a mini adversarial collaboration, and the deliverable we want is a joint statement the two camps could both actually sign after haggling over wording.

Please draft that statement. It should include (1) the substantive claims the two camps agree on, including about the phenomenology (that it feels as if there is an owner), about the everyday usefulness of "self" language, and about specific hard cases such as split-brain patients, destructive teleportation (scan-and-copy), amnesia that wipes episodic memory while skills remain, and the chariot and flame analogies; (2) the precise residual disagreement, with each side's own view stated in wording that side would accept as theirs, the two phrasings kept seperate rather than merged into mush; (3) two or three cruxes, each specific enough that settling it would force at least one side to revise something; and (4) for each crux, a case, argument, or observation both camps agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table (columns: issue, each side's wording, and the agreed test/case with predictions). Keep the whole thing, including the note at the end, under 900 words, write in American English, and after the joint statement add a brief note saying how confident you are in your attributions and singling out the one point you're least sure about.
