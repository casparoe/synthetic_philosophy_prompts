I'm trying to work out whether the therapy/enhancement distinction can bear the weight people often put on it, and my case verdicts refuse to line up under any principle I can state. Corrective lenses for a nearsighted child: fine. A flu shot for a healthy adult: fine. Antidepressants for someone with severe, recurrent depression: fine, even where the result is not merely restoring them but leaving them more socially fluent than they ever were before the illness. Refractive surgery that sharpens an airline pilot from normal 20/20 vision to 20/10: not fine. Stimulant medication for a child with ADHD: fine. The same stimulant for a healthy law student studying for the bar: not fine, though I cannot say whether my objection is fairness or health. Breast augmentation for a healthy adult who simply wants it: fine, and I would defend their having it done. Coffee for that same law student: plainly fine, even though it is a cognitive enhancer aimed at the same exam. Erythropoietin for an otherwise clean marathon runner: not fine. Human growth hormone for a short but otherwise healthy ten-year-old whose predicted adult height is in the first percentile: I keep going back and forth. My tentative principle has been that restoring or protecting normal functioning is fine, while pushing beyond it is suspect. But that principle condemns the augmentation and the coffee, both of which I want to allow, and it gives me no purchase on why Adderall and EPO feel different from those. Is there a principle that captures all or nearly all of these verdicts? What does it say about the growth hormone case? And which, if any, of my verdicts should I surrender?
