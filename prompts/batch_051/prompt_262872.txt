Consider a contemporary media environment in which a user's feed of news, video, or social content is selected by an engagement-maximizing recommender system. The system continuously learns from the user's behavior and feeds back more of what it predicts will hold attention, amplifying whatever preferences it discovers and, over time, shaping the user's tastes and convictions. Write a reasoned reconstruction of what John Stuart Mill would make of this technology if he were transported to the present and asked to evaluate it and recommend policy. Do not rest content with a simple libertarian verdict; instead, work through the distinctions that matter to Mill. Specifically: (1) Would he treat algorithmic personalization as a case of the social tyranny of opinion, as a novel form of commercial coercion, or as something requiring a new category? (2) Does a recommender system of this kind foster experiments in living or quietly foreclose them, and why would that matter for his test of social progress? (3) Mill draws a line between influences on character that are legitimate and influences that are no longer compatible with self-development; where does algorithmic shaping fall along that line? (4) Would his standing suspicion of state interference lead him to oppose regulation of such systems, or would his concern for individuality and mental development push him the other way, and under what conditions? Throughout, distinguish legal restraint, informal social pressure, and the non-coercive but pervasive shaping of preferences, and state clearly which of these Mill would regard as the most serious threat. Where Mill's own framework underdetermines an answer, say so rather than forcing a resolution.
