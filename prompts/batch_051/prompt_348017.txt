I'm a novelist plotting a book around the Dover Area School District intelligent-design trial, and the deeper I get into the testimony the more it looks like a fight about what counts as a real explanation rather than a fight about religion per se. The frame I keep turning over is Dennett's cranes-versus-skyhooks distinction: cranes are bottom-up mechanisms that do their lifting gradually, using whatever is already there; skyhooks are top-down explanations that lower in a mind, a designer, or a plan with the hard parts already finished. Natural selection is the canonical crane, and intelligent design — whatever else it does — seems to be explicitly asking for a skyhook. I know the broad story: the disclaimer the board had read in ninth-grade biology class, the Of Pandas and People mess, Behe and the bacterial flagellum, Judge Jones ruling that ID is not science and that the policy was unconstitutional. What I'd like from you is a careful mapping, not another verdict on who won. Read the two sides through the crane/skyhook lens and say what it makes visible, then be honest about what the lens can't see.

I also want the metaphor held up against the case, in both directions. Does Dover come out as a clean triumph for cranes over skyhooks, or does calling the ID side "skyhook users" mostly function as a way of not engaging their actual argument? And, separately: would explaining the whole episode bottom-up — school-board politics, the Wedge strategy, textbook economics, the local election that flipped the board, the judge's background — be it's own greedy reductionism, a crane story so committed to the lower levels that it flattens the thing it claims to explain? Say where the distinction earns its keep and where it turns into name-calling.
