Just started doing freelance copyediting for a magazine and the copy chief, who's been there thirty years, keeps sending my pieces back with corrections that dont fit any pattern i can state. I've kept a list of her rulings so far:

- lets "to really understand the pattern" pass without comment
- lets "hopefully, the meeting stays short" pass
- lets "ask Taylor what they want" pass (singular they, known person)
- changes "10 items or less" to "10 items or fewer"
- flags "I literally died laughing"
- changes "I could care less" to "I couldn't care less"
- changes "between you and I" to "between you and me"
- lets "the data shows a clear trend" pass (data as singular)
- lets "the town was decimated by the storm" pass, even though it means devastated rather than one-in-ten

Now I'm looking at a sidebar for this week's piece: "10 factoids about the Roman Empire," where every item is a small, true fact. I happen to know "factoid" was originally coined for a false item that gets repeated in print until people treat it as true, but the trivia-list sense is the only one our readers know now. I'd rather not get this sent back again.

Predict her ruling on "factoid." Then the part I actually care about: what rule is she implicitly running about bad grammar (live distinctions? register? how long a usage has had to settle?)? What other rules fit her track record just as well? And what single further case would cleanly pick between them?
