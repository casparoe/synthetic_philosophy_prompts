Last fall I visited the Hiroshima Peace Memorial and I've been reading about the Pacific air war ever since, and now something's nagging at me. Most people I know (me included, at first instinct) treat Hiroshima and Nagasaki as a special moral category, something uniquely terrible that needs a special defense, while the firebombing of Tokyo — which killed a comparable number of civilians in a single night with ordinary incendiary bombs — just gets filed away as another awful night in an awful war. Even people who think the atomic bombings were justified still seem to feel they owe an argument, in a way nobody feels about Tokyo or Dresden. Is there a principle that actually justifies treating the nuclear attacks differently — the delayed radiation deaths, the fact that one plane and one bomb did it, something about the weapon itself — or is the asymmetry mostly a kind of awe-of-the-weapon moral salience I should try to shake? I want the strongest case you can make for keeping or dropping the asymmetry. And please seperate what's historically settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this, rather than presenting one side of the moral debate as the consensus.
