I keep hearing the standard argument that a randomized trial is only ethical when the medical community is genuinely split about which arm is better — not just when an individual doctor happens to be uncertain, but when there is real, good-faith disagreement among experts. The argument seems built for a world of two fixed arms and one primary endpoint. But a lot of current trials, especially platform trials in oncology, are adaptive: arms get added and dropped as the trial runs, randomization ratios shift as outcomes accrue, and someone can be randomized to an arm that the accumulating data already makes look worse just because it hasn't crossed a futility boundary. Does clinical equipoise survive that setup? I want to know which premise of the old argument is actually under pressure, and whether the whole thing can be repaired by saying equipoise only has to hold at the level of the platform as a whole, not for each pairwise comparison. Is that a real fix or just moving the goalposts? I'm asking about whether the argument still works, not about what a trial protocol should say.
