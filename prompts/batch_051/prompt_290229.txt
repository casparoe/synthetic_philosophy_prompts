I've been arguing in a legal philosophy reading group about how far criminal strict liability can extend before the absence of a mens rea requirement stops doing its normal justificatory work. We have been using a made-up but realistic statutory provision, which I quote below. Section 4-9 of the hypothetical State Criminal Code reads:

"Release of regulated substances. (a) A person is guilty of an offense if a substance listed in Schedule 2 escapes from a facility under that person's ownership, operation, or control. (b) This offense is one of strict liability. It is no defense that the defendant did not know of the escape, did not intend the escape, or took reasonable precautions to prevent it."

The provision was written before self-adjusting, learning controllers of the sort now common in industrial plants existed. Here is the case troubling us. A repackaging plant uses an automated venting system run by a machine-learning controller that had operated without incident for several years. During a prolonged heatwave, the controller encountered a combination of sensor readings it had never jointly seen before — high ambient temperature together with an unusual pump-vibration pattern — and opened a bypass vent for nearly an hour, releasing a Schedule 2 substance. Engineers later found that the controller had learned a spurious correlation between the two input channels; no one could have foreseen the behavior before it occurred, and the company had performed all inspections and maintenance that the vendor and the state recommended. The company is now charged under Section 4-9.

I would like you to work through whether the section covers this operator by applying, in turn: the plain meaning of the words, the purpose of a strict-liability environmental offense, what the drafters would likely have said had they been asked about this case, and what a reasonable reader of the statute would take it to require. Then tell me which of these approaches should govern here, and why. Finally, say whether on that reading the statute is genuinely punishing without fault in this case, or whether it is better understood as relocating fault to the decision to operate a facility with such a controller at all. Start with a one-sentence bottom line and keep your entire response under 700 words.
