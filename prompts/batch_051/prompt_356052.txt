I am a Year 12 student in England preparing my Extended Project Qualification proposal. The area I want to work in is the debate over why some countries are rich and others poor, and specifically whether the main explanatory work is done by institutions, geography, or culture. The project itself would be a five-thousand-word essay, and I need a one-page proposal for it.

My half-formed idea is this. The standard debate treats institutions, geography, and culture as three competing causes, so the natural question is which of them matters most. But I suspect that framing is part of the problem. Institutions are not only causes of wealth; they are themselves historical products, shaped by their geographic and cultural setting and in turn reshaping it. If that is right, comparing the three as rival causes in one observational history may be asking more of the evidence than comparative history can bear. I should like the essay to ask whether the question 'which of the three explains national wealth?' is even well-posed, and if it is not, what restricted or reformulated version of it could be answered with the kinds of historical and cross-country evidence we actually have.

Please turn this into a formal one-page proposal for the EPQ. State the research question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer, explain why the existing institutional, geographic, and cultural positions leave it open, and set out an approach that would make progress rather than merely surveying the literature or defending one of the three sides. Say what the possible conclusions are and which considerations would decide between them, what would count as the project failing, and what scope is realistic for a single academic year. As this is a proposal, I will add citations and references myself; please do not include a bibliography or reading list.
