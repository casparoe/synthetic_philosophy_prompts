For a paper in my religious studies class, I am trying to work out whether a translated text can be inspired in the same sense as the original. I need a working definition of "inspired" as it applies to a text — one precise enough to actually sort hard cases, not just a slogan about God breathing through the words. Please propose the definition, run it through the nine cases below, and tell me where it gives an answer that conflicts with my starting intuitions; where it does, either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict. At the end, tell me which of your final verdicts is least secure and what additional distinction or case would settle it.

The cases I want the definition to handle are:

1. The Hebrew text of Isaiah as it hypothetically left the prophet's circle, before centuries of copying and translation.
2. The Greek Septuagint, where its phrasing is close to but not identical with the Hebrew, and which, as I understand it, many early Christians treated as scripture.
3. The King James Version, on a view I have come across that the translators were themselves divinely guided, so that the English wording is authoritative even where it disagrees with older Hebrew or Greek manuscripts.
4. A careful modern committee translation, made from the best available manuscripts, with footnotes marking uncertain readings.
5. A loose, single-author paraphrase in contemporary language that tries to capture the general sense rather than the words.
6. A translation that contains an undisputed scribal or numerical error, such as a wrong name or a mistaken count.
7. A translation of a genuinely ambiguous verse in which the translators chose the rendering that best supports their own theology.
8. A scholarly commentary that explains the passage accurately and movingly but makes no claim to be scripture.
9. A sermon that quotes the passage and applies it correctly but in the preacher's own words.

My starting views: I am confident that (1) is inspired and that (8) and (9) are not, though I cannot say exactly what (8) lacks if it conveys the same truth. I lean toward counting the Septuagint as inspired but cannot defend why; I find case (3) hard to reject even though it seems extreme; I lean toward saying (5) is edifying but not inspired; and I have no settled view about (4), (6), and (7).
