I've been working on a novel set in a near-future where a big chunk of the historical record is generated by a neural network. The premise: a company like Meta or Google, as part of a deal with several governments, agrees to reconstruct missing archival material after a major data-center fire and a coordinated ransomware attack that encrypted a lot of digitized collections. The AI doesn't fabricate from nothing — it's trained on surviving fragments, newspaper indexes, auction records, diplomatic cables, all that. But for long stretches it fills gaps with statistically probable events. My narrator is a junior historian hired to "audit" the reconstructed archive. At one point she reads Ranke's preface to the *Geschichten der romanischen und germanischen Völker* and keeps coming back to this line: "Rigorous presentation of the facts, however conditional and lacking in beauty they may be, is without question the supreme law."

I want this line to do real work in the plot, but I'm not entirely sure I understand its force, or how it would cut against the AI archive. So here's what I'm after. I'd like you to unpack this sentence: What is "rigorous presentation" doing here, and why does Ranke add "however conditional and lacking in beauty"? Does the sentence still apply when the "facts" are themselves statistical reconstructions, with confidence scores and plausible filler? If Ranke's principle is read as a prohibition — do not smooth over the gaps, the ugly fragments, the unresolvable documents — does it mean a reconstructed archive violates the principle merely by being coherent? Or is the principle satisfied as long as the AI flags its uncertainty? And is there a reading of the sentence on which a generated record, because it maximizes the probability of what likely happened, is actually the purest form of "rigorous presentation" — maybe even more rigorous than a surviving but biased chronicle? That last reading feels wrong to me, but I can't quite articulate why, and I'd like the novel not to stack the deck against the AI by fiat. Also, if you think the sentence, read in its full context, can't bear the weight I'm putting on it, I want to know that too — I'd rather find a different passage than twist this one.
