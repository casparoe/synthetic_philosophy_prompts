My friend took the other side: he said when Translate turns a Japanese sentence inside out so it sounds natural in English, it must know what the sentence means, because how else would it know which words to move where? I think that's like saying a calculator knows what addition is because it never gets a sum wrong, but I couldnt actually prove him wrong. So now I'm looking for a test that would settle — or at least partly settle — whether a translation system understands the text as opposed to just being really good statistics over words.

My only idea so far is to feed it sentences where grammar alone isn't enough, like "The bottle fell on the blanket; it was wet," where you have to know stuff about bottles and blankets to pick the right translation for "it" in a language that marks gender or animacy. But I already see the problem: the model might have seen that exact kind of sentence a million times in training and just memorized the pattern, so passing wouldn't tell me whether it understood anything at all. So what I want from you is: design the strongest test you can think of that would distinguish "understands the sentence" from "has absorbed a huge corpus," say exactly what result would count as evidence each way, and then be honest about what the test could never settle — like, could a system ace every black-box test you can design and still be hollow on the inside?
