I'm a retired physical chemist who got into philosophy of psychiatry a few years back, and the idea I keep turning over is Hacking's looping effects: a psychiatric label gets applied to people, those people take the label up and change how they think and act, clinicians adjust the criteria to fit the new presentations, and the category shifts under everyone. It's the first bit of philosophy that's made me want to do real empirical work again. But I'm not sure which open problem in this area is actually load-bearing. Suppose I decide to spend five years, with a quantitative background but no clinical training, on exactly one of the following, with the aim of understanding whether and how psychiatric categories hold still: (a) getting clearer about what sort of thing a looping category is, (b) measuring looping effects directly — cohort drift in symptom expression, feedback between diagnosis and self-report, that kind of thing, (c) the ethics of introducing or retiring a category when you can foresee people will loop on it, or (d) something more upstream that I'm not seeing. Give me a defended ranking, not a survey. For each candidate, tell me what depends on it, how tractable it is for someone like me, and what evidence would make you move it up or down. I don't want a reading list; I want your judgment about where the area is actually stuck and which jam has to be cleared first.
