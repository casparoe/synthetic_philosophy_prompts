Mencius and Xunzi are usually taught as a clean disagreement: human nature is good versus human nature is bad. People say its just a verbal dispute about what 'nature' and 'good' mean. But there looks like an empirical bet buried in it about what humans are like before ritual and teaching get to them. Mencius thought the alarm at a child about to fall into a well showed a built-in moral sprout; Xunzi thought the raw material was selfish and order had to be imposed through ritual. I want to know whether any of that bet is testable.

Design one concrete study — developmental, cross-cultural, whatever you think is best — that would bear on whether there is a pre-cultural human disposition toward helping, fairness, or some other moral-ish behavior. Specify the population, the measure, and the comparison. Say what pattern of results would count for Mencius, what would count for Xunzi, and what a positive result on either side would and would not show. Then be clear about what the test still couldn't settle: whether whatever the lab measures is the same thing as the xing (nature) they were arguing over, whether 'good' is being read in the right way, whether a disposition that shows up at six months is the same as a nature.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — is this a real empirical disagreement, a verbal one, or some mix — and then give the test and reasoning. No preamble, no restating the question.
