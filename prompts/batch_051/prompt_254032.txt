I want you to help me think through an idea I have been nursing about the Principal Principle. As Lewis introduced it, the principle says, roughly, that a rational initial credence in a proposition A, conditional on the proposition that the objective chance of A is x and on any admissible evidence, should be x. The principle is usually read as a coherence constraint between credence and chance, but I suspect that the word deference is doing more work than it gets credit for. Here is a working account of what it is for an agent to defer to objective chance, in four conditions:

(C1) If an agent is certain that the objective chance of A at t is x, and has no additional information that is inadmissible relative to A at t, then the agent's credence in A is x.

(C2) If the agent's evidence leaves several possible chance hypotheses open, the agent's credence in A is the expected chance: cr(A) = Σ_i cr(Ch_t(A) = x_i) · x_i.

(C3) Screening-off: conditional on a fully specified chance hypothesis, further admissible evidence about A's truth does not move the agent's credence in A; such evidence bears on A only by bearing on which chance hypothesis obtains.

(C4) Deference is owed only to genuine objective single-case chance relative to a time and a history. It is not owed automatically to subjective forecasts, sample frequencies in ad hoc reference classes, or probabilities defined over a different partition of outcomes.

Now I want to apply this account to a case that has always struck me as a stress test for it. Suppose a subject, Beauty, is told the following protocol and knows it will be carried out exactly. On Sunday she goes to sleep. A fair coin is tossed that night; its objective chance of landing Heads is 1/2. If the coin lands Heads, she is awakened once, on Monday, and then the experiment ends. If it lands Tails, she is awakened on Monday, given a drug that erases the memory of that waking, and awakened again on Tuesday. In the Tails case her Tuesday waking is indistinguishable from her Monday waking; she cannot tell which day it is, and she cannot tell whether she has been awakened before. When awakened, she has no contact with the outside world and receives no information that distinguishes Heads from Tails. The only new information she has is what is sometimes called self-locating: that she is awake now, under a protocol in which Heads produces one waking and Tails produces two indistinguishable wakings.

Beauty is certain throughout that the chance of Heads is 1/2. The two main candidate waking credences in Heads are 1/2 and 1/3, and I am not asking you to adjudicate the Sleeping Beauty puzzle itself. What I want to know is whether Beauty, in adopting either waking credence, counts as deferring to the objective chance of Heads in the sense specified by (C1)–(C4).

Please structure your answer in three parts. First, test each candidate credence against the four conditions one by one. For each condition, say whether the candidate satisfies it, violates it, or whether the condition is inapplicable or merely underdetermines the verdict, and justify your answer. Second, address admissibility directly: give the strongest argument you can that Beauty's waking information is admissible relative to the chance of Heads, and the strongest argument that it is not admissible because it is self-locating and the Principal Principle was never intended to regulate evidence about one's own location in a day or in a protocol. Say which argument you find stronger and why. Third, tell me whether the case shows that the working account needs revision. If it does, state the amended account precisely enough that it yields your preferred verdict in Beauty's case without being vacuous. If it does not, explain how the unamended account already handles the case.
