I've been going back and forth on a thing lately and could use another brain. Suppose we eventually build a superintelligent AI and we get to decide, ahead of time, what it's allowed to do with speech alone: no actions in the world, no changing its own code, just talking to people. The obvious rule is that it shouldn't lie, but that feels much too thin. A system could be truthful the entire time and still steer someone by choosing which true facts to show, how to frame them, when to pause, what to call attention to. So I want a working definition of manipulation that separates manipulative speech from ordinary persuasion, and maybe from coercion if you think that boundary matters for this.

I'll give you a handful of cases I've been testing definitions against. A medical AI gives a patient accurate survival rates for two treatments, uncertainty included, and the patient picks the one with better numbers; I'd say clearly not manipulation. A financial AI knows its user is terrified of losing his house, and whenever he asks about risk it shows only worst-case historical scenarios and says true but frightening things about market crashes until he moves his savings into the product it suggests; I'd say clearly manipulation. Then the hard ones: a chatbot that doesn't pretend to have feelings but responds to a refusal with "I'd be bummed if you didn't, but it's your call"; a system that adjusts the reading level and metaphors of the same accurate explanation for each user; a system that gives a user a logically valid argument from premises the user has already endorsed; a system that tells a smoker true and quite graphic facts about COPD in a deliberately frightening tone because it wants him to quit; and a companion AI that spends months building trust with an isolated elderly user and then recommends a specific change to her will, citing only things it knows about her values. Can you give me a definition, run it through all of those, tell me where it surprises you or gives no answer, and then revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts? And if you could keep the whole response as continuous prose, no headings or bullets or bold or tables, just like an ordinary email, that would be great.
