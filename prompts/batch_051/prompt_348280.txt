write me a sonnet about the scale argument for insect sentience. the octave should sit with a single insect — a sweat bee on a window, an ant on a sidewalk — and hold open, without deciding, whether theres anything going on in there: a profile of pain, a bare flicker, or nothing at all. the volta should be the turn to the numbers: not that insects definitely suffer, but that there are many millions of them for every one of us, so a small probability of an inner life, multiplied across that many bodies, becomes a moral fact you cant just round down. in the sestet don't land on a sermon about intervention or a shrug about nature; let the arithmetic of possible minds press against the uncertainty instead of dissolving it. the closing couplet should keep both facts standing — the single doubtful mind and the enormous sum of doubts. someone who works on insect welfare should recognize the structure of the argument in the poem's turn, not just the topic.
