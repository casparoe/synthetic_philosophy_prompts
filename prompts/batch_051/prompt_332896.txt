I've been trying to write down my views about how the safety of advanced AI systems should be evaluated, and I would like an audit of whether the following commitments form a consistent package. I'm not asking which individual claim is true; I want to know whether they jointly hold together, where the precise incompatibilities are, and what the cheapest revision would be.

Here are the commitments as I currently hold them:

1. For systems whose failure could be catastrophic, safety should be judged by worst-case behavior over the space of deployment conditions an adversary or an unlucky world could present, rather than by average-case performance.

2. I am skeptical of existing formal worst-case guarantees — certified robustness bounds, verified properties, and the like — as evidence about the real-world safety of large learned systems. Either their assumptions fail in deployment, or the properties they certify are too narrow to bear much weight.

3. In practice, I would rather trust a serious adversarial red-teaming effort than a formal proof about a model, and I regard a red team that tries hard and fails to elicit dangerous behavior as strong evidence that the model is safe enough to deploy.

4. I also concede that red-teaming is a form of adaptive sampling: it can only speak to the attacks people have thought to run, and it does not rule out the rare, unimagined, or out-of-distribution failure mode. Strictly, then, it supports something closer to a typical-case claim than a worst-case one.

5. I accept some form of Goodhart's law: once a safety metric or benchmark is optimized against, its correlation with the true safety property degrades. Yet I continue to believe that standardized safety benchmarks should play a real role in how models and labs are evaluated.

6. I am not convinced that the space of all possible deployment conditions is well-defined for a general-purpose system, since any specification of that space seems to smuggle in a prior over environments we do not have. But I still find the thought of retreating to average-case guarantees in this domain unacceptable.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs or larger subsets are in genuine tension, how severe each tension is, and what the cheapest modification would be to restore consistency. Also identify any apparent tension that you think dissolves on inspection, and say why.
