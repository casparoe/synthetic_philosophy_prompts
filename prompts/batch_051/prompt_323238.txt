Last week a friend and I spent a long argument on a question we couldn't settle, and since then I've been trying to pin down a pattern that keeps recurring. The question was whether a person can coherently say, while she is still deciding what to do, that there is, say, a 70 percent chance she will do it. My friend thinks this is just ordinary uncertainty about one's own future conduct, no different in kind from predicting a colleague. I kept objecting that, for a decision I'm currently making, the prediction doesn't stay neutral: the moment I assign a probability to my doing the thing, I seem to have started making the choice rather than estimating it — the number either reveals that I'm leaning, or acts as a commitment, or makes the alternative feel already settled. The same tension showed up when we talked about assigning degrees of belief to whether I would keep a resolution or take a risk I was still mulling over. There seems to be a structural conflict between taking a predictive stance toward my own future choices and taking a deliberative stance toward them. Is there an established name for this tension, and how should it really be stated? I'm not looking for a bare label. I want to know whether the correct formulation is that deliberation undermines prediction, that prediction undermines deliberation, or something more precise than either, and what the strongest considerations are for thinking the tension is genuine rather than a confusion.
