My spouse and I recently left the close religious community in which we were both raised, and the same challenge now follows us from well-meaning relatives: that participation in a religious community makes people morally better, and that by raising our children outside one we are making them less honest, less generous, less trustworthy. I genuinely want to know how much of that challenge is empirical and how much is a disguised claim about what "better" means.

Design the strongest feasible test of the claim that religious participation makes people morally better. The test should be concrete: specify the population or data you would use, the comparison, the outcome measures, and the pattern of results that would count for or against the claim. Because religious membership cannot simply be randomized, say what identifying variation you would use and what threats to inference remain.

Your design should force apart three things that are usually run together: (a) religion causing people to become morally better, (b) morally motivated people selecting into religious communities, and (c) religious settings merely making good behavior more visible to, or more narrowly directed toward, one's own group without changing underlying character. Use outcome measures that track different senses of "more moral," such as anonymous honesty, costly generosity toward out-group strangers, promise-keeping when no one would find out, and trust extended to people who cannot reciprocate. Be explicit about which sense of the claim each measure speaks to and which it cannot speak to.

Then say exactly what a positive result would establish and what it would leave untouched. I want the boundary marked precisely: which parts of the dispute would remain conceptual or normative even after the test returns—for example, what counts as a moral trait as opposed to mere habit or fear of punishment, whether motives matter more than acts, and whether trust is intrinsically part of being "better" or merely instrumentally useful. Do not retreat into saying the question is untestable; find the best partial test and defend its relevance while being honest about the residue.
