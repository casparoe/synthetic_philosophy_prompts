People comfortably say that a Chopin prelude is sad, but they would not say that its component pitches are sad, that its tempo marking is sad, or that the air-pressure waves emitted in the performance are sad. Yet the prelude just is a patterned sequence of pitches, durations, intensities, and timbres; it has no parts that are sad in a more literal way. I want to know whether this asymmetry is defensible, or whether it reveals that ordinary talk of sad music is a category mistake. Is there a principle that grants sadness to the temporal whole while withholding it from any part or physical substrate? If so, explain precisely what that principle is and how it differs from merely saying that the whole is more than the sum of its parts. If not, what should we do with the ordinary judgment that some music is sad?
