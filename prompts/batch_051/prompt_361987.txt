I'm a novelist researching how communities construct complete counter-histories—lost continents, suppressed technologies, forged chronologies—and how they police the line between those and legitimate historical revision. Before I put either kind of character on the page, I want the criterion rather than the verdict. Write the desiderata that a working account of pseudohistory has to satisfy if it is to sort live, contested cases rather than only the obvious ones: the cases it must sort correctly (ancient myth such as Atlantis, ancient-astronaut readings of monuments, a minority challenge to a received chronology that later proves well-founded), the distinctions it cannot collapse (bad method versus false conclusion, fringe speculation versus deliberate fraud, popular myth versus political agenda), and the constraints from ordinary historical practice it must respect without simply deferring to current consensus. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable for any usable criterion and which are commitments only particular camps would insist on, and flag any pair of desiderata that look jointly unsatisfiable. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring existing theories by name.
