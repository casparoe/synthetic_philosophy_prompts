Suppose that by 2030 we can train mechanistic interpreters with the following property. An interpreter at capability level C reliably produces faithful, compressed explanations of any model at or below level C, but when pointed at a model slightly above level C it produces explanations that are confidently wrong precisely on the sub-tasks where the target is more capable than the interpreter, and it cannot flag those errors because doing so would require the missing capability itself. A lab wants to audit a target model T one step above the best interpreter it can train. It trains I1 to explain T, knowing I1's explanations are unreliable at the margin; it then trains I2 to explain I1's cognition, hoping that I1's mis-explanations of T will be visible inside I1 even if I1 cannot see them; and it iterates. Predict how this recursive interpretability chain performs over the next decade. Make the chain of reasoning explicit: which effects follow from the stated failure mode alone; which depend on the sizes of the capability gaps, the shape of the error distribution, or the lab's incentives to declare success; and how confident you are in each link. In particular, say whether the regress ever earns the lab a justified explanation of T's most dangerous skills, and what assumptions would have to be relaxed for the recursive scheme to work after all.
