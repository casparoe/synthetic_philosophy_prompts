I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I've spent the last few months reporting on mosquito-control districts and orchard spraying, and I keep running into a weird asymmetry I want to get clearer on. Nobody I quote seems troubled by a program that kills millions of mosquito larvae in a season or an orchard spray that takes out a hundred thousand caterpillers, but everybody agrees that deliberately poisoning a single kitten would be monstrous. At first I filed that under ordinary speciesism plus the usual small-animal discount. But I've started to think their's a second effect layered on top, and I've been calling it swarm discounting.

Here's the claim. Moral weight doesn't just shrink as you move from one organism to a large group; at some point the group changes category, and that category change does a lot of the moral work. One aphid on a rose is an animal you might flick away; three hundred aphids is an infestation, and an infestation gets treated like a condition of the plant, not like 300 separate lives. The extra individuals don't add moral weight — they subtract it, because they're what tips the situation from individual-animal into pest-problem. It's not the same comparison as one kitten versus many kittens; the aggregate is weighed as a different kind of thing altogether. I think this is why the insecticide debate talks past itself so reliably: one side is counting individual sentient creatures, the other side is managing a population parameter, and neither notices that the unit of account switched.

What I want to know is whether swarm discounting is a real, distinct concept or just a metaphor, or maybe old scope-insensitivity findings with a new wrapper. Has somebody already worked this out under another name? And where does it break? For instance, it might be wrong to assume that moral accounting should stay additive for animals that may not have much of an individual point of view, or it might overstate how different one animal and many animals are once you already accept that a single insect counts for very little. Be candid: if the idea is half-baked, say where it collapses.
