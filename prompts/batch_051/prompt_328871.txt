The one I can't settle is political guilt. Jaspers didn't think this was the same as criminal guilt, and he didn't think it required bad individual intentions. Fine. But then I got arguing with my flatmate about generations. Suppose a 22-year-old in a democracy today pays taxes, votes, benefits from institutions, but the state comitted atrocities decades before they were born, and the current government has never apologised or returned what was taken. Does that person bear political guilt for those past wrongs, simply in virtue of being a citizen now? Not criminal guilt, not moral guilt — political guilt in Jaspers's sense.

Here's the proposition: a citizen of a democratic state can bear political guilt for wrongs committed by that state before the citizen was born, even if the citizen personally opposes those wrongs and votes for parties that want reparations. Give rough probabilities to those sub-questions, say how they combine, and tell me which one your final number is most sensitive to.
