Take Nozick's experience machine, but with one new parameter. Before plugging in, you set 'grief fidelity' from 0 to 1. At 0 the simulated life still has minor annoyances, but the machine quietly edits out anything that would cause deep grief: people you love never die or permanently abandon you, projects never altogether collapse, and the run is on net about 20% more pleasant per day than at 1. At 1, the same biography is simulated but with all its losses, mourning, and despair intact, roughly as a normal life would deliver them. Everything else works like the original: once your in, you can't tell it's simulated and you don't remember the choice. Does Nozick's anti-machine argument get stronger, weaker, or stay the same when the choice is posed this way, and would a hedonist and someone who thinks grief tracks the importance of what was lost disagree about which setting to pick?
