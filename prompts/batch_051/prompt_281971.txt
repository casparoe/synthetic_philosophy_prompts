I am writing a paper on the doctrine of double effect, and I keep returning to Aquinas's treatment of self-defense in Summa Theologiae II-II, Question 64, Article 7. I am trying to get clear on the border between an effect's being intended and its being merely foreseen and accepted, and one diagnostic I have encountered is roughly this: if an agent could have achieved the good effect without the bad effect, or with a lesser version of it, and yet did not, then the bad effect is better classified as intended rather than merely foreseen.

I want a carefully engineered case that puts pressure on that diagnostic.
