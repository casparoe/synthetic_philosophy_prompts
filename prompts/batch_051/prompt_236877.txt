In my philosophy of mind class we've been stuck for two weeks on pain asymbolia and what it actually shows about whether pain is essentially bad. The side that thinks the badness is separable from pain keeps pointing to patients who can localize a sensation, say whether it is sharp or dull, and rate its intensity, but report that they genuinely do not mind it. So their line is: those patients have pain without the normal negative evaluation, which means the unpleasantness must be something the mind adds on top of the pain, not part of pain itself. The other side says no—if the affective component is missing, then these patients don't have pain in the full sense; they have a pain-like sensory signal, and the word pain should be reserved for the whole package. After hearing both sides go back and forth I've started to suspect the whole impasse is just a disagreement about whether we define pain by its sensory character or by its badness, and underneath it everyone agrees on the neurology. Is that right, or is there a substantive disagreement about what pain is hiding in there? And if it is mostly a definitional fight, is there a principled reason to draw the boundary one way rather than the other, or should the field just split the term and move on?
