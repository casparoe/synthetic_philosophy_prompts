Cog sci PhD student here. I keep running into a pattern I can't find a single clean label for. Sometimes monitoring your own performance wrecks it: a pianist thinks about which finger comes next mid-passage and falls apart, a good hitter starts decomposing his swing and goes into a slump, a witness who describes a face afterwards does worse on a lineup than one who just picked. Other times the same kind of self-monitoring is exactly what you need: a chess player annotates her own losses and improves, therapy works by making implicit patterns explicit, and a novice benefits from step-by-step feedback while an expert doesn't. So it looks like a U-curve: turning a process into an object of thought helps when the process is broken or being built, and hurts when it's fluid and overlearned. I know the seperate labels — choking under pressure, verbal overshadowing, the centipede's dilemma, maybe ironic processes — but they get treated as different phenomena with different mechanisms. I'm after whether the general shape has a name of its own, and if not, what should we call it and what boundary conditions decide which side of the curve you're on. Don't just give me the one-liner label; I want the carving — what this is and isn't and where its edges are. Also tell me how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you're least sure of.
