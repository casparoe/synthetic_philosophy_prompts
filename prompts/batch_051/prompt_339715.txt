ok so I'm writing a one-page statement of my views on laws of nature for a philosophy of science seminar, and I'd rather have the problems pointed out now than in front of everyone. Here's where I've landed:

(1) the world is just the mosaic of particular facts—particles at places and times, and the like. There's no extra modal glue making one thing follow from another.
(2) I'm a best-system person: the laws are the generalizations in the deductive system that does the best job of balancing simplicity, strength, and fit for the entire mosaic.
(3) laws explain their instances. If you ask why this rock fell when I dropped it, the law of gravity plus the dropping explains the fall. I don't think that's just a way of talking.
(4) explanation is asymmetric. The flagpole's height explains the length of its shadow, not the other way around, even though you can derive either one from the other plus the laws.
(5) but I also think the asymmetry of explanation isn't baked into the world. It comes from our interests and explanatory practices; the world itself doesnt come pre-labeled with a direction of explanation.
(6) there's a fact of the matter about which generalizations are laws, and it doesnt depend on what we happen to find simple, useful, or interesting.
