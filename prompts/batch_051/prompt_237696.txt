I'm a senior editor on a secondary-school world-history line at a big textbook publisher, and every revision cycle we have the same argument. One editor says the 'Age of Exploration' chapter is Eurocentric because it starts with Vasco da Gama and Columbus and treats the rest of the world as places waiting to be discovered. Another editor says every chapter has to start somewhere, you can't tell the past from everywhere at once, and a book that tries just becomes a list of disconnected facts. I need a reusable procedure my editors can run chapter by chapter to decide when a chapter is actually distorting the history through a Eurocentric framing and when its just a defensible choice about focus. The people using it are trained historians, not philosophers; a chapter gets maybe 20 minutes; the stakes are that several hundred thousand students read the chapter and one state adoption commitee can reject the whole book over it; nothing ships without the editorial board chair signing off.

I want the procedure to catch the difference between adding more regions to a European script and actually changing the question being asked, and to be honest that 'telling the past from everywhere at once' might not be a real option. Ground it in that conceptual debate, not in diversity box-ticking. Give it as a numbered list of questions to ask in order, with what counts as a sufficient answer to move on, where the procedure stops and says revise, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values or editorial judgment. Mark those points JUDGMENT. Then run the procedure on three chapters: (1) an Age of Exploration chapter that begins with the Portuguese and Spanish voyages and frames Africa, the Indian Ocean, and the Americas mostly as sites of European arrival; (2) an Industrial Revolution chapter that treats British industrialization as basically self-generated and mentions India and China only as later victims of British textile exports; (3) a World War II chapter that covers Europe in detail and puts China and the Pacific war in a short closing section. I want at least one of these to come out 'revise substantially' in a way I won't like hearing, and I want the procedure to be honest about what it cannot settle. Keep the whole thing under 1,000 words, with the procedure itself no more than a page.
