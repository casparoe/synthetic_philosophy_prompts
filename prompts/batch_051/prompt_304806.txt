I am an undergraduate majoring in software engineering and currently taking a philosophy of music course, so I am fluent with classes, instances, source listings, compiled artifacts, runs, version histories, interfaces and implementations, and deterministic versus interactive execution, but the ontology of musical works is new to me. The course has covered three kinds of position: that a musical work is an abstract type or structure whose tokens are performances and whose score plays something like the role of a specification; that a work is a created abstract artifact, called into being by a particular act of composition rather than discovered as an eternal structure; and that a work is, more deflationarily, just a collection or class of its concrete performances. I keep reaching for software terms, but I am worried my mapping is doing the philosophical work for me. My working guess is that a score is source code, a work is the program's interface or intended behavior, and a performance is one execution; a recording then looks like a serialized snapshot of a particular run. But each position in the course would have to map these differently, and I want to see that done faithfully rather than by loose analogy. Please restate each of the three positions in my software-engineering vocabulary as cleanly as you can, saying what a work, score, performance, and recording correspond to under each reading. Then give me a residue report: which claims translate without loss, which claims had to be distorted to fit, and which claims have no software counterpart at all. I especially want to know where the source-code/interface/execution/snapshot partition breaks down, and whether the points where it breaks down are limitations of my vocabulary or signs that the philosophical dispute is less sharp than it appears.
