My town is going through a zoning fight that's dragged on for over a year — the plan would allow a couple kinds of attached housing and tiny storefronts in a part of town that's been single-family since before my parents were born. My dad's on the opposing side and he's actually thought about it. He's been reading Confucius and a bit of Edmund Burke, and his whole case rests on the body analogy: a town or polity is an organism, institutions and neighborhoods are organs, each does its one job, health is the parts working harmoniously rather than competing, and liberal pluralism — his phrase — is basically an autoimmune disorder. He also paraphrases that Confucian point that harmony (he) is not the same as conformity (tong), but he uses it to mean everyone has a place and should stay in it, which honestly sounds like the opposite of the passage as I understand it.

The analogy has a real surface pull and I want it audited properly rather than just called names. What maps onto what (a correspondence table is fine)? Where does it break? Which disanalogies actually matter for the anti-pluralist conclusion and which are just noise? What does the body image quietly import — a single controlling head, fixed roles, one standard of health, parts with no independent standing, conflict as disease? Does the conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying it the whole way? And if there's a better analogy that captures whatever truth hes pointing at, say what it is and what it can actually support.
