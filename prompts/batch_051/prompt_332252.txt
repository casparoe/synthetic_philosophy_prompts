I volunteer on the board of my town's juried art show, and this year the commitee is split in a way that's become a whole argument about what it means to make an image. Our eligibility rules have been unchanged since 1996 and say:

"All entries must be the entrant's own original work. Works produced with cameras, darkroom equipment, computers, or any other instruments are acceptable if the entrant directs the process and the final image is the product of the entrant's choices. No entry may incorporate work made by another person."

Now two submissions have us stuck. The first is from a painter who trained a generative model on hundreds of her own watercolours and sketches, then made the images through a long cycle of prompting, selecting, and retouching; but she says the model keeps surprising her with passages she never would have chosen, and she likes that. The second is from a guy who typed a few prompts into an off-the-shelf image generator and then culled through maybe four hundred outputs for three he liked; he says composing the prompt and curating the results is the new photography.

I have to give the committee write-up before our vote next week. Try to reason it from the words of the clause first, then from its purpose, then from what the people who drafted it in 1996 would have said if you'd asked them about generative models, then from what a reasonable applicant reading it today would take it to cover. I'd like a recommendation on which of the two entries are eligible and where the cases come apart. If the rule can't reach one without also reaching the other in a way the committee would reject, say that too.
