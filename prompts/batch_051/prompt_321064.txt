I want a number from you on the following claim, not a taxonomy of positions.

The claim: For any two systems that implement the same full input–output causal profile — meaning the same dispositions to move from broadly specified inputs to outputs and internal causal states across the full range of possible inputs, not just a finite test set — if one is phenomenally conscious, then the other is too; and conversely. Call this substrate-neutrality of sentience.

I am asking because this looks like the load-bearing step in the attribution problem. If the claim is false, then whether a system is conscious cannot be settled by looking at what the system computes, and sentience attribution depends in part on facts about the realizer. If it is true, the question shifts to which computational or causal profiles suffice, and the hard cases become a matter of drawing the boundary in profile-space rather than in substrate-space.

Give me your actual credence in this claim and, more importantly, the derivation. I want at least the following sub-questions separated and assigned numbers:

1. Broad substrate-neutrality about the mind: what is your probability that mental and cognitive properties are functionally realized rather than type-identical to biological or physical properties?
2. Conditional on broad substrate-neutrality, the probability that it survives the standard triviality worries — gerrymandered implementations, lookup-table systems that match the input–output mapping while lacking the right causal organization, and the general problem of saying what counts as implementing a computation at all. That is, conditional on (1), how likely is it that there is a principled, non-question-begging criterion of implementation that excludes the cases that would make the claim trivially false?
3. Conditional on (1) and (2), the probability that phenomenal consciousness in particular is fixed by input–output causal profile, rather than being the exception that remains substrate-bound even while other mental states are substrate-neutral.
4. A residual route to substrate-neutrality of sentience even if broad substrate-neutrality is false: for example, that sentience is multiply realizable only within a constrained class of substrates, but that class is still wide enough to make the claim true in practice.

Then combine these into the final number. I expect the combination to leave some unswept floor, so tell me how much residual uncertainty you are carrying and where it sits.

I also want a sensitivity analysis. Which single sub-question would move your final credence the most if you got a decisive answer to it, and what kind of evidence or argument would constitute that decisive answer? Be honest about whether the claim itself is too vague to have a determinate truth value, but do not let that substitute for a number; if you think it is vague, fold that into one of the sub-questions and say which.

For calibration: my own number is around 0.55; a friend with a similar background puts it at 0.15. Where do you think that gap is most likely located, and what specific disagreement would most explain it?
