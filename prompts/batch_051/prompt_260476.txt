Give me your actual credence — a single number, not a range — that at least one human whole-brain emulation will have been built by 2100, where by "whole-brain emulation" I mean something stronger than a chatbot that happens to act like the person: a simulation of a particular person's brain that is conscious in the same way you are right now, and that is literally the same subject continuing — the original stream of consciousness carried into the simulation — not a very good copy with matching memories. I don't want "it depends on your theory of identity"; I want the number plus the decomposition. Here are the moving parts I can see, but add or split if you need to:

(1) feasibility at the physics/compute level: a brain-scale simulation at whatever level matters — spike trains plus the sub-spike stuff if it matters, like dendritic integration, neuromodulation, receptors, maybe local protein synthesis — can run in something like real time on an energy budget a rich country could actually afford;
(2) the mapping and modeling problem: we'll figure out how to scan a real brain densely enough and turn the data into a working model, and someone on Earth actually does it by 2100 (the engineering-plus-social term);
(3) substrate-neutrality: if we build and run it, the simulation produces phenomenal consciousness — it actually feels something — rather than being a perfect p-zombie;
(4) numerical identity: conditional on (3), it is still you/that person, not a new person who wakes up with your memories — the original stream of consciousness survives the upload;
(5) any term you think is missing, e.g. branching (if more than one copy runs), the biological original still being alive, or whether the right grain of simulation is coarser or finer than the list in (1).

For each one, give a probability and one or two lines of justification. Then say how you combined them into the headline number — product, weighted average, whatever you actually did — and tell me which term you're least confident in and what single peice of evidence or argument in the next twenty years would move that term most. If I tell you I'm at about 0.07, tell me where the gap between us most likely lives, i.e. which sub-question the disagreement is really about.
