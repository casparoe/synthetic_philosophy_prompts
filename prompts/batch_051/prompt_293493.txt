I'm taking a political thought course and we've just finished Mill, and the thing that's been bugging me is the apparent mismatch between the Mill of On Liberty and the late Mill who, in the draft chapters on socialism published after his death, says some surprisingly warm things about cooperative production and the eventual replacement of wage labour. I don't want a reading list or anything like that, I just want to know whether a reconstruction I've been playing with actually holds up. I've tried to turn the "reconciliation" reading into a numbered argument, and I half-suspect one of the steps is doing something sneaky, but I can't quite pin down where. Here it is:

(1) Mill's real commitment in On Liberty is to individuality understood as self-development through active choice, not to private property or market competition as goods in themselves.
(2) In his later economic writings, Mill treats private property and competition as contingent arrangements that are only as good as the individuality and security they actually secure.
(3) Any economic system that preserves exit options, dispersed decision-making power, and protection against informal social coercion preserves the conditions of individuality, irrespective of whether the formal ownership is private or cooperative.
(4) A decentralised, voluntary form of socialism — mainly worker-run cooperatives competing or federating with one another, with no central state plan — could in principle satisfy all three of the conditions in (3).
(5) Therefore decentralised, voluntary socialism is compatible with Mill's liberty principle, and Mill's late hostility to centralised state socialism is not an objection to it.

My worry is that step (3) is shouldering more than it can bear — maybe exit options and dispersed decision-making quietly smuggle in something close to markets, or maybe they fail to protect against the homogenising pressure of public opinion in a cooperative setting. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and on what reading? If it is valid, would it actually establish the conclusion as stated about Mill's own position, or only something weaker about his principle? Please answer in British English.
