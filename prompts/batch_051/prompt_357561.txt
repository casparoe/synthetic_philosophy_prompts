I've just taken over a twenty-acre field from my aunt, and she's farmed it for forty years but refuses to just tell me what to plant. She says I have to work her rule out from the advice she's given other people. She's the local oracle for crop decisions when you genuinely can't know the weather ahead of time—she never uses probabilities and gets annoyed if you try to give her any.

I found my notes from three times she advised neighbors. In each case there were two crops and the same three possible seasons (wet, normal, dry), and the numbers are net income per acre in hundreds of dollars. Here are her calls:

Case 1: crop A (70, 60, 40) vs crop B (90, 50, 10) — she said A.
Case 2: crop A (50, 50, 50) vs crop B (0, 80, 80) — she said A.
Case 3: crop A (50, 45, 40) vs crop B (80, 60, 0) — she said A.

Now my field comes down to crop X (60, 50, 40) vs crop Y (100, 20, 10). I can't tell if she'd stick with X because its worst season is so much better, or switch to Y because if it turns wet and I planted X I'd leave 40 on the table and hate myself. Her past comments are no help: one day she says she only looks at the floor, the next day she says she couldn't live with herself if the other crop would've paid off.

Predict her call on my case. Say which rule she's implicitely running—maximin, minimax regret, or something else—give your best guess with reasons, and sketch one further case with numbers I could put to her that would settle whether she's a maximin person or a regret person.
