"A Dutch book is a set of bets, each of which a person considers fair given their own degrees of belief, but which together guarantee a loss no matter how things turn out. Dutch book arguments show that anyone whose degrees of belief violate the axioms of probability can be drawn into such a book without revising their judgment that any single bet is fair. This shows the person's beliefs are internally contradictory in just the way that believing a proposition and its negation is. It also means that such a person will, in practice, lose money to a sufficiently clever bookie if they keep betting. These arguments therefore prove that the probability axioms are the complete logic of rational belief, and that no further assumptions about a person's preferences or goals are needed for the proof to have that force."
