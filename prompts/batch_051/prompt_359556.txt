The professor wants me to stay away from the usual happiness-versus-money examples entirely. Instead I'm supposed to teach the distinction through three values that strike undergraduates as obviously intrinsic but might, on reflection, be purely instrumental: truth-seeking, respect for tradition, and purity (naturalness intuitions, disgust-based rules, that sort of thing). Don't write the lecture—give me a teaching plan. What has to come first, and which of the three should be the carrying example for the hour, and why not the other two? What misconceptions will first-years walk in with or form halfway through? I'm betting they'll hear "instrumental" as "selfish or shallow" and "intrinsic" as "feels really important," and I want to know where exacty to head each off. I also want them to be able to argue that truth-seeking might be entirely instrumental without thinking that makes it lesser—that seems like the move they'll resist. What should I deliberately not get into, and what's one final question that would show me they actually got the distinction rather than memorized the words?
