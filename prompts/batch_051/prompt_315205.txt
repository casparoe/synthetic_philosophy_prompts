I've been thinking about the hole argument in philosophy of spacetime — the one where you take a region of the manifold with no matter in it, apply a diffeomorphism that is the identity outside the hole and on its boundary but moves the metric field around inside, and then point out that the substantivalist seems forced to treat the original and the shifted field as two possible worlds that agree everywhere outside the hole yet differ inside, so the state outside fails to determine the state inside. The usual upshot is that spacetime points don't have primitive identity, or substantivalism is in trouble. Here's what I want to know. What becomes of this argument if spacetime at bottom is not a continuum but a causal set — a locally finite partially ordered set of elementary events, with the order being causal, and with the manifold and metric emerging only approximately? On its face the hole argument can't even get started: there's no fixed manifold of spacetime points to permute, no diffeomorphism to slide a metric field around; the closest analogue is a relabeling of the discrete events, and nobody treats that as producing a new possible world. But I worry thats too quick. Maybe the argument was never really about points — it was about the broader principle that gauge-equivalent presentations can't be distinct possibilities — and causal set theory doesn't kill that principle, it just makes it explicit below the continuum. Or maybe the argument comes back at the level of the emergent manifold, when distinct continuum approximations of the same causal set stand in a diffeomorphism-like relation. I want a committed answer, not a menu: does the hole argument survive the move to causal set theory, does it need a repaired statement, or is it dissolved? And whichever you pick, defend it against the obvious comeback and say what follows for substantivalism. If you think it's dissolved, does that mean we get to keep spacetime points after all, or did causal set theory just deliver the relationalist conclusion the hole argument was originally used to press?
