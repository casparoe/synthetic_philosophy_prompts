Next month is mine: positive, negative, and republican liberty. I want the bulk of the session to go to the piece that genuinely confused me when I first started reading — why freedom as non-domination isn't just negative liberty with a probability attached (the chance that somebody might interfere). I've already read up on the distinction myself; what I need is a plan for the room. Walk me through the order I should introduce the ideas in and what depends on what — does negative liberty have to be nailed down cold first, or do they need the idea of an unexercised power before anything else makes sense? Then give me the one example that should carry the whole hour, and say what it has to have and what it has to avoid; I was going to reach for the slave with a kind master, but I'm afraid that's either too easy to write off or to loaded for a mixed group, so tell me whether to keep it or swap it and why. After that, predict the objections they'll raise in the order they'll raise them — I can already hear 'isn't non-domination just expected non-interference with bigger numbers?' and 'doesn't positive liberty already cover self-mastery, so the third category is redundant?' — and tell me where in the sequence to head each one off. Finish with one question I can ask at the end that would show whether they actually got the distinction rather than just nodding along. And be explicit about what I should skip at this level; no reading list, the librarian always hands one out anyway.
