I am a student in an upper-level animal cognition course, and I have been turning over a question about C. Lloyd Morgan's canon that I cannot settle. The canon says: "In no case is an animal activity to be interpreted in terms of higher psychological processes if it can be fairly interpreted in terms of processes which stand lower in the scale of psychological evolution and development." I know the "scale" language is itself contested now, but I want to set that aside and think about how the canon applies to a case its author plainly did not have in mind.

Suppose a domestic dog lives in a house with a soundboard of about forty buttons, each of which plays a single recorded word when pressed. Through months of shaping, the dog has learned to press particular buttons to bring about particular outcomes: pressing "outside" opens the back door, pressing "toy" produces a toy, and so on. A research study logs every press with a timestamp. One evening the dog walks to the back door and stands there for half a minute while its owner, on a video call, does not respond. The dog then goes to the board and, within five seconds, presses "outside" and then "now." The two-button sequence has never been taught or rewarded; "now" appears in the logs mostly in isolation and often in contexts of apparent frustration, but its meaning is not established. The question is not whether the dog "has language" in an all-or-nothing sense, but what the canon requires the researchers to say about this episode. Does the canon even bite here, given that the button-pressing is mediated by a human-made device? If a lower reading is available—each press under stimulus control, the two presses chained by the immediate context, with no referential intent—does the canon oblige researchers to adopt it merely because it is available, or does "fairly interpreted" set a higher bar? And does it matter if a higher reading, on which the dog refers to the outside and requests that it happen now, also fits the logs? Argue this from the wording of the canon, from its evident purpose, and from what Morgan would most plausibly have said if shown these logs, and then say which approach should govern here and why.
