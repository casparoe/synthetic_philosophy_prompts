An article I read summarized the Malcolm–Dennett debate on dreams like this. Malcolm argued that dreams aren't experiences at all: a sleeping person cannot truly say they are asleep, and any state that deserves the name experience needs a subject who could in principle report it, so dream reports aren't reports of anything that happened during the night. Dennett accepts the negative conclusion but goes further. On his cassette theory the dream is never actually lived through during sleep; instead the brain loads a finished memory into place at the moment of waking, and the dreamer mistakes that memory for a record of an experience. So Malcolm and Dennett are supposed to land in the same spot: people describing their dreams are, strictly speaking, under an illusion or confabulating, and there is nothing it is like to dream.

I'm inclined to accept that dreams might not be experiences in the way waking perception is, but the jump to confabulation feels off to me. Which parts of this summary are accurate, where does it run Malcolm and Dennett together or overstate them, and would either of them actually say dream recollections are false memories?
