I am a novelist, and my training has made me preoccupied with point of view: the same events register differently inside different people, and part of that difference is a matter of what each person brings to the scene rather than what the scene supplies. Lately I have been drawn to a philosophical account of belief that seems to deny that any such difference can be rational — objective Bayesianism, and in particular the maximum entropy principle. As I understand it, the principle says that, once you have specified the constraints your evidence imposes, there is a uniquely rational prior: the probability distribution of maximum entropy — the standard measure of how uninformative a distribution is — subject to those constraints. I find this compelling because it promises that rational belief is a determinate and non-arbitrary procedure rather than a matter of temperament or taste.

But I have a firm intuition, earned from the inside of fiction, that some differences in prior belief are not errors. Two careful characters can face the same evidence and rationally give different weights to the same possibility because their histories have made different things salient to them; fiction would be flattened if we had to treat one of them as simply making a mistake. I also suspect I know where the defender of the principle will push back: each character's history is itself part of their evidence, so they are not really facing the same evidence after all. But that move seems to me either to save the principle by emptying it of its claim to determinacy, or to relocate the subjectivity into the choice of which constraints and which partition of possibilities count as "the evidence so far."

I do not want to be told which side wins. Give me the strongest case for each side, and then a negotiated answer: under what conditions should I debunk and drop the perspectival intuition; under what conditions should I amend or abandon the maximum entropy principle; and is there a reading on which objective priors and irreducible perspective are compatible? Tell me which move you would make and what would justify it.

Organize the response under these headings, in order, with these rough lengths: The case for the maximum-entropy prior (about 150 words); The case for the perspectival intuition (about 150 words); Where the subjectivity could hide (about 120 words); A negotiated verdict (about 150 words). Please write in American English.
