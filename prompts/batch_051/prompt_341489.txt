I have a policy about evaluating theories: I won't let a theory earn credit from evidence that was already known before the theory was proposed. If the theory couldn't have predicted it in advance—if it only tells a story that fits the data after the fact—I treat that as explanation, not confirmation. So when someone says general relativity gets support from the precession of Mercury, which had been known for decades before Einstein, I want to say that's backwards: the old data shaped the theory, so it can't also test it. He also points out that if I discount all old evidence, I basically can't confirm most historical theories, since their data was collected before the theory existed.
