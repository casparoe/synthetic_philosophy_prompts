I've been going back and forth with a housemate who's ended up a panpsychist, but his version is the specific one where my own experience is literally composed of millions of tiny experiences had by the micro-level parts of my brain. He calls this constitutive panpsychism: lots of micro-subjects adding up to one macro-subject, roughly like lots of voices adding up to a crowd's roar (his simile, not mine). I keep saying theres no observation that could tell us whether the parts of a brain each have a little bit of experience, or whether the whole thing is just built out of experience-free physical parts whose activity causes the one big experience. He says I'm wrong and that it's an empirical claim. So here's what I want you to do: design the strongest empirical test you can of the claim that macro-level consciousness is composed of micro-level experiences, as against the claim that it is merely caused by non-experiencing micro-level activity. Be concrete about what you'd measure, what result would push toward each view, and then be honest about what even the best-case result would leave unsettled. In particular I want to know whether any possible test could show that the little parts are themselves subjects, rather than only showing that they behave in ways our theory labels as experiential. Please write the answer in British English.
