Write a villanelle (the usual nineteen-line form, two refrains repeating in the standard order) about the counterfactual mugging. The scenario is the standard one: Omega has just flipped a fair coin; in the branch you actually observe, the coin came up tails and Omega asks you for $100, while if it had come up heads Omega would have given you $10,000, but only if Omega had predicted in advance that, upon seeing tails, you would hand over the $100. Omega's predictions are known to be accurate. The coin has already landed, so paying cannot causally reach the heads branch; and yet the policy that pays in the tails branch is exactly the policy that receives the $10,000 in the heads branch.

Let one refrain carry the causal-decision-theorist's protest that the coin has landed and paying now buys nothing, and let the other refrain carry the functional-decision-theoretic reply that paying in the tails branch just is what it means to be the procedure that was paid in the heads branch. Structure the poem so the two refrains pass each other the way the two branches of the coin flip do, and let the final two lines bring them together rather than resolve them. Leave the discomfort of the case intact: the causal point is not false, and the policy point does not dissolve it. Someone who knows the dispute should be able to tell which line is which and why they end up side by side.
