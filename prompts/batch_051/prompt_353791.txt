I retired from a career in physiology a few years back, and I've been reading philosophy of emotion lately. In a discussion group the other week someone defended the value of sadness and grief with an analogy I keep running into: negative emotions are like physical pain. Pain is unpleasant but it's a signal — tissue damage, infection, a joint about to give out — and people born without pain sensation are constantly injuring themselves because the warning never arrives. So, the argument runs, a life without sadness would be like a life without pain: it sounds nice but it's actually dangerous, because you'd stop registering what you've lost or that something in your life has gone wrong. The conclusion they wanted is that we shouldn't be eager to chemically or otherwise eliminate sadness, and that a life without it would probably be worse, not better.

I don't trust the analogy, but I want it audited rather than just knocked down. Give me the explicit mapping — what in sadness corresponds to the damaging stimulus, the nociceptors, the unpleasantness, the protective response, the healing — and then the places where it fails. My own suspicion is that pain issues fairly clear instructions (withdraw, protect the injured part, rest) where grief doesn't tell you anything actionable, and that pain signals an ongoing problem while grief is usually about something already lost and unfixable. I also half-remember a clinical phenomenon, pain asymbolia, where people say they feel pain but dont find it aversive — which might be a better test case for whether the unpleasantness is doing all the work. How far does the pain analogy carry, which disanalogies actually matter for the conclusion about eliminating sadness (and which are just decorative), and does the conclusion survive once they're on the table? And tell me what would change your verdict — what argument or empirical consideration would tip you the other way — and where you think your own audit is most likely to fail. If the pain analogy collapses, say whether some better analogy is doing the work, or whether the whole question is better settled without analogies.
