I've just been rereading Gould and Lewontin on spandrels and the adaptationist programme — I think the paper was late seventies, I can never remember — and it struck me that the whole thing would work beautifully as a deadpan corporate brochure. Could you write me a parody advert for a fictional consultancy, something like Panglossian Adaptation Solutions Ltd, that will supply an adaptive explanation for any trait a client names: same-day service for behaviour, a whole-organism optimisation premium package, a case-study section, a testimonials box and a small FAQ. Aim for about 650 words, in that bland upbeat register of a management-consultancy or tech-startup pitch.

The target is the habit of treating every current feature of an organism (or a society) as if natural selection had designed it for its present role. The satire should quietly land the usual objections — spandrels and by-products, developmental and architectural constraints, drift, allometry, exaptation being retconned after the fact, and the way the story can absorb any counter-evidence — without ever stating them outright or winking at the reader. I'll be using it in a first-year philosophy-of-biology tutorial, and I'd like it to be fair in the sense that someone who knows the actual critique recognises it and feels the firm is exaggerating real features of that research programme rather than a straw man.
