I'm on the policy committee for a forecasting platform where volunteers assign probabilities to political and economic events, and we've hit a recurring problem: two forecasters with similar track records will sit at .8 and .2 on the same question for weeks, and when they argue in the comment threads they just talk past each other. A faction on the committee wants a new rule: whenever two top-rated forecasters are more than 40 points apart, they have to produce a joint statement that lays out the evidence they both accept and pinpoints exactly where the divergence enters — enforced Aumann-style pooling, basically. Their pitch is that if Aumann's agreement theorem is right about how rational agents should update, then a platform that makes everything common knowledge should see real disagreements collapse, so we should force the common knowledge to actually happen. I'm skeptical, but I need to stand behind a recommendation at next week's meeting. Draft a short policy memo for the committee on whether to adopt the rule, a modified version, or none. Include criteria for when forced pooling is likely to produce genuine convergence rather than performative agreement, and be fair to the side you're recommending against — at least two members will read the memo looking for their view taken seriously.
