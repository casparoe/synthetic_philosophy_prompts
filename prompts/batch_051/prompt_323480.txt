I retired from physical chemistry a few years ago and fell into philosophy of religion, mostly because the question of proportional punishment in the afterlife kept bothering me in the same way a bad reaction mechanism does when the stoichiometry doesn't balance. The standard menu has three options and each one seems off: eternal conscious torment looks wildly disproportionate to any finite harm a finite creature can do; annihilationism avoids the infinite part but gives up on anything being rectified; universal salvation fixes proportionality but seems to make the amount of evil in a life not really matter in the end. A friend in my reading group proposed a fourth scheme and asked me to treat it as a mechanism rather than a theology, which is exactly how my instincts run. I want to know how it would actually behave.

Here is the proposal. At death, every person receives a precise harm score equal to the total subjective suffering they caused to other sentient beings, including culpable omissions and reasonably forseeable downstream effects. Instead of eternal torment or simple destruction, you then undergo exactly that much suffering yourself, not at an abstract exchange rate but from the inside: you experience, one by one, the worst moments of your victims as they actually felt them, with full awareness of how your choices produced them. Once the debt it paid, you join everyone else in whatever the normal afterlife is. There is one release valve: any victim can forgive part or all of a debt after death, and forgiveness cannot be faked because victims have direct access to whether the offender genuinely understands and regrets the harm—faked remorse is legible as faked. Total scores are deliberately not knowable during earthly life, even though the rules themselves are common knowledge.

Walk me through how this thing would actually run. What does it incentivize among the living: genuine moral change, obsessive risk-minimization, strategic passivity (never act, never harm), or something subtler? What does it do to the dead, especially victims who now hold creditor power—do they forgive quickly, hoard debts, trade them, or use them to control people? Where does the system get gamed first, and are any of the failure modes fixable without collapsing it back into one of the three standard views? I can already see one or two ways it goes wrong, but I'd like a systematic account before I bring it back to the group.
