Lately I have been reading philosophy of mind, and I keep circling David Chalmers's distinction between the hard problem of consciousness and what he calls the meta-problem. As I understand it, the hard problem asks why physical processing gives rise to conscious experience at all, while the meta-problem asks why we think, say, and report that there is a hard problem in the first place, why experience strikes us as so puzzling. Illusionists hold that a good solution to the meta-problem dissolves the hard problem; realists hold that the meta-problem is a separate, downstream matter that leaves the hard problem untouched.
