Here's something that's bugging me. In medicine, psychology, and now increasingly economics, the randomized controlled trial is treated as basically the gold standard for saying whether X causes Y. The logic seems to be: randomly split people into groups, intervene on one group while the other gets nothing or a placebo, and if the outcome Y differs between groups, you've caught X causing Y. But that practice looks like it has a whole theory of causation baked in: something like, causation is about being a difference-maker under an intervention, or being invariant under manipulation.

Then I start worrying about a circle. To define an "intervention" properly, don't you need causal language already? And running an RCT still requires assumptions — that the coin flip doesn't affect Y through some sneaky path, that blinding worked, that people don't know which arm thier in — which all sound awfully causal themselves.
