Question: Assess the claim, made most famously by Jared Diamond, that the shift from hunting and gathering to agriculture was the worst mistake in human history. Reconstruct the strongest version of the case that it was a mistake, then evaluate it against the strongest objections.

Student answer: Jared Diamond argues that switching from hunting and gathering to farming was a mistake because it made life worse on almost every measure that matters for ordinary people. Early farmers were shorter than the foragers they replaced, had more cavities and signs of malnutrition, and were exposed to more infectious disease because they lived in crowded settlements near livestock. Diamond also says hunter-gatherers only had to work a few hours a day to get food, while farmers had to work from dawn to dusk. Sahlins' idea of the original affluent society backs this up: he showed that groups like the Dobe !Kung met all their needs in about fifteen to twenty hours of work a week and had abundant food and free time. Agriculture also created inequality, because grain can be stored, and stored surplus lets a small group control the food supply; before farming there was no surplus to steal or tax, so societies were egalitarian. So the case is that agriculture made individuals poorer, sicker, and less free while making societies bigger and more stratified.

The strongest objections are that the transition was gradual and nobody chose it knowing the consequences, so calling it a mistake treats it like a deliberate bad decision; that the !Kung are just one group and their workweek doesn't prove every forager lived easily, since some foragers in harsh environments worked much harder; that foraging had its own downsides like violence and periodic hunger; and that farming made cities, writing, and technology possible, so even if early farmers suffered, the long-run payoff might justify it.
