Please write, for a reading group I am helping to organise, the list of requirements that an adequate theory of writing systems must meet. I come to this as a retired natural scientist who has only recently begun reading philosophy, so I want the same discipline I would have demanded in stating the explanatory targets of a theory in my own field: before we argue about which account is correct, be explicit about what any account must capture.

By a theory of writing systems I do not mean a historical account of how scripts were invented or deciphered. I mean a theory of what a script encodes: which units and features of a language a writing system can represent, which it must represent in order to function as a complete writing system, and which it may systematically ignore, underdetermine, or distort. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark it as either non-negotiable for any adequate theory or a preference of one camp — for example, the assumption that an alphabet approximating one grapheme per phoneme is the natural or optimal endpoint. Do not take the phoneme itself for granted: part of the task is to say what work that notion does and whether an adequate theory must posit it, or whether that is itself the alphabetic camp's preference.

I especially want the requirements that pull against one another, and your view on whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable for a single script. The obvious tension is phonological transparency (spell roughly as you speak) against morpheme constancy (keep a morpheme visually stable across phonologically different realisations, as in electric/electricity or sign/signature); a further tension is between faithfulness to the surface signal and the encoding of grammatical or lexical information that is not in that signal at all. Test the requirements against at least one shallow alphabetic orthography (such as Finnish), one deep one (such as English), and one non-alphabetic system (such as Chinese characters). Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases, not on scoring existing theories; do say which requirement does the most to exclude candidates.

Finally, say how confident you are in the overall list and single out the one requirement or claim you are least sure of, and why.
