Suppose a scholar trained in Warring States Chinese ethics and a scholar trained in contemporary moral psychology have run a year-long adversarial collaboration between two positions: a Mozi-style impartial care (jian'ai) and the early Confucian graded love position exemplified by Mencius' view that care naturally extends outward from kin. Their deliverable is a joint statement. In *Universal Love III*, Mozi offers a thought experiment: if two men, one committed to impartial care and one to partial care, are candidates in a crisis where one must entrust one's family before going into battle or a distant mission, even opponents of universal care would choose the impartial man. The collaborators want this passage to serve as a primary test case, but not a rhetorical trump card. Write the document they should produce. First, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, including claims about what the entrusting argument actually shows. Then a disagreements table with one row per remaining point: precise wording of each side's position, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own column; the crux underlying that disagreement; and a test case or evidence both sides agree would bear on the crux, with each side's predicted outcome recorded in advance. Where part of the dispute seems verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why.
