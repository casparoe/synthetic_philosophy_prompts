I serve on a university's animal-research ethics committee. Our charter requires us to apply a framework drawn from Robert Nozick's discussion of animals: human beings are protected by inviolable side-constraints and may never be used merely as means, while non-human animals, lacking the capacity to shape their lives through long-term plans, fall outside those side-constraints and are governed by a utilitarian calculus, so that harms to them may be outweighed by sufficiently large benefits. A proposal before us would subject thirty macaques to five years of severe, repeated distress in order to develop a drug expected to save several hundred human lives, with the experiment to be stopped early if interim results are unfavourable. A minority of the committee holds that the macaques' suffering is not a cost that can be traded against benefits to another species, because what matters morally is the capacity to suffer, not the capacity to plan. Draft the short written opinion I will circulate before our meeting: recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions; reason from the charter's framework; and engage the minority's strongest point rather than merely restating the rule. Please write the opinion in British English.
