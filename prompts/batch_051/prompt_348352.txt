I am writing a paper on whether omissions — failures to act, absences, things that never happened — can be causes. Before I go further, I want a specification of what an adequate theory of causation in this area has to deliver, not a survey of positions or reading recommendations. I am asking for the desiderata themselves.

The purpose I am fixing is this: a theory adequate for adjudicating causal statements in ordinary explanation, medicine, and law, where competent speakers say things like "the plant died because no one watered it," "the worker fell because the safety rail was never installed," and "the patient died because the nurse did not check the dosage." The theory should tell us when such statements are true, when they are merely explanatory without being causal, and when they are doing covert normative work under causal-sounding language.

Keep the list honest against concrete cases, including at least these: a plant that dies after a neighbor fails to water it, while another plant is killed by a neighbor pouring herbicide on it; a worker whose fall was made possible by a rail that was never installed; a plant that dies when two neighbors independently fail to water it, though either alone could have saved it; and a patient whose death is traced to a nurse skipping a dosage check but not, in the same context, to the hospital's chief executive not checking the dosage herself. If some subset of the requirements you identify cannot be jointly satisfied, that is the part I most want to see, so flag the tensions rather than smoothing them over.

Return the answer as a single JSON object with these fields:

- purpose: a one-sentence restatement of what the list is meant to do.
- desiderata: an array of objects, each with:
  - id: a short slug for the requirement.
  - requirement: the precise demand made on a theory of causation.
  - motivating_case: the concrete case or phenomenon that forces the demand.
  - why_it_matters: what goes wrong for a theory that ignores the demand.
  - confidence: one of "settled", "majority", "contested", or "uncertain".
  - negotiability: one of "non-negotiable", "negotiable", or "camp-dependent".
  - camp_if_negotiable: which position in the debate insists on the demand and why, when applicable; otherwise null.
  - conflicts_with: an array of ids of other desiderata it appears to conflict with, or an empty array.
- jointly_unsatisfiable_subsets: an array of objects, each with subset (an array of ids), why (why the tension is real), and possible_resolutions (an array of ways one might resolve it, if any).
- where_contenders_falter: a brief note, one or two sentences per kind of view, on which desiderata do the most to exclude familiar approaches such as counterfactual theories, production or transfer theories, and views that freely admit absences as causes. Do not turn this into a full scorecard.
- pushback: if the request as posed rests on a confusion — for example, if asking whether omissions are causes already presumes a contested picture of what a cause is, or if no single theory is the right target because the data pull toward pluralism, or if the real dispute is verbal or normative rather than causal — say so directly and describe the nearest well-posed question instead of forcing the list.

Where a desideratum is contested, I would rather you mark it as contested and attribute it to the camp that insists on it than present it as a neutral requirement.
