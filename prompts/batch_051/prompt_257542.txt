Suppose a cheap, pocket-sized device appears in 1985 that can produce photos, audio recordings, and eventually full-motion video of almost any person doing or saying basically anything, and the fakes are provably indistinguishable from genuine recordings by every forensic method available at the time. The trick leaks immediately and the thing is trivial to build from ordinary parts, so theres no keeping it out of circulation. Everything else about the world stays the same. I want your best argued prediction of how the next thirty years go for the role of recordings in law, journalism, and personal trust. Specifically: does the "epistemic backstop" function of recordings — the way a disputed claim about what happened can be checked against the record — get replaced by something, or does it just quietly erode, and if it erodes, what fills the gap? Walk the reasoning link by link, and for each major link say whether you're confident or guessing, and what would have to be true for the prediction to flip the other way.
