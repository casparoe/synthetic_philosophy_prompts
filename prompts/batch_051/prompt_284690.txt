I've been trying to fix my views about survival across uploading and gradual-replacement procedures, and my verdicts on cases are pulling in different directions. Here they are:

1. Slow in-place replacement. Over the course of a year, a team replaces each of my neurons, one at a time, with a synthetic neuron that exactly reproduces the removed neuron's input-output role and connections. I am awake throughout, working and remembering normally. Verdict: I survive; there is no moment at which I die.

2. Rapid replacement under anesthesia. The same end state is produced in a few seconds while I am unconscious. Verdict: I think it is still me afterward, but I would agree to this procedure much less readily than to (1), and I catch myself treating the speed as if it mattered to whether I survive.

3. Destructive upload. A full brain scan is taken, the biological brain is destroyed in the process, and a simulation of the scan is run on a computer. The simulation wakes up with all my memories and traits. Verdict: I do not make it into the machine; the original dies and the simulation is a copy.

4. Non-destructive upload. The scan is taken without destroying the brain, and both I and the simulation continue. Later my biological body dies. Verdict: my death is not survived by the copy; from the scan onward there are two people.

5. Interrupted gradual replacement. As in (1), but halfway through the entire hybrid brain is shut down for a day before being restarted and the replacement completed. Verdict: I survive the shutdown, so a gap in consciousness is not by itself death.

Is there one principle about what matters for surviving these procedures that captures all five verdicts, or nearly all of them? If there is, state it precisely, show how it handles each case, and tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to revise. If there is not, say which verdicts I should abandon and why. In either case, apply the resulting principle to a case I have not settled: a cell-by-cell migration in which each biological neuron is deactivated only after a matching synthetic neuron in a separate laboratory substrate is wired into the same networks through a high-bandwidth link, so that the pattern of activity relocates gradually from my skull to the substrate while the overall network continues to function.

Please keep your answer under 700 words.
