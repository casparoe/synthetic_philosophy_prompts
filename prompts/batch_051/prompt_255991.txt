I have been turning over an asymmetry in how people talk about two familiar economic policies. Rent control is routinely dismissed, often by the same people and often for two reasons at once: it is said to be economically self-defeating — hold a price below the level where supply meets demand and you get a shortage — and it is said to be an intrusion on the landlord's right to charge what the market will bear. A minimum wage is a price control of the same family, just set in the opposite direction: a floor rather than a ceiling. If the reasoning used against rent control is sound, a binding wage floor should create a surplus of labor, with more people seeking work at the mandated rate than employers are willing to hire. And if forcing a landlord to accept a lower price is an intrusion on ownership, then preventing a worker from offering a lower price is an intrusion of the same shape: it stops a person from selling what is most plainly their own. Yet the two policies are treated very differently. I want to know whether anything justifies that difference. Is there a principled distinction between labor and housing, or between floors and ceilings, that carries the moral weight people place on it? Or is the asymmetry just an inconsistency we live with? Say whether you would keep it, reverse it, or give it up, and on what grounds.
