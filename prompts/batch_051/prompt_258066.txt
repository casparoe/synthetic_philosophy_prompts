I run a vulnerability disclosure programme on a bug bounty platform. Any of our ~400 researchers can publish an advisory or proof-of-concept exploit whenever they like, and I can only guide and cajole; I can't actually stop them. Each individual publication looks reasonable on its own — real bug, vendor will probably fix it, reporter deserves credit — but when a dozen go up in the same week around related packages, the vendors get overwhelmed and attackers get a free exploit feed. I've read about the unilateralist's curse and it seems to have exactly this shape: mostly sensible people each doing what looks right from where they stand, and the group ending up with something nearly all of them would have voted down if they'd known. I need it turned into a proceedure I can circulate to researchers with no philosophy background and about ten minutes before they hit publish. The procedure should give the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where it says publish anyway, where it says stop, and where it hands the decision to a person because it has become a values call rather than a factual one. Flag the steps where judgement is being smuggled into a checkbox. Then run it on three cases: a single critical RCE in a widely used library; five similar SQL injection reports across different small plugins in the same week; and a case where the procedure tells me to release something I'd personally rather sit on while we wait for a patch. Write the whole thing in British English.
