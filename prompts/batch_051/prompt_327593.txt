Construct a thought experiment in which a retributivist about punishment is committed to imposing a harsher sanction than an act consequentialist would impose on the same person for the same wrong. The case should reverse the usual worry that consequentialism is the ruthless view because it can punish the innocent or over-punish for the sake of deterrence. Constraints: set it in an ordinary institution (a workplace, a school, a sports league, or the like); no serious violence; by the time of the decision the wrongdoer is genuinely reformed and poses no ongoing risk; and the retributivist's harshness must fall out of the backward-looking logic of desert—that past wrongdoing makes censure fitting, and that fitting censure is a sufficient reason to punish even when no future good is likely—rather than from an obviously fanatical version of retributivism. After laying out the scenario, show what each theory says and why, identify the structural feature of the case that reverses the usual ordering, and say how a defender of the theory that ends up looking ruthless would attack the case.
