I'm teaching an upper-level undergrad seminar called Minds, Machines, and Meaning, about 18 students and one 80-minute session, and I want them to actually feel the difference between Dennett's three stances instead of just having me name them. We've read the relevant chapter, but the terms sit flat. What I'd love is a structured in-class game where students have to predict behavior from inside one of the stances and discover, through the scoring, that each stance wins sometimes and loses sometimes, and that "which stance is right?" starts to feel like the wrong question. No lecture up front, and I'd rather not name the stances until the debrief: call them predictive strategies A, B, and C or something similar. I want a scoring system that rewards accuracy and penalizes explanation length, so the idea that the intentional stance works partly as a compression trick shows up in the points rather than in my slides.

Practical constraints: no code, no actual computers beyond maybe a projector, and everything should be printable or doable on a whiteboard. Please give me four target systems, including a vending machine or thermostat, a chess engine or poker bot, a person in a described social situation, and at least one broken or malfunctioning thing. For each system, list the specific situations the teams will predict, the actual outcomes, and which strategy you expect to profit most. Give me a timeline for the 80 minutes, role assignments, and a debrief plan that maps what happened onto the real patterns idea: that the patterns picked out from the intentional stance are real in virtue of their predictive power and compressibility rather than being merely useful fictions, and that the stances are fallible strategies that can break down and be repaired rather than rival ontologies. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (I suspect one team will start explaining instead of predicting, or the scoring will let a lucky guess dominate), and be honest about which part of the real-patterns view the game can't reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself in discussion.
