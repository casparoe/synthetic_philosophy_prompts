I’m teaching a seminar on the philosophy of revelation this term, and a student from a King James-only background got the rest of us arguing about whether a translation can be inspired at all. Her church holds that the King James Version isn’t just an accurate rendering of the original languages but is itself the inspired word of God in English, so that corrections made from the Greek or Hebrew are treated as a kind of attack. The same people wouldn’t say that about, say, a modern committee translation or a loose paraphrase. Yet the New Testament writers themselves quote the Septuagint, a Greek translation of the Hebrew scriptures made centuries before Jesus, and treat it as scripture even at places where it clearly departs from the Hebrew. So the tradition seems to have been comfortable with at least one translation being inspired.

I want a working definition of “inspired” as applied to a text, one that get’s these cases right or at least takes a defensible stand on each of them: (a) the Hebrew and Greek texts as they left their original authors’ hands; (b) the Septuagint, which is a translation and sometimes a loose one, but which the New Testament authors quote as authoritative; (c) the King James Version, which a whole movement regards as inspired English scripture; (d) a loose dynamic-equivalence paraphrase of the sort sold in Christian bookstores, which renders idioms freely and makes no claim to word-for-word fidelity; (e) a translation made from another translation rather than from the original languages, like an early English Bible done from the Latin Vulgate; and (f) a modern committee translation that prints footnotes saying the Hebrew is uncertain and then simply picks one reading. My own gut says (b) counts and (c) is crazy, and I have no idea what to do with (d). I’m not asking about which books belong in the canon; I want the concept itself. Give me the definition, run it through all six, and tell me where it forces you to say something surprising—for instance, that two different translations of the same verse could both be inspired, or that none of them are because inspiration attaches to something other than wording.
