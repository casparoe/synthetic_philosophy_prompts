I keep coming back to this passage from David Hume's An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding, Section X (Of Miracles), Part I:

"It will be sufficient to observe, that our assurance in any argument of this kind is derived from no other principle than our observation of the veracity of human testimony, and of the usual conformity of facts to the reports of witnesses."

Now think about deepfakes. Hume says trust in testimony is inductive: we trust reports because they have usually matched facts. Video and audio have historically done a lot of that work because they were hard to fabricate. Deepfakes mess that up, since one kind of report can now be made to say pretty much anything. Some people talk about needing an "epistemic backstop" to keep the whole testimonial system from collapsing. I want you to rank six possible backstops by how well they'd actually restore the kind of assurance Hume is describing.

The candidates I'm thinking of:

- Cryptographic provenance (cameras sign captures at the moment of recording; platforms verify the signature)
- AI-based deepfake detection (models that flag synthetic artifacts)
- Legal penalties and stricter chain-of-custody rules for evidence
- Institutional attestation (notaries, official records, authenticated feeds)
- Reputation systems (verified accounts, trust scores)
- Mandatory labeling of synthetic media

Please format the response like this:

1. Bottom line (1–2 sentences): which backstops, if any, actually restore induction rather than just offload trust to an authority.

2. Tier list (3 tiers, one sentence per item): sort the six candidates into "does most", "does something", and "does least" by Hume's criterion.

3. Discussion (200–300 words): whether any backstop can get us back to a world where testimony, including video, is again a regularity we observe, or whether we'd just be replacing one kind of fragile trust with another. If the ranking would change in a different epistemic context, say how.

I keep worrying this is too neat. Like, do we actually want to go back to trusting video, or should the backstop aim to get us out of the video-trust business entirely? But answer the ranking by Hume's criterion anyway, and just note if you think Hume's criterion is the wrong target.
