So I got into a long argument last night with a friend who does philosophy, about whether saying I promise to come is more like reporting that I promise or more like the act of promising itself. He kept calling it a performative, not a description, and his big claim was that under the right conditions the words don't describe the promise, they make it, and that even a plain statement of fact is doing something more than reporting. I'm a backend engineer and I think in terms of read-only queries versus writes, pure functions versus side-effecting calls, return values versus exceptions, preconditions, and whether a transaction commits or rolls back. I tried to translate what he was saying into that vocabulary and it half-worked, which is why I'm writing this.

What I want is a careful, honest translation. Take the speech-act picture roughly as he gave it to me: constatives state facts and can be true or false, while performatives do something in the world just by being uttered when the surrounding conditions hold, the right speaker, the right setting, the right authority, the participants understanding the situation in the right way. If a performative's conditions fail it isn't false, it's void or misfired; it never takes effect, which to me sounds less like returning false and more like throwing an exception or a transaction that fails to commit. Then there's the later twist he kept pushing, which is that even the fact-stating ones have a kind of force, so the constative/performative split starts to get soft. Restate all of that in the vocabulary I'm fluent in, piece by piece: the utterance, the force it carries, the conditions that have to hold for it to succeed, the way it can fail without being false, the way context and authority matter. For each piece say what translates cleanly, what you had to fudge to make it fit, and what has no counterpart at all. Then the part I actually care about: after the translation, is there anything left in his picture that my vocabulary just can't express, and if so is that because my vocabulary is missing something real or because the distinction is less sharp than he thinks. Keep it honest; if the translation starts doing the philosophy for you, tell me where.
