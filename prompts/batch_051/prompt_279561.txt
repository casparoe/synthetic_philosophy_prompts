One of my closest friends, Marit, grew up in Utrecht, spent a decade working around Osaka, and has developed a whole private jurisprudence about when bluntness is a virtue and when a white lie is the more decent move. I've been keeping a mental file of her verdicts, and now I want to predict one before it happens. File so far: a Dutch manager told a new employee, in week one, "your report was disorganized and needs to be redone from scratch"; Marit said he'd done her a favor, and the only thing she'd have condemned is saying nothing and then grumbling about the report later. A guest at a dinner in Osaka, asked by the host "is the natto all right?", answered "oishii!" while visibly struggling to swallow; Marit approved and said the host was asking whether the evening was going well, not taking a poll on the natto, and that answering "I hate it" would be childish, not honest. A neighbor gave a lost tourist with a suitcase a cheery "the station is ten minutes that way" when he knew it was thirty-five, and the tourist missed the last train; Marit called it a real lie and said the man should be ashamed. And a palliative care nurse told a dying patient "of course you'll be home by summer" purely to keep their spirits up; Marit called it wrong and faintly paternalistic, even though she also thought nobody should have dumped the full prognosis on the patient that day. Thats the file.

This week she'll have dinner with me and an oncologist friend who will mention that he told a patient whose scans were unchanged and whose jaundice is visably no better, "you look much better today." The patient had only asked whether they looked less ill before a daughter's wedding, and no treatment or practical decision rides on the answer; it was purely to make the patient less self-conscious in the photographs. I can honestly see Marit going either way. Predict her verdict, and tell me what underlying rule about directness, tatemae, and white lies she seems to be running, which other rule or rules fit the file just as well, what each of those rules says about the oncologist, and what single further case would cleanly settle which rule she actually holds.
