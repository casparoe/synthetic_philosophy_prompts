I have been reading my way through the debate about whether a political community has a duty to remember historical atrocities, and the case for forgetting keeps leaning on an analogy I do not know how to assess. In the form I encounter it most often, the analogy is this: a community's memory is like an individual's memory. An individual who cannot forget an old injury stays trapped in grievance, reawakens the pain each time, and eventually loses the ability to act well in the present; by extension, a polity that keeps commemorating old crimes traps itself in grievance and makes reconciliation impossible. The conclusion drawn is that societies sometimes do better by deliberately forgetting: quieting commemorations, withdrawing certain events from civic education, or declining to make shared memory a precondition for moving forward. I want an audit of how far this analogy can actually carry that conclusion, keeping in view the duty-to-remember position associated with Avishai Margalit and the case-for-forgetting position associated with David Rieff.
