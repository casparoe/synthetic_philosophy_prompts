In my reporting I keep returning to a structural analogy I cannot yet evaluate. Climatology distinguishes weather—the local, episodic event—from climate—the long-run statistical regime that constrains, and is slowly altered by, those events. Fernand Braudel's historiography seems to make the same cut: the historical event (a battle, a treaty, an individual's decision) is weather-like, while the longue durée (geography, climate, material and demographic structures) is climate-like, with the conjoncture of economic cycles and price trends as something like the intermediate scale. This may be a substantive claim about how both systems are organized across timescales, or it may be a loose metaphor doing no explanatory work. Please assess the analogy precisely: state the intended mapping, say what would have to be true on both sides for it to be deep rather than merely decorative, and name at least one thing the analogy predicts that turns out false—or explain why no obvious falsity follows. Also tell me what would change your verdict, which consideration would move you from deep to superficial or back, and where your assessment is most likely to fail.
