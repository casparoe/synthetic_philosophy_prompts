I've been turning over the following argument against the possibility of laws of history, and I cannot tell whether it is sound or merely tidy:

(1) If there were laws of historical development, those laws, together with a full description of present conditions, would in principle allow the future course of history to be predicted.
(2) The future course of history cannot be predicted even in principle, because history is strongly shaped by the growth of knowledge — future discoveries, inventions, and theories — and the growth of knowledge cannot itself be predicted. To predict the content of a future discovery would already be, in the relevant respect, to have made it.
(3) A "law" that cannot in principle support such a prediction is not a law of history; at most it retrodicts or labels a trend after the fact.
(4) Therefore, there are no laws of history.
