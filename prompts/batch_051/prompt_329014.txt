Suppose someone wanted an account of why mathematics settles on the definitions it does — why continuity is today a condition on preimages of open sets rather than a statement about epsilons and deltas, and why a group is an abstract structure satisfying four axioms rather than a system of permutations closed under composition. Before comparing any such accounts, I want a specification of what an adequate account must deliver. Write the desiderata: which cases it must classify correctly, which earlier definitions it must explain as special cases rather than discard, which theorems and generalizations it must make natural, which distinctions it must preserve, and which of these requirements are non-negotiable rather than matters of taste. For each desideratum, give the motivating case or consideration; flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Then, briefly, score how the epsilon-delta definition of continuity, the open-set definition of continuity, and the permutation-based conception of groups do against the list.
