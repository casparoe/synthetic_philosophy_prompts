I keep flip-flopping on a debate in cognitive science about whether mental images are actual pictures in the head or something more like structured descriptions that just feel pictorial. The picture side says that when I imagine my route to work, the representation has real spatial properties — distances, angles, relative sizes — that I can inspect and read off. The other side says no: the underlying format is language-like or propositional, and the spatial feel is an epiphenomenon. People have done experiments where the time it takes to mentally scan between two landmarks on an imagined map scales with the distance between them, and where judging whether two rotated shapes are the same takes longer for bigger rotations. The picture crowd treat these as evidence for their view. But it seems to me a sufficiently rich description plus the right inference rules could mimic all of that. So here's what I want: design the best test you can of the claim that mental images are depictive in some substantive sense, not merely that the person has spatial information available. You can test humans, a model, whatever. Tell me what specific result would push you toward the depictive claim, what result would push you away, and what such a test could never settle even in principle. Also pin down what 'depictive' would have to mean for the test to be about anything, because I suspect that term is doing a lot of quiet work in this debate.
