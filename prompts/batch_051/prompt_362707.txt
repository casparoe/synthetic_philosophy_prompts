I'm a retired experimental chemist, and I spent thirty years watching disagreements get settled by running the right experiment; in the last couple years I've gotten into philosophy of mind and cognitive science. The mental imagery debate between Kosslyn and Pylyshyn has been runing since the 1970s and, as far as I can tell, neither side has moved. Kosslyn's side treats images as depictive, laid out spatially in some functional sense, which is supposed to explain why mental rotation and scanning times track spatial distances and angles. Pylyshyn's side says the code is propositional, and the scanning and rotation effects come from tacit knowledge and task demands, not from a picture-like format. More recent retinotopic activation and patient data get cited by one side as evidence and by the other as mere implementation.

My working theory is that the two sides are really answering different questions under one name, and that 'depictive' may be doing a lot of silent work. But it might equally be a genuine empirical disagreement that's just never had the right experiment.
