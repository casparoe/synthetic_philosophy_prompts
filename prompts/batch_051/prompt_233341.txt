I'm writing a piece on the platform economy and the old idea of "suitable work," and there's a clause in our unemployment code I keep coming back to. It reads: "A claimant must be available for and actively seeking work, and must not refuse any offer of paid work which is suitable to the claimant's skills and circumstances." Straightforward enough when it was written; it predates the platfrom economy and was drafted when "work" meant showing up somewhere at a fixed time. But now I'm looking at a case from a work coach's files. A woman on unemployment support was referred to a microwork platform that pays a few cents per task for tagging images for an AI company. There's no schedule and no guaranteed hours; she has to stay logged in from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. and accept each task within 30 seconds of the notification, or her account gets deactivated. The benefits office treats deactivation as her having made herself "unavailable for work"—a sanctionable refusal. While she's logged in she may sit unpaid for hours with nothing arriving, since she's paid only for completed tasks. Does she satisfy the "available for and actively seeking work" condition while she's logged in? If she logs off because the effective hourly wage is below the minimum wage, has she refused an offer of "suitable paid work"? Walk me through what the text, the purpose of the clause, a reasonable claimant reading, and what the people who drafted the clause would have said if shown the app would each conclude, and then say which approach should govern and why.
