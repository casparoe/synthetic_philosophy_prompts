For a seminar exercise I am assembling on moral conflict, I want the actual document an adversarial collaboration would produce, not a survey or adjudication of the debate. The two parties are: (1) a defender of genuine moral dilemmas, who holds that there can be situations in which, no matter what the agent does, she will have violated a real moral requirement, so that there is sometimes no permissible option; and (2) a denier of genuine dilemmas, who holds that every apparent dilemma is a conflict between pro tanto duties (considerations with real weight that can be outweighed) and that such conflicts always resolve into a unique all-things-considered ought, however terrible the residue.

Draft the joint statement these two could sign after working through the relevant cases: conflicting promises of roughly equal seriousness, a commitment that becomes impossible to keep, and a lesser-evil choice that nonetheless leaves the agent feeling, and being treated by others, as though she has done something wrong. The statement should include: (a) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, made precise enough not to be vacuous, covering what they jointly acknowledge about the existence of hard choices, about moral remainders (the continued normative force of an overridden or violated requirement), about what agents actually experience in these cases, and about what the question "Are genuine moral dilemmas possible?" is and is not asking; (b) a disagreements table in which each row states one contested issue in neutral wording acceptable to both, gives the pro-dilemma formulation, gives the anti-dilemma formulation, and classifies that disagreement as substantive or merely verbal; and (c) a section on cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one party. Good candidate cruxes include whether ought distributes over conjunction (if one ought to do A and ought to do B, does it follow that one ought to do A-and-B), whether two requirements can have exactly equal weight so that neither defeats the other, and whether the phenomenology of agent-regret is best explained by a genuine conflict of duties or by something else. For each crux, record both sides' predictions and the kind of case or argument both would accept as bearing on it.

The part I most want done carefully is the substantive-or-verbal classification, including the possibility that part of this debate dissolves once ought and duty are given a fixed logic. If that is right, say exactly which disagreements disappear under shared definitions and which survive them. Also have each side state, in wording it would accept, what would count as evidence or argument against its own view, and indicate whether the other side agrees that such evidence would move the debate.
