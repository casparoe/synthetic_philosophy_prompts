I retired from chemical engineering a few years ago and have been working through philosophy on my own since, mostly metaethics and some recent machine-ethics material. For a reading group I need to write down my philosophical profile on indirect normativity — roughly, the idea that rather than giving an AI a list of values, you build it to figure out what you would endorse after ideal deliberation — and I'd like an audit of the set before I present it. Please act as a professional philosopher working on metaethics and machine ethics, think carefully, and brainstorm a bit before you commit to where the real fault lines are.

Here are my commitments:

(a) I'm a constructivist anti-realist: there are no mind-independent moral facts, but normativity is not whim either — it is constituted by what we would endorse under suitably idealized conditions.

(b) So I take the indirect route for machines: the target is what I would endorse after ideal deliberation — full information, logical coherence, vivid imaginative acquaintance with the options, absence of obvious bias — not what my present self reports it wants and not a fixed list of values.

(c) But I also think there is no value-neutral specification of ideal deliberation. Any concrete procedure has to settle who deliberates, with what information, for how long, which biases get corrected, and when the deliberation counts as finished; those choices already smuggle in evaluative commitments.

(d) Still, I think a well-built system should be corrigible: actual, present, non-idealized people should be able to stop, override, or redirect it, even when the system's model says their idealized selves would disagree.

(e) It's partly a temperment thing: I'm suspicious of confidence about all this. The system should be epistemically humble about its own value model, keep options open, prefer reversible interventions, and not silently lock in one picture of what my ideal self would want.

Does that package hang together? I genuinely can't tell where the load-bearing problem is. Go through the commitments pairwise or however makes most sense, rank any tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, say which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection and which don't, and tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole thing coherent and what it would cost. I'd rather have a few sharp fault lines than a pile of possible quibbles.
