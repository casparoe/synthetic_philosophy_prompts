I work as a software engineer for a missions organization, and I keep running into a problem I don't have the conceptual tools to settle. Our statement of faith contains this sentence: "The Scriptures in their original languages are inspired by God and without error, and the King James Version alone is authorized for public reading and for instruction." It was adopted long before real-time machine interpretation existed. We have now built a speech-to-speech tool that takes an English KJV passage and renders it aloud in a language that has no written form; the system runs faster than the operator can follow, though the operator can pause and revise the wording when something is plainly wrong. The organization wants to use this tool during public worship, with the audio output standing in place of Scripture reading. Does the audio output count as "the King James Version" for purposes of the clause? Does it count as "the Scriptures" at all under the sentence? I can't tell whether it is a translation of the KJV, a derivative report of the original-language text, or something else. I care less about the practical answer than about what each approach to interpretation—the plain wording, the purpose of the clause, what the people who adopted it would probably have said if asked, and what a reasonable reader of the statement would expect—actually requires here, and which approach should govern when they pull apart.
