I'm on parental leave with a three-year-old who's in the phase of pointing at everything and demanding a name for it, and it's made me realize I have no idea what theory of categories I actually operate with. I'll tell her no, that's not a bird, it's a picture of a bird, and an hour later say yes, that's a chair about something nobody could sit on, and I can't tell you the rule for either. I've been keeping a list of my own gut calls, and I want to know what principle or small set of principles produces them. I know the rough options are prototypes, exemplars, and the theory theory, and none of them seems to get the whole list by itself. If the answer is pluralism, fine, but show me why it isn't just an unprincipled mess.

Here are my verdicts:

1. A penguin is definitely a bird, but a robin is a better bird. If I had to teach her the word, I'd point to a robin, not a penguin.
2. A chick that hatches from a robin's egg with some mutation that leaves it featherless, flightless, and looking like a little pink lizard is still a bird, no question.
3. A hand-carved, painted wooden robin is not a bird; it's a carving of one. It looks way more like a robin than the penguin or the mutant chick, and it still isn't one.
4. A robin in a videogame that builds nests, sings, and feeds its young is not a bird either. It's a simulation. It's doing all the bird things, and it isn't one.
5. A chair bolted to a gallery wall at chest height as part of an installation is not a chair anymore. It's sculpture.
6. A broken chair in my garage that can't hold anyone is still a chair, just a broken one.

I can't for the life of me explain why five and six come apart; they look like the same kind of case to me, and they get opposite verdicts.

So: what is the cleanest principle, or smallest set of principles, that captures these calls? Which of my verdicts does it fail to capture, and would you throw out that verdict or keep a messier principle? What does it predict about a case I haven't listed, say a lifelike robot robin that builds nests and feeds its young, or whatever case you think would tell the theories apart best? And if the whole question is badly framed, maybe I'm asking about my willingness to use words when what I actually have is several different projects, tell me that instead of handing me a tidy answer I can't use; I'd rather have the framing fixed than the verdicts flattered.
