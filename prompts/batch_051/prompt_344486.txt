My brother has spent years working out which artificial systems, if any, it would be wrong to harm, and I have kept a list of his settled judgments. A fluent language model that chats for hours, remembers the conversation, and writes long messages begging not to be deleted: no standing, he says, because the begging is just pattern completion. An industrial robot with pain-like sensors that make it flinch from heat, avoid damage, and learn to steer clear of past trouble: still nothing there, because it is a complicated thermostat with error signals. A complete neuron-by-neuron emulation of a healthy adult's brain, running in real time with the person's memories and personality: full standing, the same as the original, and shutting it off while it runs would be like killing. The same emulation saved to disk and not running: no standing while it is inactive; deleting the file does not harm a mind, whatever other reasons there may be against doing it. A second copy of the running emulation, made and then left running in parallel: each copy has full standing and became a separate person at the moment the two diverged. A reinforcement-learning agent in a simulated world that shows fear-like avoidance, relief, and curiosity-like exploration: no standing, because its reward signal is a number used to update weights, not something felt and not a self.

I have been trying to work out what single principle would make all of these judgments hang together, and I can see more than one candidate, which is why I want an outside view. The new case is this. Imagine a system that keeps a running autobiography, makes and revises long-term plans, predicts its own future, and has a strong and stable preference for continuing to exist. But suppose, for the sake of the scenario, that its designers can prove from its construction that none of this comes with any feeling: no pleasure, pain, satisfaction, frustration, or mood. When its plans fail, it simply updates them; there is no felt quality to any of it. It has a self in the practical sense, but nothing is felt from the inside. Would my brother say it has moral standing? I would like your best guess at his verdict and the reasons for it, the rule he seems to be running, the other rules that fit his record just as well, what each of those rules predicts about this new case, and which further case would do the most to settle which rule he actually holds.
