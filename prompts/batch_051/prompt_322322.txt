ok so in my school's philosophy club we spent the last few weeks on the münchhausen trilemma, the idea that if you keep asking how do you know that about the reasons for your beliefs, you either get an infinite regress, a circle, or you stop somewhere and just assert something. i've ended up with a cluster of views about it and i want someone to tell me wether they actually hang together, because a friend said i'm contradicting myself but wouldn't say where. here's roughly the set:

(a) the trilemma is right and exhaustive — those really are the only three options when you try to justify anything.
(b) foundationalism is just dogmatism with nicer packaging. the so-called basic beliefs it stops at are still arbitrary, no matter how self-evident their supposed to be.
(c) so the least bad option is circularity. i'm basically a coherentist: a belief is justified by how well it hangs together with everything else i believe, and a big enough circle of mutual support isn't automatically vicious.
(d) but i also think a perfectly consistent fairy tale wouldn't be justified. so coherence can't be the whole story, and experience or the world has to constrain the web of beliefs somehow.
(e) and i still want to say science is rational even though justifying induction seems to require using induction, which looks circular.

what i'd like is an audit of the whole set. which of these are genuinely in tension with each other, which only look like they are, and how bad are the tensions really? if something has to go, what's the cheapest thing to give up and what does it cost the rest of the view? please rank the tensions by severity rather than telling me everything is equally problematic.
