I'm a backend engineer whose native vocabulary is event-sourced systems: append-only logs, writes and read-side projections, materialized views, transaction isolation levels, deterministic replay of committed history, snapshots. I want to check whether my habitual translation of Boethius's solution to the problem of divine foreknowledge is faithful or a distortion.

The position as I understand it: God does not foreknow future free acts as future. God is eternal in an atemporal sense, not merely everlasting within time, so the whole course of events is present to God at once; calling this foreknowledge is a misnomer. God's seeing an act does not impose necessity on it. An act is conditionally necessary — given that it occurs, or given that it is seen as occurring, it cannot fail to occur — but it is not simply necessary in its own nature, so the freedom of the will is preserved.

Restate that position in the vocabulary of event-sourced systems, assigning each element — divine cognition, the eternal present, the distinction between conditional and simple necessity, the free will of creatures — to the thing in my world it most faithfully corresponds to. Then give me the residue report: what translates cleanly, what had to be fudged, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. My own best attempt is that God is like a read-only observer executing outside the commit timeline, scanning the entire log at once, whose reads never cause the writes. But that already seems to fix every future event in the log in a way Boethius wants to deny. Is the residue a defect in my vocabulary, or a sign that Boethius's solution is doing less work than it appears to?
