I've tied myself in knots with an argument a flatmate showed me, and I need a proper diagnosis rather than reassurance. It seems to prove, from special relativity alone, that none of my future is open. Unless I've garbled it, it runs like this.

(1) In my rest frame, there are events happening 'now' in far-off galaxies, and those events are as real as the events happening here.
(2) Take any event in my future, say my deciding tomorrow whether to accept a job. Pick a galaxy far enough away that, in my frame, there's an event there happening now. Because the galaxy is hugely distant in space and my decision is only a day away in time, that galaxy-event and my future decision are spacelike separated.
(3) Relativity of simultaneity: whenever two events are spacelike separated, there's an inertial frame in which they're simultaneous. So in some legitimate frame, my decision tomorrow is simultaneous with that galaxy-event.
(4) Reality isn't frame-relative. If an event is simultaneous, in some legitimate frame, with an event we've already accepted as real, then it's real too. So my decision tomorrow is already real.
(5) If my decision tomorrow is already real, then it's already settled; I can't make it not happen, and deliberating about it is play-acting.

And you can re-run this for every future event. There must be a slide from physics to fatalism in here somwhere, but I can't find it. I'd like to keep the special relativity and throw away the fatalism. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Is there a weaker conclusion about the future being 'already there' that survives even if the fatalism doesn't?
