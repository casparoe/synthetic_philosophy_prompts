i'm trying to get clear on this stretch of the White Horse Dialogue (the Gongsun Longzi chapter). Here's the passage:

"Can 'a white horse is not a horse' be asserted?"
[Advocate] "It can."
[Objector] "How so?"
[Advocate] "'Horse' is what designates shape. 'White' is what designates color. What designates color is not what designates shape. Therefore I say: a white horse is not a horse."
[Objector] "If there is a white horse, one cannot say there is no horse. If one cannot say there is no horse, does that not mean there is a horse? Since having a white horse counts as having a horse, how can the white [one] not be a horse?"
[Advocate] "If one seeks a horse, yellow and black horses may all be delivered. If one seeks a white horse, yellow and black horses may not be delivered. Suppose a white horse were [just] a horse. Then what is sought [in the two cases] would be the same. If what is sought were the same, then 'white' would not differ from 'horse.' If what is sought does not differ, how is it that yellow and black horses are acceptable [in one case] and unacceptable [in the other]? That acceptable and unacceptable are mutually opposed is clear. Therefore yellow and black horses are the same in that they can answer to 'there is a horse,' but cannot answer to 'there is a white horse.' This is a white horse's not being a horse: it is certain."

So the part I keep getting stuck on is the relationship between the two moves. The advocate opens with a point about designation (shape vs. color), and then lands on this "seeking a horse vs. seeking a white horse" example, which seems to be about what can be delivered in each case. Could you walk me through the exact kind of "not" the advocate needs in "a white horse is not a horse" for the conclusion to come out true? Like, is he denying that the particular white horse is in the class of horses, denying that the two descriptions pick out the same thing, or something more like a point about reference-fixing? And then: does the seek-a-horse example actually get him to that reading, or does it only show something weaker? Also, the objector's reply ("if there is a white horse, one cannot say there is no horse...") seems to be just reasserting the ordinary view, but i'm not sure whether it's a flat-out miss or whether it's putting pressure on the advocate's first move. I want to untangle it strictly from the text, not from what later interpreters said.
