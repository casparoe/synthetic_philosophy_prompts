so i've been going back and forth on this and want to know if i'm pattern-matching or onto something real. In psych we talked about how struggling for a thing can make people value it more — like the effort isn't just the price you pay for the result, it can be part of what makes the result worth it. Then in computer science we covered proof-of-work in bitcoin: miners burn huge amounts of energy solving otherwise useless math, and that wasted effort is exactly what makes the ledger hard to fake. The parallel that hit me is that both treat costly, deliberate effort as something that creates value or trust on its own, not just as an annoying step you'd skip if you could. But the differences bug me too. Bitcoin's work is public and checkable; nobody can verify whether i really tried at guitar or genuinely struggled on an essay, and we also think some effort is just dumb (grinding in a game only to say you did it, or sunk costs). So I can't decide wether this is a deep structural similarity or just two things connected by the word "effort." If it is deep, what would have to be true about both sides for ideas to actually transfer between them? And what's a prediction the analogy would make that turns out false?
