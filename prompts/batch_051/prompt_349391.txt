I keep running into the claim that a hypocrite loses her standing to blame, even when what she says is accurate: she can describe exactly what you did and why it was wrong, and supposedly there is still something defective about her blame. I don't see what work "standing" is doing here. It can't be about truth, because her own track record doesn't make the content false. It also can't just be about sincerity — plenty of self-loathing or reformed hypocrites mean every word. So isn't this just a dressed-up ad hominem, a way to let guilty people dismiss true criticism? What exactly is the mechanism by which a speaker's history gets into the normative assessment of her speech act, and is there one unified phenomenon here or several distinct ones?
