Consider a perfectly ordinary future-contingent sentence: "There will be a sea battle tomorrow," uttered now. The claim I want your credence on is the open-future thesis: such a sentence is neither true nor false at the moment of utterance — there is as yet no fact of the matter — and it first becomes true or false only when the relevant event is settled by occurring or failing to occur. I put my own credence in that thesis at roughly 0.65; my colleague puts hers at roughly 0.25.
