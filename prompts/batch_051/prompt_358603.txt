Before I retired I worked as a physical chemist, and much of my research involved solving nonlinear problems by self-consistent iteration: one proposes a trial solution, computes the quantities that follow from it, uses those quantities to construct an improved solution, and repeats until input and output no longer change — a fixed point. Having since come to philosophy, I was struck by how closely this resembles Rawls's method of reflective equilibrium. There one begins with provisional principles and particular considered judgements, lets each correct the other, and revises until the two are brought into coherence; the resulting state is not grounded in anything more fundamental but is taken to be justified by the mutual adjustment itself.

I cannot decide whether this is a genuine identity of structure or only a loose analogy. On the one hand, both procedures are iterative, both aim at internal consistency rather than an independent foundation, and both treat the endpoint as vindicated by the very fact of equilibrium. On the other hand, the scientific case has features that may have no ethical counterpart, and perhaps that is instructive. In a self-consistent calculation the updating rule is fully specified and the governing equations are fixed in advance by the physics; convergence is not guaranteed; and when several fixed points exist, the one reached may depend on the starting guess. Does reflective equilibrium likewise permit failure to converge, or several rival equilibria reached from different starting points? If it does, is that a serious difficulty for the method's claim to yield justified principles, or is it something its proponents have already accommodated? If it does not, is that because the ethical case lacks the determinate revision rule and the external equations that give the fixed-point language its content in physics, so that what looks like the same structure is in fact missing precisely the features that make the scientific parallel interesting?

I should like an assessment of where the mapping holds, what would have to be true of reflective equilibrium for results about convergence and multiple fixed points to transfer, and whether the analogy predicts anything we know to be false. Please answer in British English.
