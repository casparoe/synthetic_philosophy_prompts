Take the color phi case as Dennett uses it to raise questions about the timing of experience. In the standard setup, a red dot is flashed briefly at one position and, after a short interval, a green dot is flashed briefly at a second position; subjects report seeing a single dot move across the gap and change from red to green before the second flash appears. Dennett frames the puzzle as a conflict between two ways of revising the subject's story: an Orwellian account, on which the subject's initial experience is something closer to the actual sequence but a later memory edit rewrites the timeline into the smooth motion-and-color-change narrative, and a Stalinesque account, on which consciousness itself is delayed until after the second flash so that the subject's only experience is the coherent moving dot.

Now alter the case. Instead of collecting a retrospective verbal report after the stimulus ends, have subjects perform a speeded, time-sensitive task during each trial: they must press a button as soon as they see the dot change color, and their response times are recorded alongside the physical times of the two flashes. To vary the difficulty of the judgment, change the spatial gap across trials, producing a range of response-time distributions.

What does the standard Orwellian/Stalinesque contrast commit each side to in this online-response version? Does the variant merely add another behavior that each account can revise to fit, or does the timing of the button press favor one account over the other? If the two accounts can still be adjusted to predict any response-time pattern, state precisely what auxiliary assumption each adjustment needs; if some response-time pattern would genuinely discriminate them, specify it and explain why it changes the original puzzle's conclusion.
