I've been turning over a parallel between two things I know from different parts of my life and I cant tell if its deep or just pattern-matching. As a novelist I think a lot about point of view. In a first-person novel the narrator has direct access to exactly one inner life; everyone else gets pieced together from dialogue, gesture, what theyre reported to have done. Solipsism, and the main character intuition that other people are NPCs, looks like the same stance turned on actual people: I know one consciousness from the inside and infer the rest. In fiction we treat that as a choice of narration, not a fact—switch to omniscient and we suddenly see inside every character, and a first-person narrator who treats everyone else as hollow is usually just bad craft. My question: is the parallel deep, so the solipsist is mistaking a limitation of first-person access for a fact about the world? Or is it superficial because an omniscient narrator doesn't actually discover other minds—it's given to them by an author, which is the one move real epistemology cant make. And if there's something to it, what does the craft rule that every character must have an inner life even when the narration hides it tell us about what the NPC intuition is missing?
