I'm an undergraduate in a course on early Chinese philosophy of action, and in our discussion of wu-wei a position has come up that I find both plausible and frustratingly hard to pin down. The position is that wu-wei — roughly, acting effectively without deliberative self-consciousness or strained effort — cannot be achieved by trying directly to achieve it, because the very attempt to be spontaneous installs a layer of self-monitoring that wu-wei excludes; yet the same position also says that wu-wei can be cultivated indirectly, through habituation, absorbed practice, ritual, or the way a situation is arranged. So the paradox is said to be real but one-sided: direct trying is self-defeating, indirect cultivation is not.

I want to know how much of this is a testable psychological claim rather than a piece of conceptual tidying. Set out the strongest experimental design you can for the descriptive core of the claim: that direct instructions to act naturally and without monitoring actually undermine the relevant quality of performance or experience, while indirect cultivation conditions promote it. Be concrete about what you would manipulate and measure. For example, you might use an overlearned skilled task, thought probes that sample whether attention is task-focused or self-focused, post-task ratings of effort and spontaneity, or conditions modeled on flow. Then tell me exactly which pattern of results would count for the claim, which pattern would count against it, and which pattern would leave it indeterminate.

Then do the harder part: say what the test, even if it came out cleanly, would and would not settle. How would you know that what was measured deserves the name wu-wei in the classical sense, rather than mere absence of anxiety or presence of automaticity? Could a positive result ever show that there is a self-defeating structure to trying, or only that the particular instructions and conditions you used have those effects? And is any residue of the paradox untestable in principle, or merely untested here?

Finally, say how confident you are in the specific predictions and interpretations you give, and single out the point you are least sure about.
