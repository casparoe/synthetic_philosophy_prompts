I'm on a regional conservation interventions panel — the sort of body that signs off on eradications, reintroductions and biological control — and there's a decision coming up next month that's doing my head in. To be honest I keep flip-flopping on it, and I'd rather work out what we should decide if we're being consistent with our own track record than just vote with whoever argued last.

Our past decisions, roughly:

- Approved: annual trapping of introduced mink along the Merevale catchment to protect water voles. Stop trapping and the mink come back, which we accepted as a permanent cost.
- Approved: release of a host-specific weevil to control an invasive aquatic weed in the Rindle estuary. The weevil can't survive our winters, so we top it up each spring; stop releasing and the control simply stops.
- Approved: reintroduction of a locally extinct butterfly from a captive breeding programme, with soft releases over several years. We could have halted it at any point.
- Rejected: a self-sustaining gene drive that would spread female sterility through an introduced squirrel population on the mainland. The applicants admitted it couldn't be recalled once released and could spread well beyond the target area.
- Rejected: a de-extinction proposal to clone a recently extinct frog using a related species as surrogate. There were welfare worries about the surrogates, plus nobody knew how the clones would actually behave, plus it was effectively a one-way door.

The new proposal is a self-limiting gene drive to suppress an introduced mosquito on a small island. The mosquito carries a blood parasite that's hammering the native songbirds. The drive is engineered so that it disappears from the population within about ten generations unless we keep releasing modified males; if we stop, the modification washes out. The mosquito basically doesn't leave the island. So it's genetic modification, but it's contained and reversible in a way the squirrel drive wasn't.

What should we decide if we want to be consistent with ourselves? Which of our precedents is the closest analogue? Seperately, if you think the record underdetermines it, say what principle we'd be adopting with each choice, and what further case (real or invented) would settle which principle we've actually been running.
