I spent my career in geophysics, where our public products were probabilistic hazard forecasts, and in retirement I have been trying to understand how defamation law's fact/opinion distinction should apply to statements like those. Please write a focused argument for the following position: an explicitly probabilistic forecast should not be treated as an actionable assertion of fact merely because the event it concerns would, if it occurred, be empirically checkable. The relevant question is whether the statement, taken in context, asserts a determinate state of affairs or only a probability distribution over states of affairs. Argue in particular that a forecast assigning a 20 percent probability of a magnitude 7 or greater earthquake on a named fault within thirty years asserts no determinate fact about any earthquake and should therefore be non-actionable, even though the probability value is itself an empirical claim that can be scored against the forecasting model's calibration record. Address the strongest version of the objection that such a checkable probability claim is exactly the kind of claim defamation law should police. Keep the argument to no more than 700 words, and open with your conclusion.
