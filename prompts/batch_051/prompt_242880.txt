Next week I'm giving a talk at the local LessWrong meetup. The room is a weird mix: maybe half AI alignment people who actually train or eval models and think most decision theory is navel-gazing, and half old-sequence types who still think acausal trade is one of the more important ideas anybody ever had. My talk defends a thesis I expect both halves to hate in different ways: acausal threats are not a separate, exotic problem; they're just a special case of surrogate goals. The rough argument is this. For an acausal threat to bite at all, the threatener has to model you well enough to predict how you'll respond to the threat. To do that, it has to make your future behavior one of the variables it's optimizing over. And a variable you're optimizing over inside someone else's model of you just is a surrogate goal about you. So the real question was never whether to give in to blackmail from a superintelligence you'll never meet; it's what makes a powerful predictor start treating its model of you as a target rather than as a forecast, which is the same question as when surrogate goals form in any system. So I'm going to argue we should stop treating acausal threats as a special scary thing and fold them into the general theory of surrogate goals.
