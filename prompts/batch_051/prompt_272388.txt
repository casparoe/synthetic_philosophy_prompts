I'm a university student in an intergenerational ethics course, and for my term paper I need to state my position on the nonidentity problem and then defend it. Before I commit to the paper, I want an audit of whether the position actually hangs together. My current commitments are these:

(1) I hold a narrow person-affecting view: an outcome is better or worse only if it is better or worse for particular people, and an act is wrong only if it makes some particular person worse off than they would otherwise have been.

(2) I think that a fourteen-year-old who decides to have a child now rather than waiting does something wrong, even when the child she has now would not have existed if she had waited and has a life worth living, though with a much worse start than the different child she could have had later.

(3) I think that a society choosing a depletion policy rather than a conservation policy does something wrong in the standard same-number case: the same number of future people would exist either way, and those who would exist under depletion would have lives worth living but worse lives than the different people who would exist under conservation.

(4) I accept the procreation asymmetry: we are not required to create happy people, but we are required not to create people whose lives are not worth living.

(5) I reject the repugnant conclusion: a sufficiently large population of lives barely worth living is not better than a smaller population of excellent lives.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be; rank the genuine tensions by severity; for each, say what would have to give and what the least costly revision would be. Also tell me which argument or consideration would be most likely to change your overall verdict, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
