I've been going back and forth on the Dennett–Caruso exchange about just deserts and punishment, and I've started to think the debate is stuck in a way that isn't just about unresolved arguments. Dennett thinks Caruso's free will scepticism throws away the idea of responsible agency because it can't stomach retribution, while Caruso thinks Dennett has quietly replaced real desert with a consequentialist justification and kept the word for morale. They seem to accept the same empirical picture and even agree that we should stop most punitive practices, but they still talk past each other on whether anyone ever *deserves* blame or punishment. Is this a substantive disagreement about moral facts, a disagreement about what kind of justification the practices need, or a disagreement about what it would mean for our attitudes to be coherent? Diagnose the structure of the impasse rather than telling me who's right, and say what would actually count as progress—a distinction, a case, a change of question—or whether we should expect none.
