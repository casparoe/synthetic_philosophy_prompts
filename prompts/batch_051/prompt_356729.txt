I help run a small philosophy discussion group, and the thing we keep coming back too is peer disagreement: what should actually happen to your confidence when someone who seems just as bright and just as well-informed as you comes to the opposite conclusion from the same material. I want to put together a "which view of peer disagreement do you actually hold?" quiz for the group's blog, and I'd like you to write it for me. Eight to ten questions, each built around a concrete case rather than labels, with two or three answer options per case, and a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions. I'd like the main positions to include: strong conciliationism (peer disagreement is always a reason to lower your confidence, perhaps all the way to roughly splitting the difference), steadfastness (you can rationally stick to your view without thereby being dogmatic), and at least one genuinely in-between position, for example you move a bit but not fully, or whether you move depends on something like whether you can reconstruct the other person's reasoning, whether the question is "clean" like arithmetic versus "messy" like politics, or whether the two of you share the same background assumptions.

The point is to sort people by what they say about cases rather than by which label they'd pick, so no question should just ask "are you a conciliationist?" or use the vocabulary in the answer options. Some cases you might use, though don't feel bound to them: two friends each add up a shared restaurant bill and arrive at different totals; you and a co-author disagree about what a graph in your own paper shows; two equally experienced doctors disagree about a diagnosis from the same scans; an online stranger with a long history of good posts disagrees; you find out later the other person was exhausted or drunk; a disagreement where the other side is your own past self from a year ago. The quiz should be understandable to someone who has never heard of conciliationism or steadfastness, and the final result descriptions should be one or two sentences each.

Please build the scoring key from the space of answers first rather than from the list of names: for each combination of answers, either assign it to a position, or say explicitly that no one occupies it because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag any two positions that the questions cannot seperate, rather than pretending they come apart.
