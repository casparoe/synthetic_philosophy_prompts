I work on content policy at a social media company and I keep having to write rules that use the word "propaganda," but I've realized I can't define it in a way I'd defend in a meeting. Give me a working definition that gets the cases below right. I'm confident the first one is propaganda and the second one isnt; the rest I'm genuinely on the fence about. Part of whats confusing me is that the word seems to do two jobs at once: a neutral job (some party systematically pushing a message at a mass audience) and an accusatory job (deceptive manipulation, usually for the senders benefit). Pull those two apart if you think they need pulling apart, and tell me which cases flip when you switch between them.

1. A foreign government's operation spreading a doctored video about a candidate, fronted by fake local accounts that hide where it came from.
2. A hotel's poster by the elevator showing guests where the fire exits are.
3. A government anti-smoking campaign built around graphic photos of diseased lungs.
4. An oil company's ad about its "commitment to clean energy," running while the same company lobbies against emissions limits.
5. A state broadcaster's evening news that reports only true stories but conveniently skips the ones embarrassing to the government.
6. A doctor who tells a patient "if you dont quit this year you'll be on oxygen by sixty," when the doctor knows the risk is real but much smaller than that.
7. An ordinary user who reposts a propaganda-style meme without knowing anything about where it came from or what agenda it serves.
8. A recommender system that pushes that same meme to millions of people because it drives engagement, with no one deciding to amplify it.

I want a definite verdict for each, including the messy middle ones. Run every case through the definition out loud, and say where the definition surprises you or where you had to fudge. Also tell me which verdict is the least secure.

Format: a numbered list with exactly nine items, each one or two sentences, no intro and no conclusion. Item 1 is your definition (or definitions). Items 2 through 9 are the eight cases in order, each with your verdict and the main reason. If a case breaks your definition, say so in that item and give the smallest patch you'd make to fix it.
