Ok I've been going back and forth on hedonism and I think the heterogeneity problem actually kills it, but I want to lay out the argument in steps because I can't tell if I'm cheating somewhere. Here it is:

(1) Hedonism about well-being says pleasure is the only intrinsic good.
(2) For that to be a real theory, there has to be a single thing that all pleasures share — otherwise "maximize pleasure" is just a list of unrelated experiences wearing one name.
(3) The only plausible candidate for that shared thing is a felt quality: the feeling of pleasantness itself, the positive valence that gets attached to otherwise very different experiences.
(4) But across different pleasures the felt quality doesn't stay constant. The pleasure of finally peeing after a long drive, the pleasure of suddenly understanding a proof, the pleasure of malicious gossip, the warm buzz after exercise, the taste of sugar — these don't just differ in cause, content, and intensity; they feel like different kinds of thing. A proof-pleasure and an itch-relief are no more alike in what its like to feel than a proof and a sneeze.
(5) So there is no common felt quality.
(6) Therefore hedonism either has to say pleasure is not one thing after all, or it ends up maximizing a word rather than a property.

I think (4) is just introspectively obvious, and the rest follows. But people keep telling me I've missed a move. Say exactly which step, if any, fails, what the best repaired version of it would be, and whether the conclusion could survive even if the argument as written is invalid. Also tell me which part of your answer you're least confident in and what would change your mind about it.
