For a seminar on the foundations of ethics, I have to present my position on what Sidgwick called the dualism of practical reason: the apparent conflict between the claim that I am rationally required to maximize my own good and the claim that I am rationally required to maximize the good of all. I want a consistency audit before I present, because I suspect the difficulty lies in the setup of my view rather than in any single premise, but I cannot locate it. Here is the package I currently endorse.

(1) There is a single, univocal notion of what an agent has most reason, all things considered, to do. A view of practical reason that issues an irresolvable conflict of rational requirements has therefore failed; I refuse to accept that practical reason is incoherent or merely permissive at the fundamental level.

(2) Rational egoism is correct: an act is rationally required for an agent just in case it does best at promoting that agent's own good, with all times in the agent's life counted equally and no time privileged.

(3) Rational benevolence is correct: an act is rationally required just in case it does best at promoting the good of all persons, with each person's good counted equally and no person privileged.

(4) I reject the standard reconciliations: I do not think egoism and benevolence converge once long-run effects, reputation, and the satisfactions of conscience are included; I do not think either principle is a confused version of the other, or of ordinary prudence or ordinary other-regard; and I do not think practical reason is merely instrumental, so that the supposed axioms are really just expressions of present desires.

(5) I find the underlying intuitions behind both principles equally compelling and self-evident: that no moment within a life is rationally privileged, and that no person's good matters more than any other's, considered impersonally.

(6) Still, I believe that in any actual conflict between an agent's own good and the general good, there is a determinate fact of the matter about what the agent ought to do all things considered.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly, which tensions are merely apparent and dissolve once the relevant notions are distinguished, and which single revision would do the most to restore consistency. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally serious, and for the recommended revision say exactly what it costs.
