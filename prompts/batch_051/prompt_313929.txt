I run a workshop at a community center—about twelve adults, mixed backgrounds, none of them climate scientists, two hours with a break. I want a hands-on activity on solar geoengineering (sulfate or other reflective particles in the upper atmosphere to cool the planet) that makes people feel three things rather than hear about them: (1) the moral hazard of having a cheap 'shade switch' that lets everyone avoid the expensive work of cutting emissions; (2) termination shock, when the shading stops and the postponed warming arrives fast; (3) the 'who decides' problem, where one bloc can deploy for everyone else and nobody can stop them. No lecture up front, and the word 'geoengineering' shouldn't appear until the debrief. Design a runnable session: roles, rules, materials to print, a two-hour timeline, and a debrief that maps moments in the game onto those three ideas. Explain what predictably goes wrong when the activity is run and how to steer it, and be frank about what the game can't reproduce (real climate lag, regional effects, uncertainty about whether the cooling even works as imagined), so I don't oversell the exercise. In the debrief notes, separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this rather than presenting one side as the consensus. Please use British English.
