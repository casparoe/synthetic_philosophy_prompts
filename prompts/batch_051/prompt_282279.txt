I have been developing a view I call the objective-space closure thesis, and I would like it assessed honestly rather than politely. The claim is that no learning system, human or machine, ever produces an output that lies genuinely outside the region of output space its training distribution and objective function already had it exploring: what we call an intuitive leap is interpolation across dimensions we had simply failed to encode in our description of the space. A celebrated 'impossible' move by a Go-playing system looks, on this account, not like a departure from training but like a continuation of it through dimensions the commentators had omitted, such as the objective function, the tree-search heuristics, and the body of prior self-play positions. I am aware that the thesis sounds close to the old line that creativity is mere recombination, and I am worried that it may be unfalsifiable as stated. Is 'training distribution' a determinate enough notion, especially for a human mind, for the thesis to say anything that could be false? What is the strongest example of a leap that my thesis cannot absorb without gerrymandering the space after the fact, and what is the most defensible residual version of the claim, if any? Please reply as a numbered list of exactly five items with no introduction or conclusion: first, the three strongest objections in ascending order of severity; then, the two most defensible reformulations of the thesis. Each item should be one or two sentences.
