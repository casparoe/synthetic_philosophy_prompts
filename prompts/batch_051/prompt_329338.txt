I'm a novelist, and for a book about a logician who stops being able to trust tomorrow I've ended up with what I think is an actual thesis. I call it 'descriptive priority': you may project a predicate from observed cases to unobserved ones only if it's the predicate you'd have used to sort the sample before you knew what the induction was for. So 'green' is fine because someone sorting emeralds says 'green' before they'd ever say 'grue'; 'grue' is parasitc on green and blue plus a clock. I know this sounds like Goodman's entrenchment under another name. Tell me whether it's actually the same thing in disguise or a real third option, whether the circularity worry is fatal—since 'before you knew what was at stake' might just smuggle in the projections I wanted to license—and give me the strongest counterexample you can, where a spontaneous, stake-independent description would lead induction badly wrong.
