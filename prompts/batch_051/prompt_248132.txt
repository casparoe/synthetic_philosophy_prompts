I call the theory shibboleth semantics. The central claim is this: inside a sufficiently sealed belief community, the claims that circulate gradually change their function. They may begin as ordinary descriptive statements, but over time the community enforces their repetition rather than their truth, and questioning them becomes a loyalty violation regardless of the evidence. I call this shift shibboleth drift. On my view, an echo chamber is not best understood as a place where outside information cannot enter; it is better understood as a social arrangement in which beliefs are converted into membership badges, so that incoming evidence is processed as a social gesture rather than as a reason to update.

I accept something like the standard contrast between an epistemic bubble and an echo chamber: a bubble is a shortage of outside information, while an echo chamber is a structure of trust that discredits outside sources before they can speak. My thesis is meant to explain how that second structure is maintained. The mechanism is semantic: the shared beliefs stop functioning as descriptions and start functioning as signs of belonging.

I think the theory has a diagnostic payoff. In a healthy belief community, being shown that a shared claim is false tends to produce surprise or interest. In a community undergoing shibboleth drift, being shown that the claim is false produces irritation, suspicion, or a demotion of the challenger; the truth value of the correction becomes irrelevant. The group's real argument is not about whether the claim is true but about whether the person who questioned it is one of us.

For example, take a claim to the effect that the system is rigged. It may circulate not because anyone has examined the mechanisms, but because asserting it with the right affect is how members demonstrate membership. If someone produces a counterexample, the counterexample is heard as defection. My theory predicts that fact-checking fails in these settings because it addresses the content of a sentence whose actual job has stopped being descriptive; it tries to correct a password as if it were a report.
