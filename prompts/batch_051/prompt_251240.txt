I'm teaching an introductory course with a unit on population ethics, and I want a piece of satire to use as a discussion starter before the students read the serious arguments. Please write a deadpan government FAQ, around 650 words and in no case more than 700, from a fictional Ministry of Demographic Optimisation that has adopted an explicitly totalist pronatalist policy: it holds that a larger population of lives worth living makes the world better, without qualification, even when average wellbeing falls. Format it as numbered questions and answers, and keep the bureaucratic voice level, upbeat, and quietly relentless.

The FAQ should stay in character and should never name-drop philosophers or academic labels. But a reader who knows population ethics should be able to see exactly where the ministry is taking its stands. In particular, the answers should quietly embrace the repugnant conclusion (perhaps in an answer about whether a crowded world of lives barely worth living can be worse than a smaller, happier one), treat the nonidentity problem as a reason for rather than against adding people (the added lives are good for the people who live them, and nobody is made worse off by never having existed), brush off the average-welfare objection as a confusion between how well individual lives go and how many lives there are, and sidestep worries about declining per-capita resources. The humour should come from the ministry's logic being followed exactly into places where most people flinch, not from the authors being stupid or cruel; the irony must stay deadpan but legible, so a reader can tell the view is being satirized even though the ministry never breaks character. A reader new to the area should finish with a clear sense of the policy and a nagging feeling that the obvious objection has just been handled too smoothly; a specialist should recognise the repugnant conclusion, the nonidentity problem, and the total-versus-average dispute underneath.
