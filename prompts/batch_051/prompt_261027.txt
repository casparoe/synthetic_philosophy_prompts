I've been moderating an argument between two people in my philosophy of religion reading group that has gone on long enough that they finally agreed to try an adversarial collaboration, and I got stuck writing the document theyre supposed to sign. One is a fine-tuning theist: he thinks the fact that the constants of nature land in the tiny life-permitting range is real evidence for theism over a naturalistic single universe, and hes suspicious that multiverse proposals get wheeled in just to soak up the improbability. The other is a selection-effect naturalist: she thinks the apparent improbability is exactly what you'd predict once you allow an ensemble of universes and condition on there being an observer, and she keeps saying the theists likelihoods arent defined because nobody can say what constants God would have chosen. We need a text that neither of them can complain stacks the deck.

Act as an expert philosopher of science who's written fair-minded joint statements before, and think carefully about the exact wording; maybe brainstorm a few candidate formulations before settling. The deliverable should be what the two of them actually produce after a good-faith session. Include (1) a numbered agreed-claims section with claims both can sign — about what the fine-tuning data actually are, what selection effects can and cannot do, the difference between likelihoods and priors, and what the live explanatory options are; (2) a disagreements table giving the residual disputes in neutral wording, each side's preferred statement of its own position in wording that side would accept, and your judgment on whether each dispute is substantive or partly verbal; (3) a crux list: sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side, and for each crux a concrete result, case, or argument both sides agree bears on it, plus each side's prediction recorded in advance. I suspect the cruxes include whether the multiverse needs independent support before it can do explanatory work and whether theism yields anything precise enough to count as a likelihood, but use your judgment. Don't adjudicate the debate or slip into saying who's right — the whole point is a constructive text both could put their names to, with the residual disagreement made exact. No citations or reading lists either; this should read like a self-contained document they could actually sign.
