I sit on the curriculum committee of a small independent school. Our graduation requirements include the following rule: "Every student must complete two consecutive years of study in a modern spoken language other than English, chosen from the languages offered by the school." A student has asked to satisfy the requirement with two years of American Sign Language. The student points out that ASL has its own grammar, is not signed English, is acquired by children in the same natural way as spoken languages, and is the native language of a large community. The people who wrote the rule plainly had Spanish, French, and Mandarin in mind and never considered a signed language. Walk me through what a plain-text reading, a purpose-based reading, and a guess about the drafters' intentions would each say about this request, and then give me your own ruling: does the request satisfy the rule, and if it does not, should the rule be amended?
