I’ve been thinking through a version of the Judy Benjamin problem and I keep going back and forth. Suppose Judy Benjamin is about to be dropped into enemy territory. Her briefing tells her the territory is divided into four quadrants: Red East, Red West, Blue East, and Blue West. She has no information favoring any quadrant, so she assigns each probability 1/4. After landing, HQ radios her with the message that if she is in Red territory, then she is in East. The message doesn’t tell her where she is, but it should constrain her conditional probabilities.

Here is an argument that she must end up with probability 1/3 for being in Red territory, even though the message seems to say nothing about whether she is in Red or Blue. (1) What Judy learns is that the material conditional R → E is true, where R = she is in Red territory and E = she is in East sector. (2) Learning a proposition requires ruling out exactly the possibilities at which the proposition is false. (3) The material conditional R → E is false only at Red West among the four live possibilities. (4) So Judy should rule out Red West and renormalize uniformly over Red East, Blue East, and Blue West. (5) Therefore her new probability for R is 1/3.

Is this argument valid? If it is, why does the conclusion feel as though it makes HQ’s conditional message relevant to whether she is in Red at all? If not, which step fails, and what would the correct posterior over the four quadrants be under your preferred rule for updating on conditionals?
