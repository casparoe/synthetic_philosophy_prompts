I recently had a long argument with a physicist friend about whether effective field theories support a genuine autonomy of the scales, and I came away worried that the position I was defending may be less coherent than I had assumed. The conversation kept circling back to the early part of Philip Anderson’s 1972 article More Is Different, where he argues that even if one accepts reductionism—the claim that all matter is composed of the same kinds of elementary particles obeying the same fundamental laws—it does not follow that one could, even in principle, reconstruct the behavior of large systems from those laws. At each new level of complexity, he suggests, new regularities and new concepts become necessary. I read this as a point about the structure of physics rather than merely about our computational limitations, though my friend disputed that reading.

The commitments I found myself defending were these. (a) Effective field theories are not simply low-energy approximations to a more exact theory; for a given range of scales they are the correct description, with degrees of freedom and symmetries that belong to that scale. (b) The renormalization group explains why physics at one scale is largely insensitive to physics at much higher scales, and this is what underwrites the autonomy of fields such as condensed matter physics and chemistry. (c) Some emergent phenomena, including superconductivity and the fractional quantum Hall effect, are not just consequences of the microscopic theory but require their own organizing principles. (d) The Standard Model is itself best understood as an effective field theory, and it is reasonable to expect a more unified high-energy theory from which the effective theories at lower scales derive, at least in principle. (e) The fact that we cannot actually derive most macroscopic phenomena from fundamental physics is not a serious objection to this hierarchical picture. Which of these commitments are actually in tension with one another, which only seem to be, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally serious.
