Im a philosophy PhD student working on moral methodology, and for a seminar I have to produce an adversarial-collaboration document for two positions in my own area that have been yelling past each other for years. The two positions are a parsimony-first camp and a fit-first camp about how we should choose among normative ethical theories. The parsimony-first camp treats economy—few principles, few primitive values, no gerrymandered exception clauses—as serious evidence that a theory is right, and is willing to revise a widely held case judgment if that is what an otherwise elegant theory requires. The fit-first camp treats fit with considered judgments in a wide and varied range of cases as the main test of an ethical theory, and thinks parsimony is at best a tiebreaker and most of the time just physics envy. I need you to write the joint statement they could both sign after sitting down and actually trying to locate the disagreement rather than score points. Dont let them settle for 'we just weight parsimony differently.' I want four parts. First, a numbered list of claims they actually agree on, worded tightly enough that neither would insist on a footnote. Second, a disagreements table where each side's view is stated exactly as that side would word it. Third, the cruxes—the subquestions such that a settled answer to one of them would move at least one camp. Fourth, for each crux, at least one case or comparison both camps accept as diagnostic, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. The part I most need help with is the carving. They both use 'simple' to mean at least three different things—fewer principles, fewer kinds of value, fewer exception clauses—and I want the statement to force them to say which of those each side actually needs. Also make them address whether an ethical theory is in the business of explaining our case judgments or merely systematizing them, because I suspect the whole fight collapses once that is settled. And force the threshold question into the open: is there any gain in parsimony that would justify revising a deeply held considered judgment, and if so how much? I'd rather see them disagree about a precise threshold than about the slogan 'is simplicity a virtue.' Format it as: numbered agreed claims, then the disagreements table, then a section of cruxes with the recorded predictions.
