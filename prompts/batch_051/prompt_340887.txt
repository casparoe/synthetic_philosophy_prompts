I chair the Animal Welfare and Ethics Committee of a state wildlife agency. The agency manages a large island park whose deer herd, long protected from hunting and predators, has outgrown its winter range; in hard winters a substantial portion of the herd starves slowly over several weeks. A proposal before the committee asks the agency to begin a multi-year immuno-contraception program to bring the herd down to a size the range can support, on the grounds that preventing foreseeable slow starvation is a welfare obligation that extends to wild animals, not just to animals under direct human care. The committee is split. Some members argue the agency has no mandate to manage wild systems for the subjective welfare of their inhabitants, and that sustained intervention would turn the island into a managed population with uncertain ecological side effects. Others accept that the agency should reduce the suffering but argue that a once-off cull is more humane and less interventionist in the long run than an open-ended contraception program. Still others defend the contraception proposal as the option that avoids killing. Draft the committee's decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and decide among the three options as needed — with a clear recommendation the agency director can act on and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side.
