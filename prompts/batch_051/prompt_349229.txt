I have been thinking about the philosophical exchange between Norman Malcolm and Daniel Dennett over whether dreams are experiences, and I would like a diagnosis of why the debate has never really seemed to settle.

As I understand the positions, Malcolm argues that the very idea of having an experience while soundly asleep is not just hard to verify but incoherent: our only criterion for saying that someone dreamed is their later waking report, so there is no independent standpoint from which the dream can be said to have occurred during sleep. Dennett, by contrast, tries to prise apart the waking report from the supposed earlier experience. He suggests that the report could be caused by a memory-insertion process at, or just before, awakening, in which a dream narrative is composed and then misremembered as having occurred earlier in the night. On that picture, whether a person really had experiences during sleep becomes an empirical question about the causal history of the memory report, rather than a conceptual confusion.

My working theory is that this is not a single disagreement at all: Malcolm is doing something like a grammatical or conceptual investigation into when we are entitled to say that a dream occurred, whereas Dennett is asking about the causal mechanisms that produce dream reports. But I am not sure whether that diagnosis is too neat. Malcolm could reply that Dennett's memory-insertion hypothesis already borrows the very notion it claims to test, since it still speaks of a narrative being misremembered as having occurred earlier. Dennett could reply that Malcolm's criterion is question-begging, because it rules out by stipulation the possibility that waking reports are causally misleading. So I would like your diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement: is it verbal, does it turn on a hidden empirical question about whether memory reports can be evidence for past conscious states, or does it reflect deeper differences about what an explanation of dreaming must do? And given that diagnosis, what would actually count as progress—a neuroimaging result, a conceptual distinction, or a reformulation of the original question?

Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this literature, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus.
