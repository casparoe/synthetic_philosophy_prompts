Ok so I'm taking this course on the governance of advanced AI, and we've spent two weeks on pre-AGI bargains — the idea that the time to make deals is now, before anyone has an AGI, because after the transition nobody has any levrage. The standard worry is that these deals are unenforceable; the standard reply is that reputation and repeated interaction (labs care about their credibility for future collaborations) make them self-enforcing. I want a thought experiment that isolates why that reply can fail even when everyone is perfectly rational, well-informed, and not at all evil.

Build me the simplest cleanest case you can with these specs. Two parties, say two labs, voluntarily sign a pre-AGI bargain about how they'll share post-AGI control or profits. No third-party enforcer and no technical locking (nothing like a both-keys-must-turn mechanism). The only thing holding the deal together is reputation, including their reputation with other labs they might work with later. At the time of signing, the bargain is clearly better in expected terms for both of them than a no-deal race, and both know it. Later, one of them is visibly ahead in the race — no secrets — and finds that walking away from the deal is now strictly better than complying. Crucially this happens without any surprise capability jump and without an outside buyer showing up. And both parties could see from the very beginning that this would happen to whoever ended up ahead. So the bargain is rational to sign and rational to break, and the shadow of the future can't fix it. Dont make it a prisoners' dilemma in disguise where defection beats cooperation from the start; the deal has to be a genuine mutual improvement ex ante.

After you build the scenario, do the audit I need for my paper: lay out the payoffs in words before and after the lead emerges, show why each side signs, why the leader defects, and then go through why the reputational fix fails even if both labs care a lot about their credibility. Then tell me which single feature of the case is doing the real work — if you changed that feature, does the whole thing collapse back into an ordinary enforceable bargain? And give me the strongest countermove from someone who thinks pre-AGI bargains can be made self-enforcing: what would they add to the deal or change in the background to kill your case?

And since I have to defend this in a tutorial: tell me where your own reasoning is most likely to fail, which of my specs you'd need to weaken to get the opposite verdict, and what would change your mind.
