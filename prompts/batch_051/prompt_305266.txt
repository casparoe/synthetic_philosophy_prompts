I am taking a philosophy of science course this term, and we have been working through the indispensability argument and the more recent debate over whether mathematics ever genuinely explains physical facts, as opposed to merely representing them. My instructor has been ruling on example cases as she goes, and her record is consistent but hard for me to compress into a single principle.

She counts the periodical cicadas as a genuine mathematical explanation: on her account, the arithmetical fact that 13 and 17 are prime explains the insects' unusually long breeding periods, because prime periods minimize how often the cicadas' emergence coincides with predator cycles. She also counts the bees' honeycomb as genuine: the fact that a hexagonal tiling minimizes perimeter for a fixed area is, she says, what explains why the cells take the shape they do.

Her negative rulings are instructive. She does not count a soap bubble as a case of mathematical explanation, even though a sphere encloses a given volume with the least surface area; in her view, the isoperimetric mathematics only states the equilibrium condition, while surface tension and pressure do the explaining. She says the same about a ball's roughly parabolic flight: the conic-section mathematics describes the trajectory, but gravity and initial velocity are what explain it.

Next week she has assigned the case of buckminsterfullerene, the C₆₀ molecule, whose sixty carbon atoms form a closed cage in the pattern of a truncated icosahedron, with exactly twelve pentagonal faces. Some chemists explain why the cage closes into this shape, and why exactly twelve pentagons are required for any closed cage of that kind, by appealing to Euler's polyhedron formula, V - E + F = 2, which constrains any such closed arrangement. I want to predict how she will rule if she stays consistent with her earlier verdicts.

I am asking for more than a prediction. What rule or rules could she be running? Which rival rules fit her earlier verdicts just as well, and what would each of them predict about the fullerene case? What is your best guess about her ruling, and why? And what further case, different from the fullerene, would most cleanly settle which rule she is actually using?
