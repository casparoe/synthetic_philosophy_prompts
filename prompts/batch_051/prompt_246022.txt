Something that's been bugging me for a while. I went through two exhibits about the Maya lowlands on one trip, and they described essentially the same end-of-Classic-period facts in opposite language: one wall panel called it the Maya collapse, while the other said the cities were abandoned and reorganized and pointedly refused to use the word collapse. Same population numbers, same dates, same buildings. So what does the practice of using the word collapse presuppose about what a society is—about its identity conditions, or how much a people can change and still count as the same people? Reconstruct the implicit theory of social identity, say which rival views it quietly rules out, say whether the archaeological practice actually needs that theory or whether the label is doing rhetorical work the evidence doesn't force on it, and then spell out what would have to change in how sites get interpreted if we went the other way and treated every case of collapse as mere reorganization.
