OK before I read any more theories about forgiveness I want the full spec first. Everyone I read seems to pick one or two cases and then generalize: the person who forgives a still-loving spouse for an affair, the person who forgives an unrepentant abuser, the person who says only God can forgive. I'd rather see the desiderata up front. What does an adequate theory of forgiveness actually have to get right? Give me the requirements list — the cases it must handle (forgiving the dead, forgiving someone who never asked or isn't sorry, forgiving yourself, forgiving on behalf of a victim, the person who keeps doing the harm, refusing to forgive, the so-called unforgivable), and the distinctions it can't collpase (forgiveness vs. excusing, vs. justifying, vs. condoning, vs. merely forgetting or moving on; forgiveness as an emotion vs. a decision vs. a speech act). For each requirement say *why* it's a requirement — what case or consideration makes it load-bearing. Then mark which ones are non-negotiable and which are really just commitments of one camp. Finally, flag any pairs that look like they can't be jointly satisfied; I already see the obvious tension between "forgiveness can be unconditional" and "forgiveness must track the wrongdoer's repentance," but I suspect there are subtler clashes worth pulling out. Keep any scorecard of existing views brief — the criteria are what I actually want. A table is fine.
