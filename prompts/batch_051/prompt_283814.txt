A friend and I spent most of last evening arguing about whether it is ever legitimate to treat two variables as independent simply because one lacks information about how they interact. I defended something like a presumption of independence: maximum-entropy considerations, the fact that any usable joint model has to factor somewhere, and the role of independence as the null hypothesis in causal and statistical inference all seem to give independence a special default status. He countered that in any system that matters, hidden coupling is exactly what will hurt you, and that a presumption of independence is little more than convenient blindness. We did not settle it, and I came away convinced that the interesting question is not whether independence is useful but what the default is supposed to be, and under what conditions it is genuinely warranted.
