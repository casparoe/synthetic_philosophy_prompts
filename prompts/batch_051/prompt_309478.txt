I am assembling an inventory of species concepts for a graduate seminar in the philosophy of biology, and I want to be sure I have not left out a concept that deserves its own entry. Here is my working list, with each item compressed to one sentence. (1) Biological species concept: a species is a group of populations whose members can or could interbreed with one another and are reproductively cut off from other groups. (2) Phylogenetic species concept: a species is the smallest group of organisms that can be picked out as a distinct branch on the tree of life, a lineage beginning at a branching event. (3) Ecological species concept: a species is a lineage whose members occupy the same ecological role or niche, so that an ecological gap rather than a breeding or genealogical fact marks its boundary. (4) Evolutionary species concept: a species is a lineage that begins when a population splits off and persists through time as one evolving line with its own trajectory, even as its organisms change. (5) Phenetic species concept: a species is a cluster of organisms grouped by overall resemblance across many measured traits, with no single factor doing the sorting. (6) Recognition species concept: a species is a group of organisms that share a mate-recognition system by which they identify appropriate partners within the group.

Now give me one more species concept that is not a restatement or special case of any of these—that is, it must slice the living world by a different underlying criterion, not merely combine or emphasize the six criteria above. Characterize it in the same compressed style, and then explain what it adds to the monism/pluralism debate that the six entries on my list do not: in particular, what kind of unity it attributes to a species, and whether adding it makes it easier or harder to hold that there is a single privileged way of dividing the living world into species. Lead with the concept itself—its name and one-sentence characterization—in your first sentence, and only then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
