I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and over the last couple years I keep getting pulled into writing about places that want to be called states but aren't fully recognized — breakaway regions, self-declared republics, whatever you want to call them. Our style guide has one rule: call them "breakaway regions" until they get broad international recognition. I've started to think that rule is doing more work in the sentence than the style guide realized, because it makes every dispute sound like a footnote.

I know enough to know there's a live argument underneath. Some people say statehood is just facts on the ground — a settled population, a territory with actual borders, a government that runs things, the ability to deal with other states — and recognition is just other countries catching up with the facts. Others say recognition is what makes the facts amount to anything: statehood is a status other states confer, not something you can simply have. I don't have the background to settle that, but I have to write sentences about these places roughly twice a month on a deadline of hours, and both sides read us closely and accuse us of bias whatever we print. The copy desk and the standards editor have to sign off, so whatever I adopt has to be defencible to them without me giving them a seminar.

Write me a reusable procedure for deciding what to call one of these entities in a news story. I want a checklist or a flowchart in words — tests I can run in order — not an essay explaining the theories. It should say which distinctions from the theory actually have to survive translation into copy (who recognizes it, how many others do, whether it controls its claimed territory, whether it functions like a state day to day) and which I can drop. Give me the hard cases the procedure has to handle: full control but almost no recognition, broad recognition but barely any control, recognition split down a regional line, and cases where I can't actually count recognitions because no one publishes a clean number and governments are vague. Mark the steps that are judgment calls and can't be made to look like a procedure.

Then apply the procedure to two or three hypothetical cases, including one where it tells me to use a term our style guide would hate and one where it forces an awkward construction I'd rather avoid. End by saying what the procedure cannot settle — where I'll still just have to choose and take the flak.
