Last night I had a long argument with a friend about which historical episode provides the most useful analogy for the coming transition to transformative artificial intelligence. He kept returning to nuclear weapons, stressing discontinuity, concentrated state control, and catastrophic risk. I found myself defending the Industrial Revolution, not as the closest historical match to what is coming, but as the least misleading organizing analogy: it foregrounds gradual diffusion, decades of institutional lag, labor displacement, geographically uneven benefits, and the way a general-purpose technology eventually reorganizes social life. I want to stress-test the position I defended rather than simply win the argument. What would count against the claim that the Industrial Revolution is the best organizing analogy for transformative AI, in the sense that someone reasoning about the transition would systematically make better judgments by keeping its causal structure in mind than by reasoning with the Manhattan Project or the printing press as their template? Please rank the defeaters from decisive to merely awkward, and be concrete about what the world would have to look like for each to be realized. In particular: which commitments of the Industrial Revolution analogy are empirically checkable, and which do their work mainly by being vague? What would the nuclear-weapons analogy have to capture that mine currently misses for it to become the better guide, and what would the printing-press analogy have to capture? Are there developments in AI so far that should already count as partial defeats, however mild? I should say that I expect to be moved more by particular structural features of AI or of the Industrial Revolution that make the transfer of lessons unreliable than by the generic warning that all analogies mislead. I would also like you to separate the defeaters that a defender of my position should already concede from those that only a determined critic would press. Finally, I suspect my view could be insulated by demoting the analogy to a mere lens or a source of questions rather than a predictive model; if that is the most honest description of what I am doing, tell me whether that insulation is a problem for a claim of this kind, and whether my position is better described as well insulated, badly exposed, or somewhere in between.
