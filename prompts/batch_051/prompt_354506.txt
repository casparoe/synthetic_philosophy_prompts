In an essay defending voluntary simplicity as a demand of global justice rather than a private taste, I encountered the slogan "Live simply so that others may simply live." Its role in the essay was precise: it was meant to bridge the gap between the premise that some people consume far more than their share and the conclusion that each individual ought, as a matter of obligation, to reduce what they use. But I cannot extract a single determinate proposition from it. Depending on how one reads "live simply," on whether the second "simply" means "merely" or "in a simple manner," and on whether "so that" marks a causal consequence, a teleological justification, or a normative condition, the slogan says very different things. Could you set out, as a menu, the distinct claims it might be used to assert, assess which are tautologous or trivial, which are substantive and defensible, and which are false or overstated, and then say which of these claims would actually license the conclusion that individual frugality is obligatory?
