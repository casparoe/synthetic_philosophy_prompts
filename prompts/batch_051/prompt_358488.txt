I spent most of my life as a field geologist, and in retirement I have been reading the history of biblical interpretation the way I used to read an outcrop: I want the strata named honestly and the unconformities marked. I keep returning to the long quarrel between readers who treat Scripture's plain sense as the foundation and readers who treat the plain sense as a veil for something spiritual — from Origen's Alexandrian allegorism, through Augustine's rule that a reading which does not build the love of God and neighbor must be given up, through the medieval fourfold sense and the Reformation preference for a single grammatical-historical sense, down to modern fundamentalist inerrancy and twentieth-century demythologizing. It has gradually become clear to me that 'literal' and 'allegorical' are not two poles on a single axis but shorthand for several separable commitments: whether the plain sense can be false or unedifying and still be scripture, whether a text carries more than one divinely intended sense, whether factual or historical accuracy is necessary for authority, and whether a command that is unacceptable in its plain sense must be transcended rather than applied.

For a handout I am preparing for a lay theology reading group, please write a decision tree that sorts a reader into one of the major positions along this spectrum — roughly a dozen leaves. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of labels: first state the yes/no diagnostic questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then place the named positions among the occupied combinations, and mark the empty combinations with a brief diagnosis of whether each is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely historically unoccupied. Every branch node should be a concrete question about a familiar text, phrased so that a person can answer it without already knowing which tradition they are being sorted toward. Seed questions I would like the tree to incorporate or explicitly rule on include: whether the six-day structure of Genesis 1 can be a theological or liturgical statement even if one doubts its chronology; whether the Song of Songs should first be read as human love poetry before it is read as God and Israel or Christ and the church; whether the command to destroy the Canaanite cities is edifying scripture when read in its plain sense; whether Jonah in the fish and Balaam's donkey must be historical events for those books to carry divine authority; and whether the Last Supper words over the bread can yield a real presence without abandoning the literal sense. The leaves should include at least an Origenist allegorical position, an Augustinian position shaped by the rule of charity, a medieval fourfold-sense position, a Reformation literal-primacy position, a fundamentalist inerrancy position, and a liberal demythologizing position, with further leaves added if the answer combinations call for them. Flag any two positions that the tree's yes/no questions cannot in fact separate, rather than letting a strong-sounding question do work it does not do.

Two further requests. First, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested: indicate which placements of figures such as Origen, Augustine, or the Reformers rest on fairly settled historical scholarship and which rest on contested theological judgment, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus. Second, return the instrument as structured data; specifically, a single JSON object with the following fields: title (string), audience (string), settled_and_contested (string; a short statement of what is settled versus contested), empty_combinations (array of objects, each with answers and diagnosis, where answers describes the combination of answers and diagnosis is one of incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored plus one sentence of explanation), inseparable (array of objects, each with leaf_ids and reason), and tree (an object whose fields are start, nodes, and leaves). In tree, start is the id of the first question. nodes is an array of objects, each with id (string), question (string; the yes/no diagnostic question), case (string; the concrete scriptural text or passage the question is posed about), yes (the id of the next node or leaf if the answer is yes), no (the id if the answer is no), and status (one of historical_settled, theological_contested, or mixed). leaves is an array of objects, each with id (string), label (string; the name of the position), position (string; a one-sentence statement of the view), awkward_commitment (string; the most uncomfortable thing the view must accept), and defended_by (string; names or traditions, only where you are confident, otherwise the empty string).
