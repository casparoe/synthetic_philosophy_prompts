I've been teaching a class on the modern Stoicism revival, and I keep hitting the same wall: the central move is supposed to be sorting everything into what's in your control and what isnt, but nobody can actually tell me what "in your control" means without the definition falling apart under pressure. So I want you to work as an expert on ancient ethics and contemporary Stoicism and build me a working definition of "in one's control" — or, if you prefer the older vocabulary, what the Stoics meant by "up to us."

Think carefully, and before you commit to a definition, brainstorm two or three candidate definitions and stress-test them quietly against my cases. Then give me the one definition you think best captures the term.

Here are the cases, roughly ordered from what I take to be clearly outside our control, through the murky middle, to what I take to be clearly inside it. I want a definition that gets the clear cases right and delivers a defensible verdict for the murky ones.

1. Whether it rains tomorrow morning.
2. The past — that I snapped at my partner yesterday.
3. The first thought that pops up when someone says "whatever you do, don't think about a pink elephant."
4. Whether I flinch when a kid fakes a punch at my face.
5. My reputation among my colleagues at the university.
6. Whether I call my sister tomorrow afternoon.
7. My heart rate drifting down over a few minutes while I use a biofeedback app.
8. Whether I raise my right arm right now.
9. Whether I follow through tonight on my decision not to send the angry email I already drafted.

Once you've settled on the definition, run it through all nine and flag any case where it gives a surprising verdict or no verdict at all. Then either revise the definition or defend the surprises. Here's the part I actually care about, so don't skip it: the modern revival treats the dichotomy as a practical sorting rule — that you should concern yourself with column A and stop worrying about column B. So tell me whether your best definition of "in my control" also cuts the world at the joint that matters for that advice, or whether the definition and the life-advice come apart in a way the self-help crowd is trying not to notice.
