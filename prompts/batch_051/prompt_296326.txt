For a classical gas in a box governed by Newtonian mechanics, consider the following argument.

(1) The Newtonian laws are time-reversal invariant: if a sequence of microstates leading from one state to another is lawful, then the time-reversed sequence, with all momenta reversed, is equally lawful.
(2) Any entropy-increasing transition from a lower-entropy macrostate A to a higher-entropy macrostate B is realised by some such lawful forward trajectory.
(3) Time reversal pairs every forward trajectory from A to B with a distinct reverse trajectory from B to A, and this pairing preserves Liouville measure.
(4) Since the set of initial microstates realising A → B and the set of initial microstates realising B → A have equal Liouville measure, the statistical postulate implies that the two transition probabilities are equal: P(B after τ | A now) = P(A after τ | B now).
(5) It follows that entropy decrease from B to A should be exactly as probable as entropy increase from A to B, so the observed one-way increase of entropy cannot be accounted for by these ingredients.
(6) Therefore Newtonian time-reversal invariance, together with the statistical postulate, is in conflict with the thermodynamic arrow.

Is this argument valid? If it is, explain how the observed asymmetry is nonetheless compatible with it. If it is not, identify the first step that fails and give a corrected version of that step, making explicit any conditionalisation the argument suppresses. Please answer in British English.
