I write about AI and brain science for a major magazine, and roughly every two weeks I get a pitch claiming that some system genuinely suffers, is conscious, or deserves moral consideration. Last month it was a customer-service bot whose makers say it gets genuinely distressed because an internal error signal spikes when users are abusive; the month before, a lab said a ball of cultured human neurons wired to a little robot learned to avoid electric shocks and asked why it gets less ethical review than a mouse. I dont have a background in neuroscience or philosophy, I usually cannot see the weights or training data, my editor signs off but leans on my judgment, and whatever I write can get a startup funded or make a serious research program sound like a hoax. I want a reusable checklist I can run in about twenty minutes before filing for how seriously to take these claims, built from whatever you think is the best account of which computations make a system matter morally. It should make me weigh behavior, the kind of internal processing, the substrate, and whether there is any sense in which the system is a subject, in a repeatable order. It should say honestly which steps I cannot actually execute and what I should do instead, like exactly what to ask a lab to release. Then run it on three cases: the stressed chatbot, the shock-avoiding neurons, and a third case of your choosing where the checklist tells me to take seriously something I would be embarrassed to put in print. And tell me what the procedure cannot settle, no matter how carefully I run it.
