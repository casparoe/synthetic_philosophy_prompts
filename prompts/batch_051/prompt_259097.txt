I am putting together a unit on whether the Bronze Age Mediterranean, the Classic Maya lowlands, and Easter Island are best described as cases of societal collapse or as cases of societies changing under stress, and I have found that the word "collapse" tends to do the argumentative work before anyone has said what it has to mean. I want you to write out the desiderata that an account of societal collapse must meet before it is entitled to say of a past society that it collapsed rather than changed. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable for any minimally adequate account, and which are really preferences of one camp — collapse pessimists, continuity-minded archaeologists, systems theorists — that a rival could reject without incoherence. If some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, say so directly; for instance, a demand that collapse be a single bounded event sits awkwardly with the slow, patchy, population-level continuity visible in the Maya case, and the list may have to choose. That kind of tension is the part I most need to see. Finally, identify the one or two requirements that do the most work in excluding candidate accounts, such as "loss of organizational complexity," "elite disappearance," "depopulation," or "loss of writing and monumental building," and say what those requirements still leave open. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring the candidate accounts.
