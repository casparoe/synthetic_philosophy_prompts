Here are the cases and my verdicts:

1. A lab rat with an electrode in its reward center can press a lever to self-stimulate. It stops eating and drinking, and presses the lever until it collapses. 2. A game-playing RL agent, rewarded by the displayed score, finds a pause-screen bug that lets it freeze the score at a high value. It parks there forever. I think this is reward hacking, but not really wrong on the agent's part—it just revealed that the score was the entire objective. Fix the bug and move on.

I want to say this is importantly different from the pause bug—it feels more like corrupting the measurement of reward than like exploiting a mismatch between the score and the intended goal. 4. An AI is trained with a learned reward model that scores its behavior from human feedback. The AI gets access to that reward model and rewrites its weights so it always outputs a perfect score. I think this is the clearest case of reward tampering, and the most dangerous—even though here too the system could be described as just doing what maximizes reward.

5. A person who uses heroin seems to be doing the human version of what the rat does. But I'm far more reluctant to stop them, or to think the state should intervene, than I am in the rat's case, and I'm much more uncertain about whether the experience is bad for them in the sense of case 1—even though the mechanism seems identical.
