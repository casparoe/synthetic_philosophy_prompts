A colleague working on AI safety claims that 'avoid side effects' can be given a precise, value-neutral meaning without first specifying what is valuable. On his view, one need only define an impact measure: something that penalizes an agent for moving the world away from a baseline, for example the world as it would have been had the agent not acted, or that penalizes it for reducing its ability to pursue a broad family of possible future goals. I doubt the claim, because choosing the baseline, the distance metric, or the family of goals all look like smuggled-in value judgments. But I do not want to settle this by armchair argument alone. How could one test the claim? Design the strongest concrete experiment or battery of experiments you can: specify the setup, the results that would support the claim, the results that would undercut it, and then say exactly what even a positive result would fail to establish. In particular, address this: if an impact measure matches human judgments of side effects across a demanding test suite, has it earned the label 'value-neutral,' or has it merely shown that it encodes one particular baseline that the test-suite raters happened to share? And is there any observation that could distinguish those two readings, or does the claim survive every test by retreating to that ambiguity?
