I was reading about the doomsday argument and noticed that its core move — treating one's birth rank as an observation drawn from the class of all humans who will ever live — has almost exactly the structure of the German tank problem from wartime statistics, where the serial numbers of captured tanks are treated as uniformly drawn from the full production run and used to estimate its total size. In both cases, an index observed at a single position is taken to be informative about the size of an encompassing class, and the fact that the observed index is not startlingly high licenses an upper-bound-ish inference about that total. This seems to me like the self-sampling assumption (SSA) wearing the respectable clothing of ordinary statistical estimation. Is this parallel deep or superficial? Specifically, what feature of the original tank-problem setting makes the inference there solid, and does the doomsday argument actually satisfy an analogous condition once the class being sampled is partly future and not antecedently fixed? And if the analogy is deep, does it raise the epistemic standing of SSA, or does it instead reveal that the tank-problem analogy smuggles in an assumption about how observation opportunities are generated — the very thing SSA and SIA disagree about?
