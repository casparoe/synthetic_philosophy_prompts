I teach a senior seminar on the problem of evil, and every time we get to the fall of the angels the room splits into two camps. One side says free will alone can't explain how a perfectly good, fully informed being could ever choose evil: a rational will, they say, can only choose what it sees as good, and a being with no defect wouldn't misjudge that badly. The other side says once you grant a creature genuine self-determination, you've already granted that its choices aren't entailed by it's nature or situation, so a first turn toward self-will has to be possible even from a perfect starting point. I'm tired of watching the same intuitions crash into each other, so I want to set the class a construction task instead.

Here's what I'd like from you. Build a thought experiment — call it a minimal fall scenario — about a created rational agent that meets these specifications, in this order: (a) the agent is created with a wholly good nature, with no privation, defect, or ignorance; (b) there is no tempter and no environmental pressure of any kind; (c) the agent could have refrained; (d) the first evil choice is intelligible rather than a brute stipulation, yet nothing in the agent's nature, knowledge, or situation makes that choice probable or inevitable; (e) the creator cannot reasonably be described as the author of the evil; and (f) you may not just stipulate the verdict — the choice has to be generated by the psychology you build into the case. Then walk me through each specification and show how the scenario meets it. After that, say which single structural feature is doing the real work, what happens if you remove that feature (does it collapse back into the usual free-will-defense story, or into an inexplicable event, or something else?), and how someone in the "free will can't do this" camp would attack the case. The whole point is to isolate the variable, so be honest about anywhere you've papered over a gap. Oh, and answer in British English please — my students are a mix and I'd like the handout to be in one convention.
