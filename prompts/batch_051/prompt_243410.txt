I'm a PhD student in neuroethics and I keep going back and forth on this one. Suppose that in 2027 a cheap, wearable, noninvasive BCI hits the market that can transcribe a person's inner speech—the little voice in your head—with roughly 90% accuracy, but it cannot read sensory imagery, emotional tone, or motor intentions. It only works when the wearer deliberately switches it on, and the wearer sees exactly what the device transcribes. Now imagine the tech improves steadily over the next twenty-five years, but that core limitation stays in place: it only ever accesses linguistically encoded propositional thought, not raw feeling or intentions. I want you to think through what happens to mental privacy and cognitive liberty in that world. Does the law come to treat inner speech more like speech or more like thought? How do employment contracts, police interrogation, and self-incrimination doctrine adapt? Does everyday inner monologue become more guarded, or do people just stop thinking of their inner speech as the private core of the mind? And does the fact that emotion and intention remain unread matter more than most people assume? Give me a committed prediction, not a menu of scenarios.
