I've been turning over the Volkswagen diesel scandal and I want to use it to get at complicity — the idea that you can be involved in wrongdoing without yourself causing the harm. I know the basics: for years the cars had software that recognized when they were being tested and only cut emissions during the test, and on the road they belched way more pollution; a whole lot of people must of known pieces of it (engineers, managers, testers, lawyers) without any single one of them having decided "let's cheat" or having made the pollution happen. What I want is the philosophical mapping, not the timeline: which of those people were complicit and which actually caused the harm, what the theory says complicity took at each step (making it easier? looking the other way? signaling approval? not blowing the whistle?), and what it would have told them to do instead. Then run it the other way: does VW show you need a separate category of complicity, or does it show the line between causing and just being part of it is too blurry to do the work people want it too? And where does the theory just go silent here — what in the case can't it get a grip on at all?
