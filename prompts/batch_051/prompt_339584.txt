I am trying to work out what we mean, or should mean, by "death." I need a definition precise enough to decide cases, because different definitions give different answers about when it is permissible to stop life support, bury someone, or remove organs. I want a committed response rather than a neutral survey: propose one definition of death, defend it, and then test it against every case below, revising the definition or defending the resulting verdicts where they are surprising.

Two cases I regard as fixed points, and your definition should get them right:
- A person whose heart has stopped after a failed resuscitation and cannot be restarted is dead.
- A person with locked-in syndrome, awake and cognitively intact but almost completely paralyzed, is alive.

Now the cases I actually struggle with:

1. A patient on a ventilator whose entire brain, including the brainstem, has irreversibly ceased functioning; the heart still beats and the skin is warm.
2. A patient in a persistent vegetative state whose cerebral cortex is severely damaged but whose brainstem is intact, so that the patient breathes unassisted and has sleep–wake cycles without any sign of awareness.
3. A patient under deep hypothermic circulatory arrest during heart surgery, whose heart is stopped and whose brain shows no measurable activity, but who will be rewarmed and revived after the procedure.
4. A donor in a controlled donation-after-circulatory-death protocol, whose heart has stopped, for whom no resuscitation will be attempted, and from whom organs are removed after a brief, protocol-defined interval of absent circulation.
5. A pregnant woman declared dead by neurological criteria who is kept on a ventilator for weeks so that the fetus can reach viability.
6. A human embryo four days after fertilization, before implantation.
7. A patient with an irreversibly destroyed cerebral cortex but an intact brainstem, who will never regain consciousness but who breathes spontaneously.

Two intuitions pull at me throughout. One is that death is the irreversible cessation of the integrated functioning of the organism as a whole. The other is that death is the irreversible loss of consciousness or personhood. I want you to say whether these two intuitions can be unified in a single definition, or whether one must be given priority. If you think a single definition is correct, state it and defend it. If you think two definitions are needed, say exactly which definition should govern which practical question and why. In either case, apply your view to every case above, and tell me which of your verdicts you are least confident about.
