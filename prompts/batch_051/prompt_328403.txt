I've been thinking about Thomson's lamp: a lamp starts off, and I toggle it after half a minute, then again after a quarter of a minute, then an eighth, and so on, so that infinitely many toggles have occurred by the one-minute mark. I'm drawn to the supertask way of thinking about this. There is a perfectly definite schedule, each toggle happens at a well-defined earlier moment, and the lamp's state is settled at every time before the one-minute mark; the mathematics simply says the sequence of states alternates and never settles to a limit, so the schedule leaves no fact of the matter about the state at the limit. But I also have a firm intuition that at the one-minute mark a real lamp must be either on or off. A physical object cannot lack a state, whatever the mathematics says. I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that gives way, what it would take for the supertask framework to give way, and whether there is a consistent reading on which the mathematics and the physics are answering different questions. Please write the answer in British English.
