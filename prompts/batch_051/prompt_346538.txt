I've been trying to nail down my views on the arrow of time for a reading group, and I want a consistancy check before I put it in writing. My picture is roughly this: (1) the low-level dynamics relevant to thermodynamics are, as far as I can tell, time-reversal symmetric, so the past/future asymmetry is not written into the microscopic laws; (2) nevertheless the past/future distinction is objective — there's a fact of the matter which direction is toward the past, and it isnt just a projection from how our memories are organized; (3) the Past Hypothesis, that the universe began in a very low entropy state, is a brute contingent boundary condition, not something the laws explain; (4) the thermodynamic arrow is the master arrow: the causal, epistemic, and psychological arrows all reduce to entropy increasing away from that boundary; (5) the second law is statistical, not strict — entropy decrease is just overwhelmingly unlikely; (6) but causal influence really does run only from earlier to later, and I take that to be a strict lawful feature of the world, not a statistical artifact or something that would flip if the entropy gradient flipped. Do these hang together? Rank the tensions by severity, say which apparent tensions are actually fine, and tell me what the cheapest revision would be.
