I'm a graduate student writing a paper on the Knobe effect, and I've become less interested in taking a side in the debate than in understanding why the debate itself won't move. The basic finding, as I understand it, is that people give strikingly asymmetric intentionality judgments about structurally similar side-effect cases: when a chairman approves a profit-increasing program that will damage the environment as a side effect and says he doesn't care about the damage, most subjects say he harmed the environment intentionally; when the side effect is beneficial and he says he doesn't care about helping, most subjects deny that he helped intentionally. Nobody seriously disputes the asymmetry at this point. What remains contested is what it shows. One camp reads it as evidence that moral evaluation enters directly into the ordinary concept of intentional action, so that the concept itself has a normative dimension. A second camp argues that the effect is carried by social-cognitive or pragmatic factors: the harm case implicates the agent in a norm violation and licenses a blame-oriented inference about his indifference, while the help case licenses no parallel praise-oriented inference, so the asymmetry reveals something about how we read bad versus good side effects, not about the concept of intentional action as such. A third camp suspects the effect is shallow, an artifact of wording, question order, or the particular contrast between harming and helping, and would dissolve under more careful controls.

What frustrates me is that after two decades the literature seems to consist mostly of each camp producing moderators that support its own story while explaining away the others' findings as changes to the vignette or the task rather than isolations of a mechanism. Each side can point to manipulations under which the asymmetry appears, reverses, shrinks, or survives, and each side dismisses the others' manipulations as failing to test the same thing. I can't tell whether this is a substantive empirical disagreement that has simply proven hard to resolve, a verbal disagreement about what counts as intentional action, a disagreement about what counts as evidence, or several different research programs—one studying a stored concept, another studying a judgment process, another studying the pragmatics of the word intentionally—sitting under a single heading. My working hypothesis is that the field has been treating the folk concept of intentional action as one well-defined object, when what the experiments actually engage is a bundle of separable capacities, and that the impasse is downstream of that conflation. I'd like a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement rather than a verdict on the Knobe effect itself. Is the impasse verbal, conceptual, empirical, or methodological? What, precisely, would count as progress given your diagnosis—an experiment, a formal distinction, a changed question—or should we conclude that the question as posed is ill-formed and that no progress should be expected?
