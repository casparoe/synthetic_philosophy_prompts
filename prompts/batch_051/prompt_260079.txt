Im a novelist working on a book set in a religious community where family loyalty is everything. My protagonist is about to turn in her brother for a serious crime, and she keeps telling herself that God cares about justice more than nepotism. I've been circling a passage from Plato's Euthyphro, where Euthyphro is defending his decision to prosecute his own father for murder. Euthyphro says: "The real question is whether the murdered man has been justly slain. If justly, then your duty is to let the matter alone; but if unjustly, then even if the murderer lives under the same roof with you and eats at the same table, proceed against him." Write an essay on whether the Euthyphro dilemma actually undermines this kind of picture — the idea that the right thing and the God-beloved thing are the same. Weigh the main interpretations, but don't end on a non-committal note. I need a definite conclusion I can use for my character's arc. Treat the passage as the starting point, not as a abstract decoration.
