I have been developing a distinction I call the effort/error split and want an honest assessment. The intended application is inferring, from the outside, what an optimizer is optimizing and what space it is searching. The idea: the objective is read off from effort — which candidate moves the optimizer refines, revisits, and keeps paying costs to improve — while the search space is read off from error — which alternatives the optimizer treats as live enough to try, including the wrong ones it nearly commits to. The claim is that objectives leave their signature in the distribution of effort, and search spaces leave theirs in the distribution of error. Watching a chess engine, for example, you could recover its evaluation function from the positions it keeps returning to, and recover its pruning from the tempting lines it never considers and the bad lines it nearly plays. I know this sounds adjacent to inverse reinforcement learning and revealed preference, but I intend the split differently: effort signals objective, error signals search space. Is the distinction coherent, or does it collapse once you ask what counts as effort and what counts as error? Has it been made before, and under what name? And what case most clearly breaks it — where effort says little about the objective, or error says little about the search space?
