A friend summarized Popper's critique of historicism in The Poverty of Historicism for me like this: 'Popper's central claim is that history, unlike natural science, has no regularities to discover, because every historical event is unique; so the very idea of a law of history — a law permitting large-scale prediction of the course of society — is ruled out from the start. Social wholes such as "society" or "the economy" are not real objects of study; they are theoretical constructs, so laws of historical development framed in terms of them are empty. Moreover, large-scale social prediction can never succeed even in principle, because any prediction about society will alter the behavior of the people it predicts about and thereby defeat the prediction — the Oedipus effect. So historicist programs like Marxism are not merely unscientific; they are strictly impossible.' I suspect this makes Popper sound more sweeping than he is, but I cannot tell exactly where the distortion enters. Which parts of the summary are faithful, which parts overstate or subtly misread him, and do my friend's conclusions actually follow from the accurate parts alone?
