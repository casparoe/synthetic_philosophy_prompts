I'm TAing a mid-level philosophy course called "Love and Its Reasons," and I'm grading a short-answer question on the appraisal/bestowal debate. The exact question I gave was: "Explain the difference between appraisal and bestowal theories of love, sort Frankfurt, Velleman, and Kolodny into the two seperate families (noting any awkward fits), and then give what you take to be the strongest single objection to bestowal." One student wrote:

"Appraisal theories say love tracks value the beloved already has: you love someone because of their qualities, like kindness or wit. Bestowal theories reverse the direction and say that loving someone confers value on them. Frankfurt is bestowal because he says what we love gets its importance from the fact that we love it, and that love gives us final ends. Velleman is appraisal because he thinks loving someone is appreciating their particular virtues and responding to that specific excellence. Kolodny is basically appraisal too: he says we value our relationship with the person, and a relationship is just one more valuable property of the beloved. So the three all fit the two camps. The strongest objection to bestowal is that it makes love arbitrary—if love creates the value, nothing makes the beloved more choiceworthy than anyone else, even though lovers usually can say what it is about the person that they love."

I want to grade this fairly. Give me a grade out of 10 and then exactly five numbered comments justifying that grade, each comment one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
