I keep coming across the claim that what makes something pseudohistory rather than just wrong history—Fomenko, ancient aliens, Atlantis—is that pseudohistory is insensitive to evidence: no matter what turns up, the story adjusts and survives. In my old line of work a hypothesis earned its keep by making risky predictions, so I want to know whether that claim about pseudohistory is itself testable. Design the strongest test you can of the hypothesis that pseudohistorical programs are unusually resistant to recalcitrant evidence. Take some body of claims and a stream of new evidence, and say exactly what you would measure, what pattern of results would count as showing the claimed difference between a Fomenko-style program and a normal revisionist historian, and then be honest about what even a clean positive result would leave open—for instance whether evidence-responsiveness is the real boundary or just one diagnostic symptom among others.
