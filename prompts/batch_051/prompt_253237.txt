I keep noticing a pattern in how people—and occasionally I myself—slide from “it’s possible” to “there’s a real chance,” and I’m trying to figure out whether this is one identifiable mistake, a family of related mistakes, or sometimes no mistake at all. A friend got a medical test result with a tiny false-positive rate and reasoned, “It’s possible the result is wrong, so maybe I shouldn’t start the medication.” That struck me as off, but I couldn’t say exactly what had gone wrong. In a discussion about whether we might be living in a simulation, someone said, “We can’t rule it out, so it’s basically fifty-fifty,” treating a mere lack of disproof as if it assigned odds. I’ve also seen the mirror image: someone claimed that if an outcome has probability zero—a spinner landing on exactly one point, say—then it’s impossible, even though the spinner must land somewhere.

Are these all the same phenomenon? Is there an established term for it, or for parts of it? I’m not satisfied with a bare name: I want to know what exactly the slide consists in, what it is not, and where its edges are. In particular, when is it legitimate to move from “possible” to “there’s a chance,” and does “chance” mean the same thing in the medical case, the simulation case, and the spinner case? If the question rests on a false unity—if I’m lumping together things that should be kept apart, or if the task can’t be done well as posed—please say so plainly instead of giving me a tidy answer.
