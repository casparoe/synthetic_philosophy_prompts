I'm putting together a handout for an undergrad philosophy of mind discussion group and I want it to be a decision tree that sorts people into an account of what 'physical' means in the sentence 'everything is physical.' The engine of the thing is Hempel's dilemma: if 'physical' means 'whatever current physics describes,' then physicalism is probably false, since current physics is incomplete and will very likely be revised (and it's silent about, say, phenomenal consciousness). But if 'physical' means 'whatever the final, finished physics describes,' the thesis looks either trivially true or impossible to evaluate right now, since final physics could in principle end up including whatever it needs to include and we can't inspect it in advance. So the tree should start from that fork and keep going.

I want the leaves to include at least: currentism (physical = current physics, so physicalism is a revisable empirical bet and currently false around the edges); future-physics physicalism (physical = the ideal completed theory, which makes physicalism hard to evaluate now); via negativa (physical = whatever is not fundamentally mental); methodological or attitudinal physicalism (physicalism = a policy of deferring to physics-style methods rather than a claim about what the world contains); and maybe a structuralist option (physical = whatever plays the roles and relations current-or-future physics describes, with the intrinsic nature of the stuff left open). Where two of these collapse into each other at some branches, or where the tree can't separate two leaves with any question you can come up with, say so instead of forcing a fake distinction. Where a combination of answers is one nobody actually occupies, give a quick diagnosis — incoherent, just unattractive, or unexplored. Aim for six or seven leaves, and give each leaf one sentence about the position's most awkward commitment. The questions should sort people by what they'd actually say about concrete cases — e.g., 'would you still call a world physical if final physics posited a fundamental conscious field?' — not by wether they like the label 'via negativa.'
