For a work-in-progress that I am presenting to my department, I have been asked to put my views on the epistemology of testimony into a compact list of commitments, and I would like a consistency audit of that list before I circulate it. Here is what I hold.

(1) Reductionism about testimonial justification: testimony is not a fundamental source of justification. A hearer's entitlement to believe what another person tells her is always reducible to her perceptual, memorial, and inferential capacities, together with background information about the reliability of testimony in the relevant domain.

(2) Positive-reasons requirement: a hearer is justified in accepting a particular report only if she actually possesses reasons sufficient to support the speaker's reliability on that occasion. It is not enough that she merely lacks defeaters; she needs positive inductive grounds.

(3) Early testimonial knowledge: ordinarily developing young children acquire knowledge from what their caregivers tell them well before they can articulate, and probably before they consciously hold, anything like an inductive justification for the reliability of those caregivers or of speech in general.

(4) Global dependence: an adult's epistemic position depends constitutively on the word of others across nearly every domain of belief that matters — history, current events, science, the layout of places one has not visited — and there is no non-testimonial route by which any individual could verify more than a fraction of this.

(5) Anti-skeptical upshot: this global dependence does not render ordinary testimonial belief irrational. Much of what one accepts on the say-so of others amounts to knowledge, and a skeptic who insists that each item be independently verified is holding testimony to a standard to which we hold no other source.

(6) Non-circular anchoring: reductionism is not viciously circular in accounting for our general practice of relying on testimony. Justification for trusting particular sources may in part draw on the word of other sources, provided that the network of testimonial reliance as a whole is ultimately anchored in non-testimonial evidence.

Treat this as an audit rather than a defense of the package. Which pairs of these commitments are genuinely in tension, and how severe is each tension? Which apparent tensions dissolve once the relevant notions — for example, what it is to possess reasons — are made precise? If something must give, what is the least costly revision and what would that revision forfeit? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating all of them as equally serious.
