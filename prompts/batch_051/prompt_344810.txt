My dad's cancer came back last month and the doctors don't expect him to see next spring. I've been spending most weekends at my parents' place, and a lot of my energy has gone into the question of how to think about the time left — whether the shape of the next few months is already settled or still open.

Last week a friend from my old physics study group said something that's been rattling around in my head since. He told me pretty flatly that special relativity shows the future is just as real as the past. His version: for anything in my future — including my dad's death — there's some observer somewhere, moving the right way or far enough away, whose "right now" already includes that moment. So it isn't an open question at all; it's already part of the world, just located somewhere I can't get to yet, like the far side of the country. He meant it kindly, I think, like he was offering a way to stop fighting time. But I've felt half-paralyzed since, and half-suspicious that there's a hidden step in there.

I can't untangle what's settled phsyics and what's philosophy, and I also can't tell whether any of it actually changes what I should do. If the future is already real, does that mean I should stop hoping, stop treating outcomes as open, or somehow accept it all now? Or is the whole argument only as strong as the assumption that whatever is simultaneous with something real is itself real? Give me a clear-eyed read: how much of my friend's physics is right, how much of his metaphysics is right, and what actually follows for how I spend these months.
