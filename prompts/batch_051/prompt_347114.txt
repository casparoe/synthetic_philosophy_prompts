In my political philosophy course I’m trying to get the students to see whether Mill’s late views on socialism are a genuine break from the older defense of private property in the *Principles of Political Economy*, or just a shift in emphasis. To do that I want a case built to order.

Take this passage from Mill’s unfinished *Chapters on Socialism*: “Now these are the very class which, in the vocabulary of the higher ranks, are said to have no stake in the country. Of course they have in reality the greatest stake, since their daily bread depends on its prosperity.”

Here’s the assignment I’d give my students, and I want you to do it well enough that I can use it as a model answer. Construct a historically plausible but imaginary case from, say, the 1880s English cutlery trade. The specs are these: (i) one firm or workshop with a specific productive technology and market structure; (ii) no revolution, no perfectly informed state planners, no cooperative idyll, just ordinary commercial life; (iii) in the case, the principle Mill states in that passage—that those whose daily bread depends on prosperity have the greatest stake—recommends worker control or at least a serious share in management, while the utilitarian test he uses in the *Principles*—that private property is justified only insofar as it promotes the general welfare—recommends keeping the existing owner-manager structure; and (iv) don’t stipulate the verdicts, make them fall out of ordinary facts about wages, skill, credit, and risk.

After you’ve laid out the story, show the reasoning for each test and verdict. Then identify the specific structural feature of the case that does the work—maybe high fixed capital, workers’ firm-specific skills, volatile demand, whatever—and say what would happen to the divergence if you removed just that feature. Finish by explaining how a defender of the continuity thesis, who says Mill’s late socialist sympathies were just an extension of his earlier views, would attack your case. The point isn’t to settle whether Mill was a socialist; its to force the conflict into the open.
