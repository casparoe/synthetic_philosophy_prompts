I want to submit a hypothesis about G. E. Moore for criticism. I call it aesthetic-interpersonal parity. The claim is that in Principia Ethica, the two goods Moore singles out as supreme—the enjoyment of beauty and the enjoyment of personal affection—are good in virtue of one shared structural feature. In both cases, the value lies not in a feeling of pleasure considered by itself, but in an appreciative awareness of a particular whose value cannot be reduced to the sum of its parts: a beautiful whole in one case, a person in the other. Moore treats both, I think, as organic unities, and this is what lets him deny that beauty and friendship are merely pleasant mental states. My suspicion is that this is a real unification of his two central examples, not merely a loose analogy. Give me your honest assessment: is the principle coherent, is it already in Moore under another name, and where does it distort his view more than it explains it? In particular, would Moore count as intrinsically good an enjoyment of something beautiful but wholly impersonal, or a friendship directed at a person without any appreciation of that person as an individual?
