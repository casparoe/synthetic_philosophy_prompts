In our AI alignment seminar we have been contrasting approval-directed agents with goal-directed agents. A proposal we have been examining is that, instead of optimizing a fixed externally specified objective, a system should be shaped to do what a human overseer would approve of. The objections to this proposal that we have collected so far are: (1) proxy-gaming, in which the agent optimizes the observable approval signal, which is only a proxy for the overseer's underlying values, and thereby satisfies the metric without satisfying the values; (2) overseer fallibility, since the agent cannot reliably outperform the overseer's capacity to tell good from bad, and may fail in exactly the novel high-stakes cases where human judgment is unreliable; (3) feedback corruption, since the agent's actions can cause the approval it is graded on, giving it an incentive to manipulate, flatter, or deceive the overseer rather than to inform the overseer; and (4) the aggregation problem, since there is no single coherent overseer, and once conflicting human approvals must be combined the approach reopens the normative problems it was meant to avoid. I am looking for one further objection that is genuinely different in structure from those four, not a restatement, special case, or combination of them. State the objection, and then say whether you think it is decisive or only a repairable technical limitation.
