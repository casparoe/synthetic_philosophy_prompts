I am working through the literature on the justification of deduction, and before I read one more defense of inferentialism, rule-circular justification, conceptual-role semantics, or a transcendental argument, I want the specification a theory in this area has to satisfy. The standing irritant is Carroll's tortoise. The tortoise grants the premises of a simple argument, grants that if the premises are true then the conclusion must be true, and still refuses to detach the conclusion; when a further conditional is supplied as a new premise, the refusal simply recurs. Everyone can feel that something has gone wrong, but it is surprisingly hard to say what, and the different diagnoses pull the theory in different directions.

I would like you to write the desiderata for an adequate account of why deductive reasoning is justified: the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must get right, and the distinctions it must respect, with the consideration or miniature case behind each entry. For each desideratum, say whether it is a non-negotiable constraint on any competent account or merely the favored commitment of a particular camp. At minimum, adjudicate the following candidate demands — deciding in each case whether it is genuine or a prejudice — before adding any I have missed: no account may represent an inference rule as a further premise on pain of the tortoise's regress; an account must distinguish the validity of an inference from an agent's entitlement to draw it; it must explain or defuse the apparent circularity in any deductive defense of deduction; it must respect the datum that competent reasoners apply rules without explicitly representing them; it must preserve the normativity of consequence rather than reducing it to how people happen to reason; and it must not quietly assume the correctness of a particular logic while claiming to justify deduction in general.

Finally, tell me which single desideratum does the most work in eliminating popular positions, and whether any pair of the requirements you settle on looks difficult to satisfy jointly — a real tension between the list's entries is itself a result, so do not smooth it over. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on a scorecard of named authors.
