I am an undergraduate enrolled in a module on bounded rationality and satisficing, and I am preparing my assessed essay for the course. The essay concerns a counterfactual question about Herbert Simon. Simon contrasted satisficing, understood as halting search once an option clears some aspiration level, with maximising, understood as comparing the full set of feasible alternatives. He did not live to see contemporary large language models. I want to argue for a defensible answer to this question: had Simon examined a current large language model, would he have classified its operation as satisficing, as maximising under explicit computational constraints, or as neither, and what qualification or extension of his framework would he have introduced before applying that classification? Please write a formal, structured answer in British English. Your answer should first state the classification you think Simon would most likely have settled on, then reconstruct the considerations from his framework that pull towards each of the three options, including his views on aspiration levels, stopping rules, and satisficing at the level of organisations rather than individuals, then identify the consideration you take to be decisive for him, and finally set out the qualification he would most likely have insisted upon. In that final part, please address the worry that any satisficing involved may belong to the developers who set the training objective rather than to the model itself, and explain how Simon's analysis of organisations, tools, and artefacts might bear on that worry.
