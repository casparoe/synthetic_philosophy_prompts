I keep seeing people cite the old Mikolov word2vec paper as if the vector arithmetic stuff settles a bunch of questions about whether embeddings have “real” semantic structure. In *Efficient Estimation of Word Representations in Vector Space* (Mikolov et al., 2013), they say: "Somewhat surprisingly, it was found that similarity of word representations goes beyond simple syntactic regularities." That’s a pretty strong conceptual claim, and the paper then motivates it with the king-man-woman-queen offset. But I’ve never seen a clean discussion of what experiment would actually distinguish “goes beyond simple syntactic regularities” from “co-occurrence statistics produce a useful-looking analogy test.” Suppose I want to operationalize that claim. What would a concrete test look like, what result would count as evidence for genuine semantic structure rather than surface regularity, and what would such a result still not settle? I’m less interested in “add more analogy pairs” and more in what the test would force you to believe about the geometry of meaning.
