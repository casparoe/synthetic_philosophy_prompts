I've been TAing intro real analysis this term and it's forced me to actually think about something I used to just accept: why definitions are the way they are. Every week an undergrad asks me why we define continuity in terms of inverse images of open sets instead of the epsilon-delta version they learned in calculus, and I've realized I don't have a satisfying answer beyond 'it generalizes better.' So I've spent the semester trying to build an actual view, and I want a honest verdict on it.

Here's my theory. The right definition of a mathematical notion is the weakest hypothesis that keeps the cluster of theorems you actually care about true, while still being statable in the widest range of systems where those theorems could make sense. Open sets are my main example: defining continuity as taking preimages of open sets to be open is just the epsilon-delta idea with the metric stripped away. Open sets are the minimal furniture a space needs for nearness and limits to behave at all, so you're not really defining continuity — you're defining the leanest setting in which the usual continuity theorems can even be stated. Groups are my other example: closure, associativity, identity, and inverse aren't capturing some hidden essence of symmetry; they're the weakest package that makes the symmetries of any object compose and invert the way functions do, so any theorem you prove about groups automatically lands in every symmetry system. I call the good-making feature of a definition its theorem shadow: the set of theorems it makes natural and the counterexamples it makes easy to see. A definition is bad when it's too strong, ruling out examples where the theorems would still work, or too weak, so the theorems break and you have to keep patching up exceptions. I'm aware this might just be 'we pick the useful generalization' dressed up in jargon, and I vaguely remember Lakatos or someone claiming definitions get revised when counterexamples show up — that sounds related but more about the history than the optimization.

So: is this coherent, or have I reinvented something obvious? Does it already exist under another name? And where does my account break down — give me the best case you can of a definition everyone agrees is the right one but which my weakest-hypothesis-keeping-the-theorems story can't explain.
