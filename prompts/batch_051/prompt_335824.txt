An intro ethics reader my seminar used summarizes supererogation like this: "A supererogatory act is one that is morally optional but morally better than what duty requires; it goes beyond the call of duty, like donating a kidney to a stranger or jumping on a grenade. The standard picture is that such acts cannot be required because they are unusually costly or risky, and no plausible moral theory would demand heroism of everyone. The main philosophical difficulty is just that standard deontic logic has trouble modeling this category: its permission operator marks an act as permissible, but doesn't distinguish the merely allowed from the above-and-beyond. Once you add a separate ideal, or a category of the saintly, the puzzle dissolves." I keep feeling this isnt quite fair, especially around why optionality and betterness are in tension, but I can't say exactly what it gets wrong.
