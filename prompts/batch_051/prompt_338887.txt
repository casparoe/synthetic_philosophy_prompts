I have been asked to write out my position on mystical experience and ineffability for a seminar, and I would like an audit of whether the following commitments form a stable set. I am not asking which view is right; I want a map of where the set pulls apart — which tensions are real, which are merely apparent, and what the cheapest repairs would be.

My commitments:

1. Noetic character. At least some mystical states — I use the term broadly to include certain contemplative and spontaneous peak states — are noetic: they are not just powerful feelings but states in which the subject is put in cognitive contact with something real.

2. In-principle ineffability. The content of such a state cannot be stated. This is not the weak claim that it is hard to articulate, that the memory fades, or that our vocabulary currently fails. It is the strong claim that no sentence in any possible human language expresses what is thereby known.

3. Propositional strictness. There is no knowledge of an inexpressible truth. Knowledge that something is the case is a relation to a true proposition, and every proposition is at least in principle expressible in some statement. What can remain genuinely unsayable is at most acquaintance or know-how; to speak of unsayable facts is a category mistake.

4. Acquaintance realism. Knowing a person is not exhausted by any list of true statements about them, and it is nonetheless knowledge in the fullest sense rather than a metaphor. Likewise, one can know something by acquaintance whose content cannot be exhaustively captured propositionally.

5. Apophatic transmission. The negations, paradoxes, contradictions, and silences of the mystical traditions are not merely rhetorical or therapeutically useful. They are a genuine means by which knowledge is transmitted: a teacher can bring a student to know what the teacher knows, and the student's knowledge can outstrip the teacher's explicit statements.

For each real tension, I would like it ranked by severity, with a note on what would have to be given up to resolve it and what the cheapest fix would be. I would also like the apparent tensions flagged as such, with a sentence on why they dissolve, and a suggestion about which single revision of the set would do the most work.
