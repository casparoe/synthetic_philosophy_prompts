A philosopher I know teaches a graduate seminar on the ethics of killing, and this term she has been giving her students case after case without ever stating the principle behind her verdicts. I have been keeping a list of what she says. So far she has judged:

1. The redirected trolley: a runaway trolley is heading toward five workers; you flip a switch in order to save them, though this sends the trolley onto a side track where it kills one worker. She says this is permissible.

2. The footbridge: a runaway trolley is heading toward five; you push a large bystander off a footbridge onto the track in order to save the five, and his body stops the trolley but he is killed. She says this is impermissible.

3. The transplant: a surgeon kills an unsuspecting healthy person and distributes his organs among five patients who would otherwise die. She says this is impermissible.

4. The lethal injection: a doctor injects a fatal dose of potassium chloride into a competent, terminally ill patient who has repeatedly asked to be killed, because the patient wants to die. She says this is impermissible.

5. The refused donation: a person declines to donate a kidney to an unrelated stranger who will otherwise die within a year, and the stranger dies. She says this is permissible.

6. The removed ventilator: a doctor, at a competent terminal patient's informed request, removes a ventilator so that the patient dies of the underlying disease rather than a drug. She says this is permissible.

Next week she will put the following case to the seminar. The trolley is again heading toward five workers, and one worker is on the side track. This time the agent flips the switch not to save the five, but in order to kill the one; he has been looking for a way to kill that person, and the five workers' survival is a foreseeable side effect he does not care about. Physically, it is the same event as the first case.

I want to work out what she will say before the class meets. Give me a committed prediction: what verdict would she give on this new case, which principle is she really running (something about intending death versus allowing it as a foreseen side effect, or something about not using a person's body or not initiating a lethal sequence, or something else), and what would each of the live candidate principles predict about the new case? Then name the further case you would give her to most sharply distinguish the candidates. If her earlier verdicts genuinely underdetermine the answer, say exactly which principle she would be committing herself to with each possible verdict on the new case, and which verdict you are placing your bets on and why.
