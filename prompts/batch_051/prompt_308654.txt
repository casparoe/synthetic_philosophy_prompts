A friend recently read a popular science book whose argument he summarised for me as follows: the human brain is a finite physical system, so it can occupy only finitely many total states; since conscious experience supervenes on those states, there could only ever be a finite list of distinct experiences available to a human being. The author therefore concludes that the space of possible experience is exhaustible in principle, and that an immortal person would eventually work through the list and lapse into cycles of pure repetition, though the list is so astronomically long that no ordinary lifespan comes anywhere near exhausting it. The summary seemed plausible but perhaps too tidy, and I do not know which parts to trust. Which elements of this account are likely faithful to the underlying argument, where does the summary overstate or conflate, and does the claim that an immortal life would become repetitious actually follow from the finite-state premise? Please also indicate how confident you are in the various parts of your assessment, and single out the point you are least sure about.
