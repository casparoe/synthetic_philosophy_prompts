Here's a counterfactual I've been turning over, and I'd like your reasoned prediction, not a story.

Suppose a binding international convention enters into force with teeth: any victim group, successor state, or citizen can bring a state before a standing historical wrongs panel, and if the panel finds the state bears institutional responsibility for some past wrong, the state has eighteen months to issue a formal apology that (a) names what happened, (b) accepts responsibility without hedging, and (c) is followed by no "but," no contextualizing statement, no paragraph about how the past is a foreign country. The apology must be delivered by the current head of government in a recorded public ceremony. Crucially, the apology is one-shot: once issued, the wrong is legally closed for apologetic purposes forever—no tribunal can revisit it, no further apology for the same wrong can be demanded, and the state gets a binding release from all apology-related claims for that wrong. Failure to apologize triggers escalating sanctions.

Now give me your best prediction of how this plays out over the next thirty years. I want the causal chain: which kinds of states comply early and which drag their heels, and why. How do governments try to comply with the letter while gutting the substance—through wording choices, timing, choosing a weak audience, burying the ceremony, or picking a scapegoat to deliver it? Does making the apology compulsory and terminal cheapen it, or does the ceremony-plus-closure combo give it a different kind of force? How do domestic publics and victim groups react once they realize the closure rule means a bad apology is all they'll ever get? And what gets pushed into the space apologies used to occupy—reparations claims, criminal prosecutions, memorials, or something else? For each main link, tell me whether you're fairly confident, guessing, or somewhere in between, and say what would have to be true about the world for that particular prediction to fail. Also, predict where the equilibrium settles: does the supply of demanded apologies actually go up, as groups race to get one before a state can use its one-shot, or down, as states find that the legal release makes the gesture less valuable to them than silence?
