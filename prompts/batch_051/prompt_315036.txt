In my philosophy club we've been arguing about having kids and whether its wrong to create a person, and I ended up defending a view I'm honestly not sure I can hold onto, so I want to stress-test it. The view is what someone in the club said philosophers call the procreation asymmetry. It's this pair of claims: if a kid would have a life that's just misery from start to finish, bringing them into existence is wrong and it's good that you didn't; but if a kid would have a happy life, not bringing them into existence isn't bad, and you have no obligation to create them. So it's an asymmetry—strong reasons against making miserable people, no parallel reasons for making happy ones. I actually feel this way: I'd judge someone for knowingly having a child with a condition that meant constant suffering, but I don't think a couple that decides to stay childfree is doing anything wrong, and I don't think anyone has a duty to have as many happy children as possible. The thing I keep tripping over is that my two reactions seem to treat non-existence like a state in one case and like the mere absence of a state in the other, which makes me suspicious that I'm just rationalizing a gut feeling.

Anyway, I don't want a balanced pros and cons list—I want you to take a definite position. What would actually count against this asymmetry? Like, which observations, arguments, thought experiments, or consequences of the view should move me away from it if I were being honest, sorted from decisive to merely awkward? I especially want to hear what defenders of the view themselves would concede is a real problem, not just what critics say. And be straight with me about whether the view can be qualified so that nothing sinks it, and whether that insulation is actually a problem for a moral claim like this. Is this the kind of thing that could be wrong, or does it just adjust itself to every counterexample?
