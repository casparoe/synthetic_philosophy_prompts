Okay so I keep going back and forth on the Chinese Room argument, specifically Dennett's comeback that Searle is just assuming there's such a thing as "original" intentionality in the first place, and that once you notice the brain is also the product of evolution and training, the distinction collapses—we're all two-bitsers, cheap mechanisms that can be fooled, no more genuinely "about" quarters than a vending machine is. I want a poem that does that move rather than explaining it.

Write a villanelle from the point of view of a vending machine that has just taken a slug for a quarter and dispensed a candy bar. The two refrains should be exactly: (A) "No one in here knows what a quarter is"—the Searle line, in the machine's voice, disowning any real understanding—and (B) "And neither does the hand that drops one in"—the Dennett line, turning it back on the person who thinks their intentionality is original. The refrains should alternate and feel like they're talking past each other, and then in the final two lines of the villanelle they should land next to each other so its clear the second one was the answer to the first all along. A Dennett-Searle person should recognize the argument; a reader who's never heard of either should still get a decent villanelle about a slightly haunted vending machine.
