I've been teaching an upper-level epistemology seminar for years, and this past term was the first time I felt genuinely lost about how to grade. Half the final papers were clearly sharpened by an LLM at some point in the pipeline, but only a handful of students could walk me through what their own paper was arguing when I asked in office hours. I don't want to spend my life running an AI-detection police state, but I also can't keep treating polished prose as evidence of understanding.

A colleague is now pushing a proposal for the department, and I'm trying to think through what it would actually do if we adopted it. The idea is this: every submitted essay has a box to tick, either "written without AI assistance" or "AI-assisted." If you tick "unaided," the essay counts for 90% and there's a quick 10% oral check, just enough to confirm you know what's in your own paper. If you tick "AI-assisted," the split flips to 60/40: the essay counts for 60%, and 40% comes from a ten-minute, no-notes oral, scheduled on short notice during the exam period, in which you have to reconstruct the central argument, name the weakest premise, and answer one or two questions about how you would revise it. If someone ticks "unaided" but is later found to have used AI anyway, the 60/40 split applies retroactively and the total grade is capped at 60. The oral can also be re-administered if the grader thinks it was rehersed.

Walk me through how this would actually play out. What would it incentivize among students, not just in whether they use AI, but in what they write, what they declare, and how they prepare for the oral? How do they game the oral itself, the short-notice scheduling, and the detection of false "unaided" declarations? What does it do to grading workloads, grade appeals, and the student-faculty relation? And does any version of this actually protect the link between writing and understanding, or does it just become another bueraucratic monster that selects for students who can memorize their own argument for ten minutes?
