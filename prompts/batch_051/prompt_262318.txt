A friend who has been working through Schrödinger's Vedantic reflections laid out the following argument for the unity of consciousness, and I can neither accept its conclusion nor locate the step that fails.

(1) Each of us directly knows exactly one consciousness, as a single field of experience; my changing states appear within a common awareness, and I never introspect a second awareness standing alongside it.

(2) The belief that there are many distinct consciousnesses must therefore be an inference from behavior and anatomy: I encounter bodies much like mine and, by analogy with the one case I know directly, posit a private interior behind each.

(3) That analogy is too weak to establish a fundamental plurality of minds. At best it secures a plurality of bodies, lives, and streams of experience; these could equally be currents of one consciousness without my noticing any difference.

(4) Nor is there any direct evidence of a boundary between one consciousness and another, since every boundary one might appeal to—a skull, a body, a biography—is itself given only within consciousness, as an object of experience.

(5) The apparently separate minds are therefore at best relatively isolated features of one consciousness, as waves are features of one ocean; the distinction between my consciousness and yours is a practical construction, not an ultimate fact.

(6) Hence the individual self is only a surface appearance of the one self, and the same consciousness runs through all our lives.

That conclusion seems to prove too much. If nothing ultimately separates my mind from yours, it becomes hard to explain why I should care about my own future in a way I need not care about another's, or why persons should be held responsible for their own acts; birth and death are likewise reduced to mere turns in a single current. I want to keep the ordinary conviction that there really are many distinct minds, and I also want to keep (1) and (2), which seem only to record the facts of first-person experience and the everyday route to believing in other minds. But I cannot see how to keep all of that while resisting (6). Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
