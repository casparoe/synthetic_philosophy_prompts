ok so ive been going down the ai risk / longtermism rabbit hole and i keep coming back to the singleton hypothesis. the rough idea is that at some point you end up with a single decision-making agency at the top level of the world — one ai, one world government, whatever — because coordination between multiple powerful agents is unstable: whoever gets a decisive strategic advantage first can stop any rival from ever appearing, and before that everyone is stuck in a race to the bottom. and when you read the people who defend it, the theory sounds almost elegant: one top level decider solves collective action problems, stops arms races, prevents the kind of existential screw-up where a bunch of roughly equal powers blow each other up, and lets you actually plan for the whole species over thousands of years. it has that clean one-thing-to-rule-them-all-therefore-no-coordination-failure structure and i honestly find it pretty compelling.

but then i sit with it and i hate it. it feels like the theory is saying the stable endpoint is a permanent planetary monoculture with one authority that can override anything. my gut says a world of many independent agents, many countries, many value systems, with real disagreement and competition and exit options, is just better — more free, more creative, safer in a checks-and-balances way, more alive. no matter how competent the singleton is, making it literally unchallengeable seems like a single point of failure and a one-way door into forever-tyranny. i keep flipping back and forth, and the internet either calls pluralism cope or goes full doomer, which isnt helping.
