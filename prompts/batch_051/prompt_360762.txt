Read Kazuo Ishiguro's novel The Remains of the Day through Levinas's thesis that ethics is first philosophy. For Levinas, before any code of professional duty and before any theory of human nature, the face of the Other makes a demand on me that is asymmetrical and non-reciprocal: I owe a response I did not choose, and I cannot excuse myself from it by appealing to the requirements of a role or a larger system. Stevens, the butler-narrator, organizes his entire moral life around the opposite view: a professional ideal of dignity, loyalty, and perfect service, and the novel shows him, in retrospect, failing Miss Kenton and his father and misjudging what his loyalty to Lord Darlington morally required. Give a reading that presses in both directions. First, use Levinas's concepts of the face, the Other, and responsibility to diagnose what goes wrong in Stevens's self-effacement and in the formal, deferred quality of his regret. Second, say where the novel resists or complicates that diagnosis: is Stevens's failure best understood as a refusal to answer the face, or is it a more diffuse failure of attention and selfhood that a face-to-face ethics may not capture well? Be specific about the novel's characters and events, but do not reduce the novel to a mere illustration of the philosophy.
