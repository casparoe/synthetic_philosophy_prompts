So me and my friend just went back and forth for like three hours about whether there's a level of suffering that no amount of happiness can make up for, and we both left annoyed because we were probably answering slightly different questions. I want to make a little sorting instrument for our group—a 'which position do you actually hold' decision-tree-style quiz with maybe six to eight leaves—so we can see where we actually split. The tree should be built from the logical space of possible answers, not from a list of named views: work out the key branching claims first, then locate the positions in it, and put a short label plus one sentence on the view's most awkward commitment at each leaf. Make the branching questions concrete and jargon-free for my philosophy-curious friends: is one person's year of severe chronic pain made okay by any number of other people having slightly nicer afternoons, does your answer change between a bad toothache and actual torture, are you allowed to balance one person's extreme suffering against other people's pleasures at all or only against that same person's future pleasures, and would uncertainty about how bad the pain really is change your pick. I want at least these leaves or close variants to appear: plain total utilitarianism, absolute negative utilitarianism, lexical threshold negative utilitarianism (the view that below some intensity normal tradeoffs apply but above it suffering wins no matter how much happiness is on the other side), prioritarianism, and some position that denies pain and pleasure sit on a single scale in the first place. Flag the combinations of answers that seem empty—say whether they're incoherent, merely unpopular, or just unexplored—and also tell me which positions the tree can't seperate.
