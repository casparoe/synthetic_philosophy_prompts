I'm a novelist rather than a philosopher, and I'd like you to act as a professional philosopher working on well-being and value theory. I've developed a small theory that keeps surfacing in my fiction, and I want a candid, rigorous assessment of it rather than encouragement.

I call it the freshness condition. The claim is that novelty is not simply one good among others, to be weighed against pleasure, achievement or love; it is a structural precondition for those other goods' retaining their value. By 'genuine novelty' I mean experience that cannot be fully derived in advance from what a person already knows and values, so mere variety within a familiar repertoire does not count. I also hold that the required rate rises over the course of a life, because the better one's predictive model of one's own circumstances becomes, the more of one's experience becomes derivable. A life can therefore go badly without being painful, deprived or meaningless in any familiar sense: it simply goes stale.

My clearest illustration is a character in my current novel, a widowed doctor who is healthy, respected, loved and secure, and who describes her condition through a line I gave her: 'a closed book I have already read.' By every ordinary measure her life is intact, yet on my account it is genuinely impaired. I think the same logic runs in the other direction: a failed and frightening journey can add to a person's well-being precisely because it refused to be predicted.

Is this a real concept or an overgrown metaphor? Where does it overgeneralise — for instance, does it wrongly pathologise the person who flourishes in quiet, repetitive routine? I know a little about hedonic adaptation and novelty-seeking as a personality trait, and I want to know whether my view is merely those ideas in different dress. Please think carefully, begin by brainstorming the strongest defensible version of the view, then tell me the nearest existing ideas under their proper names, and finally give the three or four sharpest objections, with a judgement as to whether any of them is fatal.
