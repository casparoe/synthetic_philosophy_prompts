Okay so I've been trying to figure out how consciousness and happiness combine into value, and I have a bunch of gut verdicts on cases but I can't seem to stablize on a principle that covers them. I started from the idea that the value of a state is something like consciousness times happiness — the more of either the better, and if either is zero the value is zero. But my own cases keep breaking that. Here they are, with the numbers there just to fix intuitions, not because I think we can really measure these things.

1. A rock that we imagine feels great: zero value. Also a being that acts blissful but has no inner life at all: zero. No experience, no wellbeing.

2. A human in a normal awake state with mild contentment (consciousness=100, happiness=10): positive, fine, a decent baseline.

3. The same human drowsy and half in a fever dream, consciousness maybe a quarter of normal (25), but feeling genuine, overwhelming euphoria (100). I would pick this over case 2 without much hesitation. Here happiness is doing a huge amount of work, and the multiplicative story (25×100=2500 against 100×10=1000) feels right.

4. But then: a fully awake, self-aware human with mild positive mood (consciousness=100, happiness=5) versus a mouse with tiny but real consciousness (3) in a state of maximal mouse-bliss (160). The products are about equal (500 vs 480). And I don't just think these are close — I think the human state is clearly better, maybe incomparably better. That seems to kill the multiplicative story.

5. Two states of the same human, both with normal waking consciousness (100): one mildly sad (−10) and one deeply miserable (−80). The second is obviously much worse, and the leap feels roughly proportional to the happiness gap. So happiness differences really matter when consciousness is held fixed.

6. A faintly conscious blob, barely above nothing (consciousness=1), with enormous happiness (1000). It's positive, I'm glad it's there, but it doesn't come close to outweighing an ordinary happy human life. And even if you offered me a billion of them, my gut still says one ordinary happy human life matters more. That also seems to clash with case 3, where I happily lowered consciousness alot to get more happiness.

7. The pair I keep going back and forth on: in case 3 I trade away consciousness for happiness inside a single person, but in cases 4 and 6 I won't let a lower-consciousness being's happiness outweigh a higher-consciousness being's. Is there a real difference between trading within one life and trading across lives, or is one of these verdicts just wrong?

So, what I want from you: is there a single principle — additive, multiplicative, lexical, thresholded, whatever — that gets all or almost all of these? State it precisely, show how it handles each case, and tell me what it says about some cases I haven't decided on: a mildly happy superintelligence against a very happy human; a fully conscious person at perfect neutral affect (happiness=0); and a being with rich consciousness and no valence at all (if that's even coherent). Then be honest about which of my verdicts your principle forces me to drop, and what I lose by dropping each one.
