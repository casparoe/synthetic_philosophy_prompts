Take the classic trinitarian identity problem: the Father is God, the Son is God, and the Holy Spirit is God; the Father is not the Son, the Son is not the Spirit, and the Father is not the Spirit; and yet there is only one God. The familiar relative-identity treatment dissolves the contradiction by saying identity is sortal-relative, so the three can be the same God while being different Persons. Now vary the puzzle in a specific way. Suppose a theologian grants that identity is absolute and not sortal-relative, and tries to preserve orthodoxy by introducing a primitive two-place relation W(p, g), read as p is a way God g exists. The Persons are not said to be identical with God; instead each Person is a way in which the one God exists. Lay out the constraints on W that would be needed for the translated doctrine to leave room for three distinct Persons and one God. Then assess whether the proposal is distinguishable from modalism in the standard sense, or whether it simply relocates the contradiction. In particular, work through what happens if W is functional from ways to God, if W is symmetric between persons, or if the identity of indiscernibles is applied to the Persons themselves.
