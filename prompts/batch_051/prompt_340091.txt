I have been trying to decide whether a structural parallel I noticed is deep or merely apparent. In programming-language semantics, a denotational model is called fully abstract when it distinguishes two programs exactly when they are observationally equivalent, where observational equivalence means that no enclosing program, run with either of the two plugged in, yields different observable behavior. A model that makes finer distinctions than this is normally rejected as too intensional. It seems to me that theories of which computations are sentient face exactly the same demand. Several accounts attribute consciousness or moral patienthood to one computational system while denying it to another even though the two are behaviorally identical in every possible external test, with the difference confined to internal structure that no observation could ever expose. Is this genuinely the same criterion, so that a theory of sentience ought to be fully abstract as well? If so, what constraints does that place on views that treat sentience as an unobservable internal fact? If not, what breaks the parallel between a semantic model and a theory of consciousness?
