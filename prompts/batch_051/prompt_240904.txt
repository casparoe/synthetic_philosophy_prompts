Fuller argued that law has an internal morality: a bunch of requirements like generality, publicity, non-retroactivity, clarity, consistency, possibility of compliance, stability over time, and congruence between the announced rule and how officials actually decide. Now suppose a city hands its traffic enforcement over to a deep learning model that gets retrained every night on new accident and citation data. There is no published rule about, say, what counts as reckless merging — the boundary drifts from week to week, similar cases get treated differently after updates, and nobody can state the rule in advance except by pointing at the model itself. People can still learn, in a rough statistical way, to avoid whatever the model flags, and the city treats the model as the source of the rule. Does Fuller's argument that these desiderata are conditions for law to exist survive this kind of system? Or does the system show you can have effective social ordering without Fullerian law, with the internal morality just being a folk theory built for finite, statable rules? Say which desideratum is doing the most work in your answer, and wether it can be restated for model-based governance.
