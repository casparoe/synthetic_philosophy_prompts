Late last night I had a long argument with a friend about the kinds of transactions money should be allowed to enter, and I came away convinced only that my considered views are less systematic than I had assumed. My friend defended a single principle: any voluntary and informed exchange between adults is permissible unless it involves force, deception, or serious third-party harm. I could not accept that principle, but whenever I tried to state my own, I found myself falling back on distinctions that sounded ad hoc, and I would like your help finding whatever principle, if any, actually makes my verdicts cohere.

The verdicts I defended and still hold, at least provisionally, are these. I think a regulated market in living kidney donation, with a fixed payment, screening, and long-term follow-up, is permissible and probably better than prohibition, though I feel some unease. I think a hardware store that triples the price of generators and bottled water after a hurricane is doing something wrong, and the law should forbid it. I think selling one's vote for fifty dollars is wrong, for reasons that are not merely about aggregate consequences. I accept paid surrogacy under a well-regulated contract, again with unease. I think paying plasma donors is unobjectionable. I think it is wrong for a family to secure a child's admission to a university with a two-million-dollar gift when the child's record falls just short, even though I think paying for tutoring and test preparation is fine. And I think paying someone to hold a place in line for a new phone and then buying the spot is acceptable, or at most mildly unfair.

My friend pressed the apparent inconsistencies. Why treat a kidney and a vote differently, when both are paid transfers of something intimately connected to a person under conditions of consent? Why allow a place in a theatre queue to be bought but not a place in an admissions queue? Why condemn a generator at triple price after a hurricane while accepting a kidney sold for tens of thousands of dollars? I could not identify a single feature that sorted these cases. Is there one principle of commodification, or a small set of candidate principles, that delivers all these verdicts? If not, which of my verdicts should I overturn? Please state the principle precisely, tie each clause to the case that motivates it, and apply your best account to a case I have not been able to settle: a public hospital that lets patients pay to move ahead of others on its waiting list for elective surgery.
