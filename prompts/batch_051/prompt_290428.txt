I'm in a distributive justice course and I've been trying to nail down what I actually think about affirmative action and quotas by going with my gut on cases. Here's where I've landed. A US university giving Black and Hispanic applicants a modest boost as one factor among many: fine. The same university setting aside a fixed number of seats that only those applicants can fill: not fine. India reserving around half the seats at its elite engineering institutes for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Other Backward Classes, with a lower cutoff for those groups: fine, even though that's basically a hard quota and part of me thinks I'm being inconsistent. Norway's law that 40% of corporate board seats must go to women: fine. A company that commits in advance to making at least 30% of its new engineering hires from underrepresented groups, and leaves roles unfilled rather than miss the target: not fine. Legacy admissions for children of alumni at that same university: not fine. My university giving a small admissions advantage to first-generation college students: fine. And the one I keep fliping on: a law school lowering its bar for male applicants to keep the class from becoming 70% women. Is there a principle that gets most of these, or do some of my verdicts have to go? What does that principle say about the law school case?
