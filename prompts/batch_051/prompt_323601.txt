I'm a journalist working on a long-read about how the old arguments over whether Black art has a political job keep coming back in new packaging. I want to use W. E. B. Du Bois as the central foil and I could use help thinking it through.

As I understand him, Du Bois argued during the 1920s, against the art-for-its-own-sake wing of the Harlem Renaissance, that no art is actually neutral: every work of art is doing propaganda for somebody or some outlook, whether the artist admits it or not. From that he pulled a duty. Black artists, he thought, should make their work serve racial uplift—beautiful work, but work that also pushes back against white-supremacist pictures of Black life and argues for full humanity and citizenship. He had little patience for creators who thought beauty could float above politics, and he defended art that was frankly useful.

Heres the part that's after his time. In the last couple of decades a loud wing of Black criticism has turned against the whole positive-images project. They treat uplift art, corrective art, aspirational art as basically respectability politics; they say a novel that offers ugly, messy, unredemptive Black life is more honest and freer than one that's looking over it's shoulder at a white audience; and a few of them even go after the idea that art should have a job at all. Which is strange, because their line that supposedly apolitical art is always sneaking in a politics is awfully close to his line that art can't help doing propaganda—yet they end up at the opposite conclusion about what art should do.

So here's the prediction I need from you. If Du Bois could spend six months with this contemporary material—the essays, novels, shows, and the criticism around them—where would he land? Would he see these critics as having inherited his central claim and just swapped out the politics, or would he see the anti-uplift turn as a damaging concession? Is there a third option, where he accepts their propaganda point but rejects their content, because for him the content couldn't be separated from the audience it was trying to move? Give me your best guess at his position, with the reasoning, and flag where you're least sure.
