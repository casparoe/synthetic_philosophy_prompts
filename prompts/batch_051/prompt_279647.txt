I'm in a giving circle with some friends — we pool money every month and have a short written agreement. It says each member "shall direct their share to the cause that will do the most good, taking into account all foreseeable consequences, near and remote." Last meeting I proposed we give the month's money to a group trying to lower the chance that advanced AI kills everyone this century. Another friend wanted anti-malaria bednets, where the numbers are solid and the good is visible. With the AI fund the whole justification rests on probabilities no one can actually compute and knock-on effects hundreds of years out. So I honestly can't tell wether the rule is telling me to skip the AI fund because I can't show it does the most good, or to include the unpriceable future in my estimate, or whether the rule just stops making sense here. I don't much care who wins the vote. I want to know: read by its plain words, read by what the people who drafted it were trying to stop, read by what a reasonable member would take it to mean, and read by what the drafters would have said if we'd asked them about far-future causes — does the rule reach this case, and which reading should decide? And if "the most good" is genuinely unknowable in cases like this, what is the rule even asking us to do?
