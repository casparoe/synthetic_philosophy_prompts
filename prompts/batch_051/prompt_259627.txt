I'm a retired industrial hygienist — thirty years of solvents, ventilation, and exposure limits — and since I stopped working I've been reading philosophy of risk on the side. Lately a single sentence keeps following me around, and I can't work out whether its empty or a whole theory of how much risk a society should tolerate hiding inside it. At a town meeting about a proposed metal-coating plant, a neighbor stood up and said 'better safe than sorry,' and that basically ended the discussion. A week later, at a scientific advisory committee, someone used the exact same sentence to argue for lowering a solvent exposure limit by a factor of ten. Same words, different job, or maybe the same job and I'm just not seeing it.

Could you take 'better safe than sorry' and pull apart the distinct precise claims it could be making? I'd like it as a menu, not an essay: for each reading, a sentence or two on what it actually asserts, plus a verdict — is it a platitude, something defensible but contestable, or just false. Then, for each reading, say whether it would actually license using the slogan to block a new facility until someone proves it safe, or whether it would only support weaker stuff like requiring monitoring, safety margins, or more study before a decision. The interesting action, I suspect, is in which reading gets smuggled in when a room moves from 'be prudent' to 'ban it.'

I don't need history or references; I want the conceptual anatomy. Keep it under 800 words, and format it as a numbered list or a table with one reading per row.
