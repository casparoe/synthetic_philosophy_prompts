After my grandmother died I noticed something I don't quite know how to talk about. When I'm about to do something dumb, I can still hear her reaction in my head — not a memory of her saying it once, but her particular timing, her sigh, the way she'd look over her glasses. My sisters report the same thing, except the version of her that runs in each of our heads isn't identical; we compared once and mine scolds more, another's is gentler and more anxious. The same thing happened, in a smaller way, when an old friend moved away and our friendship faded: a fragment of him still talks in my head years later. It made me wonder whether, in a non-supernatural sense, a person can persist after death not just as a memory but as a kind of copy distributed accross the minds of the people who knew them.

I'm trying to find out if there is a name for this idea, and more importantly, how literally it is supposed to be taken. Is it just a vivid way of saying "she lives on in us," or is there a real position that treats a self as something that can survive by being instantiated many times, at different resolutions, in different brains? And if so, what makes those partial copies the *same* self rather than several people who happen to have similar models of one person? I'm not after a bare label; I want to know exactly where the line is between the metaphor and the thesis, whether such a view has to say the copies are conscious or that the original person is somehow co-present in all of them at once, and what it would have to show to avoid collapsing back into ordinary talk about memory and influence.
