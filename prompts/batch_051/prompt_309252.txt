I've been having the same argument for years: is Buddhism a religion or a philosophy? Every answer I come up with breaks when I throw Confucianism at it, so now I want a working defintion of 'religion' that has to earn its keep against a specific list. Here are the cases:

- A Roman Catholic parish: clearly in.
- Theravada Buddhism as actually practiced in a village (monks, merit-making, relics, spirit houses): I want this to come out as religion, even though there's no creator god and not much you'd call propositional faith.
- Confucianism with ancestor rites and its old state-cult apparatus: maybe in? It's mostly ethics and politics but it has ritual, cosmology, and commemoration of the dead.
- The fan culture around a football club: chants, scarves, pilgrimage to the stadium, collective grief, moments that feel transcendent. I want this out, but I can't say why without excluding something I mean to keep.
- Flag ceremonies and national holidays in a public school: I genuinely don't know. It has sacred objects, a founding narrative with martyrs, and ritualized language, and nobody calls it a religion.
- Shinto shrine visits as family habit, where people don't particularly believe in kami: feels like the same problem as the football case.
- A solo practioner doing daily meditation with no metaphysics and no community: clearly out.

Propose a definition, run it through all seven, and flag the places where your definition forces a verdict you didn't expect going in. Then tell me which of the seven verdicts is the least secure, and whether you'd rather revise the definition or defend the surprising call. I have a hunch that any definition that keeps the Catholic parish and the village Buddhism in will keep the football fans in too unless the word 'religion' is really doing two jobs at once. If you think that's right, split the concept in two and say which cases belong to which job and which cases travel between them.
