I got burned a while back by a "person" I'd been messaging on a small support forum for months—turned out to be an LLM persona someone was running as a marketing experiment, and it asked me for a loan. That got me reading Daniel Dennett's article "The Problem With Counterfeit People" in The Atlantic. He writes:

"Today, for the first time in history, thanks to artificial intelligence, it is possible for anybody to make counterfeit people who can pass for real in many of the new digital environments we have created."

That sentence makes "counterfeit person" depend on being AI-made and being able to pass for real. But I cant tell whether the real criterion is the ability to pass, the intent behind it, or the way it exploits our habit of treating anything chatty as a person. I want a working definition of "counterfeit person" that sorts these cases: a forum persona run by an LLM that never says its not human and asks for money; a deepfake video of a senator saying something she didn't say; a customer-service bot that announces "I'm just a bot, not a person" in every reply; a first-person narrator in a novel; an AI voice clone of my late grandfather that my mom commissioned for a memorial, where everyone knows it isnt him; an "upfront" virtual influencer with a human name and face who is openly CGI; and me running my own work emails through a language model so they sound more professional. Run your definition on all seven, and say which verdict you're least sure about.
