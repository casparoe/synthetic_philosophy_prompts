In a disagreement about whether our congregation may include a short unscripted drama in its Sunday service, a member of the worship committee ended the discussion by invoking the regulative principle: 'whatever is not commanded in worship is forbidden.' Others in the meeting countered with the contrary slogan, 'whatever is not forbidden is permitted,' and I could not tell whether the two are genuine rivals or are talking past one another. I want you to lay out the distinct precise claims the first slogan might be making: about what counts as commanded (an explicit scriptural precept, a sound inference from precept or example, a general biblical pattern), about the scope of 'worship' (the service as a whole, the elements of worship, the circumstances in which they are conducted), and about what 'forbidden' amounts to (sinful, unauthorised, merely non-binding). For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive but defensible, or false, and which readings would actually support excluding the drama. Be committed: give me your preferred reconstruction of the slogan, with reasons, and state plainly whether the committee member's conclusion survives on that reconstruction.
