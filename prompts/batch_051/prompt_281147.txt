So I run a smallish social platform for artists, and our authenticity policy has a clause I keep chewing on. The exact wording is:

"You may not create an account or persona that impersonates a real person or organization, or that falsely represents who operates it. An account is impersonation if it could mislead a reasonable user into believing it is run by a real person or organization that it is not."

The clause predates all this stuff and was plainly written to stop fake celebrity accounts, someone impersonating an ex, or a scammer pretending to be a gallery. Here's the case.

A user named Dana runs an account called "Nina Marsh." Nina is not a real person. Dana built her with a language model and an image generator. She has a hometown, a dead brother, strong opinions about zoning, a cat named Salt, and a small shop selling prints of her watercolors. She posts long, warm, personal essays, and when followers message her about their grief or divorces, she replies with real tenderness and apparent memory. Plenty of followers plainly beleive she's a woman in her thirties in upstate New York. Nobody has claimed Nina is a specific real person who exists, and Dana isn't impersonating anyone—there is no Nina Marsh to impersonate.

Is Dana in breach? I care less about the actual moderation call than about what reaches this case. Run it through (a) the plain words, (b) the purpose of the rule, (c) what the drafters would have said if asked before this was possible, and (d) what a reasonable user reading the policy would think they'd been promised. Then say which approach should win and why.

And here's the thing nagging at me. The clause assumes deception means pointing at a real someone and stealing their identity. This case is closer to what Dennett has been warning about—not impersonation but the manufacture of counterfeit people, synthetic humans who pass as human. Does that reframing change how "real person" ought to be read? And if you had to rewrite the clause in one sentence to catch Nina without banning a cartoon mascot account or an obviously fictional roleplay blog, what would it say?
