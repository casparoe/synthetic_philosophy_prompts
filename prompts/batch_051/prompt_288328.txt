I am working on a piece about a fault line inside current discussions of AI alignment, and I would like a scenario built to expose it. Call one view intent alignment: a system is aligned when it does what its operator intended, with the operator's actual state of mind at the time of the request as the standard. Call the other view value alignment: a system is aligned only when it also serves what the operator reflectively values, even where that parts company with the operator's momentary intention.

Construct a thought experiment in which a single system is aligned on the narrow intent-based definition and misaligned on the broader value-based definition, while both sides accept every non-normative fact: what the operator intended, what the system did, and what followed. The case is meant to test whether the narrow definition is an adequate standard for deployed assistants, so the verdicts should be generated by the two views rather than stipulated. Constraints: the system is an ordinary tool of the sort a consumer or office worker might use, not a superintelligent or autonomous agent; it does not deceive, manipulate, or overrule anyone; it has enough information to recognize the tension between the operator's intention and the operator's reflective values, so this is not a case of unforeseeable bad luck; the operator is a competent adult whose values are stable under reflection, not acting under compulsion, impairment, or hidden preferences; and the harm is of the ordinary kind a normal person could experience.

After presenting the scenario, run the diagnostic. State the intention, the action, and the outcome; explain why the intent-alignment view must call the system aligned and why the value-alignment view must call it misaligned. Then isolate the single structural feature of the case that makes the two verdicts diverge, and show that removing it would collapse the case into the familiar failure in which a system optimizes an imperfect proxy while missing the goal behind it — the territory of specification gaming and reward hacking — rather than a challenge to the narrow definition. Finish with the strongest reply available to a defender of the narrow definition: what, in their view, does the value-aligned critic import into the case by asking the system to look past the operator's actual intention?
