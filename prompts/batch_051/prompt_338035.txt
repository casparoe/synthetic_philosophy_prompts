Say I get scanned and destroyed while a perfect duplicate is built from the scan: almost everyone says I die, however good the duplicate is. But suppose instead a surgeon replaces my neurons one at a time with functionally identical artificial ones, spread out over years, while I stay awake and talk the whole time. Most people say I survive that, even though in the end there is no biological neuron left, and in principle the end state could be made exactly like the duplicate. So we have an asymmetry: we call one death and the other survival, apparently because one is sudden and the other is gradual and continuous. Is there an actual principle that justifies drawing a line between these two cases, or is the judgment mostly a psychological quirk about how the change is presented? Give me the strongest principle you can find, test it against awkward nearby cases (say, replacing half the brain at once, or pausing the replacement halfway for a year), and then say whether you would keep the asymmetry or give it up. Also tell me how confident you are in the overall verdict, and single out the one point you are least sure of.
