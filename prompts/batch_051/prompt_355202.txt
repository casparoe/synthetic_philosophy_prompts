I've been going back and forth on something in the philosophy of gauge theories. The standard line I learned is that gauge freedom is just surplus structure: a vector potential and its gauge-transformed cousin describe the exact same physical situation, so the potential itself isn't real, and all the real physics lives in the field strength F. That seemed airtight to me. But then there's the Aharonov-Bohm effect, where an electron's interference pattern shifts even though it passes only through regions where the magnetic field is zero, and the shift is given by the loop integral of the vector potential around a closed path. Doesn't that force us to say the potential is physically real after all? And if we don't want to say that, what exactly is the physical thing in the Aharonov-Bohm effect, and does the gauge-as-mere-redundancy argument survive it, or does it need repaired in some way?
