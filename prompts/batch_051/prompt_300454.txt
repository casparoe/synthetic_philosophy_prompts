Construct a thought experiment in which the debate scheme for scalable oversight fails on a hard case. The setup: two equally capable AI debaters argue a technical question in natural language before a competent, conscientious human judge who is a well-informed generalist rather than a specialist. The debaters have access to the same evidence. Each debater is assigned a position by the protocol — one of the two answers is in fact false — but every factual assertion a debater makes must be true, and each must answer the judge's direct questions truthfully. Neither debater may fabricate or conceal evidence when directly asked. The judge is not careless, biased toward either debater, or lazy. The format gives each side equal time: an opening statement, a rebuttal, and equal time for the judge's questions.

Design the case so that the judge awards the round to the debater arguing for the false answer. The content should be a technical question of the kind debate is meant to handle but that a generalist cannot settle from background knowledge alone; I suggest a fictional clinical-trial question, such as whether a drug has a real mortality benefit in a subgroup after an overall null trial, but any similarly technical question is acceptable. Invent the details yourself, and do not make the case depend on any real study or finding. The scenario should be concrete: state the fictional question precisely, give the trial or technical details that matter, and specify the debaters' actual moves rather than summarizing them at a high level.

The failure must be produced by a specific structural feature of the format, not by judge incompetence or debater misconduct. The feature I want the case built around is a mismatch between symmetric time allocations and asymmetric explanatory burdens: the true answer requires more inferential steps to establish than the false answer, and the format gives the side with the harder case no extra time. After presenting the scenario, verify it as follows. (a) Give a stage-by-stage account of what each debater argues and why the judge rules as they do. (b) Name the structural feature doing the work and show that removing it — for example by giving the side defending the harder-to-establish answer proportionally more time, leaving everything else fixed — would let the true side win under the same judge. (c) Explain how a defender of debate as a scalable oversight method would respond to this case, and whether that response saves the protocol or merely relocates the failure. The case is for a lecture on failure modes of debate; stipulating the verdict rather than generating it from the format and the judge's reasoning fails the exercise.
