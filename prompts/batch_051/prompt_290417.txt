I've been going back and forth about integrity and incorruptibility for a few months now, ever since the town council mess involving my sister-in-law's coworker—everybody defended him by saying he never took an envelope of cash and all his votes were things he could defend on the merits, and I found I didn't actually have a settled view of what being incorruptible even amounts to. Here's roughly where I've landed, and I want to know whether it hangs together.

(1) I think integrity is basically a first-person matter: it's about the fit between what you do and the commitments you actually hold, not about whether those commitments are themselves the right ones. (2) The paradigm of integrity for me is the official who turns down a bribe instantly because accepting it would be incompatible with the kind of person she takes herself to be, with no cost-benefit deliberation involved. (3) I think being incorruptible means no amount of money, threat, flattery, or social pressure would move you to betray your commitments, and I think that disposition is a virtue. (4) But I also think that changing your mind when the evidence shifts is more in keeping with integrity than staying fixed, and it would be a vice rather than integrity to stick with a commitment after you've become convinced it was a mistake. (5) I think corruption is usually incremental: it happens through years of small favors, access, and softening-up, so that no single decision crosses a bright line and someone can end up corrupt without there being any one demonstrably corrupt act. (6) And I think an official who takes money from a developer and then votes for the project because she honestly judges it to be good on public grounds is still corrupt—the conflict counts even if the vote would have gone exactly the same way either way.

I can feel that some of these pull against each other, but I cant tell which clashes are real and which are just me using words differently, and I don't know which one I'd have to give up or weaken. Do this as an audit: go through the commitments and say which pairs genuinely conflict, which only seem to, and which are load-bearing for the others. After the prose, give me a table with these columns: tension (which numbered commitments are involved), severity (1 to 5, 5 being the most serious), status (real vs. apparent-only), what has to give, and cheapest fix plus what it costs.
