I've been thinking about a parallel between two debates I keep meeting separately. In discussions of AI safety, people ask whether a system should be interpretable — whether its internal reasoning should be legible to those who rely on it, on the theory that opacity makes a deployed system too dangerous to trust. In ordinary life there is a parallel question of whether a person has a duty to be legible to others: to explain her reasoning and motives plainly rather than leaving people guessing. Both framings treat legibility as a safeguard — against hidden failure in the machine, and against betrayal of trust in the person. But the cases may run on different rails: the machine is an artifact we built and chose to deploy, so its opacity looks like a design defect, whereas a person's opacity can seem protected by autonomy and a right to an inner life. I cannot tell whether the resemblance is deep or only verbal. Is it a real identity of structure? If it is, say where the mapping holds and where it comes apart, what would have to be true of both sides for an insight about human transparency to carry over to the AI case or the reverse, and give one prediction the parallel appears to make that turns out false.
