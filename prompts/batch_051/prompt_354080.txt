I cover philosophy and ideas for a general-interest science magazine, and for the last few weeks I've been interupting my coworkers with a very specific fight: can a person who knows they have hands also manage to know they're not a brain in a vat? I finally convinced two philosophers to do an adversarial collaboration, and I want to print the actual joint statement as a boxed sidebar next to my feature, not a summary of it. One of them is a Nozick-style tracking theorist who thinks knowledge requires sensitivity — roughly, if the proposition had been false you wouldn't have believed it — and who therefore denies that knowledge is closed under known entailment. The other is a closure defender who thinks competent deduction from known premises is about as central to knowledge as anything gets, and that the tracking view must have gone wrong somewhere before it earns the right to bite that bullet.

Could you draft the document they'd both sign? I'd like a numbered list of claims they can agree on — including whatever they can jointly say about what the brain-in-a-vat case, the lottery case, and the fake barn case establish, and wheere they each think those cases stop being diagnostic. Then a disagreements table, where for each crux you give the precise formulation of where they part ways, worded so each side would own it, plus the argument, case, or sort of evidence both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Also flag any places the fight may be partly verbal, and any agreed claim that's so hedged it isn't doing real work. The tone should be sober enough to publish, but I don't want it sanded down until the stakes disappear.
