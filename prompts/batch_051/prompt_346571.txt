My philosophy tutor spent last term on complicity, and I've been tracking her judgements to work out what rule she's actually running. So far she's said: a cab driver who unknowingly drops a passenger at a house the passenger then burgles is not complicit; a mechanic who builds a hidden compartment into a car for a smuggler, knowing exactly what its for, is complicit; a neighbour who watches the house next door being burgled and does nothing, with no special duty to act, is not complicit; an accountant who keeps a false set of books for a mafia loan-sharking front, purely for the wage, is complicit. Next session's case is this: a woman who runs a rural petrol station sells a full tank of petrol to a man she knows is on his way to torch a building a few miles away. She doesn't approve, doesn't overcharge or encourage him, and serves him exactly as she serves any other customer. Predict her verdict. Also set out the candidate rules that fit her judgements so far, say what each rule predicts for the petrol station, identify which earlier case is the closest analogy, and describe a further case that would best settle which rule she is using.
