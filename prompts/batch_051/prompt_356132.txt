Give me a working definition of humour — the property of being funny, as distinct from merely producing laughter — and make the definition explicit about which of the four standard theoretical families it draws on or rejects: superiority, incongruity, relief, and benign violation. Test the definition against these cases: a baby laughing at peekaboo; being tickled; a pun that earns a genuine laugh; schadenfreude at a rival's harmless slip on ice; gallows humour among paramedics; a joke about a recent tragedy told by someone unaffected by it; a self-deprecating joke at the speaker's own expense; a roast, the comic ritual of insults, in which the target laughs along; a high-status comedian mocking a powerless group; nervous laughter at a funeral; and finding a mathematical proof elegant but not funny. I take the baby, the pun, and the roast to be clear cases of humour; tickling, nervous laughter at a funeral, and the proof to be clearly not humour; and the middle five I have no settled view on. Run the definition through every case and report the results in a table with these columns: Case; My stated view (clear yes / clear no / unsure); Your verdict (humour / not humour / borderline); Theoretical family doing the most work; Confidence (low / medium / high). Where the definition misclassifies a case or gives no answer, either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict. Then say which single verdict is least secure and why. End with a JSON object with these fields: term (string), final_definition (string), families_drawn_on (array of strings), families_rejected (array of strings), revisions_made (array of strings), least_secure_case (string), and residual_tension (string, a one-sentence statement of the case the definition handles worst).
