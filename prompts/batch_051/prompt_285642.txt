I've been chewing on petitionary prayer for a while now on my own — no philosophy background, just Sunday school and some long drives — and I keep running into the same five ways people defend asking God for things, even though God already knows what you need and, the way I was raised, never changes his mind. I've jotted them down the best I can:

1. It changes me, not God. Naming what I want forces me to actually look at what I want, and a lot of the time the wanting gets reshaped in the asking.
2. Asking is a built-in condition. God's whole plan can settle, from all eternity, that he'll give me the thing on the condition that I ask. So my asking makes a real difference, but it isn't God changing his mind — the hinge was always built in.
3. It's commanded. Jesus told people to ask, and if the command comes from God, then whether I can see how the machinery works doesnt matter all that much.
4. It's about the relationship. A kid asking a parent for something isn't transferring information; the asking itself is the relationship happening.
5. The all-at-once thing. An unchanging God can still respond in what feels like real time, the way a call-and-response in a recording sounds like an answer happening even though the whole thing was finished before you pressed play.

I've got a feeling there's one more defense out there, and maybe it's the one that actually does the work. Give me one that is genuinely different from these five — not a rewording of any of them, not a special case of any of them, not two of them stapled together. Then tell me honestly whether it handles the omniscience-and-immutability problem or just feels like it does.
