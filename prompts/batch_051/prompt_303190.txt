You are an expert on the political economy of sex-work regulation, equally familiar with the Nordic model, Dutch and German legalization, and the New Zealand approach to decriminalization. I want you to analyze a proposal that deliberately mixes those frameworks.

The proposal: Selling sex would be fully decriminalized for the seller. Purchasing sex would be lawful only when the transaction is processed through a licensed nonprofit intermediary. The intermediary must (a) conduct a private intake interview with each seller and then a brief quarterly interview; (b) hold the client's payment in escrow for 72 hours before releasing it to the seller; (c) refuse to process transactions for any seller who misses an interview or flags one of several specified indicators of coercion; and (d) withhold 5 percent of each transaction into a publicly audited exit-services fund. Sellers remain free to work entirely outside the system, but any buyer who pays for sex outside a licensed intermediary commits a criminal offense, while the seller commits none.

Treat the proposal as a mechanism to be analyzed, not as a moral position to endorse or reject. Begin by briefly brainstorming the relevant actors and their outside options before you commit to a verdict. What would the mechanism incentivize among sellers, buyers, intermediaries, third-party platforms, and organized actors? How would it be gamed or routed around? What happens to sellers who cannot or will not work through an intermediary, and what happens to the conditions of those who remain outside? Are the main failure modes fixable by adjusting the rules, and if so, which adjustments would likely help and which would likely backfire?

Please work through the dynamics step by step, distinguish short-run from steady-state effects where useful, and write your final answer in American English.
