I plan emergency operations for a municipal disaster-response office; several times each season I must decide, on the basis of model output, whether to act in advance of a low-probability, high-consequence event. Last night I argued with a friend who does climate modeling: I said a probability range has to be collapsed to a single number before it can ground a decision; she said collapsing the range discards exactly the information that should matter, and that the honest input is the whole set of probabilities. We never settled what the range actually requires of a decision-maker.

Please write a reusable procedure, grounded in imprecise probability (sets of priors, lower and upper probabilities, and decision rules such as maximality and E-admissibility), that a non-theorist on my staff can run in under an hour without fitting new models. It should specify, in order: which questions to ask; when it is safe to collapse a range to a number and when it is not; how to choose when the range leaves acts unranked; which steps are judgment calls dressed up as calculation; and where the procedure will predictably misfire. Then apply it to two cases: (1) whether to pre-position emergency supplies when the estimated probability of a damaging flood this season is 0.15–0.45 and the costs are asymmetric; and (2) whether to close a school for the day when the probability of a hazardous event is 0.05–0.35 and the range comes from two equally credible models. The second is one where I expect an answer my office will not like; state what the procedure demands there anyway.
