I am a postgraduate student in a philosophy of medicine seminar, and over the term I have been keeping a careful record of the rulings our lecturer has given about whether particular conditions count as diseases in the medically relevant sense. Her record so far is as follows: type 1 diabetes mellitus newly diagnosed in a child is a disease; essential hypertension found on repeated measurements, with no symptoms and no end-organ damage, is a disease; ADHD in a ten-year-old whose school performance and friendships are clearly impaired is a disease; congenital red–green colour-vision deficiency discovered incidentally in a 34-year-old accountant who has never noticed any difficulty is not a disease; presbyopia in a 62-year-old that is fully corrected by reading glasses is not a disease; and male-pattern baldness in a 31-year-old is not a disease. She insists that she is applying a single principle throughout. A purely naturalist criterion, on which disease is deviation from species-typical biological function for one's age and sex, would already seem to be refuted by the colour-vision ruling, but at least two other principles remain live: a normativist criterion, on which disease is fundamentally a matter of significant harm or disablement to the person, and a hybrid criterion, on which a condition must involve both a biological dysfunction and a resulting harm.

The new case we are to discuss next week is a 44-year-old woman with six months of widespread pain, profound fatigue, and unrefreshing sleep. Every investigation has returned normal results, she has had to stop work, and she is plainly suffering.

I would like you to predict, as precisely as you can, how the lecturer will classify this case, and to reconstruct the rule she is implicitly running. For each live candidate rule, say how well it accommodates her earlier rulings and what it predicts about the new case; then give your best guess about her verdict and the reasons for it, including which of her earlier rulings is the closest analogue. If the record genuinely underdetermines her answer, say what principle she would be committing herself to with each possible decision, and specify a further case she could be asked about that would most cleanly settle which rule she is actually running. Please write your answer in British English.
