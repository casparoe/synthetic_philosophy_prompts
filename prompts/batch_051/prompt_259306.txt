Universal basic income is often proposed as a remedy not for low consumption but for domination: the condition of being subject to another person's arbitrary will because one lacks an acceptable exit option, whether from an employer, a landlord, a household member, or a conditional-welfare caseworker. Treat the claim that an unconditional income reduces domination rather than merely raising material living standards as a claim that is at least partly testable. Design the strongest empirical test you can to separate the two readings. Specify the population and comparison condition, the measures you would use to track exposure to arbitrary interference as distinct from consumption, income, or self-reported well-being, and the result pattern that would favor each interpretation. Conclude by saying precisely what even a favorable result would leave unsettled—which aspects of domination are normative, relational, or structural in a way that no behavioral or survey outcome can fully capture.
