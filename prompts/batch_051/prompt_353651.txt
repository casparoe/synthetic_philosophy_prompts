I'm a novelist, and I've somehow ended up giving the wedding toast for my two oldest friends. They met through one of those absurdly good coincidences — both of them in a tiny second-hand bookshop in Reykjavík in 2009, both reaching for the same dog-eared out-of-print novel — and the groom, who is a statistician, has spent years insisting it's just the birthday paradox doing its thing: enough people, enough moments, enough possible pairings, and some pair was bound to overlap somewhere. The bride is a poet and thinks that's a joyless way to talk about the event her whole life is built on. The room will be split between his side — proper statisticians who will wince at a misuse of 'reference class' — and her side, poets and novelists who will wince the moment I start doing probability at them, plus a big bloc of relatives who just want to be moved.

What I need is a five-minute toast where the birthday-paradox / epistemology-of-anecdote material is the actual load-bearing structure, not decoration. The speech has to get the maths right, and it has to work through the difference between 'this exact meeting was astronomically unlikely' and 'some such meeting, for some pair, was near-certain', plus the idea that a vivid anecdote is selected after the fact and isn't by itself much evidence of a hidden hand. But it has to land as affection, not as a lecture, and I dont want the lazy ending where you announce both views are right and call it done. After the toast, give me a separate note for my eyes only: how confident are you in the statistical and epistemological claims the speech relies on, and which single point are you least sure of.
