ok so I've been trying to sort out my views on the whole "humans as a stepping stone to machine intelligence" thing and I think I have a roughly coherent position but maybe not. Can you audit the set for me — like, which commitments actually pull against eachother and which only look like they do?

Here's where I've landed:

(1) I'm a hard naturalist about history: evolution and human history aren't headed anywhere, there's no cosmic plan, and natural selection doesn't aim at building AI or anything else.
(2) I think it's more likely than not that we'll eventually build generally intelligent systems that outcompete or replace biological humans. Call it succession.
(3) If succession happens, I think it would be retrospective-meaningful, not a tragedy: the human episode could have been worth it as a bridge, the kindling that caught fire and didn't survive the fire.
(4) I accept some version of the is/ought gap: the bare fact that succession is likely doesn't make it good, and doesn't obligate us to help it along or step aside.
(5) But I also have a button case: I would not press a button that painlessly ended humanity right now even if it guaranteed a flourishing successor species. And I dont think that refusal is just risk aversion or sentimentality.
(6) I think retrospective meaning is real: an event can be meaningful or fitting in hindsight even if nobody intended it and the people who brought it about wouldn't have endorsed it.
(7) I lean stance-dependent about value: nothing is meaningful or fitting simpliciter, only meaningful or fitting for someone, from some point of view.
(8) And I reject the view that the value of the human episode depends on whether some later intelligence exists to remember us or appreciate the bridge.

Where are the actual tensions? Which pairs are only apparently in tension? What's the cheapest repair, and what does it cost — which of these has to go or get weakened? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
