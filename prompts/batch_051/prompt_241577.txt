I got dragged onto a charter-revision committee in my town (long story), and one of the members keeps pitching something he calls the "designated adversary" rule, and I want to think through how it'd actually operate before I form a view. His proposal: within thirty days of taking office, every elected official names ten people they regard as personal or political opponents. The list is held by the city clerk, and each January one name is drawn by lot to serve as that official's auditor for the year. The auditor gets read-only access to the official's calendars, correspondence, campaign accounts, and personal finances, and collects a 2% bounty on any misconduct they prove before a five-citizen jury. The official is barred from using office power against that person for three years, and the same adversary can't audit the same official twice. His pitch is that this beats an ordinary ethics commission because the auditor has motive as well as access, and that it'd make our officials just about uncorruptible without paying for a permanent inspector general. Some of us think its a machine for manufacturing grudges, or that everyone will simply list their ten most manageable enemies.

Walk me through the incentives like I'm an interested amateur: how would officials choose their ten names, what would the audit year actually do to their behavior, where would the scheme get captured or gamed first, what failure modes am I not seeing, and can any of them be fixed without gutting the thing? And on the conceptual side: is someone who only stays clean because an enemy is watching better described as having integrity, or as being managed — and does the distinction matter for what we're actually trying to get out of public officials? Finally, could this arrangement be bad even if it worked exactly as designed, for instance by making officials too timid or by turning political opposition into a paid profession? As a standing request, tell me how confident you are in the specific claims, and single out the point your least sure of.
