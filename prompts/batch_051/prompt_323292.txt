I'm making a handout for a graduate seminar on group agency and want it to work as a decision tree rather than a survey. Please write a taxonomy of positions on the discursive dilemma, anchored in this case: a three-judge panel must decide liability, which follows exactly from two findings — a binding contract existed (P), and it was breached (Q). Judge 1 accepts P but rejects Q, and rejects liability; Judge 2 rejects P but accepts Q, and rejects liability; Judge 3 accepts both and accepts liability. Premise-by-premise, a majority accepts P and a majority accepts Q, which together entail liability; on the conclusion, a majority rejects liability. Build the tree from the diagnostic questions first, then place the named positions in that logical space. Each branch node should be a yes/no question answerable without already knowing the labels: whether the mismatch between majority premises and majority conclusion is a defect to be removed; whether the remedy, if any, should abandon the idea of a unified group belief set; whether, if a group belief set is kept, the conclusion should be privileged over the premises or the premises over the conclusion; and whether, if premises are privileged, the collective judgment set should be selected by minimizing total disagreement with the individual judges rather than by proposition-by-proposition majority. Leaves must include at least the premise-based procedure, the conclusion-based procedure, distance-based aggregation, and a "no unified group agent" view; for each leaf, give one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Flag any pairs of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate, and diagnose any unoccupied combinations of answers as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
