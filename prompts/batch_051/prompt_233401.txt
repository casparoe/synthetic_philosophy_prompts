I have been comparing four families of account of what it is to be uncorruptible — a character-trait view, a rational-choice view on which the uncorruptible person is simply the one whose price is higher than any offer she will actually face, a role-norm view on which corruption is deviation from the standards proper to one's office, and an integrity view on which incorruptibility is just integrity applied to inducements — and I find that each handles its own favourite case well and quietly drops the cases that embarrass it. Before I choose among them, I want the full specification of what a theory in this area is answerable for. Write the desiderata: the cases any adequate theory must classify correctly, the distinctions it must not run together, and the explanatory work it must do, with the case or consideration that makes each item a requirement. Then say which requirements you regard as negotiable and which are not, and identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude candidate views.

Work through at least the following pressure points. (i) What separates being incorruptible from merely being untested, and from being insensitive to one currency of influence — money, say — while remaining soft to another, such as status, belonging, or fear? (ii) Does corruption require an inducement to change what one does, or can it consist in accepting a benefit for a decision one would have made anyway? (iii) Does yielding to a threat count as being corrupted or only as being coerced, and should resistance to threats be explained by the same disposition as resistance to bribes? (iv) How should the theory handle the slow accumulation of small compromises, where no single step crosses a clear line but the endpoint is plainly corrupt? (v) Is incorruptibility a property of a person's character, of the norms internal to a role, or of an institution's incentive structure, and can a person be incorruptible inside a corrupt institution or an institution be incorruptible even though its members are not? (vi) Why can an incorruptible person sometimes be worse, not better, than a corruptible one, and how does incorruptibility relate to rigidity, fanaticism, and high risk tolerance? (vii) Does the concept earn its keep alongside integrity, honesty, and strength of will, or is one of those doing all the work?

Finally, score the four families briefly against the list, keep the scoring short — the criteria are the deliverable — and flag any pair of requirements you think cannot be jointly satisfied. Throughout, indicate how confident you are in the verdicts you give, and close by naming the single point you are least sure about.
