Take the Lost Cause retelling of the American Civil War and Reconstruction — the one where the Confederacy loses the war but then wins the memory war for a solid century: noble Confederates, contented enslaved people, Reconstruction as a corrupt disaster, the whole thing cemented in textbooks and statues. I want you to analyze that episode through the lens of the slogan that history is written by the victors, and then turn it back around and tell me what the case does to the slogan. Be disciplined about it: who exactly is the victor and who is the writer in this story, and what would the slogan have predicted? Does the Lost Cause fit because the people controlling the narrative were victors in some other sense — money, political power, cultural institutions — or is it a genuine anomoly that should make us stop saying the slogan? And be honest about the limits: what does this episode show about the difference between a story's grip and its truth, and is there anything here the slogan is just too blunt to explain?
