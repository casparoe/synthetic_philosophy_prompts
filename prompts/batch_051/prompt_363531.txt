I have been working through the experience-machine thought experiment on my own and keep coming back to a suspicion that the usual version rigs the result in advance. In the standard setup, the machine is the pleasurable option and the life outside is the option where your deeper aims about how the world really is get met. So one view of a good life—that it is made of pleasant experience—says enter, while another view—that a good life is getting what you ultimately want—says stay outside. The conclusions seem to be built into the arrangement rather than forced by the ideas themselves.

I would like you to construct a case that reverses this. Build a variant of the experience-machine choice in which the pleasant-experience view is committed to saying stay outside, and the getting-what-you-want view is committed to saying enter. Constraints: the machine can only generate experiences for the person inside it; it may not change the outside world or benefit anyone else there; the person should be a recognizable, mentally healthy adult rather than someone depressed, self-destructive, or fixated on a single contrived goal; the scenario should take one paragraph; and the reversal must fall out of the structure of the person's wants, not from one option being made obviously worse than the other.

After you present the scenario, walk through the reasoning each view gives, explain which feature of the case is doing the reversal, say what would happen if that feature were removed, and give an account of how a defender of the pleasant-experience view would try to resist the case.
