I'm teaching a small grad seminar in formal epistemology, and we've been going around in circles about whether Cox's theorem actually does what it's usually advertised as doing — namely, show that any "logic of plausible reasoning" satisfying a few innocuous-looking requirements has to be ordinary probability theory. I want to flip the usual discussion around: instead of starting from the axioms and checking the proof, I want my students to independently generate the list of requirements a theory of graded belief would have to meet before anyone is entitled to say that probability is the unique reasonable way to update and combine degrees of belief.

So act like an expert formal epistemologist and, thinking carefully, first brainstorm candidate requirements before committing, then write them out as a list. For each one, give the consideration that motivates it (what would go wrong if you dropped it: path-dependence, incoherence, susceptibility to Dutch books, inability to reason from evidence to hypothesis, and so on), and mark it as either non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. Then discuss the part I most want to see: which requirements are doing the real exclusionary work, and specifically whether the usual Cox-style assumptions are as innocent as they look. In particular, is the assumption that the plausibility of a conjunction A&B depends only on the plausibility of A given B and the plausibility of B (via some universal function) a genuine consistency requirement, or is it a smuggled-in piece of truth-functionality? Are continuity/differentiability assumptions legitimate desiderata or just technical conveniences? And what should we say about non-probabilistic rivals — belief functions that let evidential mass attach to sets of worlds, ordinal ranking functions where a belief's grade is how surprising it's negation would be, possibilistic logic — do they fail the list because they reject a requirement that's genuinely mandatory, or only because they reject one of the negotiable ones? If some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, or if the whole list underdetermines the probability calculus in the way some supposed counterexamples suggest, say so; that's the part I'll build the class around.
