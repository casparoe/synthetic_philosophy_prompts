At a party last week a philosophy major explained nihilism to me like this: "Nihilism is the view that only indivisible particles really exist. Tables, chairs, even people are just arrangements of particles, so strictly speaking none of those composite things exist. That means moral properties like wrongness don't exist either, because they'd have to be out there in the world as some kind of thing, and no such thing shows up among the particles. So nothing is actually right or wrong. And since right and wrong are the only things that could give human life any meaning, it follows that nothing anyone does matters at all. Nihilism can't actually be lived — nobody can get through a Tuesday without caring about things — and the fact that even nihilists get angry when you steal their wallet shows the view is false."

I walked away feeling like he ran together three seperate views and used the fact that a view is hard to live by as evidence against it, but I don't know the terminology well enough to say exactly where he went wrong. Can you go through his blurb sentence by sentence and audit it: which parts are fair glosses of which positions (I keep hearing about moral, existential, and mereological nihilism), where he slides from one to another, and whether the conclusion that nothing matters follows from the accurate parts? Also, does the fact that people can't consistently live as if nothing matters count against the truth of any of these views?
