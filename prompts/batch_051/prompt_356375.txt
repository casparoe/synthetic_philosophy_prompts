I sit on my university's ethical sourcing comittee and we're stuck on a contract renewal. The supplier that makes our branded hoodies pays above the local going rate for factory work and has a better safety record than most plants in the region, but it still falls short of the living-wage benchmark the university adopted a few years back, and shifts can run 60 hours in peak season. Here's the wrinkle: worker reps from the factory sent us a letter asking us not to pull the contract, because the realistic alternatives for them are worse-paid factory work or farm labor, and our last inspection found no forced or child labor. Half the committee says renewing is fine because everyone gains and the workers themselves want it; the other half says a mutual gain can still amount to exploitation—mutually beneficial exploitation, they call it—and our own living-wage policy makes this our problem. Draft the decision memo I'll circulate before next Thursday's vote: it should reccommend renew, don't renew, or renew with conditions and a timeframe, engage the strongest argument on the side you reject, and give us criteria we can apply when this comes up again.
