so here's a mechanism i keep sketching in my notes for a discussion site, and i want to think through what it would actually do before i put it in a study. The core idea is a split between a public score and a hidden shadow score. Every post, the moment it's published, is rated 1–5 by a shadow jury of nine randomly selected active users. They see just the raw text: no author, no karma, no vote count, no comments. These ratings are stored and revealed two weeks later. Separately, any logged-in user can upvote or downvote posts freely (one per post), but a vote's weight in the public score is the voter's predictive karma, w. w starts at 1 and is updated each month: you're scored on how well your public votes forecast the median shadow rating of posts you voted on before the shadow rating was revealed. Keep upvoting posts that end up above the sitewide shadow median and downvoting ones that end up below it, and w rises; be wrong, and it falls. Ignore the exact scoring function — assume it's a proper scoring rule and everyone can see it. The default feed is sorted mostly by the public weighted score, not the shadow score.

What does the equilibrium of this look like? It seems like anyone optimizing for influence would stop upvoting what they personally like and start voting for what they expect nine random strangers to later rate highly. But maybe that's fine, because that just turns the public score into a forecast of the shadow jury. Where does it break? Do you get users strategically never voting on ambiguous posts so they don't tank their w, leaving the public score with no signal exactly where its most needed? Does the shadow jury itself become the target — alts to get on juries, leaking the ratings early, or jurors rating posts in ways that make their own earlier public votes look good? And can any of this be fixed without bringing back the popularity-contest dynamic the shadow jury was supposed to get rid of?
