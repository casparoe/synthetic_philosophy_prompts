im a novelist, and for the book I'm working on I keep coming back to a legal question that's become a plot problem. A mid-level official in a state agency has proof the state ruined a family's life through its own incompetence, and every lawyer she talks to shrugs and says the state can't be sued without its consent. I'm trying to work out what it would actually mean to say the state did a wrong there.

I've been reading around in Blackstone, and this passage from William Blackstone's *Commentaries on the Laws of England*, Book I, Chapter 7, stopped me (he's quoting Henry Finch):

"The king hath a prerogative in all things, that are not injurious to the subject; for in them all it must be remembered, that the king's prerogative stretcheth not to the doing of any wrong."

Here's my tangle. On one side, I can feel the pull of sovereign immunity as a structural doctrine: if every administrative mistake or policy choice could be dragged into court as a private wrong, the courts would be running the state, and the legislature would never have to answer for anything because judges would get there first. That seems like a real, systematic virtue. On the other side, I cannot shake the intuition that a needed consequence of the doctrine is that the state is exactly the kind of thing that can do wrong without ever having to answer for it as a wrong. And Blackstone there seems to want both: the king's prerogative goes right up to the edge of injury and then, by law, it cannot do wrong. But if the legal machinery for saying "answer for that wrong" is blocked by immunity, how is "cannot do wrong" anything more than a pretty sentence?

I dont want you to tell me which side wins. Walk me through what it would take for the structural-doctrine side to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the intuition side to be the thing that goes, and whether there's a reading of "the state cannot do wrong" on which the Blackstone passage and sovereign immunity both survive without being a slogan.

After that, give me the bottom line as a JSON object with fields: `verdict` (one of `"doctrine_yields"`, `"intuition_yields"`, `"both_survive_under_a_reading"`, or `"neither_survives"`), `what_the_doctrine_gets_right` (one sentence), `what_the_intuition_gets_right` (one sentence), `what_each_side_must_concede` (one sentence), and `confidence` (a number from 0 to 1 for how sure you are that the surviving reading is real and not just therapy for the tension).
