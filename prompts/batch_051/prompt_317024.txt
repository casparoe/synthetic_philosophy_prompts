okay so my paper is on privacy and I've ended up with a list of things I more or less believe, but I suspect they might not all be true together. I'd like an audit: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest single fix. rank the tensions by how severe they are.

(a) privacy is basically control: you have privacy to the extent that you get to decide who has access to what about you.
(b) the value of privacy is purley instrumental—it matters because it protects autonomy, intimacy, reputation, and so on, not for its own sake.
(c) secretly reading someones diary is wrong even when the reader never tells anyone, never acts differently toward them, and the diary-owner never finds out.
(d) if you post something to a public forum, nobody violates your privacy by reading it, archiving it, or linking to it.
(e) a company can violate your privacy by assembling a detailed profile of you out of a thousand innocuous public traces (purchases, locations, likes), even if it never accesses anything that was private.
(f) what makes privacy violations wrong is always that they undercut your ability to control how you present yourself to different audiences.

for each tension, tell me what has to give and what the revision costs, and then say whether there's one change that would straighten out the most.
