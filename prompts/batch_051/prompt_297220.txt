I have a background in type theory and proof-assistant development, and I think most naturally in the vocabulary of contexts and hypotheses, judgments and typing rules, object language versus metalanguage, implication elimination treated as function application under the Curry–Howard correspondence, tactic scripts that elaborate into proof terms, and a trusted kernel that type-checks them. I want you to restate the problem of the justification of deduction, as posed in Lewis Carroll's What the Tortoise Said to Achilles, in exactly that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then tell me what does not survive the translation.

For context, here is the core of Carroll's scenario as I understand it, in case it matters for the encoding. Achilles wants the Tortoise to accept Z on the strength of A and B. The Tortoise accepts A and accepts B but declines to move to Z. Achilles then writes down a further conditional to the effect that if A and B are true then Z must be true, and asks the Tortoise to accept it. The Tortoise grants that conditional but still declines to move to Z, and each time Achilles converts the needed inferential step into a further premise, the Tortoise grants it and still withholds Z. The dialogue ends with the regress still rising.

Please give me a systematic translation, element by element: how A, B, and Z should be encoded; what the Tortoise's request to add the conditional as a premise amounts to; why the regress arises; and what, in your vocabulary, Achilles would have had to do or say to stop it. Then give the residue report. Say which parts of the philosophical problem map cleanly onto the rule-versus-axiom or metalanguage-versus-object-language distinction, which map only with distortion, and which have no expression at all — candidates include the difference between deriving a conclusion and accepting it, the normative force of an inference rule, and the Tortoise's stance as something an agent does rather than something a formal system excludes. Finally, give your verdict: does the type-theoretic and proof-assistant vocabulary solve the Tortoise's problem by diagnosing a category error, does it merely relocate the problem to the metalanguage and the trusted kernel, or does it expose that the philosophical debate is about something your vocabulary cannot see?

One further constraint: separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this. Do not present a contested reading — for instance, of what Carroll himself drew as the moral, or of whether the regress is vicious — as the consensus.
