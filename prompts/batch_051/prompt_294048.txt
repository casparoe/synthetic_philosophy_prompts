Last weekend I got into it with a friend about a group in our city that blocked a highway to protest a new state law, and at some point he said "an unjust law is no law at all" and clearly thought that settled whether they were justified in breaking it. I know its a slogan and not a literal claim; an unjust statute is obviously still a statute in the sense that it was enacted and a cop will still arrest you for violating it. But I realized I don't actually know what precise claim that line is supposed to be making, and the more I turn it over the more readings I can see: something about legal validity, something about whether it binds your conscience, something about whether it deserves obedience, maybe something about what law really is. I'd like a careful menu of the different claims that sentence could be expressing, with a verdict on each — platitude, substantive and defensible, or false — and then the part I actually care about: which of those readings would actually support the conclusion that people may disobey an unjust law, and which would do nothing for that conclusion even if they were true.

Also, please be explicit about where people who know this stuff are mostly settled and where you're giving me a live dispute; I don't want a contested reading passed off as the consensus. And formatwise: no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, just continuous prose the way you'd write a letter. American English please.
