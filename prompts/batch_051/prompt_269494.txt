After a long argument with a friend about private cities and seasteading, I am left with an asymmetry I cannot settle. We both accepted that a person who signs a private agreement—say, to abide by a housing association's rules or to arbitrate disputes under a private city's charter—genuinely binds herself because she chose it and can leave it. But we both also hesitated to say that a citizen consents to her state's laws merely because emigration is physically possible. What exactly is the difference between these two kinds of exit? I want the strongest principle that could justify treating private governance through contract as consensual while treating state governance as not consensual, and then I want that principle tested against harder cases: a private city that becomes the only realistic employer in a region, or a state that makes emigration cheap and easy. Is the ordinary asymmetry justified, or should we give it up?
