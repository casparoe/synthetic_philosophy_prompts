Here are the criteria I was taught for a tight culture: strong, widely agreed social norms; low tolerance for deviating from those norms; and reliable punishment for norm violations (with loose cultures as the mirror image). Now imagine a small intentional community whose one sacred norm is that nobody may impose or enforce interpersonal rules. Calling out another member's behavior—even ordinary moral criticism like saying that was cruel—is treated as the one real offense: being a rule-enforcer. The community shames them, and repeat offenders are eventually expelled. At the same time, the community runs its meetings under an extremely strict procedural code: exactly timed agendas, a rotating chair whose rulings are binding, no speaking off-turn, and automatic fines from the common pot for any procedural slip. Would you call this a tight culture? Work through the criteria one by one, say where they underdetermine the verdict, and consider whether the case shows we need to separate the content of norms from their enforcement, or score tightness by domain, before you settle on an answer.
