I've been going back and forth on the AI legal personhood thing after binging some rationalist-forum threads, and my roommate gave me this summary of the corporate-person analogy to settle it. His version: corporations are already legal persons in basically every jurisdiction, which just means the law treats them as a single entity that can hold property, enter contracts, and sue or be sued. Legal personhood in general is just a bundle of rights and duties, not a claim about having a mind, so there's nothing spooky about adding AI systems to the same list. If we can impose duties and liability on a paper entity like a corporation, we can do it to an AI too; the only real remaining question is whether the system has enough autonomy and assets to be worth suing, which is a practical problem, not a conceptual one.
