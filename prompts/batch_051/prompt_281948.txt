Next term I am presenting a seminar paper to a department whose members are mostly moral philosophers with broadly rationalist sympathies, together with two intellectual historians of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries who are openly sceptical of any general theory that flattens local detail. The paper defends what I call the status-first account of moral revolutions. The claim is that when practices such as duelling and slave-holding collapse, the collapse is not best explained by the force of the better argument, nor by shifts in material interest alone, but by a reversal in the social meaning of the practice: what had been honourable and shame-avoiding becomes contemptible or ridiculous, and once that reversal is established the practice unravels quickly even among people whose stated views have not moved. By honour I mean something narrower than general approval or economic interest: the standing one gains or loses by meeting or flouting the demands of a code one cannot simply opt out of. Arguments, on this view, are largely retrospective rationalisations and instruments for people already riding the status shift, not the engine of the change. I then generalise: in moral revolutions the distribution of honour and contempt shifts first, and the reasons follow.

Draft my Q&A preparation sheet for this audience. I want the objections I will actually get from these people, not the objections I might get from sociologists or from activists, ordered by how likely and how damaging each is. Aim for seven or eight objections, each phrased the way this audience would phrase it; I expect some to press on whether two cases can bear that generalisation, some on whether the abolitionist arguments of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries were doing the real causal work, some on whether a shift in honour is itself caused by a shift in reasons, and some on whether the account can distinguish moral progress from a mere change in fashion. For each objection, give a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is soft. Include the question I should most hope nobody asks, and say why.
