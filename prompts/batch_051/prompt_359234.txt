I'm working through Popper's philosophy of social science, and I keep returning to a passage in a handbook of political theory that summarizes The Poverty of Historicism. I'm inclined to trust it because the source is professional and the summary is so tidy, but a few of its moves strike me as sliding from one claim into another. The passage runs:

"Popper's The Poverty of Historicism is an attack on the idea that history has an overall direction. Historicism, as Popper uses the term, is the belief that societies develop according to iron laws that the social sciences can discover, and that the proper goal of social science is therefore large-scale historical prediction. Popper's central argument is that the future course of history cannot be predicted because the future growth of scientific knowledge cannot be predicted, and knowledge is itself a major driver of historical change: to predict tomorrow's discoveries we would have to make them today. He concludes that there are no historical laws and that history as a whole cannot be scientifically predicted. Popper also argues that, because the same events never repeat in exactly the same way, historical explanation is never nomological; it can only be a matter of narrative retelling of unique events, not of subsuming them under general laws. The lesson of the book is therefore largely negative: the social sciences should abandon all lawlike explanation and restrict themselves to describing particular events."

The handbook then draws the conclusion that "Popper showed that any social science aiming at explanation or prediction is historicist and should be given up."

Is this a fair summary? I'd like an audit that goes claim by claim: which parts a careful Popper would accept, which are common simplifications, and which are just wrong. In particular, I want to know whether the distinction between laws and trends matters here, whether the argument about the unpredictability of knowledge is really doing all the work the summary assigns to it, and whether the handbook's closing conclusion actually follows from anything accurate in the summary. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question, in American English.
