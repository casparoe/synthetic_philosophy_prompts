I've got this rule for animal behavior: when a dog or a crow or an octopus does something that looks clever, I always look for the simplest explanation that fits and I mostly stick with it unless it gets ruled out. Like a dog opening a gate latch — I'd chalk it up to trial-and-error, not to the dog understanding how the latch works. I know this is basically Morgan's canon, and to me it feels like Occam's razor for minds. My girlfriend says its just a bias in the other direction: always defaulting to the dumbest mental story means I'll systematically undercount animals that actually do understand something, and that there's good evidence against that default for some species. Is my rule defensible as is? Or is the right version something like: treat the simple explanation as a prior, not a conclusion, and let it get swamped by strong enough behavior? If so, what should count as strong enough, when I'm not running experiments and I'm just trying to form a belief from a video? Keep the response under 300 words.
