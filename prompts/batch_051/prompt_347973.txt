ok so I've been trying to pin down what I actually think about killing insects, and I keep having the feeling that I'm smuggling in intuitions that don't fit my official view. Here's the set of things I'd want to defend, more or less in my own words.

(1) I'm a welfarist and roughly consequentialist: what matters at bottom is the felt experience of sentient beings, with no separate line at vertebrates or at animals that look like us, and whether a harm is intended or merely foreseen doesn't matter in itself — only the expected experiences that result.

(2) I'm genuinely unsure whether insects are sentient. I'd put maybe a 30 to 40 percent chance that the common pest insects — aphids, caterpillars, cockroaches, flies — feel pain, and I take that chance seriously.

(3) Given that uncertainty I use expected-value reasoning, plus a precautionary lean: when there's a real chance of a lot of suffering, I won't write it off just because we cant prove it, and I'll only accept insect deaths when the human benefit is large and hard to get another way.

(4) So I think it's wrong to fog mosquitoes in a park just so an evening picnic is a bit less buggy, and wrong to spray a lawn just so it's greener. Those are small, optional benefits, and the number of deaths is huge.

(5) But I keep cockroach bait in my apartment and I think that's fine. Roaches in a kitchen are a sanitation problem, and I can't reasonably live with an infestation.

(6) I also think subsistence farmers in poor countries should be allowed to keep using cheap synthetic insecticides on staple crops, even though they kill enormous numbers of insects, because without them real people face hunger.

(7) Yet the insects a combine harvester crushes during the harvest itself don't bother me nearly as much as the same field being sprayed a month earlier. If you told me the numbers were similar, I'd still think the spraying is worse — it feels like a different kind of act, poisoning them on purpose, whereas the combine is just doing something and they're in the way.

(8) And I'll add that I swat a mosquito that lands on my arm, and I don't think that's any kind of inconsistency.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than just listing everything, say what would have to give in each case and what the cheapest fix is, and if one revision would clean up the most, say which. I want an audit of my own view, not a lecture on insect sentience. And when you write back, no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — just continuous prose, like an email.
