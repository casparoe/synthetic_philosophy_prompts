I’ve been rereading Kant’s 1795 essay on perpetual peace with a group that mostly thinks about institutional design and incentive systems rather than the history of philosophy. We got stuck on the First Supplement, where he gives what he calls a guarantee of perpetual peace. As I understand the argument, nature itself provides something like a hidden mechanism: conflict, self-interest, and the spirit of commerce push states toward a condition that approximates peace even though no state wills peace from moral motives. He raises the thought that the institutional problem can be solved even for a nation of clever, selfish beings if you arrange their competing private interests so they check one another, and that a lasting peace works by the same kind of causal channel rather than by moral improvement. I want to pressure-test that idea as an equilibrium analysis, not as a piece of Kant scholarship.

Suppose we set up a Kantian league in the form of the three definitive articles: every member must have a republican constitution; there is a federation of free states rather than a single world state; and there is a cosmopolitan right of hospitality limited to temporary sojourn and open commercial intercourse. Add one modern twist: the federation operates a standing trade and arbitration council that publishes annual reports on each member’s militarization, treaty secrecy, and compliance with the republican criteria. Members found deficient are suspended from preferential trade terms and arbitration, but the council has no army and cannot expel a state: it can only withdraw benefits, and it cannot compel a great power to participate. It is an information-and-incentive institution backed by commerce, not a court with enforcement.

Walk through how this system would behave if Kant’s guarantee thesis is roughly right: what does it incentivize for small states, commercial powers, and a militarily dominant non-republican state? Where does it get gamed first, and what second-order effects appear inside member states once compliance with the republican criteria is tied to trade access? Are the failure modes fixable by adjusting the audit rules, or does every fix pull it toward a world state or back toward an unenforceable paper league? I want the mechanism analyzed the way one would analyze a reputation system or a trade treaty, not a verdict on whether the proposal is noble.
