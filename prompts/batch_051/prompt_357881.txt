I'm retired now (I was a research biologist), and I've spent the last couple of years reading moral philosophy and some personality psychology, mostly around character. I'm drafting something for my philosophy circle on what I actually think about grit and resilience, and before I present I'd like a consistency check on the set of claims I seem to hold.

Here's where I've landed:

(a) I'm broadly Aristotelian about character: a virtue isn't just a useful habit; it's a stable trait involving judgment about what's worth doing, so a trait like determination only counts as a virtue when its pointed at worthwhile ends under something like practical wisdom.

(b) But I also think Duckworth's "grit" is best read as old-fashioned fortitude/perseverance with a laboratory gloss, so I'm happy to call grit a virtue.

(c) I think most workplace and policy talk about "resilience" is ideology: it locates the problem in individuals who don't bounce back fast enough, when a lot of it is in the enviornment.

(d) But I don't think we should admire people for grimly sticking with situations they'd be more rational to fight or leave; some of what gets celebrated as "resilience" looks to me like adaptive preference or learned helplessness with a halo.

(e) And yet I also hold that fortitude is a virtue precisely because it shows up under genuinely hard conditions — a virtue ethics that only admires flourishing in good conditions misses the point.

(f) From my old training, I'm also impressed by the psychometrics: grit shows up as a single, fairly stable disposition that predicts grades, graduation, retention, and so on across contexts.

(g) Still, I don't think being useful or predictive is what makes something a virtue; a virtue can be costly and can predict worse outcomes by worldly standards.

Are these jointly tenable? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equal; say which pairs are only apparently in tension; and for each real tension tell me what would have to give and at what cost, including what the cheapest overall fix is. Keep the response under 450 words.
