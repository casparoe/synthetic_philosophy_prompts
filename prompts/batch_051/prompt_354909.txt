At a debate tournament the resolution was something like the US should adopt European-style hate speech laws. Every time the affirmative side brought up Germany's Holocaust-denial ban or France's restrictions on inciting racial hatred, the negative kept repeating the line that the remedy for bad speech is more speech, not censorship, as if that alone answered the whole case. I'm trying to write my ballot and I want to know what that slogan could actually be claiming. Lay out the distinct readings for me — from ones so weak they're almost just tautologies to ones strong enough that only a committed free-speech absolutist would buy them — and for each reading say whether it would actually tell against a European-style ban, and which reading the negative actually needed to make their response work.
