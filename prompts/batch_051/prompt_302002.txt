I'm putting together a handout for an intro philosophy of science course, specifically the unit on scientific realism, and I've gotten myself a bit stuck on the pessimistic meta-induction. As I understand it, the argument runs: theories like the caloric theory of heat, phlogiston, and the luminiferous ether were all empirically successful in there day, made predictions, got accepted by smart people, and then turned out to be deeply wrong about the unobservable stuff they posited. So by induction, probably our current successful theories will also turn out to be deeply wrong, and realism is in trouble. The replies I've collected so far are: (1) the selective or divide-and-conquer reply — only the parts of past theories that were actually doing the predictive work got preserved across theory change, and those are exactly the parts the realist needs to believe in, so the record isn't as destructive as it looks; (2) the structuralist reply — what survives theory change is the mathematical structure (same equations, different story about the underlying entities), so the realist should only commit to structure, not to the nature of the unobservables; (3) the unrepresentative-sample reply — the induction cheats by sampling from immature science, whereas today's theories have better instruments, more mathematization, and a better track record of novel prediction, so the past failures tell us less than the argument pretends; and (4) the referential-continuity reply — lots of supposedly refuted theories actually got the reference of their central terms right and only got the descriptions wrong (dephlogisticated air turned out to be oxygen), so their success can still be explained by getting something right about the world.

I keep having the sinking feeling that these four are basically all the moves there are, and maybe the honest answer for the handout is just "there are four families of reply to the pessimistic meta-induction." But before I give up: is there a fifth reply I'm missing? It has to be genuinely different from all four — not a rewording, not a special case like a fancy version of selective realism, and not two of them stapled together. And if you've got one, tell me whether it's actually any good, and whether it competes with the standard ones or could just be absorbed by one of them.
