Write a deadpan parody of a dojo brochure for an academy that teaches epistemic rationality as a martial art. Aim for roughly 750 words, formatted as promotional material: a mission statement, a belt-rank ladder, an account of the school's signature training method (which it calls "kata"), a description of what happens in "sparring," and a five-item FAQ. Keep the voice earnest, disciplined, and quietly grandiose; the dojo believes it has solved the problem of human bias through repetition and ritual, and it must never wink.

The humor should come from sustained, accurate exaggeration, not invention. Borrow from both vocabularies faithfully: belts, dojo etiquette, kihon, kata, kumite, uke and tori on one side; base-rate neglect, motivated reasoning, updating, calibration, near transfer, and far transfer on the other. Make one philosophical point through the joke: the students demonstrably improve at the dojo's own drills, jargon, and rituals, while the evidence that any of this improves judgment outside the dojo is thin — and the dojo's explanations for that gap are themselves a case study in motivated reasoning and ad hoc rescue. Every failure of transfer should be treated by the dojo as a failure of the student's practice or character, never as a limitation of the training. Without saying so directly, show that every rank test and success metric takes place inside the dojo.

Invent the dojo, its founder, its slogans, and its students; do not name any real organization, program, teacher, or living person. A reader who knows nothing about the rationality community should still find it funny, while a reader familiar with the research on transfer of training should recognize the target and find the satire fair.
