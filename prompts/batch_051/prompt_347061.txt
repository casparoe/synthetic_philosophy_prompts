I have been trying to get clear on one question in the philosophy of biology, and I have ended up with a homemade distinction that feels too neat to be new, yet I cannot find it stated in exactly this form. The question is whether DNA really is a code, or whether the word code is a metaphor doing illicit work. I distinguish three senses. A 'cipher' sense: a finite mapping from one vocabulary to another in which the pairing is arbitrary, had to be fixed somehow, and could in principle have been different, like the codon table that maps the 64 nucleotide triplets (61 to amino acids, 3 to stop) onto the standard amino acids, a mapping that is largely arbitrary and nearly, but not quite, universal. A 'correlation' sense: a state that tracks another state closely enough that one can infer the latter from the former, as tree rings track drought years, with no sender, no message, and no rule. An 'instruction' sense: a stored specification that, when run by the right machinery, produces an organized result, like a recipe or a program. My central claim is that DNA is a code in the correlation sense trivially, in the cipher sense only for the narrow codon-to-amino-acid mapping and not for the gene-to-trait story, and in the instruction sense only metaphorically. The part I find interesting is that the one genuinely literal code — the codon table, cracked experimentally — seems to lend its prestige to the other two senses, so that many disputes about whether DNA is really a code dissolve once you fix which sense each side is using, and most of the rhetorical weight of saying that a gene codes for a trait or a disease risk is carried by the third, least plausible sense.

I would like an honest assessment of this. Is it merely a restatement of a familiar distinction from the literature under my own labels, and if so, what does the closest existing version say? Does the correlation sense do any real work, or is it so promiscuous that it applies to nearly everything and therefore says nothing distinctive about genetic material? Does the instruction sense genuinely differ from the cipher sense in practice, given that the translation apparatus — tRNAs, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, the ribosome — really does execute a mapping that no one designed? And where does the three-way split break down, for instance when a gene is said to code for a probabilistic disease risk rather than a discrete protein? If the distinction is basically sound, which of the three senses is doing the explanatory work in an ordinary sentence like: a gene codes for an enzyme?
