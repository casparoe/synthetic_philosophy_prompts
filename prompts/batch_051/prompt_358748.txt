Ok so I tried to formalize the procreation asymmetry and I can't find the hole. It goes:

(1) For an existing person, pain is bad and pleasure is good.
(2) For a person who never exists, the absence of pain is good, even if there's no actual person who benefits from it.
(3) For a person who never exists, the absence of pleasure is not bad, because there's no actual person who is deprived.
(4) So existence contains a bad (pain) and a good (pleasure); non-existence contains a good (no pain) and nothing bad.
(5) Other things equal, a state with a good and no bad is better than a state with a good and a bad.
(6) Therefore non-existence is better than existence.
(7) If non-existence is better than existence, then coming into existence is a harm.
(8) So bringing anyone into existence is wrong.

I keep thinking the culprit is probably (2), because it says something is 'good' when there's literally no one for it to be good for, but then I worry I'm just refusing Benatar's asymetry by fiat. Is the argument valid or not, and if not, which step fails and why? Walk me through it.
