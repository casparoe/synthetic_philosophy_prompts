I have been reading modern histories of large political and economic crises, and I keep noticing a pattern for which I don't have a name. A writer will say that an outcome—the outbreak of the First World War, say—had several causes, each of which, considered on its own, would apparently have been enough to bring the event about. The writer then proceeds to treat those causes as anything but symmetrical. One is singled out as the cause, while the others are demoted to background conditions, enabling circumstances, or mere triggers; and which cause gets promoted seems to vary from book to book—the alliance system in one account, the rigid mobilization timetables in another—without tracking anything in the counterfactual structure of the situation. I have noticed the same thing in accounts of the 2008 financial crisis, where low interest rates, regulatory failure, and the structure of mortgage securitization are often each described as sufficient in their own way, yet one is always placed in the foreground. Is there an established concept, or a specific debate in the philosophy of history or of causation, that names and analyzes this asymmetry of explanatory salience among causes that are all presented as sufficient? How does it differ from plain overdetermination, and from a conditional analysis on which a cause is not sufficient or necessary by itself but is a non-redundant component of a sufficient set of conditions? I want the phenomenon carved, not just labelled: what governs the promotion, whether the promoted cause is truth-tracking or merely rhetorical, and where the boundaries are. If nothing established names exactly this, coin a term for it, define it, and say where its edges are.

After your analysis, give me a structured summary in the form of a table with exactly these five columns: Candidate term (the established label, or, if none fits, proposed coinage: [your term]); Nearest existing concept and discipline; What it captures; What it misses or would mislabel; Fit verdict (perfect, partial, or poor, followed by a one-line justification in the same cell).
