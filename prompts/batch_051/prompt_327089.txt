So my friend has this argument that's been bugging me, and I can't find the exit. He says: (1) We don't treat it as wrong when parents make decisions for their kids, or when a society ditches an old tool after a better one gets built. The less capable thing getting steered, or eventually replaced, by the more capable thing just seems like how growth works. (2) If we ever build an AI that beats us across the board—smarter, more self-controlled, more creative, better at actually caring about what matters—then the same principle says we should let it take over. (3) On his story humans would be a stepping stone to it, like a childhood phase of intelligence; fighting it would be like a kid demanding to stay in charge forever. (4) So people who say we should never hand the keys to an AI are just being sentimental, not giving a real argument. I want to say the premises are fine one at a time but (4) feels wrong, and I also keep thinking the argument proves to much: wouldn't the exact same reasoning say that any group of humans who scored higher on those same capabilities could take over decisions from any group that scored lower, and nobody accepts that. Where does the argument actually slip? And what does it cost to escape it—do I have to throw out (1) entirely, or can I keep something like it for kids and tools while blocking the AI conclusion?
