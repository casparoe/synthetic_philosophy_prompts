Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between the following two positions in the evolutionary-debunking debate in metaethics would produce.

The first position, the global debunker, holds that the evolutionary genealogy of moral judgment debunks moral beliefs across the board: because natural selection shaped our moral judgments to foster fitness rather than to track mind-independent moral truth, such judgments are unjustified, or at least cannot be regarded as reliably tracking moral facts. The second position, the limited-scope realist, holds that evolutionary explanation debunks at most a restricted class of moral intuitions — for example, biologically salient taboos or disgust-mediated prohibitions — while leaving the central content of moral thought, such as judgments about harm, flourishing, and mutually justifiable rules, capable of reliably tracking moral truth, in particular if a common third factor explains both why such judgments were fitness-enhancing and why they are true.

The deliverable should be the document the two camps would co-sign, divided into four sections. Section 1 should list numbered claims both positions accept, covering what evolutionary explanations can and cannot be presumed to establish, the logical structure of a debunking argument, including why a merely evolutionary origin does not by itself debunk a belief, and what would count as a successful debunking of a delimited class of judgments. These claims should be substantive rather than vacuous compromises. Section 2 should be a disagreement table: for each genuine point of divergence, state how the global debunker would formulate the point, how the limited-scope realist would formulate it, and what each side regards as wrong or misleading in the other formulation. Section 3 should identify the cruxes, the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side, and for each crux record what each side predicts and what evidence, arguments, or cases both sides agree would bear on it. Section 4 should say whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal, and if so exactly which part and why.

In drafting the statement, give particular attention to three pressure points. First, can the limited-scope realist draw the boundary between the debunked periphery and the preserved core of moral thought without already presupposing a first-order moral view, and does that boundary line up with any independently motivated evolutionary distinction? Second, does the global debunker's argument overgeneralize to beliefs in logic, mathematics, or the empirical science on which the debunking argument itself relies, and if so, what principle would restrict the debunking so that it reaches all of ethics but spares the premises it needs? Third, is there a non-ad-hoc third factor that would make core moral beliefs likely to be true, or is any proposed third factor ultimately moral truth doing explanatory work under another name? Record each side's committed answer to these questions as part of the statement.
