My friend has spent way too much time thinking about why some people's bids to be happier work and others backfire, and her track record is annoyingly sharp. Cases she's already called: the coworker who logs every activity in a mood spreadsheet and optimizes next week's schedule around the high-scoring rows — she says he's going to stay miserable; the old cabinetmaker who just loves joinery and never once asks whether it makes him happy — genuinely content; the woman who started volunteering because she'd read it raises wellbeing, but spends each shift quietly auditing whether she feels happier yet — still flat. I can't tell wether she cares about people's reasons for starting a thing, because when she explains her verdicts she almost always talks about whether the person was monitoring themselves.

The new one: a woman who takes up pottery with zero independent interest in it, purely because a therapist said it was a reliable mood-lifter, but while she's at the wheel she's completely absorbed and never checks in with herself during a session. What does my friend predict? More importantly, which rule is she running: an intention rule — aiming directly at happiness poisons it, so it can only ever be a byproduct of something valued for its own sake — an absorption rule — what actually kills it is self-monitoring, and instrumental reasons are fine as long as you lose yourself — or some third thing? And if the pottery case wouldn't cleanly split the two, tell me what further case to run by her.
