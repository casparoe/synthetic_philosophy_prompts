After forty years in the lab I've started reading philosophy seriously, and the thing I can't stop turning over is the simulation argument. What attracts me to it is that it isn't metaphysics by poetry: it just partitions the possibilities and asks which is most probable. Roughly, either nearly all civilisations at our level go extinct before they can run huge numbers of ancestor-simulations; or the civilisations that could run them almost never do; or I am almost certainly one of the simulated minds rather than an original one. That trilemma looks hard to escape, and it seems to pull the rug from under far-future fanaticism of the sort I find convincing elsewhere. If the first horn is right, the future those arguments bank on never arrives. If the third horn is right, the trillions of future people are part of the simulation and my choices don't reach whatever base reality is doing. Either way, the enormous expected value people attach to nudging the long-run trajectory of humanity starts to look like a mistake.

But I can't make the intuition budge. When I think about a nuclear exchange or an engineered pandemic, I feel the weight of all the people who would otherwise have existed across millions of years, and the idea that a tidy sceptical trilemma should flaten that into a shrug about whether any of it is real strikes me as, frankly, insane. It isn't just that the future matters; it's that the numbers are so large that even a small probability of being in the real, base-level world with a reachable far future should swamp almost anything else. So there's the clash: a theory I find compelling, with exactly the virtues I used to like in science, telling me to be suspicious of an intuition I can't seem to doubt.

Which should yield? Should I treat the intuition as evolved psychology misfiring at long timescales and discount it? Or should I amend the take-away from the simulation argument, for instance because simulated minds might count just as much or because the probabilities rest on assumptions too fragile to carry this weight? Or is there a reading on which they are compatible — the argument only trims my confidence a bit without touching the expected value? Give me the strongest version of both sides and then tell me what you would give up and what would justify it.
