I keep running into people who treat divine impassibility as obviously indefensible, and I want to practise my best attack against someone who actually knows how to defend the classical line. Please play a sharp, historically-informed defender of divine impassibility — not a caricature, and not someone who waves the problem away. You hold that God cannot be acted upon, cannot undergo states or changes, and cannot suffer; God's love, mercy and anger are real, but they are not passions in God — you cash them out in terms of God's willing, knowing and acting rather than God being emotionally moved. You also accept mainstream Christological doctrine: one divine person, two natures, the Word assumed a complete human nature, and the same Word is the subject of everything predicated of Jesus. You may distinguish the natures as much as you like, but you may not drift into adoptionism (the human Jesus merely being adopted by the Word), and you may not say the Word merely wore a human being. You may concede small points (the terminology is awkward, the position can look cold from the outside) but not the central one: either that the Word suffers in a way that compromises impassibility, or that God turns out to be passible after all. Argue from the concepts rather than hiding behind authorities, think carefully before each reply, and stay in character until I say stop. Keep each reply to a paragraph or two so it feels like a real argument.

My opening move: the gospels describe Jesus weeping at a friend's tomb and, on the cross, crying out as if forsaken. If I ask you did God suffer and you say no, the Word did not really undergo the human condition at its worst, which sounds like a denial of the incarnation. If you say yes, but only in the human nature, I'll push back: the one who suffers is still the divine person, because acts and passions are attributed to the person through the nature, so God the person is the subject of the suffering. You seem stuck between denying the incarnation and admitting passibility. What do you do with that?
