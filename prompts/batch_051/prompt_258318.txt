So I'm a physicalist about the mind—not a hand-wavy one either—and I keep running into the following tension, which I'd like someone to actually adjudicate rather than just take a side on. I got cornered by a sharp undergrad on it last week and realized I'd been letting the two halves of myself talk past each other.

On the theory side: a mind is what a brain does, and a brain is a finite physical system, or at any rate a system with finitely many discriminable states once you factor in noise and the resolution of whatever read-out mechanism you like. So for any finite creature there should be only finitely many distinct experiential states: finitely many ways it can feel to be that creature. A finite catalog, period. This isn't just a weird corollary either; it does real work. It explains why inner life has the texture of recurrence—why déjà vu feels like something, why altered states look like a finite menu of neurochemical knobs, why after enough years things start rhyming. It also says a sufficiently long-lived finite mind would eventually repeat an experience exactly and in principle exhaust its catalog. On paper I'm fine with that sentence.

But the intuition won't budge. From the inside, experience seems inexhaustible. I don't just mean the combinatorics point—that a finite set of elements can be recombined into an astronomically large number of thoughts. I mean the stronger conviction that there is no finite list of ways it feels, because for any list I can, in the very act of surveying it, find the experience of surveying that particular list, which wasnt on it. It feels like a diagonal move, and it makes 'the cardinality of experience' sound like a category mistake rather than a hard-but-true result.

I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it actually costs to give each one up, and whether there's a reading of 'experience' or 'exhaustible' on which they're compatible. Is the intuition just scope insensitivity about big finite numbers—or the open-endedness of our descriptions of experience masquerading as open-endedness of experience itself? Is the theory smuggling in a notion of 'distinct state' that's doing the finiteness work but doesnt capture phenomenal distinctness? And if I do give up the theory, what exactly do I have to surrender in my physicalism to keep experience open-ended?
