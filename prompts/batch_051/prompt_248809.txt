I spent thirty years doing physical chemistry and only recently started reading philosophy of science, and I keep running into “assembly theory” and its claims about measuring complexity. Before I read another paper I want the spec I’ll judge it against: what does an adequate measure of the complexity of an object actually have to get right? I don’t want a survey of existing measures — Shannon entropy, Kolmogorov complexity, assembly index, whatever — I want the desiderata they’d be graded against. Give me exactly five numbered requirements, each a sentence or two, no introduction, no conclusion. For each one, say what case or consideration forces it, and flag any pair you suspect can’t be jointly satisfied. The list should at least have to settle a random string versus a bacterium, an object versus a hundred copies of it, and wether “complex” is a property of the thing or of the process that made it. American English, please.
