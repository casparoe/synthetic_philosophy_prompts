I came to philosophy late, after a career in the natural sciences, so I ask your patience where my formalizations are not as clean as a professional's would be. My reading group has been working through persistence through time, and we reconstructed an argument that seems to show that ordinary objects do not endure through changes. Each step looked individually compelling, yet the conclusion seems to me a reductio of something I am unwilling to surrender. Where does it go wrong?

(1) A poker can be straight at noon and bent at midnight; that is just what it is for the poker to change shape. (2) If the poker endures through that interval, then the very same object is wholly present at noon and at midnight: the thing that is straight at noon is numerically identical to the thing that is bent at midnight. (3) Straightness and bentness are incompatible intrinsic shapes; one and the same object cannot be both straight and bent. (4) From (2), there is a single object of which both are true: it is straight at noon and bent at midnight. (5) But an intrinsic shape must be a way the object itself is, not a relation it bears to a time. If saying the poker is straight at noon genuinely ascribes a shape to it, then the poker is straight, full stop; and the only reason to hang onto the qualification at noon is to avoid the very contradiction that follows. (6) By parity, the poker is bent, full stop. (7) So one object is both straight and bent, contradicting (3). (8) Therefore the poker does not endure through the change; what persists is a four-dimensional whole composed of distinct temporal parts, one straight and one bent, and the same reasoning applies to every ordinary object.

I would most like to keep the ordinary thought that one and the same poker is present before and after the change, but I do not see which premise that thought forces me to reject, or at what price. Please tell me which step a defender of enduring objects should reject, why that step looked unavoidable, and what she gives up in rejecting it. In doing so, separate what is settled among philosophers of persistence from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present one side's view as the consensus.
