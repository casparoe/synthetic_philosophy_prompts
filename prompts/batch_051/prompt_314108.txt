I am a sixth-form student, and for a school science project I have been running simple computer simulations of the iterated prisoner's dilemma, using strategies such as always defect, Tit for Tat, and generous Tit for Tat. The results are easy enough to report in the language of payoffs, but the write-up keeps sliding into the word 'cooperation' as though it had a settled definition, and I am no longer sure it does. Some of my classmates seem to mean an act that costs the actor and benefits another individual, which would count a bird's alarm call as cooperation but would also count a shopkeeper lowering a price in a one-off sale in a way that feels wrong. Others seem to mean mutual assistance maintained by repeated encounters, which would exclude a single act of help between strangers. I would like a working definition of cooperation, as the term is used in the prisoner's dilemma and in discussions of how cooperation could have evolved, that gets the clear cases right, excludes the clear non-cases, and gives a definite verdict — or an explicit 'no answer' — on the borderline ones.

Please test the definition against these cases: (a) two firms maintaining a price-fixing cartel; (b) a cleaner wrasse servicing a client fish; (c) a bird giving an alarm call that alerts its kin; (d) a predator and its prey locked in an evolutionary arms race; (e) a sterile worker honeybee; (f) a one-off purchase from a corner shop; (g) two strangers who push a stalled car off the road and never meet again; (h) mitochondria inside a eukaryotic cell; (i) a state signing a climate treaty while knowing it can defect later; and (j) vampire bats that regurgitate blood to roost-mates that failed to feed. For each case, apply the definition to the behaviour described, say whether the resulting classification matches ordinary usage, and indicate whether you would revise the definition or defend a surprising verdict.

Please format the entire response as a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "revisions_made": an array of strings, each a sentence describing a change made to the definition after testing it; may be empty.
- "surprising_verdicts_defended": an array of strings, each naming a case where the definition's verdict clashes with ordinary usage but you would keep it, with a one-sentence defence; may be empty.

- "bottom_line": a string giving the final definition in one sentence and stating its weakest point.
