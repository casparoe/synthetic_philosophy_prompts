For my AP European History class, I am designing a discussion handout that sorts students into an interpretive position on the French Revolution based on what they believe about the evidence, rather than on which school of interpretation they already prefer. Please write a decision tree, or a taxonomy formatted as a tree, built from the logical space of two questions.

The first question concerns the engine of the Revolution: whether the decisive driver was class conflict, the autonomous force of revolutionary ideology, or the contingent cascading of political and fiscal crises. The second question concerns whether the Revolution's turn toward terror and its destruction of its own early leaders was a structural tendency internal to the revolutionary process, or instead a contingent outcome of war, counterrevolution, and circumstance — whether revolutions devour their children.

Please build the tree from the logical space first: identify the possible combinations of answers to the two questions, then locate the actual interpretive positions in that space. Each branch node should be a question about concrete material a high school student could answer without already knowing the historiography — the cahiers de doléances, the Civil Constitution of the Clergy, the flight to Varennes, the September Massacres, and the levée en masse are all acceptable examples, but do not feel limited to them. Do not label the branch nodes with the names of schools of interpretation. The leaves should include the Marxist or class-struggle interpretation, the revisionist or ideology-centered interpretation, the contingency or political-crisis interpretation, and any stable hybrids, each placed according to its answers to both questions. For each leaf, give the position's name — or "unnamed" if no major historian occupies that cell — and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Indicate which combinations of answers are unoccupied or unsustainable, and briefly diagnose whether each is empty because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely underexplored. Finally, flag any pair of positions the tree cannot cleanly separate with a single diagnostic question. Aim for roughly six to eight leaves and no more than five branch nodes, since the handout needs to fit on one double-sided page.
