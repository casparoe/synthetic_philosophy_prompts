I'm making a revision hand-out for a second-year metaphysics unit on Boethius's answer to the foreknowledge problem. We're working from Book V of the Consolation of Philosophy, specifically the part where he distinguishes between conditional necessity and simple necessity using the example of a man walking: if you see him walking, his walking is necessary in one sense, but that doesn't make the walking itself necessary in the simple sense. That's my paraphrase of the passage, not a quote.

I want a decision tree that sorts students into a position on divine foreknowledge and free will. It should work from beliefs rather than labels, so someone can answer it without already knowing what open theism or Molinism are called. The splits I want you to use are roughly these: whether God is inside time or outside it; whether the real problem is the infallibility of the knowledge or the fixedness of the thing known; whether free will requires the ability to do otherwise given God's actual knowledge, or only the absence of external coercion; and whether an eternal act of knowing should count as a cause. Phrase each split as a concrete question about a running case: God knows from eternity that Imogen will lie to her supervisor on Thursday. For instance, ask whether Imogen can still not lie on Thursday while God knows she will, whether God's knowledge is more like remembering the past or watching her from above, whether the necessity at issue is more like the necessity of a theorem or of a past event, that sort of thing.

Boethius's own position should be one of the leaves, but don't make the tree built around him as the default. If some combination of answers has no occupant, say so and briefly say whether that's because the view is incoherent, too costly, or just unexplored. Each occupied leaf should get its usual name if it has one, and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Keep it to around seven leaves max, since it's for a tutorial. And please write the handout in British English.
