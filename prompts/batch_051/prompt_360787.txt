Here is a passage from Frege's preface to *Begriffsschrift* (1879):

"If the task of philosophy is to break the domination of words over the human mind [...], then my concept notation, being developed for these purposes, can be a useful instrument for philosophers [...] I believe the cause of logic has been advanced already by the invention of this concept notation."

Write a sonnet in which the octave presents the older, syllogistic way of treating a proposition as subject and predicate joined by a copula, and the volta—when it comes—turns on what Frege's concept notation forced logicians to see instead: the function–argument structure of predication and the need for quantifiers to bind variables. The closing couplet should state exactly what changed in what a sentence is, not merely what changed in the symbols used to write it. The poem should be accurate enough that a logician or historian of philosophy would recognize the point without wincing. Afterward, add a short prose note separating what is settled about Frege's replacement of term logic from what remains genuinely contested about how to interpret the philosophical significance of that replacement, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus.
