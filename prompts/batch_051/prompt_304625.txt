Start from the taxonomy of kinds of minds: Darwinian creatures adapt only across generations by natural selection; Skinnerian creatures add within-lifetime adaptation by trial and error, with reinforced behaviors surviving; Popperian creatures add the capacity to test candidate behaviors offline, so maladaptive hypotheses need not be acted out; Gregorian creatures add external mind-tools such as language, notation, and other symbols, through which one mind can import discoveries and designs from another. Now consider a counterfactual lineage of hominins that, three million years ago in East Africa, evolves the full Popperian capacity while remaining otherwise Australopithecus-grade and never acquires language, notation, or any other Gregorian mind-tool. Predict, with reasoning, what their material culture, foraging system, and social organization would look like after a million years. Your answer should identify the mechanisms that would have to do the work: how much cumulative culture is possible when each generation can learn from others only by imitation and its own offline hypothesis-testing; which later human technologies, such as controlled fire, composite tools, snares, watercraft, or exchange networks, would still plausibly arise and which would stall; what compensating adaptations natural selection would likely favor; and where the largest uncertainties sit. For each major prediction, distinguish what follows robustly from your assumptions from what is a fragile guess.
