I run design review for a consumer app with about ten million monthly users, and every week we decide whether to ship features such as autoplay, streaks, notification timing, defaults, and personalized recommendations. I want a reusable procedure—usable by designers with no philosophy background and runnable in under thirty minutes before the launch decision—that flags features as manipulative rather than merely persuasive. The working principle is this: a feature is manipulative when it intentionally exploits a cognitive or motivational weakness to steer users toward behavior they would not reflectively endorse; merely influencing users, or competing well for their attention, does not by itself make a feature manipulative. Turn that into a step-by-step decision procedure (a checklist or flowchart in words) with tests to run in order, and say explicitly which steps are genuine judgment calls rather than mechanical checks. Then apply the procedure to three cases: an infinite-scroll feed with no stopping cues, a subscription that auto-renews unless the user opts out, and notifications scheduled from a user's late-night activity patterns. Make sure at least one application returns a verdict my team will not like. End by stating what the procedure cannot decide.
