So I've been trying to design argumentation as an actual sport — rankings, seasons, maybe commentators — and I hit on a scoring idea I can't stop thinking about. I call it "shape scoring" as opposed to "point scoring," and I want to know if it's a real distinction or if I'm just renaming something obvious.

Here's the distinction as cleanly as I can put it. Point scoring is the obvious thing: you get a point when the other side concedes a premise, when you show their example doesn't cover the case, when they have to qualify what they said. It basically a ledger of local wins. Shape scoring is about what the argument looks like after your speech, the way in go you care about the shape of your stones and in soccer you care about field position even though neither one is the score. A move earns shape points if it leaves the overall question in a simpler, more tractable state for the next person — fewer live subquestions, sharper disagreement about one thing instead of murky disagreement about five.

My actual thesis, the thing I'd defend, is that shape should dominate points. A debater who wins ten little factual skirmishes but leaves the central question more confusing than they found it should lose to someone who concedes three skirmishes but leaves everyone with a much clearer sense of what would actually settle the issue. Call it "tractability value" if you like, the way a move can be worth more than the local points it won because of what it does to the position.

I can already hear the objection that this rewards big-picture rhetorical hand-waving over being right about the details, and that it games too easily — you could "clarify" a debate by arguing for a dumber, cleaner version of the question and collect shape points. But I keep thinking the alternative is worse: pure point scoring encourages people to churn out cheap local wins that add up to nothing, which is exactly what makes most debate feel pointless.

So my questions: is this distinction coherent, or am I smuggling a bunch of epistemic values into the word "simpler"? Has something like it already been proposed under a name I'd recognize? And honestly — if shape scoring were the dominant criterion, would that actually improve argumentation, or would it just select for a different kind of cheap trick?
