I have been trying to work out how much philosophical weight the experience machine can actually bear, and I have found myself stuck between two readings of the case. The set-up is familiar: a machine could give me a lifetime of experiences far richer than those I am likely to have — more pleasure, more felt achievement, more felt intimacy, more felt discovery — while my body lies in a tank, and I would not know the difference from the inside. Most people say they would not plug in. What is contested is what that refusal amounts to. My own credence is about 0.6 that the refusal is best explained by ordinary people correctly registering that a good life is not purely a matter of how things feel from the inside: that actually doing, actually loving, or actually being in contact with reality matters in itself. But I can tell a debunking story almost as well: the judgement may be an artefact of status quo bias, of an inability to keep the machine's guarantee fixed in imagination, or of a moral queasiness about artificiality that has no bearing on wellbeing.

I would like your own credence, not a survey of the debate. The proposition to assign a number to is this: the standard experience-machine judgement is better explained by the hypothesis that wellbeing has a non-experiential component than by debunking explanations — status quo bias, imaginative failure, or unease with artificiality — taking 'better explained' to mean that a rational observer should increase their confidence in the first hypothesis by more than they increase their confidence in the second, given the judgement. In decomposing your reasoning, please attach a rough probability to each of the following and show how they combine: (i) experientialism about wellbeing — the view that how a life goes for a person is fixed entirely by the character of that person's experiences — is false; (ii) conditional on experientialism being false, the experience-machine judgement is a reliable diagnostic of that fact rather than a coincidental echo; (iii) the anti-machine judgement survives a version of the case in which the machine supplies the felt texture of achievement, friendship, and autonomy, so that only brute contact with reality is missing; and (iv) whatever residual judgement survives is best housed in a theory of wellbeing rather than in a separate ideal of authenticity or self-authorship.

Then tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, what evidence or experimental variation would move it most, and whether you think my 0.6 is overconfident or underconfident.
