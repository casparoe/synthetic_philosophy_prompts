Recently I have been reading about the evolution of social behavior, and almost without meaning to I have acquired a standing rule for judging any claim that some trait, behavior, or institution is an adaptation. The rule is this: before I accept the claim, I try to restate it in the vocabulary of replicators and their vehicles. I ask which alleles are supposedly favored, in which bodies or lineages, and through what effect on their frequency in later generations. If I cannot produce at least a rough story of that kind, I downgrade the explanation to the status of a just-so story. And I now treat appeals to the good of the species, the health of the group, or the stability of the ecosystem as red flags rather than explanations, unless they can be shown to be downstream consequences of competition among genes.

I know this is close to the gene's-eye view I have absorbed from Dawkins and Williams, and I know that view has critics. The policy has genuinely helped me: it dissolved a recent conversation in which someone explained a piece of self-sacrificial behavior as existing for the species, because I could not see how a gene for it would outcompete an alternative that accepted the benefit without paying the cost. But I have also noticed that the rule is very easy to satisfy after the fact. When I try hard enough, I can usually tell some story about allele frequencies for nearly any human practice, which makes me worry that I am not filtering explanations but merely translating them into a language that flatters my prior commitments. I am also aware, in a general way, that group and multilevel selection are now defended more seriously than the earlier polemics suggested, and that a gene-level description can in some models be a different accounting of the same process rather than a rival explanation of it.

What I want to know is this. Is my replicator-first policy defensible as an epistemic habit, or have I turned a useful heuristic into a bias? What is the most defensible version of the rule, where should it be granted authority, and under what conditions would it lead me systematically astray? And how would I tell, from the inside, whether I am using it to analyze a claim or to shield myself from one?
