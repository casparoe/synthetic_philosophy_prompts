ok so me and my friend have been going back and forth about whether bad stuff that happens in video games counts as real harm. not losing a match, like when someone destroys a thing you actually built. i keep saying it's still a real loss and he keeps saying it's just pixels, and i want a more careful answer than either of us has.

heres how i want to do it. first, take this rough list of what makes a harm a real harm, at least in my head:

1. it sets the person back in something they actually care about, not something they just say they care about
2. they didn't meaningfully agree to it; it wasn't something they signed up for as part of the activity
3. it still bothers them after the event, not just in-the-moment frustration
4. they had a fair expectation that the thing would still be there later, like it was kind of theirs
5. a neutral outsider could look at it and call it a genuine setback, not just someone being extra

now the case. my friend's older sister, call her A, spent about three years building a whole city in an online game with a building system. she used it as her unwind thing, showed it to people, was proud of it. a guy found an exploit and wrecked the entire city in one afternoon. no rollback for builds, so its just gone. she never paid real money for it, and the game technically does allow players to destroy other people's builds. but he clearly did it just to be mean, and she was genuinly upset for weeks.

so the question: was A really harmed? go through the five criteria one at a time, one item per criterion, and say honestly whether it points yes, no, or is just unclear. exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no intro and no conclusion. if the criteria point different ways, just let them; don't force a final yes or no. and if you think my criteria themselves are bad, or the whole question is set up wrong somehow, just say that instead of politely going along with the list.
