For a graduate-seminar handout on promissory obligation, write a decision tree that sorts a reader into one of six named positions on why promises bind: the consent/authority-transfer view, social-practice conventionalism, the assurance view, a trust-relationship view, a virtue view, and a reductionist view. Build the tree from logical space first, not from the labels. Each internal node should be a yes/no claim about a concrete case, phrased so someone can answer without knowing the theories—for example: a promise made to someone who never learns of it and never relies on it; a promise to someone who dies before performance; a promise renewed in a community where the practice of promising has collapsed; a voluntary promise to do something wrong; a promise made only to oneself; a promise the promisee accepts but says they will place no weight on. The distinctions doing the sorting should be: whether the wrong is owed to the promisee or is free-standing; whether uptake, expectation, or reliance is necessary; whether the obligation depends on a standing social convention; and whether the promisor's voluntary act is the source of the obligation or only a precondition for something else to generate it. Each leaf should give the position's name and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Note any combination of answers no named position occupies, saying briefly whether that spot is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely underexplored. Flag any two positions the tree cannot separate. Supply no citations. Max 900 words.
