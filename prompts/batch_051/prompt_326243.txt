At work the other day, two engineers on my team got into a long argument after a postmortem, and they kept trying to pull me in to settle it even though I've never studied any of this. The incident was simple. During a migration a while back, Rob disabled a cron job that cleaned up old backups because it was interfering with the migration, and he left himself a ticket to re-enable it once the migration was done. The migration finished, the ticket sat there, and nobody re-enabled the job. Months later the disk filled up with stale backups, writes started failing, and we had an outage. In the postmortem someone wrote that the outage was caused by "the failure to re-enable the cleanup job," and that sentence set them off. Marcus said that's just right, and omission cause things all the time: if Rob had re-enabled the cron, the disk wouldn't have filled, and that but-for reasoning is exactly how we decide what caused an incident. Elena said no, an omission isn't a thing, it's the absence of a thing, so it can't sit in a causal chain; the disk filled because bytes accumulated, and those writes are what caused the outage, not Rob's non-action. Rob might be responsible, she said, but responsibility and causation are different questions, and postmortems talk causally because they're really about blame and prevention. Marcus came back with a lifeguard example: if a lifeguard watches someone drown and does nothing, we don't quibble about whether his inaction caused the death, we just say it did. Elena said that actually proves her point, because the lifeguard only seems like a cause because he has a special duty, so it's the duty doing the work, not the causation. Marcus said fine, but Rob also had a duty to re-enable a job he disabled, so her own point applies to Rob too. Elena said it doesn't collapse: the duty explains why Rob is to blame, not which physical events filled the disk; if you refuse to separate those, you'll end up saying every engineer who could of noticed the low-disk alert and stayed quiet also caused the outage, and then the word "caused" stops meaning anything useful. That's about where I stopped being able to follow, and honestly I couldn't tell you who was winning.
