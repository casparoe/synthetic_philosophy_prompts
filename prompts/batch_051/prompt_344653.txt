ok here's a homemade distinction i keep reaching for when i'm trying to explain why mutation trips people up, and i want an honest check on it before i keep using it. i call it name-time vs thing-time. a variable that points at a mutable object is actually two different lives running in parallel. the name's life is the list of objects the name has pointed at over time: rebinding, reassignment, whatever moved the arrow. the thing's life is the history of what has happened to the object itself: mutations, fields changed, items appended. my real claim isn't just that there are two timelines; it's that people can handle each timeline fine on its own, and the meltdown happens because natural language hands you one protagonist per story and everyone defaults to making the variable the protagonist. so when `y = x` and then `y.append(2)`, the sentence "x changed" is perfectly true in thing-time and perfectly false in name-time, and the student's confusion is exactly the failure to notice they've switched timelines mid-sentence. i also think this is why every diagramming trick only works for half the bugs: box-and-arrow pictures pick thing-time as real, name rebinding diagrams pick name-time as real, and a student who internalizes either one still breaks on the other class of bug.

so: (1) is this just the value-vs-reference / aliasing distinction with a cuter name, and if not, what's actually new about it? (2) is the one-protagonist thesis (that the hard part is the single-subject bottleneck, not the mere presence of two timelines) actually true, or is that a just-so story? and (3) give me the cleanest counterexample you can: a mutation bug where the confusion clearly is not name-time vs thing-time.
