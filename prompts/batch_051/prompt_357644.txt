Been going back and forth on the Kuhn–Popper question of whether science stays rational during a revolution. A colleague keeps insisting it does — that when a field changes paradigms (say phlogiston to oxygen, or Newton to relativity), scientists are still reasoning, just by standards that shift with the paradigm rather than by some timeless Popperian rulebook. I can't tell whether that's an empirical claim, a conceptual move, or just defining "rational" so it can't lose. How would you test it? Give me the strongest concrete design you can — historical comparison, simulation, natural experiment, whatever you'd bet on — and say what pattern of results would push toward "rational after all" and what would push toward "rationality is post hoc." Then be blunt about what even a decisive-looking result wouldn't settle, e.g. wether we're reading our own standards into the episodes when we code particular moves as rational. And keep clear the line between what's actually settled here and what's genuinely contested; if you're taking a side on a contested point, flag it rather than presenting it as the consensus.
