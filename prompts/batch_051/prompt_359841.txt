I'm a retired chemist and I've recently gotten into epistimology—specifically the debate over whether knowledge is closed under known entailment. The standard moves against closure that I've already got are: (1) Dretske's zebra—I can know the animal in the zebra pen is a zebra while failing to know it isn't a mule cleverly disguised to look like one; (2) the Nozick sensitivity point—I know I have hands, and that entails I'm not a handless brain in a vat, but I don't know I'm not envatted because if I were envatted I'd still believe I wasn't; (3) the lottery case—I know I can't afford a safari next year, which entails I won't win the lottery, but I don't know I won't win the lottery.

I'd like a fourth move against closure that isn't just one of these in a new costume—not a rewording, not a special case of the sensitivity point, not another deceptive zoo setup. If you can make it come from scientific measurement, I'd especially like that: I keep thinking about how I can know what an instrument reads while not knowing that the whole calibration chain isn't fooling me. Then be straight with me: is the new case actually any good, and how does it compare to the three I listed?
