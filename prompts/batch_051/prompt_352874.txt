I teach a third-year undergrad seminar on conditionals and probability, about 15 students, 80-minute sessions. They already know the probability calculus, conditional probability, and the Ramsey test idea, and I've floated Stalnaker's hypothesis as a live proposal: for all A and C, the probability of the indicative conditional "if A then C" equals Pr(C|A). What I want them to feel in this session is the force of Lewis's triviality result — that this tidy equation can't hold in general, because in a class of probability functions closed under conditioning it collapses Pr into something trivial (positive probability on at most two mutually exclusive outcomes). I'd like them to run into the collape themselves before anyone says "triviality."

So design me a runnable in-class game or activity. They could be bookies, run a small prediction market, or trade assets whose payoffs are keyed to conditionals — the format is up to you, as long as no real money is involved. The rules should be set up so that participants naturally use the equation Pr(if A then C) = Pr(C|A) to set prices, and then discover that updating on C or on not-C forces their prices into something absurd. (The clean way to see the trap: expand Pr(A→C) by the law of total probability over C and not-C, apply the equation to the two conditioned probability functions, and you get forced to Pr(A→C) = Pr(C); but the equation also says that's Pr(C|A), so you're stuck with Pr(C|A) = Pr(C), which can't coexist with three genuinely live alternatives.) The facilitator should not spell any of this out until the debrief.

Please include: (1) concrete rules, materials, and a points-based payoff scheme; (2) a timeline for 80 minutes; (3) a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto the pieces of the real result — the law of total probability, conditioning on C versus not-C, and the algebrac collapse; (4) what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it; and (5) an honest note on which feature of the actual theorem the activity cannot reproduce and the facilitator has to supply afterwards.
