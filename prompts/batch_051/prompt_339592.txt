I'm a science-and-ideas reporter, and I'm about to write a piece weighing the Confucian idea that care should be graded—strongest for family, thinner for strangers—against the Mohist idea that partiality is the root of most social ills and impartial care is what a decent society would run on. The problem is I've ended up with a bundle of commitments I'm not sure are consistent, and I'd rather find out before I file than after. The set, as best I can state it: (1) my duty to my own kids isnt just a convenient heuristic, it's genuinely weightier than my duty to a stranger; (2) Mozi is basically right that partiality—each family looking out only for its own—is what drives a lot of war, hoarding, and inequality, and that impartial concern would produce better outcomes; (3) a moral ideal ordinary people can't live out without constant self-condemnation is a bad ideal, not just an inconvenient one; (4) I'm not comfortable saying distance or difference lets me off the hook entirely—walking past a stranger who could be saved by small effort still seems wrong; (5) the familiar caricature of Mozi, that he demands literally equal love for everyone with no special place for your own, is probably a misreading. Which of these actually conflict with which? Rank the tensions by severity, say which ones dissolve on closer inspection, and tell me what the cheapest repair would cost.
