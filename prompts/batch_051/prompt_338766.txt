In my reading group this came up, and a friend shut down the argument by saying "the self is an illusion" — he used it to dismiss someone who was trying to seperate the claim that there's no little homunculus in charge from the much stronger claim that there's no one home to have an experience. To him the slogan just covered both and settled it. What are the distinct precise claims "the self is an illusion" could be making, from the nearly trivial to the genuinely radical, including something like the idea that the brain runs a self-model that's transparent in the way a clean window is — you see through it and mistake the seeing-through for there being no glass at all? For each reading, tell me whether it's a platitude, a defensible but contentious claim, or probably false, and say which one my friend's dismissal actually needed. Lay it out as a menu, because I want to put different pieces of it in different characters' mouths rather than settle on one position.
