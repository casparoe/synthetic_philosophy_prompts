I'm a novelist, and I've been stuck on a scene where a character argues that one person's account of an event is truer than another's even though it gets fewer facts right. I want to get comfortable with truthlikeness — the philosophers' sense of verisimilitude, not just literary believability — well enough that the argument in the scene works instead of sounding handwavy.

Construct a thought experiment to that specification: two false reports of one mundane event, such that a content-style measure and a likeness-style measure of truthlikeness come apart in the interesting direction. By content-style I mean: rank a report by the number of explicit true claims it makes minus the number of explicit false claims it makes (I know real content theorists aren't this crude; I want the crudeness examined, not ignored). By likeness-style I mean: rank a report by how similar the world it describes is to the actual world, where small peripheral errors count as less of a loss than a big central error. I need the content-style measure to rank Report B above Report A, while the likeness-style measure, along with ordinary judgment, ranks A above B.

Constraints: the incident is ordinary (an office, a kitchen, a commute), nothing exotic or science-fictional, and no contrived philosophical gimmicks. Both reports should read like honest but slightly mistaken recollections, not like lies or paranoid reconstructions. Each report should make the same number of explicit factual claims — pick whatever number you like, but no more than six apiece. The reversal should not come from B being padded with irrelevant true trivia or from A being better written; the real difference should be that B's error is central or large while A's errors are peripheral or small. Keep the scenario itself to a short paragraph before the check.

After you give the case, do the check. State the actual facts and the two reports in full. Tally the true and false claims for each and show that the content-style measure ranks B above A. Explain why the likeness-style measure ranks A above B, and name the single structural feature of the case that is doing the work. Then say what happens to the reversal if you remove that feature. Finish by explainng how a defender of the content-style measure would attack the case, and what concession a defender of the likeness-style measure ought to make. The scene turns on this, so I need the verdicts to be generated by the two measures rather than just stipulated — if the case cheats anywhere, tell me that too.
