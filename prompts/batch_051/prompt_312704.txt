I have been thinking about the proposal, floated in AI safety discussions, that two AI systems could be trained to debate a question before a human judge, and that a wrong or dishonest system would eventually be exposed because its false claims would fall apart under sustained questioning. What bothers me is the possibility of obfuscated arguments: a debater with a bad case could bury a small number of real errors inside a long, confident, technically dense presentation, and whenever one of those errors is challenged it could generate another plausible-sounding elaboration, leaving the judge unable to locate the flaw even though the flaw is there. Does the debate proposal survive once this possibility is taken seriously, or does obfuscation defeat the core argument? I want the reasoning made explicit: say which premise of the proposal is under pressure, what would have to be true about judges, arguments, and training for the original argument still to go through, and whether a repaired version--rewarding compressibility, limiting length, requiring checkable intermediate claims, or something similar--would recover the original argument's force or merely push the problem to a different level.
