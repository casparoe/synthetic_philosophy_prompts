I'm a cog sci PhD student and I keep coming back to one part of Huxley's essay On the Hypothesis that Animals are Automata, and its History. He says something like: consciousness in animals is related to the mechanism of their body as a collateral product of its working, with no more power to alter that working than a steam whistle has to alter a locomotive's machinery. I want to understand the most important implications of taking that specific claim seriously for the paradox of phenomenal judgment. If the whistle view is right even for human beings, then when I judge that my pain is painful or that I'm having a vivid red afterimage, the judgment itself is caused by the machinery, not by the pain or the afterimage. Does Huxley's picture imply that my introspective reports about phenomenal properties are unjustified, or that the difference between a true and a false phenomenal judgment is itself not caused by the phenomenal facts? What would that actually commit us to about the reliability of first-person reports about conciousness? I'd like the implications laid out as sharply as possible, including which common defenses of introspection stop being available.
