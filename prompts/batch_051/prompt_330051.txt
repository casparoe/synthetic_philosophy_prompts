Nyāya epistemology lists four pramāṇas, or ways of coming to know something: perception, inference, comparison, and testimony. For testimony (śabda) to count as a source of knowledge, the speaker has to be an āpta: someone who actually knows the fact and isn't trying to mislead you. So the rough criteria I want to use are: there's a speaker, they assert a proposition, the proposition is true, and the speaker knows it's true and intends to communicate it. Now take a language model that tells me the capital of Kyrgyzstan is Bishkek. The output is true and about as reliable as a well-informed person, so it seems to do the same epistemic work as testimony. But criterion four looks like it fails: the model doesn't know anything and has no intentions at all, its just predicting next tokens. Does the model's output qualify as śabda in the Nyāya sense?
