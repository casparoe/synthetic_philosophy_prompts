A friend and I spent most of last evening arguing about what a musical work is, and by the end we tentatively converged on four criteria. First, a work is repeatable: distinct performances or playback events can all be of one and the same work. Second, it is deliberately made: someone fixes at least some of its sonic features—pitch, rhythm, timbre, large-scale structure—in a medium from which the piece can be produced or reproduced. Third, it is normative for its instances: a given performance or reproduction can be judged faithful or unfaithful to it, correct or mistaken, complete or truncated. Fourth, it is not identical to any single physical object or event: the work remains when a particular score, master recording, or performance is lost.

Afterwards a case kept nagging at me. A composer builds a generative music system: a program containing several hundred short fragments she composed, together with rules for altering and sequencing them and stochastic parameters, which produces an endless, continuously varying stream of sound. Every run differs in detail, though the same procedures govern all of them. She titles the system, exhibits it as a piece, and also publishes one forty-minute rendering of it as an album track. The program can be copied, run, and rerun; a bug or a badly distorted speaker would make its output wrong in some intuitive sense; but there is no fixed sequence of notes that a correct run must produce.

Is the generative system one musical work, a generator of indefinitely many distinct works, or neither? Go through the four criteria honestly. Where the criteria underdetermine the verdict, say whether the problem lies in the criteria or in the case, and propose the smallest revision to the criteria that would settle it. I am especially interested in whether our four tests were unconsciously built for notated, performable works, and what a work that exists primarily as process and as recording should force us to change.

Begin your reply with your bottom-line verdict in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
