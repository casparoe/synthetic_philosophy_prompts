I'm calling it "drip preferences." The idea is that preferences aren't over states at all; they're over transitions between adjacent states. You don't compare where you are to where you'll end up. You compare the next step to not taking it, and since the next step is always tiny, it wins. So the self-torturer's path isn't a failure of transitivity; it's a category error. Transitivity is a property of a ranking of states, but an agent who moves through a space by local choices never has a ranking of states, so there's nothing to be intransitive.

This matters to me because if i'm right, then my day-to-day choices aren't irrational at each step, and no amount of "picture the whole month" will fix me, because that's asking me to use a kind of preference i don't have.
