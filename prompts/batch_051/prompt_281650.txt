I keep noticing a resemblance I can't decide about. The gradual-replacement argument for mind uploading goes: replace one neuron with a chip that's functionally equivalent, and you notice nothing; repeat for every neuron and, since no individual swap mattered, the person at the end is still you. A zero-downtime migration in software works the same way procedurally: you swap instances of a running service one by one behind a load balancer, no user sees a failure, yet engineers say the service has been replaced with a new build running on new processes — though they may also say the service is the same one. Is this a genuine structural identity, loose analogy, or confusion? I'd like to know what the person is supposed to correspond to if the analogy is real — the service contract or the running instances — and where the two cases come apart, not just whether the comparison sounds right.
