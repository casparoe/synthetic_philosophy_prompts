I'm a journalist on the science-and-ideas beat, mostly covering AI, and I'm a few weeks into a reported essay on whether "honesty" and "deception" mean anything stable when we're talking about large language models. My editor is fine with the philosophical turn as long as the piece lands for a curious non-specialist and doesn't turn into a glossary. The thing that's bugging me is that the whole debate seems to run on underdefined terms. People say an LLM "lied" when it confidently produced a false medical citation, say "that's not lying, it's just miscalibrated" about a wrong factual claim, and "that's misleading" about a customer-service bot that gives true answers but omits the cancellation fee — and then everyone argues past each other because they mean different things by deception. I don't want my article to be one more vibes-based tour of failure modes; I want it organized around whichever conceptual questions are actually doing the work.

So here's what I'd like from you. Treat the open questions in this area as a set I could spend an article on, and rank them. Candidates include (but don't feel stuck with my list): whether deception for AI should be defined behaviorally (by the predictable effect on a reasonable user's beliefs) or mentalistically (by something like the system's goals, representations, or perhaps "intent" where that's even applicable); whether honesty just is calibration about one's own outputs or whether a calibrated system can still mislead by ommission; whether intent-free systems can be blameworthy for deceptive effects at all; and whether the ordinary moral distinction between lying and misleading survives contact with systems that don't have a point of view to misrepresent. For the top two or three, tell me why they're upstream of the others — what downstream questions would settle or stop looking pressing if we settled them — and give me a working definition or distinction I can use in the piece without pretending the argument is closed. I care a lot about the ordering and the reasons for it, less about completeness. Oh, and if there's a question I didn't list that you think is more worth settling first, say so and defend it.
