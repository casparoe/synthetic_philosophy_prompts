I serve on the board of a private foundation that funds early-stage research on machine intelligence. A donor has offered an endowment that would roughly double our annual grantmaking, but the gift deed contains what our general counsel calls a pre-AGI bargain: we would amend our charter to provide that if the foundation ever funds, develops, or gains control of an artificial general intelligence, day-to-day discretion over the foundation's resources would pass to a distribution formula and an oversight committee fixed now, and the amendment could only be reversed by a supermajority of three successive boards. The donor's case is that once AGI exists, whoever controls it, or the system itself, will have overwhelming temptations and capabilities to rewrite the rules, so the only moment to make a credible commitment about post-AGI outcomes is now, while the parties and their interests are still ordinary. Several of my fellow board members reply that a board cannot legitimately handcuff future boards to terms they never accepted about a situation no one now understands, and that taking the money would effectively sell the foundation's future governance. I want to circulate a written opinion before our vote. Draft it for me: recommend accepting the gift, rejecting it, or accepting with modified conditions, and give the reasoning a deliberating board would need, including a serious treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Please write it as continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
