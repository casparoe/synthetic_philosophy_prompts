I started caring about this because I'm dating someone who cares a lot about food and I mostly don't, and we keep coming back to whether the pleasure of a perfect egg and the pleasure of finally understanding a hard proof have anything in common. My old view was the standard one: all pleasures share some felt quality, a warm glow or a hedonic tone. But when I actually pay attention to my own experience, I cannot find one common felt thing among the relief of a cramp stopping, the buzz of being praised, the pleasure of a back scratch, and the small nasty satisfaction of watching someone I dislike fumble a presentation. It's starting to look like pleasure is just a covering label for a bunch of different states we group together because they all matter to us in the same rough practical way.

Over the last few months I've been trying out a replacement I call the held-attention account. The idea: a pleasure is an experience whose continuation the person having it would, from inside the experience, want for its own sake — not by a later judgment, not for any further reason, but as part of the experience itself — and this wanting shows up phenomenologically as an absorptive pulling or a holding, not as an extra sweetness painted on top. So what unifies pleasures is not a shared feeling but a shared way the experience has a grip on you: it presents itself as not-yet-to-be-let-go-of. I think this also handles the fact that people sometimes later disavow a pleasure: in the moment the holding was there, afterward the person refuses to endorse it.

Tell me honestly whether this actually dissolves the heterogenity problem or just moves the lump in the rug over to the wanting. Reconstruct the strongest version before you attack it, say whether I've accidentally reinvented a view already on the table (and roughly what that view is for), and then give me the cases it handles worst — experiences that are clearly pleasant without any felt wanting-to-continue, and experiences that have the wanting but aren't pleasant. I'm not after a bibliography; I want to know whether this idea has a pulse and where it fails first.
