I keep noticing a structural parallel that I cannot decide about, and I would like someone who knows both sides to stress-test it. In gauge theories, the vector potential overdescribes the physical situation: two potentials related by a gauge transformation describe the same electromagnetic field, so the extra mathematical structure is usually called surplus, and only gauge-invariant quantities are taken to be physically real. This looks to me like the relation between a proposition and the sentences that express it. The sentence that snow is white, its counterpart in another language, and a flat-footed paraphrase all carry the same proposition; the proposition itself does not select any one vehicle, and the particular wording seems like surplus structure over and above what is communicated. But I worry I am only pattern-matching on the fact that both mappings are many-to-one. Is the proposition/sentence relation a genuine instance of gauge freedom and surplus structure, or does it come apart in precise ways? What would have to be true of meaning — for example, about whether anything is lost in translation, or whether a proposition can be separated from its expression at all — for the analogy to be exact, and what would the parallel predict that turns out false once you try to make it precise?
