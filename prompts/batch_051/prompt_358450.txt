You are a professional epistemologist working on pragmatic encroachment and epistemic contextualism. Begin by briefly stating the core commitments of classical invariantism, contextualism, and subject-sensitive invariantism, then think through the following variation on the standard stakes-shifting cases of the bank-open-Saturday kind.

In the standard cases, the subject's evidence is held fixed while the high or low stakes are known throughout. Now introduce a temporal split. On Friday evening, Ava checks the credit union's opening hours online and forms the true belief that it will be open on Saturday; in a low-stakes setting she tells her partner, 'I know it will be open tomorrow,' and the only cost of being wrong is a wasted walk. Overnight, through no action of hers, the situation changes: on Saturday morning she learns that if she fails to deposit a form that day she will incur a severe financial penalty. Her evidence about the opening hours is unchanged, the hours themselves are unchanged, and she has not rechecked.

Run each of the three positions on this variant. What does each say about the truth of her Friday knowledge ascription and about whether she knows on Saturday morning? Where does the stakes-shift do its work in each account: in whether the proposition counts as knowledge for her, in the standard set by the ascriber's context, or only in what it is conversationally proper to say? Does the variant yield a determinate verdict for every position, or does it expose an underdescribed timing parameter concerning when, relative to belief, action, and ascription, the subject's stakes must obtain? Finally, does this variant strengthen, weaken, or merely complicate the usual case for pragmatic encroachment, and why?

Write your answer in British English.
