I cover science and ideas, and here's a dispute that seems stuck in a way I can't quite name. The argument over whether quantum indeterminacy helps free will has been running for decades: one side says that if determinism is true, the past plus the laws fixed everything you'd ever do, so genuine freedom needs real alternative possibilities, and quantum randomness, if it shows up in the brain, gives you that elbow room. The other side says that's no help — a random neural event is not something you controlled, so swapping determination for chance just replaces being pushed by the past with being pushed by dice. Each side finds the other's point almost to obviously wrong to engage, and the debate never moves. I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this a verbal dispute about the word 'free', a substantive dispute about what control or authorship requires, two groups answering different questions (one about alternative possibilities, one about being the source of the action), or a sign the question is ill-posed? My hunch is the two sides never agreed on what it would take for an event to be 'up to you', but I want that pressure-tested. And on your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a distinction, an experiment, a formal result, a reframing — or is progress unlikely?
