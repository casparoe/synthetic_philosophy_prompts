I am a retired research chemist who has recently taken up philosophy, and I have been trying to think carefully through the apparent disagreement between Paul and James over faith and justification. I would be grateful for help with one short passage from the Epistle of James, at 2:18–19 in the King James Version:

Yea, a man may say, Thou hast faith, and I have works: shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works. Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble.

I can see that James is attacking the idea that a bare profession of faith is sufficient, but I am struggling with the logical structure of these two verses. Who is speaking in each clause of verse 18, and is the challenge to "shew me thy faith without thy works" a challenge James thinks his opponent cannot meet? Does the remark about the devils in verse 19 work as a reductio of a particular conception of faith, and if so, what exactly is that conception—mere assent to a true proposition? I would appreciate a close reading of the passage, and especially a statement of the notion of belief James must be presupposing if his argument is to succeed.
