I chair a three-member admissions committee for a graduate program. Our written procedures require us to vote separately on three findings for each applicant: that the applicant's academic preparation is adequate, that the proposed project is feasible within the degree timeline, and that the funding plan is realistic, with admission recommended whenever each of the three findings wins a majority and with no separate vote on the applicant as a whole. In our latest meeting, Piper voted yes on preparation and on feasibility but no on funding; Souza voted yes on feasibility and on funding but no on preparation; and Tan voted yes on funding and on preparation but no on feasibility. Each finding therefore passed two to one, so by our rules I must record a recommendation to admit. Yet I am confident that if I had put the single question, should we admit this applicant, each member would have voted no, because each believes one of the three necessary conditions has not been met. I am unsure what to do with the file in front of me, and I am also beginning to doubt the procedure itself. Is it right to record a recommendation to admit, procedurally and substantively? Is there any sense in which the committee has a unified judgment here, and could any voting procedure express that judgment without generating this kind of conflict? Please answer in continuous prose, as in a letter or email, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
