Hey, I'm a systems designer at a mid-sized game studio, and I've been put in charge of the karma system for an open-world RPG we're building. In our world, karma is real and observable: morally weighty actions leave "stains" or "merit" on a character, which change how NPCs react, what quests open up, even ambient dialogue. We want the system to implement a Buddhist-flavored theory of moral desert, not the usual "nice act = +karma" reputation meter. The writers keep having the same fights about hard cases, so I want a reusable procedure they can literally follow when they script a quest beat, instead of going by vibes.

Our working interpretation, which you should treat as the brief and can refine: karma is about the quality of the agent's will -- the intention, what they knew or should have known, and the act they actually chose. Outcomes count, but mainly as evidence about the intention and as a separate "aftermath" track; they don't make a deed good or bad on their own. So donating a fortune for status shouldn't generate merit, and a well-intentioned act that goes badly wrong shouldn't be scored like deliberate cruelty.

Write the procedure. The people using it are quest designers without philosophy backgrounds; they get 10-15 minutes per beat to assign karma, and they make dozens of these calls a week. The stakes are medium: players notice when the system feels inconsistent, and the narrative team will make you pay for it. The lead writer signs off on anything flagged as hard.

Please structure your answer exactly like this:

1. Core principle -- ~250 words: the portable version of the theory a designer can keep in their head.
2. Inputs to record first -- ~150 words: what a designer has to nail down about the character and action before scoring.
3. Step-by-step procedure -- ~400 words: the ordered questions or tests, with what counts as a sufficient answer at each step.
4. Escalation rules -- ~200 words: where the procedure stops and a human has to decide, and why, so we don't loose consistency across the team.
5. Worked examples -- ~300 words: run the procedure on three cases, at least one of which is not mentioned above. Make one of them a case where the answer the procedure gives is one our team won't like.
6. Limits -- ~100 words: what this procedure cannot settle.
