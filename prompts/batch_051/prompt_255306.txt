For an undergraduate philosophy of language handout, write a "Which theory of names do you actually hold?" sorting quiz. It should have eight to ten questions, each built around a concrete case or pair of sentences: substitution inside belief reports, an identity sentence like Hesperus/Phosphorus, a name with no bearer, a name introduced by a definite description that later turns out false of the person it was attached to, two speakers who associate the same name with different descriptions, a speaker who has forgotten the description through which they learned a name, and a name whose bearer changes through legal adoption or a similar convention. Design the questions so they separate positions along at least two independent dimensions: where the semantic content of a name is located (in the object, in an associated description, or in a rule of use), and how the reference of a name is fixed and transmitted across speakers and over time. Every question must be answerable by someone who knows none of the theoretical labels, and no question may ask directly what the meaning of a name is or which theory the student prefers. Include a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto at least these positions: Fregean descriptivism, Russellian descriptivism, Millianism, the causal-historical theory, and a predicative or metalinguistic view; the key should also say what to do with mixed answer patterns rather than forcing every respondent into a single position. End with a diagnostic note identifying which pairs of positions the quiz cannot cleanly separate, and for each such pair, say whether that is because the positions genuinely agree on the test cases, because their difference is mostly a dispute about the word "meaning" rather than about truth-values, or because a test case was under-described.
