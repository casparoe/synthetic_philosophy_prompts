Recently I have been in a situation where the same body of evidence seems to support the same factual claim in very different ways depending on what turns on it: I readily treat a statement like The valve is safe as knowledge when the only cost of being wrong is a five-minute delay, but I hesitate to call it knowledge when being wrong would mean shutting down a process for days. I want to understand whether that difference is best explained by a difference in what I genuinely know, by a shift in what the word knows requires across contexts, or simply by a fixed body of knowledge paired with a higher threshold for acting. Propose an empirical test of the claim that practical stakes can make a real difference to whether a subject knows a proposition. The test should distinguish a stakes-sensitive account of knowledge from a purely contextualist account of knowledge ascriptions and from an invariantist account on which only action or assertion thresholds change. Specify the design, say exactly what pattern of results would count as evidence for each hypothesis, and then be precise about what even a positive result would not settle about the underlying concept of knowledge.
