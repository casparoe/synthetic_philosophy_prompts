I am putting together a short piece for my school's computing and philosophy club, and I would like the centrepiece to be a sonnet about the Church-Turing thesis and the kind of claim it is. The octave should present the comfortable picture: the informal notion of an effective mechanical procedure has been captured exactly by the formal notion of a Turing machine, so the boundary of the computable looks fixed and certain. The volta should be the reader's realisation that the thesis cannot be proved like an ordinary theorem, because one of its terms is informal; its standing is better described as a careful adequacy claim, supported by the convergence of many independent formalisms and by the fact that nobody has produced a persuasive counterexample. The closing couplet should name that unusual epistemic standing without pretending the issue is settled. The sonnet should be accurate enough that someone who has worked through the Church-Turing thesis would recognise its epistemology rather than find it fudged, and good enough that a newcomer could use it as an introduction. Please write the poem in British English.
