I keep coming back to Francis Crick’s 1958 paper On Protein Synthesis, where he says, in effect, that the specificity of a piece of nucleic acid is expressed solely by the sequence of its bases, and that this sequence is a code for the amino acid sequence of a protein. I’m trying to figure out whether I can keep a set of commitments I have about this, and I want a consistency audit before I write my thesis chapter. My commitments: (a) DNA is a code in a literal, not merely metaphorical, sense because the cell transcribes and translates it. (b) Shannon information gives the right way to quantify what a gene carries: information is just correlation between a source and a receiver, and neither needs to mean anything. (c) The mapping from codon to amino acid is arbitrary in the way a symbol is arbitrary, since nothing about the bases dictates the amino acid. (d) Actual codes like Morse or English require a sender, a receiver, and a convention, none of which the cell has. (e) There is no fact of the matter about whether a sequence “misreads” the code except relative to fitness, and fitness is just selection, not meaning. Which of these are actually in tension, which only seem to be, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity, don’t just say everything is fine.
