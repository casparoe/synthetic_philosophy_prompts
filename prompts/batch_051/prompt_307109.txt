I am putting together a graduate seminar on illusionism about consciousness, and before we evaluate any particular theory I want a specification of what any adequate view in this family must do. By "this family" I mean views that treat phenomenal consciousness — the what-it-is-likeness of seeing red or feeling pain — not as a realm of intrinsic, private qualitative properties, but as something closer to a user interface: a simplified, representationally mediated presentation of the system to itself, which the system does not experience as a presentation. Please write the desiderata such a theory must satisfy, with the case or consideration behind each one. For each criterion, say what it is meant to capture: the contrast cases between conscious and unconscious processing that it must get right (masked priming, blindsight, change blindness), the dissociations it must accommodate without strain (pain asymbolia, depersonalization, lucid dreams), the fact that the illusion is systematically compelling rather than merely a false belief, the question whether the interface has a function and what it would be, and whatever else you judge belongs on the list. Mark which desiderata are non-negotiable for a theory of this kind and which reflect the commitments of particular camps — for example, the insistence that the interface be adaptive, or that introspection be treated as wholly error-theoretic.

I suspect some of these demands pull against one another. The sharpest tension, as I see it, is between the requirement that the view not simply deny the appearances — that it take seriously that there is something it is like — and the requirement that it explain why introspection is systematically misleading about the nature of those very appearances. Tell me which subsets of the desiderata, if any, cannot be jointly satisfied, and what that means for the shape of the space of viable views. A brief scorecard at the end — how illusionism proper, a higher-order thought account, and a global workspace version of the interface idea fare against your list — would be useful, but keep it short; the criteria and their tensions are the point.
