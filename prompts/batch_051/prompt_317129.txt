A transitional government emerging from a period of mass political violence is considering a truth-for-amnesty mechanism in which protection accrues by degrees rather than being granted once. Applicants — perpetrators of politically motivated crimes — register for a provisional stay of prosecution covering only the acts they have already described in enough detail for the commission's verification chamber to accept. Each subsequent substantive disclosure, if accepted, moves the applicant one rung up an immunity ladder: the set of protected acts widens, until at the top rung the applicant holds immunity from prosecution and civil suit for all disclosed politically motivated acts. Every disclosure is published immediately in redacted form, so later applicants can see what earlier applicants have said. And the ladder is revocable: if any later disclosure from anyone contradicts an accepted account, and the verification chamber finds the earlier account materially incomplete or misleading, the applicant loses all rungs, drops behind first-time applicants in the queue, and the file is reopened publicly.

Treat this as a mechanism, not a moral position, and walk through how it would actually behave. Who comes forward first, with how much, and why? Does real-time publication push the record toward the truth or toward a jointly defensible minimum of verifiable facts? How would applicants game the ladder — by disclosing peripheral atrocities to climb it while burying the worst conduct in a mass of detail, by waiting to tailor their accounts to what the commission already appears to know, or by coordinating narratives before entering? What does the clawback do to incentives: does it deter omission, or does it deter entry and produce minimal, grudging disclosure? What second-order effects fall on survivors, on the verification chamber itself (which may prefer to accept accounts to keep the process moving), and on post-conflict stability? Then say which of those failure modes could be fixed by changing the design, and which are intrinsic to conditioning amnesty on disclosure at all.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning; no preamble, and do not restate the question.
