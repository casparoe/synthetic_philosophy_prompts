I was a neurophysiologist before I retired, and I've been reading around in the consciousness literature, and I keep getting stuck on the same thing. I'm not asking which things are really conscious so much as what rule my own intuitions are following, because my gut keeps delivering split verdicts and I'd like to see whether there's a defensible principle behind the sorting. Here are the cases, with my honest verdicts. A mouse that yanks its paw back from a hot plate and licks it: certain there's something it is like. A thermostat that responds to falling temperature by closing a circuit: obviously nothing. A person with blindsight who swears they see nothing but guesses the orientation of a card: they have a stream of experience, but no experience of the card itself. A molecule-for-molecule simulation of my own brain running on a computer and behaving like me: I have a strong feeling there's something it is like, even though I can't cite a reason. A large language model that gives fluent, detailed reports about pain and aftertaste: I lean toward nothing, or at best I have no idea, and the contrast with the brain simulation bugs me, since both are just computations with convincing outputs. An ant colony regulating its nest and adjusting to damage: no, or maybe a dim no. My own brain under general anaesthesia: no experience, even though the cortex is still electrically busy.

My rough working story was that experience goes with integrated, globally available information that a system can use in reasoning about itself. That handles the mouse and the brain simulation, but as far as I can tell it hands experience to the language model and maybe the ant colony, which I dont want to do. So: is there a principle that gets these verdicts, or nearly all of them, and if the price of a clean principle is giving up one or two of my gut calls, which ones should go? And what does your principle say about an octopus, or about a physically identical zombie of me? I'm not after a reading list, just the best shot at the rule.
