Something's been nagging me at work and I can't make it precise, so let me lay out the practice first and then ask the question.

We train text-only models from scratch on next-token prediction over a huge crawl of books, code, forums, whatever. The model never sees an image from the world, never has a body, never gets feedback from acting — it's entire universe is symbols. Then we do RLHF (which, if you squint, is also just more text: the model sees which response a human preferred), and then we give it multiple choice exams, coding tasks, questions about physical scenes, and it does surprisingly well. Here's the inconsistency that bugs me: the same engineer who says "it's just statistical patterns, there's no meaning there" will ten minutes later get annoyed that the model "hallucinated," or ask it to explain a bug in a system it never ran and actually trust the answer.

So here's the question. What theory of meaning is this whole practice presupposing? Reconstruct it: what view of how symbols get their content makes text-only training plus treating outputs as assertions about the world coherent? Where does the practice force the commitment, and where is it compatable with several different views? Is there a version of meaning-as-just-relations-among-symbols that can handle how we actually use these systems, or is the practice internally inconsistent? And if we instead believed the older idea that a symbol only means anything because it's hooked up to perception, action, or something outside the symbol system, what concretely would have to change in how we train, evaluate, or deploy these models? I'm not after a reading list; I want to know what we're already committed to.
