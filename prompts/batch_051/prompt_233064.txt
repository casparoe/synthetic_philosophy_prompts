I have followed rationalist-adjacent blogs and forums for years, and recently one of them had a long thread about whether hedonism about well-being deserves another look. Someone had argued that a good life can be understood as the accumulation of states that feel good — a warm bath, a solved proof, relief after a finished chore, petty schadenfreude. A senior commenter ended the thread, or tried to, with a one-liner: "There is no such thing as pleasure — there are only pleasures." The work the line was doing was to dismiss hedonism in a single stroke: since these states share no common nature, any theory that makes "pleasure" a single currency of well-being is built on a mistake.

I recognize this as the heterogeneity problem compressed into a slogan, and I can gesture at the standard replies — sensory versus attitudinal pleasure, family resemblance, "liking" as the common core. What I want before I write a response is a clean menu of everything the line could be claiming, ordered from readings a hedonist can grant for free, through readings that force hedonism to reformulate, up to the readings that would actually justify dismissing the view. For each reading: state the precise thesis, say whether it is plausible or question-begging, and say whether it does the argumentative work the commenter needed. Finish by saying which reading the one-liner most plausibly intended and, on that reading, what the strongest hedonist reply is.
