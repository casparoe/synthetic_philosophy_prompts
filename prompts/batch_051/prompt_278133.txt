Two members of a municipal planning committee I sat on recently got into an argument I could not settle. We were comparing three flood-defense investments under three possible future storm regimes, and the scientists said they could not responsibly assign probabilities to the regimes. The payoffs, in agreed welfare units, were:

- Plan A: 100 in mild conditions, 0 in moderate, 0 in severe.
- Plan B: 10 in mild, 10 in moderate, 10 in severe.
- Plan C: 0 in mild, 40 in moderate, 40 in severe.

Their exchange went roughly as follows.

(1) The first argued for Plan B: with no probabilities, the best one can do is guarantee the worst case, and B's worst case is 10 while A's and C's are 0. Choosing A gambles with constituents' safety.

(2) The second said that fixates on a single cell per row. Under genuine ignorance, one should minimize how much one would regret not having taken the best option in whichever state occurs. Maximum regret is 40 for A, 90 for B, and 100 for C, so A minimizes maximum regret.

(3) The first replied that regret is measured against a counterfactual, and a counterfactual cannot make anyone worse off. In moderate conditions under Plan B, people would have 10; the foregone 30 relative to Plan C is not something anyone suffers. If we know nothing, the floor is the only thing we know.

(4) The second answered that if we truly know nothing, the states should be treated symmetrically, which gives A the highest expected value; this is the Harsanyi-style objection to Rawls's maximin. Concentrating on the worst case is no less a substantive assumption than assigning equal probabilities.

(5) The first said the Harsanyi argument belongs to the original position, where the uncertainty is about which social position you will occupy; here the uncertainty is about weather, and equal weighting is invented precision.

(6) The second replied that maximin is equally invented: it privileges the worst cell of each row simply because that cell has the smallest number. A rule that always buys the highest floor would systematically starve high-ceiling projects.

Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: identify what is right and what is wrong or overreaching in each move, then say who is more right overall, and what, if anything, both parties are missing.
