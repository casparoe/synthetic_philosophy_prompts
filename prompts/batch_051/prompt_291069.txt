I am a high school student writing a long paper for a philosophy and technology course on what privacy is and why it matters. Before I read any more theories, I want the specification they will be tested against rather than an argument built from one or two cases. Please write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of the nature and value of privacy must deliver, if it is to serve as a basis for moral and legal judgment about information concerning a person, online and offline. For each requirement, give the case or reason that makes it a requirement, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable for any serious theory and which reflect a particular camp's preference and could be dropped.

Make sure the list addresses at least these pressure points, and add any others a full list needs:
(1) the uncontroversial core — a locked diary read without consent, and a hidden camera in a bedroom;
(2) being photographed or recorded from a public vantage point while doing nothing unlawful;
(3) a company that never accesses anything conventionally private but infers something sensitive — political views, a medical condition, financial distress — from ordinary browsing or purchase records;
(4) someone who openly posts intimate details online and is then startled when strangers use them — has that person lost all claim to privacy, or only to secrecy?
(5) a violation in which no information is ever acquired: say, a camera installed in a changing room that is discovered but never turned on. For each case, state what an adequate theory would have to say about it to remain credible.

Also list the distinctions a theory cannot collapse — secrecy, anonymity, solitude, data protection, and autonomy are candidates, but tell me which collapses are most damaging and which distinctions matter least. Take a position on whether privacy must have value beyond preventing concrete downstream harms, or whether a purely instrumental account could satisfy the list. Where your list implicitly assumes something about how people actually experience privacy — for example, that being watched changes behavior, or that people can value privacy while routinely disclosing personal information — state that assumption explicitly and flag the one you are least sure about.

Then do the part I most want to see: identify any pairs of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say what the tension is. After that, say which single requirement rules out the largest number of otherwise natural theories, and briefly score against the list the two most intuitive pictures — privacy as control over one's information, and privacy as concealment. Be explicit about how confident you are in the list as a whole, and which one requirement you are least sure is a genuine desideratum. End with the single most exclusionary requirement written on its own line as \answer{...}.
