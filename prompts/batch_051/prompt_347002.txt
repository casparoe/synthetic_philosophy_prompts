I am a features writer at a magazine that covers science and ideas, and I am preparing a long interview with a video-game critic whose judgements over the years have been so consistent that I suspect she is applying a single aesthetic criterion without ever having stated it. The broader question in the field is whether a game's claim to be art lies in what is authored for the player to interpret, or in the particular shape of agency that the rules make available. Her published record can be read as evidence for either side. She has called art: Journey, a wordless desert crossing in which the only real interaction is choosing whether to stay with a stranger; Shadow of the Colossus, in which the player is sent to kill one vast and largely passive creature after another, and the killing is something the player performs rather than merely watches; Papers, Please, a border-post simulation in which the player decides whom to admit and whom to turn away; and Gone Home, in which the player explores an empty house and reconstructs a family's story from the objects left behind. She has declined to call art: Tetris, chess, the annual FIFA football simulation, and the battle-royale game Fortnite, which she treats as a contest of skill with cosmetic dressing.

She has never reviewed Dear Esther, a short work in which the player's entire activity is walking across a Hebridean island while a narrator reads fragments of a letter; there is no puzzle, no goal, and no choice that changes the story. I want to push her on this case in the interview and to know in advance what her position commits her to. Predict her verdict on Dear Esther, and lay out the strongest principles consistent with everything she has already written. At least three seem to fit the record: first, a game is art when it presents an authored world or story for the player to interpret; second, a game is art when the agency it designs is itself the thing being expressed, so that winning or optimising cannot count; third, a game is art simply when it is not primarily a test of skill, which only excludes the competitive and the score-based. These principles agree on the earlier cases but pull apart on the new one, and on others. Give the prediction, say which principle her record most supports and why, and identify the further case, real or hypothetical, that would settle which principle she is actually running, including what that case would need to contain and lack. Please answer in British English.
