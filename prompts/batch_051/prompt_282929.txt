You are a philosopher of science with expertise in Bayesian confirmation theory and the epistemology of high-energy physics. I am preparing a position paper on non-empirical theory assessment in string theory and multiverse cosmology, and before I defend my views I would like you to audit my current package of commitments for internal consistency. Treat this as a diagnostic exercise rather than an occasion to expound the literature or supply reading.

Here is the package of views I currently hold.

1. Bayesianism about confirmation. All rational changes in confidence in a scientific theory are in principle representable as Bayesian updating: there is no route to rational credence that cannot be modeled as a prior probability combined with conditioning on evidence.

2. High credence in string theory on largely non-empirical grounds. I assign string theory, as a framework for quantum gravity, a current rational credence above one half. My main reason is the no-alternatives argument: after decades of sustained search, the physics community has found no viable rival framework, and I take this absence of alternatives to be rational evidence for string theory even though it has so far produced no confirmed novel empirical predictions.

3. Novel-prediction privilege. A surprising observable consequence that is later confirmed, as general relativity did with the bending of starlight, confirms a theory more strongly than post hoc accommodation of data the theory was designed to fit. I am inclined to say that no theory deserves credence above one half without at least one such confirmed novel prediction.

4. The multiverse is unconfirmable in principle. Any observation is necessarily made from within one universe, so no observation could discriminate between a multiverse and a single-universe theory that treats the observed constants as brute initial conditions. I therefore place multiverse hypotheses outside the scope of empirical science.

5. Qualitative underdetermination. For any theory consistent with current evidence there is at least one empirically equivalent rival, so empirical success can rule out many theories but can never single out a unique true theory.

6. Explanatory coherence without prediction still carries some confirmatory weight. I accept the unexpected-explanatory-coherence consideration associated with Richard Dawid's program of non-empirical theory assessment: string theory's apparently unforced ability to account for black hole entropy through microstate counting, and the web of dualities connecting its formulations, count in its favor even though these are post hoc explanations of already known phenomena rather than risky novel predictions.

Please begin by brainstorming the possible conflicts among these six commitments without yet deciding which conflicts are real. Then examine the most plausible tensions one by one. For each, determine whether it is genuine or whether it dissolves once the relevant terms—evidence, confirmation, testability, alternative—are made more precise; where a tension dissolves, name the distinction that removes it. For the genuine tensions, rank them by severity, say which commitment or commitments would have to be revised to remove each one, and identify the least costly revision. Finally, tell me whether there is a coherent position in the neighborhood of this package, and which single revision to my stated views would do the most to make the whole set defensible.
