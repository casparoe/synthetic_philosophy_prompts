You're a political theorist who works on institutional design, not a generalist opinion-giver, so I want you to actually work through the mechanism. Take your time, start by laying out who stands to gain and lose, and don't reach for a verdict until you've traced the incentives.

I keep coming back to this bit of Aristotle, from the Politics, Book IV, 1294b (the Perseus text):

[quote]
I mean, for example, that it is thought to be democratic for the offices to be assigned by lot, for them to be elected oligarchic, and democratic for them not to have a property-qualification, oligarchic to have one
[/quote]

The lot = democratic / election = oligarchic line is usually treated as a historical curiosity, but I want you to use it as a live test for a modern proposal. Suppose a country replaces its lower house with a 300-seat chamber filled by random lot from all adult citizens. Members serve a single three-year term; anyone who has ever held elected office, been an officer of a political party, or worked as a political staffer in the previous ten years is ineligible. They're paid the median wage and barred from running for elected office for ten years after their term. An elected upper house keeps it's veto, except that the lottery chamber can override it by repassing the same bill after a twelve-month cooling-off period with a two-thirds majority. The civil service drafts all bills; lottery members can only vote sections up or down, not rewrite wording.

Treat this as a mechanism, not a cause: walk through how it would actually behave. What does it incentivize among the citizen members, among the parties and the remaining elected politicians, and among the civil servants? Where does careerism go once you've banned it from the lower house, and where does the system get gamed first? Then bring in Aristotle's distinction: does the lot genuinely keep the arrangement democratic, or do the oligarchic elements just migrate into bill-drafting, agenda control, and the upper house? Finish with one or two failure modes you think could be fixed without gutting the sortition, and one that probably can't.
