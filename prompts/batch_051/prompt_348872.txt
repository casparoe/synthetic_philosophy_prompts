I run a small fiction-writing workshop — twelve people, mostly first-novel people and a few short-story writers — and we meet for 90 minutes. Lately I've been chewing on Hofstadter's old claim that creativity is mostly knob-twiddling: you start from a pattern or theme you already have, and the craft is knowing which conceptual knobs to turn, and how far, until the result feels both genuinely new and somehow inevitable, rather than bolt-from-the-blue originality. I want my writers to feel that in they're fingers instead of hearing me lecture about it.

Design a 90-minute workshop activity for about 12 people where they generate many variations on a single small narrative seed and only afterwards realize they've been working exactly the way the knob-twiddling model describes. Ideally they should start from the same mundane scene — something like a woman in a bus station being mistaken for someone's mother — and each round should make them turn a different knob (point of view, level of detail, moral valence of the mistake, size of consequences, time pressure, whatever you think works), producing variations that actually diverge. They should come away able to say, concretely, what a knob is in fiction and why constrained variation felt more productive than sitting around waiting for a great idea. No lecture up front; Hofstadter and knob-twiddling should only appear in the debrief.

Give me the result as a JSON object with these fields: activity_title (string), materials (array of strings), setup (string — room arrangement and how to frame the task without leaking the name of the idea), rounds (array of objects, each with round_name, knob_to_turn, instructions, time_limit_minutes, and what_they_hand_back_and_how_its_judged), debrief (array of objects, each with moment_in_the_room, part_of_the_model_it_maps_to, and question_to_ask), likely_failures (array of objects, each with symptom, likely_cause, and fix), and what_the_activity_cannot_reproduce (string — the part of the idea I'll have to supply myself).
