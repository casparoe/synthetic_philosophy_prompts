I have been reading David Hume’s essay Of National Characters, not as part of any course but on my own, and a passage in it has left me confused. As I understand him, he argues that the differences in manners and temper among different peoples come almost entirely from moral causes—laws, customs, government, and similar conditions—while physical causes such as climate and air matter very little. But later in the same essay he appears to grant that physical causes do produce observable differences in the bodily constitution of nations, and he even says something similar holds for some animals when they are transplanted to new climates. If physical causes can shape bodies, and if the mind is closely connected to the body, why is it not a contradiction for him to deny that physical causes shape national character? Is his distinction between moral and physical causes really stable enough to carry his argument, or is he quietly reintroducing physical explanation through the back door?
