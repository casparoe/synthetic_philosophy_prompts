Two years ago my sister received a diagnosis that was revised three times on the strength of tests that never wholly settled the matter, and I watched otherwise careful people around me treat each new finding as though it simply established something. Since then, in matters of my own health and my family's, I have followed a policy: whenever a test or an expert's hedged remark changes how likely I take some proposition to be without making me certain of any new fact, I update in the manner of Jeffrey conditionalization, redistributing probability across the serious possibilities rather than conditioning on an evidence claim I do not fully accept. But the redistribution requires new probabilities, and I have no method for choosing them except intuition. Is this policy a defensible way to stay honest about uncertain evidence, or am I simply giving arbitrary shifts between beliefs a formal appearance? How could I tell the difference from the inside, and is there a version of the policy that keeps the honesty without the arbitrariness?
