I'm a script editor at a public history podcast that does narrative seasons, and we're mid-production on one about the early Cold War. Every draft that comes across my desk has a sentence like "Stalin started the Cold War," "Truman started it at Potsdam," "the Marshall Plan made it inevitable," or "nobody started it, it was structural." I need a reusable procedure my editors can run on any sentence that assigns responsibility for the Cold War's origins, before we record, to decide whether it's defensible, needs hedging, or should be cut. We make these calls maybe twice a week during production, we get an afternoon per script, and the final call is signed off by a senior producer who is not a historian and gets annoyed by wishy-washy answers, so the procedure has to output a verdict plus a suggested rewrite, not a seminar.

Ground it in whatever historians and philosophers of history actually know about causal and moral responsibility in big messy events — counterfactual tests, the difference between a trigger and a cause, overdetermination, structural vs agential explanation, whether blame is even the right category — but translate all of that into questions an editor without the theory can run. I want a checklist or decision tree in words: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with "cut this" or "rewrite this," and where it has to hand the decision to a person because its a values or framing call rather than a fact question. Flag the cases it cant settle. And make sure it includes a gate near the top for deciding when a draft should drop the "who started it" framing entirely.

Then run the procedure on three sentences from actual drafts:

1. "Truman started the Cold War by dropping the atomic bombs."
2. "Stalin started the Cold War by imposing communist regimes on Eastern Europe."
3. "The Cold War wasn't started by anyone; it was overdetermined by the structure of the postwar world."

Make at least one of the three come out with a verdict the senior producer will hate, and say why the procedure gives it anyway. Then tell me where the procedure would silently break — what kinds of claims it would greenlight that a careful historian would still wince at.
