I have been defending something I call the 'principle of costless transparency', and I should like it assessed honestly rather than kindly. The weak form is, I take it, Good's theorem: for an expected-utility maximiser who will update by conditionalisation, the opportunity to observe costless evidence before choosing can never lower the expected utility of the subsequent decision; looking is always at least as good, in expectation, as not looking. My principle is the strong form. It holds that an ideal rational agent faced with such evidence is not merely permitted but rationally required to look, and that any preference against looking is a defect that must be redescribed before it can be taken seriously.

I also make a more ambitious claim. I claim there is no terminal preference for ignorance as such. The person who avoids a genetic test to preserve hope, the spouse who does not want to learn how little they are loved, the manager who resists an audit of a favoured subordinate, the parent who declines to learn the sex of a baby to keep the surprise — all of these, I maintain, are not valuing ignorance itself. They are valuing the pleasant state, the relationship, the confidence, or the surprise, and the information is costly precisely because it destroys those goods. So their refusal is a cost-of-acquisition case, not a counterexample to the strong form. Ignorance, unlike food or shelter, is not an object of ultimate preference; it is only ever a state maintained for the sake of some further good.

That may sound too neat, and I want to know where the neatness is doing the real work. Give me an honest verdict. In particular: is the step from the weak theorem to the strong requirement valid, or does it confuse 'never worse' with 'mandatory'? Are there decision-theoretic or psychological cases in which the preference for not knowing is genuinely final and cannot be reduced to a disguised cost? Do such cases refute only the strong form, or do some of them also threaten the weak theorem? And tell me what existing position or principle I am unwittingly approximating, if any.
