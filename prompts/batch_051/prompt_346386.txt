Locke's labour theory of acquisition is built from examples of rivalrous material goods — acorns, land, ore — and his proviso, that appropriation from a common stock must leave others enough and as good, gets its force from the fact that taking some of a finite stock leaves less or worse behind. Suppose, however, that the resources being appropriated are non-rival: code, data, algorithms, trained model weights, or genetic sequences, where one person's claim does not diminish what remains but does allow exclusion from something that could have been used by everyone at once. Does the Lockean argument survive this changed assumption? Identify which premise — in the labour-mixing argument or in the proviso — is put under pressure, and say whether the right response is to abandon the argument, restrict its scope to rivalrous resources, or repair the proviso so that it constrains the appropriation of non-rival goods. If you hold that no repair is needed, show why the Lockean case for excluding someone from an idea is as strong as the Lockean case for enclosing a field. Answer in British English.
