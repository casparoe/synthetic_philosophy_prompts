I'm putting together a dataset of writing prompts and this one's for a political-philosophy-adjacent slot. I'd like a satirical piece in the form of a corporate compliance FAQ — maybe from a fictional multinational's "Global Anti-Bribery and Gifts Policy: Frequently Asked Questions" — that deadpans its way into showing how unstable the line between a gift and a bribe gets once you're outside a setting where all relationships are supposed to be impersonal, arm's-length transactions. Make the voice exactly the bland, legalistic HR-compliance tone, and keep it legible: a reader should be able to tell what's being ironized without any winking.

The FAQ should cover, as numbered entries: a field manager in a country where hiring one's cousin isn't nepotism but a standing social obligation; a lavish client dinner that also creates a reciprocal debt; a donation to a local politician's constituency project; and the blanket rule that any gift over $50 is presumptively a bribe. The jokes should come from getting the philosophy right — the reciprocity and obligation in gift-giving, clientelism as a form of social insurance and mutual expectation rather than simple quid pro quo, and the pretense that Western anti-corruption standards are culturally neutral rather than one particular way of drawing the boundary. Someone whose read around the anthropology of corruption, clientelism, and gift economies should laugh in recognition, not cringe; someone who hasn't should still find it funny and faintly unsettling. Around 650 words, hard cap 700, formatted as a short preamble plus numbered FAQ entries. Don't state the thesis outright anywhere — let the policy's own rationalizations do the work.
