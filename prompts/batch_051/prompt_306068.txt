Design a one-off choice problem in infinite ethics: a decisionmaker must choose between two policies, A and B, each of which assigns an integer utility level to every generation t = 0, 1, 2, ... in a countably infinite sequence of future generations. The two utility streams should be bounded and eventually constant, neither stream should dominate the other generation by generation, and under ordinary summation each stream should total to positive infinity, so that unadorned summing cannot rank them. Do not stipulate the verdicts; they should fall out of the arithmetic.

Engineer the case so that the following two explicit criteria disagree:

- Overtaking criterion: A is chosen over B iff there is some T₀ such that for every T ≥ T₀, the cumulative difference Σ_{t=0}^{T} (u_A(t) − u_B(t)) is strictly positive.
- Exponential discounting at rate δ = 1/2: A is chosen over B iff Σ_{t=0}^{∞} (1/2)^t u_A(t) > Σ_{t=0}^{∞} (1/2)^t u_B(t), with both sums required to converge.

The case is meant to isolate how back-loaded permanent gains interact with front-loaded costs under different degrees of patience, without any hyperreals, ultrafilters, or other exotic machinery.

Give the two utility streams, then show that the overtaking criterion strictly prefers A while discounting strictly prefers B, with the computations written out so they can be checked by hand. After that: (a) identify the structural feature doing the work; (b) say whether removing or shortening the back-loading makes the two criteria agree, and in which direction; and (c) give the strongest objection a defender of the discounted rule would raise against the overtaking verdict, followed by the strongest reply from the overtaking side, keeping both sides honest.
