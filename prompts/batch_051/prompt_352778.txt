I am about to work through the main contending answers to the boundary problem: how the demos — the set of persons with a genuine claim to participate in, or be represented in, a given democratic decision — is to be constituted, and what justifies drawing its edges where they are drawn. Before I read those answers, I want the standard of assessment fixed. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of the demos must meet.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a genuine requirement rather than a preference of one camp. At minimum, the list will have to take a position on the following pressures. First, the constituting regress: deciding who is entitled to decide seems itself to require a decision procedure, which in turn seems to presuppose a demos — so a theory must say where the regress stops or why it is illusory. Second, the distinction between being affected by a decision and being subjected to a legal or coercive order, and whether only one of these relations can ground membership. Third, the cases that embarrass any simple binary rule: long-term residents who cannot vote, tourists, children, future persons, and adults with severe cognitive disabilities. Fourth, the fact that actual demoi appear issue-relative — a city's demos for a zoning question differs from a national demos for a treaty — while membership must not be redrawn ad hoc to secure a preferred result. Fifth, the difference between being included and being weighted: a theory may include all affected persons yet still owe an account of whether and why some should count for more.

State which of these desiderata are non-negotiable constraints and which are contested commitments only some theories will accept. Flag any subset of the list that cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify the single requirement that does the most to eliminate the standard candidate views. Conclude with a brief scorecard of the leading families — all-affected, all-subjected, and social-membership accounts — against the list; keep the scorecard subordinate to the criteria, since the criteria are what I actually want to own before assessing the theories.
