I'm a PhD student in a group that does AI safety, and I've watched the same argument stall out for about two years now. The two sides, roughly: on one side, the control crowd says that since we can't count on a model being aligned, safety should look like treating a deployed or soon-to-be-deployed model as an adversary you have to contain — monitor its outputs, sandbox it, run honeypots to see if it tries to defect, put in tripwires, limit its capabilities or the information it can get, that sort of thing. On the other side, the alignment crowd says that's a patch, not a fix: once the system is smarter than the overseers, any control scheme is just a thing it is trying to get around, and the only real safety is making the model not want to defect in the first place (through scalable oversight, corrigibility, value learning, whatever).

What's weird to me is that both sides mostly agree on the nearby facts — jailbreaks happen, evals get gamed, models can sandbag, current RLHF doesn't make a system robustly honest — and yet neither side moves an inch. I have a half-baked hypothesis that the real disagreement is about which "adversarial game" we're even playing: the control people are playing against a capable-but-bounded system that mostly does what it's asked, and the alignment people are playing against a superintelligent planner that is already gaming the measurements, the overseers, and the deployment pipeline. So it's not really one disagreement; it's two different threat models wearing one trenchcoat.

But I keep flip-flopping, and I want a proper diagnosis of the disagreement itself, not a verdict for one side. Is this mostly an empirical bet about whether a model much smarter than its overseer can be contained, a difference in threat models, a verbal squabble over the word "safety," a difference in what counts as evidence, or something more like different starting intuitions about what optimization by gradient descent gives you? And under your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a distinction, a concrete experiment, a formal result, a reframing? Dont give me "both sides have a point"; I want you to locate the disagreement.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating the question.
