I'm writing a novel set in a small ex-mining town in the Midlands, twenty years after the colliery, the chapel and the working mens club have all shut, and I keep realising I dont actually know what it is my characters have lost. I don't want to just write a haze of nostalgia for something I can't specify, so I want the philosophical spec first. If I want a theory — call it an account of the good of belonging, or of community as a good — what does it have to get right? Give me the desiderata, with the case or consideration behind each. It has to handle at least: a person who stays in a community that treats them badly; a community that survives while every single member slowly leaves and is replaced; someone who feels belonging toward a group that doesn't regard them as a member; the difference between losing a community and losing the sum of the friendships you had in it, including the bit that is just the mutual knowledge that each of you will keep showing up; and the fact that some of what people miss about a dissolved community is real and some of it is confabulated after the event. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are only my novelist's preferences, and if some pair of requirements can't be jointly met, say so — that tension is honestly the part I'd most like to see. Then briefly score the usual ways of talking about this against the list (communitarian, social-capital, plain individualist, materialist), and tell me which single requirement does the most to separate the serious accounts from the flattering ones. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases, not the scorecard; I'll do the scorecard myself.
