I have been defending a version of the thesis that there can be no laws of history in the strong, predictive sense: no lawlike generalizations that let a social science predict the future course of human affairs the way physics predicts the path of a projectile. I want to put the argument I find most compelling into numbered form and determine whether it actually goes through. Here is my reconstruction.

(1) If there were laws of history in the strong sense, then from a description of past and present conditions they would license predictions about major future historical developments.
(2) A great many major future historical developments will be brought about, in significant part, by future advances in scientific and technical knowledge: new energy sources, new ways of preventing disease, new information-processing technologies.
(3) Therefore a would-be predictive historical science would have to predict the major future advances in knowledge themselves, not merely their downstream consequences.
(4) To predict a future advance in knowledge, one must be able to state now the content of what will be discovered. For example, to predict the future energy source, one must state now the mechanism by which it works.
(5) But if one can state that content now, then the discovery has already been made; predicting the content of a future discovery collapses into making the discovery now.
(6) Hence major future advances in knowledge cannot be predicted in the sense required by a predictive historical science.
(7) Therefore no predictive historical science is possible, and there are no laws of history in the strong predictive sense.

I am half convinced by this, but I also suspect that somewhere in steps (3) through (5) there is a slide between two senses of predicting future knowledge, and I cannot make the diagnosis precise. Is the argument valid? If not, specify the exact step that fails, distinguish the reading of any ambiguous phrase under which it fails from any reading under which it survives, and say whether the argument still reaches its conclusion on either reading.
