I'm putting together a longread piece on whether beauty is objective, and I've been moderating an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers I'll call the Humean sentimentalist and the aesthetic realist. The Humean's line is basically that beauty isn't a property of objects but a pleasure felt by properly situated perceivers, yet there's still a real standard of taste because some people—Hume's "true judges," with delicate sentiment, practice, comparison, and freedom from prejudice—are more reliable than others. The realist, by contrast, thinks beauty is a genuine feature of the world, not just a response, and that ideal critics are better detectors of something already there. They've been arguing for months and keep talking past each other, so I'd like you to draft the document they should produce together. I want the numbered agreed-claims section first, then a disagreements table where each position is phrased in wording its own side would accept, then the cruxes. For each crux, give a concrete case or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it, and say what each side would predict. One likely crux is whether convergence among ideal critics under ideal conditions is better explained as discovery or as a shared sensibility taking shape. Another is whether information about provenance—say, finding out a admired painting is a forgery—changes the work's beauty itself or just changes the viewer's response. Another is whether there's a fact of the matter about beauty that a sufficiently good critic could in principle get wrong but would ever admit she got wrong in the past, or whether ideal critics form the standard they also detect. Please don't settle it; the deliverable is the document they could both sign, with the remaining disagreement made exact. American English, and a typo or two is fine.
