I teach a postgraduate seminar in philosophical theology; twelve students, one eighty-minute session. I want an activity built around one short text: Anselm of Canterbury, *Proslogion*, chapter VIII, which Sidney Norton Deane translates under the heading 'How he is compassionate and passionless'. The chapter's problem is that compassion seems to require being affected by another's suffering, yet God is said to be impassible—incapable of being affected. Anselm's solution, as he usually read, distinguishes being compassionate in terms of the effect one has on the sufferer from being compassionate as an internal feeling; but I don't want to say that in advance.

Design a structured role-play or constrained deliberation for the seminar that makes participants feel the pull of both sides before they read the chapter. Ideally, by the end of the activity they should find themselves wanting to say that a certain figure is both genuinely compassionate and not subject to being moved by anyone else's state—and they should also notice how strange that sounds. Rules, roles, materials, a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened onto Anselm's distinction. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and which feature of the doctrine the activity cannot capture and I must supply myself.

If I'm wrong that this distinction can be made felt through a game or role-play at all—if the theological point is too essentially verbal or intellectual—then say so plainly and propose the nearest alternative.
