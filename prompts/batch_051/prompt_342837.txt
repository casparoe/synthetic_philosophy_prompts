So I work on a team evaluating interpretability tools, and I've noticed I'm following a rule that I can't quite justify. The rule is this: I won't accept an explanation of a model's behavior from another AI unless the explaining AI is itself understood. Not just accurate — actually understood. If the interpreter is a black box, I treat everything it says about the target model as a prediction about the target, not an explanation of it, no matter how well the claims hold up.

Last month a vendor's model claimed to explain why our loan classifier was flagging some applications. It said the classifier was keying on a combination of zip-code features and an interaction term, and that if we ablated those features, the false-positive rate would drop by X. The predictions about ablations were actually pretty good on a held-out set. But the interpreter was a huge transformer, and nobody could say how its producing the claims. I blocked the rollout and told my boss we'd be replacing the black box we don't understand with another black box we don't understand.

My colleague says I'm being unreasonable. His view is that if an interpreter's explanations let you predict the effects of interventions, the interpreter's internal opacity doesn't matter — the explanations earn their keep. He also says that if I demand every explainer itself be explained, I'm committing to an infinite regress, because then I'll need an explainer for the explainer, and so on. And he thinks I'm smuggling in a requirement that humans be able to understand the explanation, which is a separate demand and maybe not a defensible one.

I'm torn. Is my policy defensible, or is it a bias dressed up as a rule? Under what conditions does an opaque-but-accurate interpreter give you genuine understanding of the target? Is there a version of my rule that keeps what's right about it without the regress — say, requiring the interpreter to be simpler than its target, or requiring that its explanations be validated by interventions on the target rather than just by prediction? And are there cases where I should just accept a black-box explainer even though it violates the rule?
