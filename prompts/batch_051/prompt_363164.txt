I help run a monthly discussion group at a public library for adults who follow local politics closely but have no background in economics. We keep arguing about whether decisions like road projects or school budgets could be made by betting markets instead of elected officials, and I want our next session to explain futarchy. I have read enough about it on my own that I do not need the explanation itself; I need a teaching plan. What ideas have to be in place first, such as the difference between predicting an outcome and valuing it, or the difference between an unconditional prediction and one conditional on a policy being adopted, and in what order should those pieces come, with the dependencies between them made explicit? What single running example should carry the whole session, and what must it have and what must it lack? Which misconceptions should I expect from this audience, for example that a market price just reflects what wealthy traders want, or that a favorable conditional market result proves the policy caused the improvement, and at what point in the sequence should each be headed off? What should I openly leave out at this level? And what question could I ask at the end to check whether they have grasped the underlying idea rather than merely learned the vocabulary?
