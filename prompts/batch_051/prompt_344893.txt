So I run a little sci-fi film night with some mates, and last week we watched The Terminator, and it turned into a proper row about time travel that's been bugging me since. One of them says the plot is just broken: Skynet only exists because of the bits of the Terminator it sent back in the first place, and John Connor only exists because he sent his own dad back to conceive him, so both are bootstrap loops with no real origin. Another says the film actually has a pretty disciplined view of time: the time travel was always already part of the past, nothing can be changed, and the characters are all mistaken about being able to alter anything. Then someone else points to T2, where Sarah and John destroy the arm and the chip and that's meant to avert Skynet, which seems to be its a completely different, changeable model of time. I'd like you to read The Terminator (and Judgement Day if it helps) through the philosophy of time travel: the grandfather paradox, the bootstrap paradox, and the split between fixed-timeline conceptions and mutable or branching ones. Does the film, or the two films taken together, have a consistent position? Does it actually face the grandfather paradox, or does it quietly turn it into something else? And is there a reading on which the loops end up neither incoherent nor a denial of the characters' agency? I want a real verdict and reasons, not just a catalogue of possible readings. Also, if you think the films get something right philosophically that straightforward essays about the paradoxes tend to miss, say so.
