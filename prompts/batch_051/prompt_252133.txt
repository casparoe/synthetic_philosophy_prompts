Write a deadpan terms-of-service agreement, around 550 words, for a subscription community built around an invented large-scale conspiracy theory (do not use any real, named theory, group, or event). The humor should come from the theory's epistemology rather than from the absurdity of its content: encode as contractual clauses the actual moves that make self-sealing belief systems resistant to evidence. For example, include a clause under which the subscriber agrees that any apparent disconfirmation is itself evidence of the cover-up's reach; a clause specifying that official accounts are presumptively less credible than the theory's internal sources, while those internal sources are exempt from symmetric scrutiny; a clause by which each new anomaly is accommodated by adding an auxiliary clause rather than revising any existing one; and a clause stating that the theory's continued survival counts as vindication while its failure to produce promised disclosures does not count against it. Keep the legalese straight-faced throughout — no winking, no breaking character — but make sure a careful reader can tell that what is being ironized is the reasoning, not the intelligence or character of the subscribers. The agreement should leave the reader with the philosophical point that a theory that treats every possible observation as confirmation has insulated itself from evidence.
