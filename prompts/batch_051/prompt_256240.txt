I am preparing the position section of a paper on what makes the semantic paradoxes paradoxical, and I would like a consistency audit before I commit to it in print. My current view is composed of the following commitments.

(1) Every genuine semantic paradox is traceable to self-reference or, more broadly, to some circularity in how the truth or falsity of a claim depends on itself; in the absence of such circularity, no paradox would arise.

(2) The right remedy is to restrict the offending self-referential or circular constructions — through typing, stratification, a context-sensitive truth predicate, or some comparable device — while leaving ordinary classical logic, including its structural rules such as contraction, intact for everything that is not circular.

(3) Curry's paradox does not force me into a nonclassical or substructural response. The Curry sentence refers to itself directly, so a principled restriction on circularity should block the derivation at the outset, and I should not have to abandon contraction or accept a paraconsistent logic in order to handle it.

(4) Yablo's liar is not a counterexample to this diagnosis. Although no individual sentence in the Yablo sequence attributes falsity to itself, the paradox still depends on a form of circularity — whether in how each sentence quantifies over all later sentences or in how a finite diagonal construction fixes the entire infinite sequence — so a suitably general version of the circularity restriction will catch it as well.

(5) The account should not be a collection of special-purpose patches. I want a single unified principle that explains why the offending constructions in Curry's paradox and in Yablo's liar both fail, rather than one fix for negation, one for the conditional, and one for infinitary dependence.

Please audit these commitments. I would like to know which of them are genuinely in tension, which pairs merely appear to conflict, and how the genuine tensions should be ranked by severity. For each genuine tension, identify what would have to be given up and what the least costly revision would cost. Finally, tell me which single revision, if any, would do the most to make the package hang together.
