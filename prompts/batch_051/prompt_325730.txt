I have been thinking about a strange difference in the way I react to photographs and paintings. Last year I saw a photograph of my grandmother taken just before she died, and it felt less like looking at a picture than like seeing her. A painted portrait of her, even if it had been accurate, would not have done that. The same difference shows up in ordinary practice: courts will treat a photograph of a crime scene as showing the scene, but a careful courtroom sketch is treated as a mere illustration. We say we saw an event if we watched live video of it, but not if we watched a photorealistic animation. These are not just differences of emotional force; there seems to be a whole unspoken rule that photographs are transparent in some way paintings and drawings are not.

I came across Kendall Walton's essay Transparent Pictures, and he makes this literal rather than metaphorical. As I understand him, photographs really are transparent: when you look at a photograph, you see, through it, the object it is a photograph of, in something like the way you see through a telescope or a mirror. His reason is that the photograph's details were formed by light from the objects themselves, so the visual information is counterfactually dependent on the scene automatically, not filtered through a human being's beliefs and skill the way a drawing is.

I cannot decide whether this justifies the asymmetry. A photorealistic painting is also based on light from the object reflecting off the artist's retina, and a photograph also reflects choices: framing, lens, exposure, development, digital processing, even what to point the camera at. Why should the absence of a human hand at one point in the chain matter so much? I want the strongest principle you can give that draws the boundary where ordinary practice draws it. Then tell me honestly whether that principle survives when we consider paintings made with a camera obscura, composite photographs, or a photograph that has been heavily altered. If it does not survive, then which should give way: the principle, the practice, or my feeling that the photograph of my grandmother was different?
