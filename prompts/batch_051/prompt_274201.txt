My reading group has spent the better part of a year watching two of our members argue past each other about whether culture actually evolves by Darwinian selection on memes. One is a fairly committed memeticist in the Dawkins and Blackmore line (though he has backed off the old talk of beliefs as viruses infecting people's heads); the other thinks meme is a loose metaphor that does no real explanatory work, and that cultural transmission is too low-fidelity and too reconstructed to support anything like natural selection — she would rather talk about cognitive biases, attractors, and dual inheritance at the population level. They have finally agreed to do an adversarial collaboration, and I've been asked to draft the joint statement they would both sign.

Write that document for me. It should include a numbered agreed-claims section covering what both camps accept about cultural transmission, variation, and differential spread, including what Darwinian selection minimally requires; then a disagreements table stating their residual disagreements in neutral phrasing, with each side's own position worded so that side would sign off on it; then the cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one of them. For each crux, give at least one concrete case or kind of evidence that both sides agree bears on it — a chain letter, an urban legend, a dietary taboo, an earworm, a maladaptive belief that spreads anyway — and record each side's prediction about it in advance. I suspect some of the cruxes are whether cultural items are copied with high enough fidelity to count as replicators, whether meme fitness can be specified independently of the very spread it is supposed to explain, and whether internet memes are the paradigm case or a weird special case. But add whatever cruxes you think the two sides would land one after a year of arguing.
