Ok so I just had a long argument with a friend about AI safety and I'm trying to get my own position straight. He thinks the whole 'control' side is confused: either you think the model is misaligned, in which case no box or monitor will actually contain it, or you think it's fine, in which case all that safety machinery is pointless paranoia. I defended the idea that you can act as if the system might be misaligned even when you aren't sure—like wearing a seatbelt without believing you'll crash. But when he pushed me to say what a good control meassure actually has to do, I realized I was just listing techniques (sandboxing, monitoring, interpretability, approval, tripwires) without a principled list of what they're supposed to deliver. So before I go read anything else, I want the desiderata. By a control measure I mean a measure whose job is to keep a possibly-misaligned system from doing bad things, not one whose job is to change what the system wants. What does any safety measure that assumes the model may be misaligned have to deliver to count as adequate? I'm especially interested in which requirements are non-negotiable (if it doesn't meet this it's not a control measure at all), which are preferences of particular camps, and which pairs probably can't be fully satisfied at once—that's the part my friend will pounce on. Keep the weight on the criteria and the reasoning behind each, not on scoring existing proposals.
