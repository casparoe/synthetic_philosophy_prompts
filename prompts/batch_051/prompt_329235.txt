I volunteer as a docent at a local history museum, and next month I'm running a ninety-minute workshop for the other docents on how not to tell Whiggish history. The group is bright and opinionated but none of them have any historiography background; mostly retired teachers, a pharmacist, an engineer. I dont want to give them a talk about Butterfield or about historiographical debates. I want them to come out the other end able to spot the Whig move in one of our own exhibit labels or tour scripts and to say what a non-Whig version would do differently.

Plan the session for me. What has to be in place before the term "Whig history" is even worth introducing, and why does it have to come in that order? What should the one carrying example be: what does it need to have and what does it need to lack? This group will bring their own defenses — "but things really did get better though," "so youre saying we cant judge the past at all," "isn't this just political correctness about Western history" — where in the sequence do I head each of these off, and with what distinction, so they dont think I've asked them to give up on truth or progress entirely? And give me a five-minute end-of-session check that would show whether they've actually got the concept, not just learned to say "Whig history is bad." Also tell me what I should explicitly leave out at this level, and why.
