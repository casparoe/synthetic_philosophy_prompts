I've been trying to pin down when I should trust my future self's opinions, and my own reactions to cases keep pulling apart. I'm hoping you can find the principle behind them—or tell me which reactions I have to give up.

Here are the cases, with my verdicts.

(1) Tomorrow I'll get the results of a medical test. If I somehow learned tonight that tomorrow-me ends up 70% confident he will recover, I'd adopt 70% right now without hesitation.

(2) I know a sleeping pill tonight will make me forget exactly one thing: an email I accidentally saw suggesting my company is about to be bought. Right now I'm 85% the deal happens. I'm also sure tomorrow-me, having forgot the email, will be 60%. I don't think I should lower myself to 60% tonight.

(3) Tonight someone offers me a drug that reliably turns people into terrible reasoners for a day—they hold the same evidence but become wildly overconfident about whatever they believe. I know tomorrow-me will be 90% confident my brother is still angry at me. I won't budge from my current 15%.

(4) Next week I'm going to a retreat that showers people in genuine new information, but is intentionally designed to produce overwhelming confidence in the leader's claims. I'm pretty sure I'll come back 80% confident in the leader's story. Right now I can't tell whether I should in advance move to 80%, move partway, or stay put. This is the case I keep going back and forth on.

(5) Tomorrow-me will have exactly my evidence and exactly my brain—no new evidence, no drugs—but he'll have spent three hours thinking hard in the bath. If I learn he comes out at 40% on the same question, I'd move to 40%. That also feels right. But it makes me nervous: if a few hours of extra thought makes him worth deferring to, why doesn't thirty years of cognitive decline do the same? In the decline case, where future-me is 95% sure the nurses are stealing from him, I wouldn't move an inch.

So my rough candidate is something like: defer to future-me only when he'll be at least as rational as I am now and at least as well-informed. But that can't be the whole story. Case (4) seems to show that being better informed and just as rational might not settle it, and case (5) seems to show I'm willing to defer to someone only marginally ahead of me, not some expert.

Is there one precise principle that gets all these, or close? What does it say about cases I haven't listed—like a future-me who is smarter than me overall but has forgotten one key detail, or a future-me who has read everything I've read plus a single extra article? And which of my verdicts does it force me to drop?
