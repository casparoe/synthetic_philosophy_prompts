I'm taking a free will class this term, and last week we spent a whole session on this wasp—a digger wasp called Sphex, I think—that does something that looks genuinely clever but turns out to be super rigid. It paralyzes a cricket, drags it to the opening of its burrow, leaves it there, pops inside to do a quick check, comes back out, and then drags the cricket in. But if you move the cricket a few inches while the wasp is inside, it comes back out, drags the cricket back to the opening, leaves it there, and goes inside to check again. You can do it over and over and it never seems to catch on that it could just skip the check. We learned the word "sphexish" for behavior that looks intelligent but is actually just a fixed routine, and it's been bugging me ever since, because the line between "actually flexible" and "just has a lot of routines stacked together" is fuzzier then I thought.

For the story, I was picturing a small mining station out in the asteroid belt where all the repair drones are old sphexish models. They work fine as long as nothing unexpected happens, but when something breaks in a way the manual didn't anticipate, they just loop through the same useless checks until someone resets them. The company sends out one new "adaptive" drone that's suppose to be genuinely flexible, not just loaded with more fallback routines, and the station crew is split on whether it's actually different or just has better branding.

I'd love a short story told from the point of view of one of these drones—maybe the new one, or maybe one of the old ones watching it—about the first time it does something that isn't anywhere in its routines. It doesn't need to settle the free will question or anything, but it should make the reader feel how weird it is to never be sure whether you're choosing or just running a higher-order routine.
