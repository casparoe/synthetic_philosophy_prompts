A friend of mine, Dana, teaches a class on interpretation, and I've been trying to predict her rulings on disputed readings the way I'd try to reverse-engineer a black-box scoring rule. Over the semester she's given a bunch of verdicts, and I've been keeping them as a little dataset.

First: a poem contains the line "he carried the weight of the field," and no authorial remarks survive. One reading says the man is literally carrying harvested crops; the other says he's burdened by the memory of a battle that happened in the field. The surrounding stanzas are all about hauling hay, so Dana says the literal crop reading is the meaning.

Second: a poet published a letter to a critic after a sonnet came out saying that the stopped clock on the landing should be taken literally as a detail from her aunt's house and not as a memento mori. The poem is an elegy, with imagery of evening and endings, so the memento mori reading is the natural one and would be the better poem. Dana says the literal aunt's-house reading is the meaning, because the poet said so and it's a possible reading.

Third: a novel has the sentence "she let the leash slip," and an interview with the novelist published after the book says he meant the character to be deliberately releasing the dog, even though the text, with all its talk of trembling hands, more naturally suggests an accident. The accidental reading is also the flatter one dramatically, but Dana says the deliberate-release reading is the meaning.

Next week she's going to rule on this one: a poem contains "the ledger was blank by the end of the year." The public reading is something about accounts being wiped clean, and it fits the poem's concern with debt and memory. But the poet's private diary, found after her death and never intended for publication, says she called her favorite garden bed "the ledger," so the line just means the garden was empty at year's end—a flatly literal private joke.

Predict Dana's verdict on the ledger case. Say whether the pattern so far points to actual intentionalism, hypothetical intentionalism, textualism, value-maximizing, or some hybrid, what each live option would say about the ledger case, and what future case would cleanly seperate the candidates I'm still torn between. I'm mostly trying to figure out which way the prediction market inside my head should move.
