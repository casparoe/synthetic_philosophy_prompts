I am a philosophy PhD candidate and the graduate-student representative on my university's Student Philanthropy Board, a small endowment-funded body that awards $60,000 each spring to outside organizations. This year the shortlist has produced a standoff. The three finalists are: (i) a community-based snakebite envenoming program in southeastern Nigeria that would train lay health workers to administer antivenom and coordinate motorcycle-ambulance referrals; (ii) a project to build red-team evaluation suites for open-source AI models with capabilities relevant to biological pathogen design; and (iii) a pilot to field-test electrical stunning equipment at a commercial shrimp-processing facility, aimed at the welfare of farmed shrimp at slaughter. Proposal (i) is neglected and operationally tractable, but its total contribution to global welfare is comparatively modest; proposal (ii) addresses a catastrophic tail risk, but its marginal value is hard to estimate and the field is crowding; proposal (iii) concerns an enormous number of animals, but the intervention's feasibility at processing speed is thinly evidenced.

The Board's charter instructs us to justify awards by reference to importance, tractability, and neglectedness, but it says nothing about how those three considerations should be combined, and that silence is the source of the current standoff. One camp argues that the framework, together with expected-value reasoning, requires concentrating the entire award on whichever project has the single highest marginal impact. The other camp argues that our uncertainty about all three estimates is deep enough, in something like the Knightian sense, to justify a diversified portfolio, and that high neglectedness cannot by itself salvage proposal (iii). I need to circulate a decision memo before Friday's meeting. Please write that memo: recommend a concrete allocation of the $60,000 among the three finalists (including the option of allocating all of it to one proposal or zero to some), apply the importance-tractability-neglectedness framework explicitly, and give an honest treatment of the strongest consideration favoring the camp whose position your recommendation rejects.
