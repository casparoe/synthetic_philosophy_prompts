I am a control systems engineer by training: I think naturally in terms of state spaces, plants, feedback controllers, reachable sets, trace properties, and supervisory controllers with override authority. My philosophical background is thin, so I would like you to restate the debate over Frankfurt-style cases and the principle of alternative possibilities in exactly that vocabulary, as faithfully as possible, and then report what did not survive the translation.

By a Frankfurt-style case I mean the familiar setup in which an agent is deciding between two courses of action, and an intervener monitors the agent and would force one course of action if the agent were about to choose the other; as it happens the agent does the action on their own and the intervener never intervenes. The principle of alternative possibilities, which the case is meant to refute, says that an agent is morally responsible for what they did only if they could have done otherwise. The two sides are roughly these: the defender of the principle takes the absence of an open alternative to undermine responsibility, while the Frankfurtian locates responsibility in features of the actual sequence of the agent's own deliberation and treats the blocked alternative as irrelevant.

I want the translation to be systematic. In my vocabulary the agent looks like a plant or lower-level controller, the intervener like a supervisory controller that enforces a specification by overriding the plant whenever a triggering state is reached, and the overall system admits only one completed trajectory even though the agent's internal dynamics might have branched. So where does the philosophical issue land in that picture? Does could have done otherwise become reachability of some alternative state or trace? Does the distinction between the agent's own selection and the intervener's guarantee become a distinction between which policy selected the actual trajectory? And what becomes of the defender's fallback move of pointing to a flicker of freedom, some residual alternative that remains even in the Frankfurt setup?

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the debate translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in the control vocabulary at all? In particular, I want your view on whether the moral sense of an alternative's being genuinely open, as opposed to merely reachable in a counterfactual model, is lost in translation, and whether what is lost is a distinction my vocabulary should import or a sign that the philosophical dispute is less precise than it looked.
