Compare the bearing of Hilary Putnam’s multiple realizability argument on the identity theory with David Lewis’s role–occupant reply. In Putnam’s The Nature of Mental States, there is a passage in which he asks us to suppose that a Martian with a radically different physical constitution, or a robot running a machine table, could be in the same mental state—pain—as a human. He takes this to show that pain cannot be identified with a particular brain state or physicochemical condition. Lewis’s later treatment in Mad Pain and Martian Pain grants that pain can have different realizers but argues that the concept of pain refers to the occupant of a causal role, so that the identity claim is about roles, not first-order physical states. Which of these two positions gives the better account of what a mental state is? In particular, does Putnam’s argument refute psychophysical identity theory, or does it at most force identity theorists to ascend to a more abstract level of description?
