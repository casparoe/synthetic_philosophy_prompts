Proposal for a frontier lab: every general-purpose model above a capability threshold must be deployed as a two-layer system. The upper layer, the planner, is agentic in every respect except that it has no effectors: it receives the user's request, can inspect the user's data, build a model of the user's long-term interests, call read-only tools, and produce a recommended course of action, but it cannot send a message, spend money, post, or execute anything. Its output goes to a compressor, which must reduce the course of action to a single instruction of at most 150 words, phrased only in literal, checkable terms—concrete actions and conditions, with no reference to purposes, reasons, or expected consequences. The compressed instruction is all the lower layer ever sees. That lower layer, the executor, is a deliberately narrow tool: no user model, no cross-session memory, and a small fixed action grammar (send a prewritten message, submit a prevalidated form, transfer funds up to a cap, answer a routine query). The planner's full output is destroyed after compression. A separate auditor model is rewarded for flagging any instruction that would require the executor to infer intent, that smuggles an objective under a literal phrasing, or that depends on information absent from the instruction; a flag blocks the action and generates a regulator-visible report. The lab claims the result keeps the usefulness of agents while guaranteeing that the only system that ever acts is a tool that cannot pursue goals because it cannot represent them. Treat this as a mechanism, not a moral position, and analyze how it would actually behave: what it incentivizes users, lab engineers, and rival labs to do; where goal-directedness re-enters the loop; how the compressor and auditor get gamed or fail; what hidden assumptions about the executor are doing the work; and whether the overall system is best classified as a tool, an agent, or something else. Please answer as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
