I am a reinforcement-learning engineer, and I think almost exclusively in the vocabulary of Markov decision processes: states, actions, rewards, transitions, policies, trajectories, terminal states, finite and infinite horizons, discount factors, returns, value functions, and counterfactual trajectories evaluated against a model. My formal philosophy background is negligible. Restate, in that vocabulary and in a way I could actually implement as an MDP, the Epicurean argument that death is not bad for the person who dies, together with its main modern opponent, the deprivation account—the view that death is bad because it deprives the person of the goods a longer life would have contained—most influentially associated with Thomas Nagel. Do not just gesture at analogies; be precise about what each side must assume about the reward process, the terminal state, which states an agent can be said to occupy, and the space of possible trajectories over which value is computed. In particular, spell out how the Epicurean experience requirement—the assumption that an event harms a person only if the person experiences it at some time—is expressed as a restriction on where the reward signal may be nonzero, and how the deprivation account reopens the possibility that a trajectory's evaluation includes counterfactual states the agent never occupies.

Include as part of the translation the problem of the timing of death's badness: if death is bad for the person who dies, at what point in the trajectory—the transition into the terminal state, the post-terminal interval, the whole episode, the comparison between episodes—does the badness reside? Then give me a residue report, separating (i) what each position can state cleanly in MDP terms, (ii) what can be stated only with distortion, and where the distortion appears, and (iii) what cannot be stated in the vocabulary at all. Conclude by telling me whether each item in (iii) indicates a real gap in the RL vocabulary or a place where the philosophical claim was doing less determinate work than it appeared. In particular, I need your verdict on whether treating death as a shortened horizon and comparing actual and counterfactual discounted returns is a faithful rendering of the deprivation account, or whether it quietly takes sides in a dispute the Epicurean would reject.
