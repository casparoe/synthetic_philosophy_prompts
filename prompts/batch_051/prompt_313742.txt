I keep going back and forth on Twin Earth. The externalist conclusion — that "water" means H2O on Earth and XYZ on Twin Earth, even though Oscar and his twin are molecule-for-molecule identical — strikes me as basically right: the meaning of a natural kind term is fixed partly by what's actually in the environment, not just by what's in the head. But I also have a very firm intuition that I have priviledged access to my own thoughts. Take someone who grew up using "water" competently on Earth, then is relocated to Twin Earth without being told, so slowly that they never notice anything odd: their old Earth uses and new Twin Earth uses blur together in memory. Externalism seems to imply that at some point the word in their mouth changes meaning while their subjective sense of what they mean stays exactly the same. That would make a thinker systematically wrong about the contents of their own occurrent thoughts, and I can't get myself to believe that's possible.

So here's the clash. On one side: the theoretical case for externalism is strong — Twin Earth, deference to experts, natural-kind terms, maybe the idea that thought contents are partly environmental. On the other side: the intuition of first-person authority, that if I'm thinking about water, the fact that I'm thinking about water isn't the kind of thing I can be wrong about in the way I can be wrong about whether the stuff in my glass is H2O.

I want the strongest version of each side, including the best compatibilist moves — e.g. that the relevant self-knowledge isn't a matter of discriminating contents from the inside but of self-ascribing whatever content the environment plus my history already fixed, or that externalism and basic self-knowledge are fine together once you seperate the content of a thought from the ability to know you're having it. Then give me a negotiated verdict: should I drop the intuition, abandon externalism, or does the compatibility story actually work? Be specific about what would justify each move.
