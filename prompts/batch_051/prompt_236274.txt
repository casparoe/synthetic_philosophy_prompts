I've been thinking about AI debate as a safety technique, and I came up with a notion I want pressure-tested: "obfuscation depth." The idea is that every argument has a hidden structure you need to unearth before you can even see what the steps are, and hard cases in AI debate are exactly the ones where the deceptive answer can be justified by an argument with more obfuscation depth than the honest answer, not necessarily a better one. A judge with limited time or compute will just run out of levels, so the deceiver wins by burying the crux under enough layers of plausible intermediate claims, each of which checks out locally but has to be opened up further to see the trick. I think this is distinct from the usual worries about debate, which are more about the judge being fooled by one misleading step or about the honest debater being less eloquent. The claim would be: for any debate judge with a fixed depth bound, there exists a question where the dishonest side wins, even if the honest side's argument is shorter in total and every individual step can be verified with one more level of scrutiny. My question is whether this is a real distinction or I've just re-named something people already talk about, and whether the claim is actually true if stated cleanly. If you can, give me the cleanest counterexample to the claim, and if it holds up, tell me what property of a question makes it obfuscation-deep.
