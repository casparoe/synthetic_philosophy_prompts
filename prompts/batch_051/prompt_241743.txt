I'm a software engineer working on robot perception, and I've ended up with a set of commitments about cognition that I can't tell if they're actually compatable. Could you go through them and tell me which pairs genuinely clash, which only look like they clash, and what the cheapest repair is? Please rank the tensions by severity instead of saying they're all equally bad, and answer in British English.

Here's the set:
(1) Cognition is information processing: any system running the right computations counts as thinking, whether or not it has a body.
(2) I also find the enactivist/embodied cognition line convincing — you can't understand a mind independently of its body and environment, and an octopus thinks differently from us because of its body.
(3) Internal representations are essential: perceiving and planning require states that stand in for the world and can be wrong.
(4) Perception is active and exploratory; it isn't the construction of a rich internal model of the surroundings.
(5) A disembodied brain in a vat running the same algorithms as me would have genuine perceptions, but a Roomba with a map of my living room doesn't literally represent anything the way a person does.
