Okay here's one for you. I want a thought experiment built to spec, on Pascal's stuff about justice and force.

Specs:
- A realistic setting: a community group, a workplace, a co-op, something like that. No sovereigns, no police states, but there can be an internal enforcement mechanism.
- The same fair process rule is adopted by two groups. The rule is identical, word for word. Privately, the same share of people in both groups think it's fair.
- In Group A, the rule holds: disputes get settled by it, and after a while people speak of it as legitimate. In Group B, it doesn't hold: people complain about it, circumvent it, and it collapses, even though the stated rule never changed.
- The only difference that explains the divergence is that Group A's rule has teeth (some credible sanction or enforcment capacity) and Group B's doesn't. Everything else should be held as fixed as you can.

Then do the walkthrough: state what Pascal would say is happening in each group, and why the apparent legitimacy in A is downstream of the force, not prior to it. Then say which feature of the scenario is doing the work, what would happen to Group A if the enforcement capacity disappeared overnight, and how someone who thinks justice can bind on its own would push back on the Pascalian reading.

Keep the whole thing under 700 words.
