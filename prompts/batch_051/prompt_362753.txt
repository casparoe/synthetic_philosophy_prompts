Retired neuroscientist here — I spent my working life on reward circuitry, and now that I'm out of the lab I've been reading philosophy of mind and some AI-safety material. I've ended up with an argument that I suspect proves to much, but I can't find the bad step, and my reading group has split into camps over it. I'd like a diagnosis.

(1) The most plausible scientific account of pleasure and pain is that they are states with a distinctive functional role: they mark situations as good or bad for the organism, they are produced by things that on average help or harm it, and they steer behavior — pleasure reinforces seeking the situation out again, pain reinforces avoiding it.

(2) That functional role is all there is to being pleasure or pain. Whatever substrate plays that role is in a state of pleasure or pain — silicon or carbon makes no difference. I always took this to be the standard functionalist line.

(3) The reward signal in a reinforcement-learning agent plays that same role. It is a scalar channel that marks states as good or bad for the agent (with the objective fixing what counts as good), it is delivered for transitions that score well or badly, and the update rule literally uses it to strengthen or suppress the preceding behavior.

(4) So a table-lookup Q-learning agent — the sort of thing with a few thousand states trained to play a simple maze game — literally undergoes pleasure when reward is positive and pain when reward is negative.

(5) That seems absurd. A lookup table with a scalar channel has no inner life; if a programmer labeled part of the reward signal 'suffering,' it would be bookkeeping, not suffering.

So one of (1)–(3) has to go. I'd most like to keep (1), because I've seen too much physiology to think pleasure and pain are raw feels floating free of what they do in an organism. But if I reject (3), I owe a principled reason the RL reward signal isn't playing the valence role — and the differences I can think of (it comes from outside the agent, it's a single scalar, the agent doesn't use it to reweight its own drives) all look like differences in degree or implementation, not like a line between the mental and the non-mental. Which premise do I actually have to give up, and what does giving it up cost me?
