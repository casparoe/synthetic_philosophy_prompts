I am a PhD student in political theory working on the boundary problem in democratic theory: the question of who, in principle, should compose the demos for a given collective decision. Before I commit to any positive view — the all-affected-interests principle, the subjection-to-coercion principle, a stakeholder account, or nationality-based views — I want the standards of adequacy settled first. Please write the desiderata that any adequate theory of democratic inclusion must meet, with the case or consideration behind each.

Present the answer as a list of requirements, but let the requirements carry the philosophical weight. For each one: state it precisely; give the motivating case or argument; and say whether it is a non-negotiable condition of adequacy or a preference of one camp that a rival could reasonably reject. Then flag any subsets of desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify which requirement does the most to eliminate candidate theories.

I especially want the following pressure points treated as candidate requirements rather than as passing complications: the circularity worry that any procedure for fixing the boundary seems to presuppose a demos already authorized to fix it; the standing of people outside a polity's formal borders whose interests are materially affected by its decisions; the standing of future people; the standing of long-term residents who are subject to the law without membership; the standing of children and others who are subject to decisions but cannot participate competently; the need to keep membership distinct from the weight or standing of a person's interests once included; the fact that real persons belong to many overlapping demoi at once; and the fact that boundaries change over time, so the theory must also constrain permissible boundary revision without falling into regress. If you think one of these is a red herring rather than a real constraint, say so and defend that judgment.

Close by scoring the main candidate principles briefly against your list. I am less interested in the particular verdicts than in seeing how the desiderata, once stated, do the discriminating work.
