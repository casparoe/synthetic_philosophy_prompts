for a seminar on the epistemology of online life I have to hand in a short statement of where I stand on echo chambers and epistemic bubbles, and I'd like someone to check it for consistency before I send it. here's the whole thing, as flat as I can make it.

one, I take a bubble and a chamber to be different in kind, not just degree. a bubble is a gap in your exposure—you don't hear the other side. a chamber is an active practice of discrediting people and sources from outside. one is a hole in your diet, the other is a trained reflex against certain foods, roughly.

two, I think you can be perfectly rational while inside a bubble. if you'd update once someone actually handed you the missing side, then you're unlucky about your environment, not unreasonable.

three, I think being in an echo chamber is always epistemically irrational, full stop. the problem isn't that you havent heard the other side; it's that you've adopted a standing policy of discounting it because it's outside.

four, I also think distrust is only as good as the track record behind it. if your total evidence makes a source look unreliable, then refusing to rely on it is exactly whats rational; if it doesnt, it isnt.

five, so I want to say a person from a community that mainstream outlets have verifiably misreported, sneered at, and talked over for decades can be rational to treat those outlets as systematically less credible when they cover that community. that distrust is earned and defeasible, and I wouldn't call it an echo chamber even though it wears the same clothes.

six, I'm also inclined to think that inside a mature echo chamber, the member's total evidence will usually make outside sources look unreliable too—there's a self-confirming record, corrections get reread as manipulation, and the community keeps passing the tests it sets itself.

seven, and I think the bare fact that a person can't tell from the inside whether they're in the situation from five or the situation from six shouldn't by itself settle whether they're rational.
