At the pub after five-a-side last week, two of my mates got into it about our other mate Sam, who'd been money-pumped at the village fete drinks stall. Sam started with a lager, paid 50p to swap it for a stout because stout sounded better, then paid 50p to swap the stout for a bitter because bitter sounded better, then paid 50p to swap the bitter for a lager because lager sounded better, and walked off with the same drink he'd started with, £1.50 lighter.

Dan said Sam is straightforwardly irrational: the three swaps reveal a cycle in his preferences (stout > lager, bitter > stout, lager > bitter), and a cycle means he can in principle be made to pay 50p forever going round in circles. Preferences have to be transitive or the whole idea of 'the option Sam wants' stops making sence.

Priya said each choice was fine on its own: at each moment he got the drink he'd rather hold at that moment, and 50p is a fair price for an upgrade you actually want. The mistake, if any, was tactical — not clocking that the stallholder would keep offering a deal each time and stopping the pattern after round one. Being a bit slow and paying £1.50 for a ride on a preference carosel isn't the same as being irrational.

Dan replied that's exactly wrong: failing to see the pattern is the irrationality. Rationality isn't just taking whatever appeals at each instant; it's having a coherent policy across time, and Sam's step-by-step choices committed him to ending with exactly what he began with, minus money, even though every swap was voluntary. Priya said Dan is smuggling in one fixed preference ranking where there isn't one: 'lager while I'm holding bitter' and 'lager while I'm holding stout' are different options with different contexts, so the swaps don't form a real loop. Dan said that individuating options that finely makes transitivity vacuous and makes the £1.50 impossible to even call a mistake, which is absurd.

They asked me to referee and I honestly couldn't. Please go through their exchange move by move, say who's right about which specific point, what both of them are missing, and who is more right overall.
