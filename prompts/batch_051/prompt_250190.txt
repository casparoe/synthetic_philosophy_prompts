I'm a PhD student working on Sidgwick's ethics, and the other day a colleague in history of science gave me their potted version of his psychical research, which I've been turning over since. They said roughly this. Sidgwick was the first president of the Society for Psychical Research, and he got into it because The Methods of Ethics had left him in an uncomfortable spot: reason seemed to show both egoism and utilitarianism were rational, but they could come apart, and reason gave no way to settle the conflict. So he thought the only way to make morality fully rational was to show empirically that the universe is morally ordered — perhaps that consciousness survives death — and psychical research was his attempt to find that evidence. He was, to be fair, a scrupulous sceptic: he caught a lot of fraudulent mediums and never claimed anything had been proved. But by the end, the whole thing was a dead end, and in his later presidential addresses he more or less said the project had failed.

I know the broad shape is partly right, but the last clause feels of to me, and I don't trust my own reading. What I'd like is an accuracy audit of this summary: which parts would a careful Sidgwick scholar accept, which are compressed in a misleading way, and whether the claim about his later presidential addresses is fair. Please don't just tell me what he believed; I want to know where the summary does and doesn't track The Methods of Ethics and his actual published remarks to the Society for Psychical Research. And since I keep going back and forth: please say explicitly what would change your verdict and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail. British English, please.
