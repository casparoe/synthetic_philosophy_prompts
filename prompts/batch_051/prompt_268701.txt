I have been mediating an adversarial collaboration between two members of my seminar who have argued for a year about whether the world is becoming less violent, and I need you to draft the document they should have been able to produce at the end of it. One of them, the declinist, holds a broadly Pinker-style view: over the long sweep of history, rates of lethal violence have fallen dramatically, especially per capita, and the rise of states, courts, trade, and norms has made the decline robust even through the horrors of the twentieth century. The other, the skeptic, holds that the question as usually posed is too ill-defined to have a single answer: violence is not a natural kind with a theory-neutral boundary, per-capita and absolute counts can reverse the verdict, and the apparent long-run decline is highly sensitive to what gets counted — battlefield deaths, homicide, state violence against civilians, structural harm, or something broader. They now agree they have mostly been talking past each other and want a joint statement that records how far their agreement really extends.

Draft that statement. It should include (a) a numbered agreed-claims section containing only claims both parties will sign, including what both grant about the definitional ambiguities, what the data minimally show on both per-capita and absolute framings, and what institutional and material changes have undeniably altered the landscape of violence; (b) a disagreements table in which each row gives a neutral formulation of one residual dispute, followed by the precise commitment each side accepts as a fair statement of its own view; (c) a cruxes section naming the sub-questions that would most move at least one side if settled — I suspect two will be whether structural harm should count as violence at all, and whether per capita or absolute is the right framing when the question is about actual lives — but include any others the document needs; and (d) for each crux, one concrete case or kind of evidence both parties agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where an apparent disagreement is merely verbal, say so and dissolve it. Do not include citations, a bibliography, or a reading list; the statement should stand on the stated positions. Keep the whole thing to 800 words or fewer.
