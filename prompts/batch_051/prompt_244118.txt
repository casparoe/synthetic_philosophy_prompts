(1) Take a borderline case for the predicate "tall": a person Tex such that competent speakers are neither willing to assert nor willing to deny that Tex is tall, and the established facts of usage do not decide the matter either way.
(2) Supervaluationism entails that, in such a case, the sentence "Tex is tall" is neither true nor false; likewise for its negation, "Tex is not tall."
(3) The truth value of a disjunction is determined by the truth values of its disjuncts: a disjunction is true just in case at least one disjunct is true, false just in case both disjuncts are false, and neither true nor false otherwise.
(4) Therefore, from (2) and (3), the sentence "Tex is tall or Tex is not tall" is neither true nor false: both disjuncts are neither true nor false.
(5) But "Tex is tall or Tex is not tall" is an instance of the law of excluded middle and is a classical logical truth.
(6) Any theory that makes a classical logical truth neither true nor false abandons classical logic.
(7) Supervaluationism is standardly presented as a theory that preserves classical logic.
(8) Therefore, supervaluationism is internally inconsistent.
