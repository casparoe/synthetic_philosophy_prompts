I am organizing a graduate colloquium and will be moderating an interview with a philosopher of cognitive science who has recently published a book defending a version of Dennett's picture of real patterns and the three stances. Her central claim is that the patterns we attribute to systems from the physical, design, and intentional stances are real just insofar as they let us predict and compress the system's behavior, and that this is enough to license intentional-state talk even when the underlying mechanism is known to be quite different from a human brain. I want the interview to get past the standard rehearsed defenses and probe the parts of the view that usually get smoothed over.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions, each with a likely reply from her position and a follow-up that presses on the escape route. The questions should include at least one on each of the following: whether the reality of a pattern can survive the availability of a lower-level description that predicts better but compresses less, and what this says about the relation between the three stances; how the intentional stance applies to a system trained to produce human-like text without any stable internal map of environment or self, especially when its success depends partly on the interpreter's own projections; whether the design stance is anything more than a temporary epistemic convenience in biology and engineering; and whether the view can distinguish a real pattern from a merely projectible regularity in the way the observer's interests and training data are set up. End with one question about what empirical result or conceptual argument would most embarrass the picture, and include a follow-up that would prevent her from retreating to the claim that the question misunderstands the view.
