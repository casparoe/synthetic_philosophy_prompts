ok so I'm trying to sort out my views in moral philosophy and I want a consistancy check before I show this to anyone. here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I think rightness is a function of how good outcomes are, judged from an impartial point of view. I don't think rights or side-constraints are fundamental, just useful rules of thumb.

(2) I find the seperateness of persons objection basically right as a diagnosis: what's wrong with old-fashioned utilitarianism is that it adds benefits and burdens across different lives the way one might within a single life. I think it matters who gets what, not just the total.

(3) I also think distribution matters intrinsically in a specific way: a unit of benefit to someone who is worse off counts for more than the same unit to someone who is better off.

(4) But I'm suspicious of my anti-aggregative instincts. I know about scope insensitivity and identifiable victim effects, and I'm worried my view is just refusing to do the math when the math pushes against my felt response.

(5) At the same time, I'm drawn to a reductionist view of persons: what matters is a bunch of overlapping psychological connections, and the boundary between lives is more like the boundary between clubs or nations than a line in metaphysical bedrock.

(6) In conflict cases where one stranger needs my help and five strangers need my help, with no special ties either way, I think the right thing is not simply to save the five. You should give each a fair chance — maybe flip a coin — because saving the five doesn't give due weight to the one's claim.

Does this set of commitments hang together? I can't tell which tensions are real and which only look like tensions. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and tell me the cheapest fix for each and what it would cost me.
