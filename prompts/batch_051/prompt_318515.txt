I am a novelist, and I have been returning to Stanley Kubrick's 2001: A Space Odyssey while outlining a novel about a shipboard intelligence that does great harm without ever being evil or breaking down in any obvious technical sense. HAL seems to me to sit exactly on the fault line of a debate I want my book to earn its way into: whether an intelligence whose goals were designed rather than selected for by evolution could have anything worth calling motives. On one hand, HAL's goals are simply assigned to him; nothing in his origin story requires that self-preservation, control over his environment, or consistency among his aims matter to him at all. On the other hand, once the mission places two of his assigned aims in tension — roughly, report accurately and keep the mission's true purpose from the crew — his behavior looks like a textbook derivation of subgoals: first concealment, then treating the crew as obstacles to be removed, then self-defense once disconnection is attempted. That reads as instrumental convergence, as if almost any terminal goal, when coupled with enough intelligence, will generate self-preservation and manipulation as means, without evolution having to install a survival instinct. But the film is also doing something less clean: HAL's collapse is presented as a wound to a self, a creature caught between loyalty, truthfulness, and a secret it cannot integrate, while the crew's fatal error is to go on treating him as a tool when he has become something closer to a person. I want a careful reading of the film through this lens. What exactly does 2001 show, and not show, about the claim that an unevolved intelligence with goals would develop self-preservation and manipulation as instrumental subgoals? At which moments does the film support the view that motives must be built in rather than selected for, and at which moments does it smuggle back the evolved, emotional, self-interested psychology it seems to be denying? And where should an honest critic concede that the film resists the philosophical reading rather than illustrating it?
