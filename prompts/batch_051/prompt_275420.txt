I am enrolled in a course on Marx's account of exploitation and alienation, and I have been trying to get clear on what the course means by "exploitation." In the technical sense developed in the readings, exploitation appears to consist in the appropriation of a worker's surplus labor by the owner of the means of production, independently of whether the wage is generous or the working conditions are agreeable. In ordinary speech, by contrast, the word seems to mean something closer to taking wrongful advantage of another person's vulnerability. I would like a working definition of exploitation that gets the following cases right:

1. A garment worker in a developing country, paid the statutory minimum wage, producing clothing for a firm that earns a reliable profit on each worker's output.
2. A retired homeowner who pays a neighborhood teenager the going hourly rate to mow the lawn; no product or service is sold on from the teenager's labor.
3. A worker cooperative whose members jointly own the firm and divide the surplus in proportion to hours worked.
4. A landlord who owns an apartment outright and rents it at the local median market rent to a tenant who works full time.
5. A six-month unpaid editorial internship at a small nonprofit literary magazine, in which the intern does substantive editing and receives mentorship and a reference but no wage.
6. A large streaming platform that pays recording artists a fraction of a cent per stream while its owners receive large profits; the artists are classified as independent contractors rather than employees.
7. A friend who repeatedly asks for help editing applications, moving house, and getting rides to the airport, never reciprocates, and relies on the other person's reluctance to say no.
8. A grain trader who, because he controls local storage and transport, buys maize from smallholders well below the price it would fetch in the nearest city market and resells at that price.

I am confident that case 1 is exploitation and that cases 2 and 3 are not. I am also confident that case 7 is exploitation in the ordinary sense, but I cannot see how it could count as exploitation in Marx's technical sense, since it involves no wage relation and no production of commodities for profit. I have no settled view about cases 4, 5, 6, and 8.

Please propose the definition, run it through all eight cases, and tell me where it returns the wrong answer or no answer at all; then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. If you find that the cases force a split into two definitions rather than one, say so, but only after trying to make a single account work.
