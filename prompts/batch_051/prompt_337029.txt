I mostly think in Bayesian terms these days—posterior over models, Bayes factors, calibration, Solomonoff-ish priors—and I'm fluent enough to translate arguments into that vocabulary, but I dont entirely trust my own translations. Take the recurring argument about whether physics could have a final theory, and whether we'd know if we'd found it. People seem to mean very different things: one says a Theory of Everything is plausible and science is converging on it; another says even if we had it we could never have evidence that it's final, only that it fits the data so far; a third says physics might just run out of theoretically interesting questions without literally finishing. Restate each of those positions, one by one, in the vocabulary of Bayesian model comparison. For each, say what translates cleanly, what has to be distorted to fit, and what has no expression in that vocabulary at all. Then tell me whether the residue points to a hole in the Bayesian picture or to the 'final theory' positions being fuzzier than they look. I'm more interested in the failures of translation than the parts that map neatly.
