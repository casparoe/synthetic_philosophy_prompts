In my ancient philosophy course, students keep comparing social media to Plato's cave: the feed is the wall, the algorithm is the fire, and the creators are the pupeteers. They use it to argue that we're all prisoners of the algorithm and that anyone who says otherwise is just watching shadows. I'm not sure they've thought through what the actual setup at Republic 515a does to that analogy, especially the detail that among the people carrying the artifacts along the wall, "and shapes of animals as well, wrought in stone and wood and every material, some of these bearers presumably speaking and others silent." I'd like an audit of the social-media-as-cave analogy with that quote as the load-bearing point. What maps to what, where the mapping snaps, and whether the conclusion that we're prisoners of the algorithm survives once you notice the puppets can talk or keep quiet. Is there a better analogy available in that passage, or does the whole analogy do more rhetorical work than philosophical work?
