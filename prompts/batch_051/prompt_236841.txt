Last month my uncle was diagnosed with a terminal illness. He's an atheist and is completely clear-eyed that after he dies there's nothing left *for him*. But he's spent the last year building a house that he wants to be lived in by families long after he's gone, and he keeps describing it as the thing that will "still be there." I asked him why it matters to him that the house keeps going after he's dead, if he won't be around to see any of it. He couldnt really answer.

So I tried a different question: if he learned that a comet would strike the earth and instantly, painlessly kill every human being the day after he died, would finishing the house suddenly seem pointless to him? He didn't even pause. That would make everything feel like a waste, he said. I told him he's being inconsistent: either the time after your death matters to you or it doesn't. He insists the two cases are different, but he cant articulate how.

I have a feeling hes onto something anyway, and its making me rethink my own projects and how I think about the future. Whose side should I take, and what's the difference he's reaching for? Does this say something about why the extinction of humanity might be bad in a way that goes beyond the sum of the deaths involved?
