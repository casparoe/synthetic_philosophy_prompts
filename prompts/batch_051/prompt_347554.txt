For my reporting on a scientific dispute, I want to stop letting the charge "that's unfalsifiable" do unexamined work. A cosmologist recently used it against a rival model that explains galactic rotation curves without dark matter, and I realized I couldn't say exactly what claim was being made, or whether it justified dismissing the model. I know the textbook line that falsifiability separates science from non-science, but that feels too crude for this argument. Please break down the distinct precise claims the phrase "unfalsifiable" could be expressing — from trivial to radical — and indicate which readings are defensible, which are false or overstated, and which ones would actually support treating the rival framework as unscientific or not worth pursuing. I'd like a list I can draw on in a feature about how much work this one charge is made to do in science journalism.
