A social media platform adopts as its sole content-policy rule this sentence: "The only ground on which a user's expression may be restricted is that it directly harms some other person; expression that merely offends, disgusts, or undermines the speaker's own character is not a proper ground for restriction." A user then posts a true account of a private individual's spent criminal conviction from decades ago, taken from public court records; the subject is fired and receives a flood of abusive messages from strangers. Another user posts a synthetic voice recording of a different private individual making bigoted remarks, labeled in the original post as parody; the clip circulates widely without the label. The platform's reviewers must apply only the quoted rule. Does it require removing either post? Does reputational ruin brought about by true disclosure count as harm, or only as offense and consequence? Does a fabricated quotation cause harm, or does the harm lie in the unlabeled resharing rather than in the original expression? Argue from the text of the rule, from its Millian purpose, and from what its adopters would have said about a medium where a single utterance can be algorithmically amplified to millions, none of which they had in mind. State which of these approaches should govern the two cases and why.
