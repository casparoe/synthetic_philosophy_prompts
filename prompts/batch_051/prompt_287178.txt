I am working through a puzzle about whether the logic of the scapegoat can be extended to wrongs that are not acts, and I would like a careful analysis rather than a yes or no.

A religious community observes an annual rite of expiation. Its founding manual reads: "On the day of expiation the assembly shall lay its transgressions upon a living bearer, whole and without blemish, kept apart for this purpose; the appointed man shall lead the bearer beyond the boundary, and it shall not return. The transgressions of the assembly depart with it." The manual was composed when the community was a small pastoral settlement, and the wrongs it had in view were particular and datable: a stolen animal, a broken oath, a boundary stone moved by the hand of an identifiable person. In our own time the community is large and dispersed, and the wrongs that most trouble it are cumulative and structural: decades of decisions by committees, investors, consumers, and officials, no one of whom performed the injurious act, through which the soil was poisoned, the river was burdened, and the poorest households were displaced. There is no single subject, no single act, and no moment of commission.

The community proposes to observe the same rite by leading out, not a living animal, but a stone monument inscribed with an account of these harms; the monument is to be set beyond the boundary and thereafter treated as having carried the guilt away. Work through what the rule, read closely as a text, requires and whether the proposal satisfies it; what purpose-based reasoning about the rite would say; what the manual's authors would likely have said if asked about a case of this kind; and whether the underlying mechanism of substitution that made the original rite coherent can carry guilt with no subject, no act, and no commission. Then say which interpretive approach should govern this case and why, and specify what the rite would have to give up or change for the proposal to work under the logic of substitution — or else explain why that logic cannot survive the change.
