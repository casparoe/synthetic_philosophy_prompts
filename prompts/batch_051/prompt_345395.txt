I've been reading the rationalist corner of the internet for way too long, and lately I've gone back down the rabbit hole of the hinge-of-history idea: the claim that our era might be unusually pivotal because of AI, nukes, synthetic bio, and the astronomically large future that's at stake if we don't blow it. I was arguing about it with a friend who works on medieval heresies, and she rolled her eyes and said every generation with a printing press thought it was the special one. That got me wanting something more careful than the usual takes, because I keep flip-flopping between finding the hinge view obviously right and finding it a kind of temporal chauvinism with better graphs.

Could you write a dialogue between two people who both actually know the arguements — one defending the hinge-of-history view, one pushing back? The skeptic should make the strongest version of the base-rate/selection-effect objection (why assume you'd be alive at the hinge? isn't the feeling of historical importance evidence about your psychology rather than the world?), and the defender should answer it on the merits rather than just asserting that the tech is different because it's his. I'd like genuine back-and-forth where each character responds to the specific point the other just made, and neither is a strawman. Maybe around 900-1200 words, ending with them having sharpened the disagreement rather than one of them capitulating. If you can work it in, have them argue at some point over whether 'hinge of history' is even a natural kind or just a useful story we tell ourselves to make our moment feel load-bearing.
