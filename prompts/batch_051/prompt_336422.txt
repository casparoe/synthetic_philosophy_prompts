I cover inequality stuff for a living, and every time I sit through a panel on whether more equal societies are better societies, the same two people end up talking past each other. One's a prioritarian: she thinks what matters is raising the position of the worse off, and that worse-off people get extra moral weight, so inequality itself carries no independent value. The other's a telic egalitarian, the kind who thinks equality is good in itself, not merely good because it makes someone better off. The leveling-down case is where they always get stuck. Make the rich worse off without making the poor any better off, and you've got a more equal world, but nobody in it is better off. The prioritarian says that's no improvement in any respect at all. The egalitarian says it is an improvement in the equality respect, even if it's worse on balance.

I want you to draft the joint statement these two could actually sign after an honest adversarial collaboration, the sort they'd circulate to colleagues rather than a publicist's summary. Start with the claims they've both come to accept, written tightly enough that neither would later say the wording was rigged against her. Then give the exact points were they still part ways—each side's own position phrased so that side would say yes, that's exactly what I mean. Then walk through the cruxes one at a time: the sub-questions where, if an answer got settled, at least one of them would have to move. For each crux, attach a concrete case or line of reasoning both agree bears on it, and record in advance what each camp predicts or how each would judge it. Don't resolve anything; the point is to get the residual disagreement stated so exactly that an outsider could see what would actually change their minds.

I suspect one crux is whether an outcome can be good in any respect when no individual in it is better off; another might be whether the prioritarian can absorb the egalitarian's complaints about relational unfairness into the priority-weighting function, or whether the egalitarian can dodge the leveling-down conclusion by caring about unfair treatment rather than unequal outcomes. But don't let my guesses railroad the document; include whatever the real pressure points are.

Write it as continuous prose only—no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables—the way you'd write an email or a letter to someone you've been arguing with for years.
