In a workshop run by our university's rationality society, the facilitator defended the view that rationality can be trained the way a martial art is trained. A fighter drills forms (katas) until sound movement becomes automatic; a reasoner, he said, should likewise drill reasoning forms — Fermi estimation, calibration exercises, Bayesian updating, pre-commitment to decision criteria — until good reasoning becomes automatic. But drilling alone is insufficient: just as a fighter needs sparring for realistic feedback, a reasoner needs adversarial collaboration, forecasting tournaments, and a public prediction record. And just as a dojo supplies a community, a hierarchy of belts, and a culture of correction, the rationality community supplies accountability that private study cannot. I am taking a course on judgment and decision-making, and I cannot locate the exact point where this analogy fails, though I suspect it is quietly doing more argumentative work than it can bear.
