I've been collecting physicalist responses to Mary's room for a while — the kind of thing that comes up on the forums every few years — and I'm trying to figure out whether my list is complete or whether I'm missing a structurally distinct move. Here's what I have so far:

1. The ability hypothesis (Lewis, Nemirow): Mary doesn't gain any new fact; she gains abilities — to imagine red, recognize it, remember it. Knowing what it's like is know-how, not know-that.

2. The acquaintance hypothesis (Conee): what Mary gains is direct acquaintance with the phenomenal quality itself. Acquaintance is a relation to a thing, not propositional knowledge, so no new fact about the world.

3. The phenomenal-concept strategy (Loar, Papineau, and others): Mary acquires a new phenomenal concept for a property she already knew under physical concepts. Same fact, new mode of presentation.

4. Dennett-style debunking: the intuition depends on not taking the phrase all physical facts literally. If Mary really had complete physical knowledge, the surprise wouldn't survive; the setup is at best misleading.

5. The a-posteriori-physicalism move: physicalism doesn't require phenomenal truths to be a priori derivable from physical truths, so even if Mary can't deduce what red looks like beforehand, that's an epistemic gap, not an ontological one.

Is there a sixth response I'm missing? I want one with a different structure from these five — not a restatement of ability/acquaintance/concept, not a special case of the phenomenal-concept strategy, and not just Dennett plus the a-posteriori move. If it attacks a premise of the argument rather than trying to accommodate what Mary learns, even better. Tell me what it shows that the ones on my list don't, and whether it's actually any good or just the kind of thing that sounds clever in a blog comment.

Also, in your answer, please seperate what's settled about these moves (e.g., that a given response is a recognized option in the literature) from what's genuinely contested among people who work on consciousness. Don't present a contested reading as the concensus.
