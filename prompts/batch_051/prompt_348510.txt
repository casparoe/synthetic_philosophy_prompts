I've ended up with a view about what makes something a disease, and I want to stress-test it. The view, in my own words: a condition counts as a disease when some part or system of the body (or mind, I guess) isn't doing the job it's biologically supposed to do, where "supposed to" just means what that kind of system normally does in humans. So blood pressure of 180/110 counts as a disease even if the person feels fine, because the circulatory system is failing at its job. Near-sightedness counts because the eye isn't focusing properly. Deafness and infertility count too, even if the person doesn't mind and society doesn't punish them. But homosexuality doesn't count on my view, even though plenty of people used to call it a disease, because no system is malfunctioning. And I don't think it matters whether something is statistically rare or common: if everyone's spine were poorly supported, that would still be a disease, not just "normal."

What I'm after is this: what would count against my view? I don't mean the obvious objection that real doctors mostly care about pain and suffering, so my definition doesn't describe how the word is actually used — I already grant that. I mean the cases and arguments that should make me think my view is wrong even as a story about what diseases really are. Which specific cases does it handle badly? I can already feel it start to wobble around mental illness, aging, and some reproductive or hormonal things, but I can't articulate exactly why. And here's a worry that's been nagging me: is my view unfalsifiable, because I can always just decide that some system is or isn't malfunctioning to get whatever answer I wanted? If the view is insulated like that, should that actually bother me, or is that just the normal situation for views like this? I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I'd like to know which ones a person holding my view would have to honestly concede rather than easily dismiss. Also, its probably wrong in some way I can't see, so tell me where it actually hurts.
