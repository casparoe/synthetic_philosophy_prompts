Here's the anchor I keep coming back to: in his 1975 paper An Explanation for the Absence of Extraterrestrials on Earth, Michael Hart makes an argument that I'll paraphrase from memory. He says that if even one civilization in the galaxy had developed interstellar travel, it could spread across the entire galaxy in a few million years using self-reproducing ships or probes, which is a tiny fraction of the galaxy's age. Since the Earth is not full of alien artifacts or probes, and no one has colonized us, there must not be any such civilization. His conclusion is that there is no one out there. The responses all seem to reject that conclusion but for wildly different reasons: some say interstellar colonization just isn't feasible or desirable, others say there's a Great Filter that kills everyone before they get to that stage, others say we're being quarantined or are in a zoo, and others say you can't infer absence of life from absence of colonization because expansionist civilizations might be rare or we might not recognize their artifacts. The frustrating thing is that everyone accepts the same basic observation—we haven't been visited—but they treat it as evidence for totally different claims.
