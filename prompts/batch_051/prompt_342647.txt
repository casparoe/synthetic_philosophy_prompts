At a dinner party last night my friends Mara and Jonas got into it about whether guilt or shame is the better moral emotion, and I honestly couldn't tell who'd won.

Mara argued guilt is better because it's act-focused: you feel bad about the specific thing you did, which points you toward apology and repair. Shame, she said, is self-focused—you feel like a bad person—so people hide, get defensive, or spiral. Jonas said that's a cartoon of shame. Guilt can be a cheap private "I broke a rule, I'll pay my debt" that never touches who you're trying to be, whereas shame tracks something real—that you failed to be a loyal friend or a fair colleague—and that's exactly the knowledge that can make someone genuinely change rather than just patch things up. Mara replied that even when shame tracks real failures, it does so at too high a cost: it's global and paralyzing, and shame-based cultures enforce conformity through exposure and humiliation. Jonas answered that guilt cultures have their own pathologies—scrupulosity, private guilt without repair, treating collective failures as individual ones—so the real question isn't guilt versus shame in the abstract but what practices a culture builds around them, like public acknowledgment, forgiveness, and a way back in. Mara said that's dodging, because a culture cant just pick its emotional repertoire like a menu; it's trained in early and tied to language and institutions. Jonas said cultures do choose the pieces slowly, every time they decide what to punish kids for, what to teach, and what to praise.

I'm supposed to referee. Please explain who's right about which specific point, what both of them are missing, and who is more right overall. Also tell me which argument or consideration would most change your verdict—or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
