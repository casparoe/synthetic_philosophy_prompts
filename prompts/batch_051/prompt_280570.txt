Where I work, a screening algorithm shortlists job applicants before any human reads the applications. The vendor contract promises two things. First, the algorithm is calibrated: among applicants who receive any given score, the same percentage turn out to be strong hires in every demographic group. Second, the algorithm's errors are evenly distributed: the percentage of strong applicants wrongly rejected, and of weak applicants wrongly advanced, is the same across groups. Our data scientist now tells us that no score can satisfy both promises at once except in special cases. Management nonetheless says a fair system must do both, and has put that in our official policy. What theory of fairness does that policy presuppose? Is the theory internally consistent? If we had to abandon one promise, what would each remaining one commit us to, and what would change in how we hire?
