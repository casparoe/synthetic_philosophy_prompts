Two solutions to this puzzle are often proposed. First, the partitioning solution: the mind contains relatively separate cognitive subsystems, so one subsystem can keep the true belief and intentionally cause another subsystem to acquire the false belief, without a single unified subject holding both beliefs at once. Second, the temporal solution: the self-deceiver genuinely knows the truth at one time and intentionally engineers a later state in which he is ignorant of it or no longer attends to it, so the incompatible beliefs never occur simultaneously.

Does the temporal solution simply replace the contradiction with a suspicious gap in the agent’s awareness? Is there a third option that avoids both problems?
