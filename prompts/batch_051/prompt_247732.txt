I've been chewing on the old problem of God knowing the future and human free will, and I keep going back and forth between two answers. The first is Boethius's move: God doesn't strictly know the future in advance, because God is outside time and sees all of it at once, the way someone on a hill can see a whole road in one glance while the travelers only see it bit by bit. The second is the simpler move that foreknowledge doesn't cause the act — me knowing my friend will order the burger doesnt force him to order it, so God knowing what I'll do doesn't force me either. What I want is a real comparison of the two. Are they even answering the same problem, or two different problems? Does Boethius's outside-time move make the 'knowing doesn't cause' point unnecessary, or do you still need both? Which one actually does more work against the worry that we're just running a script? And is there a way Boethius's answer just relocates the problem instead of solving it? Also, some people say Boethius's view makes God know less, not more, because he never knows the future as future — does that objection stick, or does it miss the point? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating the question.
