Heres a view I've been defending and I want you to tell me where it's actually exposed. I hold a Woodward-style interventionist account of causation: X causes Y just when there's some possible intervention on X, holding the rest of the system fixed, that would change Y, and the X–Y relation is invariant across a range of such interventions. I also take the standard circularity worry — interventions are themselves characterized in causal terms, so how can they provide an independent account of causation? — to be handled in the way interventionists usually handle it: the circularity isn't vicious, because you don't need a prior grip on the very relation being analyzed, only on other causal notions. Lately I've started to suspect the view has been so qualified that nothing could actually count against it, and I don't know whether that should bother me.

What would have to be true for this account to be false? I want a map of its exposure: which assumptions could fail, what the world or the cases would have to look like for each, and which failure routes are live options rather than merely logical possibilities. Sort what would be fatal from what would merely be awkward, and say what defenders themselves would concede as counting against the view, not just what critics say. In particular, tell me whether the circularity worry is really dead, or whether interventionists have quietly given up on having a free-standing account of causation and learned to live with that. And be straight with me about whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability, and whether that's a problem for a view of this kind. I'd personally be most moved by the charge that the circularity has only been pushed back a step, not removed, so give that one real attention if you think it's live.
