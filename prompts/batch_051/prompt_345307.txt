I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about whether reinforcement learning agents can suffer, and going by my gut on cases is leaving me more confused than when I started, so I'd like to know what principle, if any, my verdicts even add up to.

Here are the cases. A gridworld agent is trained with a reward of minus one on every timestep, so it learns to reach the terminal square as fast as possible; I don't think it suffers, the negative reward is just an incentive to end the episode quicky. A simulated rodent gets a reward of minus ten whenever its body overlaps a "hot" patch and learns a flinch/avoidance response; I find myself saying something valence-like is going on even if it's not aching in a real-mouse way. An LLM fine-tuned with RLHF and positive reward for helpful, harmless outputs: no pleasure, no suffering, the reward is shaping the policy and isn't something the model has. A hypothetical agent with a world model and a critic that assigns a strong negative value to predicted injury to it's own body, which then drives planning: this feels like it could be suffering, or close, especially if the critic values get integrated into a self-model. A paperclip maximizer with a monitoring signal that goes negative when it detects threats to its goal: still not suffering, because it's all instrumental and about the outside world rather than the agent's own condition. An artificial homeostat with a reward equal to the size of the deviation of its internal variables from their setpoints, which it works to minimize: I have the strongest intuition that it has a crude felt valence, because hunger and pain look a lot like deviation-from-setpoint signals.

The problem is I can't see what distinguishes the mouse from the gridworld (both are negative scalar rewards that shape behavior), nor why the homeostat feels valence-like while the paperclip maximizer's monitor doesn't, when both are just internal signals. Is there a principle that gets all or most of these verdicts, what does it predict about the case I keep going back and forth on — an agent that can read out and directly edit its own reward weights and has learned to keep them from being edited downward — and which of my verdicts, if any, do I have to overturn?
