Consider the design of a technical question-and-answer site with a single reputation score. Users earn points almost exclusively by posting answers that other users upvote; downvotes subtract points; and at fixed thresholds that same score automatically unlocks powers over other users' contributions, first the ability to edit other people's posts and later the ability to vote to close questions as off-topic or low-quality. A user can therefore gain considerable editorial and moderation authority without ever having demonstrated editorial or reviewing judgment. What theory of reputation, expertise, or trust does this design presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory in its strongest form, and be careful to distinguish what the design strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with. Does it presuppose that the trait the score tracks is domain-general — that the ability to produce well-received answers, combined with community approval, is a good proxy for judgment about other people's contributions — or is the practice also compatible with a role-specific view of reputation, on which editing and moderation are separate competencies? Identify the single feature of the design that carries the philosophical weight, and say what would have to change if the presupposition were dropped. Answer in exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion, covering: the reconstructed theory; the distinction between strict presupposition and mere fit; the tension with role-specific views; the carrying feature; and the resulting design change.
