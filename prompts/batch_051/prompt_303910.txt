Hey — I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on decision theory, and I'm sketching an essay I'd love comments on befor I commit to it. The rough idea is that maximin and minimax regret get taught together as the two big non-probabilistic rules for decision under ignorance, but they really maximize the minimum of two different things: maximin looks at worst-case levels, while minimax regret looks at worst-case shortfalls relative to what you could have gotten in that same state. I want to use that distinction to recast the Rawls/Harsanyi dispute about the original position. If you accept Rawls's no-probabilities-behind-the-veil setup but ask the regret question instead of the level question, you get a third view — something like "choose the principle whose worst-case regret is smallest" — that neither Rawls nor Harsanyi occupies. My hunch is that the fact this third position has no obvious defender shows how much of Rawls's argument depends on the level/shortfall framing, not just on the ban on probabilities. Do you think this is a fair way to frame the debate, or am I overstating how unprepared the literature is for this move? Also, does the level/shortfall distinction do any real work, or is it just a redescription of risk aversion? Let me know which point you're least sure about and how confident you are in the overall read.
