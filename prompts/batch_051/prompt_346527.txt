So I've been falling down a rabbit hole about how archaeologists reconstruct beliefs from artifacts, and one thing keeps bugging me. If a worked bone pin shows up in a grave, the excavation report calls it a ritual object and treats it as evidence for beliefs about death or an afterlife. But if the same kind of pin shows up in a trash pit, it gets listed as everyday gear — a needle or fastener — and nobody reads anything spiritual into it. There's no writing and no one to ask; the only real difference is the context it was buried in. What does the practice of sorting finds into ritual versus practical presuppose about objects, action, and meaning? Does it quietly assume some activities are symbolic in a way that cooking or dumping waste isnt? And is the ritual label actually doing evidential work, or is it partly just the name for what they cant explain by practical use?
