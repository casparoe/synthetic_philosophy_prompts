My professor says the doctrine doesn't require mathematical precision, but that just relocates the question: precision enough for what?

I'm trying to reverse-engineer the picture of legal language and law that the doctrine, as actually applied, is committed to. Does it presuppose that ordinary terms have a settled core and only a fuzzy fringe, so vagueness is fatal only when there's no core at all? Can it tolerate those vague-but-familiar terms consistently with its stated reasons (notice and checking arbitrary enforcement), or is the pattern genuinely in tension? And what would have to change in how criminal statutes are drafted and reviewed if the underlying view of language were false—say, if meaning were radically context-dependent or there was no shared 'ordinary person' understanding to fall back on.
