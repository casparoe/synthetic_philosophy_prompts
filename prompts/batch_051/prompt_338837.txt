I keep noticing that people who would never describe themselves as apologists for authoritarianism still carve out an exception for Singapore. Restrictions on speech, assembly, and opposition that get called tyranny in a poorer, more chaotic country get treated in Singapore as a reasonable trade for stability: the economy works, the streets are safe, the government is competent and mostly clean, so the illiberal stuff reads as a quirk rather than a scandal. Ordinary judgments, in other words, seem to make the legitimacy of undemocratic rule track its results. My question is wether that asymmetry is defensible. Can prosperity and order justify restrictions that would otherwise be illegitimate? If your answer is yes, state the principle in a form that gives Singapore its due without handing a blank check to any regime that jails critics and promises the prosperity is on its way. If your answer is no, then say what the Singapore exception is actually tracking — maybe just a bias toward winners. Either way, I want a verdict, not an it-depends.
