Malthus's classic argument holds that population tends to outrun the food supply: population can grow geometrically while subsistence grows only arithmetically, so some check—vice, misery, or moral restraint—must intervene. The argument appears to assume that improved material conditions get converted into more surviving people. But the demographic transition seems to break that link: in societies where income and education (especially female education) rise, fertility has fallen below replacement and population growth has slowed or reversed without mass starvation. Does Malthus's core argument survive this development, or does it need repair? Say exactly which premise the demographic transition undercuts, whether the argument can be re-expressed so that it still has force (for example about resource use, pollution, or aging and dependency), and what the repaired version would predict.
