I want one essay question on Anselm's *Proslogion* that can't be answered by reheating the textbook version of the ontological argument. In particular, I want students to work with the opening chapter, which most of them skip because it looks like devotional throat-clearing. Here's the passage I want the question to turn on, from Chapter 1:

"Enter into the secret chamber of thy heart: leave everything without but God and what may help thee to seek after Him, and when thou hast shut the door, then do thou seek Him. Say now, O my whole heart, say now to God, I seek Thy face; Thy face, Lord, do I seek. Come now then, O Lord my God, teach Thou my heart when and how I may seek Thee, where and how I may find Thee? O Lord, if Thou art not here, where else shall I seek Thee? but if Thou art everywhere, why do I not behold Thee, since Thou art here present?"

The question you write whch I'd like should make students confront whether the later proof — the "that than which nothing greater can be conceived" argument — is doing something different from this opening. I want the question to push them past the stock objection that existence isn't a predicate, which I'm deliberately not asking about.

Please write the question itself, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain. The exam is closed-book, 90 minutes, and this essay will be worth 25 points. In the model answer, please separate what is settled about Anselm's text from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and don't present a contested reading as the consensus.
