My metaphysics reading group has been going back and forth on endurantism vs perdurantism for months, and I've started to suspect the debate is stuck for reasons nobody in the group is articulating. We all agree on the obvious data: a desk and the lump of clay it's made of can coincide, I survive a haircut, and a single thing can't be both bent and straight at the very same time in the very same respect. The perdurantists keep saying the four-dimensionalist picture handles change, coincidence, and temporary properties more cleanly; the endurantists keep saying temporal parts multiply objects weirdly and do violence to ordinary talk about persons and artifacts. Nobody has moved an inch. My working hypothesis is that the real disagreement isn't about what exists at all but about what counts as the fixed points a theory has to save: one side treats facts about intrinsic change and material coincidence as non-negotiable, the other treats the way we count and refer to ordinary objects as non-negotiable, and each side is happy to swallow counterintuitive results in the other's favored domain. Is that diagnosis right, or is the dispute something else — a verbal disagreement about the word part, or two groups answering different questions under one heading? And if my diagnosis is roughly right, what would progress actually look like: a neutral way to weigh those two kinds of data, a formal result, a new puzzle case, a metasemantic distinction? Or is this just bedrock metaphysical taste, and we should expect no one to move?
