I keep coming back to a reductionist line I used to find pretty convincing: fix the microphysical facts and the micro laws, and every higher-level thing—temperature, a hurricane, maybe a mental state—is nothing over and above those micro facts. That picture leaned hard on a vision of exact derivation: you could in principle crank the macro behavior out of the micro theory step by step, and the macro was just a compressed way of talking about that crunching.

But renormalization-group methods and universality in condensed matter seem to change the situation. Take two pretty different micro theories—different building blocks, different short-range interactions—then coarse-grain them, and many of them flow to the same fixed point: the same large-scale behavior with no dependence on the fine details that made the micro theories distinct in the first place. In many cases the higher-level description is more stable across changes in the micro-level than it is a summary of any particular micro-level. And intervening on macro variables—pressure, an order parameter, whatever—is often the practical way to steer the system too.
