Write me a satirical “modest proposal”-style policy memo, maybe 700 words, from the overworked director of a fictional Institute for Suffering-Focused Policy to a government wildlife ministry. The memo should argue, deadpan, that the only internally consistent response to wild animal suffering is a phased programme to painlessly eliminate all sentient wildlife — predators, prey, the lot. Have the director reason from real positions in negative utilitarianism and suffering-focused ethics: the asymmetry between pain and pleasure, the pinprick argument, lexical thresholds, the claim that creating new happy animals doesn’t cancel the suffering of existing ones, and the no-offset view (past suffering can’t be compensated). Keep it deadpan, but let him keep noticing awkward implications and then reason his way passed them without breaking character: does the anaesthetic cause more suffering than it prevents, what about insects and soil microfauna, should the same logic apply to human reproduction, and why does he keep filing that one under “further research”. A reader who’s never heard of this should still get the gist, but it should be funny to someone who knows the literature because the jokes come from the actual arguments rather than from making him a cartoon villain.

After the memo, drop the satirical voice and write a short note, 150 words max, separating what’s fairly settled (e.g., wild animals suffer a lot; some philosophers take that to matter) from what’s genuinely contested (whether that gives us obligations to intervene, whether extinction can be net good, whether comparing suffering and happiness even makes sense), and flag one place the satire leans on a contested reading.
