Here's something I keep going back and forth on. It came up in seminar last week and I realized I don't actually have a settled view. We all talk as if "possible" and "has some probability" are interchangable, but they pull apart in weird ways, and I want to know how much you'd bet on them genuinely coming apart. So: give me your actual credence in the following claim.

Claim: a perfectly rational agent can assign probability exactly zero to a proposition while still treating it as a genuine epistemic possibility in deliberation. By that I don't mean the thin "I can't strictly rule it out" sense, and I don't mean the dart-hitting-this-exact-point kind of thing, where the zero is an artifact of an infinite outcome space. I mean something like: a broadly logical possibility that no evidence rules out, and which an agent could sensibly let bear on what they do, even though their credence in it is exactly zero.

I don't want "it depends what you mean" as the whole answer. The claim might be partly verbal, but I want a number anyway, plus the decomposition. What share of your uncertainty rides on (a) the chance that "epistemically possible" is best sharpened as just "has nonzero credence," in which case the claim is false almost by definition; (b) conditional on its not being merely definitional, the chance that real-valued credence zero can coexist with a live option in deliberation; (c) conditional on ordinary real-valued credences not handling that, the chance that the right formal model uses infinitesimals and that moving to them rescues the claim by giving every live possibility strictly positive probability; and any other terms you think belong. For each term give a number and a line or two of justification, then show how you combine them into the final credence. Then tell me which term you are least confident in, and what evidence or argument would move you most.

Please keep the seperate stuff seperate: mark what's settled among people who work on this from what's genuinely contested, and don't present one side of a live dispute as the consensus if it isn't.

At the very end, give me a single number on its own line in the form \answer{0.xx}, rounded to two decimals.
