Can you give me a working definition of heritability? I know it isnt just "how genetic a trait is," but I want something precise enough that it doesn't slide back into that. I've got a list of cases I want the definition to get right, or at least say something definite about.

- Height in a well-nourished, genetically mixed population: people routinely say its about 80% heritable. The definition should make that claim non-mysterious.
- Number of fingers: in ordinary environments almost everyone has five, and most of the exceptions are environmental (thalidomide, accidents). I've read that its heritability is near zero even though having five fingers is obviously "genetic" in the everyday sense. The definition should explain why that isnt a contradiction.
- A field of corn plants that are all clones of a single plant. There's still variation in height among them, but all of it has to be environmental. What should the definition say about heritability in that population?
- PKU: a single-gene condition that used to cause severe intellectual disability but is now largely preventable with a restricted diet. I want the definition to make clear why "highly heritable" (if that's even the right thing to say here) doesn't mean "environmental interventions can't change it."
- The one that starts fights: suppose height is 80% heritable within each of two populations, and the two populations differ in average height. Does the definition license any inference about whether that between-population difference is genetic? I'd like the definition to give a definite answer, even if the answer is "the concept is simply silent on this."

Run the definition through each case, say where it gives weird or wrong answers, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. Also, tell me how confident you are in the definition, and flag the one point you're least sure of.
