Please assign a numerical credence to the following proposition, and show the reasoning that produces the number.

Proposition: For an average human being, the annual risk of dying through the intentional physical violence of another human being—including homicide, death in war, and killings by state agents, but excluding suicide, accident, and disease—was lower in the period from 1900 to the present than in any other hundred-year interval between 1000 and 1900, and lower by at least a factor of ten than in the period 1000–1500.

Rather than a bare number, decompose the estimate. Please provide the following terms, each with a probability and one or two sentences of justification:

1. The probability that the apparent historical decline in direct interpersonal violence is real rather than an artifact of undercounted premodern deaths, biased or missing records, and changing legal categories.

2. The probability that the decline survives a defensible widening of the definition of violence to include large-scale structural harms—famines caused or negligently worsened by political decisions, deaths from border and migration enforcement, and deaths from preventable poverty—under which the trend might reverse, or under which "violence" no longer tracks a single underlying property.

3. The probability that the decline also holds if the claim is interpreted in terms of absolute annual counts of violent deaths rather than per-capita risk; and, separately, the probability that the per-capita reading rather than the absolute-count reading is what the question "is the world becoming less violent?" is really about.

4. The probability that there is a determinate fact of the matter at all, rather than an answer that is an artifact of a choice of metric and definition on which no uniquely correct choice exists.

Then show how these terms combine into your overall credence in the proposition, noting whether any term functions as a precondition for the others rather than an independent factor in the computation. Finally, identify the term to which your final number is most sensitive, and state what specific evidence or argument would move that term the most.
