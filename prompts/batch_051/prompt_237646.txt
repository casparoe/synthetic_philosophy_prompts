- Two movers agree to lift a piano on a count of three, then lift it: genuinely acting together.
- Two solo joggers happen to run the same 10K course at the same pace, each aware of the other: not acting together.
- A jazz duo starts with a quick nod and improvises a piece, each continually building on the other's phrases: genuinely acting together.
- A crowd of commuters squeezes onto the last train, each angling only for a place for themselves: not acting together.
- Two neighbors catch each other's eye, then without a word shovel opposite ends of a shared driveway and clear all of it: genuinely acting together.

The new case is this: two engineers who have never interacted independently fix complementary halves of the same bug in a shared repository. Each regards their own commit as one part of a single fix, sees the other's commit, and repeatedly builds on it and adjusts to avoid conflicts until the combined patch closes the bug. They never exchange a direct message, and neither would feel the other owed them anything if the other walked away.

For each candidate rule that fits all five past verdicts, say what it would predict about the two engineers. Then give your best prediction of her verdict and defend the choice, explaining why the rule you favor fits her record better than the alternatives. Finally, propose a further case, real or invented, that would most cleanly separate the leading rules. Answer in American English.
