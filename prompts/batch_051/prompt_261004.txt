I'm giving a five-minute toast at my old advisor's retirement party. He spent most of his career defending modal realism: every possible world is just as real as this one, "actual" is indexical in the way "here" is, an the guy in another world who looks exactly like him is a counterpart of him, not him. Half the room are metaphysicians who'll wince if I slip into "he could have been a lawyer" or "in another life"; the other half are his kids and neighbors who just want a warm toast. Write it so the modal realism does the actual work of shaping the speech — what I can and cannot honestly say about him — and so it lands as genuine affection rather than a lecture. One joke is fine, but only if it gets the metaphysics right.
