A referee pushed back on the interventionist account I defended, and now I realize I might be holding several things at once without having checked whether they fit together. Before I revise, I want an audit. I think I accept all of these:

(a) X causes Y just in case Y would change under an appropriate intervention on X; there's nothing more to causation than invariance under interventions.
(b) But "appropriate intervention" can't be cashed out in non-causal terms: an intervention on X with respect to Y has to affect Y only through X, be independent of the other causes of Y, and override whatever else influences X—each of those tests is causal through and through.
(c) I get off the boat when an analysis is circular. If an account of causation uses causal ideas to explain causal ideas, it hasn't told me what causation is.
(d) Still, I don't want a deflationary story on which causal talk in science is just a useful instrument; there are real causal facts, and interventionist reasoning seems to me the best way to get at them.
(e) And I want to say the moon causes the tides even though no one can intervene on the moon; a merely possible intervention should be enough.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and whats the smallest change that makes the whole set tenable? Please rank the tensions by severity.
