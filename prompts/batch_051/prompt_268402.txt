Me and a friend argued for like two hours last night about whether Gen Z is a real thing or just a made-up marketing category. She kept saying everything people attribute to Gen Z is either an age effect (people in their early twenties have always been broke, online, and anxious about the future) or a period effect (the pandemic and smartphones hit everyone at once). I went home and wrote up my side as a numbered argument. I think its airtight; she says step 2 is doing all the work and I haven't earned it. Can you tell me if the argument is valid, and if not, exactly which step fails and why?

(1) If "Gen Z" names a real kind, then at least one feature of its members must be a cohort effect: something attributable to being Gen Z over and above age effects and period effects.

(2) There is no such feature. For each trait people attribute to Gen Z — being extremely online, economic pessimism, anxiety, progressive attitudes — the data can be fully explained by age, by period, or by age-plus-period together.

(3) If there is no cohort effect over and above age and period, then "Gen Z" is just a name for an age-band and a period-band crossing, and a crossing like that is not a real kind.

(4) So "Gen Z" has no cohort effect of its own.

(5) Therefore Gen Z is not a real kind.

My friend says step 2 is the problem, because of the age-period-cohort conundrum: cohort plus age equals period (cohort = period − age), so the three are perfectly collinear, and you can never pull out a unique cohort effect without adding arbitrary assumptions. So, she says, I can't establish that there's no cohort effect. I think the conundrum actually helps me: if no unique cohort effect can even be identified, the concept of one is doing no work. Where does the argument actually fail, if it does? Be specific about the step and the reading of it.
