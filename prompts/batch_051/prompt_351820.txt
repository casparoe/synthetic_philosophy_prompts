My parents recently told me that I will inherit substantially more than I expected, and the news has forced me to think through an argument about inheritance that I cannot seem to reject, although I want to.

(1) A society secures equal opportunity only if two children of equal ability and effort have roughly the same life prospects regardless of their parents' wealth.
(2) Inherited wealth is a parental fact no child controls, and it materially shapes a child's prospects through education, housing, social networks, and the practical freedom to take occupational or entrepreneurial risks.
(3) Therefore, a society that allows wealth to pass from parents to children untaxed fails to provide equal opportunity to the next generation.
(4) Inheritance taxes reduce the extent to which parental advantage is transmitted across generations.
(5) So, other things equal, inheritance taxes move a society closer to equal opportunity.
(6) Hence, if equal opportunity is a requirement of justice, justice requires at least this much taxation of inherited wealth.

I find the conclusion unwelcome given my own position, yet I cannot identify the error. Is the argument valid? If not, exactly which step fails, and how should the offending premise be stated correctly? If the argument is valid, say which premise someone who resists the conclusion would have to reject and why it is harder to reject than it first appears.
