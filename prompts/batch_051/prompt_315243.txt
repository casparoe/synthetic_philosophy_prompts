A friend of mine is building a tiny private forum for discussing what he calls antimemes—ideas that resist being remembered or repeated—and in particular for studying genuinely infectious bad ideas (panic rumors, cult slogans, demoralization clichés) without making them easier to spread. The house rules he's proposed: you can only post about a source at least 24 hours after first reading it; no quotes, screenshots, copy-paste, or links to the source; you must restate from memory in your own words; an automated checker rejects any post above 70% lexical overlap with the source or with any already-accepted post; the most accurate low-overlap restatement each week, as chosen by the two moderators, earns "mnemonic credit," which is the only currency that buys access to the archived originals; verbatim or near-verbatim repetition triggers a posting cooldown.

I want this analyzed as a machine, not judged morally. What does it incentivize among posters, lurkers, the moderators, and whoever tunes the similarity checker? Over a year, which ideas survive mostly intact, which mutate into something else, and who ends up holding the reliable memory of the material? What breaks first—or rather, where do people start gaming it—and can that be fixed without gutting the anti-memetic point? Also please mark clearly what's reasonably established about memory, transmission, and norm enforcement versus what's your own speculation or a genuinely contested point; dont present a contested reading as if it were settled.
