So my brother and I have this reccuring argument and I'm hoping someone can tell me what's actually going on, because I can't find the flaw in either of our rules.

We live together and we basically run the house on two principles. First, each of us has final say over his own body and his own stuff, so nobody can force the other to eat something, do something, etc. Second, if we both prefer one option over another, that's the one we go with. Both of these feel totally reasonable on their own.

Last night it was a slice of pizza that had been sitting out on the counter overnight. I'm a little cautious about that kind of thing. I'd honestly rather no one eat it. But if somebody has to, I'd rather eat it myself than let my brother risk getting sick, cause I'd feel awful if he did. My brother thinks I'm being paranoid, and he'd actually prefer that I just eat the damn pizza and stop making such a big deal out of it. If I won't, he'll eat it himself before he lets it get thrown out, and his least favorite option is tossing it.

Here's where it gets weird. If we go by our rules, no one eating it beats me eating it, because it's my call whether I put that pizza in my own mouth and I'd say no. But me eating it beats him eating it, because we both prefer that. I'd rather it be me than him, and he'd rather I have it than him. Yet him eating it beats no one eating it, because it's his call whether he eats it and he'd rather eat it than waste it. So we get a loop: nobody beats me, me beats him, him beats nobody. Every option loses to something.

This isn't really about the pizza, it keeps showing up whenever one of us has a say over his own thing but the other one also has a stake in the outcome. Are our two rules somehow incompatible? Should we drop one, or is there some third rule that fixes the loop? I want to know what's actually wrong here.
