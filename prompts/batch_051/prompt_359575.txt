Longtime rationalist-blog lurker here. I've been reading the public moderation log of a small forum that cloned the old Slashdot-style karma system — post karma and comment karma, thresholds for downvote weight, a monthly token distribution — and I'm trying to reverse-engineer the unwritten policy their "vote integrity" crew actually runs. I'm not asking what the *right* policy is, I want a prediction about what *they* would do, plus the rule that makes the most sense of their track record.

The precedents, compressed from the logs:

1. Alice linked her own post on a big off-site platform with "go upvote this, important read." A wave of new accounts showed up and upvoted. Mods stripped the post's entire karma and pinned a brigading notice but didn't ban her.
2. Ben offered 200 of his own forum tokens to anyone who upvoted a governance proposal. Mods zeroed the karma, banned him, and explicitly said they didn't care whether the proposal was good: buying votes is manipulation, full stop.
3. Carol ran two obvious alt accounts that added early upvotes to each new post to get it past the new-post visibility threshold. Mods removed the alt votes, kept the organic ones, and issued a warning.
4. Dave, in the weekly "post your own stuff" thread, asked regulars to read his new post and "upvote if you found it useful, downvote if you didn't." No action; a mod commented that this was exactly what the thread was for.
5. Erin posted a one-line joke in the middle of a Very Serious debate. It got more upvotes than every serious contribution in the thread combined. No action.
6. Frank's post depended on a statistic that later turned out to be wrong, and it got heavily upvoted before the correction. He edited a correction into the top of the post. Mods left the karma alone and pinned a correction comment.

Now the case I'm trying to call. Sam, a regular and generally respected member, writes a fairly standard op-ed on a cause and ends it with: "I'll donate $3 to a malaria charity for every upvote on this post, up to $600." The post gets hundreds of upvotes fast, and several comments openly say "I disagree with your actual argument, but take the upvote for the donation."

I can see at least two rules that fit the six precedents. One says all that matters is whether the votes came from real, regular members who weren't being paid; on that rule Sam's post is fine, since the voters aren't getting the money. Another says upvotes have to function as evalutions of the post, not as tokens for some other purpose, so votes that are really donations corrupt the signal and should be stripped the same way paid votes are. Maybe there's a third rule I'm missing.

Which rule do you think the mods are actually running, what do they do in Sam's case, which of the six precedents is the closest analog, and what further case would force the two readings apart? If you think they'd split the difference somehow — say, keeping only the karma from before the donation edit or counting only the first N upvotes — what principle would that actually express?
