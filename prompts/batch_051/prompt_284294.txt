I'm putting together a short paper on what's actually wrong with free riding, and before I commit to the structure I want someone to stress-test the set of claims I keep falling back on. I find myself drawn to all of these:

1. Free riding is wrong because it's unfair, not because it causes harm — my skipped fare or underpaid tax makes no palpable difference to anyone else's life.

2. But not every instance of taking a benefit without contributing is wrong. I dont owe anything to the guy with the speaker in the park, and I don't have to chip in for my neighbor's fireworks just because I can see them from my window.

3. The unfairness in the fare or tax case comes from the fact that these are ongoing cooperative schemes whose participants provide the good on terms of shared contribution; you don't need to have signed anything — being inside the scheme is enough.

4. I don't think a conscientious refuser who stops paying taxes to protest her government is a free rider, even though she still uses the roads and courts that other people's taxes pay for.

5. And I deny that the duty to contribute switches on only once your individual contribution would make a difference — the fact that the bus runs anyway or the war gets funded anyway is irrelevant to whether I'm obligated.

Is this package consistent? Which of these genuinely pull against each other, which only seem to, and what's the cheapest single revision that would do the most work? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and tell me what I'd have to give up.
