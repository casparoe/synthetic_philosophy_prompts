I've been chewing on a little argument that says it's *always* a mistake to let sunk costs influence you, and I can't tell whether I've actually proved something or just dressed up the conclusion as a premise. The argument runs:

(1) A rational decision should be based only on what will happen as a consequence of the available options.
(2) A sunk cost is a cost that has already been paid and cant be recovered no matter which option you pick.
(3) So a sunk cost doesn't differ across the available options — it will be gone either way.
(4) So a sunk cost shouldn't affect how you rank the options.
(5) Therefore, honoring sunk costs — staying in a degree you hate because you've done three years of it, pouring more money into a failing project, refusing to sell a losing stock — is always irrational.

Feels airtight to me, but then I think about someone who finishes the degree to avoid looking flaky to future employers, and that seems like a *forward-looking* consideration, not a sunk cost at all. So is the argument valid? If not, which exact step fails, and on what reading of "irrational" does the conclusion actually follow? Answer in American English.
