Construct a thought experiment with the following target: the whole-brain criterion and the circulatory-respiratory criterion of death should both eventually deem a patient dead, but assign different times of death, with the circulatory criterion assigning the earlier one. This should be the reverse of the familiar mismatch in which a brain-dead patient on a ventilator is pronounced dead under the whole-brain criterion while circulation continues. The point is to isolate whether a decision not to intervene can shift one criterion's time of death without shifting the other's.

Keep the scenario to one paragraph, set in an ordinary contemporary hospital, with no exotic technology. The load-bearing feature should be a valid do-not-resuscitate order, and the cardiac arrest should be of the sort that prompt CPR would clearly have reversed, so that the stoppage of circulation is "permanent" (it will not resume, because no one will act to resume it) without being "irreversible" (it could have been resumed, had someone acted). Let the circulatory-respiratory criterion count permanent stoppage as enough for death, while the whole-brain criterion requires that all brain functions have ceased and cannot return.

After the scenario, show your work: the verdict each criterion delivers, the time of death it yields, and the reasoning behind it. Then say which element of the case is doing the work, what happens to the time-of-death disagreement if the do-not-resuscitate order is removed and the same arrest is instead promptly reversed, and how a defender of the whole-brain criterion would most fairly attack the earlier time. Finish with a one-sentence summary as the last line.
