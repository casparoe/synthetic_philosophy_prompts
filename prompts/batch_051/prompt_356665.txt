I have been trying to make my views on price controls coherent, and I keep finding that I hold them at different levels of generality. In the abstract I accept the standard line that a binding price ceiling creates a shortage and a binding price floor creates a surplus, with the usual downstream distortions: misallocation, quality degradation, queuing, black markets. But my confidence collapses in concrete cases. I am much friendlier to a legislative cap on insulin than to a cap on concert-ticket resale, even though both are ceilings on genuinely scarce goods, and I suspect my intuitions are tracking who I imagine bearing the loss more than any economic principle. Conversations with friends have only made this clearer: our disagreements rarely turn on estimates of elasticity and mostly turn on whether we or someone close to us has had to buy the good in question.

I would like a forced ranking that exposes the structure of the judgment rather than a verdict delivered from orthodox economics. Consider these six policies: (1) a binding minimum wage set moderately above the market-clearing rate for low-wage labor; (2) rent stabilization that ties annual increases to a local consumer price index, with vacancy decontrol when a tenant moves out; (3) anti-price-gouging statutes that cap prices for bottled water, generators, and hotel rooms during a declared natural disaster; (4) a legislated cap on insulin list prices set at some fixed multiple of production cost; (5) usury laws capping consumer loan APRs at, say, 36 percent; and (6) a cap on resale prices for event tickets at face value. Please rank these six from most to least morally defensible all things considered, and assign each a score out of 10. If your ranking depends on empirical magnitudes, state the assumptions under which you are scoring each policy. Then, in a sentence or two per item, say whether its position is driven mostly by the size of the allocative harm, by who bears the loss, by whose claim to the good you treat as prior, or by how administrable the control would be. Finally, tell me which single item is most likely to change tiers under the largest reasonable empirical variation, and which is most stable.
