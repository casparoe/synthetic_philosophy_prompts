I've been trying to build a proper outside-view forecast for how much longer the current international order has left—I mean the arrangement where one country is visibly dominant, call it the present unipolar moment. The obvious method is to look at past great powers and ask how long they lasted once they got to roughly where the current one is now, but the reference class problem keeps wrecking me. My gut accepts some reference classes and rejects others, and I cant tell if there's one principle behind the pattern or if I've just got a pile of vibes.

Here are my verdicts as they currently stand:

1. Taking the full set of states and empires in recorded history: no. There are too many city-states, micro-polities, and short-lived conquest regimes; the base rate comes out meaningless and I don't feel bound by it, even though a strict outside-view voice in my head says I should be.
2. Restricting to great powers only—say, polities that at their peak represented something like 10% of world GDP or the rough military equivalent: yes, this feels like the class I actually care about.
3. Restricting to the same institutional form, i.e. only democracies or only federations: no. I don't see why constitutional form would be a major driver of how long hegemony lasts, and the sample basically evaporates.
4. Restricting to the modern period only, however you draw the line: I flip-flop. Technology and nationalism are real discontinuities, but the further you restrict era the more the outside view stops being a base rate and starts being a story about one or two famous cases.
5. Restricting to the decline mechanism you're actually worried about—in this case peaceful internal decay rather than defeat in major war: no, even though that's exactly the scenario I'm trying to forecast. It feels like picking the cases by how they came out, which will flatter the number and gut the whole point of using history.
6. Abandoning the class and using the single closest historical analogy: no, that's just the inside view in a costume.
7. Keeping all great powers but weighting them by similarity to the target case: I want this one to be true, but I dont know where the weights come from, and I worry they just let me smuggle my preferred conclusion back in.

What I want from you is the principle, if any, that captures these verdicts. Please state it precisely, including what it says about two further cases I keep stalling on: restricting the class to powers that had already survived at least as long as the current one has by this point, and excluding cases that were already visibly declining at their start date. Then tell me honestly which of my verdicts the principle can't accommodate, and whether I should change that verdict or complicate the principle instead.
