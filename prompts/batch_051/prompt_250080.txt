Was arguing with a friend last night about whether the golden rule can be saved, and he kept hitting me with the same four objections. I wrote them down so I dont lose track:

(1) The masochist objection: if I would genuinely want someone to hurt me, the rule seems to say I should hurt them, which is absurd.
(2) The preference-mismatch objection: people often don't want what I would want in their shoes. I'd want a surprise party; my roommate would be mortified. The rule gives the wrong answer whenever tastes diverge.
(3) The role objection: some relationships are asymmetrical, and the rule ignores that. A judge who followed it literally might let a defendant off because the judge wouldn't want to be locked up.
(4) The fanatic objection: if someone would sincerely want to be corrected or converted for their own good, the rule seems to license them to go around correcting and converting everyone else, no matter how unwelcome it is.

Im trying to figure out whether there's a fifth well-known objection I'm missing — one that doesn't reduce to divergent preferences, roles, or fanaticism, and isn't just a restatement, special case, or combination of these. If there is, what is it, and does it actually land?
