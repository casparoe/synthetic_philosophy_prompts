You're a legal and political philosopher working on how constitutional orders handle gaps and interregna in their own rules. Think the concepts through carefully before you write, maybe by brainstorming the moving parts first.

One influential account, associated with H.L.A. Hart, says law isn't just a pile of rules; it also includes secondary rules — rules about how rules get made, changed, and identified. On this picture, when a secondary rule is silent or indeterminate, what settles the question of valid law isn't a fancier reading of the text but the convergent practice and acceptance of the relevant officials (roughly, a social rule of recognition).

Use that framework to analyze the Tyler precedent. In 1841, William Henry Harrison died about a month into his presidency. The constitutional provision on succession was maddeningly unclear about wheter the vice president became the president outright or merely assumed the powers and duties of the office as acting president. John Tyler just took the presidential oath and insisted he was fully the President. Congress and the cabinet mostly went along, and his reading hardened into precedent.

Your job is a two-way analysis. First, run the case through the Hart-style lens: who are the relevant officials, what exactly was the indeterminate secondary rule, and what did their acceptance settle? Say whether the theory has anything to say about what Tyler or Congress should have done, or whether it goes quiet on the normative question. Second, flip it around: which features of the episode does this picture explain well, and which does it miss or distort — the raw politics, Tyler's personal stake, the legitimacy anxieties, the personality of the actors? Finally, judge whether the episode is better read as a confirmation of the Hart-style view, an anomaly for it, or a case it never really anticipated. Be explicit about where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak.
