I write descriptive grammars of underdocumented languages for a cross-linguistic database, and I am responsible for the inflectional morphology chapter. For every apparent irregularity in a paradigm, I have to assign one of four database codes: productive rule; rule with listed exceptions; suppletive; or irregular residue. These codes drive typological queries by other researchers, so a wrong call — coding English go/went as a phonologically conditioned rule rather than suppletion, or foot/feet as a rule rather than listed umlaut — quietly corrupts work I never see. My materials are realistic for fieldwork: a few thousand elicited and recorded forms per language, some metalinguistic judgments, no experimental production data, and no way to test novel words with native speakers. I can spend about two hours per paradigm, and the database editor reviews a sample of my codings, so the procedure must be reproducible and defensible to a skeptical colleague who thinks I over-count rules.

Please turn what is actually known about what a grammatical rule is — in particular, the difference between a rule and a list, and the difference between an exception and a counterexample — into a decision procedure I can follow. I want a fixed sequence of tests with the order justified, a statement of which corpus evidence distinguishes each code, and an explicit mark of the steps where judgment cannot be eliminated. The procedure should handle at least these hard cases: (1) when a pocket of exceptions shows a rule is false rather than lexically restricted; (2) when two forms are best coded as suppletive even though they partly resemble each other; (3) when an apparent exception is the regular outcome of a sound change that applied only in a describable environment; and (4) when the same surface pattern should be split into two different rules rather than one rule with two conditions. Work the procedure through two or three cases — at least one familiar case and at least one field case you construct — and make one of them come out with a code you expect me to resist, explaining why the procedure nonetheless gives it. End with what the procedure cannot decide for me and what I should say to the editor in those cases.
