I've been arguing with my friend Elena about whether a translation can be the inspired word of God, and I've started keeping a list of her verdicts because she's maddeningly consistent. She treats the Septuagint, the old Greek rendering of the Hebrew Bible, as genuinely inspired — partly because the early church used it as its Old Testament, and partly because of the old story that the translators worked independently and their finished versions came out matching word for word. She cheerfully admits the Septuagint departs from the Hebrew in places and doesn't think that matters. She's also basically KJV-only about English: the King James Version is inspired, full stop, while modern critical translations like the NRSV are just study aids, not the word of God. And free paraphrases like The Message are right out, no matter how moving, because at that point you're basically reading commentary.

The new case: a conservative denomination in a small country spent fifteen years producing a careful, fairly literal translation of the New Testament into the local language, done by a team of trained linguists using the best available manuscripts. Nobody involved claims any miraculous guidance — they'd roll their eyes at the idea. But the translation has now been read liturgically, preached from, and memorized across the whole region for two generations, and for most believers there it just is the Bible. Would Elena call it inspired? I'm less interested in the bare yes or no than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her past verdicts equally well, and which further case would cleanly separate them. I keep going back and forth between three test cases: (a) a translation produced under claimed miraculous circumstances but never adopted by any actual congregation, (b) the two-generations-old regional translation I just described, and (c) an ultra-literal translation that tracks the original wording as closely as possible but has never been read in church. What does each candidate rule predict about all three, and which single case is the real discriminator?
