For my intro ethics class I'm putting together a unit on the golden rule — I want to show them why the treat-others-as-you'd-be-treated principle is a lot less decisive than they think, and I'd rather do it by making them laugh than by lecturing. Write me a satirical FAQ page, deadpan customer-service voice, around 500-600 words, for a fictional app called DoUnto that automates the golden rule: the user enters there own preferences, and the app tells them exactly how to treat everyone else.

The help-center page should include at least these four questions:

- A masochist who can't figure out why DoUnto keeps telling him to smack his coworkers, and the support reply treats this as the system working exactly as designed.
- A zealot who's delighted that the app has concluded he should keep trying to convert his neighbors, since he'd obviously want to be converted if he were in the wrong; the reply congratulates him on his rigorous consistency.
- A gym-goer who's confused because who's grandmother blocked him after weeks of unsolicited form corrections, since he'd genuinely want someone to correct his form; the reply offers no fix and maybe suggests a gift card.
- A follow-up from the masochist after support pushes the new "negative formulation" mode (do not do what you would hate), noting that the app still tells him to smack his coworkers because he doesn't happen to hate being smacked; support replies that this is a preference-onboarding issue, not a bug.

The jokes have to come from the actual structure of the golden rule — that it's a consistency test with no substantive content of its own, that it universalizes whatever preferences you happen to have, and that the positive formulation is especially good at turning one person's private taste into everyone else's problem. Don't state those objections in the FAQ; let the deadpan help-desk answers walk right into them. Also don't quote Kant or anyone real; make up the customer-service boilerplate and any fake user quotes. I don't want it winking at the reader — the fun is that the support team is sincerely baffled by the complaints.

Give me maybe 4-6 Q&As, a little header for the FAQ, and a fake "related articles" line at the bottom.
