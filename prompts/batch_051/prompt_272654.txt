im in year 12 and help out with the school literary magazine, which does all its workshopping online. We keep having the same fight: someone posts a reading of a poem or story, the author turns up and says actually that's not what I meant, and then everyone argues about whether the author gets the final say. One of the editors has now proposed a formal rule: every accepted piece must include an author's note stating what the piece means, and in all discussion on the site (comments, workshop threads, the letters page) nobody is allowed to attribute a meaning to the piece that conflicts with the note. If you think the words support a reading the author didn't mention, you have to call it a reading the words support, not what the piece means. Authors can edit their note at any time, but the edits are logged and displayed next to the piece, and the text itself can't be changed once it's published, so over time the note can drift away from the original piece.

I'm not asking for the usual English essay about whether the author is dead or whatever. I want to treat this as a rule for a community and work through how it would actually function. What would it do to how authors write their notes, how critics behave, what gets submitted, and who ends up with power over the discussion? Where does it get gamed first, and do small fixes help or just shift the problem around? Please write in British English.
