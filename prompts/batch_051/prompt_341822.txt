Ok so I've been trying to get clear on what utility actually is, and I wrote down my current commitments to see if they make a coherent package. I'd like a consistency check before I bring this to a reading group.

Here's where I've landed:

(1) Utility is not a mental state like pleasure or desire strength; it's just a number that represents someone's preferences.
(2) Revealed preference is basically the whole story: to say that x has higher utility for me than y just is to say that, under normal conditions, I would choose x over y. There's nothing else hidden behind the choice.
(3) But choice only reveals preference when things are going well—full information, no addiction, no framing tricks, no weakness of will.
(4) For welfare purposes, we should generally take people's actual choices as authoritative, even when the choices look self-destructive.
(5) Still, I can't shake the thought that some preferences are adaptive: a person who has stopped wanting something because it's out of reach isn't better off for not wanting it, even if they'd now choose not to pursue it.
(6) Utility is ordinal only; interpersonal comparisons of utility are meaningless.

I suspect there's a real tension in here, but I don't want to bias you by naming it. Please go through the pairs and tell me which are actually in tension, which only look like they are, how severe each real tension is, and what the cheapest fix would be. If there's one revision that would clean up the most, tell me that too. And as you do this, please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
