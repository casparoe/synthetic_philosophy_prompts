For a paper on free will I want to check an argument of my own against a careful audience. Start with the stock example of sphexishness. The digger wasp Sphex performs an elaborate sequence when depositing a paralyzed cricket: it drags the cricket to the burrow entrance, leaves it there, goes inside alone to inspect, comes back out, and only then drags the cricket in. If someone moves the cricket a few inches while the wasp is inside, the wasp comes back out, drags the cricket to the entrance again, and goes inside to inspect once more; move the cricket again and it repeats the routine. It can keep this up rather than simply pull the cricket straight in. The behavior looks planful on first viewing, but it is rigid.

Here is the argument:

(1) What makes the wasp's behavior seem mechanical and unfree is that it is controlled by a fixed routine, one that cannot register the moved cricket as a reason to revise its sequence.
(2) A being acts freely only if its behavior is controlled by a structure of the opposite kind: one that can update its representation of the circumstances and change the plan accordingly.
(3) Humans do update representations and change plans; that is precisely the basis of the everyday contrast between our flexibility and the wasp's rigidity.
(4) But any such representational updating must be implemented in some rule-governed physical system — the brain, which, like any physical system, unfolds according to deterministic (or at least probabilistically lawful) rules on its physical states.
(5) So our flexible planning is, at bottom, no less the unfolding of a fixed causal routine than the wasp's sequence is.
(6) Therefore the difference between human flexibility and wasp rigidity is only a difference of complexity, not of kind; and if the wasp is unfree in virtue of the feature identified in (1), humans are unfree in virtue of the same underlying feature.

I have a nagging sense that this argument commits a level-confusion — that 'fixed routine' means one thing in (1)–(3) and another in (4)–(5) — but I cannot make the diagnosis precise. Is the argument valid? If valid, are the premises true? If invalid, identify the exact step that fails, give the strongest reading of that step on which the argument remains plausible, and state what the correct version of the principle behind (2) would have to say before (6) could follow.
