I work on a product team whose models try to infer what users want from what they click, buy, and bypass, on the assumption that choices made under cost reveal preferences more reliably than anything a user reports in a survey. Over the past couple of years I have found myself carrying that rule into my personal life. When someone tells me that they value something — their health, a friendship, a creative project, a cause — I no longer treat the statement as evidence of much at all. I wait to see whether they spend time, money, or social standing on it, and only then do I update my picture of what they want. The clearest case is a friend who has told me for two years that she wants to leave her job and write full-time, but who has twice had realistic exit paths and did not take them. I have stopped treating her announced desire as a claim about her preferences; I treat it as a mood or an aspiration that is not costing her anything to express. Our mutual friends think this inverts the evidence: on their view, her statements are the best access we have to her desires, and her inaction is explained by fear, inertia, and the genuine difficulty of large change, not by a lack of preference.

I would like to know whether this habit is defensible, or whether it is a bias dressed up as a decision rule. Framed generally: under what conditions is costly behavior a strictly better signal of a person's preferences than that person's stated preferences, and under what conditions does testimony carry information that behavior cannot? What assumptions does the move from observed choice to underlying preference require — about noise, about whether a single observed choice reveals a stable preference rather than one element in a bundle of competing concerns, about whether people have privileged access to their own motivational states, and about how behavior and self-report should be combined rather than simply ranked? I am not principally asking about this one friend, though she prompted the question; I am asking whether the general policy can be restated so that it keeps whatever signal behavior provides without discarding what people say.
