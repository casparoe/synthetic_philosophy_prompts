I chair the rules committee of our city council, and a member has moved to amend our rules of procedure so that whenever a councilor argues for or against a measure, any reason that rests on religious doctrine must be accompanied by at least one reason stated in terms a reasonable person who does not share that doctrine could accept. The sponsor says a body whose decisions bind everyone must justify those decisions to all reasonable citizens rather than only to those who already accept a particular religion; an opponent says the amendment would force religious members to translate their convictions into an approved idiom before they may speak, making them second-class participants. We meet Thursday, and I need a draft decision memo. Recommend whether we should adopt, reject, or amend the proposal; if adopting or amending, specify workable criteria for applying the rule; and give an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side, not just its slogan. Argue from the values that should govern democratic deliberation rather than from any particular constitutional or statutory text.
