I sit on the board of a small nonprofit that manages a repair fund for publicly owned rental housing. Last year we discovered that one of our staff accountants, over nearly a decade, had never once misdirected money she could easily have diverted; the complication is that every disbursement she handled was logged in a system she believed was being watched, and the books were audited monthly by an outside firm. In a board discussion about whether to honor her at our annual dinner, a fellow board member argued that she does not deserve a reputation for integrity, because "integrity is doing the right thing even when no one is watching," and in her case someone effectively always was watching. I find the slogan persuasive at the level of a slogan, but I cannot get clear on what precise claim it is making about her. It seems to run together claims about literal observation, probable detection, incentives, acting from principle rather than from hope of credit or fear of exposure, being the same person in public and private, and being unbribeable. Act as a professional philosopher working in moral psychology and virtue ethics. Take the question seriously, and begin by brainstorming a range of candidate precisifications of the slogan before committing to any. Then, for each candidate reading, give (i) a careful statement of the thesis, (ii) a verdict on whether it is trivial, plausible but substantive, or false as a general claim about integrity, and (iii) whether that thesis would actually support my fellow board member's conclusion that the accountant should not be honored for integrity. I especially want to know which precisifications concern what a person is actually like here and now, as opposed to what the person would have done under counterfactual monitoring conditions, since only the former can license a judgment about her character. End with a final line beginning "Verdict:" that states in one sentence which reading is doing the real work in the board member's argument, and whether the argument goes through on that reading.
