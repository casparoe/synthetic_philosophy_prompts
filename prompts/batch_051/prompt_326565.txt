I find myself committed to a cluster of views about truth-seeking, tradition, and purity, and I want a consistency check before I put them in writing. The claims, as I would currently state them, are these.

(a) Truth-seeking, fidelity to a tradition, and purity — by which I mean the maintenance of boundaries against what would contaminate or corrupt a practice or community — each strike me as worth pursuing for their own sake, and not only for the further benefits they bring.

(b) In each case, though, the strongest case I can actually make is instrumental: truth-tracking beliefs improve prediction and coordination; traditions store hard-won tacit knowledge and stabilize cooperation without anyone having to rederive it; purity norms protect groups and practices from parasitic, contaminating, or corrupting influences.

(c) For practices and institutions generally, I hold a broadly consequentialist standard: a practice is justified only when, and because, it makes lives go better overall than the feasible alternatives, where better is understood in welfare terms rather than in antecedently moralized terms.

(d) I also believe that these particular values tend to work best when they are not held merely instrumentally. Someone who treats truth as optional whenever it is inconvenient, or tradition as dead weight whenever it slows change, or purity as nothing but superstition, tends to lose the very benefits that justify them.

(e) If something is worth having only as a means, a rational person should regard it as replaceable: whenever the underlying benefit can be obtained more cheaply or reliably another way, they should be ready to set the value aside.

Please audit this set rather than survey positions I could have cited. Which commitments are actually in tension, and which pairs only look as though they are once the relevant distinctions are drawn? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what the least costly revision would cost.
