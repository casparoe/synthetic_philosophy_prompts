I went down a rabbit hole the other night reading about the East India Company, and now I cant stop thinking about a weird double standard. Nobody today thinks it was legitimate for a private, for-profit chartered company to raise an army, collect taxes from millions of people, negotiate treaties, run courts, and govern a huge territory; we all file that under corporate overreach, or worse. But list what a modern state does and its basically the same list: standing army, taxation, diplomacy, courts, territorial rule. And the continuity isnt even hidden. When the British government took over the Company's Indian territories, it kept the same administrative machinery and carried on ruling them. So heres the puzzle. If "a private organization exercising sovereign powers" is an outrage, why does the same package of powers become legitimate the moment we call the organization a state?

Dont just say that states serve the public good and companies serve profit, because thats exactly what's in question: plenty of states have served a narrow clique, and the Company always insisted it was bringing peace, law and good order. I want the strongest possible justification for the asymmetry, and I want it tested against the hard case where the difference between a chartered company and a state comes down to ownership structure and the name on the building. If the asymmetry cant be defended, say so, and tell me whether that means the ordinary condemnation of the Company and the ordinary acceptance of the state are just an inconsistency weve inherited.
