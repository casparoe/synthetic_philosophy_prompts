In a philosophy of biology seminar a fellow student quoted the phrase "survival of the fittest" and then dismissed natural selection as empty: if the fittest are just the ones that survive, the principle says nothing more than "survivors survive," and so cannot explain adaptation. I know enough to suspect this is too fast, and I dimly recall that something called the propensity interpretation of fitness is supposed to be relevant, but I cannot state that reply with any precision. Take "survival of the fittest" and lay out the different precise claims it could be taken to make — about who lives and dies, about actual reproductive success, about expected reproductive success, about organismic dispositions in a given environment, about the spread of trait types — and evaluate each: which readings are trivially true, which are substantive and defensible, which are false, and which are the ones the tautology objection really targets. Then say which reading a propensity interpretation of fitness involves, and whether adopting it is enough to make the slogan non-tautological without collapsing it back into a mere definition.
