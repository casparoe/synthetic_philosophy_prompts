Here's an argument I keep hearing from people who think perception is shot through with belief, and I can't tell if its actually good. (1) Perception is cognitively penetrated when a person's beliefs or expectations make them see the same physical stimulus differently from someone without those beliefs. (2) A trained radiologist looking at a chest X-ray sees the tumor; a novice looking at the same X-ray just sees blurry gray shapes. (3) The only difference between them is the radiologist's stored knowledge and expectations about what shadows and white spots mean. (4) So the same physical stimulus produces different visual experiences in the two of them, and it does so because of cognition. (5) Therefore perception is cognitively penetrated. I've heard someone reply that the radiologist's expertise gets baked into the visual system over years, and that this isn't the same as an occurrent belief or expectation reaching in and changing how things look, so step (3) is too quick. But I don't see why that should matter: either way, the person's cognitive background is making the difference to what they see. So is the argument valid? If not, say exactly which step fails, and what the distiction between baked-in expertise and occurrent expectation does to the argument.
