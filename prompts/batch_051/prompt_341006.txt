Benatar's asymmetry is the claim that, for a person who never exists, the absence of pain is good even though no one enjoys that good, whereas the absence of pleasure is not bad because there is no one to be deprived of it. It is usually treated as an axiological claim that one either sees or fails to see. I want to know how much empirical traction we can get on it anyway. Design the strongest test you can of the hypothesis that this asymmetry is a stable feature of people's evaluative thinking rather than an artifact of how the antinatalist conclusion is motivated or described. What untutored judgments, comparison cases, or forced choices would you elicit; what pattern of results would count for or against the asymmetry; and what, exactly, would those results fail to settle about whether the asymmetry is true? I am less interested in whether people can repeat the asymmetry after it is explained to them than in whether it shows up in their judgments before the dialectical stakes are visible.
