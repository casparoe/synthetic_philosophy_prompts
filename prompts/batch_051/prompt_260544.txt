I'm working on a piece about the euhemerist habit — named for Euhemerus, an ancient Greek writer who argued that the gods began as great humans, kings and benefactors, who got promoted into divinities after they died. I keep finding people mean quite different things by euhemerism, so I'd like a working definition of it — roughly, the view that a god or hero started out as a flesh-and-blood human being — that I can actually use to sort stories.

Cases I keep worrying over:

- Augustus, a real emperor whom the Senate formally made a god when he died, with temples and priests.
- Imhotep, a real Egyptian vizier and architect who, centuries after his death, came to be worshipped as a god of medicine.
- Paul Bunyan, a giant lumberjack from American tall tales who was never a real person and never worshipped.
- A local mountain spirit that nobody ever claimed had once been human.
- King Arthur, who maybe goes back to a real fifth- or sixth-century British warlord but was never the object of worship.
- A friend's great-grandfather, who definitely existed and now gets offerings and small requests at a family altar.
- A folk saint whose existence is well documented but whose miracle stories are plainly later embroidery.

Give me the cleanest definition you can. Then run it against each case and say honestly where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. It would also help if you said where euhemerism stops being mere inflated biography of a real person and starts being actual deification — or wether that line can't be drawn.
