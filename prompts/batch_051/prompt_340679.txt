I teach philosophy of biology, and every year the tautology objection to natural selection stalls in exactly the same place. The debate has been running since people first noticed that if fitness gets measured by survival and reproduction, then the claim that fitter organisms survive and reproduce is empty. One camp thinks the propensity interpretation settled this decades ago: fitness is not actual survival or reproductive success but a probabilistic disposition to survive and reproduce, so claims about differential fitness are empirical claims about a disposition and its outcomes, not definitions. The other camp says this just relocates the problem, because the only way we can measure such a disposition is through it's outcomes, so we never get an independent grip on fitness to test anything. Nobody moves. My suspicion is that the word tautology is doing way too much work and covering several distinct disagreements at once. Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is—about the meaning of fitness, about whether a disposition can be measured independently of its manifestation, about what makes an explanation testable, or something else—and given your diagnosis, say what would count as progress: an operationalization, a distinction, a reformulation of the question, or something else entirely.
