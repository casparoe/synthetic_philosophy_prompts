I keep seeing people on both sides of the Sellars debate act like the myth of the given is just a label you slap on any view where experiences justify beliefs without concepts. Sellars in Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind says at the start that all sorts of things—sense contents, material objects, universals, propositions, even givenness itself—have been treated as given, and that there is a shared framework behind these moves. I want the full spec before I decide whether a contemporary perceptual seemings view is just that framework again. Write me the desiderata a theory of perceptual justification would have to meet to avoid the myth of the given, or if you think no theory can fully avoid it, the requirements that show why. Give me the requirements list: the cases it has to handle (ordinary seeing, expert recognition, hallucination, maybe blindsight), the distinctions it can't collapse (being caused by the object vs. being justified by it, non-inferential vs. non-conceptual, the role of experience in causal history vs. its role in the space of reasons), and the empirical psychology it can't contradict. For each requirement say why it counts, and tell me which single requirement knocks out the most popular theories. A table is fine.
