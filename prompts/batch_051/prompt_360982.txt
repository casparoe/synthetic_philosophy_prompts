Ok so I've been going down a rabbit hole since my cog-sci seminar covered the color phi, and I want someone to actually work through a version of it with me instead of repeating the usual talking points. The basic setup, in case it's hazy: a red dot flashes on the left, then a short blank gap, then a green dot flashes on the right, and with the right timing people don't report two separate flashes—they report one dot gliding across that changes from red to green around the midpoint of its path, before the green dot physically appeared. The standard fight is between the Orwellian revision (you had some conscious experience in real time, but your memory of it gets silently overwritten afterward, newspeak-style) and the Stalinesque revision (consciousness is delayed while the stimulus is held backstage, so what finally reaches you is an already packaged, coherent movie with the color change built in from the start). Dennett's whole move, as i understand it, is that the two stories only look like rivals if you assume a Cartesian theater where every conscious event has a definite time and place; drop that assumption, and there's no fact of the matter between them.

But here's the version that keeps me up at night. Suppose the display is exactly: red dot on the left for 100 ms, a 60 ms blank gap, then a green dot on the right for 100 ms. And now the subject has to press a button the instant they see the dot change color—not a retrospective report, but a real time-stamped motor act. The report still says the change happened mid-trajectory, which is temporally before the second dot even appeared; but the press can only be triggered by information that's actually arrived at the retina. So on the face of it the two accounts should predict different latency distributions relative to the second flash, which would finally give the Orwellian/Stalinesque question some empirical content. But i'm not convinced: maybe any latency distribution can be redescribed either as a fast reaction to an already-edited percept or as a slow reaction to a pre-edit percept plus subsequent memory-editing, in which case the disagreement just relocates—into the timing of the decision to press, or into access-vs-phenomenal consciousness, or somwhere stranger. Work out exactly what each account predicts about the button-press latency relative to the onset of the green second dot, then say whether this speeded-response version actually breaks the tie or merely moves the ambiguity, and if it moves it, be specific about where the equivocation now lives.
