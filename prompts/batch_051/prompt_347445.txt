You're a philosopher of mind. Think carefully and start by brainstorming a couple of candidate definitions before you commit. I want a working definition of a conscious mental state in the phenomenal sense — a state that has a felt quality from the inside — as opposed to a mental state that does its work in the dark. A friend keeps insisting on a higher-order theory: a state is conscious only if the subject is in some way aware of being in it, i.e., only if another mental state is about it. Give me the best version of that idea, or a rival if a rival handles the cases better, and run it against these: the pain of stepping on a lego; a subliminally flashed word that speeds up my next response; a blindsight patient who correctly guesses the orientation of a letter while saying they see nothing and are just guessing; a pain in my foot that I only notice after an hour of driving and then realize has been there the whole time (was it conscious during the drive?); a dream I'm fully absorbed in while it lasts; the first second or two after normal waking, before Ive formed any thought about my own experience; and an implicit bias that shapes who I hire but that I'd disown if someone pointed it out. For each case, say whether your definition counts the state as conscious, and when the verdict clashes with ordinary judgment tell me which I should give up: the definition or the judgment. Also say where the definition just goes silent.
