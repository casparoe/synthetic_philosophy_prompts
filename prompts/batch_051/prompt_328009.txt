Before I read any more philosophy of scientific models, I want the requirements list in advance: what must an account of idealization deliver if it is to explain why some unrealistic assumptions in a model are harmless and others are not? The background is the position usually called the F-twist, associated with Milton Friedman — the view that the realism of a model's assumptions is irrelevant so long as it predicts well. Frictionless planes, point masses, perfectly rational agents, zero transaction costs, infinite populations: some false assumptions look like tractability concessions, others look like they do the real explanatory work or quietly presuppose the result. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of idealization and unrealistic assumptions in scientific models must satisfy. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and mark it non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp. Pay particular attention to distinctions the account cannot collapse: idealization versus abstraction versus outright error; assumptions that are false but buy tractability versus assumptions that are false and carry explanatory weight; the standards for a predictive model versus an explanatory model. Flag any pair of desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most to exclude candidate views. Keep the scoring brief: one or two lines each on how F-twist instrumentalism, mechanism-centered realism, and one further position of your choosing fare against the list.
