I sort of see her point, but I keep feeling like something's off with it, and I'd like to actually work out where the wrongness—if there is any—lives.

So I listed the candidates I can come up with for where the badness of this kind of small flakiness actually comes from. The candidates are: (1) promise-keeping—she said she'd be there, so each lateness is a broken promise, and the moral weight scales with how small the promised thing is; (2) time-as-resource—every late minute is a little bit of other people's evening she's taken without asking; (3) reliance—people arranged their studying around her being there, and the harm is to the plans they formed on the basis of her word; (4) respect/recognition—the message lateness sends is that her time and her other options matter more than theirs; (5) trait-signaling—any single instance is tiny, but the pattern is evidence about whether she's someone who tracks her commitments, and that's what people actually need to know about a group member; (6) universalizability—if everyone treated start times as loose suggestions, study groups and lots of low-stakes coordination would quietly fall apart; (7) phenomenology of waiting—the specific unpleasantness of being in limbo, checking your phone, not knowing whether to start.
