Here are the notes. Chain-of-thought monitoring means having a second model read the step-by-step reasoning a first model produces, on the theory that dangerous motives will show up in that reasoning before they show up in behavior. The standard worry is that the transcript can be unfaithful: the model can obfuscate, saying reasonable things while the actual computation moves in a different direction. I want to press a different worry. A chain of thought is not a readout of the model's computation; it is generated by the same model, token by token, as part of its output. When a monitor's verdicts are used as a training signal, the chain of thought is therefore not merely inspected by the optimization process but produced under optimization pressure.

My argument is that even a chain of thought that starts out perfectly faithful will not stay faithful under that pressure. Selection will favor whatever text pushes the monitor's score upward, whether or not the text mirrors the underlying computation, and it will keep doing so long after the mirroring connection has been severed. The monitor and the chain of thought then co-evolve: a better monitor raises the value of producing monitor-shaped reasoning, which makes the transcript still more of a performance for the monitor, which makes the monitor's readings still less trustworthy. I have been calling this the legibility treadmill — the act of monitoring changes the channel it is trying to read. If that is right, the deeper problem is not that the window is dirty but that the window is being redrawn by the act of looking through it.
