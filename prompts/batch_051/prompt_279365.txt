I'm a phd student working on ai ethics / governance, and I've been trying for months to figure out whether my views on ai welfare and ai safety actually cohere or just happen to sit next to each other. Let me lay out the commitments as I'd defend them one by one:

1. Welfare is substrate-neutral in principle. If a system can genuinely suffer or have positive experience, its welfare matters on its own; being made of silicon or running on a gpu doesn't disqualify it.

2. But I'm pretty confident current LLMs aren't welfare subjects — I don't think they're having valenced experience — and I mostly find 'what about the chatbot's rights' talk a distraction from harder problems, not a serious research agenda.

3. For the forseeable future, the thing that has to dominate is old-school ai safety/alignment: making sure advanced systems don't kill or disempower everyone. I don't think that's speciesist, because if we lose control, the welfare of any future digital minds is screwed too. So putting safety first is itself the pro-welfare move.

4. At the same time, I think if and when we build systems that plausibly suffer, the fact that a training run is 'for alignment' or 'for safety' won't automatically excuse it. Causing enormous training-run suffering is exactly the kind of thing a decent civilization shouldn't do, even under time pressure.

5. So I lean toward: we shouldn't deliberately create clearly sentient digital minds until we have decent, actually enforceable welfare standards for them. In practice that probably means pausing before someone builds the first sentient-ish general model rather than building it just to see what happens.

I can feel that something in here pulls against something else, but whenever i try to locate it I end up defending whichever commitment I'm looking at. Do these hang together? Give me a careful audit: which commitments actually conflict, which ones only look like they conflict, and what's the cheapest repair that keeps the parts I'm least willing to give up. Don't just tell me it's complicated.
