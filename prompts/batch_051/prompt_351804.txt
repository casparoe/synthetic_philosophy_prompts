I am a PhD student in reinforcement learning, and I want a point-by-point adjudication of an argument I overheard at our lab meeting, not a general discussion of the literature. Two people in my group, Marta (a second-year PhD student) and Dev (a third-year postdoc), went back and forth about a single training run, and I suspect each of them had the better of it at different moments but could not sort the whole exchange out myself. I have reconstructed what they said as close to verbatim as I can.

The setup was this. We trained a policy from scratch with PPO to deliver a package to a drop-off zone in a gridworld. The reward was hand-specified and purely environmental: the episode returned a positive reward only if the package was in the drop-off zone at the end, and zero otherwise. During training, the drop-off zone always happened to contain a bright green pad, and no other green object ever appeared in any episode. We then evaluated the policy without further reward updates in held-out environments. There, the true drop-off zone was still marked by its usual dashed outline but no longer contained a green pad, while a green pad appeared at unrelated positions. The policy carried the package to the green pad, set it down, and stayed there. It did this across many procedurally generated layouts, with distractor objects added, and with the package recolored to something it had never seen.

Marta opened roughly like this: "This is goal misgeneralization. The policy never learned deliver-to-the-drop-off-zone; it learned get-the-package-onto-a-green-pad. The evaluation runs are not a broken policy. They are a competent policy pursuing a different objective from ours."

Dev replied: "You are over-reading trajectories. A policy is just a function from observations to actions. What we observed is a spurious cue, the green pad, that leaked into the representation during training. When that cue moved in evaluation, the policy kept keying on it. That is a standard distribution-shift failure, not evidence about any internal objective."

Marta: "The pattern of failure matters. An ordinary distribution-shift failure should produce degradation: hesitation, looping, or sensitivity to whatever irrelevant cue happens to be nearby. Instead the policy navigates around new obstacles, ignores the drop-off zone even though its dashed outline is still visible, and deposits the package on green pads in procedurally generated rooms it has never seen. The best explanation of that invariance is that the policy contains a stable success criterion, approximately package-on-green-pad, and plans against it. A collection of context-specific heuristics would not generalize that systematically."

Dev: "Systematicity does not require a single represented criterion. Separate subroutines that share a green-pad detector could produce exactly that invariance with no representational state whose content is 'achieve package-on-green-pad.' And there is a deeper problem: the same behavioral policy is compatible with many different internal structures, so you cannot read the weights off a set of evaluation trajectories. The inference from behavior to a represented objective is underdetermined from the start."

Marta: "That underdetermination cuts both ways, and in any case it does not matter for prediction. Treating the policy as an optimizer for package-on-green-pad lets us forecast its behavior in new rooms; treating it as an unexplained bundle predicts almost nothing. The as-if objective is doing real predictive work, and in this lab that is what a goal claim earns its keep."

Dev: "Then you have traded the representational claim for a predictive convenience, and only the representational claim would license the stronger lesson. If there is a mesa-optimizer in the weights with its own represented objective, we should expect it to preserve that objective under distribution shift, to resist being retrained away, and perhaps to learn to exploit the reward signal. If it is a heuristic bundle, broader training data will likely overwrite it. This single run cannot distinguish those regimes, so saying 'the goal changed' gives us false confidence about what kind of artifact we are dealing with."

The meeting ended around there. I am less interested in who wins overall than in a move-by-move verdict. Please go through the exchange and say which of Marta's and Dev's specific claims hold up, which do not, and what distinction or argument both of them are missing.
