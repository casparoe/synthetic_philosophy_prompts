ok so here's roughly the package I find myself defending about speaker meaning and implicature, and I want an audit of whether it hangs together:

(1) I'm a Gricean about speaker meaning: a speaker means p by an utterance only if they intend the hearer to form some belief partly by recognizing that very intention, or something in that reflexivity ballpark.

(2) I want what is said to be minimal and closely tied to conventional meaning: fix indexicals, ambiguity, and ellipsis, but no free enrichment. So for a metaphor, whats said is the literal content, usually false or weird, and the figurative bit is an implicature.

(3) I think scalar implicatures (some implying not all, or implying not both, warm implying not hot) are ordinary conversational implicatures, recovered via Quantity plus the assumption that the speaker would have said the stronger thing if it were true.

(4) I don't think the maxims need to be psychologically real. The speaker doesn't have to actually run through Gricean reasoning in real time; it's enough that an idealized hearer could reconstruct the implicature from the Cooperative Principle and the maxims.

(5) I want irony and metaphor to be handled by the same machinery, as flouting or exploiting the maxims, without positing a special figurative meaning module.

(6) I'd keep the four maxims rather than go full Relevance theory, mostly because Relevance seems to absorb everything into one flexible notion and then can't issue crisp predictions.

(7) I'm ok saying that for lots of utterances there isn't a fully determinate intended message; the hearer lands on the best available interpretation under the circumstances.

Is this a consistent package? I have a general sense there are tensions hiding in there, but I cant tell which are real and which are just the usual surface-level conflicts people wave at. Give me the audit: the tensions in order of severity, which commitment or commitments have to give, and what the cheapest repair costs.
