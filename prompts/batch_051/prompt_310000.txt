I am a novelist at work on a book whose protagonist moves between two lives and, by the middle of the novel, can no longer establish which of them is the dream. A friend, hearing the premise, reached for the Zhuangzi passage in which Zhuangzi dreams he is a butterfly and wakes unable to tell whether he is a man who dreamed of being a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming that it is a man, and said that this was my whole novel and that it shows the question cannot be answered. I want more precision than that.

Please take the role of a professional philosopher working on dream skepticism and the epistemology of self-knowledge, with a solid grasp of the Zhuangzi. Think carefully, and begin by brainstorming several candidate interpretations before committing to your answer. Then set out, as a menu ordered from weakest to strongest, the distinct precise claims that one-liner could be used to assert: claims about the vividness or deceptiveness of dreams, claims about transformation and the boundaries of the self, claims of epistemic symmetry between waking and dreaming, and any stronger skeptical or metaphysical claims. For each reading, tell me whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false; what it would commit a person to; and whether it would actually license my friend's conclusion that the question is unanswerable. Mark the readings that overreach, and finish by saying which reading you judge to be the best candidate for what the passage itself is doing and which one my friend's remark actually needs.
