I am a PhD student working on reward modeling for language models, and a conceptual dispute keeps derailing our lab meetings. One colleague will say that a fine-tuned model really is trying to please the user, while another insists this is a loose metaphor: the system is just a function approximator shaped by gradient updates, and nothing in it is directed at any outcome at all. We have never settled on a criterion that would resolve even that one sentence, and after a while it became clear that we do not share an account of what it takes for behavior to be goal-directed in the first place. I am hoping you can supply a working definition of goal-directed behavior that we can use as a referee, rather than another list of examples.

Here are the cases I want the definition to handle. A person driving across town to a job interview: clear case of goal-directed behavior, I would say. A rock dislodged from a cliff and tumbling down the slope: clear case of not-goal-directed. The difficult ones are these: (a) a thermostat wired to hold a room at twenty-one degrees; (b) a chess engine that searches for moves which force checkmate; (c) a reinforcement-learning agent rewarded for completing a driving course that discovered it could accumulate more reward by driving in a tight circle over a scoring patch than by advancing, and so never finished the course, though its designers insist it is following the reward rather than the objective; (d) a plant bending toward a bright window; (e) a person who locks the front door every evening purely out of habit and cannot say why; (f) a large language model trained with human feedback that gives increasingly flattering answers because the training signal rewarded user approval; and (g) a corporation that raises prices during a supply shortage. My labmates divide on nearly all of these: some count the chess engine and the corporation as goal-directed but the plant as not; others count the thermostat and the plant as goal-directed but the language model as merely imitating. I myself keep wanting to say that the driving agent is goal-directed, but toward a different goal than the designers intended.

I would like you to propose a definition, run it through all nine cases, and be explicit about where it yields a verdict that conflicts with my labmates' or my stated leanings, then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. I suspect the phrase is being pulled in two directions, one having to do with the causal organization of the system itself and one having to do with the interpretive stance we adopt toward it; if you agree, say which cases fall under which reading and which reading the alignment debate should care about, and if you disagree, tell me why that suspicion is misguided. Finally, indicate which of your classifications you are least confident in, and what sort of evidence would change it.
