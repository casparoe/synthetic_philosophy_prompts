The Great Filter hypothesis explains the apparent absence of visible extraterrestrial civilizations by positing an extremely improbable transition somewhere between non-living chemistry and a galaxy-spanning civilization. I am puzzled, though, about what our seemingly empty sky tells us about where the filter lies. If the filter is behind us—for example, in the origin of life or the evolution of intelligence—then we have already passed the hard step, and so the galaxy should be full of civilizations at roughly our stage on their way to becoming conspicuous. But it looks empty. Does that observation therefore favor the view that the filter lies ahead of us, and that humanity will almost certainly fail before it becomes a galactic civilization? Or does the silence fail to discriminate between these possibilities in the way I am assuming?
