I am preparing a paper for a metaphysics seminar and would like a consistency audit of the position I intend to defend. The argument is built around this passage from Spinoza’s Ethics, Part I, Proposition 5:

PROP. V. There cannot exist in the universe two or more substances having the same nature or attribute.

Proof.—If several distinct substances be granted, they must be distinguished one from the other, either by the difference of their attributes, or by the difference of their modifications (Prop. iv.). If only by the difference of their attributes, it will be granted that there cannot be more than one with an identical attribute. If by the difference of their modifications—as substance is naturally prior to its modifications (Prop. i.),—it follows that setting the modifications aside, and considering substance in itself, that is truly, (Deff. iii. and vi.), there cannot be conceived one substance different from another,—that is (by Prop. iv.), there cannot be granted several substances, but one substance only. Q.E.D.

On the strength of this passage, I hold the following five theses.

1. Priority monism: exactly one concrete entity is absolutely basic—the whole cosmos—and every other concrete entity is either a part of the cosmos or depends upon it.

2. The argument in the quoted passage is best read not as a merely intra-attribute restriction, but as ruling out any plurality of basic concrete entities whatsoever.

3. Ordinary composite objects such as tables, trees, and frogs are genuine constituents of reality, not just convenient ways of speaking and not illusions.

4. A broadly Quinean criterion of ontological commitment is correct: we are committed to whatever our best scientific theories quantify over. Since our best physics quantifies over elementary particles, fields, and spacetime points, these exist, and their existence is not merely that of dependent aspects of a single basic whole.

5. The basic structure of reality should not be brute; whether there is exactly one basic thing or instead many, there must be a reason why.

Do these five theses form a coherent package? For each pair or triple that is genuinely in tension, tell me how severe the tension is and what would have to be revised, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. Also tell me which apparent tensions dissolve on closer inspection. Finally, identify the single least costly revision that would make the whole set consistent. Please treat this as an audit of this specific combination rather than a general survey of monism and pluralism.
