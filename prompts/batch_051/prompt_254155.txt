I recently moved into a position where I have to choose between models of different complexity — deciding how many parameters to include, how heavily to regularize — and the same analogy keeps pressing itself into the conversation. Several of my colleagues, when they want to stop a model from growing, argue that Occam's razor is, after all, a razor: it shaves away superfluous complexity, and what remains is what we are entitled to trust. The work the analogy is doing there is not merely mnemonic; it is meant to license discarding complicated hypotheses outright, as something a principle of induction requires, rather than merely betting on them less.

Yet when I look at the formal machinery this talk is usually meant to gesture at — Solomonoff induction and Kolmogorov complexity — the picture seems importantly different. A Solomonoff-style prior assigns every program p a probability proportional to 2 to the power of negative |p|; no hypothesis is simply cut away, and a complicated one is merely given exponentially less prior weight, with the data still free to overcome that if they insist strongly enough. The prior is also defined only relative to a choice of universal machine, and invariance buys only a multiplicative constant, which can still matter when there are finitely many observations.

I would like a careful audit of how far the razor analogy actually holds. Please give me an explicit correspondence table — what in the formal theory plays the role of the blade, the hand, the act of cutting, the hair being removed, and so on — and then the places where the mapping breaks, separating disanalogies that matter for my colleagues' conclusion from those that are merely cosmetic. Most importantly: does the conclusion that we are licensed to discard complex hypotheses survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the razor carrying an argument the mathematics does not support? And if the razor is the wrong vehicle, what would be a better analogy, and what practical difference would it make to how one actually selects a model?
