I work as a software engineer, and a disagreement about model selection has been nagging at me for weeks. Our team had to choose between a simple linear model with a few hand-picked features and a much larger model that performed about the same on all our validation data. A colleague argued that the data can't actually choose between them, and moreover that this holds for any finite dataset: for every model that fits, you can always construct another that fits just as well and differs on future cases. He tells me philosophers call this underdetermination of theory by evidence. I don't think his strong conclusion follows, but I can't locate exactly where the reasoning goes wrong. We reconstructed his argument like this:

(1) For any finite set of observations and any theory consistent with them, one can construct infinitely many rival theories that agree with the theory on all of those observations while disagreeing about unobserved cases.
(2) So whenever someone claims that the data support a theory, there will always be some rival theory that fits the same data exactly as well.
(3) If two theories fit all the available data exactly as well as each other, then the available evidence supports them to exactly the same degree.
(4) If the evidence supports two theories to the same degree, then that evidence gives no reason to prefer one over the other.
(5) Therefore, for any theory and any finite body of evidence, that evidence gives no reason to prefer the theory over all of its rivals; and since all the evidence we ever actually possess is finite (even after more data arrives), no body of evidence ever gives reason to prefer one theory over another.
(6) So theory choice is never determined by evidence; it always comes down to taste, convenience, or something outside the evidence.

Tell me whether the argument is valid, and if it is not, exactly which step fails and under what reading. If the problem is a slippage in what "supports" or "fits" means, I'd like the corrected version of the relevant step and a statement of what the argument still shows after that correction. Please lead with your verdict in a single sentence, then explain. Cap the answer at 500 words.
