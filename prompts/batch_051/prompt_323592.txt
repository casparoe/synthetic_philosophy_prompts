i've been going back over the color-phi stuff from Dennett and i keep thinking the argument would land better as a poem than as another diagram. Quick setup so the poem has real content to work from: two brief flashes, red then green, a short distance and a split second apart. People report a single dot gliding from the first spot to the second and turning green roughly halfway along its path — except that in real time that midpoint sits before the green flash even fires. So the brain must be doing one of two kinds of rewriting: Orwellian (the memory is edited after the fact, like a Monday-morning rewrite of what you really saw) or Stalinesque (the whole show is held back, edited, and then released, so what you get was already the doctored version). Dennett's point is that the two are indistinguishable from the inside, and the whole wich-happened question is a trap — it assumes a little theater with a final cut and an audience, when on the multiple-drafts picture there are only drafts, constantly revised, never a master print. i want a sestina built around exactly that. Use these six end-words and keep them constant across the stanzas: red, green, dot, gap, screen, draft. Proper 39-line sestina, six six-line stanzas and a three-line envoi. Each stanza should retell the same brief scene as a revision of the one before, so the end-word permutation is doing the philosophical work — the sixth stanza should feel like yet another draft, not a recovered original, and the envoi should gather all six words but walk away from the screen and settle on draft. A time-consciousness person should find it accurate and not just name-dropping; someone who's never heard of color phi should still get a real poem out of it. 39 lines, no more than 250 words.
