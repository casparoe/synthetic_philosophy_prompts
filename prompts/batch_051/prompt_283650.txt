Write a satirical customer-support FAQ (roughly 600–800 words) for a fictional company, Apauruṣeya Texts Ltd, which runs a subscription service giving access to an eternal corpus of canonical instructions. The FAQ should be deadpan and in the voice of a harried technical-support team. The underlying target should be the Mīmāṃsā view that the Veda is authorless (apauruṣeya) and that testimony carries intrinsic validity (svataḥ prāmāṇya): credibility is the default, and the burden of proof falls on anyone who would defeat a claim rather than on the claim to certify itself through an identifiable author or independent check. Let the commitments emerge through policy rather than being explained. For example, the FAQ might treat the absence of an attribution page or revision history as a feature, because a text without a fallible author cannot have been corrupted at source; reply to requests for a source history by explaining that an eternal text never began; respond to reports of an apparent falsehood by treating the report as a claimant-side defect that must defeat the passage before the passage needs defending; and refuse to justify the text by appeal to anything outside the text, since its validity is self-established. The comedy should come from getting these epistemological details right, and from the collision between the doctrine and a genre whose conventions presuppose authorship, accountability and error correction, rather than from mocking the tradition. A specialist should recognise a fair satirising of the position, while a reader unfamiliar with Mīmāṃsā should still be able to tell what is being sent up. Avoid quotations and citations; paraphrase rather than quoting any primary text.
