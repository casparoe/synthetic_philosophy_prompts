I chair the Climate Resilience Commission of a mid-sized city. We must approve one of three ten-year capital plans for heat mitigation: Plan A (incremental services), Plan B (distributed hardening), or Plan C (deep hardening). Outcomes depend on which of three heat-stress futures — Mild, Moderate, or Severe — materializes over the decade, and there is no agreed probability distribution over these futures. Several defensible distributions have been proposed, and they disagree widely.

Outcomes are measured in thousands of disability-adjusted life-years averted citywide over ten years:

| Plan | Mild | Moderate | Severe |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 120 | 20 | 0 |
| B | 75 | 45 | 50 |
| C | 70 | 75 | 90 |

The Commission is split three ways, and each faction's preferred rule picks a different plan. A Rawlsian group argues that under genuine ignorance we should use maximin: choose the plan with the best worst case. That is Plan C (floor of 70, versus 45 for B and 0 for A). A regret-minimization group argues for minimax regret: minimize the largest shortfall relative to the best-performing plan in each future. That is Plan B (maximum regret 45, versus 50 for C and 90 for A). The city budget office argues for Harsanyi-style expected-utility reasoning using an elicited distribution of .60/.20/.20 over Mild/Moderate/Severe. That yields A = 76.0, B = 64.0, C = 75.0, so it recommends Plan A — by a margin of only one thousand DALYs. Under an alternative distribution of .50/.30/.20, the expected-value ranking flips to C.

I need you to draft the Commission's decision memo, which the full body will vote on. The memo should: (i) open with the bottom-line vote recommendation in a single sentence — approve A, B, or C, with conditions if you think conditions are needed; (ii) justify that recommendation, stating which decision rule it rests on; (iii) honestly address the strongest consideration for the losing side; and (iv) say how much the probability dispute should matter given that the expected-value recommendation flips under small changes in the probabilities.

Hard cap: 600 words.
