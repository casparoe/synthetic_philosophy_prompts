Lately I have been turning over the thought that history bends toward justice: that over the long run the circle of beings treated as full moral patients has widened, and that the widening has been hard to reverse, like a ratchet. A political reversal in a country I know well has made this personally urgent rather than abstract. I want to know whether the strict version — that there is a long-run directional process, not merely a selected trend — is the kind of claim evidence can bear on at all. Please treat it as a conceptual claim that may nonetheless be partly testable, and design the strongest feasible test: say what data would favour it and what would count against it, and how you would avoid the usual traps of measuring only the recent past, sampling only the institutions that survived, or reading the record through the winner's eyes. I would especially like you to develop a ratchet-style test, since a genuinely directional process should resist deliberate attempts at reversal while a drift or an uncontested trend should not. Then be exact about what a positive result would and would not settle, in particular whether it could ever distinguish a moral arc in history from a merely causal or demographic one, or give any comfort to talk of providence. Please answer in continuous prose only, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
