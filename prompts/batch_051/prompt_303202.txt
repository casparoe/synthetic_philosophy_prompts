I'm on the advisory board of a foundation that funds pilots in 'new governance', and we've got a proposal in front of us to co-fund a charter city in a developing country: a private operator gets a 99-year lease over a large site, runs its own commercial law, courts and private security, and residents and firms move in. The host government will use compulsory purchase powers to clear part of the site that's currently farmed. The board is split between people who see it as letting workers and firms genuinly choose their own rules, and people who say you can't meaningfully opt out of a state you never consented to in the first place—and that the compulsory purchase makes the consent story hollow from the start. Could you draft a memo for the board with a clear recommendation on whether to fund it? It should engage seriously with the strongest argument for the side you reject, and set the practical feasibility stuff to one side; we've already got that covered.
