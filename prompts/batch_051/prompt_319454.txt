One of my coworkers has gotten weirdly systematic about discrimination law and keeps a running tab of which traits she thinks should count as suspect grounds. Her verdicts so far: race, sex, religion, disability, and sexual orientation are in; age, obesity, pregnancy, and smoking status are out. She insists all of it follows from a single principle, not from whatever courts happen to do. We're about to argue over a new case: a genetic marker that sharply raises someone's risk of early-onset Alzheimer's. Which way do you think she'll go? I'm less interested in the yes/no than in the principle she's actually running, the other principles that fit the same track record just as well, what each of those predicts for the genetic marker case, and which follow-up case—height, criminal record, or citizenship—would best seperate the candidates.
