Question: Explain Lakatos's account of research programmes, focusing on the hard core, the protective belt, and the positive and negative heuristics. Then respond to this worry: if the hard core is never refuted directly, what makes abandoning one programme in favour of another rational rather than a mere Kuhnian conversion? Your answer should say what makes a programme progressive or degenerating and what role novel predictions play.

Student answer:

Lakatos's unit of appraisal is the research programme: a sequence of theories that develops over time by modifying a shared protective belt around a fixed hard core. The hard core contains the programme's central claims—Newton's three laws plus the law of gravitation in the Newtonian case—and scientists decide to treat it as unfalsifiable, so when an apparent refutation comes in, modus tollens gets directed at the belt instead. The belt is the ring of auxiliary hypotheses about instruments, observation conditions, approximations, and the like, and it gets adjusted to absorb anomalies. The negative heuristic is the rule never to modify the core; the positive heuristic is the plan for building the belt outward, and it specifies in advance the particular adjustments to make by anticipating the anomalies that will arise. A programme is theoretically progressive if each successive theory makes novel predictions—predictions of facts that were not used in constructing the theory—and empirically progressive if those novel predictions are corroborated. A programme that merely patches the belt after the fact without generating new corroborated predictions is degenerating.

One might object that if the core is unfalsifiable, no evidence can ever disprove a programme, so choosing between programmes would be arational in the way Kuhn described. Lakatos escapes this objection because he relocates Popper's falsificationism to the level of the sequence: a degenerating programme has failed tests, so scientists are rationally required to abandon it as soon as it stops producing novel confirmed predictions. That is what saves him from relativism. Crucial experiments get recognized only with hindsight, after one programme has been progressive and the other degenerating.
