I work on the safety review board at a company that runs a fleet of autonomous delivery vans. After every incident — near miss, property damage, pedestrian injury — we run a "responsible entity review" before anything goes to legal or to the regulators. The categories we currently have are operator error, maintenance failure, supplier/component defect, design/spec defect, and "unforeseeable," which everybody hates but nobody can define. I keep running into cases where the system did something nobody specifically programmed, nobody was clearly careless, and "unforeseeable" still feels like a dodge.

I'm a longtime rationalist-forum lurker, so I've seen the phrase "responsibility gap" tossed around, usually attached to the idea that you can get a bad outcome where each human did the locally reasonable thing and yet the harm is still nobody's fault in the ordinary sense. I want you to turn that idea into an actual procedure we can use on the board, not a summary of the literature.

Give me a reusable decision procedure — a checklist, or a sequence of tests in words — that takes an incident and sorts it into: clear human culpability (and which layer it lives at: operator, maintainer, engineer, supplier, management), a genuine responsibility gap, or one of the hard in-between categories. The people using it haven't read the philosophy: they're engineers, fleet managers, and one lawyer. We have about a week per incident and usualy about two hours of actual review time. The head of safety signs off on the classification and can override it, but I want the procedure to make overrides rare and explainable.

For each step in the procedure, tell me which conceptual distinction it's trying to preserve and why that distinction matters rather than being academic. Be explicit about the points where the procedure has to stop and a human has to make a judgment call, and say what the judgment is actually about. Then apply the procedure to two or three short incident sketches (invent them), including at least one where the procedure spits out an answer our team will not like — say, "nobody is culpable" when the company is obviously going to pay, or "this operator was culpable" about someone who sure feels blameless. Finally, say what the procedure cannot do: which part of the underlying question it has to bracket or fake in order to be usable at all.

American English throughout, please, since it's going into our internal docs.
