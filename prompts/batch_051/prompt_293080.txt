I am preparing a lecture on why the measurement problem in quantum mechanics remains unsettled, and I want to take an explicit stand on which commitments the rival interpretations actually trade away. Consider the following five claims:

1. The wavefunction is a complete description of an individual quantum system.
2. The wavefunction always evolves unitarily, exactly as the Schrödinger equation specifies.
3. Measurements have a single, determinate outcome.
4. The statistics of repeated measurements obey the Born rule.
5. Macroscopic apparatuses are themselves quantum systems governed by the same physics as everything else.

Please rank these five claims from the most to the least worth holding fixed, for the purpose of building a philosophically satisfactory resolution of the measurement problem, and assign each claim a score out of 10 for how firmly I should keep it. I want the ranking to speak to the interpretation debate, so in each item name at least one of the three main families — Copenhagen/collapse, Everett/many-worlds, or de Broglie–Bohm/pilot-wave — and say whether that family is committed to keeping the claim or to giving it up. Give me exactly five numbered items, one per claim, presented in rank order; each item should be one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.

If you think this task as posed is confused — for instance because the claims are not independent, or because "worth holding fixed" has no determinate content until a specific theoretical virtue is prioritized — tell me so plainly and propose the better version of the task, instead of producing a tidy ranking.
