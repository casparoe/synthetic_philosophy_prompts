I've been circling back to the White Horse Dialogue — Gongsun Long's old School of Names move where he defends bai ma fei ma, the claim usually rendered in English as: a white horse is not a horse. The standard dismissal makes it look like wordplay: horse picks out a shape, white picks out a color, and a request for a horse is satified by a yellow horse while a request for a white horse isn't, so the two can't be the same thing. But it struck me this morning that type theory has something close to this distinction built in for free. Let Horse be a type and White a predicate; then the dependent sum type WhiteHorse := Sigma(x : Horse). White(x) is genuinely not the type Horse, even though every inhabitant of WhiteHorse projects to a Horse via first projection. So a type theorist can say, without punning, that the type of white horses is not the type of horses — and only the projection blocks the conclusion that white horses are not horses.
