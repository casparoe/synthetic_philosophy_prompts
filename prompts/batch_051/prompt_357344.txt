I am a PhD student in formal epistemology, and I have been assisting with an adversarial collaboration between two probabilists who have finally stopped talking past each other. One is a Coxian logical probabilist: she holds that probability is the unique generalization of deductive logic to uncertain inference, forced by consistency desiderata of the kind made explicit in Cox's theorem. The other is a de Finetti-style subjectivist: he holds that probability represents an agent's degrees of belief, that the probability calculus is justified by coherence or Dutch-book arguments rather than by a logical notion of unique rational plausibility, and that any prior consistent with the axioms is rationally permissible. They now want a single document both can sign. Write it.

The document should contain four sections.

(1) A numbered list of claims both camps can accept. These should include a precise, conditional statement of what Cox's theorem shows under its stated assumptions; a precise statement of what coherence and Dutch-book arguments show; the shared formalism both accept, including the treatment of conditional probability, Bayes' rule, and the axioms of finitely additive probability; and what each side grants about the other's account without conceding the main dispute.

(2) A disagreements table. Each row should give a neutral statement of the exact point at issue, then the formulation each side would sign on its own behalf. Do not round the positions off into slogans.

(3) A crux section. Identify one or two sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, specify an argument, formal example, or class of cases that both parties agree would bear on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. I suspect, without precommitting you, that one crux will be whether the assumptions of real-valuedness and total comparability of plausibilities are rationally compulsory or merely a modeling convenience; another may be whether finitely additive probability is all that coherence requires or countable additivity has a deeper consistency-based justification.

(4) A closing note on which remaining disagreements, if any, now strike both parties as merely verbal.

Do not include references or a bibliography. The aim is not to reconcile the two camps into a vague both-have-a-point conclusion; it is to make the residual disagreement exact enough that a third party could inherit it and run a decisive test.
