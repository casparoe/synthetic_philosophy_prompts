(1) I'm a hard egalitarian about the franchise among adults: one person one vote, and weighted voting by education or political knowledge is wrong even in principle, not just because it would be abused or administered badly.

(2) But I'm fine with threshold competence exclusions: excluding young children and adults under guardianship for severe cognitive disability doesn't bother me, and I think the justification is that they lack the decision-making capacities voting requires.

(3) I think the basic point of the vote is equal standing in collective self-rule, not tracking an independent political truth.

(4) I think better-informed publics make better decisions, so I support civic education and deliberative reforms that raise the median voter's knowledge, but only when they raise everyone's information together rather than filtering out or stratifying the less informed.

(5) I also think a basic civics test as a condition of naturalization is fine, not demeaning.

Are these jointly tenable? Walk me through which pairs actually pull against eachother and which only look like they do.
