I teach an undergrad seminar on language and thought, and next week I want to run a class on whether a language could in principle get by without indexicals — I, you, here, now, this, tomorrow — or whether there's something those words do that no pile of objective descriptions can replace. I've got about 14 students and one 75-minute block, and I really don't want to lecture: I want them to bump into the problem themselves and only afterward have me put a name on it. Can you design me an activity where the class has to operate under a rule that bans indexicals (or bans them in some contexts), and where the cheerful workarounds — 'the bespectacled person carrying two water bottles is leaving the north lecture hall at 14:32:07' — eventually stop working? Ideally there should be a moment like standing in an unfamiliar train station with a map and knowing every fact about the city except which dot on the map is you. Give me roles or rules, materials, a timeline for the 75 minutes, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the philosophical issue — the irreducibly self-locating bit.

Also flag what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be straight about which part of the idea the activity can't reproduce and I'll have to supply myself. My vague image is something with people radioing in to get picked up while hiking and not being allowed to say 'we,' 'here,' or 'at our position,' but maybe that's a terrible idea.
