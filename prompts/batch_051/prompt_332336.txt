I keep running into a puzzle in the foundations of mathematics that I do not have a name for, and I would like to know whether one exists. The real numbers are usually introduced to me not as something we find but as something we build — and there appear to be several different building sites that do not obviously produce the same materials. One construction builds a real number as a Dedekind cut: a nonempty, downward-closed, proper subset of the rationals with no greatest element. Another builds it as an equivalence class of Cauchy sequences of rationals. Another builds it as an infinite decimal expansion, with the convention that 0.999... and 1.000... are two names for the same number. Each construction is then shown to yield a complete ordered field, and the textbooks say that the several constructions are the same up to isomorphism. But the underlying objects are completely different: a cut is one kind of set, a Cauchy-sequence equivalence class is a set of sequences, and a decimal is a sequence of digits. If each construction can say with equal right that its objects are the real numbers, then which of them, if any, are the real numbers really? And if that question has no well-posed answer, what should this lead us to conclude about whether the continuum is discovered or defined?

I have the impression that a similar point is sometimes made about the natural numbers — that several equally good set-theoretic reductions exist and there is no principled way to choose among them — but I do not know whether this problem has a standard name when it is raised for the real numbers, or how it interacts with the fact that the reals are often characterized, up to isomorphism, as the unique complete ordered field. Is there an established term for this exact configuration? If there is, I would like to know how it is usually stated and how it should be distinguished from adjacent questions: the categoricity of the reals, the uniqueness of a complete ordered field, and the broader debate about whether mathematical objects exist independently of our constructions. If there is no exact term, I would be grateful for a proposed name, a definition, and a few boundary cases. The label matters to me less than the carving: I want a precise account of what this phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.
