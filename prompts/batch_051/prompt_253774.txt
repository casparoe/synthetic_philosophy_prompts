I'm a science journalist, and for the last few months I've been reporting on how labs that build autonomous systems actually think about machine ethics — not the trolley-problem essays, but the design reviews for autonomous vehicles and for hospital robots that coax patients into moving around after surgery, where somebody has to write down what the system can and can't do. I've been trying to work out my own position before the peice runs, and I'm worried it's a pile of reflexes rather than a view. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm convinced that moral judgment isn't the application of rules; it's more like skilled perception. You notice that this feature of the situation matters here, the way an experienced nurse notices a small change in how a patient is holding themselves, and no finite list of principles is going to capture that. (2) But when it comes to machines, I'm a top-down hardliner: I want a small set of explicit, inspectable rules that a regulator could read, not a system that "learned values." (3) I'm also pretty down on the whole reward-modeling / reinforcement-learning-from-human-feedback route — it seems like you'd just be heating up whatever preferences and blind spots are already sitting in the data and then multiplying them. (4) And I don't think "the model decided" can ever be an acceptable account of a harmful action: you should be able to point to a rule or spec it followed or violated. (5) Finally, I've told people I'd take a somewhat worse but legible system over a somewhat better black box any day, because when you're deploying something with power over people, auditability is itself a moral requirement, not an engineering nicety.

I genuinely can't tell whether this hangs together. For instance, does (1) undercut the rule-based safety approach in (2) and (4), or is that only a surface clash? And does (1) sit comfortably with (3), since a skill is exactly the kind of thing you'd learn from experience? Go through the set, tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly, which only look that way, and what's the cheapest single revision that would make the whole thing cohere, and what it costs.
