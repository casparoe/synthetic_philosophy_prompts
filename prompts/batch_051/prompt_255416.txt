I've been going back and forth on how tiny harms add up, and I want to know whether there's any one principle that gets all my snap verdicts on the cases below, or close to it. I'd also like you to test whatever principle you come up with agains a couple of cases I'm still undecided on, and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it can't keep.

Here are my verdicts, for what they're worth.

1. A person gets one severe migraine that lasts a full day vs. ten thousand people each lose thirty seconds of sleep one night. I think the migraine is clearly worse. Thirty seconds of lost sleep is just not a real event; I'd barely register it.

2. One person gets a sharp pinprick on a fingertip vs. a million people each get the same pinprick. I think the million is worse. No hesitation.

3. One person is tortured for an hour vs. every person alive right now gets a brief, mild itch on the forearm, the kind that's gone before you finish scratching. I think the torture is worse. I realize this sounds like case 2, but I can't get myself to pick the itches.

4. One person is moderately nauseated for a week vs. a hundred thousand people each get a minute of mild stomach unease. I genuinely don't know. My gut says the nausea is worse, but case 2 makes me unsure.

5. Within a single person's life: one badly stubbed toe vs. a year of a very mild background ache that never quite goes away. I think the year of background ache is worse. So I do aggregate across time within a life.

6. One person gets an eyelash in their eye that blinks out in a second vs. a trillion people each get a single mildly itchy sensation that fades in under a second. I keep saying the eyelash is worse, and I worry that's just me refusing to do arithmetic.

I keep flirting with a two-part rule: harms below some threshold of noticeability don't count at all, so no number of them can outweigh one real harm; but once a harm passes the threshold, you aggregate in the normal way. Case 1 supports the first clause and case 2 supports the second. But then the rule seems to force the conclusion that with enough mildly itchy forearms you should outweigh the torture, and with enough minutes of mild stomach unease you should outweigh the nausea, and I don't know whether to bite those bullets or give up the rule.

So: is there a principle, or a small set of candidates, that captures my verdicts? If it's the threshold-plus-aggregation rule, what exactly does it commit me to, and which of my verdicts do I have to revise? And for the unsettled ones: one hour of torture vs. some truly enormous number of one-second itches, is there a number at which it flips? One week of nausea vs. how many minutes of mild stomach unease? What should the principle say about a case where the tiny harm is repeated in the same person rather then spread across people, say one person gets a lifetime of one-second itches at random moments vs. a single three-hour torture?
