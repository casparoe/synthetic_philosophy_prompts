Suppose that, starting in 2030, a one-time, permanent, and nearly costless intervention becomes available that induces a carefully circumscribed form of pain asymbolia. After treatment, nociception and the sensory-discriminative aspect of pain remain intact: people can still tell where an injury is, how intense the signal is, when it began, and whether it burns or stings, and they still withdraw reflexively from tissue damage. What the intervention removes is the affective-motivational dimension—the felt badness, the urgency, the compulsion to stop what one is doing and escape or protect the injured part. A treated person notices injuries the way one notices a dashboard warning light: the information arrives, but it does not hurt. By 2040 nearly every adult on Earth has been treated, with no possibility of opting out afterward.

Give me a reasoned prediction of what the world of 2060 looks like under this change, focusing on medicine, criminal justice, child-rearing, and everyday risk-taking. I want the chain of reasoning made explicit: for each predicted change, state the mechanism, which effects follow from durable incentives and constraints, and which depend on downstream behavioral or cultural adaptation. In particular, address whether people become more or less careful about injury once the sting is gone, and whether the answer differs for chronic pain patients, athletes, manual workers, and children; what happens to practices that use pain as a deterrent or sanction, such as corporal punishment, harsh prison conditions, legal damages for pain and suffering, and even torture, and whether inflicting tissue damage comes to seem more or less wrong once it no longer causes suffering; and whether demand for palliative and protective care expands or contracts. Also say which functions of ordinary pain are fully served by the retained detection system, which social functions were being performed by its unpleasantness alone, and which institutions would adapt versus break. For each link, mark your confidence as robust, plausible, or speculative, and state the single behavioral assumption—for example, about whether prudential motivation can survive the loss of felt aversion—whose failure would most overturn your prediction. Where the reasoning bears on it, indicate what the scenario implies about which feature of pain carries its badness: the felt unpleasantness, the damage signal, or both.
