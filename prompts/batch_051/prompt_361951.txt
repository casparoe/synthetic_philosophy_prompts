My grandkids' claping games are what got me stuck on this. The same basic game exists in a dozen slightly different versions across the playground, and every few months one version takes over the whole school while the rest die off. The winner usually isn't the best game in any functional sense—it's just the one that's easiest to remember or has a line that gets the biggest reaction. I've noticed the same thing with recipes at the church potluck, with the office jokes I used to hear before I retired, and with the new slang my nephew brings home. It looks for all the world like variation and selection. But the analogy to genes seems to break exactly where it should be strongest: genes get copied with high fidelity, and these cultural things don't really get copied at all. Each kid is re-performing the game from memory and patching over bits they half-remember, and a recipe drifts every time someone makes it because they adjust to what they have in the cupboard. So: is there a name for treating pieces of culture as units that get copied with variation and then get selected by how well they spread? And is there an actual theory behind that name, with a real mechanism, or is it just a biological metaphor people got stuck on? I want more than the label. I want to know the nearest competing concepts, how my description differs from each, which one fits best, and where the edges of that concept are. If the honest answer is that the name is a metaphor and nothing more, say so and defend it.
