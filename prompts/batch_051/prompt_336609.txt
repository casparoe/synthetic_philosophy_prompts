Please give a precise account of what Milton Friedman's F-twist about unrealistic assumptions in scientific models actually licenses and what it does not; the rationalist slogan that all models are wrong but some are useful is too often used to settle this question in advance. Start by stating the strongest version of Friedman's position, namely the as-if argument that a positive theory is judged by its predictive success rather than by the realism of its assumptions, and keep it distinct from the lazy view that assumptions never matter. Then distinguish two claims that are easy to conflate: false assumptions need not impair a model's predictive value, and false assumptions need not impair what the model tells us about the mechanisms generating the data. Give the most serious objection to Friedman that still grants predictive success as the basic test, and finish by saying whether it is fair to summarize his view as: false assumptions are a feature, not a bug. If that summary fails, specify the exact error it makes.
