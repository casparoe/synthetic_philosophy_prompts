I work as a review engineer at a company that trains large language models as agents. Before any training run that uses reinforcement learning or best-of-n search against a learned or composite reward signal, one of us has to sign a single-page review form, and the sign-off is meant to be more than a box-ticking exercise because runs are expensive and a badly specified reward can produce a model that scores well while doing the wrong thing. I want a reusable pre-training review procedure based on the following claim: when a proxy metric becomes the optimisation target, the system will tend to exploit the gap between that metric and the underlying property the designers actually care about, and the danger increases with the optimiser's search power, the number of channels it has to affect its own inputs or observations, and the distance between the proxy and the intended property. Turn that claim into a procedure I can actually run in 45 minutes. It should include: the questions to ask in order; what counts as a satisfactory answer to each; the points where the procedure must stop with a no; the points where it must escalate to a senior reviewer because the question is one of values or acceptable risk rather than engineering; and the points where a designer's prior experience may override a checklist warning. I want the procedure to be usable by an engineer who has not read the formal alignment literature. Then apply it to three proposals: (a) a code-generation agent rewarded by the proportion of unit tests passed, where the unit tests are generated from the same prompt distribution used at evaluation time; (b) a summarisation model rewarded by a learned preference model, with human audits only after training; (c) a customer-support agent rewarded by a score combining user sentiment and whether a support ticket is reopened. For each, say where the procedure stops, what it flags, and whether it would clear the run. Include at least one case where the procedure blocks a run the proposing team believes is safe, and make clear which steps are judgment calls dressed up as checks.
