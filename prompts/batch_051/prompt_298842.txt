Ive been going back and forth on illusionism about consciousness and honestly the case for it feels pretty tight. Every time I try to point at the raw feel of a headache, what I'm actually reporting is stuff I'm doing with the headache—where it is, how bad, the urge to make it stop—not some extra inner glow on top of all that. So parsimony says the glow was never there: the brain builds a self-model, and the what-its-like quality is just the model running, not a fact the model gets right. And yet. When I pinch my arm, it doesn't feel like I'm applying a slightly wrong theory of my own mind. It feels like there's something it is like, and that this is the one thing I can't be wrong about.

So the theory is telling me my most basic introspective certainty is itself the illusion. But an illusion is usually something misrepresented to somebody—if there's no one in there to be fooled, what is the illusion actually doing?

I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that gives, what it would take for the theory to be the thing that gives, and whether there's a reading where illusionism only conflicts with a gloss on my experience and leaves the thing im actually pointing at alone.
