An investment committee is about to vote on a trade. Its new rule: each of the five members privately writes down (a) their own probability the trade makes money, (b) their guess at the other four members' average probability, and (c) their guess of the average of what the other four will write for (b). The numbers are displayed to everyone at once, then each member can revise their own (a) once, and then the vote happens. The designers think this makes disagreement common knowledge and forces people to update on private information instead of defering to the room. Analyze it like a mechanism: what does someone strategic actually do at each stage, do the higher-order guesses improve information aggregation or mostly create new ways to game the rule, and what would you expect to happen to the quality of the vote? Where would you put the failure, and would removing the higher-order guesses fix it? Answer in American English.
