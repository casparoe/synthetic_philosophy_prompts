In my sociology class this semester we did a group project on how to get more parents involved at our school, and during the debrief two members of my group got into an argument I still can't settle. Early in the discussion, Leila — one of the quieter members, whose parents mostly speak Spanish at home — tentatively floated the idea of holding a parent meeting at the community center instead of the school library. She said something like she wasn't sure, but maybe the community center would reach more parents, and she didn't push it when nobody responded. Later, Dan proposed essentially the same thing, but firmly and with reasons: families already go there, it's on the bus line, and the library feels like an academic space. The group praised the idea and built on it. When Leila pointed out quietly that she had suggested it earlier, Dan said he hadn't registered it as a proposal. Afterward, Ana and Ben argued about what had happened.

Ana said it was a textbook case of testimonial injustice. "The group heard the same idea from Leila and did nothing, then treated it as a good idea when Dan said it. The conclusion was identical, so the only thing that changed was the speaker. That means her credibility was being discounted because of who she is." Ben replied that the epistemic position had changed, not just the speaker. "Leila offered an unsupported 'maybe' with no reasons. Dan made an argument. You can't demand that a group treat a guess and a defended proposal as equivalent just because they end in the same sentence." Ana pushed back: "But why was she unsupported? She's learned to hedge because when she asserts things flatly, people treat her as pushy. The confidence gap is part of the injustice, not an excuse for ignoring her. Requiring her to sound like Dan before she's heard stacks the deck." Ben said that might be true as a story about her psychology, but the group was under a deadline and had to evaluate proposals as they were actually made, not as they would have been made in a fairer world. "If we credit every tentative 'maybe' as a full proposal, we lose the ability to reject weak ideas. Some people are tentative because they're not actually committed to the idea." Ana answered that she wasn't asking the group to accept every maybe, only to notice that the group's procedure wasn't neutral. "Dan's version got follow-up questions and people building on it. Leila's version got silence. Silence after a suggestion is itself an epistemic signal: it tells her, and everyone watching, that what she says doesn't count as a contribution until a higher-status person repeats it." Ben granted that the silence was bad group process and that a good group would have asked Leila to say more, but he said that was a practical failure, not an injustice done to her specifically. "I don't see evidence that what drove the assessment was prejudice rather than the plain fact that one contribution was argued for and the other wasn't." Ana said she wasn't accusing anyone of being a bigot. "I'm saying the bar for being heard is set by people who already have authority. If the only way to be listened to is to sound like Dan, then the standard isn't neutral, and Leila's word was assigned less weight than the same content would get in a more authoritative style." Ben said that was a different accusation than the one she started with. "First it was 'the only difference was identity.' Now it's 'the group uses standards that favor the confident.' The second is a real problem, but it applies to almost every discussion everywhere, and it doesn't show that Leila suffered testimonial injustice rather than an ordinary failure to draw out a quiet member. If every confidence-favoring norm counts as an injustice, the term stops picking out anything fixable."

I want a committed adjudication, not a "both sides have a point" answer. Please go through the main claims in the order they actually made them, say explicitly which I should accept and which I should reject, and then say what both of them are missing about how testimonial injustice is supposed to work in a case like this.
