The earlier, competent self has authority over the later, incapacitated self because only the competent self can survey and endorse the life as a whole; its critical interests, recorded in the directive, should therefore override the later self's merely experiential preferences, which arise without the capacity for narrative self-government. Now suppose a treatment can transiently but reliably restore something close to full decisional capacity in a person with advanced dementia, so that the later self can occasionally give current, considered, competent refusals that contradict the earlier directive.
