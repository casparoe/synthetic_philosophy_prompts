I've been thinking a lot about Bongard problems and how theyre basically the cleanest case of concept induction from a handful of examples. For those who haven't seen them: a Bongard problem gives you two panels, each with six small diagrams, and you have to figure out the rule that separates the left six from the right six. What's odd is that people routinely get the rule from just twelve examples, even when the underlying concept is disjunctive or relational rather than a simple feature. I want a working definition of 'inducing a concept from a few examples' that handles the following cases, because I'm not convinced there's a single thing going on under that phrase:

1. A person solves an unfamiliar Bongard problem correctly and can state the separating rule in words.
2. A person solves it, and can reliably sort new diagrams, but can't articulate the rule and says the answer just looks right.
3. A neural network trained on the same twelve diagrams that generalises perfectly to held-out diagrams, but also assigns high confidence to diagrams a human would clearly reject.
4. A large language model given six example pairs and told something like: here are some zorps, here are some non-zorps, classify the rest. It handles novel pairs, but a trivial rephrasing of the same training pairs flips its answers.
5. A child who saw a single echidna at the zoo and then confidently misclassifies every hedgehog as an echidna.
6. A mathematician who proves there is a unique natural transformation with certain properties, and says she had the concept of that construction before she could state it.
7. A pigeon trained to peck at images containing water, that then pecks at a mirror.
8. Two different but equally simple rules that both separate the same twelve diagrams perfectly.

I want a definition that says, for each case, whether it does or doesn't involve concept induction, and why. Then, because I can see this becoming one of those debates where everyone insists their favourite case is the central one, I'd like you to say which of your verdicts is least secure, and what would change your mind about it: which argument or consideration would lead you to move a case from one side to the other, or abandon the definition altogether. Also tell me where your reasoning is most likely to fail. British English, please, and don't tidy up my cases — I know the pigeon one probably isn't concept induction to most people, but that's exactly what I want pressure-tested.
