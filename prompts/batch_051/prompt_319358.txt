Something I keep going around in circles on. Take a couple. One tells the other "don't get emotionally close to anyone but me," or "don't go on long late-night walks with a friend," or "don't cook someone else an elaborate meal just to see them light up." Everyone calls that jealousy, control, a red flag. But switch the thing being restricted to sex and the exact same posture becomes one of the most ordinary sentences in the world: "of course you don't sleep with other people." That strikes me as strange. Of all the things a person can do with another person, sex is the one where we all assume the partner gets a default veto — not something a couple negotiates, but a rule that's already sitting there. And violating it is treated as a wrong on its own: a one-night stand gets counted as a betrayal even when the partner never finds out, nothing is caught, no one gets pregnant, and the relationship keeps sailing along fine. Meanwhile a partner who belittles you every day for years is "going through a rough patch," not "breaking the rules." So sex is doing special moral work here. I want to know if anything actually justifies that. The easy justifications — disease, pregnancy, emotional bonding — all seem to stop applying in the case I just described, so any real defense has to be deeper than that. Build the strongest case you can that sexual exclusivity genuinely deserves this default-moral-rule status, and then tell me honest whether it survives the countercases. If it doesn't, say what we'd be left with: is the whole thing just a convention wearing a moral costume?
