Okay so my friend and I spent like an hour arguing about this after a seminar, and I still cant get it straight. The setup is an old-ish idea in action theory: reasons have to belong to someone. A reason isnt a free-floating consideration; it is the kind of thing an agent weighs, can be asked to give, and can revise its behavior in light of. If a fact counts in favor of some action but there is no one whose reason it is, no one who would change course or feel the force of it if it were pointed out, then it isnt a reason at all, just a cause or an explanation or a string of words. My friend thinks this whole picture just got killed by large language models. His point was that a modern model will walk through a hard call, say whether to shut down a failing project, and produce a genuinely good rationale, weighing sunk costs against expected value, and that rationale is detatched from any agent in the old sense. There is no subject who holds it, no commitment, no continuity between one token and the next. But people read the rationale and it functions exactly like a reason: it changes their mind, they cite it, they act on it. So maybe reasons dont need reasoners, and the old view was just chauvinism about what kind of thing can be the source of a reason.
