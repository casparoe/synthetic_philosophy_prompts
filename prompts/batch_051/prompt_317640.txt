My friend Elena and I have been arguring about this for months and last night it went till 2 a.m., so I'm trying to get it on paper. The fight is whether simplicity is actually a theoretical virtue in ethics the way it is in, say, choosing between scientific theories. I take the parsimony side: if a single principle — say one consequentialist or contractualist principle — covers 90% of our considered judgments but lands wrong on a few cases, that's still better than a four-principle pluralism plus a priority rule that fits all the cases, because the pluralist picture is just curve-fitting. She thinks that's backwards: in ethics the considered judgments about cases aren't noisy data you're trying to predict, they're the very thing a theory has to answer to, so stripping out principles to make the theory prettier is an aesthetic preference dressed up as an epistemic one. Neither of us is a skeptic about moral theory as such; we both think reflective equilibrium is roughly the right method, which is what makes the disagreement annoying and precise.
