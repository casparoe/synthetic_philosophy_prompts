I want help assessing a parallel I keep seeing between the epistemic regress problem and the theory of recursive and coinductive definitions in computer science, because I cannot tell whether it is a genuine structural identity, a loose analogy, or a category mistake.

Here is the mapping as I see it. Foundationalism — the view that every chain of reasons must terminate in basic, non-inferentially justified beliefs — looks like the requirement that a recursive definition be well-founded: evaluation must eventually reach a base case. Coherentism — the view that beliefs may support one another in a loop — looks like a set of mutually recursive equations with no base case, which ordinary programming rejects as nonterminating but which other formal frameworks handle as least fixed points or constraint-satisfaction problems. Infinitism — the view that an endless, non-repeating chain of reasons can justify a belief — looks like coinduction, where an infinite structure is legitimate only if the corecursive rules are productive: each step must yield something determinate rather than merely deferring to another step.

I want to know whether this mapping is deep or superficial, and where it breaks. If it is deep, one would expect a notion close to productivity to do real epistemological work, separating benign infinite regresses from vicious ones by whether each deferral also contributes some justificatory content. Does that criterion correspond to anything in actual debates about infinitism, or is it a criterion imported from the wrong kind of theory? If it is superficial, then the coherentism-as-fixed-point idea should merely restate coherentism without adding anything — does it? And what is something the parallel would predict that turns out false?

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
