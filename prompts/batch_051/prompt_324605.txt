I teach an upper-level seminar on language and artificial intelligence with 14 students and a single 90-minute session. I want that session to make the spatial picture of semantic meaning felt from the inside before any technical vocabulary is introduced. Design a runnable classroom game in which the students themselves act as words being embedded in a shared semantic space, with rules, roles, and a scoring scheme arranged so that the following insights emerge from what participants do rather than from an opening lecture: (1) similarity is nearness, and a word's meaning is largely its neighborhood; (2) a stable word-to-word relation is a reusable direction, so that once the class discovers one instance of a relation they can move other words along the same offset — the way king minus man plus woman lands near queen; (3) the dimensions of the space are latent and recovered from similarity judgments rather than announced in advance; and (4) a single vector per word is an idealization that breaks when a word has two incompatible senses or shifts with context. The debrief should map those four moments onto the standard machinery of distributional semantics: nearest-neighbor retrieval, cosine similarity versus Euclidean distance, the vector-offset account of analogy, and polysemy as the clearest failure of a flat one-vector-per-word model. Please give me: a materials list; a minute-by-minute timeline for the 90 minutes; a payoff or scoring system that keeps the exercise a game rather than an assessment; a prediction of what will go wrong the first time I run it and how to correct for it in the moment; and a frank note on what the activity cannot reproduce about real embedding spaces — above all their high dimensionality and the fact that they arise from large-scale distributional co-occurrence — which I will have to supply in the debrief. The students should leave seeing that the spatial picture is a genuine discovery about how meaning can be structured, not merely a metaphor, while also seeing exactly where it distorts.
