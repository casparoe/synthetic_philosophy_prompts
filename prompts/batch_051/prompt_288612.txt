I'm writing a one-pager on the ethics of war for my seminar and I'd like someone to check where the seams are before I present it. My current view, as I've been describing it to people:

(1) I accept the moral equality of combatants. A regular soldier on the unjust side who obeys the in-war rules (jus in bello) doesn't wrong the enemy soldiers he kills just because he's fighting for an unjust cause. The wrongs of the war itself sit with the state, not with him. Obeying the rules of engagement puts the two soldiers on the same footing when it comes to killing each other.

(2) But I'm not a "war has its own morality" person either. I think the exact same principles of self-defense and liability to defensive harm that apply to civilians apply to soldiers. Merely being a "combatant" isn't what makes someone killable; it has to be something about what they're doing.

(3) In ordinary life I think a reasonable false belief excuses you from blame while still not giving you the permission a true belief would. If you reasonably but wrongly think I'm attacking you and you shoot me, you're not blameworthy, but your act is still objectively wrongful, and I don't wrong you by using defensive force in return.

(4) I also think there's a difference between being excused and being non-responsible. An excused attacker is still responsible for the threat they're posing, and that's what makes them liable to defensive force. Contrast the person who gets thrown at you by someone else: they aren't responsible for the threat, so they aren't liable, and you'd have to accept a lot more cost before harming them.

(5) So I dont think conscripts being lied to, propagandized, and coerced means they're not responsible for the threats they pose. It usually just means they're not blameworthy for those threats. Responsibility for a threat and blame for it need to be kept apart.

I'd like to keep all five, but I'm not sure they hang together. Go through them and tell me which parts are actually in tension, which pairs only look tense and why they dissolve, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest fix is and what I'd have to give up for it. Please write your answer in American English.
