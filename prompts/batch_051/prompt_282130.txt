Alan Turing argued that the most promising route to machine intelligence was not to program the rules of an adult mind directly but to build a relatively unformed child machine and educate it, so that a teacher's problems, rewards, and corrections would supply what explicit programming could not. A later development seems to bypass this route: present-day large language models are not educated in anything like that sense. They are pretrained by next-token prediction on a huge, undifferentiated corpus and then briefly shaped by fine-tuning and human feedback. Treat this as a test of Turing's education argument. Does the argument survive the existence of capable systems that never passed through a teacher-structured course of education?
