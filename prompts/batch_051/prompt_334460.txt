I'm teaching a third-year undergraduate module on rationality and strategic choice, cross-listed between philosophy and economics, and I'm setting the final exam. The final unit is on dominance reasoning and rationalizability, and I've noticed a recurring pattern: students can define a dominant strategy and mechanically delete dominated strategies, but they stumble the moment a strategy is dominated only by a mixture of other strategies rather than by any single alternative. They often deny that a mixed strategy can have normative force because, as they put it, you cannot literally randomize for no reason, or they conflate the mixed strategies used in dominance arguments with mixed-strategy equilibria. I want an exam question that separates students who understand the conceptual rationale for mixed-strategy iterated elimination from those who have only memorized the procedure.

Please write one structured 25-mark question, with two or three parts, for a 50-minute closed-book final, centered on iterated elimination via dominance where mixed strategies are allowed. Invent a small finite game — a 2×3 or 3×3 payoff matrix — in which some pure strategy is strictly dominated by a mixed strategy but not by any pure strategy, and in which further elimination becomes possible once the first strategy is removed. The question should require students to (i) perform the elimination and specify the relevant mixture, (ii) explain what it means for a mixed strategy to strictly dominate a pure strategy and why this comparison does not require anyone actually to play the mixture, (iii) justify why iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies remains legitimate even though the eliminating mixture may itself later be eliminated, and (iv) say briefly what the resulting surviving set tells us, if anything, about rationalizability in a finite two-player game. Then provide a model answer of roughly 600–800 words and a marking scheme that allocates the 25 marks across the parts. For each type of wrong answer that should lose marks, the scheme should name the underlying misunderstanding it reveals — for instance, the belief that a strictly dominated strategy is one that is never a best response only to pure strategies, or that an eliminating mixture must be a plan the player would actually adopt. Please favour conceptually revealing mistakes over mere arithmetic slips.

One further constraint: if any part of this request rests on a misconception on my side — for example, if the link between surviving mixed-strategy iterated elimination and rationalizability is not as clean as I have implied, or if the question as I have described it cannot be made internally coherent without taking on a contested game-theoretic commitment — say so directly and give me the nearest defensible version of the question instead of quietly writing around the problem. I would rather be corrected than receive a polished exam item that tests a false or confused idea.
