ok so for english class i have to turn in a satirical piece, and i want it to be a deadpan parody of one of those school guidance counselor handouts about managing your emotions — like a worksheet that's suppose to help you stop feeling envious or resentful of other students, with little checkboxes, "reflection prompts," and a gratitude exercise at the end. the thing i want the satire to actually land is that the school keeps treating resentment like it's purely a private attitude problem you fix with breathing and journaling, when some of the stuff kids are bitter about is real unfairness (favoritism, who gets extra time on tests, whose parents got them an internship, whatever). i read this idea somewhere — rawls? i think? — that envy can be excusable when the inequality behind it is genuinely unjust, and i want that running underneath the whole handout but never said out loud. so it should be completely straight-faced and official-sounding, like an actual wellness program, and it should gradually make the reader feel like the school is just training kids to relabel genuine wrongs as personal attitude problems. the jokes should come from exaggerating how these things actually work — like how they put "i'm jealous she has nicer shoes" in the same category as "i'm angry the same teacher let him redo the essay and not me" — not from inventing cartoonish stuff. deadpan, no winking, and the target should be the practice/genre, not any real person. around 600-700 words, formatted like a worksheet with sections and a final "challenge."
