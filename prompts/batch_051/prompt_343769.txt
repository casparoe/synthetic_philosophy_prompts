I'm a retired physical chemist — thirty-odd years doing NMR and reaction kinetics — and I've only recently started reading philosophy seriously. Lately I've been going through Huizinga's Homo Ludens and Pieper's Leisure: The Basis of Culture, and the thing that keeps striking me is that the culture these books diagnose is exactly the culture that now co-opts their ideas. So I want a piece of satire that picks at that.

Write a deadpan internal memo from the Chief Wellbeing Officer of an invented tech company (call it something like Veridian Dynamics) announcing a new company-wide initiative: mandatory, quantified play and leisure. Onion or McSweeney's style. Give me a subject line plus about 450–550 words. The voice should be HR-corporate and never wink.

The jokes should come from getting the philosophy right, not from generic "HR says something dorky" stuff. So the memo should misappropriate the actual concepts in a way that someone who's read the books would recognize: scheduled unpromptedness, mandatory voluntariness, logged "autotelic episodes," KPI targets for the felt sense that an activity is its own end, a "magic circle" room with entry and exit surveys, leisure pitched as a core competency whose non-instrumental character is precisely what will drive Q3 productivity. Maybe have the officer loosely paraphrase Huizinga or Pieper and then draw exactly the wrong conclusion — but don't invent a verbatim quotation. The deadpan should escalate until the thing is clearly deranged without ever breaking character.
