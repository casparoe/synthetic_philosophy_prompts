Recently, a close friend and I have been reading about artificial general intelligence, and he made an unusual proposal. He suggested that we write out a formal agreement now about what we would each do if either of us ever gained a position of real influence over, or within, such a future system. Under the agreement, if he ends up with meaningful power over a system like that, he would have to use part of it to protect me and my family and to give me a genuine voice in important decisions, and I would owe him the same. At first this seemed prudent, since neither of us knows where we will end up. An ordinary written contract would not help, of course, because no existing court could enforce it after the world has changed that much. But the more I consider it, the less certain I am that the pact means anything even between the two of us. If the future version of my friend — or a copy of him, or an AI built from his values — has changed enormously, why would he still care about a promise made in high school? And if the promise cannot actually constrain him later, is there any point in making it now? Could the act of making the promise itself still matter, perhaps by shaping what he would later want or by making our friendship more likely to survive the transition? I cannot tell whether our pact is a genuine commitment, a real way of influencing the future, or just a reassuring story. How should I think about this kind of agreement, and would you advise me to enter into it?
