I've been turning over a four-move centipede game and I've managed to derive a contradiction out of it. Hoping someone here can tell me which move in the arguement is doing the sneaky work, because my reading group all pointed at different lines.

The game: P1 moves at nodes 1 and 3, P2 at nodes 2 and 4. A player can Take (game ends) or Pass (move on). Terminal payoffs in (P1, P2) order:

- Take at node 1: (4, 1)
- Take at node 2: (2, 8)
- Take at node 3: (16, 4)
- Take at node 4: (8, 32)
- Pass at node 4: (64, 16)

Standard backward induction gives: at node 4 P2 takes, since 32 > 16. So at node 3 P1 takes: 16 now beats the 8 he'd get after passing to a taking P2. At node 2 P2 takes: 8 now beats the 4 he'd get after passing to a taking P1. At node 1 P1 takes: 4 now beats the 2 he'd get after passing to a taking P2. So P1 takes on move one.

Now the part that's messing with me.

(1) At nodes 1–3 the whole proof is just reapplying the rule: *if I pass, the next player will take, and my payoff after that take is lower than my take payoff now; so I take now.* Call it R.

(2) The only thing node 4 adds is a boring base-case fact: P2 prefers 32 to 16. Everything upstream is pure R.

(3) So if we flip that one terminal preference—change Pass at node 4 to (64, 48), making P2 prefer to pass—R should still force a Take at nodes 1–3, because R never mentions the terminal payoffs.

(4) But actual backward induction on the flipped game has everyone passing to the end: P1 passes at node 1, P2 at 2, P1 at 3, then P2 at 4, because everyone prefers the eventual (64, 48) to taking early.

(5) So either R doesn't actually do the work the proof says it does, or backward induction is fragile in a way that makes the early steps just decorative.

I'd rather keep R, since it feels like plain sequential rationality, but something has to go. Which numbered claim is the bad one, and what exactly does R need added to it (or to it's surroundings) before its a valid move? Keep the answer to at most 300 words, and start with a one-sentence bottom line.
