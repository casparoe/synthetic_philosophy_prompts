In my reporting on generative AI, I keep encountering the word 'hallucination' used for everything from a wrong date to an invented citation, and I have come to suspect that the loose vocabulary is obscuring a real conceptual question. The position I am inclined to defend is that the characteristic failure of a large language model — the fluent, confident, plausible falsehood — is neither lying nor ordinary error, but bullshit in something like Frankfurt's familiar sense. On that account, the liar has some grip on the truth and deliberately misleads; the person in error aims at the truth and misses; but the process that produces an LLM's confabulations is not aimed at the truth in the first place. A model trained to continue text in a statistically expected direction can assert something false without any belief-like representation misfiring, because the constraint of truth was never governing the output. If that is right, then treating a hallucination as a corrigible mistake made by a sloppy informant is itself a category error, and the sensible policy is to regard the model's prose as text that may or may not happen to coincide with the facts rather than as a report from a truth-tracking process.

What I want is a map of what would count against this view. I want to know which findings about how these systems actually behave — internal representations that track truth independently of generated prose, reliable self-correction in the absence of external feedback, calibrated uncertainty about their own outputs, or failure patterns that look like ordinary retrieval lapses — would force me to reclassify some or all hallucination as error, or even as something closer to lying. I also want the philosophical pressure tested from the other side: Frankfurt's category may require an agent who is indifferent to truth, and a language model may not be an agent with attitudes at all, in which case the whole taxonomy could be an anthropomorphic projection rather than an explanatory classification. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say explicitly which objections a defender of the bullshit classification should concede count against it rather than dismiss, and give an honest assessment of whether the position can absorb all the qualifications and thereby become unfalsifiable — and, if it can, whether that is a problem for a claim of this kind.
