You are a scholar of early Christian thought with training in analytic philosophy. I want you to work through an apparent collision between two New Testament statements and tell me how it applies to a concrete modern case. James 2:24 (NRSVue) reads, “You see that a person is justified by works and not by faith alone.” Romans 3:28 (NRSVue) reads, “For we hold that a person is justified by faith apart from works prescribed by the law.” On their face these seem to pull in different directions, and I want you to take the disagreement seriously rather than dissolving it too quickly.

The case is this. A friend of mine runs a large online Christian community whose central claim is that a believer is already wholly justified by faith, so that later failures cannot affect her standing before God. She has begun to teach that, because Paul says justification is by faith apart from works prescribed by the law, feeding the hungry and making restitution to people she has defrauded are at most matters of discipleship and witness—never something on which her justification in any way turns. When someone quotes James against her, she replies that James’s “justified by works” must mean only “shown to be righteous before other people,” and that James was addressing a first-century quarrel that could not have foreseen a community like hers.

I want a verdict, not a survey of scholarly positions. How does James 2:24, read as its original audience would have read it, reach or fail to reach her teaching and practice? How does Romans 3:28, read the same way, license or fail to license the inference she draws from it? If the two passages would pull apart in this case, which should a Christian in her community feel bound by here, and why? Make clear which senses of “works,” “faith,” and “justified” you are relying on, and think carefully before settling on an answer; begin by brainstorming the main ways the apparent conflict has been handled, then defend the one you judge best.
