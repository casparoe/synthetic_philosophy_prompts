For our film-philosophy group I somehow ended up running a session on Ex Machina, and I don't want it to be another could-AI-be-conscious chat. What I actually want to think through is how the film handles the question of what grounds sentience or moral status. It's built around a test that mostly measures whether Caleb will take Ava to have a mind, and it treats that attribution as evidence about whether she really does; then near the end Caleb starts cutting himself open to check whether he's human, as if the real criterion is what you're made of or how you're wired. Meanwhile Kyoko can't pass a verbal test and the film still seems to grant her an inner life. I'd like a reading of the film through that tangle—passing for a person, running the right kind of computations, being capable of suffering, being the kind of thing whose interests can be thwarted—where the film genuinely complicates the question and where it quietly answers it without earning the right. I'm fine with the conclusion that it's less coherent than it looks.
