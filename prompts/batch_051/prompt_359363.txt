There is a dispute in moral philosophy that has seemed to me to be stalled for as long as I have been reading about it, and I want a diagnosis of why it is stuck rather than another brief for one side. The dispute is whether there is a morally significant difference between lying and merely misleading. By lying I mean asserting what one believes to be false; by misleading I mean deliberately bringing someone to a false belief without strictly asserting a falsehood, for instance by saying something true that one knows will be interpreted wrongly. One side holds that the difference is real and significant. On this view, the liar makes an assertion, a speech act governed by a norm of truthfulness, and thereby betrays a trust that the misleader does not betray in the same direct way; the misleader may be manipulative, but he has not bound himself to the falsehood as the liar has. The other side holds that the difference is morally idle. On this view, what matters is that both agents deliberately bring about a false belief for their own purposes; whether the falsehood travels by assertion or by implicature is a technical difference that should not carry moral weight, and the misleader's capacity to say that he never said the false thing is, if anything, an aggravating circumstance rather than a defence.

The strange feature of the debate is that the two sides largely agree on cases. Both allow that a carefully constructed half-truth can be more damaging than a careless lie, and both allow that some lies are harmless. Yet the comparative judgment—whether lying is in general worse than misleading—never settles. My working hypothesis is that the two sides are grading different things: one is grading the act as a betrayal of the norm of assertion, while the other is grading it as a strategy for inducing false belief and evading accountability. If that is right, then the word "worse" is doing double duty, and the dispute is partly but not wholly verbal: a substantive question would remain about which dimension, if either, is the morally fundamental one.

I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this really is: a hidden ambiguity in the phrase "morally worse," a clash between deontological and consequentialist starting intuitions, an empirical question about how people actually weigh assertion against implicature, or an ill-posed question that runs several distinct inquiries together. And, given your diagnosis, what would count as progress—a distinction, an experiment, a formal model of assertion and implicature, a reformulation of the question, or an argument that no shared standard could settle it?
