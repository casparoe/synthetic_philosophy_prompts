I'm running a small reading group on Abelard's ethics, and we're about to hit his claim that the whole sin is in consenting to what you believe is wrong — the external deed adds nothing. I want to open the session with a 'which theory of culpability do you actually hold?' quiz that sorts people by how they judge cases, not by which label they'd pick for themselves. Please write me the quiz. Eight to ten questions, each a short concrete case where the intention, the outward act, and the outcome come apart. Use mostly medieval-flavored cases (a hunt gone wrong, a serf defending himself, a monk in a situation he didn't seek out, alms given for the wrong reason, two people committing the same deed with different inner consent) — you can invent them, but include at least one modern case if it actually does diagnostic work. Two or three answer options per question, each forcing a choice about where the sin lies: in the inner consent, in the deed, in the damage, in what the person should have known. Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns to positions: the Abelardian consent-only view, the external-act-adds-to-the-sin view, a negligence view, an outcome-based view, and any others the cases genuinely tease apart. Flag pairs the quiz can't seperate instead of pretending a question does. Make sure there's at least one case where someone consents to a sin but never does the deed, one where the deed happens without consent (accident, coercion, or ignorance), and one where same deed, different consent. The questions must be answerable by people who've never read a line of Abelard, and please don't have any question telegraph the scoring by using the word 'consent' in the options. I want someone who actually is Abelardian about these cases to land on the consent-only leaf, and someone who thinks the outward act makes it worse to land somewhere else — ideally they'd be a bit suprised by where the quiz puts them.
