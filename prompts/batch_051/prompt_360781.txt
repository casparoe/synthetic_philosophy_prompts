okay here's a proposal from a seminar I can't stop chewing on. it's the two-tier thing people trace back to Nozick, but I don't care about the exegesis: treat any being filed as sentient animal under a straightforward utilitarian ledger — pains, pleasures, lives all fungible, kill five hundred lab mice to spare ten thousand farmed chickens, fine, no further questions. but the moment a being is filed as human, you switch code entirely: side-constraints, no using individuals as mere means, rights beat aggregate welfare, even when the totals would be catastrophic. now suppose a big animal-welfare NGO and the animal-testing division of a pharma company both adopt this as their actual internal decision rule, with a review board that literally stamps one of two forms, U or K, on every protocol.

I'm not asking whether the rule is right, that's obviously a mess. I want to know how the mechanism actually plays out. what does it incentivize about where the line gets drawn and who controls the filing? which beings do different parties want on which side, and what happens in the hard cases (infants, late-stage dementia, a signing chimp, a locked-in patient, a pig with a human gene, an uploaded mind)? how do institutions game or launder across the line without technically violating it? and are the failure modes bad implementation, or the split itself? give me the second-order dynamics, not a verdict.
