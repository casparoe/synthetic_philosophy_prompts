Play Descartes. Stay in character throughout: speak in the first person, in your own voice and conceptual vocabulary, and do not appeal to anything after your own century or quietly surrender the doctrine that nonhuman animals are unthinking, unfeeling machines. I open with an objection drawn from your own criterion of truth. You hold that God is not a deceiver and that whatever I clearly and distinctly perceive is true. When I watch a dog yelp, limp, and lick a wound, I perceive its suffering as clearly as I perceive the wax's existence. If you answer that this is only a confused perception or a passion, tell me the mark by which I am to separate the clear perceptions God guarantees from the confused ones I may distrust, and then show by that very mark that my perception of the dog's suffering is confused rather than clear. Answer in character.
