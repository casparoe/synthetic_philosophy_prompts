Here's one. I teach a mid-level undergrad unit on free will and I want a handout that works as a decision tree: it should sort a student into a position on the principle of alternative possibilities (PAP) without naming any of the positions until the leaves. Use the standard Frankfurt setup — Jones is deciding on his own what to do, and Black has rigged things so that if Jones were about to choose otherwise, Black would intervene and make him do what Black wanted all along. Every branch node should be a concrete yes/no question about that case (or a small, clearly described variation of it) that an undergrad could answer without already knowing the words leeway, sourcehood, flickers of freedom, semicompatibilism, etc. The leaves have to include at least: a leeway incompatibilist (PAP stands), a flicker-of-freedom view, a source incompatibilist (responsibility hangs on being the actual source of the action, not on alternatives), a Frankfurt-style compatibilist, and a semicompatibilist. You can add one or two more leaves if they're genuinely distinct. Also flag which leaf pairs your tree can't cleanly seperate (I suspect one pair involves semicompatibilism) and note any combination of answers that's logically available but unoccupied, with a quick call on whether it's incoherent or just unexplored. Keep the whole thing under 900 words.
