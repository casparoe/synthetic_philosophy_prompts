I have no background in philosophy or law, but I keep returning to a question: whether it ever makes sense to say that a state itself, rather than the particular people running it, has done something wrong. The position I keep falling into is that, strictly speaking, it does not. A state has no single mind and cannot form the kind of intention that wrongdoing seems to require. Saying that a country committed an injustice is shorthand for saying that identifiable officials made culpable decisions while acting within their roles. On this view, moral responsibility always bottoms out in individual officeholders, and the state is best thought of as a structure, or a set of roles and procedures, rather than as an agent that can be blamed. The old legal idea that the sovereign cannot be sued looks to me like a clumsy expression of a basically sound instinct: the sovereign is not the kind of thing that can be a wrongdoer in the first place.

I want to know how exposed this view actually is. What would have to be true for me to be wrong? I can see a few pressure points, but I do not know how seriously to take them. A state persists while its officials come and go, and it keeps obligations such as debts and treaties across those changes, which suggests it is something over and above its current members. We also treat corporations as capable of wrongdoing in at least a loose sense, so singling out states may be arbitrary. And there are cases where a widely shared practice produces serious harm even though no single official had a culpable intention; my view seems unable to name the wrong in such cases. But I am unsure whether these are fatal, merely awkward, or just confusions.

Please lay out what would count against the position, as precisely as you can: the strongest cases, arguments, and intuitions, sorted from fatal to merely awkward. For each, say whether a committed defender of the view would grant that it counts against the position or would dismiss it, and why. I would also like an honest assessment of whether the view can be adjusted to absorb every apparent counterexample, and, if it can, whether that is a problem rather than a virtue. Finally, tell me what the view commits me to that I might not have noticed, and which of those hidden commitments leave it most exposed.
