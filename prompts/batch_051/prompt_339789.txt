A friend and i got into this over whether a scribble on a whiteboard was "really an a", and he walked me through the following, and now i can't find the exit:

(1) What makes a mark an a has to come down to either how the mark looks (shape) or what the mark does (role: where it sorts in the alphabet, what sound it maps to, what key types it, what readers take it as). Anything about the writer's intention or history can only matter through one of those two, since a mark with no known writer still is or isn't an a.

(2) Shape has to matter at least a little. Take a glyph shaped exactly like a b, and use it in every a-role — sorted under a, read as a, typed by the a key. It would still just be a b doing the a's job, not an a.

(3) But shape can't settle letter identity on its own, because shape space is continuous. Start from a paradigm a (say the single-storey one in Comic Sans) and morph it into a paradigm b through a long series of tiny tweaks, each tweak no larger then the difference between two sloppy handwritten a's that any reader would unhesitatingly call the same letter.

(4) If shape were the whole story, there would have to be an exact step in that chain where the mark stops being an a. But no one can point to that step, and any particular step looks arbitrary.

(5) If the only available cutoffs are arbitrary, then there is no fact of the matter about where the a's end and the b's begin in the chain.

(6) So shape alone can't be the whole story, and by (2) role alone can't be either. But by (1) those were the only options, so nothing makes a mark an a rather than a b.

(7) So the letters aren't real kinds; the alphabet is a rough convention over a continuum, and the difference between a reader who knows the alphabet and one who doesn't is a kind of collective illusion.

That conclusion is obviously wrong — learning to read is not learning an illusion — but i can't see which premise I'm allowed to throw out. I'd most like to keep (2), since the idea that a b-looking mark could literally be an a feels like a joke. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost me?
