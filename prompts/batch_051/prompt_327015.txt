Daniel Dennett distinguishes four kinds of minds, arranged as a hierarchy of generate-and-test. Darwinian creatures are designed only by natural selection: their variants are tested by survival across generations. Skinnerian creatures can also be shaped within a lifetime: their overt actions are tested by the consequences the world returns. Popperian creatures add offline testing: they run candidate actions inside an internal model and discard the mistaken candidates there, so that the creature need not suffer every error in reality. Gregorian creatures add one further step: they can offload parts of their cognitive work into public, shareable structures—words, notations, algorithms, records—and then let later thoughts, their own and other people's, run further tests on those structures.

Compare the Popperian and Gregorian rungs. Is the Gregorian addition best classified as a difference in degree—a faster, wider, socialized version of the Popperian trick of offline trial-and-error—or as a difference in kind—a distinct structure of mind rather than merely a more powerful one? State a criterion for a difference of kind rather than degree that does not simply stipulate the conclusion, apply it to the two rungs, and take a definite side. In doing so, spell out exactly what public language contributes to the Popperian economy of simulation, and say whether a solitary Popperian creature with unlimited internal computational power could in principle dispense with the Gregorian level.
