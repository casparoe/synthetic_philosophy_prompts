Since retiring from chemistry, I have begun reading philosophy of science, and one of my side interests has become archaeology. The debate I want help diagnosing is the long-running argument over what to make of so-called societal collapses. One camp—often the one that reaches a popular audience—treats cases such as the Classic Maya lowlands, Rapa Nui (Easter Island), and the Late Bronze Age Mediterranean as more or less clear cases of failure: populations fell or dispersed, monumental construction stopped, and centralized political systems came apart. Another camp, increasingly prominent within archaeology, insists these were not endings but reorganizations: people persisted, communities reconfigured themselves, and elite institutions or urban centers gave way to different but viable social arrangements. The odd thing is that the two camps largely accept the same sites, dates, and material evidence, yet the argument continues without much movement.

My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is at root conceptual: the two camps are using the word 'collapse' with different implicit criteria—one keyed to the end of a state-level political order and its elite culture, the other keyed to continuity of a population and its everyday practices—and much of the dispute is a failure to make those criteria explicit. But I am unsure whether that is the whole story, or whether there is a substantive empirical disagreement lurking underneath about the abruptness, scale, or human costs of the changes.

My question is this: why has this debate stalled, and what kind of disagreement is it really? I am not asking for a survey of the archaeological cases nor for a verdict on who is right about Rapa Nui or the Maya. I want a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement: is it verbal or substantive? Do the two camps disagree about the facts, about which evidence matters, about the criterion for applying 'collapse,' or about background values—for instance, whether continuity of elites or of ordinary people matters more when deciding whether a society has ended? And given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a sharper definition of 'collapse,' a particular kind of evidence, a change of question, or something else? Please indicate how confident you are in your diagnosis and single out the point you are least sure of.
