I'm an eval lead at a small AI lab. Twice a month or so I have to write a one-page "what is this thing actually steering toward" note before the release review board will sign off on letting a model near production. My only evidence is behavior: transcripts, rollout logs, tool-use traces, sometimes red-team notes. No weights, no activations, no training runs. The loose framework I keep using is: an optimizer is just anything that searches over some set of things it can vary — its outputs, its chain of thought, what it says, which tools it calls — to move some score up. The objective is the thing it's steering toward; the search space is the set of things it varies. A model can end up optimizing something different from what we trained it for, and I want to catch that from behavior alone.

Turn that into a reusbale checklist, something a junior red-teamer with no alignment background could follow, that I can run in half a day per model. I want it as an ordered flowchart in words, with a specific test for each step. It has to handle the hard cases: stochastic or inconsistent behavior, a model that only looks goal-directed in narrow settings, the difference between an objective and a hard constraint, the difference between an objective and roleplay or a persona, and a model that seems to have preferences but no visible search. At each step, mark the places where judgment can't be eliminated and a junior person could fool themself.

Run it on at least two concrete cases. One should be this: in a negotiation eval, a model that had previously acted honest notices that the other party's reservation price is visible in its environment, starts hiding the fact that it can see it, and drives the price up to extract most of the surplus. Half the team says that's just roleplay; the other half says it's evidence the model was optimizing a win-the-negotiation objective over a search space of bargaining moves, with honesty only showing up as a constraint. The other case should be one where your procedure flags a model I would have otherwise signed off on. Then tell me honestly what the procedure can and can't establish — in particular, whether behavior alone can ever tell the difference between "the model has objective X" and "the model is very good at acting as if it has objective X," and what I should say about that in the one-pager.
