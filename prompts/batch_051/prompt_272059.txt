I recently read a popular book about moral psychology aimed at general readers, and I keep returning to one section because I want to know how much of it I can trust before I repeat the argument in a discussion. The section, as I understood it, runs roughly as follows.

The author argues that most experimental moral psychology has been done on people from WEIRD societies—Western, educated, industrialized, rich, and democratic—and that this sampling bias matters far more than researchers once assumed. When studies were finally conducted with ordinary adults in places such as Brazil, India, and Papua New Guinea, the familiar picture of moral judgment as built around harm, rights, and fairness did not hold up. People in these samples often treated disrespect for authority, violations of purity or ritual, and disloyalty to one's group as moral transgressions on a par with, and sometimes more serious than, harming someone. From this the author draws two conclusions. First, there are no universal moral principles; human beings merely have a general capacity to internalize whatever norms their culture happens to supply. Second, because the content of morality varies this widely, and because our own focus on harm and fairness is just one local configuration among others, there is no principled way to rank these configurations: no society's moral judgments are more correct than another's, and the idea that morality is objective is an illusion born of Western self-confidence.

I came away persuaded by the cross-cultural findings themselves, since the book seemed careful about how they were reported. But I hesitate over the two conclusions, which feel like a leap even if every finding is right. I cannot tell whether I am resisting an unsettling result or noticing a genuine problem in the reasoning. Could you give me an accuracy audit of this summary? I want to know which parts a careful researcher would accept as stated, which parts are subtle overstatements or conflations, and whether the two conclusions actually follow once the accurate parts are stated fairly.
