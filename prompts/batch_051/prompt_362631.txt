I'm a novelist, not a data scientist, and I sit on the board of a small literary prize. We get far to many submissions to read in full, and our administrator wants to use an algorithmic tool to triage the slush pile: it scores anonymised manuscripts and flags the top few hundred for human reading. The vendors pitch is that the tool is 'fair' because it's calibrated across demographic groups. I've read enough to know there are impossibility results in algorithmic fairness: roughly, you cant have calibration and equal false positive/negative rates across groups at once when the base rates differ, so 'fairness' means choosing between criteria that mathematically can't all be satisfied. I've been asked to draft the board memo recommending whether to adopt the tool, with conditions if any, and it needs a clear recommendation the board can vote on. It should engage the impossibility results honestly rather than just hand-wave about bias, and should give a fair hearing to the strongest argument on the losing side.
