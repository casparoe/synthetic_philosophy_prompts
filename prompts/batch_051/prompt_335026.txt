I recently moved from a mostly academic background into a job as a data scientist on a team building a pretrial risk assessment model for a county court system, and I keep coming back to a philosophical problem I had only ever read about on the side. The job forces me to confront a choice that gets treated as merely technical: before we fit anything, we have to pick the development population whose outcomes define the model's base rates—all people booked on a given charge over the past five years, only those booked on the current charge type, only those with a comparable prior record, and so on. The score the model assigns to a particular defendant changes substantially depending on that choice, and there is no independent fact about which group the defendant really belongs to. The choice is ours, but it drives the numbers that then get presented to a judge as if they were facts about the defendant.

Lately this has struck me as the reference class problem from the philosophy of probability: on a frequentist account, the probability of a single event is the relative frequency in the appropriate reference class, but there is no uniquely correct reference class, and different classes can yield very different probabilities. I want to know whether I have found a genuine structural identity or am just doing loose pattern matching between two things that both involve groups and percentages. If the parallel is genuine, what would have to be true of both the philosophical problem and the modeling problem for results to transfer in either direction? Does anything from the reference class discussion carry over to algorithm design—for example, about why the problem does not dissolve as one gathers more data, or why the narrowest available class is not obviously the right one? Conversely, does the engineering setting offer a resolution the philosophers lack, such as out-of-sample validation, calibration, or an explicit decision-theoretic framing in terms of what the score will be used to do?

I would also like an honest account of where the mapping breaks. Name something the parallel would lead me to expect that turns out false on one side or the other, and say what that failure tells us about whether the two problems are really the same problem. Please write the answer in American English.
