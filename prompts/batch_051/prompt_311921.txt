I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and a recent argument at work has left me puzzled about intentionality. One colleague argued that our production machine-learning models don't genuinely understand what they process, citing Searle's Chinese Room: an English speaker inside a room follows written rules to manipulate Chinese symbols and produces convincing Chinese replies without understanding a word of Chinese, so running a program cannot by itself produce understanding. Another colleague answered with Dennett's two-bitser example, which was new to me. As he presented it, a vending machine that was being cheated with quarter-sized slugs was retrofitted with a detector that distinguishes real quarters from slugs by their physical properties, and Dennett treats the machine as genuinely detecting quarters; the lesson is supposed to be that Searle's boundary between original and derived intentionality is less principled than it looks.

That sounds right to me, but I suspect I am overreading it. If the two-bitser already has beliefs about quarters, should I conclude that our fraud classifier literally believes transactions are fraudulent, and that the Chinese Room system literally understands Chinese? And if that follows, why is Searle's argument still treated as a serious challenge to strong AI rather than dismissed as a rhetorical trick? Please answer in at most 200 words.
