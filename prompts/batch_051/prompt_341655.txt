Here's an asymmetry about names I can't get straight. Nobody treats it as a loss when a first name dies out — if nobody calls their kid Edith for a couple generations, no committee forms, no fund is set up. But if a family name is down to one elderly person with no kids, people act like something is about to vanish from the world: relatives beg a nephew to hyphenate, wills include instructions, people genuinely grieve. And yet both names are just strings of sounds assigned to you at birth, both inherited without your consent, and both do the same basic referential job of letting other people pick you out. Is there a defensible principle that makes a surname more worth preserving than a given name, or is it just lineage-worship plus legal inertia? I want the strongest justification you can find, and then an honest verdict on whether it survies a case where the given name is unique in exactly the way the endangered surname is.
