In a reading group on political thought, one participant keeps glossing Kant's essay on perpetual peace through the domestic analogy: just as individuals escape the state of nature by authorizing a common sovereign to enforce their rights, so states escape the international state of nature by submitting to a world government — and she reads Perpetual Peace as, at bottom, an argument for exactly that. Audit this analogy for the group. Build the explicit correspondence table (what plays the role of the individuals, the state of nature, the social contract, and the enforcing sovereign at the international level), then show where Kant himself resists the mapping — his preference for a voluntary pacific federation over a coercive world republic and the reasons he offers for it. Separate the disanalogies that matter for her conclusion from those that don't, name what the domestic picture quietly imports into the international case, and give a definite verdict on whether the analogy can carry the conclusion that perpetual peace requires a world government — and, if not, what a better analogy would look like.
