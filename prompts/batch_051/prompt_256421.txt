I run a philosophy discussion group for graduate students, and every semester the same two topics—whether the universe had a beginning, and whether free will exists even if every event has a cause—turn into grinding arguments where each side is convinced it has a proof. I want a reusable procedure I can actually run at the table, not a reading list, to decide whether we’re in a Kantian antinomy or just a normal unresolved dispute. Anchor it in this passage from Kant, Critique of Pure Reason, Meiklejohn translation:

"Human reason, in one sphere of its cognition, is called upon to consider questions, which it cannot decline, as they are presented by its own nature, but which it cannot answer, as they transcend every faculty of the mind."

I want the procedure to be a checklist or flow of questions, in order, with what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with “yes, this is an antinomy” or “no, this is ordinary disagreement,” and where it hands the decision to a person because a step is a judgment call. Then run it on two cases: (1) whether the world has a beginning in time, and (2) whether free will exists even if every event has a cause. Make sure at least one case comes out a way I won’t like. Finally, tell me what the procedure cannot do, and if this whole setup rests on a misreading of Kant, say so plainly instead of forcing the procedure to work.
