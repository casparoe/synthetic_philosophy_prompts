I run a philosophy club at my high school, about 15 of us, and we have an hour. We were reading Epictetus's *Enchiridion* and got stuck on this passage:

'Remember that the promise of desire is the attainment of what you desire, that of aversion is not to fall into what is avoided, and that he who fails in his desire is unfortunate, while he who falls into what he would avoid experiences misfortune.'

Everyone online talks about Stoicism like it's just "focus on what you can control," but this seems way stronger: it says wanting something outside your control is set up to fail, and avoiding something outside your control is also set up to fail. Design a 60-minute game or activity for us that makes that stronger claim feel real, not just like a lecture. It should have actual choices and stakes, and probly a way for people to change what they want or switch their aversions mid-game. Then a debrief that maps what happened back to this passage, says what will predictably go wrong when we run it, and is honest about what the game can't capture about the Stoic view. No lecture up front, and don't say "Stoicism" until afterwords.
