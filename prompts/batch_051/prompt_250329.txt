I am a retired research chemist. For years my professional life was governed by material balances and the discipline of not wasting reagents, and in retirement I have turned to moral philosophy with much the same cast of mind: I collect commitments and then look for places where they might react badly with one another. Lately my reading has circled around frugality — not miserliness, but the older idea of using resources well, avoiding waste, and refusing to substitute consumption for flourishing. I have set down the commitments I currently hold, and I would like a careful audit of whether they can all be kept at once.

(1) Frugality is an excellence of character, a settled disposition to judge well what a project or a life actually requires; its value is not exhausted by whatever good consequences it happens to produce downstream.
(2) An act is right just in case it produces at least as much aggregate well-being as any alternative open to the agent.
(3) Because waste is a collective problem, frugality should be promoted as a public norm — through education, pricing, and social expectation — rather than only cultivated privately.
(4) There is something morally more important about my living frugally myself than about my devoting the same effort to successfully persuading many other people to live frugally.
(5) A person of ample means who habitually wastes resources — food thrown away, things bought and never used — but who does no direct, identifiable harm to anyone has not thereby acted wrongly, though I do consider their manner of living a defect.

I would like you to take on the role of a professional philosopher working on the structure of moral theory, especially the relation between the virtues and act-consequentialism. Please begin by brainstorming the pressure points you see among these five commitments, then deliver an audit rather than a general lecture. Go through the pairs; distinguish the tensions that are genuine from those that merely seem so and dissolve on inspection; rank the genuine tensions by severity; and for each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would cost. I am not asking you to tell me which commitment is false; I want to know where the set itself is under pressure and what my options for repair are.
