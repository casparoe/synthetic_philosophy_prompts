I make games, not philosophy—mostly short "quiet" games about grief and loss. Players regularly message me some version of "I sobbed through the whole last act, 10/10." That mismatch between negative emotion and positive evaluation is apparently what philosophers call the paradox of tragedy, and I want it translated into the vocabulary I already use as a designer: intended experience, the magic circle, safe failure, emotional beats, valence, flow, tough-but-fair difficulty, agency, pacing, replayability. The claim I want pressure-tested is that the whole "paradox" collapses into something every horror or sad-game designer already does: you stage negative emotions inside a safe, optional frame and players enjoy being moved. Do a claim-by-claim translation of the main positions in that debate into that vocabulary—not just an explanation of the debate. Then give me the residue report: what survives the translation intact, what you had to fudge and exactly where, and what has no counterpart in design vocabulary at all, with a view on whether the residue is a blindspot in how designers think or a sign that the philosophical version was undercooked. And if I've set the task up wrong—if the "paradox" is really several different puzzles, or my framing smuggles in an assumption—say so plainly rather than handing me a tidy translation.
