I've been asked to run a two-hour seminar for philosophy graduate students on Spinoza's claim that understanding things as necessary changes how the mind is affected by them. The session will turn on a passage from Part V, Proposition 6 of the Ethics, where Spinoza argues, in effect, that insofar as the mind understands all things as necessary, it has greater power over the emotions and is less subject to them. I want the seminar to make that claim intelligible rather than merely therapeutic. Please plan the explanatory arc: what do students need in place about Spinoza's modal metaphysics and his account of the emotions before the proposition can do any real work; what is the best concrete example to carry the idea, and what must it avoid; which misconceptions should I expect—especially the slide into fatalistic resignation or the thought that this is just a psychological trick—and where in the sequence should I head them off; and what final question should I ask to check whether they see how the intellectual love of God is supposed to develop from this kind of understanding?
