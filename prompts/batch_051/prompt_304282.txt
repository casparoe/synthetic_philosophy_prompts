I've got a draft essay in progress on methodological individualism vs. holism, and I want comments more on the philosophical moves than on style. The seed is this passage from Mill's preface to *A System of Logic*:

"Whether moral and social phenomena are really exceptions to the general certainty and uniformity of the course of nature; and how far the methods by which so many of the laws of the physical world have been numbered among truths irrevocably acquired and universally assented to, can be made instrumental to the formation of a similar body of received doctrine in moral and political science."

My essay basically says that Mill's question already contains the mistake: he treats the issue as whether social phenomena are "exceptions" to the uniformity of nature, and that framing quietly stacks the deck for methodological individualism. I argue that if you instead start with the idea that social entities are relationally constituted—like markets, statuses, languages—then the real dispute isn't whether social laws are uniform, but whether the right units for explanation are individuals-with-properties at all. I want to claim many holist theses are not about mysterious group minds but about explanatory dependence: the properties of an individual that matter (e.g., being a landlord, being solvent) can only be individuated by reference to social structures, so individualist explanations are incomplete unless they smuggle in social facts. I then try to show Mill's own "chemical method" discussion in Book VI actually supports this point, because chemical combination is precisely a case where the compositive elements don't explain the properties of the compound, so the analogy undermines the individualist reading.

Before I flesh it out: which parts of this will a skeptical reader find too quick? Is my reading of the Mill passage over-strong, since it's just a preface sentence and he spends the rest of the book arguing moral sciences can be sciences of tendencies? And is my use of "relational constitution" doing too much work without a worked example? Comments on accuracy of the Mill reading, the structure of the argument, and what would make it interesting rather than another rehash would be appreciated.
