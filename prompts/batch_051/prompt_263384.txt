I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar in ethical theory and am drafting this year's final examination. I would like one closed-book essay question, to be answered in ninety minutes, on moral particularism and moral generalism. The question must be unanswerable by merely reciting the distinction between the two positions; it should turn on a scenario I have devised for the exam in which a committed particularist moral educator refuses to give her trainees rules or decision procedures, yet claims that she can reliably teach them to judge cases well and that her own consistent verdicts across similar cases do not commit her to any principle. The students must assess whether this position is stable, and in particular whether whatever explains successful teaching and consistency across cases is best described as case-based moral perception or as a principle in disguise. Please write the question, a model answer of approximately 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 marks. The marking scheme should name the specific moves that separate an A from a B and identify two characteristic misunderstandings that should cost marks. Please write in British English.
