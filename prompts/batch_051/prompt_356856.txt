I'm a PhD student in cognitive neuroscience who also spends too much time with the philosophy of mind crowd, and I keep going in circles on a disagreement with my advisor. She says that if we ever get good enough at targeted cortical stimulation, we could in principle give someone a genuinely new color — not just an exotic blend or a reshuffling of familiar hues, but a simple, novel quale outside anything human vision has ever had access to. Her favorite toy example is the forbidden colors: the classic opponent-process story says reddish-green and bluish-yellow can't appear as unitary colors to human observers, even though they're not logically impossible, and maybe a brain interface could pry the opponent channels apart and deliver exactly that. I think the claim is either untestable or collapses into "a new combination of old feelings," and she says I'm being defeatist. The deeper problem, I think, is that color space as we measure it — hue, saturation, brightness — might be the wrong frame for asking about genuinely new qualities.

So here's what I want from you. Act as an expert in philosophy of mind and psychophysics, and think carefully before answering — maybe start by brainstorming how you'd operationalize "new simple quale" at all. If a lab actually managed to stimulate cortex and a subject reported "I'm seeing a color I've never seen before and I can't describe it," what could tell us whether they are having a genuinely new simple quale rather than just a new compound of familiar hues, or even a weird illusion? Design the strongest experiment you can — things like discrimination training, similarity ratings, multidimensional scaling, decoding of responses, whatever is fair game — and say exactly which result would favor each reading. Then be honest about what even a positive result would not settle: could we ever rule out that the subject just acquired a new compound, or that the experience is only "new relative to their past experience"? And is the distinction between simple and compound qualia even well-defined enough to be the target of an experiment, or do we need to fix that concept before running any subjects?
