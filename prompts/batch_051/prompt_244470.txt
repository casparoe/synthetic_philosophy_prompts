I am the student chair of my high school's Honor Council, which meets weekly to recommend disciplinary responses for academic dishonesty and peer mistreatment; any recommendation goes to the assistant principal for approval. Our members are split between those who favor guilt-based responses, aimed at making a student see the act as wrong and repair it, and those who favor shame-based responses, aimed at making the student feel the community's condemnation and lose standing. I need a single reusable decision procedure we can run in a 20-minute deliberation, written for people without psychology training, that decides which kind of response, if either, a given offense should receive. Ground it in the distinction that guilt is about a bad action while shame is about a bad self, and in whatever is known about how the two emotions affect repair, avoidance, and re-offending; also make the procedure state explicitly when we are making a value choice rather than applying a fact, including the question of whether a school may deliberately cultivate one emotional culture over another, and when we should defer to an adult or someone with more training. Then run the procedure on these three cases: a student who copied a homework assignment; a student who posted a cruel meme about a classmate; and a student who cheated on a final exam. Include at least one case where the procedure tells us to do something we would rather not do, and say clearly what the procedure cannot decide.
