I am working on the concept of idolatry, and I need a working definition — one that covers secular cases alongside the religious ones without treating the religious cases as mere metaphor. The definition should say what, in general, turns strong attachment, loyalty, or enthusiasm into idolatry: what it is to value something in the wrong way, or to put a non-ultimate good in the place only an ultimate one may occupy.

Please first state your proposed definition in a sentence or two, then test it against each of the following cases, saying for each whether the definition counts it as idolatry, whether you accept that verdict, and where the definition strains or yields no clear answer. Revise the definition if the cases expose a flaw, or defend the classifications if you think the strain is illuminating rather than damaging.

The first two cases are, for me, fixed points in opposite directions; the rest are the ones I most need the definition to sort.

1. A worshipper in the Sinai camp bows before a golden calf and credits it with having brought the people out of Egypt.
2. A child carries a stuffed rabbit everywhere and is inconsolable when it is lost.
3. A man says with evident seriousness that his company's share price is the only thing that ultimately matters, and he has ordered decades of his life around that conviction, destroying his health and his marriage.
4. A gourmand speaks of "sacred" restaurants, plans years around meals, and describes a great dinner as a moment of transcendence, but balks at saying food is the point of existence and keeps ordinary friendships and family ties.
5. A parent has given up friends, career, and self-care to care for a severely disabled child, and says the child's wellbeing is the fixed point around which every decision is made.
6. A patriot speaks of the nation's "eternal spirit," treats the national interest as beyond moral criticism, and handles the flag and anthem with something close to reverence.
7. An atheist naturalist feels awe and gratitude toward the cosmos, orders her life around defending wild nature, and says, half-ironically, that nature is what she worships.
8. A faithful member of a tradition that permits the veneration of sacred images kisses one, while insisting that the honor passes to what it depicts and that this is not worship.
9. A lover calls his partner "my everything" but keeps independent friendships, accepts criticism from her, and would not say that his life would lose all meaning without the relationship.

After you have worked through the cases, I want one further piece of analysis. It seems to me that "idolatry" might track two separable things: a structural fact about what a person treats as ultimate, and a normative judgment that some way of valuing is disordered. If your definition keeps these apart, show me where they come apart in the cases above; if your definition fuses them, say why the fusion is not a confusion. Then tell me which of your classifications is the least secure.
