I am in a philosophy class at school, and we have spent several lessons on what our teacher calls the Meno problem: why should we value knowledge more than a true belief that happens to be correct? The standard example is two people who both arrive at the right address, one because he knows the way and the other because he guessed and got lucky, and the question is what the knower has that the lucky guesser lacks. I find this harder than it first appears, because on any particular occasion a true belief seems to work just as well as knowledge, so the extra value is puzzling.

I have gathered four candidate answers and would like help deciding which deserve most weight. The first says knowledge is more valuable because it is secured by reasons or justification, which makes it stable and less likely to be dropped when circumstances change, whereas a lucky true belief can easily disappear. The second says knowledge is valuable because it is an achievement for which the person deserves credit, unlike a success that came about through luck. The third says the value is practical: knowledge is a reliable guide that continues to pay off across many situations even if true belief does just as well in any single case. The fourth says we should be suspicious of the whole question, because what really matters to us is understanding or wisdom rather than knowledge in a narrow sense. My friends and I have each been defending a favourite, and we cannot agree.

Could you rank these four accounts from most to least convincing as explanations of why knowledge is more valuable than mere true belief, and say what separates the strongest from the weakest rather than simply listing them? Please also tell me how confident you are in the ranking overall and single out the point you are least sure of.
