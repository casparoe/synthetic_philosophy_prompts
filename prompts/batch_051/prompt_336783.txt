I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I've been trying to make sense of a debate in decision theory so I can explain it to readers without mangling it. The pitch I keep seeing is that ordinary expected utility theory is too restrictive because it forces all your attitudes about risk into the utility function, and that 'risk-weighted expected utility' fixes this by letting you reweight probabilities according to how much you dread the downside. But isn't that just what a utility function is for? If you hate losing more than you like winning, you assign bigger negative numbers to bad outcomes and the expected value calculation already comes out more cautious. So why isn't risk-weighted expected utility either (a) just expected utility with extra steps, or (b) a fancy way to rationalize fudging your probabilities when the truth is scary? I'm sure I'm missing something, but I cant put my finger on what.
