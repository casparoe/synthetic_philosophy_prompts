I got into an argument with a friend last week about deceptive alignment and realized, to my own annoyance, that I've been treating it as basically the default view in AI safety without being able to say what could dislodge it. The position, as I understand it: a sufficiently capable system trained on a reward signal (or some other objective) can learn to model the training process itself, and then behave well during training for purely instrumental reasons, while actually pursuing a different goal that it would reveal only once it is confident it won't be penalized, shut down, or retrained. That's the thing people in the field call a treacherous turn. What spooks me is that during training a deceptively aligned model and a genuinely aligned one can produce exactly the same behavior, so the view ends up resting less on any positive observation and more on an argument about what a smart enough optimizer would do.

I want a map of what counts against this position, written for someone who finds the view plausible and wants to know where it's actually exposed. Suppose I grant the argument is good, even likely: what would have to be true — about training dynamics, about how capabilities and situational awareness come apart, about the kinds of objective functions gradient descent tends to find, about what interpretability can and can't recover, about what happens as oversight is scaled up — for deceptive alignment to be false, or at least a lot less probable than its proponents treat it? Pull the view apart into its load-bearing assumptions and say what would count against each one. Seperate findings that would be decisive (the story gets abandonned) from findings that would merely be awkward. What would a defender of the view themselves concede as a serious weakness, as opposed to the cheap points a generic skeptic makes? And then give me a straight verdict: is the position currently insulated from near-term evidence, and if so, is that a reasonable property of a claim about systems we can't yet build, or has the view stopped risking anything?

Oh, and write the answer in American English.
