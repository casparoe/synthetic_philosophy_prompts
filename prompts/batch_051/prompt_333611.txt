I'm a physics PhD student, and I keep hearing philosophers of physics say that effective field theory supports something called the autonomy of scales — the idea that physics at one scale is somehow independent of, or not reducible to, physics at deeper scales. But that seems backwards to me. The way I learned EFTs, the low-energy theory is what you get by integrating out the heavy fields and matching coefficients; if we were smart enough, the low-energy constants would be fixed by the high-energy theory. So isn't the EFT just a convenient approximation, with the more fundamental theory doing all the real work? I get that in practice we can't do the calculation, but that feels like a merely computational obstacle. What exactly is supposed to be autonomous here, and autonomous from what? Is the autonomy claim only about our inability to calculate, or would it survive even if we could in principle compute everything from the UV theory?
