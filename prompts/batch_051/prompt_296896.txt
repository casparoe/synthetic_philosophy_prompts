I am compiling a list of objections to the gene's-eye view of evolution, the position associated with George Williams and Richard Dawkins according to which the gene (or replicator) is the fundamental unit of selection and organisms are best understood as the vehicles that replicators build. The objections I already have, and do not need to see repeated, are the following.

1. The category-error objection. Genes have no intentions, interests, or strategies, so describing them as selfish or as manipulating their hosts is either literally false or a metaphor that systematically misleads. This is the line pressed by Mary Midgley and later critics of the rhetoric of the selfish gene.

2. The levels-of-selection objection. The gene's-eye view privileges one level of biological organization without sufficient justification. Selection can in principle operate at several levels at once, and a complete account must be hierarchical, as in Stephen Jay Gould's hierarchical selectionism and in the multilevel selection theory of David Sloan Wilson and Elliott Sober.

3. The developmental-systems objection. Genes are not the only channels of inheritance, nor the privileged locus of causal control. Epigenetic marks, cytoplasmic factors, symbionts, and niche construction all recur reliably across generations, so treating the gene as the unit of selection is an arbitrary bookkeeping choice.

4. The genotype-phenotype objection. Because of pleiotropy, epistasis, dominance, and gene-environment interaction, there is often no stable, well-defined quantity that is the fitness of an individual gene. The notion of a gene for a complex trait, and the corresponding notion of a genic selection coefficient, dissolves under scrutiny.

5. The interactor objection. Selection requires interactors whose integrated traits actually confront the environment, and for most traits the interactor is the whole organism rather than the gene. The gene's-eye view therefore underdescribes how integrated phenotypes, phenotypic plasticity, and developmental robustness arise and persist.

6. The unfalsifiability objection. Gene's-eye adaptation stories, like adaptationist storytelling generally, can be reverse-engineered to fit almost any observed trait as an optimal strategy, so the view risks becoming empirically empty unless it is constrained by independent optimality models or comparative tests. This is the Panglossianism charge associated with Gould and Richard Lewontin.

I suspect this list already contains every standard line of attack, or at least every one that gets repeated in the literature. It should be an objection that a thoughtful critic of the gene's-eye view could genuinely press, not a straw man.
