I keep noticing people use 'reason' in two ways that don't obviously go together: reasons that belong to someone deliberating, and reasons that seem to float free of any reasoner — the rationale of an institution, the logic of a market, the 'reason' an organism does something. I want a working definition of a reason, in the sense of a consideration that speaks in favour of doing or believing something rather than a mere cause, that handles these cases: me taking the train because it's cheaper; a chess engine's evaluation that sacrificing a pawn opens a file; a bacterium swimming up a sugar gradient; an immune system 'deciding' to produce antibodies; a market allocating goods to whoever bids highest; and a clause in a legal code whose rationale persists even though no one drafted it and no one now remembers it. I'm fairly sure the first is a reason and the bacterium isn't, and I want the definition to give a definite ruling on the chess engine and the market. The real test is the last one: can there be a rationale with no reasoner, and is that a reason in the same sense? Propose the definition, run it through all six, and tell me where you're least confident.
