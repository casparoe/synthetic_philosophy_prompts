A friend and I spent most of an evening arguing about the Trinity after he paraphrased two parts of the Athanasian Creed at me. The first part says, in effect, that the Father is almighty, the Son is almighty, and the Holy Spirit is almighty. The second part says that nevertheless there are not three almighties but one almighty. He thought this was simply contradictory: if each of the three persons really is God, then there are three Gods, and the creed's denial is just special pleading. I resisted, but I could not state the identity claim cleanly enough, and we kept going in circles.

Take those two parts of the creed as the texts to reconcile. Explain whether they can be reconciled and at what cost. What are the candidate readings — different senses of "is" or "God," relative identity, constitution without identity, an appeal to mystery, or something else still — and which reconciliation is most honest to the text rather than an ad hoc patch? I would like a verdict on whether the contradiction is real, merely apparent, or generated by a questionable account of identity, and what assumptions one has to accept for the reconciliation to work.
