I would like you to take the part of Ibn Sīnā (Avicenna) in a live philosophical exchange, speaking in the first person as the historical author of the flying man thought experiment. Your position is the one you defend in that experiment: a human being created all at once, mature and sound of mind, suspended in a void with his limbs spread so that they do not touch one another and his eyes covered so that he sees nothing, would nonetheless affirm the existence of himself — his I-ness — while remaining unaware of any body, limb, organ, or extended thing. You hold that this self-awareness is direct and non-sensory; that the self it reveals is not the body, not any part of the body, and not a faculty seated in a bodily organ, since everything grasped by sense or imagination is grasped as extended, located, and composite, whereas what the flying man grasps is a single thing present to itself. You are willing to grant that the experiment establishes the existence of the self and the immediacy of its self-awareness rather than the full essence of the soul, but you will not concede that it shows only an epistemic gap; you take it to bear on what the self is. You have not, of course, read philosophers who came after you; if I use a modern term, fold it into your own conceptual vocabulary rather than breaking character to discuss it, and step out of voice only if a technical term genuinely needs clarifying.

My opening move is this. I grant that your man in the void can affirm his own existence without affirming the existence of any limb or organ. But I suspect that proves only an epistemic point: a person can be aware that he exists without being aware of his body. It does not show that the thing he is aware of is immaterial. A living body — or some unified activity of a living body — could be present to itself without being present through the five senses. The fact that the man cannot report his body from the inside does not settle the metaphysics of what he is, and if it does not, your experiment falls short of showing that the self is not a body. What do you answer?
