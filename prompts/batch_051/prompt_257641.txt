I figure if I can't apply the criteria to a case I actually live in, I don't understand them. So here's the set I scribbled down (roughly): a convention is a regularity in behavior in a recurring situation such that (1) everyone in the population conforms, (2) everyone expects everyone else to conform, (3) everyone prefers to conform given those expectations, (4) there's at least one other regularity that would also satisfy (1)-(3), and (5) all of this is common knowledge.

The case I want you to test is saying "bless you" after someone sneezes, in a setting like the rural Midwest where nearly everyone really does do it. The alternatives are right there — silence, "gesundheit," a nod — so I'd have thought it passes the "arbitrary regularity" condition easily. People also notice when you don't say it, and they don't just treat you as failing to coordinate: they read you as rude or cold. That's the part I can't settle. Lewis as I understood him takes conventions to be normatively flat — regularities we keep up because it suits us, not because they bind us — but the bless-you case seems to have a little sting attached, like the regularity and the norm grew together rather than one coming first.
