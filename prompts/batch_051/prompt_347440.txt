I am a retired natural scientist now reading my way into philosophy, and I have been working through a comparison I would like checked. In the typogenetics toy systems behind the DNA–Gödel isomorphism, a strand is a string over two complementary base pairs; a substring can be read as a program for an editing enzyme; and a strand self-replicates when the strand, acting first as data and then through the enzyme it codes for, reconstitutes itself from its complement. The central move seems to be that one object plays two roles, data and program. That looks to me like the central move in Gödel's first incompleteness theorem, where a formula is, by coding, at once an object in the system and a statement about its own unprovability.

How deep is this parallel, exactly? Is it a real identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion? State the proposed mapping in as much detail as the two sides can support, say what would have to be true of both sides for the Gödelian machinery—representability of the system within itself, diagonalization, incompleteness, the unprovability of consistency—to transfer, and name at least one claim the analogy naturally suggests that fails or does not carry over. Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this material, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
