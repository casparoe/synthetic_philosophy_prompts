I've read the value-drift and lock-in material on and off for years, and a friend recently handed me one I can't find the exit from. He sets up it like this.

(1) If it's worth trying to keep the far future from locking in bad values, we need a standard that says which values are bad. (2) But any standard we could actually use is itself a product of the same kind of drift — evolution, culture, quirks of upbringing — as the future values we want to judge. (3) So the standard has no authority over those future values; it's just the values our particular contingency happened to leave us with. (4) But then the whole steering project is one contingent value configuration trying to get itself locked in. (5) And one contingent value configuration locking itself in is precisely what we were describing as the disaster.

From there he says the conclusion is inescapable: the person worried about bad value lock-in is committed to doing the very thing they warn about, so 'bad values in the far future' is incoherant and the only honest move is to admit our values are drift and stop pretending otherwise.

I want badly to keep the first premise: a future full of flourishing minds still seems better than one full of minds maximizing suffering, no matter how dug-in my current position is. But every way I try to reject a later step pushes me toward a kind of moral realism I've spent years being suspicious of. Where does this actually go wrong? If it doesn't, which premise is cheapest to give up, and what does giving it up cost?
