My go-to objection to LLM understanding used to be the symbol grounding problem: a purely symbolic system can only define symbols in terms of other symbols, so unless some symbols are grounded in something non-symbolic like perception or action the whole thing floats free — like being fluent in a dictionary and nothing else. That argument was built with the paradigm of a hand-coded symbolic system in mind. But the systems everyone actually argues about now are trained on raw images, audio, video, and even robot control data, not just text; a vision-language-action model is doing something that looks a lot more like perceptual-motor learning than dictionary lookup. Does the original grounding argument survive that shift? I'm not asking whether today's models understand anything — that's a different argument. I'm asking whether this particular argument's premises are obsolete, or whether there's a premise in it that multimodal training doesn't touch (maybe something about needing the right kind of causal contact with the world, not just cross-modal prediction). Go premise by premise and say which one the new development attacks, whether it can be rebuilt, and what the strongest surviving version of the objection still rules out.
