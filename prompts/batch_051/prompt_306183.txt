I keep noticing an asymmetry in how I (and everyone I try this on) react to coincidences, and I can't figure out whether it's defensible or just a bias. Everyone can recite the birthday-paradox point: in a room of 23 people it's more likely than not that some pair shares a birthday, and nobody finds that eerie — it's just arithmetic. But last month I was at a dinner party with about twenty people, and the only shared birthday in the room was between me and the host's sister, who I'd just found out grew up on my own street. The whole table went "what are the chances?" and I had to fight the feeling that something was going on. The same table would have shrugged if you'd told them before dinner that some two people there probably shared a birthday. So we treat the general fact — some pair will coincide — as unremarkable, and the particular fact — me, that woman, plus the street overlap — as spooky.

There's an obvious killjoy move: of course the birthday match had to land on somebody in the room, and the street overlap only looks extraordinary because I'm scanning an event that's already happened for unusual features. But here's what keeps me stuck. If a friend had written down a month in advance, "You'll meet the host's sister; you two will share a birthday and will have grown up on the same street," and then it happened, I'd take that as genuinely strong evidence that he somehow knew. The physical event is identical; the only difference is when the pattern got specified. Is there a priniciple that justifies my asymmetry — shrugging at the general coincidence but being spooked by the particular one — or does the pre-specification example show I'm just smuggling hindsight into the particular case? I want the strongest case for treating the two differently, tested against nearby cases, and an honest verdict on whether the asymmetry should be kept or dropped.
