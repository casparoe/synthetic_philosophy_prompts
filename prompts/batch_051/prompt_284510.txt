I am preparing a short position statement for a theology seminar and want a consistency check before I circulate it. My proof text is the Epistle of James, 2:20–24 (King James Version):

"But wilt thou know, O vain man, that faith without works is dead? Was not Abraham our father justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar? Seest thou how faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect? And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God. Ye see then how that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only."

Against that passage, here is what I hold. (i) Justification before God is by faith alone, and no work of ours contributes to the verdict. (ii) Paul's "works of the law" are the ceremonial boundary markers of the Mosaic covenant—circumcision, dietary rules, calendar observance—whereas James's "works" are deeds of mercy and obedience; the two writers mean different things by "works." (iii) In James 2, "justified" means demonstrated or vindicated before others, not declared righteous by God. (iv) The faith that justifies is never alone: it necessarily produces good works, so that a faith that produces none is dead and is not saving faith. (v) Abraham was already fully justified in the forensic sense at Genesis 15:6, and his standing before God did not change when he offered Isaac. (vi) A deathbed convert who truly believes and dies before any opportunity to act is nonetheless justified.

Which of these commitments are actually in tension with one another or with the passage, which only appear to be, and how would you rank the tensions by severity? For each genuine tension, say what the least costly revision would be, and then identify the single revision that would do the most to make the set cohere. I am asking for an audit of my own set rather than a survey of the Paul–James debate.
