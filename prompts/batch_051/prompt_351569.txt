I'm putting together a workshop on epistemic dysfunction in online communities, and I want a worked-through borderline case as the centerpiece. I understand the standard distinction between an epistemic bubble and an echo chamber roughly as follows. A bubble is a structure of omission: the community's information environment simply doesn't contain certain relevant voices, evidence, or counterarguments, so members never really encounter them, but there is no particular machinery of distrust aimed at those sources. An echo chamber is a structure of active discrediting: members do encounter outside voices, or at least know of them, but the community cultivates and reinforces distrust of those sources, so contrary testimony arrives pre-marked as untrustworthy and fails to move anyone. I expect real communities are usually mixtures, but I want to know which side of this line the following case falls on, and whether the line itself is doing enough work.

The case is an online support and advocacy forum for patients with a chronic illness who have repeatedly had their symptoms dismissed by physicians. The forum's main discussion threads prohibit linking to or citing mainstream clinical guidelines and publications; that material is confined to a monthly "Reading Room" thread where volunteers summarize current studies and practice guidelines and members critique them. New members are told that mainstream medicine systematically discounts patient testimony, that its research agenda is shaped by pharmaceutical and insurance money, and that outside sources should be treated as guilty until proven innocent. At the same time, the moderators maintain a public, dated list of exactly which sources are excluded from main threads and why; the criteria are revisable, and twice the community has rescinded an exclusion after a member showed that the original rationale no longer applied.

Is this forum an epistemic bubble, an echo chamber, both, or neither? More importantly, does the case reveal that the bubble/chamber distinction needs a third category, a modification, or a clearer account of why revisable institutionalized distrust differs from mere echo-chamber distrust?
