So I've been working on a novel for a few years now — an alt-history where Song China gets something like a coal-and-steam takeoff in the lower Yangzi around the 1200s, and the book follows a family of ironfounders as their city changes. Its not really about the Big Counterfactual, it's about the people. But at a workshop last month an editor read my first chapter and said, in effect: you can render all the hang-together details you like, but you still haven't answered why Britain and not China — the burden of proof is on you to show China could have broken through. And the whole room nodded like that settled it.

I've kept turning that phrase over since, and I realize I genuinely can't tell which of several different things "why Britain and not China?" is doing in his argument. Sometimes it sounds like: what did Britain have that China lacked? Sometimes: what blocked China? Sometimes: what would have had to be different for China to have gotten there first? Sometimes it seems to be the claim that because China didn't, there must have been something about it that couldn't — which is a different beast entirely. Those aren't the same question, and I don't think the editor's objection works for all of them.

Could you take that one-liner apart the way you'd untangle a line of dialogue that's carrying too much? For each precise claim it might be making, tell me whether it's a platitude, something substantive and defensible, or something false or badly posed. Then — the part I actually care about — say which of those readings would justify the editor's move that an alt-history set in Song China has to discharge a special "why not China?" burden just to be plausible, and which ones he'd be smuggling in without argument. I want a menu I can bring to the next workshop, not a single answer decided in advance.

And one formality: make the very last line a one-sentence summary starting with "Verdict:" that says, in one sentence, which precise reading or readings of "why Britain and not China?" actually do the work the editor was trying to make it do.
