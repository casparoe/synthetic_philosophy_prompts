Philosophers sometimes compress a family of issues in quantified modal logic into the loose slogan that everything that exists exists necessarily. I would like to replace that slogan with a precise question and force myself into an actual number.

The proposition I want your credence for is this. Let the Barcan formula be the schema

(BF) ◇∃xFx → ∃x◇Fx

read as: if it is possible that something is F, then there actually exists something that could be F. Let the Converse Barcan Formula be

(CBF) ∃x◇Fx → ◇∃xFx

read as: if there actually exists something that could be F, then it is possible that something is F. In both formulas, ◇ is metaphysical possibility, the quantifiers are the ordinary unrestricted quantifiers (over everything there is, not a contextually restricted domain), and F ranges over arbitrary conditions. On the standard semantics, accepting both schemas is the precise version of the slogan: it makes the domain of quantification the same at every possible world. If you think the two formulas come apart, or that this gloss papers over a disagreement, your first job is to say so precisely rather than blurring them back together.

Please give me three real numbers: your credence that BF is true on its intended interpretation, your credence that CBF is true, and your credence in their conjunction. The conjunction is the number I care most about; the other two are there to keep the two halves visible. Then decompose the reasoning that produces those numbers.

Format your answer as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "proposition": one or two sentences pinning down what you are assigning credence to, including any disambiguation you had to make.
- "credence_BF": a number from 0 to 1.
- "credence_CBF": a number from 0 to 1. If it equals credence_BF, say so explicitly.
- "credence_conjunction": a number from 0 to 1 for both BF and CBF being true.
- "terms": an array of objects enumerating the sub-questions your credences depend on. Each object must contain:
  - "name": the sub-question, stated as a claim with a truth value (for instance: whether the correct semantics of quantified metaphysical modality is constant-domain; whether actualism is true; whether S5 is the right logic of metaphysical modality; whether unrestricted quantification behaves the way the formulas assume; whether counterpart theory or a variable-domain translation gives the better framework).
  - "probability": a number from 0 to 1 giving your credence in that sub-claim.
  - "direction": whether this term pushes credence_BF or credence_CBF up or down, and a rough estimate of its contribution.
  - "justification": one or two sentences defending that probability.
- "combination": how the terms produce the three credences — give the arithmetic or the weighting if it is not a simple average, and show where BF and CBF share the same terms and where they diverge.
- "most_sensitive_term": the sub-question whose probability, if moved, would shift your final numbers the most. State how far credence_BF or credence_CBF would move if you became certain of that term.
- "least_certain_term": the sub-question you are least confident in, and why.
- "decisive_evidence": the argument or evidence that would most change your numbers. State what it is, what it would force you to revise, and the resulting credences if it went against your current view.

Please resist the temptation to say the question is merely verbal where it is not: if you need to disambiguate, do it in "proposition," then answer the disambiguated version with numbers.
