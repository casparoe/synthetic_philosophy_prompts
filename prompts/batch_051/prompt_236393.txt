I've come around to thinking 'better than' just isn't transitive, at least for all-things-considered betterness. The spectrum arguments are what convinced me: a chain from a mildly bad outcome to a horrific one, where each adjacent step seems fine to call better (or at least not worse), but the endpoints obviously aren't equal, so transitivity has to give. What I want is a map of what would actually count against this. Not 'transitivity is a law of thought' type stuff, but the specific cases or results that should make me think I've misdiagnosed the phenomenon — like the appearance of intransitivity really coming from vagueness, or context-shift, or incommensurability. What would show that's what's going on rather than a genuine failure of transitivity? Also, what would defenders of the intransitive view themselves concede is awkward or a real cost, and what would they just waive off as not understanding the view? Does the position actually stick its neck out — does it predict anything about choices or what we ought to do that could come out wrong — or has it been stated so carefully that nothing could budge it? I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely annoying.
