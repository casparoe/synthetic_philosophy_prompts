I've been trying to nail down when one of those griefbots that people train on a dead relative's texts counts as actually talking to the person, and when it's just a very good puppet. The checklist I keep coming back to is: (1) the replies get their shape from how the person actually was—her memories, her blind spots, the way she'd misread a situation—not just from whatever a polite chatbot would say; (2) it can respond to new information instead of just recycling old messages; (3) when it messes up, it messes up in her particular way, not in the usual AI-confident-nonsense way; and (4) it stays the same across conversations, remembers last week, and doesn't drift into a different character every time the model gets updated.

Now suppose I built one from my sister's texts, emails, and voice notes. It does remember past chats and it does surprise me with things she plausibly would have said but never actually wrote. But it also tacks on a disclaimer every so often that it's just a simulation and it isn't her, it sometimes glues her style onto generic chatbot filler, and it never starts a conversation on its own. When I ask it about a memory she never recorded anywhere, it confabulates one in her voice.

Does that count as talking to her? I keep going back and forth, and I can't tell whether the disclaimer should disqualify it or whether that's just the company covering its ass. Walk me through which of my four criterias is doing the actual work, and whether this case shows one of them is wrong or that the whole checklist is missing something.
