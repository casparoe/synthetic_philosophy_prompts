I want a thought experiment built to spec, for a paper I'm writing on whether we literally see through photographs. Two views on the table. Production-history view: a picture is transparent (you see the scene through it) iff the causal chain from scene to picture runs through the scene's imprint on a surface without passing through anyone's beliefs about what's there — so photos usually count, handmade pictures usually don't. Perceptual-role view: a picture is transparent iff what it's like to look at it is importantly like looking at the scene through a window, so the richness and accuracy of the visual information is what matters, not how the picture was made.

Construct a case that flips the usual lineup in both directions at once: same scene, two pictures. Picture 1 is not a photograph (a handmade mechanical tracing or the like), and the production-history theorist says we see the scene through it; Picture 2 is a photograph, and the production-history theorist says we do not see through it. Meanwhile the perceptual-role theorist gives exactly the opposite verdict for each: no to Picture 1, yes to Picture 2. Constraints: present-day technology, ordinary scene, both items are genuinely pictures rather than sculptures or diagrams, and dont just stipulate the verdicts — the production method and the photograph's processing should generate them.

Then do the check: walk through each theory's verdict for each picture and the reasoning behind it, say which structural feature of the case (production history vs. available visual information) is doing the real work, and what happens if you remove it — does the case collapse back into the standard photograph-vs-photorealistic-drawing pair? Finish with where your reasoning is most likely to fail: which objection from either side would force you to redesign the case, and what tweak would you make.
