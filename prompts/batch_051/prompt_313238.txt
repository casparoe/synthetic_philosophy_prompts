Here's the setup. Take the argument behind Turing's imitation game: roughly, the question whether machines can think is to vague to make progress on, so we should replace it with a behavioral test—if an interrogator can't reliably tell an entity from a human in typed conversation, that's as good an indication of thought as we ever get, and no worse than our evidence for other minds. Now make that view reckon with large language models. We have systems that clear weak, casual versions of the game—people chat with them and often can't tell for a while—but we also know they're trained mostly to predict text rather than to form beliefs, cares, or a stable point of view, whatever thinking turns out to require. And the result cuts both ways: humans are easy to fool with shallow trickery, so conversational indistinguishability may be a much lower bar than Turing assumed.
