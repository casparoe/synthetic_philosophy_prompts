ok i keep coming back to this idea for a satire assignment. I want a mock class action settlement notice, in deadpan legalese, from a fertility clinic (invented, something bland like GenesisCare) that accidentally used the wrong sperm samples for a cohort of IVF patients in the early 1990s and has only just owned up to it. The notice is going out to former patients now. The whole gag should rest on origin essentialism: the view that the particular sperm and egg a person came from are essential to them, so that had a different sperm fertilized the egg, the person now reading the notice wouldnt exist. I want the legal language to commit to this literally and then follow it all the way. The class is defined as all persons who would have existed had the correct samples been used; damages are owed to class members who, by the notice's own account, don't exist; each recipient is told they are not the injured party. You can add a section where you waive any emotional distress claim because the distress is being experienced by a merely possible person, and maybe a certification form at the end where you have to check a box confirming whether or not you are the you that would have existed.

Keep it deadpan the whole way, no winking, about 700 words, with real section headers and fine print. A philosopher should be able to tell the writer actually gets origin essentialism and isnt just mocking a phrase, and an undergrad who only heard the idea that afternoon should still follow what is going on. One more constraint: don't let it slide into the idea that the wrong-sperm child is damaged in some ordinary medical or psychological way — the joke has to be that the metaphysics and the remedy are talking past each other across possible worlds.
