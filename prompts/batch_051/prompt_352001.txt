I’m in a reading group on the Galileo affair, and we spent a long time on his Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina. A passage that stuck with me reads:

“Showing a greater fondness for their own opinions than for truth they sought to deny and disprove the new things which, if they had cared to look for themselves, their own senses would have demonstrated to them. To this end they hurled various charges and published numerous writings filled with vain arguments, and they made the grave mistake of sprinkling these with passages taken from places in the Bible which they had failed to understand properly, and which were ill-suited to their purposes.”

My question is not about 1615 but about a present-day congregation that treats its doctrinal statement as binding. The statement says that God directly created the first human beings and did not bring them about through descent from earlier animals. A member who is a paleoanthropologist tells the elders that the genetic and fossil evidence for common ancestry is robust enough that continuing to teach the statement as a required article of faith injures the church’s credibility. The elders reply that Scripture clearly teaches the special creation of Adam and Eve, and that Scripture is the final authority in all matters of faith and practice. I am not asking you to settle whether evolution is true; I am asking how the quoted passage bears on the elders’ move.

Treat Galileo’s passage as a rule about how not to use Scripture in quarrels over the natural world. How does that rule apply to the elders’ refusal? Please answer in four titled sections, each one to two paragraphs: “The quoted principle,” “Application to the paleoanthropology dispute,” “Three interpretive approaches,” and “Verdict.” In the third section, argue the case from the words of the passage, from the purpose of the letter, and from what Galileo would most plausibly say if asked about modern paleoanthropology. In the last section, say which approach should govern here and what exactly it would require the elders to do or refrain from doing.
