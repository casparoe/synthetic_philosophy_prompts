I’m a novelist, and I’m working on a book where a small deindustrialized town tries to pull itself out of a slump by issuing its own paper money in the spirit of George Berkeley’s The Querist. The mechanic I’ve got in mind is this: the town council pays people in scrip for public works, the scrip is accepted for local taxes and rents, local retailers can use it to buy from local farmers, and the scrip trades at a discount against national currency, so people prefer to spend their scrip rather than hold it. The intellectual hook is Berkeley’s idea that money is basically a ticket or counter for goods, useful mainly because it lets people exchange real things, and that creating paper money can put idle hands to work. I dont want a verdict on whether this is a good idea in the abstract. I want you to game it out for me as a mechanism: who hoards it, who spends it fastest, what happens to local prices, what happens when the town needs outside supplies, and what scams or workarounds would show up by month six. Keep it to 450 words max. And tell me which one empirical fact about the town, if it turned out different than I’ve assumed, would most change your read of where the scheme falls apart.
