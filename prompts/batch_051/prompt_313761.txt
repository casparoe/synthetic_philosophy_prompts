For a jurisprudence course handout, write a decision tree that sorts a reader into a position on what moral value, if any, the rule of law possesses: intrinsic, merely instrumental, or none. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names: each branch should be a question someone could answer without already knowing the positions, and each leaf should state a named position or be marked unnamed. The tree must include as leaves Fuller's inner-morality view, Raz's instrumental-virtue view, Waldron's procedural/argumentative view, and a minimalist formal-legality view that denies any necessary moral value. Use these branching questions somewhere, rephrasing them in your own words as needed: (1) Does a rule-system that satisfies the standard desiderata (generality, publicity, prospectivity, clarity, non-contradiction, stability, possibility of compliance, congruence between rules and official action) still realize the rule of law when it is used to organize grave injustice? (2) Is the rule of law primarily a property of the rules themselves or of the institutions that apply them through argument and hearings? (3) When the rule of law yields value under an evil regime, does that value depend on the ends it serves or count independently of them? Flag any two leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate, give each leaf one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and include at least one coherent combination of answers that no prominent view occupies, briefly diagnosing it as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. End by stating which parts of this classification are settled among contemporary philosophers of law and which are genuinely contested, without presenting a contestable view as the consensus.
