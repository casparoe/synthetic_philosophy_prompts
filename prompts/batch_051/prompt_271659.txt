I run the incident-review unit at a mid-size eldercare company. We get two or three files a week where a resident was harmed and the question is whether someone's not doing something — a nurse not checking, an aide not telling anyone, a maintenance person not fixing a rail — should be listed as a cause in our internal report. Those reports feed insurance, discipline, and sometimes lawsuits, so writing "caused by staff omission" is a big deal. The reviewers are non-philosopher staff with about an hour per case, and my boss has to sign off on every classification.

I have a vague memory from college that philosophers talk about causation by omission and think omissions are weird: they're not really events, and which non-action gets picked out as a cause seems to depend on what people were expected to do, not just on what would otherwise have happened. But i've never seen that turned into a reusable checklist. Please write me a decision procedure for classifying whether an omission counts as a cause of a bad outcome in our reviews. Make it a flowchart in words: numbered questions in order, with what counts as a sufficient answer to each, and explicit stop rules for "not a cause." It has to be usable by a sleepy reviewer with an hour and no training. At every step, keep the causal question separated from blame and liability, and mark the exact point where a value judgment can't be eliminated and the call has to go to a person instead of the checklist. Also handle the ten-people-could-have-acted problem: when many people didn't do something, the procedure should say which omissions are even candidates and why.

Then run it on these three cases, showing the step-by-step results:
1. A night aide notices a resident has eaten almost nothing for two days and doesn't tell the nurse; on day three the resident is hospitalized for dehydration.
2. A weekend receptionist gets a complaint that a handrail in the day room is loose but doesn't pass it on to maintenance until Monday; a resident falls using that rail on Sunday afternoon. Assume maintenance couldn't have repaired it before Sunday even if they'd known at once.
3. A resident's daughter visits daily, notices her mother is much more confused than usual, and doesn't mention it to staff; that night the mother wanders and falls.

After the procedure, give me a JSON object that captures it, with exactly these fields:
- "procedure_id": a short slug
- "steps": array of objects, each with "step_number", "question", "answer_options", and "passes_to_step" (a step number, or "not_a_cause", or "needs_judgment")
- "value_judgment_points": array of objects with "step_number" and "why_judgment_cant_be_eliminated"
- "worked_cases": array of objects with "case_number", "step_results", "verdict" (one of "cause", "not_a_cause", "needs_judgment"), and "unwelcome" (true/false)
- "predictable_failures": array of strings
- "cannot_do": array of strings

If one of the cases comes out "not_a_cause" even though someone on staff or in the family would be furious, flag it as unwelcome and say why the procedure is still right.
