I've come around to the view that generational categories—Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z—aren't real kinds. There isn't a set of shared properties that all and only Millennials have doing the kind of work real kinds are supposed to do; the labels look more like demographic conveniences. And the tricky part is that once the labels circulate, people start describing themselves and making choices in their terms, which makes the categories look predictive when they're partly self-fulfilling—a looping effect, not a discovery of a pre-existing kind. What would count against this? I want the defeaters ranked from decisive to merely awkward: what evidence or arguments should actually move me toward thinking generations are something more than that—cohort studies, cross-cultural regularities, effects of shared events like a recession or a pandemic, personality or value differences, whatever. Also tell me whether I've set things up so that no possible data could count against me, and whether that's a problem for a view like this or just the cost of being careful about social kinds.
