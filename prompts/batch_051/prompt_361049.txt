A friend of mine is taking a philosophy of quantum mechanics class and sent me his attempt to summarize the psi-epistemic side of the debate, which he says he got from a blog post. I dont know enough to check it, and something about it feels off, so I figured I'd get someone to walk through it. Here's his summary:

"The wavefunction isn't a real thing out in the world; its a bookkeeping device for what we know, like a probability distribution. Probabilities aren't floating around in nature—they're in the head of whoever assigns them—and the wavefunction is the same kind of thing. So when we say a particle is in a superposition, that isn't a fact about the particle. The particle is always really in some perfectly definite state, and the wavefunction is just our ignorance about which one. Measurement doesn't do anything to the particle; it just updates our probability assignment the way a Bayesian updates on any new evidence, which is why epistemic views think the measurement problem is basically a non-problem and no collapse law is needed. After the PBR theorem a lot of people said this whole picture was dead, but that's overstated: PBR only ruled out a specific hidden-variable version where the same underlying state is compatible with different wavefunctions, and epistemic views don't all have that structure. The upshot is that choosing a wavefunction for a system is like stating an opinion: two people with different information can assign different wavefunctions to the same particle and both be equally right."

Can you go through this and tell me what a careful person working on the foundations of quantum mechanics would accept, what they'd wince at, and what's just equating two different claims? I especially suspect the stuff about the wavefunction being "just an opinion" and the particle being in a "perfectly definite state" is doing more work than the rest of the summary actually supports, but I can't articulate why. And does the final upshot follow from the parts of the summary that survive correction, or is it exactly the part that doesn't?
