Some Christian and Jewish traditions treat the Septuagint — the old Greek translation of the Hebrew scriptures — as itself inspired, not as a mere translation of an inspired original; there's even a legend that the translators worked independently and produced indentical wording. At the same time, a certain strand of Protestantism holds that the King James Version is itself the inspired word of God in English, and that it doesn't just point back to a more authoritative Hebrew or Greek text. I'm trying to get clear on what these practices presuppose about where inspiration lives: in the original author's meaning, in the words, in the content, in the community receiving it? Do the two practices presuppose compatible things, or do they quietly commit their holders to different theories of what a text is? If a translation can be inspired, what does that force you to give up about meaning — for instance, that meaning is in the author's head, or that it can't survive rewording? And if it can't, what exactly is lost in translation? I may be overreading this, and if you think the question is badly posed or rests on a confused premise, say so instead of trying to answer it.
