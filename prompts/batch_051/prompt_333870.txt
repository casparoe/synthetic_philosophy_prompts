I'm applying for a one-year postdoctoral fellowship and need a two-page research proposal. My half-formed idea is this. Writing on historical fiction and biopics tends to pose the central question as whether filmmakers owe the real dead truth or accuracy, and the familiar positions then divide between fidelity views (the dead are owed a depiction that tracks the record) and artistic-license views (the filmmaker owes the dead little beyond avoiding cruelty, and audiences are expected to know the difference between drama and history). I suspect this framing mistakes a bundle of directed duties for a single two-party relation. The duties plausibly run in different directions: to the deceased's surviving intimates, who can be harmed by public distortions; to living communities whose collective memory the fiction rewrites; and to audiences as epistemic agents who cannot reliably quarantine dramatized falsehoods. Turn this into a proposal with a precise statement of the question, an account of why the fidelity-versus-license framing leaves it open, a month-by-month method for a year of philosophical work, a justification of that method over the obvious alternatives (thicker case judgments, a single master principle, or a legal-doctrine inventory), and a clear statement of what would count as the project's failing.
