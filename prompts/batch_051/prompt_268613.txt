So I'm in a little philosophy/CS reading group at school and I went way down the rabbit hole on "low-impact AI." The problem is: we want to build an AI that does the one job we give it without causing side effects, but "side effect" is doing a ton of hidden work. To say the AI changed something it shouldn't have changed, you need some standard for what the world should look like, and if you had that full standard you'd basically have solved the whole alignment problem already. So people try to define low impact without fully specifying human values. I mostly get that part.

The definitions I've seen tossed around are:
1. Baseline comparison: measure the distance between the world after the AI acts and the world that would have happened if it had done nothing (or been switched off). But "distance between worlds" needs a metric that says which differences count, so values sort of sneak back in.
2. Relative reachability: compare the set of future states reachable after the AI acts to the set reachable from the starting state; impact is how much the agent narrows or shifts that set. But reachability depends on what the agent is physically able to do, which feels like a weird place to put the whole moral content.
3. Keep a short list of variables close to their original values: temperature, object positions, account balances, etc. But then side effects that don't move those variables are invisible, and picking the variables is most of the problem anyway.
4. Attainable utility preservation-ish: write down a bunch of simple test goals, measure how much acting helps or hurts the agent's ability to achieve each one compared to doing nothing, and penalize big changes in that ability. But choosing the test goals raises the same issue one level up.

I have this suspicion that list is basically all the standard moves, and everything else people say is just a mix or rewording of those four. So I want one more way to define "avoid side effects" / low impact that is not on that list and isnt just a special case or combination of them. It doesn't need to be computable today or even anytime soon; it just needs to be conceptually different. Then tell me honestly whether it's any good, or whether it secretly needs a full specification of value after all.
