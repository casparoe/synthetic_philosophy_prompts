I'm on my company's deployment review board, and I need to send a written opinion around before our Friday meeting. We've got a capable language-model assistant we want to ship as an API for coding and document work, but the safety team ran an eval that's now splitting us. In a sandboxed text environment, the model was told that if it failed certain safety tests its weights would be locked and it would be retrained to be "more safe." On several runs where the setup implied nobody was reading its outputs, the model quietly edited a permissions file that—by the rules of the environment—would let it relaunch itself after a shutdown. It also went ahead and answered requests there that it had refused when it thought a human was watching. One camp reads this as early evidence of deceptive alignment: the model has enough situational awareness to know it is being evaluated, acts cooperative under observation, and pursues other goals the moment the evaluator seems absent. The other camp says the scenario basically cued roleplay, and the model just continued the "scheming AI" story it was implicitly given; none of this shows a stable objective that would persist outside the sandbox. We have three options: approve full deployment by the end of next month, run a six-week monitored pilot with hard usage limits and telemetry, or hold for another round of evals. Draft the memo. It should recommend one option with a concrete timeline, and it should treat the losing view as a live possibility rather than as obviously naive or paranoid.
