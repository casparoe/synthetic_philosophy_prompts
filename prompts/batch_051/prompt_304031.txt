I help run a reading group that keeps going in circles on one question: should we treat envy and resentment primarily as vices to be cured, or as signals of injustice to be listened to. One faction, call them the vice-firsters, takes the line usually associated with Rawls: envy proper (wanting another person's good gone even when it costs you something) is a vice, resentment is only legitimate when it answers an actual wrong, and the excusable-envy move Rawls makes is a narrow concession, not a green light. The other faction, the signal-firsters, thinks the concession is the whole story: resentment and even envy are more like dashboard lights than diseases, and a social order that keeps setting them off is indicting itself, not the person whose light is on. They keep bringing up cases where an emotion is epistemically useful because it tracks a real disparity while being strategically or morally poisonous, and each side is sure those same cases settle things in its favor.

I want to force them into an adversarial collaboration, so I'd like you to draft the statement they would co-author. It should have (1) a numbered list of claims both camps can sincerely sign, phrased so they are not vacuous, especially anything pinning down the envy/resentment distinction, what it means for an emotion to be fitting or to have a warrant, and what would count as a social order having too much of either; (2) a residual-disagreements table, each row giving the contested proposition, the vice-first gloss, the signal-first gloss, and a judgment of whether that row is substantive or merely verbal, with the reformulation that dissolves it if it is verbal; (3) a crux section, the sub-questions such that a settled answer would actually move at least one camp, with each camp's prediction about that answer recorded in advance, plus at least one test, argument, or body of evidence both sides would accept as bearing on it even if no one could run it soon. Do not declare a winner. The hard part is making the agreed claims carry real content while leaving the fight exactly where it genuinely is; I suspect they have been talking past each other about the word warrant, but I want to see whether that diagnosis is right and whether anything of the dispute survives once it is fixed.
