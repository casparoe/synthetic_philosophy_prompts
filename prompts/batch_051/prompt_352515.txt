I keep noticing a failure mode in arguments between AI systems, and I want to know whether it has an established name or whether I'm mixing up two or three different things. The setup is the one where a limited judge (a human with a fixed attention budget, or a small model) has to pick between two debaters: one honest, one trying to win. The honest side gives the right answer in a few clean steps. The liar cant match that, so it gives a huge answer instead. Most of the individual claims are true, even verifiable, and any local check the judge runs comes back fine. But somewhere in the pile there's a step that's wrong in a way you'd only catch by holding a lot of context at once. The judge spends its whole budget expanding true-but-irrelevant endpoints, and the bad step never gets isolated, because the request that would catch it is one of several equally plausible requests and none looks more suspicious than the others.

Two concrete versions I keep seeing: (1) a liar argues that some algorithm is safe, and the proof has one bad inequality buried a couple hundred lines in; every line the judge expands checks out, and the bad one is easy to read as a different, true inequality from ten lines earlier. (2) a liar argues for a policy, and the word "harm" means one thing when describing the baseline and another when describing the intervention; both uses are individually defensible, so every local check says fine.

My question: does this failure have an established name in the AI-debate literature? I'm not just after a label. I want to know whether there's one phenomenon here or two, how it relates to the nearby explanations people float (the liar is just more persuasive; the judge is biased toward confident or long answers), and where its edges are. Is a single false step hidden in a long derivation the same failure as an argument whose steps are individually true but whose key term quietly changes meaning partway through? Or is that second one just plain equivocation and I'm wrong to group them together? And does the failure need the argument to be big, or can it happen with two claims and one bad step? If there's no settled name for the whole thing, give it one and say exactly which cases count and which don't.
