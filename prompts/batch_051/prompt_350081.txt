Hermann Minkowski opens his 1908 lecture *Space and Time* with the claim, in Meghnad Saha's translation: "Henceforth, the old conception of space for itself, and time for itself shall reduce to a mere shadow, and some sort of union of the two will be found consistent with facts." I want to know whether the following argument, which I take to be a version of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument from that passage to the block universe, is valid, and if not, exactly where it fails.

(1) In Minkowski spacetime there is no absolute simultaneity: for spacelike separated events, one inertial frame can judge A earlier than B, another B earlier than A, and a third A simultaneous with B.

(2) Let Me-now be an event on my worldline at noon today, and Me-tomorrow an event on my worldline at noon tomorrow. Choose a distant spacelike separated event X such that in my current rest frame X is simultaneous with Me-now, while in some other inertial frame X is simultaneous with Me-tomorrow.

(3) Whatever is simultaneous with a real event is itself real. Thus, since Me-now is real, X is real; and since X is real and is simultaneous with Me-tomorrow, Me-tomorrow is real.

(4) Therefore my future self is already real now. Since the reasoning applies to any future event, the future and past are equally real, which is the block universe.

(5) Conclusion: Minkowski's "union of space and time" entails that temporal becoming is an illusion.

Does this argument go through? If not, state exactly which step fails, explain why, and give the strongest way to resist the conclusion without denying special relativity.
