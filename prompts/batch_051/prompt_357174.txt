Hey, I'm an early modern history PhD student with a side interest in philosophy of history, and I keep running into the Ranke line. You know the one — usually rendered *how it actually was*, or in the German everyone repeats, *wie es eigentlich gewesen*. In my department it functions as an argument-ender. Any time someone proposes a project that's interpretive or theory-driven — my thing is social memory, so I get this a lot — a certain kind of senior historian will say something like, well, Ranke already settled this, and then they produce the line. Last week one of them literally quoted it at me to shut down a chapter proposal I'd made on sixteenth-century chronicles: 'Man hat der Historie das Amt, die Vergangenheit zu richten, die Mitwelt zum Nutzen zukünftiger Jahre zu belehren, beigemessen; so hoher Ämter unterwindet sich der gegenwärtige Versuch nicht. Er will bloß zeigen, wie es wirklich gewesen ist.' The implication was that my whole memory-studies framework is just the kind of presentist moralising Ranke disowned.

But I'm suspicious, because the sentence seems to slide between a bunch of different claims without telling me which one is doing the work. It could mean just that historians shouldn't hand out moral judgments; or that there's a single determinate past independent of the historian's perspective; or that the past can be described without imposing narrative form; or that only description is legitimate and explanation is not; or that a sufficiently critical reading of the sources just yields the past without any theoretical mediation. Some of those are boring, some are false, some I'd actually accept.

What I want is a menu, not a single reading. Take the slogan and lay out the distinct precise theses it could be expressing, from the platitudinous to the radical. For each, say briefly whether it's plausible, indefensible, false, or just under-defined. Then tell me which of those readings would actually license using the line to dismiss a social-memory reading of early modern chronicles, and which only license a weaker complaint like 'your framework adds nothing' rather than 'your framework distorts things'. If the two uses come apart, seperate them. Also: people in my field mix up *sagen* and *zeigen* and *eigentlich* and *wirklich* across editions; I'd love to know if any of the interesting precisifications hang on those word choices, cause that would mean the slogan is doing even more work than it looks.
