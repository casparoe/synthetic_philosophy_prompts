I'm an undergraduate in a social epistemology seminar, and for my final assignment I have to draft the joint statement that would result from a good-faith adversarial collaboration between two camps studying online political polarization. The first camp holds that the central problem is informational: people are trapped in epistemic bubbles, cut off from relevant and credible outside views, so the remedy is exposure to better information, outgroup sources, and fact-checks. The second camp holds that the central problem is relational and doxastic: people are in echo chambers, communities whose members have been trained to distrust outside sources and to grant authority only to insiders, so contrary evidence offered from outside is ignored, explained away, or treated as proof of the source's bad faith.

Write the document the two camps could both sign. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both accept about how online communities sort themselves, which regularities about selective exposure, correction, and persistent false belief both treat as settled data to be explained, and which outcomes both want their accounts to explain; (2) a disagreements table in which each position is stated in wording its own side would accept, with the residual disagreement made exact; (3) the cruxes, stated as sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one side, including what happens when insiders are presented with balanced or contrary evidence from sources they have been taught not to trust, and whether exposure-based or trust-based interventions better predict durable change; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case, argument, or test both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. The agreed claims should be neither vacuous nor slanted, and if part of the dispute now looks verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why.
