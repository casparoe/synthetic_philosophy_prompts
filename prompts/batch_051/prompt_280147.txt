I spent forty years as a physical chemist doing process safety, so my old way of thinking about risk was basically arithmetic: estimate p, estimate the severity, multiply, compare to a threshold, document it, move on. Since I retired I've been reading around in the philosophy of risk and in public debates about nuclear power, air travel, vaccines, pandemics, and chemical exposure limits, and I keep having the feeling that the engineering approach is missing something, but I can't yet say exactly what the missing pieces are. So before I read still more theories, I want the full specification: what would an adequate theory of how much risk a society should tolerate have to get right?

Give me the requirements list — the cases it must handle, the distinctions it can't collapse, the results from decision theory or risk-perception research or elsewhere that it can't contradict — with the case or consideration behind each, and flag which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's preferences. I also want to know whether any subset of the list is jointly unsatisfiable (that, honestly, would be the most useful result), and which single requirement does the most work in knocking out the usual contenders, by which I mean cost-benefit analysis, the precautionary principle, consent-based views, that sort of thing. Skip the full scorecard; I'll do that myself. Two distinctions I'm especially unsure about: tiny risks spread over millions of people versus the same expected harm concentrated in a few identifiable people (in the lab we treated those as equivalent, but I'm not sure a society should), and voluntary versus imposed risk (smoking or climbing versus living downwind of a plant). Are those genuine requirements or just my intuitions? And is there a tension between a requirement to respect individual consent about imposed risk and the fact that any collective decision changes someone's risk profile without their signature? I dont need you to resolve that tension now, but I do want it flagged if it means the list itself can't be fully met.
