Back in the summer of 2019 a Facebook event called "Storm Area 51, They Can't Stop All of Us" went from a joke made by some guy in California to one of the biggest online pile-ons of the year. Something like two million people clicked "going," the Air Force had to put out a statement warning people not to actually do it, the joke mutated into Naruto-running memes, and in late September a few thousand people did show up in the Nevada desert near the base—but almost nobody tried to storm anything; it turned into a weird mix of a music festival, an alien-themed costume party, and a handful of arrests. I keep thinking this is a near-perfect experiment for the Darwinian theory of culture, the memetics view that cultural items replicate, mutate, and get selected like genes or parasites. But my friend in a cultural evolution course keeps insisting memetics cant handle the gap between "clicking going" and "actually driving to Nevada."

Could you analyze the Storm Area 51 episode through the lens of memetic theory? I want three things. First, say what the actual unit of selection is here: the Facebook event page, the phrase itself, the joke premise, the Naruto-run variant, or something finer-grained—and walk through its replication chain from one host to the next. Second, identify the selection pressures that made this meme beat out the enourmous population of other dumb Facebook events that summer; be specific about why its features (absurdity, low effort, plausible deniability, etc.) mattered for transmission. Third, confront the awkward part for the theory: the meme generated huge copy number but essentially zero real-world behavior of the kind it named. What should a memeticist do with that? Is the storming-behavior just a phenotype that never got expressed, or does the whole episode show that memetics overstates the link between a meme's spread and its power to drive action? End by saying whether you think the case is a point in favor of the gene/parasite view of culture, an anomaly, or evidence that the framework is just silent where we'd want it to speak.

Please write your answer in American English.
