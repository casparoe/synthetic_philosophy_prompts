My brother's been on this kick lately where he says his phone is literally part of his mind. His argument is that the only reason a memory in your head counts as yours is that it's there when you need it and you trust it, and his phone beats his biological memory on both counts — it's on him all day, he consults it without a second thought, and he never has to wonder whether a password is "really" in his head or in the phone. From this he concludes that the whole hand-wringing about AI assistants and cognitive offloading is misplaced: we've been extending our minds into artifacts for decades, and a device you lean on as automatically as he leans on his phone just is part of your cognitive system, no different in principle from the wet stuff between your ears.

I keep telling him he's strecthed a metaphor into a metaphysics, but I can't exactly say where the stretching happens. Can you lay it out properly? I want the correspondence table first — what plays the role of a stored memory, of recall, of forgetting, of a belief you'd act on, of the feeling of having something on the tip of your tongue — and then the places the mapping breaks. In particular I want to know which disanalogies actually matter for his conclusion (that offloading to a device is the same kind of thing as remembering, and that worries about AI assistants are confused) and which ones are just cosmetic. And tell me whether his conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it the whole time.
