For the non-logicians who'll be in the room: Curry's paradox is the one where a sentence says, roughly, 'if this sentence is true, then the dean is a mailbox.' The thing bootstraps — suppose the sentence is true, then its condition is met and you get the consequent; but that conditional is exactly what the sentence asserted, so you've proved the sentence after all, and then the consequent just falls out. Ordinary-looking rules, absurd conclusion. I want the toast to hang on one good Curry's-paradox joke. The room will be half logicians and philosophy grad students who'll catch every reference, and half family and old friends who couldn't tell you what a conditional is. So write me something about five minutes long, with the logic explained just enough that the non-logicians aren't lost, but not so much that the logicians feel like their back in a review lecture. Ideally the toast should actually do what Curry does — set up a harmless little self-referential sentence and then pull something warm and slightly overstated out of it — rather than just name-drop the paradox. It has to land as affection for her, not as a technical tribute, and it can't be treacly; she'd spot a fake argument or an empty compliment from across the room.
