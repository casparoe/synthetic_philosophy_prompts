People often defend idealized deliberation — asking what you would want if you were fully informed, vividly aware of what each option would actually feel like, and free of bias, fatigue, and weakness of will — by comparing it to restoring a blurred photograph. On this picture, your present preferences are a degraded image of your true values: ignorance, self-deception, prejudice, and momentary distortion are the blur; the idealizing procedure (more information, more vivid imagination, more logical coherence) is the deblurring algorithm; and the output of the procedure is what you really wanted all along. The analogy does real work, because it is used to conclude that there is a determinate fact about your true self, and that idealized deliberation merely reveals rather than changes you.
