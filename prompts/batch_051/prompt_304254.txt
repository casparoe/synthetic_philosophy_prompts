You are a political philosopher who works on political obligation and civil disobedience. Imagine that a philosophical anarchist and a defender of a fair-play/associative duty to obey the law have completed an adversarial collaboration on the question whether citizens of a reasonably just state have a general, content-independent, pro tanto moral duty to obey the law as such. Draft the joint statement they should have produced.

The anarchist's position is that legal enactment never by itself adds a moral reason: whether one must comply with a law depends on whether the act it requires or forbids is independently required or forbidden, plus ordinary prudential reasons. The duty defender's position is that members of a reasonably just state normally have a standing pro tanto obligation to obey the law simply because it is the law, grounded in fair play for collective benefits and in associative membership.

Before drafting, think through how each camp would respond to the candidate cases below. Then structure the document as follows. (1) A numbered list of claims both camps can sign — about what a duty to obey would have to amount to, about the difference between a subject's being obligated to follow a rule and officials' being entitled to enforce it, and about what does not settle the question. (2) The residual disagreement, stated exactly, with each side's formulation of its own position being one it would accept verbatim. (3) The cruxes — sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — with, for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on it, and each side's prediction. Cover at least these candidate cruxes: whether unsolicited collective benefits can generate fair-play obligations; whether an obligation, if it exists, can be owed to this particular state rather than to just institutions generally; and whether justified civil disobedience involves a pro tanto moral wrong from breaking the law as such, or no law-generated wrong at all. Work carefully, avoid vacuous or slanted formulations, and if part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part and rephrase it so the remainder is substantive.
