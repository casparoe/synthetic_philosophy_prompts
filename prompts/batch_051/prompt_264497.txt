ok so i went down a youtube rabbit hole about panpsychism (the idea that everything has some kind of mind-stuff going on in it, even electrons) and now i'm stuck on the combination problem. If a bunch of tiny particles each have their own little scrap of experience, why would adding them together ever give you one big unified experience like the one in my head right now? Shouldn't it just be a pile of tiny separate feels? The usual answers I saw felt like hand-waving. So I made up my own idea, which I've been calling 'perspectival compression.' The basic claim: when two or more subjects interact in the right kind of organized way, their individual points of view don't 'merge' at all — they get compressed out of existence and replaced by a single new point of view, kind of like how two overlapping wave patterns make one interference pattern that isn't either of the original waves. Or like a bunch of scattered notes getting compressed into a chord: you don't hear the notes anymore, you hear one chord. So for a human brain, the trillion micro-subjects are canceled and a new macro-subject (me) shows up. This solves the combination problem, I think, because there's no need to explain how many minds fuse into one; the many get squeezed out and a fresh one replaces them.

But I'm not sure this is actually a theory and not just a metaphor with a confident name. If the micro-subjects are really canceled, is my view secretly saying they die every time a mind forms, and should I be worried about that? And maybe 'compression' is doing all the work — I can't say exactly what it is except by pointing at waves and chords, which are physical, not experiential. I want a honest assessment: is this coherent, has someone already said it under a real name, and what's the closest actual position I tripped into? Give me the three strongest objections and tell me if any of them is fatal rather than just uncomfortable. And if the whole thing collapses, say what the combination problem still needs from me before I can call it solved.
