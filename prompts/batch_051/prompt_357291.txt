I am not an academic, but I have been turning an analogy over in my mind, and I want a clear-eyed assessment of whether it is a genuine structural resemblance or just a pattern-match between two things that happen to sit next to each other in my head.

On one side there is a distinction I keep coming across in thinking about concepts: between the project of carefully spelling out what an established concept already means, and the project of deliberately altering or replacing a concept because it is defective, whether through incoherence, unhelpful shape, or the bad work it does. I have seen the second project called conceptual engineering or ameliorative analysis. On the other side there is a distinction in software: between producing a faithful specification or model of what a legacy codebase actually does, and refactoring that codebase so it better serves its intended use.

The analogy that keeps occurring to me is this: analysing a concept is like documenting existing code, while engineering a concept is like refactoring it. A specification is answerable to what is there; refactoring treats what is there as something to be changed.

My question is whether this analogy is deep or superficial. I would like you to state the mapping precisely, say where it holds and where it breaks down, and say what would have to be true of both sides for anything to transfer from the software case to the conceptual case. I am especially stuck on one point, and it may be the test: software refactoring is normally disciplined by the requirement that observable behaviour is preserved even while the internal structure changes. Does conceptual engineering preserve some equivalent of observable behaviour, such as the extension of the concept across an agreed range of cases, or does it deliberately change the concept's behaviour? If it changes the behaviour, the better software analogue would be rewriting or forking rather than refactoring, and that difference would matter to whether the analogy supports the philosophical project or quietly undermines it.

Please begin with your bottom line in your first sentence, then give your reasoning. Do not preface your reply or restate my question.
