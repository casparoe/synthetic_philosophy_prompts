After a bitter political defeat last month, my sister tried to steady me by quoting the line, "The arc of the moral universe is long, but it bends toward justice." She was using it as if it guaranteed that our cause would eventually prevail, and from that she concluded that losing heart is just a failure to take the long view. I have heard the line all my life, but I realized I cannot say what exactly it asserts. I want to be able to go back to her with a clear account. Lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making—from a bare empirical generalization about some past moral improvements, through a law-like claim about inevitable progress, to a full teleological or providential claim that history has a direction set by something beyond human agency, and perhaps to a merely expressive reading that is not trying to state a fact at all—and for each reading say whether it is trivial, substantive but defensible, or false or question-begging. Then say which reading, if any, would actually license the move from "the arc bends toward justice" to "I am warranted in remaining confident about this particular struggle."
