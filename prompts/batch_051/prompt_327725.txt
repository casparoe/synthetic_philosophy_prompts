Two people in my philosophy of medicine group have been going at it for months: one's a Boorse-style naturalist about disease (disease is value-free failure of species-typical function), the other's a normativist who thinks you can't even fix what counts as a dysfunction without evaluative judgments. They've finally admitted they're mostly talking past eachother and want to try an adversarial collaboration. Draft the joint document they'd produce.

Use this structure, with rough lengths:
- Agreed claims (about 250 words): 8 numbered claims both would sign. No platitudes about values playing some role in medicine; make them get specific about what diagnostic categories actually track, why mere statistical atypicality isn't enough for disease, and what each side grants the other before they start disagreeing.
- Where they differ (about 250 words): a 4-row table. Columns: the exact question; the naturalist's wording of their own view; the normativist's wording of theirs. Each wording should be something that side would actually sign, not the other side's caricature.
- Cruxes and predictions (about 300 words): 3 cruxes. For each, give the sub-question that would move at least one side if settled, a concrete case both agree bears on it, and what each side predicts in advance. Make two of the cases (a) the 2017 lowering of the hypertension threshold to 130/80 and (b) the removal of homosexuality from the DSM in 1973. For the third crux, I suspect the real fight is over whether proper function is itself value-free — whether a selected-effects account and a causal-role account could ever converge on a natural fact of the matter — so build the third case around that.
- Verbal or substantive? (about 100 words): close by saying which disagreements are substantive and which are mostly about words.

Keep it under 900 words total. No citations, no reading list, no bibliography — this is meant to be something both could sign, not a survey of the field.
