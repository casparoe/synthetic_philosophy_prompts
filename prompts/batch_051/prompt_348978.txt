I've been reading the constructivist line on nationalism — Hobsbawm and Anderson — the claim that nations are mostly recent inventions, held together by print media, state schools, shared stories, and deleberate tradition-making rather than by deep pre-existing ethnic facts. That argument was made at a time when ordinary people had no cheap way to check anything that looked like an objective measurement of descent. Now everyone I know takes consumer DNA tests and gets a percentage breakdown of their ancestry, and they argue about whether someone is really Irish or really Jewish on the basis of those reports. Does the constructivist argument survive this development, or does one of its premises quietly break? If it survives, is the repaired version something like: nations are still imagined, but the imagining is not unconstrained, and even the genetic categories on the reports get built to match modern borders? I'm asking what happens to the argument, not what the policy should be.
