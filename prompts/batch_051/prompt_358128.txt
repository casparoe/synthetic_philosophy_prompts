I host a small philosophy of physics interview series, and next week I am recording an episode with a philosopher of physics who defends a version of Everettian quantum mechanics that takes the universal wave function alone to be physically real and holds that the appearance of a classical thermodynamic arrow is fully explained by the information-theoretic structure of Everett’s long thesis. I want the interview to go beyond restating the view, so I would like you to generate eight to ten interview questions built around a specific passage. The passage is from Hugh Everett III’s doctoral thesis, the long version circulated under the title Wave Mechanics Without Probability and later published as The Theory of the Universal Wave Function. He writes: “if one were to define the total entropy to be the negative of the total information, one could replace the usual second law of thermodynamics by a law of conservation of total entropy, where the increase in the standard (marginal) entropy is exactly compensated by a (negative) correlation entropy. The usual second law then results simply from our renunciation of all correlation knowledge (stosszahlansatz), and not from any intrinsic behavior of classical systems.” The guest’s position is that this passage generalizes to the quantum case and dissolves the worry that unitary-only mechanics cannot account for branching asymmetry or the observed thermodynamic arrow. I want questions that test whether that move works. For each question, anticipate the answer the guest is most likely to give and give me a follow-up keyed to that answer. I want at least one question each on the following: whether the entropy-information identity in the passage is a formal identity or a physical claim; whether the “renunciation of correlation knowledge” reintroduces an epistemic or observer-relative element into a purportedly objective account; whether an argument from classical Liouville mechanics can carry over to Everettian branching given that the latter must also explain the appearance of definite measurement records; and how the guest would compare the defense with a Bohmian or Copenhagen treatment of the same apparent irreversibility. I also want at least one question about what would count as empirical disconfirmation of the information-theoretic story. Please press where the literature presses, and avoid generic questions. Write the questions as continuous prose in an email-like format, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
