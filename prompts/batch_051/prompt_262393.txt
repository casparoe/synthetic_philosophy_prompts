Coming from the sciences, I want a definition precise enough to be applied case by case, not a philosophical slogan. Give me a working definition of "acting of one's own free will"—one that does not quietly presuppose either compatibilism or incompatibilism—that classifies the following six cases correctly: (1) a person who, after months of deliberation and under no threat, turns down a lucrative post because it conflicts with their values; (2) a person who signs a false confession with a gun to their head; (3) a person with an undiagnosed brain lesion that has altered their impulses and who commits a theft they would previously have found unthinkable; (4) a person acting on a post-hypnotic suggestion, who confidently confabulates an explanation afterward; (5) a person in a Frankfurt-style case, where a device would intervene only if they were about to choose option B, but they choose option A on their own and the device never activates; and (6) a person who agrees to donate an organ under aggressive, unnoticed "nudges." I take the first as clearly free and the second as clearly not free; I have no settled view about the rest. Propose the definition, run it through all six cases, and tell me which of your verdicts surprises you or feels most revisionary. Finally, identify the single verdict you hold with least confidence, say what fact, argument, or counterexample would most likely change your mind about it, and tell me where your reasoning as a whole is most likely to fail.
