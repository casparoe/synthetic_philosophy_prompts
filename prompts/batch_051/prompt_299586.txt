The regional health authority's Crisis Standards of Care, drafted in 2007 for a pandemic-influenza scenario, contains the following provision (Section 4.2), which has never been invoked until now:

"In a declared critical resource emergency, scarce life-sustaining interventions shall be allocated so as to maximize the aggregate number of quality-adjusted life-years (QALYs) expected to be gained. Each candidate shall receive a priority score equal to the product of (a) the probability of survival to discharge with the intervention, (b) the expected additional years of life with the intervention, and (c) a quality-of-life weight reflecting the patient's pre-existing functional status. Candidates whose expected post-discharge survival is less than twelve months, or whose pre-existing quality-of-life weight is below 0.3, shall not be offered scarce life-sustaining resources."

This July the city water main failed during a heatwave. For the estimated three weeks of repairs, the hospital's back-up generator can run the operating theatres or the renal dialysis unit, but not both. The hospital has declared a resource emergency and plans to apply Section 4.2 to decide which of its roughly forty chronic dialysis patients will keep receiving dialysis and which will be asked to forgo sessions, with the near-certain consequence that some of those refused will die.

The provision says nothing about dialysis, about maintenance therapy on which a patient has depended for years, or about withdrawing an intervention already in place. I care less about the practical answer than about whether the rule actually reaches these patients. What do the words of the provision, its purpose, the drafters' likely answer had they been asked in 2007, and a reasonable reader's understanding each yield? Where do these approaches conflict, and which should govern here?
