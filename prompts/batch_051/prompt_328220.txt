After a long career in the natural sciences, I have recently started reading the philosophy of causation, and I have been trying to understand whether the faithfulness assumption is genuinely needed in causal discovery, or whether a weaker minimality condition together with the Causal Markov Condition already suffices to ensure that the Bayes nets one selects track the true causal structure. I worked through the following argument, which seems to show that minimality alone already guarantees that no true causal arrow is omitted. In fact, the argument looks as if it proves something even stronger — that any DAG whatsoever that satisfies the Markov condition must contain every edge of the true graph — and that makes me suspicious. But I cannot locate the faulty step. Could you say whether the argument is valid as stated, and if it is not, identify the first step that fails and explain exactly what it assumes that has not been granted?

(1) Let G* be the true causal directed acyclic graph over a finite set V of variables, and let P be the joint distribution generated by that causal structure. Assume the Causal Markov Condition in its global form: every d-separation relation entailed by G* is a conditional independence in P.

(2) Say that a DAG H is Markov for P when every d-separation relation entailed by H is a conditional independence in P; say further that H is minimal for P when H is Markov for P and no proper subgraph of H is Markov for P.

(3) Suppose H is any DAG that is Markov for P. If the argument works for any Markov DAG, it works a fortiori for minimal ones. I claim H must contain every edge of G*. Suppose for contradiction that X→Y is an edge in G* but is absent from H.

(4) Since H is acyclic, X and Y cannot each be a descendant of the other in H. Suppose, without loss of generality, that Y is not a descendant of X in H. By the local Markov property of H, which follows from the Markov condition, X is independent of its non-descendants in H given its parents in H. Since Y is a non-descendant of X and is not a parent of X — X and Y are not adjacent in H — it follows that X is independent of Y conditional on S, the set of X's parents in H. If instead X is not a descendant of Y in H, the symmetric move conditional on the parents of Y gives an analogous conclusion. Thus P contains a conditional independence between X and Y given some set S.

(5) But X→Y is a direct causal arrow in the true graph G*. Conditioning on S does not block this direct causal path, and S contains neither X nor Y. If X really is a direct cause of Y, the influence along that arrow cannot be removed by conditioning on further variables, so X and Y must remain probabilistically dependent given S. This contradicts step (4).

(6) Therefore any DAG that is Markov for P must contain every edge of the true causal graph, and so any minimal DAG for P must do so as well. Hence, under the Causal Markov Condition alone, minimality prevents any true direct causal connection from being lost, and faithfulness is unnecessary for that part of causal discovery.

Where does this break down, if it does?
