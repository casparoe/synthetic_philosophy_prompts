So people who study science education say that when a kid answers physics questions wrong in a systematic way—like saying a ball keeps moving after you throw it because the throw put some force into it that gradually runs out—they're not just making random mistakes, they're applying something like a little theory of motion. My question is how you would actually test that. Because the alternative is that the kid just has a bunch of disconnected wrong rules ('moving things slow down,' 'heavier things fall faster') that happen to give the same wrong answers on the test, without any of the coherence or generative power that would make it a theory. So design the best test you can to distinguish 'the kid has something theory-like' from 'the kid has a patchwork of memorized rules that only look theory-ish when you squint.' Be concrete about what you'd present to them, what pattern of responses would count for the theory view and what pattern would count against it, and then say what your test still couldn't settle—like wether the kid can introspect the difference, or whether calling it a 'theory' is even doing real work at that age. If part of the dispute turns out to be about the word 'theory' rather than anything in the kid's head, say which part.
