I'm a self-taught programmer and I've spent most of my working life looking after a messy legacy codebase, so the vocabulary I actually think in is git: commits, branches, merge conflicts, reverts, regression tests, branches that get abandoned, all that. Lately I've been trying to work out whether philosophy makes progress, because from the outside it looks like people have been argueing about the same problems for centuries. From what I can gather there are about three positions. One says philosophy doesn't really progress at all, it just cycles through fashions and renames old arguments. Another says it does progress, but not by settling the big questions: it progresses by sharpening concepts, making distinctions, and working out what a good answer would even have to look like. The third says progress does happen, but it keeps getting hived off into science, logic or mathematics, and the leftover 'pure philosophy' hasn't got any closer to answering anything.

I'd like you to restate each of those three positions in the vocabulary of version control. For each one, say what translates cleanly, what only fits if you stretch the git metaphor, and what has no expression in that vocabulary at all. Then tell me whether the residue — the bits that don't survive the translation — is just a limitation of my framework, or whether it shows that some of these claims about progress are actually less clear than they look. I'd rather you be honest about where the translation breaks than force it to fit.
