I am a novelist, and a subplot in my current book turns on a defamation claim against a service that rates and forecasts how reliable an author's sources are. I have already written several decisions for my invented appellate court, and I need to know how it should decide the new case if it is to remain internally consistent. It need not match any real jurisdiction; I care about coherent extrapolation from the court's own record.

The court has held as follows:

- In a restaurant-review case, the court ruled that a critic's description of a chef's tasting menu as "inedible" and "absurdly overpriced" was non-actionable: these are subjective judgments of taste and value, not verifiable statements.
- In a rankings case, the court ruled that a magazine's inclusion of a property developer on its list of the city's "shadiest developers," with no supporting examples, was non-actionable, as loose evaluative language and rhetorical hyperbole.
- In a bribery case, the court ruled that a trade journal's statement that a named contractor "bribed a housing inspector to pass substandard work" was actionable, because it asserted a concrete, verifiable allegation of misconduct.
- In a bond-rating case, the court ruled that an agency's unexplained downgrade of a town's bonds from AA to B− was non-actionable: a rating expresses a forward-looking evaluative judgment about creditworthiness, not a present fact.
- In a score case, the court ruled that a service's publication of a "management-integrity score" of 5/10 for a public company, with no accompanying factual claims, was non-actionable: a bare numerical evaluation discloses no verifiable facts.

The new case is this: a literary-analytics firm gives a debut memoirist a "source-verifiability rating" of 3/10 and appends only the note, "Projection: our model estimates that the memoir's central factual claims are unlikely to be confirmable." She sues for defamation, arguing that the rating and the note imply she fabricated or misreported events.

Should my court, if it follows its own precedents, treat that rating and note as actionable or not? Identify the candidate rules that fit the earlier decisions, say which precedent is the closest analog, and state what principle the court would be adopting under either outcome. Then name the further case that would most sharply force the court to choose between those principles.
