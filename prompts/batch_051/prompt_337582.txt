Compare the Stoic ideal of personal self-sufficiency with the classical political ideal of national autarky, and defend a definite view about how the two are related.

To frame the comparison, distinguish two senses of self-sufficiency: independence, the condition of not needing external things in order to flourish or function well; and completeness, the condition of containing within oneself everything necessary for the good at which one aims. The Stoic sage is traditionally described as autarkēs: because virtue alone is said to suffice for eudaimonia, health, wealth, reputation, and political circumstances are treated as externals or indifferents rather than as constituents of happiness. By contrast, the political ideal of autarky, prominent in Aristotle's political thought, treats self-sufficiency as a property of the polis: a city is self-sufficient when it can supply from its own resources the material, social, and deliberative conditions of the good life for its citizens, and collective self-sufficiency is taken to be something like a prerequisite of individual flourishing.

Compare these two ideals. Are they best understood as one and the same value applied at two different scales, or as structurally different and perhaps even competing ideals? In your answer, address at least the following: (i) which sense of self-sufficiency, independence or completeness, each ideal primarily embodies; (ii) whether an individual could achieve Stoic self-sufficiency within a polis that fails the test of autarky, and whether a fully autarkic polis could be composed of people who lack Stoic self-sufficiency; and (iii) whether the ideal of national autarky tends to presuppose, produce, or undermine the self-sufficiency of individual persons. End by stating and defending a definite comparative thesis rather than merely summarizing the two positions.
