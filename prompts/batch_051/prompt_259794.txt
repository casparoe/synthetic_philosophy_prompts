I run a small giving circle with some friends, and we've been arguing for weeks about where the money should go. I keep pushing for a local tutoring program and a fundraiser for a neighbor's cancer treatment, and one member keeps saying we should give to a global health charity because it's way more effective per dollar. I genuinely like the idea of helping people we can see and know. But I've noticed something that makes me nervous. When we vote on a spreadsheet with just numbers and no names, I pick the global health option. When we see a photo and a personal story, I switch to the local one. My friend says that proves my "care about community" thing is really just a bias, and I'm getting defensive, which maybe means she's right. But it also feels like hiding the names is rigging the test: maybe caring about concrete relationships really is the point, and the spreadsheet just edits out the moral part. How do I tell whether I have a genuine moral view here or am just rationalizing a bias? What would actually settle it?
