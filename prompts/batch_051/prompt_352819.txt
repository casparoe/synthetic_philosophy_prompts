The exam question was:

"Explain Bradley's regress and assess its bearing on the problem of the unity of the proposition."

The student's answer was:

"Bradley's regress arises when we ask what a relation's holding consists in. Suppose a binary relation r relates a and b. If r is simply another entity, comparable to a and b, then a further relation r′ would appear to be needed to relate r to a and to r to b; applying the same reasoning to r′ yields an infinite regress. Bradley himself took the regress to show that relations are ultimately unintelligible, and concluded that reality is a single unified whole, with distinct terms being appearance rather than ultimate truth.

The problem of the unity of the proposition is usually introduced by noting that a proposition's constituents are not by themselves enough for something truth-evaluable. If 'a is F' were just the collection of a, F, and an instantiation relation, we could still ask what unifies that collection into a proposition. The natural move is to let the instantiation relation do the unifying, but that reinstates Bradley's regress, since the instantiation relation would then need to be related to a and to F by further relations. Russell originally raised this as a difficulty for theories of the proposition, and his multiple relation theory of judgment was intended to bypass it by making judgment, rather than the proposition, the fundamental truth-evaluable unit.

The regress also establishes a positive conclusion: instantiation cannot be a universal. Since treating instantiation as a universal forces an endless chain of further relations to be posited, the only tenable view is that instantiation is a primitive tie that does the binding without itself being another constituent of the proposition."
