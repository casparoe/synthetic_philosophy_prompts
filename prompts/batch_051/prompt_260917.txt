I keep coming back to this question of what actually makes me the same person as the kid I was. Obviously the atoms have changed, so it can't be the exact same stuff. My first answer was memory: I'm that kid because I remember being him, or at least a chain of memories links back to him. But then a friend gave me a puzzle that I can't shake. An old general remembers being a brave young officer, and the young officer remembered being a boy caught stealing apples, but the old general honestly cannot remember the boy at all. If being the same person is just memory, then the general is the officer and the officer is the boy, but the general isnt the boy. That seems to break the transitivity of identity, which I thought was non-negotiable.

So I started wondering whether any of this can be settled by something like a test instead of just arguing in circles. You hear about amnesia, general anesthesia, ordinary sleep, even the idea of uploading someone's memories into a new body. Could you design a case or experiment — even a thought experiment, but a concrete one with a clear result — that would tell us whether memory is what carries identity, or whether what really matters is the continued brain or body? Like, if you slowly replaced every memory one at a time over years, or erased ten years and then confirmed the person still felt like themselves, what exactly would that show? I want the best test you can come up with, what specific result would support each side, and then an honest accounting of what even a clean result would not settle — because I suspect there's something in here no experiment can reach.

After the main response, give me a table with columns: Test, Result favouring memory continuity, Result favouring brain/body continuity, and What it still leaves open. A few rows is fine.
