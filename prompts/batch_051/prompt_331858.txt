Construct me a case where an interventionist theory of causation says a higher-level property is a cause of an effect, but Kim-style causal exclusion reasoning says it can't be. Setup: the lower-level realiser of the higher-level property is present and sufficient for the effect, the higher-level property is distinct from that realiser, and there's no overdetermination by two independent causes. The higher-level property should be mental or intentional-ish -- a belief, a desire, a decision, a representation -- so the case bites on mental causation rather than generic macro-causation. Constraints: keep it ordinary and concrete, no demons, no quantum hand-waving, and don't simply stipulate the causal verdict; the interventionist verdict has to come out of what we'd say about manipulating the higher-level variable while holding other causes of the effect fixed. Then run the check: state the property, the realiser, the effect, the intervention that establishes higher-level causation, why the exclusion argument says the realiser screens it off, and which premise a non-reductive physicalist should reject. Finish by saying which feature of the case does the work, whether the case survives if the higher-level property is only multiply realisable in principle rather than in the actual world, and how an exclusionist would attack the case.
