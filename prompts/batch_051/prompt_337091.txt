Scholars of nationalism often hold that nations are modern constructions: nationalism creates nations rather than expressing pre-existing ethnic communities, and many supposedly ancient national traditions were in fact assembled quite recently. If that is right, why do historians still speak of the English or French nation in the Middle Ages, and why do so many national traditions look authentically old? Doesn't the older usage of the word and the apparent antiquity of the traditions refute the modernist account? In answering, please separate what is broadly settled among scholars of nationalism from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.
