I spent thirty years as an environmental geologist, and I now sit on a six-person municipal board that meets monthly to decide whether to request a formal county investigation into reports of possible water contamination. We have no staff and no member with formal training in philosophy; a report usually gets fifteen minutes of discussion before a vote; and we can only refer a matter upward, though a referral affects property values, public confidence, and the board's credibility. I have been reading about Nyāya epistemology and its four pramāṇas, and I want you to turn that framework into a decision procedure we can actually run, one that yields a clear recommendation rather than a private hunch. As I understand them: pratyakṣa is direct perception by a reliable observer; anumāna is inference from an observed sign to what it indicates, where the sign is known to be invariably connected with the indicated thing, in the way smoke indicates fire; upamāna is knowledge of something unfamiliar through likeness to something already known; and śabda is knowledge obtained from the words of a trustworthy person. Please give us a step-by-step protocol: the questions to ask about any report in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure terminates in no action, where it must hand the decision to a person because the remaining question is one of values rather than evidence, and where a pramāṇa supports only probable belief rather than knowledge. Then run the procedure on three reports: a resident's report of an intermittent solvent smell in tap water; a consultant's sample showing a compound that in our region is strongly associated with one kind of industrial discharge, though the consultant will not name the source; and a county official's statement that a neighboring town's contamination was essentially the same as ours and should be acted upon. Make sure at least one of the worked cases produces a recommendation the board will find unwelcome, and say explicitly what the procedure cannot do. Flag each step as genuinely grounded in Nyāya, as your own reconstruction from it for this setting, or as a value choice that no epistemology can settle for us. Throughout, please separate what is settled among scholars of Nyāya from what is contested, and do not present a contested reading as if it were the consensus.
