I keep going back and forth on a claim, and I'd like less of a philosophical argument and more of a how would we actually find out answer. The claim is that there isn't really a scientific method — not that science is irrational, or that scientists do whatever they fancy, but that any attempt to codify how science should proceed (observe, hypothesise, falsify, and so on) ends up either so vauge it excludes nothing, or so rigid that some breakthrough you'd have to count as good science broke the rules. Feyerabend's strong version says that, methodologically, anything goes. So the underlying question is whether the phrase scientific method picks out anything real, or is just a story we tell after the fact.

Here's the thing. Suppose someone asserted there is a single scientific method, and someone else asserted there isn't. What would actually test that claim? I want the best test you can design — historical, sociological, experimental, whichever feels strongest — concrete enough that it could in principle be carried out. Tell me what result would convince you one way or the other, and then tell me what part of the question no test could ever settle, even in principle.
