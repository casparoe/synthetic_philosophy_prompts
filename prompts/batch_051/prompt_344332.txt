While reading about the free will defense for the problem of evil I got stuck on a sharper version aimed at angels, and I wrote the worry out as an argument. (1) Before he fell, Satan was good in the only sense that matters here: no prior defect in his will, and nothing outside him tempting him — unlike Eve, he had no snake. (2) Any bad act by a rational creature needs an explanation, and the only candidates are a prior bad condition in the agent, something outside the agent, or the bad act itself. (3) In the very first bad act by that creature there is no prior bad condition, and nothing outside the agent can force a rational will to sin against its own nature. (4) An act can't explain itself. (5) So a good rational creature couldn't commit a first sin. But the whole point of the doctrine is that Satan did exactly that: an originally good angel fell by his own choice. Is the move from (1)–(4) to (5) valid? If you take (5) to be wrong, say exactly which step has to go and lay out the strongest replacement reading a defender of the free will defense could give, then check whether that reading actually saves the defense. I half-suspect the list in (2) is too short but I can't articulate the missing option.
