My philosophy of biology seminar is framed as an inquiry into which of Aristotle's four causes still do explanatory work after Darwin, and the professor has a standing practice: each week she rules on one candidate explanatory claim and classifies it either as a legitimate function claim or as something else. The running record so far is:

- "The heart is there because it pumps blood" — function.
- "The kidney is there because it filters metabolic wastes from the blood" — function.
- "The butterfly's eyespots are there to startle predators" — function.
- "The giraffe's long neck is there because long-necked ancestors out-reproduced their shorter-necked rivals" — function.
- "The panda's enlarged wrist bone is there because it lets the animal strip bamboo" — function.
- "Water flows downhill in order to reach the lowest point" — not a function; she calls it a mere disposition.
- "Human earlobes are for supporting earrings" — not a function.
- "Blood is red because it contains hemoglobin" — a legitimate explanation, she says, but not a function claim.

Next week's case is invented, not drawn from the readings. In a species of freshwater fish, a recent mutation causes a small patch of skin near the tail to fluoresce under ultraviolet light. The fluorescence arose as an unselected side effect of a pigment change that was itself selected for camouflage. In controlled trials, predators are significantly less likely to strike at fish with brighter fluorescence, so the fluorescence genuinely contributes to predator avoidance. But the allele is only a few generations old, and no one has yet measured allele-frequency change driven by the fluorescence; selection on the fluorescence itself has not been established.

Please predict how the professor would rule on the claim "The skin patch fluoresces in order to deter predators." In your answer: identify the general account of function or teleological explanation that best fits her record; identify at least one rival account that also fits her past rulings and say what it would predict about this case; give a best-guess prediction of her verdict with your reasons; and propose one further case, real or invented, that would most cleanly discriminate between the live accounts.
