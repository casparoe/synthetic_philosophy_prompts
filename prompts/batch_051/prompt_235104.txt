I just had a long argument with a friend about the burden of proof, and it left me stuck on an asymmetry in how people apply it. In ordinary reasoning, if someone posits a hidden intelligence that influences events but is in principle undetectable, we treat the absence of evidence as a good reason to reject the claim, and we do not think the skeptic owes a proof of nonexistence: whoever posits the extra entity owes the evidence. Yet when the hidden intelligence is God, many people who would never accept the retort that one cannot prove the thing does not exist in the first case treat that same retort as weighty in the second, and they regard agnosticism as the intellectually safe default rather than a position that itself needs justification. Is there a principle that makes God a justified special case, or is this only a psychologically motivated inconsistency? Please give the strongest defense of the asymmetry you can construct, pressure-test it against relevantly similar cases, and state clearly whether the asymmetry should be kept, reversed, or abandoned.
