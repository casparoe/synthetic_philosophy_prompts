I've spent the last few months going down the program equilibrium rabbit hole, and I'm trying to work out whether it's worth another two years of my life. The basic picture I have in my head is this: instead of modelling agents as choosing actions, you model them as submitting computer programs that can read each other's source code before they run, and the equilibrium is over programs rather than actions. The famous upshot is that in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma, two agents can each submit a program that cooperates iff the other program does, and mutual cooperation becomes an equilibrium rather than a defect. But whenever I try to push the framework toward anything real—contracts between anonymous parties, open-source norms, DAOs, LLM agents that can inspect each other's prompts or weights—I hit the same small set of snags, and I can't tell which snag is actually worth the time. For instance: is the relevant source the code, the training data, the prompt, or the full causal history? Does the result survive once programs can lie about, partially reveal, or strategically redact their code? Can anything be said about which cooperative program people will actually submit when there are many equilibria, without just gesturing at focal points or fairness? And does any of this constrain the learning dynamics by which agents might converge on such programs, or is the whole thing an existence result that tells us nothing about how we get there?

So my question is agenda-setting rather than a survey. I'm finishing a masters with a background in mathematical game theory and some programming but no serious logic background, and I have about two years before I need to publish something. If I want program equilibrium to become more than a neat toy model—something that helps design or regulate systems where agents can inspect each other's code—which two or three open problems in the area do you think matter most, and why those rather than the others? Please actually defend the ordering using explicit criteria (tractability, neglectedness, how many other questions hang on the result, whatever you think matters), and say what would change your ranking. I don't want a reading list or bibliography, just argued priorities. British English throughout, please.
