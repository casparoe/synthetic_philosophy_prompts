I spent much of last night in an argument with a friend about Benjamin Libet’s 1999 Journal of Consciousness Studies paper, Do We Have Free Will? We kept returning to the passage near the end where Libet, after presenting the timing of the readiness potential and the subject’s reported time of the conscious wish to move, declines to infer that free will is entirely illusory. His proposal instead is that the conscious will may operate during the final 100 to 150 milliseconds before the act is executed, not by initiating the act but by vetoing or permitting what unconscious cerebral processes have already set in motion. My friend’s view is that this is a purely verbal rescue: the veto, if it occurs, must itself be caused by unconscious antecedents, so free will has simply been shifted one step back. I think Libet may have identified a genuinely distinct functional role, even if it is narrower than traditional free will.

What is your actual credence in the proposition that at least one component of the conscious veto Libet describes is causally effective in a way that is not itself fully fixed by prior unconscious neural events—where fully fixed means that the presence or content of the subject’s conscious reasons at that moment makes no causal difference to whether the veto occurs? I would like a number, but the number should be the output of a decomposition. In particular, assign a rough probability to each of the following and show how they jointly yield your credence:

(a) The veto is a genuine causal process, rather than a retrospective confabulation or a by-product of the same unconscious mechanism that initiated the act.

(b) The veto is conscious in the relevant sense, rather than an unconscious process that is merely accompanied by awareness after the fact.

(c) Even if the veto is real and conscious, it is not itself fully caused by unconscious antecedents—that is, it can be responsive to conscious reasons at the moment of veto.

(d) The temporal resolution and subjective-timing assumptions of Libet’s experiments are sufficiently reliable for them to bear on this question at all.

Then tell me which of these four terms your final credence is most sensitive to, and specify one experiment or body of evidence that would move that term most, and in which direction.
