I've been going back and forth on Hacking's looping-effects picture of psychiatric classification, and I think I've basically come around to it. The view, as I understand it, is that when you classify people—give a cluster of symptoms a name like ADHD and start diagnosing it—the classification doesn't just sort a pre-existing group. People learn the label, fit themselves to it or push against it, clinicians adjust what counts as the disorder in response, and the whole category starts moving. So the kind of person being classified is partly reshaped by the classification itself, which is why psychiatric categories behave so differently from, I don't know, kidney stones. I find this convincing as a description of what's happened with things like depression, ADHD, maybe borderline personality disorder.

But I've also noticed something about myself: I find the view convincing partly because every historical case I look at seems to fit it after the fact. So now I want to know what would actually count against it. Not the crude "it's all brain disease anyway" reply, but the subtler stuff. What would a psychiatric category have to look like for a looping-effects person to say the loop isn't doing any work here? For instance: a category that stays stable for decades even though patients and clinicians talk about it constantly—how damaging is that? If people change after being labelled but the official defintion doesn't need to change, is that still a loop or just a half-loop? And how do you tell a genuine looping effect from the boring fact that doctors revise definitions when they learn more about the underlying condition? I'd want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with an honest note on which ones a committed looping-effects theorist would themselves concede as counting against the view, versus which they'd just wave off. It would also help to know where the looping picture and the plain biological picture actually make different predictions about a fresh diagnostic category, so I can see what in principle would pick between them. And if the view can be adjusted to survive all of these, say that, and tell me whether that's a strength or a sign I've insulated it so much it no longer risks anything.
