Argue for the position that the term meme is more than a metaphor: at least one delimited class of cultural variants satisfies the conditions for Darwinian replication well enough that selection on those variants explains something about cultural change that a story appealing only to receiver-side reconstruction cannot. Do not defend the strong thesis that all cultural transmission is high-fidelity copying. Instead, proceed as follows: state the minimal conditions you take to be required for natural selection to get a grip on a population of variants; identify one plausible class of cultural items that meets them; say what explanatory payoff this yields; and end by answering the strongest reconstructionist objection — that in typical transmission a receiver rebuilds a representation from cues rather than copying a structure from its source. Present the argument as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion, and make the fifth point the rebuttal to that objection.
