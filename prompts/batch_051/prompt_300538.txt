I've been spending an unhealthy amount of time on the cogito, and I've managed to talk myself into a corner. On one side, I'm increasingly convinced that the argument proves almost nothing. Once you stop reading it with the conclusion already in mind, all it seems to license is that thinking is occurring — that there's mental activity right now, while I'm attending to it. It does not, on its own, get you a thinker, a continuing self, a thinking substance, or even a single "I" that was there before this thought and will be there after it. Lichtenberg's old point keeps nagging at me: just as it's raining doesn't commit you to something that does the raining, maybe thinking doesn't commit you to something that does the thinking. The "I" might just be grammar, not something delivered by the experience. And the austere reading has real virtues — it's metaphysically abstemious, it refuses to smuggle in more than the argument can pay for, and it explains why Descartes has to work so hard to rebuild a self he maybe never got in the first place.

On the other side, I have an intuition I can't shake, and I'm not even sure I want to. When I actually catch myself thinking, it doesn't show up as a detached episode of thought floating around. It shows up as mine — as something done by the very thing that was thinking five minutes ago and will be thinking in an hour. If someone insisted the cogito only proves that thought-events occur, I'd want to answer that I'm directly aware of the subject of those thought-events, and that awareness feels firmer then any premise in the argument. So I'm stuck between a theory I find nearly irrefutable and an intuition I find nearly ineradicable. What I want is a negotiated verdict, not a winner. Tell me what it would take for the intuition to go — is it a grammar-induced illusion plus some phenomenology I'm overreading? Tell me what it would take for the theory to be amended — is there a thin enough notion of "I" or "subject" that the cogito really does entitle me to it? And is there a reading on which the austere claim and the ordinary sense of being an I are compatible because they're answering different questions, or is the clash genuine? I want to know what each concession costs and how I would tell.
