I sit on the budget and planning committee of a public university, and at our last meeting a dean moved to phase out general funding for the philosophy department so that state money could be directed toward programs with clearer, shorter-term payoffs. When pressed, he ended the discussion with a single sentence: philosophy bakes no bread. He clearly regarded it as the sort of line that settles the matter, and several committee members nodded. I want to come to the next meeting with something more precise than the claim that he is being philistine.

Please take that sentence as your object and lay out the distinct claims it could be used to make. I can already see that on one reading it is almost trivially true—academic philosophers do not operate bakeries—and that on other readings it is making a much stronger point about value, knowledge, or what a public university is for; there are likely several readings in between. For each reading, say whether it is true, false, or merely beside the point, and whether it is a plausible interpretation of what the dean was actually doing. Then make explicit which of these readings, if any, would genuinely support cutting public funding for philosophy, and which would not survive being stated precisely. Please present this as a numbered list of readings, each with a verdict and a note on whether the reading supports the dean's conclusion. End with a one-sentence summary of your overall assessment as the final line, beginning with 'Summary:'.
