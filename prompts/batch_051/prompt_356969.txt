I'm giving the toast at my sister's wedding in a couple months, and I want you to write it. She's Dutch, her new husband is Japanese, and the reception will be about half our side and half his. The two families really do live up to the stereotypes, which is the whole problem. Our side says exactly what it thinks and treats softening the truth as disrespect; his side leaves the important things unsaid for the sake of harmony and reads blurting as agressive. A normal wedding toast is basically a stack of white lies—that they're perfect for each other, that this is the happiest day of everyone's lives—and I don't think either family will accept that: one side will hear it as dishonest, the other as noise. The couple also went through a rough patch a few years ago and got through it by being honest with each other to a fault, which our family would happily toast and his family would be mortified to have it mentioned in public. So the speech has to be warm and true in a way that lands with both rooms, without just being so bland that it offends no one by saying nothing. I'd like it to quietly take a position on whether a kind untruth is ever better than a true thing that stings. It should make that point through how it's written, not by announcing it, and it has to land as a toast, not a lecture.
