You are a philosopher of art who specializes in the ontology of music. I want a careful assessment of whether a standard argument in that field survives a specific technological development. Begin by reconstructing the argument in its strongest and most charitable form, then identify precisely which premise the development bears on, and only then give a verdict. Do not provide a bibliography or reading list; reason from your own understanding of the concepts.

The argument. A familiar and influential way to understand music runs as follows. A musical work is an abstract, repeatable thing—a type, or at least something type-like—such as a structure of sounds specified by a score. A performance is a token of that type: a concrete, dated event in which the structure is instantiated in sound. A recording, on this picture, is not itself a token of the work; it is a document of a token. Its role is to capture a particular performance event, so a recording of a symphony is first and foremost a trace of something that happened in a room, with musicians producing the sounds continuously over time.

The development. Contemporary studio practice now routinely breaks the causal chain between performance event and finished recording. A track may be assembled from dozens or hundreds of separate takes; individual notes are pitch-corrected or time-aligned against a grid; recorded drums are layered with or replaced by samples; synthesized or AI-generated material is inserted; and with stem separation and voice cloning, a finished song may contain no continuous performance by any single musician at all—in the limiting case, no one ever performed, in one place and time, the piece the listener hears. The object people consume is less a trace of an event than a constructed artifact fabricated in the studio.

Question: Does the standard type/token/document picture survive this development, does it need repair, or does it simply stop applying? Is the finished composite best classified as (a) a token of the original work, (b) a document of a merely possible or merely implied performance, (c) a new kind of work or track with its own identity conditions, or (d) something the standard ontology cannot represent? More importantly, tell me which premise of the standard argument is actually under pressure—the claim that works are abstract types, the claim that tokens of works must be performances, or the claim that recordings derive their status from performances—and whether a weakened or revised version can still do the needed work. Be candid: if the development forces only a small amendment rather than a collapse, say so.
