To what extent does Hobbes’s handling of the fool who says in his heart there is no justice change if the fool is not an exception in a largely just society, but rather a rational actor in a society where defection is already widespread and commonly known to be so? Consider the following passage from Chapter XV of Leviathan:

The Foole hath sayd in his heart, there is no such thing as Justice; and sometimes also with his tongue; seriously alleaging, that every mans conservation, and contentment, being committed to his own care, there could be no reason, why every man might not do what he thought conduced thereunto: and therefore also to make, or not make; keep, or not keep Covenants, was not against Reason, when it conduced to ones benefit. He does not therein deny, that there be Covenants; and that they are sometimes broken, sometimes kept; and that such breach of them may be called Injustice, and the observance of them Justice: but he questioneth, whether Injustice, taking away the feare of God, (for the same Foole hath said in his heart there is no God,) may not sometimes stand with that Reason, which dictateth to every man his own good; and particularly then, when it conduceth to such a benefit, as shall put a man in a condition, to neglect not onely the dispraise, and revilings, but also the power of other men.

Hobbes answers the fool by arguing that covenant-breaking cannot be reasonable once one considers the need for confederates in a state of war, and that he who declares it reasonable to break covenants cannot be received into any society but by the error of others. Suppose, however, that the fool’s reasoning is not confined to a single defection against a background of general compliance, but is adopted by a substantial proportion of agents in a population. Does the argument from the need for confederates still go through? If so, what exactly is the fool’s error in the high-defection version of the case? If not, does the passage contain the materials for a different reply, or does Hobbes ultimately need a sovereign already in place to make the fool’s policy irrational?
