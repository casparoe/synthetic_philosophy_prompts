I've been using this version of Morgan's canon for deciding when it's ok to say an animal is doing something cognitive: you can attribute a mental representation or a plan only if (i) the behavior is flexible in situations the animal wasn't trained on, (ii) the animal is tracking something not currently available to its senses, and (iii) no deflationary associative story—fixed action patterns, chained reflexes, simple reinforcement—handles the data. Now take a Portia jumping spider hunting another spider. It leaves its perch, moves out of view of the prey, takes a long detour that first takes it away from the prey, and ends up at a strike point; if the prey shifts mid-route, the spider sometimes adjusts its path. Run the spider through the three criteria honestly. Does it come out cognitive, and do the criteria genuinely settle that, or do they just push the fight into what counts as a "thin" associative story? Also say whether the case shows one of the criteria is redundant, or shows that Morgan's canon can't be the neutral tie-breaker it's supposed to be.
