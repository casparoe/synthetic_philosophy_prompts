I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I recently listened to two coworkers argue about food ethics over lunch. I couldn't tell who was getting the better of it, so I'd like a rigorous adjudication. Please act as a professional philosopher specializing in applied ethics and decision theory. Start by briefly mapping the structure of the dispute, then work through each move on its own before giving an overall verdict. Take time to reason carefully, and don't just declare a winner without separating the individual claims.

Here is the exchange, reconstructed as faithfully as I can:

(1) Dana said she'd stopped eating beef and switched to chicken for ethical reasons. Her stated reasoning was that beef has the largest carbon footprint of any common food, and climate change harms enormous numbers of humans and animals over time. Chicken, she said, is much lower in emissions per serving.

(2) Amit replied that by her own lights the switch was backwards. A single steer yields hundreds of meals, while a chicken yields only a couple; so each chicken meal requires killing a much larger fraction of a sentient animal. If she cares about animal suffering, he said, chicken is worse per meal than beef.

(3) Dana answered that Amit was counting only direct deaths and ignoring the harms of emissions. Beef's methane and land footprint, she argued, cause animal and human suffering over time through heat, drought, fire, and habitat loss. That diffuse suffering, she claimed, dwarfs the direct deaths from chicken.

(4) Amit said the wild-animal harms from climate are speculative and impossible to quantify, whereas the chicken deaths are certain. He added that if she was going to count indirect animal harm, crop harvesting kills mice and other small animals, so even a fully plant-based diet is not death-free; by some reckoning, pasture-raised beef could kill fewer animals than a vegan diet.

(5) Dana replied that crop deaths don't help his side: chickens eat crops, so the feed crops alone cause field deaths on top of the chicken deaths, and most beef is grain-finished anyway. She said the pasture comparison was cherry-picked because grass-finished beef is a niche product that cannot replace current consumption.

(6) Amit then proposed a compromise: if she isn't willing to go vegan, and if the climate harms are diffuse and uncertain while the direct deaths are certain, why not keep eating beef but much less of it? That would cut most of the emissions while avoiding the large number of direct animal deaths a chicken-based diet creates.

Please go through the exchange move by move. For each of the six moves, assign a score along a continuum from unsupportable to sound as stated, and briefly justify each score. Then state, separately, who is right about which specific points. Finally, identify the most important considerations, distinctions, or comparison metrics that both of them are missing.
