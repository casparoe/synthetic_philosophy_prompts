I am a software engineer with several years of experience in data integration and entity resolution, now taking my first metaphysics course. The course has been working through de re modality and possible worlds, and we have compared two positions. Transworld identity, as the course presents it, holds that an individual such as Ida can have a different property in another possible world only if Ida herself, one and the same individual, exists in that world and bears the property there; to say that Ida could have been an engineer is to say that the very same Ida has the property of being an engineer in some non-actual world. Counterpart theory, as the course presents it, rejects this: no individual exists in more than one world, and the sentence is made true instead by a counterpart of Ida in another world, where the counterpart relation is a matter of similarity in respects fixed by context, not of identity. I want you to translate these two positions into the vocabulary I actually use as a data engineer: records and entity resolution, record linkage, distributed databases and shards, primary keys and natural keys, source-of-truth tables, exact versus fuzzy matching, deduplication, and foreign keys versus join tables. Do this systematically rather than by loose analogy. For each position, say what it would claim in my vocabulary, what has to be forced to fit, and what has no counterpart at all. Then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which ones you had to distort, and which ones cannot be expressed in the entity-resolution vocabulary. Finally, tell me whether the residue suggests that the metaphysical disagreement is really a data-modeling disagreement, one global entity identifier assigned across shards versus separate records per shard linked by a similarity relation, or whether the untranslatable parts show that the philosophers are tracking distinctions my framework lacks. I care more about a faithful translation and what does not survive than about a flattering mapping.
