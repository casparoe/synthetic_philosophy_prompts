What would an adequate theory of how much risk a society should tolerate have to get right? I keep seeing people defend positions on seatbelt laws, mass vaccination, civilian surveillance, nuclear power, or novel chemicals by pointing at one case, and I want the full specification first—good enough to guide a public body that has to make actual permitting and safety/security decisions. Give me the requirements list: the cases such a theory must handle, the distinctions it cannot collapse (for example, voluntary versus imposed risk, statistical versus identified victims, diffuse versus catastrophic harm, first-party versus third-party exposure), and the facts about risk perception and risk-risk tradeoffs it must accommodate rather than dismiss—with the consideration behind each requirement. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are only the commitments of particular camps. Then tell me which single requirement does the most to eliminate the familiar positions, and whether any two of the non-negotiable requirements are in tension with each other. Keep the focus on the criteria, not on a scorecard of the theories.
