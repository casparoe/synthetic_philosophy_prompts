Last month I inherited a small landscape painting from my uncle, and the appraiser I took it to said the canvas and pigment are seventeenth-century but the signature — supposedly a minor Dutch master — was probably added a hundred years later to make it sell for more. She said it wasn’t exactly a forgery but also wasn’t exactly not, which has been bugging me ever since. I’m not asking for legal advice, I just want help getting the concept straight.

Could you give me a working definition of “forgery” that handles these cases? An art student makes an exact copy of a Vermeer and writes “after Vermeer” on the back; that seems clearly not a forgery to me. If somebody later scrapes that off and adds a fake signature and consigns it to auction, then it clearly is. A forger who paints a whole new picture in an old master’s style and passes it off as a rediscovered work: also clearly a forgery. A gift-shop reproduction of the Mona Lisa, clearly not. Those are easy. The hard ones are: a genuine seventeenth-century painting by nobody famous that a well-meaning but sloppy dealer sells as a Vermeer without altering a brushstroke; a portrait whose face was repainted so heavily by a restorer that most of what you see is modern, but the museum still labels it as the original artist; an artist making a near-exact replica of one of her own earlier works and signing it as a new version, while the buyer thinks it’s the earlier one; a bronze cast made from the artist’s original mold but decades after her death, authorized by the estate; and an AI-generated image in a living photographer’s exact style posted online without any attribution. I keep flipping on those.

Please define the term, run it through all the cases, and tell me where your definition surprises you or gives a verdict you had to talk yourself into. And if it’s not too much, say which of your verdicts is least secure and whether the definition explains why my uncle’s painting dropped in value the moment the appraiser said “not exactly not.” Don’t format your answer — no headings, no bullets or numbered lists, no bold or tables, just continuous prose like an email.
