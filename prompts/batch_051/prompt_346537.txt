Imagine a country at full employment with a developed banking system and an independent central bank. The government announces a one-time household debt jubilee: the central bank will create new money equal to about 10% of GDP and use it to buy existing mortgages at face value from banks, insurers, and pension funds. It buys the mortgages of the lowest-income households first and keeps going until the fresh money runs out. Mortgages bought this way are simply cancelled, so the former homeowners own there houses free and clear. Treat this as a mechanism, not a moral position: walk me through the balance-sheet effects, who actually ends up richer and poorer, what happens to liquidity and asset prices, and whether the finance minister is right that this creates real wealth for households rather than just relabeling claims on existing wealth. Then tell me how households, banks, and investors would game the rule if they expected a repeat, and which of those failure modes could be patched without turning the thing into ordinary tax-funded redistribution.
