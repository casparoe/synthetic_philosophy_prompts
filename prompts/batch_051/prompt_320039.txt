Please argue for the following position. A Nozickian truth-tracking account of knowledge—on which knowing that p requires, among other things, that one's belief that p be sensitive, in the sense that if p were false one would not believe that p by the same method—should treat the failure of epistemic closure under known entailment as a feature of the method-relativity of knowledge, not as a cost to be explained away. Your argument should do several things. First, reconstruct why the sensitivity condition yields the familiar result that one may know that one has hands without knowing that one is not a handless brain in a vat: the former belief is produced by a perceptual method that would not deliver that belief in the nearby worlds where one lacks hands, whereas the latter belief would still be held in the very worlds where the skeptical scenario obtains. Second, defend the substantive claim that the difference between the two cases is best located not in the contents of the two beliefs but in a change of belief-forming method. The move from the ordinary premise to the entailed anti-skeptical conclusion is an inferential act that introduces a method under which the conclusion is insensitive, and sensitivity is not inherited across such an inferential move. Third, draw out the consequence that valid inference should not generally be understood as transmitting knowledge from premises to conclusion; it should be understood as producing a new belief by way of a new method, whose tracking profile must be evaluated afresh. Finally, use this account to explain why the conjunction consisting of one's knowing that one has hands and one's failing to know that one is not a handless brain in a vat strikes us as incoherent even though the account declares it consistent: the discomfort arises from a shift in attention between two method-relative knowledge ascriptions, not from a semantic incompatibility. Do not merely restate the standard counterexample to closure; the aim is to defend the method-change diagnosis as a positive account of inference under a sensitivity condition.
