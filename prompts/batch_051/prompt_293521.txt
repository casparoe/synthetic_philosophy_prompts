Here are my verdicts on a bunch of cases about knowing things from different sources, and I can't find one principle behind them. (1) I glance at a tomato on the counter and it looks red. I'd say I know it's red right then, without first having evidence that my color vision is reliable. (2) I use a cheap no-name thermometer on my kid for the very first time, before checking it against anything else. I'd say I don't know the number is her actual temperature. (3) After a month of checking that thermometer against the pediatrician's, I'd say I know both that the reading is right and that the thermometer is reliable. (4) My friend wears a heart-rate watch she's never once checked against a manual pulse. After months of plausible-looking numbers she says she knows the watch is accurate because it's been consistent the whole time. I'd say she doesn't know that. (5) But here's the snag: I'd also say I know my own memory is reliable. Yet when I try to justify that, all I can point to is a lifetime of remembered cases where my memory turned out right—which is pretty much memory vouching for itself. Isn't that the same move as my friend's watch?

My tentative principle is: you can know p from a source without prior evidence that the source works, but a source can't by itself give you evidence (or justification, whatever) that it works. Does this principle fit all five cases? If not, what's the better one, or which of my verdicts should I give up? And apply your principle to a case I haven't listed, like whether I know an acquaintance's name is correct on a first introduction. Keep the answer to at most 500 words.
