In my scientific representation seminar we've been reading Otto Neurath's stuff on picture languages, and I've kind of unthinkingly started using his idea as a general test. Now the policy is: before I'll accept a claim from a reading — especially philosophy or social theory — I try to turn it into a little diagram or a sequence of pictograms. If I can't draw it in a way that would make sense to a non-specialist, I quietly treat the claim as not established, like its leaning on words doing hidden work. It does feel like it saves me from a lot of verbal fog, and it's won me a few arguments. But I'm worried this might just be a bias against claims that are hard to visualize, dressed up as a principle. Is this a defensible filter for conceptual ideas? What exactly can and can't a Neurath-style picture test do, and how would I tell the difference from the inside?
