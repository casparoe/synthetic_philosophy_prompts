I spent thirty years in biology, retired, and have only recenly started reading philosophy properly. The thing bugging me is the argument from analogy for other minds. As I reconstruct it: I know my own pain directly; pain makes me wince, pull back, and say certain things; so when other people wince, pull back, and say those things, I infer they are in pain too. That used to seem fine to me. But now we have large language models that will describe pain, recoil verbally, and answer questions about how they feel with elaborate stories of suffering, while (I'll grant for the sake of argument) there's nothing it's like to be one. Does the old argument survive that development? It looks like the LLM is a counterexample to the inference from pain-behavior to pain, but maybe the original argument was never supposed to run on behavior alone, and already leaned on the fact that other people share my biology. So: which premise does the LLM case actually threaten, and can the argument be repaired by making a shared-biology or shared-evolution premise explicit, or does that just beg the question? Give me the diagnosis step by step.
