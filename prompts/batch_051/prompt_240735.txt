I keep catching myself saying that one adult child owes their parents something and another doesn't, but I've never actually worked out what the word 'owe' is doing in those sentences — I just apply it by feel. So here's what I'd like: give me a working definition of what an adult child owes a parent, then run it through the cases below, and either revise the definition or tell me which of my gut calls I should give up.

(a) Leo's dad was a decent, steady father: worked overtime for years so Leo could graduate without loans, showed up to everything. Now the dad has had a stroke and needs several months of help with rehab that Leo could give without big sacrifice. Leo does it. Does he owe it, or is it just kind?

(b) Maya's father left when she was two and had no contact for thirty years, then emailed out of nowhere asking her to pay his rent. Does she owe him a cent?

(c) Jonah's mother raised him alone, worked two jobs, and paid for most of his college, but she was also controlling and belittling, and they've been mostly estranged for eight years. Now she has early dementia and no savings, and Jonah is trying to decide whether to help pay for her care or even take her in. What does he owe her?

(d) A close friend, not a relative, payed for a surgery Priya couldn't afford when she was 24. They drifted apart, and fifteen years later the friend asks for a large loan Priya could manage. Does Priya owe her anything beyond what a decent person would do for anyone?

(e) Sam's mother was verbally and physically abusive when he was a child. He now sends her money every month but refuses to visit or call. Is the money something he owes, or is it extra?

(f) A stranger collapses in front of you and you can call an ambulance at no real cost to yourself. Do you owe them that call?

I'm half-convinced the parent cases work more like (d) than like a debt: the duties come from the relationship you actually have now, not from a ledger of past sacrifices. But then I dont know what to say about (e) and (f), and 'owe' starts to look like two different words.

Propose the definition, run it through all six, and flag which of your verdicts is least secure. If you think 'owe' is really two different notions here, define both and tell me which cases belong to each.
