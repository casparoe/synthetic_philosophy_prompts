I'm teaching a grad seminar on historiography this term, and I keep going back and forth on something that's making me worried the whole "avoid presentism" rule is a bit of a sham. If you accept the fairly standard line that all inquiry is situated — that we can't step outside our own historical moment to get a god's-eye standpoint — then some kind of presentism looks unavoidable, not like a mistake to be corrected. But if that's right, why do we spend so much time marking students down for "presentist" readings of, say, Locke or Aquinas? It's starting to seem like the rule is just training them to hide judgments they're inevitably making anyway, which makes the rule either incoherant or a little dishonest.
