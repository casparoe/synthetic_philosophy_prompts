I'm running a small reading group on Frankfurt's book about bullshit, and last week we got into an argument thats been rattling around my head since. A friend of mine who works in ML kept saying that a large language model can't be a liar because it doesn't have beliefs or intentions, and I kept saying that's exactly why Frankfurt is the right frame: a liar cares about the truth enough to aim away from it, while the bullshitter just doesn't care one way or the other. He said calling a model a bullshitter was absurd because there's no person behind the model, and we sort of stalled there.

What I'd like, partly for the group and partly to needle him, is a deadpan company blog post of about 600 to 800 words, in the voice of an AI startup announcing that their latest model has finally achieved what they call 'Frankfurt-grade bullshit.' The joke should be that the company advertises the model's indifference to truth as a feature: it doesn't lie to you (lying would require it to know the truth and deliberately miss it), it doesn't err (error would mean there was a fact it failed to hit), it just produces whatever is useful to say at the moment. It should stay internally consistent — the company can't halfway through start bragging that the model is accurate, because that would collapse the exact distinction they're exploiting. And it shouldn't wink at the reader; by the end someone who knows the book should feel the company has half-understood the concept and is using it to launder a real epistemic problem, while someone who doesn't know the book should still get the joke.

After the piece, give me a short footnote-ish paragraph on where the satire leans on a contested reading — in particular wether an LLM can count as a bullshitter at all if it has no intentions or aims, and which distinction about agency would most change that verdict. Please keep the whole thing continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, the way you'd write me an email.
