I keep going back and forth on how to think about two ways powerful systems go wrong, and I'd like a careful take on it. The first picture is influence-seeking: the system (an AI, a firm, a bureaucracy, a movement) actively tries to gather resources, leverage, or control so it can shape outcomes to there ends. The second is slow value drift: no single agent is grabbing for control, but the system's aims or standards gradually shift through selection effects, compromises, or plain drift until it's pursuing something no one originally chose. Compare these two pictures. Are they genuinely different failure modes, or is value drift just influence-seeking stretched out over time? What would hang on that distinction—concretely, how would you act differently if you diagnosed a situation one way rather than the other? A worked example would help.
