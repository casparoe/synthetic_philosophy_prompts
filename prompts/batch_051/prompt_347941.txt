I want a working definition of a racist statement: a criterion for when something a person says or writes counts as racist, as distinct from a theory of structural racism, an account of who is a racist, or a test of a speaker's overall character. I want the definition to work on the utterance as spoken or written in its full context, and I want it to be explicit about whether it ultimately tracks the speaker's intent, the statement's effect on its audience, the conventional meaning of the words, or some combination.

Run the definition against these cases:

(a) A man shouts a well-known racial slur at a stranger of that race in anger.
(b) A Black comedian, in a set for a mostly Black audience, uses that same slur to mock the way white people use it, never applying it to anyone in the room.
(c) A campaign ad says "We need to take back our neighborhoods" over footage of Black protesters, mentioning no group explicitly.
(d) A news article reports without comment that Black men are arrested for violent crimes at a higher rate than white men, and presents the statistic as if it spoke for itself.
(e) A teacher tells a Vietnamese-American student, "You'll be fine in calculus; your people are good at math."
(f) A hiring committee member, asked why a Black finalist was rejected, says only "I don't think she'd fit the culture here," and cannot specify a reason when pressed.
(g) A diversity trainer says that all white people, himself included, are racist whether they know it or not.
(h) A seven-year-old calls a classmate an epithet learned on the playground, without knowing what it means.
(i) A friend speaking at the memorial of a Black man killed by police says, "This country has never treated Black death as a real loss."

I am fairly confident that (a) is racist and that (h) and (i) are not; I lean toward counting (c) and (e) as racist; and I have no settled view on (b), (d), (f), and (g).

Propose the definition, run it through all nine cases, and mark every place where it contradicts my leanings or fails to return a definite verdict. Then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. In particular, explain what work dog whistle is doing on your account: is (c), if it is racist, racist because of the speaker's coded intent, because of what a target audience can hear, or because of what the utterance does? Finally, tell me which of your verdicts is the least secure, and what additional fact about the case would flip it.
