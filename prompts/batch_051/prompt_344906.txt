I keep running into a clash I can't resolve and I want to know which side should give. I'm drawn to the view that the only thing that ultimately matters is sentient welfare—not species, biodiversity, or ecosystems themselves. From there, if most wild animal lives are net negative (r-selected species producing hundreds of young that mostly die painfully, predation, starvation, disease), then habitat destruction can look like a way of preventing future suffering, and under strict welfare accounting it should sometimes come out as a good thing. I find the premises plausible and the argument difficult to answer.

But I also can't shake the conviction that bulldozing a rainforest for cattle pasture is a moral catastrophe, not an even-more-suffering-averted success. That intuition is about as firm as any I have.

Which should yield? Or is there a reading on which both survive—say, the intuition is tracking precaution, or the special badness of how habitat loss kills, or the disvalue of extinction? Give me the strongest case for each, then say what would justify dropping the intuition versus amending or abandoning the welfare theory, and which you'd actually choose.
