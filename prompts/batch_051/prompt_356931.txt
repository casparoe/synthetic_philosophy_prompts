There's something I see all the time at school and online but I've got no word for. Depending on the group, the same situation — say, someone getting insulted in front of everyone — seems to have three different 'correct' ways of being handled. In some groups, you have to fire back straight away or people start treating you like a doormat, and it only settles once you've shown you won't be messed with. In other groups it's basically the reverse: the person who stays calm and brushes it off looks strongest, and if it keeps going, reporting it to a teacher is seen as sensible rather than grassing. Then there's a third style where people seem to compete over who's been hurt the most, and you get respect not by shrugging it off but by getting everyone to take your side and pile onto the person who did it. I can see these are three different ways a group handles being wronged, and people in each group tend to think the other two are obviously broken. Does this split have a name in psychology or philosophy, or am I just making patterns out of nothing? I'm not only after a label — I'd want to know what actually distinguishes the three, where the boundary between them is, and whether there's a decent argument that one of them is morally better to live in than the others.
