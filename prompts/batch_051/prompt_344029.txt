The driver cannot distinguish the two exits and has no memory, counter, or clock available during the trip, so when he sees an exit his entire observation is just "there is an exit here"; the same observation occurs at both decision points. A precommitted plan can be any mixture over "exit" and "continue," applied at whatever exit is encountered. The usual planning-stage calculation says the optimal such plan exits with probability 1/3 and continues with probability 2/3, for an expected payoff of 4/3. The decision-theoretic position I want translated is the argument this puzzle is taken to support: that under imperfect recall, the rational plan made before the trip and the rational choice made by the agent who is actually at an exit can come apart, because the agent at the exit must also answer a self-locating question—which exit am I at?—whose answer depends, awkwardly, on the very plan he is following. The position is often put as: imperfect recall breaks the usual equivalence between planning and acting, or forces a choice between dynamic consistency and something like expected-utility maximization at the decision point.
