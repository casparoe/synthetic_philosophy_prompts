Build me a thought experiment on whether quantum-level randomness can do any real work in a libertarian account of free will. Here's the remit. Take two versions of one ordinary, everyday decision: same agent, same action, same stated reasons, same history; the only difference is where the quantum event lands. Version A: the indeterminism sits in which considerations come to mind during deliberation, and once she's weighed them the choice runs deterministically. Version B: the same event is moved to the final moment and directly determines which option she picks. The expected upshot is that on a libertarian reading version A preserves responsibility while version B falls to the luck objection. Constraints: no magic coins, no external randomiser the agent consults, no hidden manipulators, and no life-or-death stakes — I want something like choosing a route home, not deciding whether to push someone off a footbridge. After the scenario, walk through the two verdicts and say which single feature of the case is doing the work. Then do the honesty bit: what would change your mind about whether the case succeeds, where you're reasoning is most likely to fail, and how a compatibilist (or just a sceptical reader) would attack version A.
