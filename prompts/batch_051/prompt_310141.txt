I'm not an academic, I just read essays and listen to debates, so apologies if this is something with an obvious name. For a while now I've noticed the same thing happen in arguments about whether someone really desreves blame for what he did. Not the practical question of what we should do with him, but whether he's truly at fault.

Someone says: he did it on purpose, so he owns it. The other person says: sure, but look at his temper, he's been that way his whole life. First person: but he could have worked on his temper. Second: and where would the wanting-to-work-on-it have come from? His wiring, his upbringing, his luck. First: at some point you have to own the kind of person you are. Second: and how did he get to be someone who would own it, rather than someone who wouldn't? And it just keeps going back like that.

I've seen the same shape in small everyday cases. My brother-in-law won't say a kid is lazy; he says the kid didn't choose to find effort aversive, and didn't choose to be the sort of person who wouldn't push through it either. It sounds like excuse-making, but here's the thing: it works just as well for people with perfectly good childhoods, so it isn't really about bad parenting or anything like that. Any feature of the person you point to as the thing that finally makes him responsible turns out to have causes behind it, and those causes have causes, and the "real him" keeps reatreating out of reach.

I can't tell if this is a known argument with a name, or just a move people make, or an actual airtight case that nobody can genuinely be at fault. If it's known, how is it different from "his childhood made him do it" or from simply refusing to let the buck stop anywhere? I don't just want the label. I want to know where the edges are: is there an accepted place where the chain gets to stop, and if there's really no floor, then why would anyone still talk about people deserving blame at all?
