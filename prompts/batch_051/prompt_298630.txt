so my uncle drove home after a couple drinks last month and hit a pedestrian. the guy survived but will have a limp. a family friend did basically the same thing a year ago, same BAC-ish, same amount of sleep, and got pulled over with no crash. my family now talks about my uncle like he's morally way worse, even though the difference was a pedestrian crossing a second later. i've started pushing back with this line from Kant's Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals, basically that "the basis of obligation must not be sought in the nature of man, or in the circumstances in the world in which he is placed, but a priori simply in the conception of pure reason." my rule has become: when judging someone's action morally, i ignore all the stuff that happened after the choice was made, because it's not up to them and kant says the basis can't be in circumstances. i apply it to drunk driving, to decisions at work, even to how i think about my ex. is this a defensible policy, or am i overreading kant as a blanket ban on outcome sensitivity? on what assumptions about control and luck would my no-outcomes rule be right, and where would it break?
