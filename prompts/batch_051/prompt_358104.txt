I help run a local rationality dojo—basically a LessWrong meetup in a rented room with a whiteboard—and we keep hitting the same wall. Everyone can recite the standard moves: notice confusion, steelman the other side, don't let the belief become identity. And they can do them fine in tidy exercises. The moment we run a session where people argue about something they actually care about, it all evaporates. So I want to build one session around the claim that rationality genuinely trains like a martial art: katas, spaced practice, escalating resistance (cognitive load, time pressure, social stakes), and explicit transfer drills that take the skill out of the dojo.

Design a runnable 90-minute activity for around 16 adults, mixed experience, all at least familiar with basic rationalist vocabulary. The activity should make participants feel the dojo-to-real-life transfer gap in their own behavior, not just have me explain it afterwards. Pick one specific micro-skill as the kata—my default would be "notice the exact moment you flip from evaluating a claim to defending a position you already hold," but your welcome to swap in a better one if you have a reason. Give me rules, exact timings and prompt wording, materials, a scoring/incentive scheme that doesn't need real money, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the martial-arts picture: the kata, the controlled drill, the high-load drill, and the transfer attempt. Include a near-transfer task that visibly resembles the drill and a far-transfer task that doesn't, so the failure mode is legible when it happens. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it—especially people pattern-matching to the exercise and performing the skill only because they know it's being watched—and how to handle that. Also be honest about which part of the dojo-to-real-life gap the activity can't reproduce and I'll have to just name during the debrief.

Start straight into the activity, no preamble and no restating of the question. A one-line "what they should leave having noticed in themselves" as the first line is fine, then the rules.
