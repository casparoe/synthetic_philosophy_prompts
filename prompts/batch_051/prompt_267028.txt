Longtime LessWrong/ACX reader here, so I've spent a while on embedded agency, and I want to see how it cashes out in a very specific mechanism rather than in the abstract.

Suppose a forum installs a moderation bot that predicts, for every new post, the probability that the post will be reported by at least three distinct users within a week. If the predicted probability is above 0.7, the bot deletes the post immediately; if it's below 0.7, the post stays up. Each night the bot is retrained on all posts whose outcomes are now resolved. Posts that stayed up are labelled 1 if the three reports actually happened and 0 otherwise. Posts the bot deleted are handled with a lazy shortcut: they are all labelled 1, on the theory that the bot only deletes posts it already thought were very likely to be reported. The bot also gets its own past predictions and deletion decisions as features — so it is not modelling a fixed environment; the thing being predicted is partly caused by the thing doing the predicting.

Walk me through how this system behaves over a year. Does the bot's callibration converge, and to what — or does it oscillate, or drift while looking perfectly calibrated? What happens to the distribution of posts that survive, and what does the label 'reportable' drift toward meaning once the bot has been deleting the top of the distribution every day? Where, if anywhere, does the bot's behaviour flip from mere prediction into a self-fulfilling or self-defeating loop? Is there an honest fixed point, and is it stable?

Then the fix question. If the team says the bot should predict the counterfactual — would this post be reported if I left it up — can it learn that from the data described, or is the training signal structurally missing for exactly the posts where the estimate matters most? What extra data, randomisation, or redesign would you add, and what new failure modes does that introduce? And does the whole scheme come apart anyway once users know reports are a scarce input to a bot with a hard threshold — e.g. people start reporting as a super-downvote or as a performative show of being on the right side?

Please write your answer in British English.
