I'm a TA for an undergrad bioethics course, and I have to grade a midterm short-answer on Benatar's procreation asymmetry. The professor said this is one of those questions student's tend to get almost right, so I want to be careful about the difference between a small slip and a real misunderstanding. The exam question was:

"State Benatar's procreation asymmetry as precisely as you can. Lay out the four relevant cases (an existing person with pain or with pleasure; a person who never exists and the absence of pain or of pleasure) and label each good, bad, or not bad. Then say what Benatar thinks follows from the asymmetry about whether procreation is wrong."

A student wrote:

"The asymmetry says that the presence of pain is bad and the presence of pleasure is good. When a person never exists, the absence of pain is good, because they are spared that pain; the absence of pleasure is not bad, because there is no one there to be deprived of it. So if you compare existing and never existing, existing has one bad (pain) and one good (pleasure), while never existing has one good (absence of pain) and one neutral (absence of pleasure). Since never existing has an advantage and no matching disadvantage, nonexistence comes out better overall. Benatar takes this to show that every life is not worth living, and that's why he thinks procreation is wrong."

What grade would you give this on a 10-point scale, and what point-by-point comments would justify that grade? I'm less interested in a general impression and more in the specific comments I could write in the margins, especially where the student is right and where the reasoning slips.
