I'm the only philosopher on my funding agency's astronomy panel, and we're deadlocked on a proposal for a next-generation survey that would measure the dark energy equation of state to much higher precision. The agency's rules require every project to show a plausible pathway to public benefit; half the panel says cosmology can't honestly show one — knowing whether the universe's expansion is accelerating a little faster or slower then we thought doesn't feed anyone or fix anything. The other half says that's a misreading of the rule, since applied strictly it would have killed most pure mathematics and much of particle physics, and that understanding the universe is itself a benefit. The chair has asked me to write the decision memo. Please draft it: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with reasons the agency can act on, and state the strongest version of the argument for whichever side loses, worded so that side's advocates would call it fair. Pay special attention to the difference between 'no application yet' and 'no application even in principle' — the panel keeps running those together, and any decision here will set a precedent for how we read the benefit rule. Don't lean on invented spin-off stories.
