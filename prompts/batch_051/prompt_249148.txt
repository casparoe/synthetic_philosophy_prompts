I sit on the design review board for a meditation/wellbeing app, and I'm trying to settle a new feature by looking at our own track record, which is a bit of a mess. Over the last couple years we approved a pre-checked free trial that rolls into an annual subscription, but only after the auto-renewal was spelled out on the same screen in normal-size text and we added a reminder email three days before the charge. We rejected a cancellation flow that made people phone a retention line and sit on hold, because the app had never mentioned that requirement at signup. We approved a streak counter that just shows consecutive days and doesn't send 'you're about to lose it' notifications, but we rejected a cancellation screen that said the user would lose their 30-day streak forever, with a big 'keep my streak' button and a tiny grey 'continue cancelling' text link. We rejected a countdown timer that said 'only 2 spots left' for a digital course with no cap, and we approved a progress bar tied to the user's own weekly goal.

Now the team want to add a pause flow. When a user taps 'cancel subscription,' the first screen would show a large 'pause for 30 days' button, a smaller but still clearly visible 'cancel subscription' button below it, and a line explaining that pausing automatically resumes billing after 30 days, with a reminder email three days before it resumes. Nothing is false or hidden, and cancel is one extra tap away. Half the board reads our precedents as 'nothing false or hidden, and leaving has to stay reasonably easy'; the other half reads them as 'don't lean on status quo bias or loss aversion to keep someone who's already trying to leave.' I'm trying to figure out what we should decide if we want to be consistent with ourselves. Which precedent is the closest analog? And if the precedents genuinely underdetermine it, what principle would we be adopting with each choice?
