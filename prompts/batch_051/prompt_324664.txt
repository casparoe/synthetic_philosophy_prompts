Notes: "An asymmetry between the doomsday argument and the presumptuous philosopher"

SSA and SIA are often introduced as carrying parallel embarrassments: the SSA side gets the doomsday argument, and the SIA side gets the presumptuous philosopher. I want to suggest that this symmetry is misleading, because the two unwanted conclusions fail in different ways.

The doomsday argument depends on at least two independent inputs: an empirical claim about where my birth rank sits in the sequence of humans so far, and a reference-class choice that tells me to regard myself as uniformly sampled from all humans rather than from observers with exactly my evidence, or from my civilization, or from all sentient beings. Once the reference class is allowed to shift, the doomsday conclusion can be postponed or reversed without changing the underlying anthropic principle. That is why the argument can feel slippery even to people who accept SSA.

The presumptuous philosopher, by contrast, does not depend on my rank in any observed sequence. It depends only on the total number of observers a cosmological theory entails, so there is no reference-class narrowing that dissolves it. SIA, as standardly stated, weighs hypotheses by the number of observers simpliciter, not by the number inside some independently chosen class. The presumptuous conclusion is therefore structurally closer to a consistency requirement of the principle, whereas the doomsday conclusion is an artifact of a revisable auxiliary choice.

If this is right, then the usual table-thumping—SSA has doomsday, SIA has presumptuousness, so pick your poison—misdescribes the choice. SSA is not really one assumption but a schema with a free parameter: the reference class. SIA is closer to a single, parameter-free updating rule. The real trade-off is about where free parameters live in one's epistemology, and about whether one prefers flexibility purchased at the price of an arbitrary-looking auxiliary choice, or uniformity purchased at the price of conclusions that cannot be softened by redescription.
