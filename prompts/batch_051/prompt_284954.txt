I want to stress-test a funding rule before we implement it. Our foundation takes a broadly effective-altruist approach and intends to allocate a fixed annual budget across four program areas: global health delivery, pandemic preparedness, long-term AI risk, and policy advocacy. Under the proposed rule, every proposal must state a point estimate and a 90% confidence interval for total lives saved, derived from the proposal's own causal model; a scoring script then ranks proposals by the lower bound of the interval divided by dollars requested, and any proposal that cannot produce these quantities is returned without review. Program officers receive a bonus proportional to the point estimate in each grant they recommend, and the bonus is clawed back only if a later audit establishes that the officer deliberately inflated a number. I am not asking whether the foundation's mission is right. Analyze how this rule would actually behave: where it gets gamed first, how each of the four program areas fares over a five-year budget cycle, whether the audit clause can discipline the estimates, and what specific changes would fix the failure modes you identify without giving up the goal of allocating money by expected impact.
