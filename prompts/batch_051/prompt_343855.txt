I'm leading a ninety-minute session for a secular philosophy reading group on sola scriptura and the regress of interpretation. The group is sharp but their Reformation knowledge stops around "Luther nailed some theses." I want the session to be about one conceptual knot: if Scripture is the only authority, someone still has to say what it means, and whatever settles disputes about that starts to function as a second authority. Plan how I should teach this, not the lesson itself. What idea has to be in place before the regress even bites, which pieces depend on which, and what order should I introduce them in? Pick one example to carry the whole discussion and say what it has to do, and what it must not do, or it'll accidentally smuggle in an authority. Which misconceptions will this crowd show up with ("the text has a plain meaning," "Protestants have no authority but Catholics do," "if everyone read more carefully the problem disappears") and where in the sequence should I defuse each? What should I openly leave out, and what's one question I can ask at the end to tell whether they got the regress rather than just the history?
