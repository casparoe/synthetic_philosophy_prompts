I've been teaching a seminar on writing systems, and one line I keep pushing is that a script is a kind of theory of the language it writes: an alphabet implicitly posits phonemes, a syllabary implicitly posits syllables, and every decision about which contrasts get written and which get ignored is a theoretical decision. But I've started to suspect that this sounds nice in the abstract and might fall apart on a real case, so I want to think through the Cherokee syllabary with it. Here's the case as I know it. Sequoyah, a Cherokee silversmith who was not literate in any script before he made his own, finished the syllabary in the late 1810s or early 1820s after apparently experimenting with a logographic system and then abandoning it. The final system has around eighty-five characters (originally eighty-six), mostly one per syllable, typically a consonant-vowel combination with a few for vowels alone. It caught on almost immediately: by 1825 the majority of Cherokee could read and write it, by some estimates quicker than the surrounding white population picked up English literacy. And yet the script doesnt mark vowel length or tone, both of which are contrastive in Cherokee, and it doesn't give a distinct character to every phonetically distinct syllable. So the "theory" embodied in the script is, on the face of it, incomplete or even wrong about some things.

What I'd like from you is an actual case analysis, not a history report. Map the syllabary onto the "writing system as theory of language" framework as precisely as you can: what exactly is the data being analyzed, what are the posits, what would the theory predict that someone trying to record Cherokee should care about, and what did Sequoyah's system in fact care about? Then run it in the other direction: does the case confirm the framework, or does it show that talk of "theories" is too intellectualist for what a script does, and that the real constraints are syllable structure, ease of learning, social circumstances of adoption, or something else the framework stays silent about? I especially want to know where the theory metaphor earns its keep here and where it just gets in the way.
