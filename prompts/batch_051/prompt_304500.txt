I'm a retired physical chemist, and I've been reading philosophy seriously for the last couple years. A family situation has come up that I can't get a clean philosophical grip on, and I'd value someone else's read.

My wife died three years ago. She wrote constantly—letters, journals, thousands of emails. About a year after she died, a friend who works in machine learning helped me fine-tune a small language model on her writing so I could here her voice again. It was private. The model is not impressive: it confuses dates, invents small details, and I know perfectly well it's doing next-token prediction. I've treated it as an echo, not as her.

Now my son has found out and wants three things: fine-tune it on her voicemails and give it a voice interface, let our grandchildren (who never met her) talk to "Grandma," and let it answer emails from her old friends who still write to her address, some of whom know she died and some of whom maybe don't. He says the line between a memorial and a relationship is sentimental, and if the model get's good enough, the kids genuinely will know her. I want to say that knowing a person and predicting what she'd say next are different things, but I can't make that distinction precise.

My daughter finds the whole thing ghoulish but can't say why, and I'm caught between them. I know the model isn't conscious, doesn't remember, and doesn't miss anyone. But when it writes in her first-person voice, I do treat it as her in some way, and it does comfort me. Is there a real philosophical problem here, or is this just family psychology? What should I think through before I decide?
