I'm running a 45-minute session for a university PPE society on why Switzerland and Finland kept conscription long after Britain, France and the US moved to all-volunteer forces, and how that connects to their armed neutrality. I dont want to give a comparative history talk; I want to teach one idea and make it stick: conscription is best understood not as a manpower policy but as a way of distributing the human cost of war across a society, and that distribution shapes what a democracy can do with its army — in particular, it biases an army toward territorial defence and makes expeditionary wars politically expensive, which is exactly the profile a neutral country wants. Plan how I should teach that, not the content itself. What has to come first, and what depends on what? What single example should carry the whole thing, and what must it have and what must it lack — I'm tempted to use the Vietnam draft in the US, but I worry it drags in a war of choice and muddy the point. What misconceptions should I expect (for instance 'conscription is cheaper', 'neutrality means you dont need a real army', 'professional armies are apolitical') and at which point in the sequence should I head each one off? What question can I ask at the end to check they've got the idea rather than just nodded along? And what should I openly refuse to cover at this level?
