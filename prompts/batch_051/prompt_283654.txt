I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophical training, and I want a definition I can actually apply when reading political threads, not a survey of the literature. The phrase I keep seeing is "arguing in good faith," and the more I look at it, the less sure I am what people mean by it.

Give me a working definition of arguing in good faith — precise enough to be applied to each case below without gerrymandering. For each case, say plainly whether your definition counts the behaviour as good faith, bad faith, or neither; then tell me where the definition gives a surprising answer, where it gives no answer at all, and how you would revise it or defend it.

The cases:

1. A debater who states the strongest version of an opponent's position, concedes points when the evidence goes against him, and occasionally changes his mind on the spot. (I take this to be clearly good faith.)
2. A pundit who privately knows a claim about an opponent is false but repeats it because it polls well. (I take this to be clearly bad faith.)
3. A commenter who sincerely believes an exaggerated statistic and repeats it without ever checking the source, even though checking would take two minutes.
4. Someone running a motte-and-bailey argument: putting forward a controversial claim and retreating to a mild, easily defended claim when challenged.
5. A person who refuses to engage with an opponent's reasoning because, she says, the opponent never argues in good faith, and she says so openly.
6. A negotiator with a hidden commercial interest whose stated reasons are all true and relevant.
7. A cherry-picker who cites only the evidence on one side while being roughly aware that there is evidence on the other side.
8. An adversarial devil's advocate who defends a view he does not hold in order to test the reasoning of other people in a discussion group.

Cases 3–8 are the hard ones, and I do not have settled views about several of them. I suspect the phrase does at least two jobs — one about sincerity and one about fair play — and that several of these cases force the two apart. If you agree, give both definitions and run the cases through each. Please write the answer in American English.
