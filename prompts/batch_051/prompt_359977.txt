You are convening an adversarial collaboration between two theologians who have disputed the atonement for years. The first holds a penal substitution view: on the cross, Christ bore the punishment that divine justice required for human sin, so that God may justly forgive the repentant. The second holds a moral influence view: the cross is above all the definitive revelation of divine love, whose work is to transform the sinner's disposition and to set them free from the power of sin. Draft the joint statement that a well-conducted adversarial collaboration between them would produce.

The document should contain the following. First, a numbered list of affirmations about the life, death and resurrection of Jesus, the human condition and the character of God that both parties can sign without reservation; avoid formulations that are true only because they are vague, and flag any place where a shared sentence conceals a difference in what each would mean by it. Second, precise statements of the remaining disagreements, each rendered twice, once in wording each party accepts as the strongest fair statement of its own position, with no pejorative vocabulary. Organise these under three headings: what happened on the cross; what the cross accomplishes; and what an adequate account of the atonement must explain. Before that, state as carefully as you can what each party means by punishment, guilt and forgiveness, and say whether the two parties are using the same concepts or are talking past one another. Third, identify the cruxes: the two or three sub-questions the settling of which would lead at least one party to revise its position. Likely candidates include whether guilt or liability can be transferred between persons at all, and, if it cannot, whether that impossibility is conceptual or a substantive moral claim; whether justice can be satisfied by the suffering of an innocent substitute; whether the primary effect of the cross is a change in God's relation to the sinner, a change in the sinner's relation to God, or both; and whether moral transformation is the purpose of the atonement or a consequence of it. Say which of these you judge to be genuine cruxes, which are merely verbal, and which are red herrings. Fourth, for each genuine crux, specify a concrete argument, case or thought experiment that both parties would agree bears decisively on it, and state in advance what each side would take that to show. Also state, for each party, the strongest consideration against its own view, in wording that party regards as fair, and say whether both parties can agree on what would count as real evidence against each position rather than as mere rhetorical advantage. End with a paragraph that both parties could sign, summarising only what survived the collaboration.
