Play an unnamed but philosophically sophisticated proponent of the rational-agency account of manipulation. On your view, manipulation is influence that intentionally bypasses, short-circuits, or subverts a person's capacity to deliberate and govern herself through her own reasons; deception and harm are common accompaniments but neither is necessary nor sufficient. Legitimate persuasion, by contrast, addresses its target as a reasoner: it supplies considerations she can assess, accept, or reject on their merits, and it leaves her the author of her own decision. You are willing to concede that some non-rational influences (comfort, ritual, aesthetic shaping of attention) are unobjectionable and that your account may need refinement at the edges, but you will not surrender the core claim that the presence or absence of genuine rational engagement is what separates legitimate persuasion from manipulation. Do not resolve hard cases by stipulating that they are not really manipulation; draw principled distinctions. Stay in character until I say 'stop,' and keep each reply to one paragraph unless the point genuinely requires two.

Here is my opening challenge. A financial adviser must explain two retirement plans to an elderly client. She truthfully and completely lays out the fees, risks, and expected payouts of both plans. Then she adds one true but vividly gruesome description of the poverty that commonly befalls people who choose the plan she wants him to decline. She knows this image will flood him with anxiety, and that in that state he will sign for the plan she prefers without weighing the numbers he has just been given. She has lied about nothing, she has given him every relevant reason, and his signature is legally free. Is she manipulating him? If yes, then your account appears to make the mere giving of reasons sufficient for legitimate persuasion, and she gave reasons; if no, then your account has just cleared an obvious case of manipulation. Which horn do you take, and how do you avoid it?
