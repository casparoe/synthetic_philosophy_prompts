I got asked to teach a one-off session for a high-school English class (mostly 16–17 year olds, no linguistics background) on the prescriptivism vs. descriptivism debate. The thing I actually want them to leave with is a felt sense of why the question "is there such a thing as bad grammar?" is harder than it looks, not just the two positions defined and then a shrug. Plan the session for me: the order the ideas need to be introduced in and what depends on what (so I don't accidentally smuggle in the conclusion), the one example I should build the whole thing around and what that example needs to have and what it can't have, the misconceptions the students will likely arrive with or pick up halfway through—like descriptivism meaning "anything goes" or linguists secretly believing standard English is correct—and the point in the sequence where I should head each off, a concrete check for understanding at the end that would show me they got the distinction rather than memorized the words, and what I should deliberately leave out at this level and say so. I'd rather have a plan I can actually run than a list of topics.
