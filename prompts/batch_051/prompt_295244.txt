I am a novelist and a member of a writers' union, the Guild, which is currently engaged in a lawful strike against several publishing houses. The Guild's strike rules include the following provision:

"During a strike, no member shall perform work for an employer against which the Guild has declared a strike, where that work is of a kind ordinarily performed by Guild members under this Agreement."

An AI company that the Guild has recently added to the struck list, because it supplies machine-generated novels to the struck houses, has offered me a one-time fee to license the full text of my already-published novels as training data. The agreement commissions no new writing, editing, or revision from me; I would only be licensing existing text. But the corpus will be used to train a model that produces novels for the struck houses.

Does the quoted provision apply to this arrangement? On the words, on its evident purpose, and on what its drafters, writing before such systems existed, would most plausibly have said if asked, does licensing my backlist count as "work of a kind ordinarily performed by Guild members under this Agreement"? If the words and the purpose come apart, which should govern, and what does that do to a union's ability to bind members to obligations they could not have anticipated? Please keep your response under 900 words.
