I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, and in our department's reading group one of the more hard-nosed members keeps deflecting the paradox of phenomenal judgment with the same one-liner: "Consciousness is the steam whistle of the brain, not the engine." The context is usually this. Someone presses him on how epiphenomenalism about qualia can account for the fact that we reliably form and report judgments like "I now feel a sharp pain"; if the felt quality itself is causally inert, then the judgment and the report are produced entirely by the underlying neural machinery, and the pain itself seems to contribute nothing even to our introspective testimony about it. He waves this off with the steam whistle: the whistle sounds only because the engine has already done its work, and our talk of consciousness is just that sound.

I want to bring him a careful menu of what that line could actually be claiming, because I suspect he is exploiting its ambiguity. The modest readings might include: qualitative properties are effects but never causes; the feeling is a late accompaniment to decisions already made elsewhere in the brain; or the felt character of a state never explains any behavioral difference, including report behavior. The stronger readings might include: introspective talk is a useful fiction, or at best a systematically false commentary on the brain's real workings; or the whole of conscious life is a byproduct with no function whatsoever. I'd like you to lay out the distinct precisifications, from the trivial through the defensible to the false, and say for each whether it would actually support dismissing the paradox as he uses it. The deliverable should be a menu with verdicts, not one reading argued at length. Please also be explicit about your confidence in each verdict and about where the philosophical literature is genuinely unsettled. And if the paradox as I've set it up is being stated unfairly, or if no reading of the line does the work my colleague needs, I'd rather be told that directly than have the menu massaged into agreement with him.
