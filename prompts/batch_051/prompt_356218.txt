A city is considering replacing its ordinary one-person-one-vote council elections with an epistocratic weighted franchise — a system in which electoral weight is supposed to track demonstrated political competence rather than equal citizenship alone. Under the proposal, every registered voter keeps one base vote. In the two years before each election, a voter may voluntarily enter a forecasting scheme: she submits a point prediction for up to ten indicators chosen from a public menu of roughly forty locally measured outcomes — median rent, violent crime, school test scores, transit ridership, building permits issued, and so on. Each prediction is locked at submission and scored by an independent statistical office against a fixed scoring date, with a tolerance band announced in advance for each indicator. A voter earns 0.3 additional votes for each prediction that falls within its band, up to a maximum of three extra votes, so a fully accredited voter carries at most four votes into the election. Forecasts and scores are published under the voter's name, and the menu of indicators is revised each cycle by the statistical office through a public rulemaking process.

Treat this as a mechanism, not as a moral position in the abstract. Walk through how it would actually behave. Who self-selects into the scheme, and with what information and resources? How would candidates, parties, incumbent officials, public employees, and businesses with some control over the measured indicators respond once indicators carry electoral weight? How would the statistical office and its choice of menu become a new object of political capture, and what does the public naming of forecasts and scores do to voters' behavior? After several cycles, would the scheme actually concentrate weight in the better-informed, or would it produce a self-perpetuating forecasting caste that is good at hitting target numbers but no better at judging policy? Identify the first-order failure modes and the second-order effects, and say which of them could be fixed by design changes — a longer scoring horizon, anonymized forecasts, or having the menu chosen by a randomly selected citizens' panel — without the scheme collapsing back into plain universal suffrage or turning into rule by forecasters.
