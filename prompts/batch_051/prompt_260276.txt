In The Philosophy of Grammar, Otto Jespersen insists that the distinction between a noun and a verb is not a distinction in the extra-linguistic thing or event being spoken about. The same action can be presented as a verb, as when one says that someone moved, or as a noun, as when one speaks of the movement, without any change in the event itself. What changes, he argues, is only the manner of expression. I find this observation persuasive, but I am unsure what it is supposed to show about grammatical categories. If the same bit of reality can be packaged now as a noun and now as a verb, doesn't that imply that the noun–verb distinction is just a projection of the grammar of a particular language, with no claim to being a genuine or natural division among concepts? And if that is right, what exactly are we doing when we say that a word is a noun or a verb—are we making a factual claim about the word, or only assigning it a role it happens to play in a sentence?
