Im a PhD student in religious studies, and I’ve been turning over a passage in Pausanias’s Description of Greece because it reminds me of something from population genetics, which I only half-remember. The passage is from the description of Corinth (2.3.6–7 and 2.3.9). Pausanias writes:

“Their names were Mermerus and Pheres, and they are said to have been stoned to death by the Corinthians owing to the gifts which legend says they brought to Glauce. [7] But as their death was violent and illegal, the young babies of the Corinthians were destroyed by them until, at the command of the oracle, yearly sacrifices were established in their honor and a figure of Terror was set up.”

Then he gives a completely different version:

“The Greeks have an epic poem called Naupactia. In this Jason is represented as having removed his home after the death of Pelias from Iolcus to Corcyra, and Mermerus, the elder of his children, to have been killed by a lioness while hunting on the mainland opposite. Of Pheres is recorded nothing.”

He doesn’t say one is the true story and the other is corrupt. He lists them, attaches them to landmarks and rituals, and moves on. The parallel I keep seeing is with the way coalescent theory treats gene trees and species trees: different genes in the same population can have genuinely different genealogical histories, and you don’t pick which gene tree is canonical—you explain the discordance through processes like incomplete lineage sorting and introgression. No single gene tree is the species tree, but the discordance is informative, not just error. That seems to map onto Pausanias’s method: the stoned-children version and the lioness version are like gene trees from different lineages, each locally real, and asking for the canonical myth is like asking for the one true gene tree.

But I worry this is just a fancy analogy. In textual criticism there’s an archetype and variants are corruptions, and that’s probably the more natural model for a mythographic text. What would have to be true about how myths are transmitted and recombined for the coalescent model to do real work here, and what would it predict that the textual-criticism model wouldn’t? Can you push on the mapping and tell me where it breaks down? Also, whatever verdict you reach, say what argument or consideration would most likely change your mind, or where your reasoning is least certain.
