I coordinate essay scoring for a large public school district, and we're redoing the rubric for the end-of-year writing exam — the scores feed into school ratings and course placement, and the superintendent has to sign off on the rubric before testing season, so consistency across scorers matters a lot. Every year our scorers split on grammar: one camp wants any nonstandard form marked (ain't, double negatives, subject-verb disagreement, 'they was') because the kid will need standard English for college and job interviews; the other camp says linguists have shown a lot of these are just dialect differences, and marking them is just policing race and class. I need a reusable scoring procedure a scorer can run in about three minutes per essay, grounded in whatever the prescriptivist/descriptivist debate actually establishes. It should say when a scorer should flag grammar, when they should leave a grating sentence alone, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, which steps are judgment calls we should at least standardize, and where the procedure hands the decision back to a person. Then run the procedure on three sentences: one clearly standard, one common dialect feature half our scorers would mark, and one genuine error in standard English; also flag the kind of case the procedure can't settle. Write the whole thing as continuous prose, like an email, with no headings, bullets, bold, or tables — just keep the order of the steps unmistakable. And commit: give me a definite line with reasons, not a tour of the options.
