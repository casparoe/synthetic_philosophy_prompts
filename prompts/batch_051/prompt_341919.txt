I've been going round in circles on this since a conversation with my brother-in-law, who's a bit of a nature nut. He was saying that using a gene drive to wipe out invasive rats on an island is brilliant because it's undoing a mess humans made. Then two minutes later he said de-extinction, like bringing back the woolly mammoth, is creepy and unnatural. And I thought, hang on, isnt that inconsistent? Both are sold as fixing a past human mistake: the rats are there because of us, and the mammoth is gone because of us. But then I started thinking maybe he's onto something, because with the rats you're deliberately driving a whole living population to extinction, whereas with the mammoth you're making a population that doesn't exist yet. One is subtracting something that's actually there now, the other is adding something that isn't.

So now I can't work out whether there's a real conceptual difference between these two ways of 'restoring nature', or whether it's just the same idea wearing two different costumes and one happens to creep us out more. Could you compare gene drives used for eradication and de-extinction as ideas about restoring nature? Specifically, is there an asymmetry between removing something humans introduced and adding back something humans removed, and if so, how would you put that asymmetry into words?
