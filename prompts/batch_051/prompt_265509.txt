I have been trying to decide, on my own, whether calling cultural variants "memes" says anything more than "culture changes over time," and I have worked my way to the following argument. I would like you to check it rather than just reassure me. (1) For any process to count as natural selection, later versions must inherit the variant from earlier versions; where variants are not inherited, selection has nothing to build on. (2) Biological inheritance has this because a dedicated copying mechanism duplicates the underlying sequence, so copying errors are the exception and lineages stay true to type. (3) Cultural transmission has no counterpart to that mechanism: when a tune, a recipe, or a social norm passes from one person to another, nothing in the learner is duplicated from the teacher; the learner reconstructs a version from what they happened to notice and from what they already knew. (4) Therefore cultural variants are not inherited in the required sense—each transmission is a reconstruction rather than a copy—and so a meme cannot be a real replicator; the word is only a metaphor. I keep suspecting step 3 is doing the real work, but I cannot make the objection precise. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and what is the strongest true version of that step?
