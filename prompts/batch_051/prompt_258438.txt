I maintain a small open-source library that parses and validates a niche configuration format. The LICENSE has had the same clause since the original author wrote it, long before I got involved:

"You may not use the Software, in whole or in part, to develop any product or service that directly competes with the Software."

At work we're fine-tuning a small code-generation model. Two things happened during training: our pipeline used the library's validator to score the model's generated config files, and some of the library's source ended up in the training corpus because it sits in our dependency tree and whoever assembled the corpus forgot to exclude vendored deps. Somebody then demoed the model emitting a near drop-in reimplementation of the library's core functions when asked for a parser for this format. There's now a proposal to let users paste a config and get back a generated standalone validator, which is basically what people install the library for. We don't ship the library, and no one on the team sat down and wrote a competing parser; the model just sort of contains one after training.

The wrinkle is that the original author is still active on GitHub and has said the clause was added to stop a specific company that forked the repo, renamed it, and sold it as their own. He has also said he never imagined model training back then and doesn't think the clause reaches it. A coworker says the author's private intention is beside the point — the text now sits in the LICENSE with whatever meaning a reasonable reader gets from it, and the author can't quietly shrink that after the fact. I've never taken a philosophy course, so go easy on the jargon, but I want to understand what each approach to interpretation — the plain words, the purpose of the clause, what the author would have said if asked, and what a reasonable reader today would take the clause to mean — says about whether we're in breach. Then tell me which of those should win when they pull apart, and whether the meaning of a clause like this is fixed when it's written or drifts as new uses show up. If it turns on wether we actually ship the generated-validator feature, say how.
