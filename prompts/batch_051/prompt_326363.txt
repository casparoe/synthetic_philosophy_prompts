I've been reading Kepler's Tertius Interveniens, the one where he says astrology is astronomy's foolish daughter and that the mother would starve without what the daughter brings in. I want to turn his half-apology into a numbered argument and figure out whether it actually does what he wants. Here's my reconstruction:

(1) Astronomy needs money.
(2) In Kepler's situation, the money for astronomy came mostly from patrons who wanted horoscopes.
(3) So if you got rid of astrological practice, astronomy would lose its main source of support.
(4) A practice that is necessary to keep a science alive is justified, at least as a side-practice, even if its own claims are partly false.
(5) So astrology is justified as the thing that keeps astronomy alive.

I can't tell if this is a pragmatic defense of astrology or if it quietly shifts the meaning of "astrology" between step 3 and step 5. Is the argument valid? If not, which exact step fails? And is there a stronger reading of step 4 that would make it valid while still being accepted by Kepler, or does he need an extra premise about astrology's utility that he hasn't supplied?
