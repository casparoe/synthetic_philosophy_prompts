Compare envy and resentment as candidate responses to perceived injustice, with special attention to Rawls's treatment of excusable envy. Rawls treats resentment as a moral sentiment: it is a reaction to what one takes to be a violation of principles of justice. Envy proper, by contrast, is not a moral sentiment but a hostile response to another's greater good, though Rawls allows that envy can be excusable when severe background inequalities undermine self-respect. Assess whether this distinction can do the work Rawls wants it to do. In particular, address the following: whether the same distribution can reasonably count as an occasion for resentment from one party and only for excusable envy from another, and what that difference would mark; whether envy ever contains an implicit normative claim about the legitimacy of the distribution, or whether it is only an aversive comparison that happens to track injustice; and whether treating envy as a signal of injustice risks dignifying a vice. Where your comparison reveals the sharpest divergence between the two emotions, say which treatment you find more defensible and why.
