Suppose that from 2027 onward, a perfect verification regime covers every frontier AI effort: each lab, and the military and intelligence agencies behind it, can observe in real time and with cryptographic certainty everyone else's compute purchases, algorithmic progress, training-run plans, safety-test results, and deployment preparations. Nothing else changes — in particular, there is no enforcement body, no one can stop anyone from acting on what they learn, and any commitment to slow down remains unenforceable. On the common reading of Fearon, information asymmetries are a central reason competitive situations escalate into costly conflict even when some bargain exists that would leave everyone better off. Predict how the AI race unfolds over the following decade under this regime. Work through Fearon's three mechanisms explicitly — private information with incentives to misrepresent, commitment problems, and issue indivisibility — and say which effects you would bet on, which predictions reverse under assumptions like offense-dominance, winner-take-all AGI, or roughly symmetric labs, and why.
