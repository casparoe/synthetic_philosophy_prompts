I have been working through the philosophical literature on open borders, specifically the arguments for the state's claimed moral right to exclude would-be immigrants. I want to stress-test whether my notes have captured all the main families of argument or only the famous ones, since I keep seeing the same moves under different labels.

The six headings I have so far are these:
(1) the associational argument: a polity, like a club or a household, may choose its own members;
(2) the self-determination argument: a self-governing people has a right to decide its own future, and decisions about who may enter and settle are among the most consequential such decisions;
(3) the cultural-preservation argument: a community may act to keep its language, traditions, and institutions from being changed by immigration it has not chosen;
(4) the welfare-state argument: redistributive institutions depend on social trust and solidarity, and large-scale immigration can erode those foundations;
(5) the democratic-boundary argument: meaningful democratic rule presupposes a determinate citizenry, so a state must be able to specify who belongs to the people; and
(6) the territorial-jurisdiction argument: a state cannot reliably perform its basic functions of law enforcement, public order, and security unless it controls admission to its territory.

I have the impression that these six headings capture the standard repertoire and that nearly everything else I find is a refinement or a combination of them. I would like you to prove me wrong if I am wrong. Give me exactly five further arguments for a state's moral right to exclude would-be immigrants that are not restatements, special cases, or combinations of the six above. For each candidate, state the argument in one sentence, and in a second sentence say whether it is, in your judgment, stronger, weaker, or roughly on a par with the most promising items on my list, and why. Present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five entries, each exactly two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
