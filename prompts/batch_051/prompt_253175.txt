I keep seeing people say that autonomous systems create a "responsibility gap"—for instance, when a self-driving car or an autonomous weapon causes harm and supposedly no one is responsible. I don't see why that's a conceptual problem. The car didn't design itself: engineers wrote the code, a company chose to sell it, a regulator approved it, and someone chose to deploy it. The causal chain back to human decisions is all there; it's just messy. So isn't the "gap" really just an evidence problem, or a legal liability problem, not a philosophical one? What would it even mean for responsibility to "gap" if every link in the causal chain has a human in it? Also, if you could separate what's settled from what's still genuinely contested here and not present one side as consensus, that'd be helpful.
