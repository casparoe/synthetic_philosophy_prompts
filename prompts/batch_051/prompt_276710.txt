You are outside counsel to a credit rating agency that publishes numerical issuer ratings, sector outlooks, and written commentary about the creditworthiness of companies and municipal governments. The agency has just been sued for defamation by a company whose rating was downgraded and whose outlook was labeled negative. The company alleges that the following statements in the rating commentary are actionable assertions of fact: first, that the company’s management has consistently underestimated refinancing risk; second, that the company’s liquidity position is weaker than it publicly discloses. The agency maintains that these are protected opinions.

Write a reusable procedure for the agency’s analysts and editors to follow before publishing a rating, outlook, or accompanying commentary, translating the constitutional defamation doctrine that distinguishes protected opinion from actionable fact into operational steps. In doing so, take account of the considerations courts in the line of cases running from Gertz through Ollman v. Evans and Milkovich have used: verifiability, the common meaning of the specific words, the journalistic context, the broader social context, and whether the statement has a defamatory factual connotation or merely implies undisclosed facts.

The procedure must include: (a) a checklist or decision tree that a non-lawyer can apply; (b) tests for determining whether a statement is verifiable, would be understood by a reasonable reader as asserting fact, or would be understood as a subjective assessment of creditworthiness; (c) special handling for numeric ratings, risk scores, and forecasts as opposed to assertions about past or present facts; (d) at least two worked examples, one of which must be a case where your procedure instructs the analyst not to publish the statement as drafted and to revise it; and (e) an honest statement of which steps remain judgment calls and where the procedure will not protect against litigation risk. Do not provide legal citations or a bibliography; give only the operational rule and its justification.
