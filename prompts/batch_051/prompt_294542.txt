You're a philosopher who works on the formal structure of value, especially how different welfare components stack. Think this through carefully: steelman the idea first, then go after the strongest version you can build.

I've been fussing over how different kinds of value combine, and I think I've got a general answer. Take the slogan consciousness-times-happiness: the worth of a mental state is how present or intense the consciousness is, multiplied by how good or bad it feels. But why multiply instead of add? I now think there are exactly three ways two value-dimensions can combine. Additive, when both are independently good and trade off smoothly. Multiplicative, when each axis is a precondition for the other to matter — zero consciousness zeroes out happiness, and zero happiness zeroes out the value of consciousness. Lexical, when one axis has a floor or ceiling the other can never buy back (no amount of happiness compensates for zero consciousness; no amount of pleasure compensates for a life stuck below some level of suffering, if you set the threshold there). And the bigger claim is how you tell which mode applies: ask whether there's a stable exchange rate between small amounts of the two axes, or whether the rate blows up at an edge. If it stays stable, they add. If zero on either side is a kill-switch, they multiply. If the rate goes infinite somewhere, they're lexical. I call it the Modal Theory of Value Combination. I know it sounds like a taxonomy more than a theory, but I think the real content is the diagnostic rule — its that substitution facts settle the whole thing, and most of the famous fights (adding billions of lives barely worth living, barely-sentient insects, torture versus mild pains spread across huge populations) are just people assuming different modes without noticing. So: is this a real theory or just relabeling additive versus multiplicative utility? Does the diagnostic rule actually decide the consciousness-times-happiness question, or does it just push the hard call into how we measure the axes? Where does it break?
