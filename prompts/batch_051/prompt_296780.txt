I got into it with my brother the other night about whether a state can commit terrorism, and he laid out a chain of reasoning that I cant get out of my head. He put it more or less like this. (1) What makes an act terrorism is a matter of what the act is and what it's for, not who does it: you use violence against ordinary people, or threaten it, to terrify a wider population into some political change. That's why a separatist group bombing a café counts as terrorism and a soldier shooting an enemy soldier in battle doesn't; the café bomb is aimed at everyone who hears about it, not just the people at the tables. (2) If the definition is about the act, then the actor can't be what makes the difference. A government that shells a crowded market to make the surrounding population too scared to shelter rebels is doing the same kind of thing described in (1): violence against ordinary people, meant to send a message that changes political behavior. (3) So either that government is committing terrorism, or the only reason it isn't is that it's a government. (4) But if the government exception is real, then terrorism just means "this kind of act, but only when non-state groups do it," which makes the word a label for who holds the gun rather than what the gun is doing. And if the exception is not real, then major states commit terrorism as a matter of ordinary strategy, and the line between terrorism and war starts to give out. (5) So either the plain definition of terrorism is broken, or the word is basically a political label for violence by groups we happen to dislike, and anyone who uses it the way the definition does is just failing to follow their own definition. He thinks it's the second. I want to resist that, because I also want to say that states can commit terrorism — that's half the point of the word for me — but I dont want terrorism to swallow all of war either. Where does the argument go wrong, if it does? Which step would you reject, and what does giving it up cost for how the rest of it hangs together?
