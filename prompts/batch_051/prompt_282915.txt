I keep coming back to one of Pascal’s arguments in the Pensées, in the force-and-justice fragments, where he says roughly: justice is endlessly disputable, force is easy to recognize and not disputed, so since we couldn’t make the just strong we made the strong just. He seems to be treating recognizability, not just enforcement, as the reason force has to be the foundation of legal order. Here’s the change I’m interested in: suppose a society develops a highly transparent legal-adjudication system, maybe an AI or something close to it. For any dispute it gives a full public audit trail showing exactly which precedents, tradeoffs, and principles it used, and people can check and broadly do accept its judgments as the just ones before any enforcement kicks in. So justice becomes about as legible and uncontested as Pascal thought force was. The enforcement machinery still exists in the background. Does Pascal’s argument still go through in that setting? Or has the development only forced him to a different premise, something like: people will always start disputing justice once there are rival auditors or rival inputs into the system? I’m less interested in whether such a system is realistic and more in what it does to the argument. Also say how confident you are and pick out the point you’re least sure of.
