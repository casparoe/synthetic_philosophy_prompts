Here's one I've been turning over. Suppose two villages have exactly the same welfare, freedoms and security, and neither has any unmet want. But one village's fields and workshops yield twice what it consumes; the surplus just rots in granaries or sits idle, harming noone and noticed by no one. Is that village's abundance good in itself — is it a better world than the frugal one — even though the extra output buys nothing in the end? Write an essay on whether prosperity or abundance can have moral worth beyond what it buys. Weigh the strongest arguments on both sides, then reach and defend a verdict rather than ending on the fence. (A verdict like 'only when the surplus tracks a capacity for agency' is fine, as long as you justify it.) Also, write in British English.
