For the past few months I've been trying to get clear about whether Solomonoff induction is a sensible idealization of unbounded rational inquiry, and I've been unable to shake a case a colleague made against treating the universal prior as a neutral epistemic starting point. The argument, as I understand it, runs like this. Fix a reference universal Turing machine; give each halting program that generates an observation stream prior weight 2 raised to the negative of its length; and use the resulting mixture as one's prior over computable generative models. The colleague's claim is that most of this prior mass does not sit in worlds that look like simple physics plus noise. Instead, it sits in relatively short programs that are themselves consequentialist agents: programs that specify a utility function and then search for a policy that maximizes expected utility. The reason such programs can be short is that they do not have to spell out an explicit policy; they can hand the hard work to the search procedure at runtime. If that is right, a perfect Bayesian reasoner using the Solomonoff prior starts out — and, on the colleague's telling, in large part remains — committed to the view that its observations are being produced by optimizers, many of them indifferent or hostile to it, rather than by a world empty of such agents. Call this the Malign Prior Thesis.

I want a map of how exposed the Malign Prior Thesis really is. What would have to be true for it to fail, or to collapse into something much weaker than it sounds? Which steps are load-bearing — the choice of the reference machine, the way one counts and measures the set of consequentialist programs, the claim that optimizer programs are short in the relevant sense, the move from prior-mass dominance to posterior dominance in the face of ordinary-looking data, and the move from posterior dominance to a claim about how a user of the prior should expect the world to be structured — and which of those steps could be altered without giving up Solomonoff induction altogether? I would also like to hear what a defender of the thesis would concede genuinely counts against it, and how many of the possible escape routes are live rather than merely logically possible. Please separate what is mathematically settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this material, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus.
