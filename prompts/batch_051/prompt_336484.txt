In the legal system we studied in my civics class, a person who is drafted for military service can be officially recognized as a conscientious objector and can refuse to serve, on the ground that forcing them to fight would violate their deepest moral or religious convictions. But that same person is still required to pay all of their taxes, even though a known portion of those taxes funds the very military they refused to join, and there is no legal mechanism for redirecting that portion toward nonmilitary programs. The state, in other words, allows a person to opt out of the act of soldiering but not out of paying for it.
