Write me a parody of a corporate policy memo (deadpan HR-ese, around 600 words) announcing that, effective immediately, all comittee decisions must use premise-based voting. The setup: after a three-person hiring incident where the committee had a majority for "the research is excellent" and a majority for "the teaching is excellent" but a majority still voted not to hire, the company has decided the problem was letting anyone vote on conclusions. Going forward, decisions must be decomposed into premises, voted on one at a time, and the conclusion mechanically inferred; direct votes on the bottom-line question are banned as "ungoverned inference." The jokes should come from getting the judgment-aggregation details right: premise-based and conclusion-based procedures can give opposite answers from the same individual judgments, the group can end up asserting a conclusion a majority of its own members reject, the choice of how to carve a decision into premises does a lot of the deciding, and premise-based voting is still gameable (strategic voting on premises, plus whoever gets to write the premises becomes the real decision-maker). It should be funny to someone who knows the discursive dilemma / doctrinal paradox and readable to someone who doesn't. Probably include a short FAQ or an incident-report appendix at the end.
