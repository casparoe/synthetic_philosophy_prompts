Section 7(b) of the Meridian Coastal Health System's 1998 Code of Clinical Conduct reads: "No member of staff shall, by any positive act, cause or hasten the death of a patient. The withholding or ceasing of a treatment that is futile or excessively burdensome to the patient is not a positive act, and death that follows from such withholding or ceasing shall not be treated as caused or hastened by the member of staff."

The System now operates a closed-loop weaning protocol in its long-term respiratory unit. An algorithm assigns each ventilated patient an hourly probability of survival to discharge. If a patient's score has remained below 0.12 for six consecutive hours, the ventilator begins reducing delivered support by a fixed step every forty minutes until support reaches its minimum, unless a clinician has entered a timed override. The system does not page anyone or require an individual clinician to review the patient before weaning begins; staff are simply expected to monitor a dashboard during rounds.

At 03:10 a patient's score crossed the threshold. Weaning began automatically at 09:10. The covering clinician, occupied with two cardiac arrests, did not open the dashboard, no override was entered, support fell to minimum, and the patient died at 11:04. No clinician had formed the judgement that ventilation had become futile or excessively burdensome, and the family was not consulted.

Did anything occur that Section 7(b) prohibits? Treat the provision as one the drafters wrote before such systems existed. Determine, for each of four approaches of interpretation — (i) the plain words, (ii) the purposes of the second sentence, (iii) what its 1998 drafters would most likely say if asked whether the provision reaches this case, and (iv) what a reasonable member of staff would understand it to require — whether the automatic weaning, the failure to override, or the original decision to deploy the protocol is caught by the clause.

"overall_assessment" should be a short paragraph giving the best reading of the clause on the case and saying which interpretation is doing most of the work.
