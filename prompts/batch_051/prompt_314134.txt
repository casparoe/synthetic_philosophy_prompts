I've come around to a perfectionist line on achievement: that doing something genuinely excellent — succeeding at something hard through your own competence — is valuable for its own sake, and it stays valuable even if no one ever finds out and no one's welfare is improved one bit, including mine. I want to know what would count against it. Not the lazy "that's just self-indulgence" stuff, but the specific commitments the view stands or falls with. There's the isolation-test move, the question whether the value is in the striving or the attaining, the case of achievements that are evil or grotesque, the worry that so-called achievement value just collapses into agent-relative satisfaction or pride in one's own competence, and the picture of an excellent life that's entirely wasted. Which of these should actually move me, which would a defender of the view concede as counting against it, and which can be absorbed with a bit of qualification? I'm also prepared to hear that the view ends up unfalsifiable-ish in practice — but if so, tell me whether that's a defect for a view of this kind. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and flag the one I'm probably most tempted to wave off but shouldn't.
