I keep running into the Barcan formulas in quantified modal logic and I cant figure out whether to accept them or revise the semantics. Heres what hooks me. In the simplest setup — S5, one fixed domain for every world, quantifiers ranging over that domain, and 'x exists' just meaning 'x is in the range of the quantifier' — both formulas come out valid:

(BF) ∀x□Fx → □∀xFx

(CBF) □∀xFx → ∀x□Fx

They're the two directions in which the universal quantifier commutes with the box. BF corresponds to the domain not growing as you move to another possible world, CBF to its not shrinking, and a fixed-domain S5 model gives you both. But then it seems to follow that every actual individual is in the domain of every world — so I couldn't have failed to exist, and there couldn't be a merely possible child of mine, because any child who exists in some world would already be an actual object, just by being in the fixed domain. I find the theory compelling: the semantics is clean, quantifiers and boxes behave the way I want them to, and the apparatus of possible worlds stays simple. But the intuition that I might not have existed — that my parents could have never met and no actual person would have been me — is about as firm a conviction as I have, and the thought that there could have been a child of mine even though no actual person is that child feels just as undeniable.
