Draft the joint statement that a defender of strong computational irreducibility and a defender of explanatory optimism about complex systems could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, in the format of a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.

The irreducibilist position: for an important class of rule-based systems, the behavior of interest cannot reliably be anticipated by any computation substantially shorter than running the system itself, and this is a principled limit on explanation, not an artifact of our current mathematics. The optimist position: apparent irreducibility is always relative to a chosen representation or level of description, and the history of science suggests that finding the right coarse-graining or change of variables typically replaces an irreducible simulation with a compressed, predictive account at a higher level.

The agreed-claims section should cover what both sides accept about the formal terrain: that there are systems whose future states cannot be produced faster than by simulation under any presently known method, that some questions about such systems are undecidable, and that a proof of incompressibility in one description language does not by itself rule out compression in another. It should also settle, in terms both accept, what each side means by explanation, and separate the absence of a known compression from a demonstrated impossibility of any compression.

The disagreements table should record, in wording each side would accept for its own view, where they part ways. The cruxes should include at least these: whether a well-defined space of admissible descriptions can be stated precisely enough for the claim that no shorter description exists at any level to be provable or even meaningful; whether algorithmic incompressibility and explanatory irreducibility are the same claim or merely analogous; and whether coarse-grained higher-level descriptions can achieve compression without losing the very feature the explanation was meant to account for. For each crux, specify a concrete case, formal result, or argument that both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. Close with a short note on which residual disagreement, if any, each side now regards as merely verbal.
