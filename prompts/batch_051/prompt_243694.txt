ok so I'm writing the conditionals chapter of my prospectus and I'd like a consistency check before I send it to my advisor, becuase I can't tell whether these commitments actually hang together. Here's where I've landed.

(1) For indicatives I buy Adams' thesis in its strong form: the probability of the conditional if A then B just is the conditional probability P(B|A). I also think of assertability the same way — high conditional probability is what makes an indicative assertable.

(2) But I also think indicatives have truth conditions and express ordinary propositions. They embed fine under negation, disjunction, and under probably, so I don't want a semantics that treats them as lacking a propositional core.

(3) For counterfactuals I'm a Lewis/Stalnaker person: if A had been the case, B would have been the case is true iff B holds in all the closest A-worlds, with the usual similarity ordering and no backtracking (reasoning from the antecedent back to earlier causes) unless the context forces it. I also take might-counterfactuals as the dual: if A had been the case, B might have been the case is true iff B holds in at least one of the closest A-worlds.

(4) I accept the standard Oswald/Kennedy contrast: the indicative if Oswald didn't shoot Kennedy then someone else did is acceptable, but the counterfactual if Oswald hadn't shot Kennedy then someone else would have is false. I take this to show that indicatives and counterfactuals need different treatments.

(5) I also think contraposition is valid for indicatives: if A then B licenses if not-B then not-A, in the sense that confidence in the premise should carry over to the conclusion.

(6) Methodologically I think the Ramsey test is the unifying core of the whole area: evaluate a conditional by supposing the antecedent and checking the consequent, making minimal adjustments. For indicatives the adjustment is doxastic (revising beliefs); for counterfactuals it's factual (revising the world).

Is this a coherent package? Which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and which single revision would clean up the most? Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and for each one say what would have to give and what it costs.
