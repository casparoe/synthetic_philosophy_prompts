there's this fight on the rationalist side of the internet that's been going in circles for as long as i've been reading, between the people who think causal claims basically need a randomized experiment (or at worst a clean natural experiment), and the pearl/do-calculus people who think if you draw the right DAG and accept something like no unmeasured confounding you can compute interventional distributions from purely observational data. its weird because everyone involved has read the same posts and can state the other side's argument back perfectly, and yet nobody moves. my working theory is that the real disagreement isnt about statistics at all: it's about what gets to count as an assumption versus a fact about the world — the RCT side thinks "no unmeasured confounding" is smuggling in the answer, while the graph side thinks randomization is just another structural assumption that's often less checkable than people pretend. how would you diagnose this impasse — verbal, substantive, different standards of evidence, different tolerances for model error — and given the diagnosis, what would actually change someones mind? or is the impasse itself the point?
