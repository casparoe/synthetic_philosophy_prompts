A school was founded in 1712 under a charter whose “Instruction” section reads: “Let no child be beaten, bribed, or flattered into learning. The master shall govern children by respect for their natural temper; he shall make knowledge agreeable to them, and shall use praise and rebuke sparingly, so that the child prizes the good opinion of the wise and fears the shame of the idle, more than any penalty or prize.” The drafters explicitly looked to Locke’s views on education. The school now uses an online adaptive-learning system that issues points, streaks, and public badges for every completed exercise and withdraws them when an algorithm judges that a child has been guessing rather than working. I just spent an hour arguing about it with a friend: I said the point system is a bribe in another form, and she said it is exactly the esteem-and-disgrace mechanism the drafters would have updated for today. Work through what the clause requires here, considering the text, the purpose of the provision, what the drafters would have said if asked about gamified software, and what a reasonable reader today should take them to mean. Tell me which approach should govern and why. Also tell me which argument or consideration would change your mind, or where your reasoning from purpose or from the drafters’ likely intent is most likely to fail.
