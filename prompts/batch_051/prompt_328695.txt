I've recently started riding for a food-delivery co-operative, and I've been reading Mill's late chapters on socialism (the posthumous ones). Its made me wonder whether what we're doing actually matches what he had in mind, or whether we've just built a gig platform with a co-op sticker on it.

I tried to extract a list of criteria from Mill for what makes an enterprise genuinely worker-controlled. Here's what I've got:

1. The workers jointly own the instruments of production (the bikes, the app, the customer list, etc.).
2. The workers manage the work democratically, rather than being managed by a separate owner or manager.
3. Pay is tied to labour, not to capital contributed.
4. The arrangement doesn't create a new form of despotism over workers.
5. It can sustain productivity without a class of owners skimming off the top.

Now here's the borderline case. Our co-operative is a bit odd. The couriers own the company through one-member-one-vote shares, and we elect a board. We all pay a small membership fee, seperate from the 15% cut that goes to the tech company. But we've licensed our delivery app from a private tech company. The app assigns orders, sets the price per delivery, tracks our acceptance rate, and automatically deactivates anyone whose rate drops below 80% for a week. The tech company has a veto over any change to the app's core algorithm. So formally we own the business, but the day-to-day labour process is run by software owned by outsiders. Is this still a worker-controlled enterprise in Mill's sense, or is it just a gig platform with extra steps?

You're an expert on Mill and the political economy of co-operatives. Could you start by brainstorming the tensions here, then go through each of my five criteria and say whether our setup satisfies it, where the criteria might be wrong or not really necessary, and whether this case shows that I need to revise the criteria themselves? I'd rather you give me a nuanced answer than a yes/no. Also, do you think Mill would have seen the algorithmic control as importantly different from a human manager, or is that just a new form of the same old problem?
