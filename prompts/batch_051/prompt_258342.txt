I'm not sure exactly how to put this, but I've ended up with a view about mathematical objects and I want you to tell me what could break it.

The view: when a structure has nontrivial automorphisms, there are no unmarked facts about which element plays which role. The two square roots of -1 in the splitting field of x^2 + 1 over Q are the clean case. Conjugation swaps them and fixes Q pointwise, and nothing internal to the field distinguishes i from -i. So calling one of them i is just a labeling convention, like picking an orientation. The actual mathematics is the whole structure together with its automorphism group, not a distinguished element sitting inside it. If some piece of math seems to depend on which root got the name i, I think it either secretly depends on something extra—an orientation, an embedding, an ordering—or it isn't doing mathematical work at all, just bookkeeping.

What I want is a hunt for things that would count against this. Not the standard line that an algebraic closure of Q exists only via choice or isn't canonical; I take that to be part of the view, not an objection to it. I mean cases where a privileged choice actually seems to carry mathematical weight, or where the view forces me to say something I should be embarrassed about. For instance, are there places where i versus -i matters even after you've tried to quotient by the automorphism—in complex analysis with contour integrals and residues, in the fact that complex conjugation is outer rather than inner, or in Galois theory where naming a particular root of an irreducible polynomial seems needed to identify a generator of a Galois group? Which of those are genuine trouble for the view, and which are just the view correctly describing a convention? Are there situations where the symmetry itself is the object being studied and the view can't even state what's going on? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and then tell me whether you think the position should survive. I'd rather you commit and be wrong than give me a list of maybes.
