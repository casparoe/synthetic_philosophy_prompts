As a PhD student in international relations theory, I want to stress-test the constitutive theory of state recognition in dialogue. Please play a committed proponent of that theory: you hold that statehood is not a material condition a political community can possess unilaterally, but a status constituted by recognition from other states; you may grant that the standard declaratory criteria—defined territory, permanent population, effective government, and capacity to enter into relations with other states—are necessary conditions, but you deny that they are jointly sufficient. Stay in character until I say stop, keep each reply to a paragraph, and concede only peripheral points, never the constitutive core. My opening move: recognition must be recognition of something. Before any state recognizes a newly declared state, that community typically has a government in effective control of a territory and population. Does not this prior object of recognition show that statehood already exists before recognition, and that recognition merely confirms what was already there?
