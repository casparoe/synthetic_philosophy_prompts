I've been reading about Bayesianism mostly through blog posts and forum threads, no philosophy background, and I've ended up with a sort of personal doctrine about how to update beliefs when the evidence is fuzzy. I'm about to write it up for a small reading group and I want an honest audit before I stand by it, because I can't tell if the pieces actually fit.

Here's where I've landed:

(1) When you learn something for certain — a reliable person tells you the die came up even, say — you should conditionalize: your new probability for any claim is your old probability of that claim conditional on the thing you learned.

(2) But almost no real evidence is like that. Usually you just get more or less confident about something without becoming certain of anything new — like seeing a piece of cloth in dim light and becoming a bit more confident its green. For cases like that I think the right rule is Jeffrey conditionalization: split the space into a partition, keep your conditional probabilities on each cell of the partition fixed, and update the probabilities of the cells themselves to match your new confidences; everything else falls out of that.

(3) I think order shouldn't matter. If I get the cloth-glance evidence and then the texture-feel evidence, I should end up in exactly the same place as if the order had been reversed, as long as the total evidence is the same.

(4) I also think the way the evidence is carved up shouldn't matter. Whether I treat the glance as "green vs not-green" or as "green vs blue vs something else," those are just labels; they shouldn't change my final probability for the hypothesis I actually care about.

(5) And I've been treating plain conditionalization and Jeffrey's rule as rival theories — like you have to pick one camp or the other — and I genuinely can't tell whether (1) and (2) are a contradiction hiding in my own view.

What I want is a consistency audit of these five, not a lecture. Go through the pairs, tell me which ones are really in tension, which ones only look like they are, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each real tension, say what would have to give and what it would cost. And if I've got Jeffrey conditionalization wrong, or the whole setup is confused, say so plainly rather than humoring me.
