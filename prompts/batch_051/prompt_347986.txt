I joined my university's intellectual property committee last fall, and I have been asked to write the recommendation for the case we will decide next month. We treat our prior decisions as precedent, but we have never codified a single rule; each decision was written as a short, case-specific judgment. I want my recommendation to be consistent with that record, and I have come to see that the record points in more than one direction.

The relevant precedents are these. We declined to list a technician who had spent months building a prototype from the named inventor's exact specifications; the technician, we reasoned, had executed a conception rather than contributed to it. We listed a statistician who supplied the closed-form expression that became the core of the claims, though the statistician did no implementation. We declined to list a lab director who had secured the funding, proposed the general objective, and supervised the work, but made no suggestion about how the result was to be achieved. We listed a colleague who proposed using a particular sensor, on the ground that the sensor appears in the final claims, although the colleague did no further work. And we declined to list a research assistant who ran the full set of simulations specified by the eventual inventor; the simulations required considerable skill, we acknowledged, but the assistant had designed nothing.

The case before us now is this. A doctoral student proposed a control strategy that the team pursued for eight months and then set aside. The final patent contains no trace of the student's strategy. Two of the named inventors have submitted statements saying that the team reached the claimed strategy only by working through why the student's proposal failed, and they describe that failure as essential to the result. I have to decide whether the student should be listed.

Please give me a recommendation and the reasoning behind it. In particular: identify the competing principles that fit our precedents; say which precedent is the closest analog and whether it controls; state which decision each candidate principle would require; explain which decision best coheres with our record and why you prefer it; and specify one further case that would most cleanly settle whatever ambiguity remains. Please answer in American English.
