I am a student in a seminar called Violence and Progress: Is the World Becoming Less Violent? Our instructor has spent the term building what she calls a violence ledger for comparing periods, and she has issued a series of verdicts about which deaths and injuries may enter it. She counted each of the following as violence: a man beaten to death during a robbery; soldiers and civilians killed in an interstate war; and a demonstrator killed by a police officer’s baton. She refused to count a pedestrian struck by a sober driver who ran a red light, which she called an accident rather than violence, and a hiker killed by an avalanche, which she called a natural event rather than violence. When we compared two towns, she also ruled that a town of 50,000 people with 40 assaults per year was more violent than a town of 200,000 with 80 assaults per year, on the ground that the smaller town had the higher rate.

The new case under discussion is this. Over a decade, one country’s homicide rate falls from 8.0 to 6.0 per 100,000, while its population triples, so the absolute number of homicides rises. During the same decade, the country is under an international economic embargo that blocks imports of insulin, antibiotics, and surgical supplies; the resulting shortages kill roughly 4.0 per 100,000 people in the final year, up from zero a decade earlier, with none of the victims individually targeted but all of the deaths foreseeable to the embargoing states. We need to determine whether the embargo deaths belong in the ledger, and whether she would say the country became more violent or less violent over the decade.

What would our instructor most likely say, assuming she aims to remain consistent with her earlier verdicts? Lay out the candidate rules that fit her record so far, explain which of them genuinely diverge on this new case and what each rule commits her to, give your best prediction with the reason it deserves the most weight, and then specify one further case, not already on her record, whose ruling would do the most to settle which of the remaining rules she is actually using.
