I recently spent an evening reading the members’ handbook my sister brought home from the rationality dojo she joined last year, and it struck me that the genre deserves a proper send-up. I would like you to write a parody of a dojo’s membership agreement and grading syllabus for an organisation that trains rationality as a martial art. Please write it in the deadpan voice of sports-club literature: numbered clauses covering induction, fees, grading examinations, sparring etiquette, injuries and insurance, and the criteria for promotion from white belt to black belt. The satire should be aimed at the real difficulty beneath the enterprise — the problem of transfer, that is, whether drills performed in the dojo, such as spot-the-bias katas and calibration exercises on toy cases, carry over to high-stakes judgement outside it. The clauses should exaggerate genuine risks of this kind of training rather than inventing straw men: near transfer being mistaken for far transfer, belt rank hardening into credentialism, epistemic humility curdling into performance, and success on dojo exercises being treated as success in life. The parody should achieve this by implication rather than by lecturing, and it should stay deadpan throughout; it should be funny to someone who knows the relevant debates while still working for someone who does not. Around 700 words.

Please append a short commentary, clearly separated from the parody, on where it takes liberties with the underlying ideas. In that commentary, say which claim about transfer the piece leans on most heavily, where a sympathetic defender of such dojos could most reasonably object, and what evidence or consideration would lead you to write the piece with a different emphasis. I would rather the note overstate its uncertainty than pretend the satire has no commitments. Please write the whole thing in British English.
