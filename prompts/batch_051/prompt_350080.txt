For a graduate seminar in formal epistemology, I have been asked to write up my commitments about diachronic norms of self-deference, and I would like a consistency audit of the package before I circulate it. Here is what I believe.

(1) I accept conditionalization as the only normative rule governing rational changes of credence in response to evidence: on learning E and nothing else, a rational agent should move to her old credence conditional on E.

(2) I hold a restricted version of van Fraassen's Reflection principle: my present credence in a proposition A, conditional on learning only that my future self will assign some value r to A, should itself be r—but only when I now expect that future self to be at least as well informed as I am and no less rational than I am.

(3) I think the familiar forgetting case is a genuine counterexample to the unrestricted principle. If I now know that a fair coin landed heads, and I also know that I will be given an amnesia-inducing drug before I am asked again, I should not lower my credence in heads to one-half merely because my later self will report one-half. My restriction handles this, since the later self is less well informed.

(4) Anticipated irrationality also blocks deference, and I think it can be rationally permissible to bring about. An agent may rationally take a drug she knows will make her overconfident about some proposition without giving her evidence, and she need not defer to the resulting credences. I take such a change to fall outside conditionalization's scope, because it is not a response to evidence.

(5) I think that, once restricted in this way, Reflection is not an additional diachronic norm over and above conditionalization. It is simply what conditionalizing on whatever the future self has learned yields whenever that future self is rational and at least as well informed; the two conditions in the restriction do all the work, and no independent principle of self-deference is needed.

I cannot tell whether this set hangs together. Please (i) distinguish the commitments that are in genuine tension from those that are only apparently in tension, (ii) rank the genuine tensions by severity and say for each what would have to give and at what cost, and (iii) identify the single revision to the list that would clean up the most. Throughout, separate what is settled among people who work on this material from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested claim as the consensus. End with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing a short label for the single revision you recommend.
