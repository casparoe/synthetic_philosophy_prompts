Last night I got into a long argument with a friend about whether a politician who took bribes can still criticize a current politician for taking bribes. My friend kept saying the first guy is a hypocrite, so his criticism doesnt count, full stop. I think that conflates two different things: whether the criticism is true, and whether this particular person has any business making it. I want to say the criticism can be true and even useful no matter who says it, and the speaker's hypocrisy is a separate flaw in him, not a flaw in the criticism. But then I got tangled up trying to explain what 'having any business making it' even means, becuase if the criticism is true, why should the speaker's record matter at all?

Could you write a dialogue between two people who hold these two positions? Please make them really engage with each other, not just trade slogans, and make sure neither is a strawman. In particular, have them dig into whether a hypocrite's blame is more like a judge who lost jurisdiction (so the blame is somehow void) or more like a rude guest at a party (out of line, but still pointing at a real fact). And have them consider a trickier case: someone who committed the same wrong years ago, fully came clean, and made amends — does he regain his standing to blame, and what would that tell us about what standing actually is? If it fits naturally, also have them touch on the worry that silencing hypocrites can cost us information, since insiders often know the details best.
