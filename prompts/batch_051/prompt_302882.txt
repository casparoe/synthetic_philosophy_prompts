I serve on the parks and recreation advisory commission for a small town built around a shallow freshwater lake in a hot southern climate. The public swim beach is the center of town life every summer, and much of the seasonal economy — the bait shop, snack bar, canoe rental, and weekend cabins — depends on people coming to swim. Last month, a child who had been swimming at the beach died from a brain infection caused by an amoeba that lives in warm lake water. The state health department briefed us that the organism is present in many warm freshwater lakes, that serious infections are extraordinarily rare, but that the disease is almost always fatal once contracted. We were also told that the risk is thought to be higher in warm, shallow water where sediment gets stirred up, and that most reported cases involve children.

We are now caught between two groups. A group of parents has petitioned us to close the swim beach during the warm months, or at minimum to ban children and wading in the shallow area, arguing that no risk of this kind is acceptable where children are concerned. On the other side, business owners and many residents say the beach is the heart of the town, that a summer closure would gut the local economy, and that the short drive to the lake is almost certainly more dangerous than swimming in it — yet no one proposes closing the roads. Middle positions have been floated: posting prominent warnings, requiring signed waivers, keeping the swimming area open only when the water stays below a certain temperature, or moving the roped-off zone to deeper, cooler water. The commission is genuinely split, and we vote next month.

I have no training in public health or law — I run a small feed store — but I keep returning to the same underlying question: how safe does a public place have to be before we are justified in keeping it open? Is a very small chance of a very bad outcome enough of a reason to take away something so many people depend on and enjoy? Please draft the written opinion I would circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action (close, restrict, keep open with changes, or some specific combination), give the reasoning in plain terms, and honestly address the strongest argument from whichever side does not get what it wants.
