I cover complexity science for a general-interest magazine and am preparing a long-read on the current debate over assembly theory, so I would like a precise conceptual explainer I can work from. Please assume a reader who already understands the difference between Kolmogorov complexity and statistical complexity. Explain, first, what the assembly index of an object is supposed to capture — the minimal number of recursive joining operations needed to build the object from an elementary set of parts — and why proponents think a high assembly index is evidence that the object was produced by selection rather than by unguided processes. Then give the strongest form of the objection that the assembly index is, in effect, a compressibility measure tied to a particular fixed repertoire of allowed operations, one that may not reveal anything about the object's causal history beyond what the choice of operations already encodes. Close by separating the empirical and conceptual components of the dispute between assembly theorists and their information-theoretic critics, and say what would have to be true of a physical process for a high assembly index to be genuinely informative about whether that process was shaped by selection.
