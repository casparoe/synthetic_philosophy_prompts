After I had a general anaesthetic for a minor op last month, the anaesthetist told me I'd been squeezing her hand on command for about a minute, despite having no memory of any of it. That's got me reading about consciousness, and I've ended up going through Baars, Dehaene and Block. I've arrived at an argument that seems to show Global Workspace Theory either is false or explains something other than what I actually care about, and I can't see where it goes wrong.

Here's the argument as I've set it out:

(1) On GWT, a state is conscious iff its content is broadcast on the global workspace, i.e., made globally available to a range of consumer systems for reporting, reasoning, planning and action selection.
(2) Global broadcasting is a functional property: it's a matter of what the information can do in the system, not of anything essentially first-personal or felt.
(3) But being globally available for use by the rest of the system just is Block's access consciousness.
(4) So GWT says access consciousness is necessary and sufficient for consciousness, including phenomenal consciousness.
(5) Access consciousness looks like the sort of thing a nonconscious machine could have. A well-designed system could make information globally available to its own subsystems for report and control while there's nothing it is like to be it.
(6) So if GWT is right, any globally broadcasting creature is thereby phenomenally conscious; but by (5) there could be a globally broadcasting creature that isn't. Contradiction, or else GWT has quietly changed the subject from phenomenal to access consciousness.
(7) Therefore either GWT is false, or it tells us nothing about phenomenal consciousness and contributes nothing to the hard problem.

I want badly to keep (1), because the broadcasting/ignition story fits what the anaesthetist described, and it feels like the only scientific story in town. But I can feel something sliding between (2) and (4), or maybe (5) is working harder than its entitled to. Which step would you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If the least bad option is to say GWT is only a theory of access consciousness, then what do you say to people who insist they're giving a scientific theory of consciousness?
