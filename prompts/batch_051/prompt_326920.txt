I've been in a reading group since I retired from the lab, and one of the members — Ted, a retired neurosurgeon with no patience for the literature — has very consistent gut answers on upload-and-identity cases. I wrote down his verdicts on four of them:

(1) Destructive upload: your brain is scanned in enough detail to run a perfect emulation, and the scan itself destroys the tissue; the emulation starts running afterward. Ted says the original dies then and there, and the emulation is a different person with the same memories.

(2) Gradual in-place replacement: over several years, your neurons are replaced one by one with silicon parts that preserve each neuron's connections and function; you're awake and talking through the whole process, with an unbroken sense of being yourself. Ted says it's obviously the same person the whole way — no death, no new person.

(3) Non-destructive copy: while your ordinary biological brain is running, a scan is made and an emulation is started from it in a computer. Ted says the biological one remains you, and the emulation is a seperate person from its first moment, even though it can't tell the difference.

(4) Overnight swap: you're anesthetized to the point that brain activity flatlines, and during the night the whole brain is replaced by a functionally equivalent hardware system; the next morning it wakes up with all your memories and personality. Ted says this is just the destructive upload again: the pre-op person dies in the shutdown, and the morning person is new.

We haven't gotten to cases where there's branching. So here's what I want to know. Take the person from (2), whom Ted counts as the original, and now fork the hardware they run on: a single continuous process splits into two, each with an unbroken experience stretching back through the pre-upload life. What does Ted say — both are the original, neither is, or exactly one is? I'm less interested in the label than in the rule. What are the candidate rules that fit his four verdicts, does his track record actually force one answer in the fork case, and if it doesn't, what further case would cleanly settle it?
