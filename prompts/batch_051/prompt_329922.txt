I am currently taking a course in the metaphysics of science, and after several weeks on laws of nature I find myself drawn to a broadly Humean position: there is no modal glue holding the universe together; laws are regularities and, on the version I now find attractive, they are simply the theorems of the best systematisation of the total mosaic of particular matters of fact, with best fixed by a trade-off between simplicity and strength. I would like you to play it straight and tell me what would count against this view. Which observations, possible cases, or results in adjacent debates (about counterfactuals, chance, explanation, or natural properties) should a committed best-system Humean themselves concede as counting against the position, rather than dismiss as question-begging? Please rank these defeaters from near-fatal to merely awkward, and say whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability and, if so, whether that is a problem for a view of this kind. I am less interested in exegesis of particular authors than in an honest assessment of how exposed the position actually is.
