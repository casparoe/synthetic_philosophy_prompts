I'm in a history of economic thought seminar and were reading William Stanley Jevons's Theory of Political Economy (1871), and I keep getting stuck on something nobody in the room wants to slow down for. The instructor described Jevons as still doing the old hedonic thing—utility as a felt quantity of pleasure—whereas the modern revealed-preference people (the Samuelson line) treat utility as just a label for whatever choices happen to reveal, with no introspection needed at all. But when I actually read Jevons, the two stories look tangled together in the very paragraph where he defines his terms. From the start of Chapter III, "Theory of Utility":

"By a commodity we shall understand any object, substance, action, or service, which can afford pleasure or ward off pain. The name was originally abstract, and denoted the quality of anything by which it was capable of serving man. Having acquired, by a common process of confusion, a concrete signification, it will be well to retain the word entirely for that signification, and employ the term utility to denote the abstract quality whereby an object serves our purposes, and becomes entitled to rank as a commodity. Whatever can produce pleasure or prevent pain may possess utility."

What I cannot decide: when Jevons calls utility an "abstract quality whereby an object serves our purposes," is that still a claim about the pleasure an object causes in a mind, or is it already shading into something like revealed preference—utility as whatever serves whatever purposes the person turns out to have? It dosen't get easier right after, because he says things that sound hedonic (utility as what "can produce pleasure or prevent pain"), and then, a few lines further on, says something close to: anything a person is found to desire and labour for must be assumed to possess utility for him. So is the criterion the object, the pleasure, or the wanting? Is Jevons confused in this paragraph, or is there a reading where these come apart consistently and I'm importing a later distinction? Walk me through what he is actually doing here: what should I take "utility" to mean by the end of the paragraph, and which of the three candidates is doing the real work?
