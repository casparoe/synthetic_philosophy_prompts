Act as a professional philosopher whose specialty is conceptual ethics and the philosophy of the social sciences. I've been developing a concept I call the consent-budget account of violence, and I want a rigorous but charitable appraisal of it, not a pep talk. Here is the claim. An act is violent to the extent that it imposes on someone who has not consented a cost consisting in damage to, blockage of, or commandeering of something that person is entitled to control, where the imposition does not occur under a standing arrangement the person can reasonably exit. Physical assault is the central case. On this account, consensual combat sports are not violent, and law enforcement is not violent when it operates inside rules that citizens could reject through ordinary politics; but severe forms of economic coercion that meet the test would turn out to be violent in a literal, not merely metaphorical, sense. The account is supposed to split the difference between an overly narrow view on which violence is only direct physical force and an overly broad view on which any avoidable harm counts as violence. It also, if accepted, recalibrates the familiar question whether the world is becoming less violent: a long decline in interpersonal killing may coexist with rising unconsented-to cost-transfer through economic and bureaucratic channels, so the overall answer depends on how transfers are measured.

Please do this in order. First, restate my view in its strongest, most precise form, fixing ambiguities without changing its core. Second, identify the nearest existing positions and say precisely where mine departs from them. Third, raise the three strongest objections—in particular to the 'reasonably exit' condition and to whether 'cost transfer' can be specified non-circularly—and say for each whether it is fatal or merely uncomfortable. Fourth, tell me to what extent accepting this account would turn the question whether the world is becoming less violent into a stipulation rather than an empirical discovery, and whether that should worry someone attracted to my account.
