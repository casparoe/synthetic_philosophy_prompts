Act as an expert on self-knowledge, rumination, and clinical psychology. Spend a bit of time brainstorming candidate principles before settling on one. I've spent years in rationalist-adjacent spaces, so I've absorbed two contradictory norms: do after-action reviews of everything, and also "rumination is the problem, get out of your head." I want a principle that tracks my gut verdicts on episodes of self-examination. For each episode I'm asking whether it was helpful or harmful overall.

1. After a friendship ended, I reconstructed a concrete timeline of what happened and what I could have done differently. Helpful, I learned something and moved on.
2. Same friendship: lying awake asking "why am I always the one who gets dropped, what is wrong with me." Harmful, stuck for months.
3. After flubbing a job interview, I reconstructed it question by question to find the mistakes. Helpful.
4. After a bad first date, I reconstructed it remark by remark to find the mistakes. Harmful, I just replayed the cringe and got more self-conscious, not less.
5. For a few months I kept a nightly journal asking whether I acted from my values or merely performed them. Harmful, it made me hesitant and drained.
6. Back when I was a socialist, I spent months steelmanning the best criticisms and tracing where my political views came from. Helpful, I changed on some points and trust the rest more.
7. After a fight with my mother, I spent a week asking whether my apology was genuine or just conflict-avoidance. Helpful for a day or two, then it became a loop where no motive felt real. Harmful overall.

My working rule is: concrete, backward-looking reconstruction of a closed event is good; abstract, open-ended "what am I like" dwelling is bad. But cases 3 and 4 look like they should be the same under that rule, and cases 6 and 7 are both motive-exploration, one good and one bad. Is there a principle that gets all seven, or close? If not, tell me which verdicts to drop. Then apply your principle to the eigth case I keep going back and forth on: a weekly review where I go through anything socially awkward from the past week and try to extract a lesson for next time. I can't tell, and its exactly the kind of habit that could be either a postmortem or a rumination.
