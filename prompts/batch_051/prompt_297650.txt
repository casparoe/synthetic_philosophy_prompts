"The indispensability argument says mathematics must describe real objects, because science cannot get by without it. Biologists explain the periodical cicada's 13- and 17-year life cycles by pointing out that 13 and 17 are prime: a prime-numbered cycle keeps the cicadas out of sync with the shorter, regular cycles of predators and parasites, so the cicadas dodge the years when predators synchronize. Biologists explain why honeybees build hexagonal cells by pointing out that the hexagonal grid is the most efficient way to divide a plane, using the least wax for a given storage space. These explanations cannot be restated without talking about numbers, primes, and geometrical objects. Numbers and primes are therefore indispensable to our best scientific theories. But a scientific theory earns our belief precisely in the entities it cannot stop talking about — that is why we believe in electrons and genes. So we have just as much reason to believe in numbers, primes, and hexagons as we have to believe in electrons and genes. The only way out is to insist that these cicada and honeycomb explanations are not real explanations at all; but evolution itself 'knows' the mathematics, so that starts to look desperate."
