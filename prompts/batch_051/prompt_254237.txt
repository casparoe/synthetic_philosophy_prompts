Ok so I'm a novelist and I'm stuck on a scene I've rewritten too many times. It's after a wedding, late, two people in a kitchen. Liam is about to make a hard call—maybe whether to take a job overseas—and Dana, who assigns probabilities to everything, tells him she'd put it at 75% that he goes. Then she pushes him: so what's your number? Liam says he can't have one, not from inside the choice. His own future act isn't an open empirical question he can stand back from while deciding; a high number would either be a prediction or it would just be the decision, and pretending otherwise is self-deception. Dana thinks that's special pleading—his future acts are just events in the world, and if he genuinely doesn't know what he'll do, he has a credence, and refusing to give one is hiding from his own uncertainty. Liam thinks she's smuggling in a third-person stance and that the whole point of deliberating is that the options stay live in a way a probability can't capture.

I want you to write the dialogue as a scene. Both of them have to be sharp and sympathetic—no strawman, please, and no one monologuing while the other just waits. Make them respond to the actual moves the other one just made. Let the dialogue surface what the real disagreement is—wether it's about self-knowledge, or what it means for an option to still be live, or the difference between the inside and the outside of a decision—but don't resolve it. Keep it short, like 800 words, mostly spoken lines with a little action. I can write the ending.
