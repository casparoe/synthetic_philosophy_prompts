The student wrote: "Rachels's bathtub example is meant to show that the bare distinction between killing and letting die has no intrinsic moral significance. Since the cases differ only in that Smith kills while Jones lets die, their moral equivalence shows that whether death is caused by an act or an omission cannot by itself make an action better or worse. It follows for Rachels that killing is never morally worse than letting die, and that active euthanasia is in general no more objectionable than passive euthanasia; the profession's blanket prohibition on active euthanasia is therefore inconsistent. Against the view that in passive euthanasia the doctor only lets the disease kill the patient, Rachels replies that the decision to withhold or withdraw treatment is itself an action, which means the doctor is just as much the cause of death as one who administers a lethal injection. Any remaining discomfort with active euthanasia, on his view, is just an irrational attachment to the vocabulary of killing and letting die."
