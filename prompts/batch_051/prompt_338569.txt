I've been trying to understand what "common knowledge of rationality" amounts to in game theory, and I keep getting stuck on a feature of the definition. It seems to require an infinite chain: each player is rational; each knows the other is rational; each knows that the other knows the other is rational; and so on with no stopping point. But real people have finite minds, and no one can actually entertain more than a few levels of such reasoning. If two players are each rational, and each knows the other is rational, and each knows that the other knows it, haven't we already got all the shared understanding that could matter in practice? Does treating the chain as endless rather than stopping after a few levels ever change what a rational player should do, or is "common knowledge" just a formal way of saying that everyone is on the same page? In your answer, please distinguish what is settled about this from what is genuinely contested among people who study it, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Please write in British English.
