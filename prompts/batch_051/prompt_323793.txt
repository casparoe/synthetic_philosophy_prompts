I read a popular science piece recently about pain asymbolia and what it is supposed to show about the badness of pain, and I want to know how much of it to trust. The piece summarizes the phenomenon and its significance roughly like this:

"Pain asymbolia is a rare neuropsychological condition in which patients can still report that a stimulus is painful, locate it, and rate its intensity, but they no longer seem to mind the pain. They do not wince, withdraw, or complain, and they may even laugh during a procedure that would make anyone else cry. This shows that what is bad about pain is not the sensory signal itself, which is fully preserved in these patients, but a separate emotional-evaluative layer that the brain normally adds on top of the raw sensation. In pain asymbolia that layer is disconnected, so the patients feel the pain and yet it does not hurt. The unpleasantness of pain, on this picture, just is the brain's way of saying 'this is bad for you.' These patients have lost that message while keeping the underlying sensation, which is why people sometimes say they feel pain without suffering. It also explains why the condition is taken to show that the badness of pain is an evaluation rather than anything in the sensation itself, and why the patients, once the evaluation is gone, have nothing left to be distressed by."

Something in this summary feels off, but the piece was written by someone with scientific credentials, so I want an audit rather than a verdict from my hunches. Please go claim by claim and separate what is accurate, what is a common simplification, and what is genuinely contested among philosophers and scientists who work on pain. In particular, tell me whether the move from the clinical reports (no wincing, no withdrawal, no complaint, and so on) to the conclusion that the badness of pain is an evaluation is actually supported, or whether that is one theoretical interpretation among several; say what someone who defends the article's evaluativist reading would reply. Throughout, separate what is settled from what is contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
