I'm trying to decide whether a particular training signal counts as process-based or outcome-based supervision, and I suspect the usual distinction is less clean than people pretend. I'll stipulate three criteria. A signal is process-based to the extent that (1) it is a function of the intermediate steps rather than of the final output alone; (2) two trajectories that reach the same final output can receive different feedback; and (3) the feedback tells the model something usable about which specific steps were good or bad. A signal is outcome-based to the extent that it is a function only of the final output.

Here is the case. A language model is being trained on problems whose final answers can be checked automatically. A reward model is trained only on completed solutions, labeled by final-answer correctness, so it maps a full solution to a score. During training, each intermediate state is then scored by sampling many completions from that state and taking the highest reward-model score among them, as a value estimate for the state. As a result, every step receives a score; a step that makes a correct final answer unlikely is scored badly even if the particular trajectory happens to end in the right answer, and a step that makes a correct answer nearly certain is scored well. But every step score is derived from a reward model that was itself trained only on final outcomes.

Is the resulting per-step signal process-based supervision, outcome-based supervision, or neither? Work through my three criteria one at a time, say exactly where they leave the case undetermined, and then say whether the criteria need revision or whether the process/outcome contrast itself is not exhaustive. Keep the response to at most 400 words.
