I just started volunteering with a water rescue team, and in training they threw a scenario at us that I can't stop chewing on: your boat can reach one side of a channel and pull out one trapped person, or the other side and pull out five, water's rising, no time for both. The trainer brushed it off with "save the five, obviously," but a friend later told me about Taurek's view — that the numbers shouldn't settle it and that you should flip a coin so the single person gets a real shot. I keep going back and forth, and I'd rather watch the two positions actually argue than read a balanced summary.

So: write a dialogue between two equally competent rescuers in the boat, one defending Taurek-style numbers-don't-count (somewhere from "flip a coin" to "bare headcount isn't a reason at all"), the other defending "save the five." I want genuine back-and-forth where they answer each other's actual points, not two adjacent monologues. Make each of them steelman the other and concede at least one subpoint. Pressure them on the hard spots: can the five complain if the coin lands against them; what happens at one versus a million; does a weighted lottery (five-sixths for the five) fix fairness or just smuggle aggregation back in; would the one person consent to a rule that always saves the five; and is there a difference between rescuing people already in danger and dividing up a good sitting in front of you. Dont settle it by fiat — let the strain show on both sides.
