I'd like a working definition of understanding — one that separates genuinely grasping a subject from merely being able to extract correct answers from a system, including a system composed of yourself plus an AI. I find this distinction under pressure now that a large share of my intellectual work consists of prompting, checking, and editing model output. Give me a definition that correctly classifies each of these seven cases:

- A student who can solve novel physics problems by prompting a language model and checking the answers dimensionally, but cannot reproduce the derivation unaided.
- A mathematician who can fluently re-prove a theorem she has used for a decade, though she can no longer say what originally motivated the crucial step.
- A doctor who prescribes safely by querying an interaction database and can explain the relevant pharmacological categories, but not the underlying chemistry.
- A translator who produces good translations only by correcting a language model's draft, reliably catching wrong nuance but unable to render the source unaided at that speed.
- A child who catches a thrown ball consistently but can offer no account of ballistics.
- A lookup table that answers every item in its fixed test set correctly and replies random nonsense to one held-out item.
- A former expert who has lost the details of her field but still knows which questions to ask and can reliably tell when an AI's answers are wrong.

I am confident the lookup table lacks understanding and the mathematician has it, and I lean toward saying the child understands in a predictive-motor way. The doctor, the translator, and the former expert are the genuinely hard cases for me. Propose the definition, run it through all seven, tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and revise it or defend the surprising verdicts.
