The dark forest hypothesis has been argued over for years without anyone changing their mind. One side treats it as a game-theoretic prediction: a civilisation faces unavoidable uncertainty about other civilisations' technology, intentions, and ability to coordinate; any signal could be a lure, detection may be one-sided, and the cost of being found is existential, so silence or pre-emption is the uniquely safe strategy and the observed silence of the universe is just what rational caution predicts. The other side replies that this follows only if one assumes civilisations cannot communicate or credibly commit, cannot distinguish a threat from a benign contact, and gain nothing from trade or information; weaken any of those assumptions and the conclusion is no longer forced. I suspect the real divide is not about whether the universe is empty but about which idealisations are legitimate in a model of rational agents when extinction is at stake.
