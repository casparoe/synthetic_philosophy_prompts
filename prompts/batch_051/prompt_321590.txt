A colleague keeps arguing that a sufficiently sophisticated reinforcement learning agent can literally suffer, on the grounds that a reward signal just is what valence is in an animal: positive reward is pleasure, negative reward is pain, and once the reward signal tracks the right states, the lights are on. The analogy doing the work is that the reward signal is to an RL agent what valence is to an animal, and he treats it as an identity claim rather than a loose metaphor, so the right reward architecture is sufficient for suffering. Audit how far this analogy carries: map what corresponds to what between biological valence and a scalar RL reward signal; identify where the mapping breaks; say which features of felt valence the picture quietly imports that a reward signal need not supply; separate the disanalogies that matter for his conclusion from the red herrings; and state whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If it does not survive, say what would have to be added before reward would amount to suffering. End with a line beginning Verdict: and give, in one short phrase, how far the analogy carries (for example, Verdict: instrumental analogy only or Verdict: carries as stated).
