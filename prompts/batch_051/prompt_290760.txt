Consider an analogy widely used in philosophy of biology: a species is not a class or natural kind but an individual — a particular, spatiotemporally continuous lineage whose organisms are parts rather than members. The analogy used to defend this is that a species is like an organism: speciation is its birth, extinction its death, and individual organisms are like the organism's cells. The work the analogy is made to do is anti-essentialist: if species are particulars, there need be no property shared by all and only a species' members throughout its existence, and a species can change while remaining the same species. Audit how far this organism analogy actually holds. Give an explicit correspondence table showing what in the organism maps onto what in the species and whether each mapping works; then separate the disanalogies that matter for the anti-essentialist conclusion from those that do not; identify what the organism picture quietly imports that species lack; and say whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. After the analysis, provide a structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: correspondences (list of objects with fields source, target, maps_well, note), disanalogies (list of objects with fields disanalogy, matters_for_conclusion, why), imported_assumptions (list of strings), verdict (object with fields conclusion_survives and summary).
