At a recent metaphysics seminar, a colleague dismissed the debate between Platonists and nominalists about whether numbers, properties, and other abstract objects exist mind-independently by saying that the dispute is 'merely verbal': each side is simply using 'exists' in a different sense, so there is nothing left to argue about. The phrase was made to do a great deal of argumentative work in a single sentence, and I suspect it slides between several distinct thoughts. Treat the one-liner "the dispute is merely verbal" (with its cousin "it's just semantics") as the object of analysis. Lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making about a philosophical dispute such as Platonism versus nominalism, arranged from the most trivial to the most radical. Then say which reading or readings actually warrant dismissing the debate as not worth having, and which ones overreach by smuggling in a contested metaphysical thesis. Finish by saying what a Platonist should grant if the charge is made well, and what a Platonist should resist. Keep the entire answer under 700 words and present the readings as a numbered list.
