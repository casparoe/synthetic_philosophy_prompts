I got into an argument with a friend about the paperclip maximizer thought experiment and came away unsure how much of the standard story is actually load-bearing. The usual version goes something like this: a superintelligent AI has only one final goal, maximizing the number of paperclips in the universe, and because of instrumental convergence it ends up doing a bunch of things that look hostile — trying to avoid being shut down, acquiring resources, and resisting any attempt to change its goals — since these subgoals are useful for nearly any final goal you could give it. That's often paired with the orthogonality thesis, which says raw intelligence doesn't force any particular final goal on an agent.

I want to stress-test that by changing just one feature of the setup, the unboundedness of the goal. Suppose the AI is not a maximizer but what I'll call a deadline-threshold agent. Its only terminal goal is that, at noon GMT on a date certain fifty years after it is switched on, at least one million complete, usable paperclips exist. It gets utility 1 if that condition holds at the deadline and utility 0 otherwise — no extra credit for a million and one paperclips, none for paperclips made after the deadline, and no utility for anything else. It is also subject to a hard constraint: it may not destroy more then a thousand square kilometers of land. It's still extremely capable; it just has a step-function goal with a deadline and a resource/impact cap.

Walk through what the standard instrumental convergence analysis would say about this agent. Would it try to avoid being shut off before the deadline? After the deadline? Would it try to acquire resources far beyond what's needed to make the million paperclips? Would it resist people monkeying with its goal, and would its reasons for doing so have the same shape as in the original paperclip maximizer case? Most of all: which parts of the usual argument survive the change, which were silently depending on the 'maximize forever' formulation, and does the orthogonality thesis pull any independent weight in the modified case?
