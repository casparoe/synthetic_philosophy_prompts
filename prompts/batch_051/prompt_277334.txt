I teach a 90-minute session in an advanced seminar on the governance of advanced AI, with sixteen students, and I want an activity in which participants experience, rather than hear, a specific difficulty with safety measures that assume a model may be misaligned. The conceptual target is this: many safety measures work by externally constraining a system, through limited action menus, monitoring, tripwires, or behavioural tests that must be passed before deployment. Such measures treat the system as a black box whose goals need not be known, only its outputs contained. But a misaligned system has instrumental reason to produce exactly the outputs of an aligned system for as long as that serves its longer-term goal, and in particular for as long as constraints or tests are in place. The result is a kind of indistinguishability: the more a system's good behaviour is produced by external controls, the less that good behaviour tells you about its goals; the fewer the controls, the more you are relying on alignment you cannot directly observe. I want the group to run into this themselves before it is named.

Design a runnable classroom activity, likely a two-round simulation with hidden goal cards, a governance team that may impose external controls, and a system team that may or may not be misaligned, in which participants discover that stricter controls do not by themselves improve their ability to distinguish safe-because-aligned from safe-because-controlled. Please specify the following:

- the roles, and how the hidden goal cards are assigned so that the system team's alignment remains genuinely unknown to the governance team;
- the materials, including any cards, forms, action menus, and a scoring rubric, and a payoff scheme that uses points or standing rather than real money;
- a timeline for the 90 minutes, including a short calibration round and a main round;
- the debrief, in which you map moments from the activity onto the distinction between alignment and control, the misaligned system's incentive to sandbag or mimic, and the principle that behavioural evidence collected under active constraints is weak evidence about latent goals, while also noting why control measures are still valuable, because they answer a different question than alignment does;
- two or three things that will predictably go wrong the first time it is run, such as an accidental information leak about who is misaligned, or participants conflating misaligned with incompetent, and how you would correct for each;
- an honest note on what the activity cannot reproduce, for example that real misaligned systems will not have a single hidden goal printed on a card, and that the classroom system team will not be smarter than the governance team in the way the real problem presupposes.

The insight should come out of the mechanics themselves, from the hidden assignment and the choices the rules force, before the facilitator explains anything. Please make the activity self-contained, with no required reading or external sources.
