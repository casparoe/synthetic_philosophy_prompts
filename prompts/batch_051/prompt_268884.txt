I'm a third-year philosophy major taking a seminar on selfhood and the first-person perspective. We have been working through Metzinger's account of the phenomenal self-model: the view that what we call the self is not a thing or a substance but a transparent representational model of the organism as a whole, and that transparency just is the property that prevents the system from experiencing the model as a model—it simply lives through it. For my final paper I want to read Being John Malkovich through this lens, because the film seems to literalize several of the theory's commitments while also, I suspect, betraying them. I would like a critical reading that works in both directions. Can the portal be productively interpreted as a metaphor for the transparency of the phenomenal self-model, or does the film inevitably fall back into a homuncular or Cartesian picture in which a little self or point of view travels from one head to another? Does the film illuminate Metzinger's claim that the felt self is generated, body-relative, and potentially replaceable, or does it surreptitiously presuppose a substantive I that survives every transfer? Where does the film dramatize what it would be like for a self-model to become opaque, and where does it trade on spatial or theatrical metaphors that Metzinger's account is meant to replace? I am not looking for a literature review or citations; I want a reading that treats the film and the theory with equal seriousness and is willing to say where the film resists the theory rather than forcing it into an illustration.
