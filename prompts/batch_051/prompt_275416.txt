A city, persuaded by Dewey's experimentalism, passes a rule requiring every new ordinance to be written as a hypothesis. Each ordinance must specify in advance: the concrete harm it targets, a measurable indicator of that harm, a falsifiable prediction about how the indicator will move, and a fixed expiry date. When the ordinance expires, a randomly selected citizens' review panel, not the city government, assesses whether the prediction was borne out; if it was not, the ordinance lapses automatically unless the council re-enacts it with a revised hypothesis. Treat this as a mechanism, not a moral position. Analyze how it would actually behave: what it incentivizes for legislators, agencies, and the citizens on the panel; where it is gamed first; what happens to important harms that resist short-horizon measurement; and whether the failure modes can be fixed without abandoning the experimentalist core.
