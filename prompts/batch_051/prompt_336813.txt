I am giving a talk at a philosophy of mind workshop in a few weeks, and I want to be ready for the question period. The thesis I am defending is that Searle's distinction between original and derived intentionality cannot do the work he asks of it. I will argue that intentionality comes in degrees, and that the intentionality of a human belief, a word-processing program, and a vending machine's two-bitser is in each case fixed by design, history, and functional role; what Searle calls original intentionality is, on my view, only the limiting case of the intentional stance. My central move is Dennett's two-bitser reply to the Chinese Room. I will press the contrast between simulating a hurricane and simulating a two-bitser: a simulation of a hurricane is not a hurricane, but a two-bitser wired up to a coin slot and a supply of Coca-Cola is a real two-bitser even if its internals run on a program, because being a two-bitser is a functional role rather than an intrinsic physical kind. I will then argue that Searle has given no reason to think being a Chinese-understander or a believer is more like being a hurricane than like being a two-bitser.

The audience is mostly philosophers of mind: a Searle contingent that treats the Chinese Room as having shown that syntax is never sufficient for semantics and that only certain biological processes have intrinsic intentionality; several junior faculty sympathetic to teleosemantics, who reject Searle's biological chauvinism but still want a naturalistic notion of original intentionality; and a few cognitive scientists who will worry that my view cannot make sense of misrepresentation. Prepare me for the question period. Give me the objections in roughly the order they are likely to come, phrased the way these people would themselves phrase them, with what I should concede and what I should hold. Tell me the objection I should most hope nobody raises, and where my reply to it is weakest. In all of this, please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people working on intentionality, and do not present a contested reading—for instance, about what the Chinese Room actually establishes—as the consensus.
