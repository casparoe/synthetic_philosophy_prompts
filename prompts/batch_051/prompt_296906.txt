I spent most of yesterday arguing with a friend who works in clinical research ethics, and I came away with an argument that seems to prove too much, but I can't find the exit. It runs like this. (1) A physician may enroll a patient in a randomized controlled trial only if she is genuinely uncertain whether the experimental treatment or the control is better for a patient like this one. (2) Genuine uncertainty cannot mean a perfect fifty-fifty split in her judgment, since that almost never happens; it must mean she lacks evidence sufficient to make one arm rationally preferable to the other, so that either arm is a reasonable choice for the patient. (3) In any real trial, however, there is almost always enough evidence from earlier studies, biological mechanism, or accumulated clinical experience to support at least a weak preference for one arm — that prior evidence is usually exactly why the trial is being run. (4) So, by (2) and (3), the condition in (1) is almost never satisfied. (5) Yet if we refuse to randomize until it is satisfied, we never run trials, and medicine remains stuck with the weak hunches that the whole enterprise of trials was supposed to replace. (6) The natural fix is to shift from individual to community uncertainty: a trial is ethical when responsible expert clinicians disagree about which arm is preferable, even if the physician at the bedside has a private leaning. (7) But that seems to license a physician to randomize a patient even while she is fairly confident that one arm is worse, and directing a patient toward what you believe is the worse option looks like a betrayal of the duty to the patient in front of you.

So we seem forced to choose between an individual standard that forbids nearly all trials and a community standard that permits knowingly doing worse by one's own patients. I would like to keep both the duty of individual care and the possibility of running at least some randomized trials, but the argument suggests I cannot have both. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it commit me to? Please say how confident you are in your diagnosis, and single out the point you are least sure of.
