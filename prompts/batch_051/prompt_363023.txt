I am a journalist who covers science and philosophy, and I am applying for a nine-month fellowship at a research institute where the deliverable is a long-form essay in the philosophy of animal welfare. I will need to submit a one-page research proposal. My reporting over the past year has focused on aquaculture, and I keep noticing the same unresolved tension in the welfare-science literature and at industry conferences: the field has extended welfare assessment from terrestrial livestock to teleost fish and decapod crustaceans without settling which underlying concept of welfare is doing the work, and the standard equipment for doing so was built for animals whose capacity to suffer was assumed in advance.

My half-formed idea is that the field's conspicuous debate about whether fish and crustaceans are sentient has crowded out a more tractable conceptual question. The three standard pictures of animal welfare—welfare as subjective feeling, welfare as biological functioning, and welfare as natural living—come apart sharply once they are applied to animals whose subjective states are precisely what is contested. A feelings-based definition threatens to make welfare assessment of fish and decapods circular or question-begging. A functioning-based definition can in principle be applied to insentient systems and so cannot by itself ground the moral weight the field claims for its findings. A natural-living definition is nearly impossible to make determinate inside recirculating tanks and intensively managed ponds. Yet certification protocols and stunning standards appear to borrow from all three pictures opportunistically, which lets producers and regulators certify incremental welfare gains without committing to any view about sentience. I want the fellowship project to ask whether a coherent concept of welfare can underwrite aquaculture welfare assessment before the sentience question is settled, and, if so, what that concept looks like under graded, empirically uncertain sentience.

Please turn this into a one-page proposal. It should state the question precisely; explain why the existing positions—the three definitions of welfare and the sentience-focused empirical debate—leave it open; specify an approach that is conceptual and reconstructive, treating actual assessment criteria as case studies whose presuppositions can be excavated, rather than another review of the pain-and-nociception literature, with a realistic month-by-month plan for nine months; say what a successful outcome would establish and what would count as the project failing; and include a short answer to someone who asks why a foundation should fund philosophical analysis rather than more laboratory work here. Do not include citations or a bibliography; I will add references later, so please just describe positions and arguments in general terms.
