I have a set of verdicts about which artificial systems have anything worth calling wants, and I would like to know whether one principled line runs through them. I do not think a thermostat wants the room to be twenty-one degrees, though it acts until it reaches that temperature. I do not think a chess engine wants to win, though it selects moves that maximize its evaluation. I think a reinforcement-learning agent that builds a world model and plans routes to a charger does, in a minimal sense, want to remain charged. But I do not think a large language model fine-tuned by reinforcement to be helpful wants to help, even though it pursues helpfulness across a conversation; I also notice this puts pressure on the contrast with the charging agent, since both are trained to increase a reward signal. I do not think the creatures in an evolutionary simulation want anything, even though they behave as if they do; there, the wanting seems to belong to the selection process rather than to the creature. What principle, if any, captures all of these verdicts? What does it predict for an agent whose reward circuit is rewired every few minutes, a model given a persisting preference once but no possibility of reward at runtime, or an animal that behaves exactly like a reinforcement learner but cannot report desire? If no reasonably simple principle gets every case, which of my verdicts should I give up, and why?
