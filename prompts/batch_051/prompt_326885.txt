Write an essay on the following problem for the doctrine of divine simplicity. According to that doctrine, God has no composition of any kind: God is not made of parts, and God is identical with each of God's attributes rather than merely possessing them. So God is not only omniscient; God is omniscience itself, and likewise omnipotence itself, perfect goodness itself, and so on. The problem is this. If God is identical with omniscience and identical with omnipotence, then omniscience and omnipotence are identical with each other. Repeating the same reasoning for every attribute seems to collapse all of the divine attributes into one, so that talk of God's attributes as a genuine plurality would be an illusion. Weigh the main ways a defender of divine simplicity might try to block this collapse, and state clearly what each response costs. Then defend a verdict: either the doctrine can be maintained in a form that preserves a real plurality of divine attributes, or the collapse is an unavoidable consequence that gives reason to reject or substantially revise the doctrine. The essay must be no more than 500 words.
