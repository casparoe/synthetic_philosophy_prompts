In a graduate seminar I teach on artificial minds and moral status, the discussion keeps collapsing into a question none of us has managed to state precisely enough to answer. Let me put it to you as a decomposition exercise. The proposition I want your credence in is this: at least one reinforcement learning system of the kind that currently exists — a deep network trained to adjust its action probabilities in the direction of positive scalar reward and away from negative scalar reward — has at least some states that are negatively valenced in the sense relevant to whether the system can suffer. I am not asking whether we owe such systems moral consideration, and I am not asking whether they possess full phenomenal consciousness; I am asking only about the narrower claim that, in at least one such system, the punishment signal is doing something valence-like rather than serving as a scalar that merely reshapes a policy.

Please give me an actual number, not a refusal on the grounds that the question is underdefined, and decompose that number into the sub-questions it depends on. To seed the decomposition, I would distinguish these. First, the probability that the badness of pain is fully functional — that aversion, avoidance learning, attention capture, and the like exhaust what matters about suffering — rather than requiring an additional qualitative residue. Second, the probability that a negative reward signal in at least one current architecture realizes those functional roles in the right way, rather than serving only as an error signal that adjusts which behaviors are repeated or avoided. Third, the probability that realizing those roles suffices for morally significant negative valence even when the system lacks a rich self-model, narrative memory, or a unified point of view. Fourth, the probability that at least one current system lies above the threshold your answers to the first three questions imply. Assign a rough probability to each, show how they combine to produce your overall credence, and then tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to and what evidence or argument would move that term the most.

I am less interested in the particular number than in forcing the reasoning into the open; if you think I have omitted a sub-question or mischaracterized the relevant functional role, say so and redistribute the credence accordingly.
