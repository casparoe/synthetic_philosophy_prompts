The biological immune system and the purity/sanctity side of morality—the side organized around disgust, contamination, and the sacred—look to me as though they share more than a loose metaphor. Both maintain a boundary between a protected self and an outside marked as foreign or unclean; both treat harm as contamination, so that mere contact, not just injury, registers as a threat; both keep memory, so that the history of exposure shapes present reactions, and in the moral case taint can be inherited or transferred across generations; and both have characteristic failures: allergies treat harmless substances as hostile, autoimmunity attacks the body's own tissue, and moral purity systems can do the analogues of both, panicking over harmless contact and purging internal polluters. I am trying to decide whether this is a genuine identity of structure or only a suggestive resemblance. What would have to be true of each system for a result to transfer from one to the other—say, the way induced tolerance quiets an immune overreaction—into something usable in moral psychology? And name one specific prediction the parallel makes that is false on at least one side.
