Half the time they look great in the historical data and then blow up the moment we try to use them going forward. The classic one last year was a segment of users who signed up before we changed the pricing page: that whole pre-pricing-change cohort looked super sticky in the data, but thats just an accident of when things happened, not a real trait of the users.

I heard somewhere about an old philosophy puzzle involving "grue"—a predicate meaning green before some date and blue after, which is just as confirmed by everything seen so far as "green" is, even though nobody would use it to predict. Then run it on three examples: (1) "users who signed up before the pricing change churn less," (2) "users who use the search box in their first week churn less," and (3) one more you think the procedure will reject even though we'd probably be attached to it.
