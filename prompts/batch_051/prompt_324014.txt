For a unit on well-being theory I need a case built to spec. Construct a realistic, ordinary-life scenario — meditation, therapy, career burnout and the like are fine, but no sci-fi, no demons, no desire-rewriting pills — where tranquilism and actualist desire-satisfaction theory come apart in a specfic direction: tranquilism says the change improves the person's life, desire-satisfaction says it makes the person's life worse. By tranquilism I mean the view that the only thing of intrinsic value is the absence of craving and suffering, so once those are gone nothing can make your life better and having no pleasures or satisfied desires is no deficit. Actualist desire-satisfaction says well-being is getting what you actually desire. The change should eliminate one craving while leaving behind a standing desire that the person thereafter no longer satisfies, and the case should make clear that this desire would have been satisfied without the change. Then do the check: give the relevant mental states before and after, state each theory’s verdict and why it gives it, then identify the single feature of the case doing the real work, say what each theory would say if that leftover desire were removed too, and end with how a defender of desire-satisfaction would attack the case as rigged. Put the final answer in the form \answer{...} with the working feature as a short phrase.
