I'm a PhD student in philosophy of biology, and I've been spending a lot of time in a fly lab lately just to watch how molecular biologists actually talk. One thing keeps tripping me up: the grad students will say an allele "codes for" a slightly altered protein without a second thought, but when I repeated that in my philosophy reading group two people basically refused to let me finish the sentence. Their line is that DNA isnt literally a code, there's no sender, no receiver, no one doing the encoding, and treating the sequence-to-protein mapping as "information" quietly imports a metaphysics the biochemistry doesn't license. The biologists I've talked to find this baffling: there is a real table of codon correspondences, with redundancy and wobble and all that, and if you forbid code-talk you can't state a good chunk of molecular biology. This argument has been going on in one form or another for decades and neither side seems to have moved.

I'm not asking you to tell me who's right. I want a diagnosis of the stalemate. Is this a merely verbal dispute where "code" and "information" mean different things in different registers? Is there a hidden factual question doing the work, like whether the sequence-structure relation is arbitrary in the way a symbol system requires? Or is it a disagreement about what counts as an explanation in biology, or about when a metaphor has earned the right to be literal? My working hypothesis is that the two sides are mostly answering different questions under one word, and that no experiment about codon reassignment or the origin of translation would settle it, but I could be wrong. What kind of disagreement is this actually, and what would count as progress given the diagnosis, or is stalling just what we should expect? Please write it as continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, like an email.
