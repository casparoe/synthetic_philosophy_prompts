As a journalist who covers science and ideas, I keep coming back to a thought experiment I first met as Thomson's lamp. A lamp is off at time zero. After one minute it is switched on; after another half minute, off; after another quarter minute, on; and so on, with each wait being half the previous one. Since 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + ... converges to 2, all the flips are scheduled to be finished by the two-minute mark. At two minutes, though, the lamp cannot be off, because every off state is followed by a later on state; and it cannot be on, because every on state is followed by a later off state. Yet a real lamp must be in exactly one of those two states.

The view I am drawn to is that the completed supertask is coherent. The mathematics of convergent series applies to time in the most straightforward way; each flip is individually well-defined; and a process is completed once all its steps have occurred, even if there is no last step. The mathematics does not owe us a final flick of the switch. But I also cannot shake the intuition that after two minutes the switch is somewhere: it is on or it is off, and a story about a physical lamp that ends without assigning one of those states is not completed but incomplete.

I do not want a verdict that simply waves the case off as pathological. I want a negotiated decision about which side should yield, or whether the two can be held together under some reading. Please structure the response as follows:

- **The setup made precise** (about 150 words): Restate exactly what is true at each flip, why the total duration is finite, and why the state after two minutes appears to be undetermined by the description.

- **The strongest case for the completed supertask** (about 250 words): Give the best defense of the claim that the process can be coherently completed even though the sequence of lamp states does not converge. Bring out the systematic virtues of the theory, not just its mathematical convenience.

- **The strongest case for the firm intuition** (about 250 words): Give the best defense of the claim that a physical lamp must end up on or off, and explain what this requirement tells us about which supertasks are coherent.

- **What it would take for each side to give way** (about 350 words): Spell out the conditions under which the intuition should be dropped as an illicit demand for a final step in a process that has none, and the conditions under which the theory should be revised or restricted — for example, by requiring every relevant physical quantity to converge as a condition on a supertask's coherence. Distinguish the moves that would justify each surrender from the moves that would merely relabel it.

- **Negotiated verdict** (about 300 words): Lead with the bottom line, then give the reading on which both sides survive if one exists, or say which side should give way and why. If the right conclusion is that the original story is incomplete rather than contradictory, say what was missing.
