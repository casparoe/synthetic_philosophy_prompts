I have been a teaching assistant for the same philosophy of science course for three years, and I have started keeping a private list of my professor's snap verdicts about what counts as science, because they are far more stable than her official lectures about there being no single criterion. She will say, without hesitation, that Newtonian mechanics, Darwinian natural selection, and plate tectonics are science; that Ptolemaic astronomy was science, though false; that Freudian psychoanalysis, Marxist theory of history, astrology, homeopathy, and intelligent design are not science; and that string theory is science, notwithstanding its lack of confirmed experimental predictions so far. The thing I cannot work out is what single principle does all of that work. She plainly is not using truth or predictive success as her test, since she counts Ptolemaic astronomy as science and does not require confirmed predictions from string theory. But she also will not count homeopathy, which is at least testable, so she is not a straightforward falsificationist either.

The new case came up in office hours last week. A student described a research program in parapsychology that studies whether focused human attention can bias the output of well-shielded random number generators. Methodologically, the program is exemplary by the standards the course has been teaching: hypotheses are pre-registered, null results are published, the data are public, and when a predicted effect fails to replicate, the researchers revise the model rather than explaining the failure away. The awkward feature is that the claimed effect would be drastically inconsistent with the rest of physics: it appears not to weaken with distance, shielding makes no difference to it, and there is no candidate mechanism for it. The professor said she would give her ruling next week.

Before she does, I want to work out what she should say if she is to stay consistent with her own record. What are the defensible rules of demarcation that fit all of her prior verdicts? Which of those rules genuinely diverge on the parapsychology program, and what verdict does each predict? Give your best prediction of her actual verdict, with the reason you prefer that underlying rule over the alternatives, and then identify one further case, deliberately designed to sit exactly on the fault line, that would cleanly settle which rule she has been running.
