Here's a question I put on an intro bioethics midterm, plus a student's answer. I need a second grader. Grade it out of 20, and give me the specific marginal comments you'd write — where it earns marks, where it goes wrong, and what costs it. Don't just tell me your general impression.

Question: "Explain how Thomson's violinist argument and Marquis's future-like-ours argument relate to each other. If Thomson's argument is sound, does it refute Marquis's? Why or why not?"

Student answer: "Thomson's violinist case shows that even if the violinist is a person with a right to life, his right to life doesn't include the right to use your kidneys for nine months, so you can unplug yourself. Applied to pregnancy, this means that even if the fetus is a person, it doesn't have a right to use the woman's body, so abortion is permissible. Marquis's future-like-ours argument says abortion is wrong because it deprives the fetus of a future full of experiences, activities, and projects like ours. Since that future has value, destroying it is a major wrong. Thomson's argument refutes Marquis: Marquis only argues that the fetus is a potential person, so his whole argument depends on the idea that the woman has a duty to sustain it. Once Thomson shows there is no such duty, Marquis has nothing left. They don't really speak past each other; Thomson just wins."
