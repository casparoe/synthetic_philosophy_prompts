Here's an asymmetry I can't get straight. "What if everyone did that?" lands with full force against the guy who jumps the turnstile, the non-voter, and the neighbor running his sprinklers during a drought. But it sounds like a non-sequitur when aimed at someone choosing to become a philosophy professor, move to the same overheated city everyone else is moving to, or not have kids — even though the worlds where everyone does *those* are at least as bad or worse. What exactly is the supposed difference? Is it a real distinction between kinds of maxims (Kant's contradiction-in-conception vs. mere bad consequences), or the Hare/Singer point that universalizing a prescription isn't a prediction about what would happen if everyone acted on it at once — or is "what if everyone did that?" just rhetoric we wheel out when we already think the act is wrong? Give me the cleanest line you can, then run it against a genuinely hard case and tell me wether the asymmetry survives.
