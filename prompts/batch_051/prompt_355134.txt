Two members of my disinformation working group have been arguing for a year about deepfakes and I've been asked to mediate. One of them holds what he calls the backstop collapse view: ordinary people and institutions use photos, video and leaked audio as a last-resort check on official and secondhand testimony, a defeasible but real epistemic backstop for settling what happened; once cheap generative media eat through it, we lose not just particular pieces of evidence but the background sense that some recordings are untouched, and that background sense is what makes any verification fight winnable in the first place. The other holds what she calls the scaffolding view: the self-authenticating photo was already a folk myth, because what actually did the checking in courts, newsrooms and emergency rooms was chains of custody, institutional gatekeepers and cross-reference with other records; deepfakes mostly change the price of verification and shift more of that load onto institutions, they don't collapse a backstop that was doing its work unaided.

Write the joint statement the two could sign after a good faith adversarial collaboration, formatted as (1) a numbered agreed-claims section and (2) a disagreements table. The agreed claims should be substantive, not mush: they should include claims about where chains of custody already do the real epistemic work, about what manipulation is versus merely unattested provenance, and about the difference between a fabricated recording and an authentic one that can no longer be used because its source can credibly cry fake. Then the table: each row a subquestion, with a neutral wording both sign, a column with his exact position, a column with hers, each written so its holder would accept it, and a column flagging whether the row is a real disagreement or just a wording problem. Then list the cruxes: the subquestions such that if one of them were settled, at least one of these two would change their view. I'd bet money the cruxes include weather the Photoshop transition is a good precedent for generative video, whether the main damage is people believing fakes or people successfully denying real recordings, and whether provenance infrastructure like signed cameras and ledgers could restore whatever was lost. For each crux, give a concrete legal or journalistic or historical case both would accept as bearing on it, and record what each side predicts that case would show if fully litigated. Don't resolve their dispute and don't smuggle in a verdict under the guise of neutral wording.
