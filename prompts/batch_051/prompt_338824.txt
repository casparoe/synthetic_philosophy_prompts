I am a novelist, and in the book I am currently drafting a warehouse engineer and a philosopher argue over whether the robot the engineer maintains, which whines, limps, and learns to avoid the pallet that struck it, genuinely feels pain or merely behaves as though it does. For the central chapter I want their dispute to turn on one isolable structural difference between two machines rather than on science-fictional whole-brain emulation or blanket intuitions about silicon, and I would like you to construct a thought experiment to the following specification for use as the armature of that chapter. The case is for a novel, so the verdicts must be generated from the two theories rather than stipulated by the setup.

Construct a pair of systems, A and B, beginning from a single recurrent network that implements a defensible computational profile of felt pain: a sensory-discriminative mapping from tissue-damage-like input to an aversive appraisal state; a causal connection from appraisal to withdrawal motivation; and a one-trial aversive learning rule. Derive A by cutting every internal feedback path so that appraisal and withdrawal are driven purely feed-forward; A should retain the complete pain-relevant input–output behaviour and the learning update, but its components should constrain one another so weakly that, by the lights of integrated information theory, its integrated information is negligible. Derive B by cutting both the sensory inputs and the outputs to the withdrawal and learning machinery while leaving the recurrent core intact and spontaneously active; B should have substantial integrated information but no pain-relevant input–output role at all, and it need not be capable of anything we would recognise as goal-directed conduct. The scenario must be one in which a computational functionalist, who holds that felt pain is a matter of implementing the right pain-related computational role, is committed to attributing felt pain to A and not to B, while a defender of integrated information theory, who holds that experience tracks the amount of causally integrated information in a system, is committed to attributing some experience to B and not to A.

After describing the scenario, work through the reasoning for each theory and each verdict, making explicit why each verdict follows from the theory rather than from a stipulation in the setup. Then isolate the single feature of the pair that does the argumentative work, most plausibly the orientation of the cuts and the resulting trade-off between recurrent integration and pain-related causal role, say what would happen to the disagreement if that feature were equalised across the two systems, and finish with the strongest objection available to a defender of whichever theory comes out badly in one of the two cases. Please write your answer in British English.
