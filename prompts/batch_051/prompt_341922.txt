I recently moved into a policy research role where I am responsible for stress-testing governance proposals for frontier AI development, and I have become convinced that one proposal my colleagues are treating as settled deserves harder scrutiny than it has received. The proposal is a "registered-run covenant" among leading AI labs, intended to slow race dynamics without imposing an outright moratorium. I want it analyzed strictly as a mechanism, not as a moral position.

The covenant would be overseen by an independent registry staffed by engineers seconded from the signatory labs and by academic auditors. It has three provisions:

1. Registration. Before beginning any training run above a publicly specified compute threshold, a signatory lab must file a pre-training notice stating the architecture family, the compute budget, the approximate data mix, and the safety evaluations it plans to run. The notice itself is public; detailed evaluation results remain confidential.

2. Standing hold. A model from a registered run may not be deployed or made broadly accessible until eighteen months after the filing date. During the hold, the registry audits the run and publishes a redacted capability summary, and other signatories may review the safety case under a nondisclosure agreement.

3. Tripwire release. If the registry finds evidence that a signatory trained a qualifying model without filing — via supply-chain or energy monitoring, whistleblower reports, or discrepancies in a deployed model's behavior — it issues a tripwire determination. All other signatories in good standing are then released from their own hold periods for the next ninety days, and the non-filing lab's future filings are subject to double the usual hold period. The registry has no enforcement powers beyond publication, exclusion from shared evaluation infrastructure, and the reciprocal release itself.

Analyze how this institution would actually behave. What does it incentivize among the labs, the auditors, and the engineers on secondment; how would each party game it; and which provisions are load-bearing? In particular, test it against the three standard rationalist explanations for why bargains among rivals fail: private information with incentives to misrepresent, credible commitment problems, and issue indivisibility. Which of these does the covenant genuinely address, which does it merely relocate, and where does it break? I am especially interested in whether the tripwire release functions as a coordination device or as a detonator, and in what happens the first time a lab is found to have trained just above the threshold or to have structured its work to stay just below it.
