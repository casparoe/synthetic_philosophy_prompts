My flatmate and I have been arguing about what we actually owe each other after a friendship of mine blew up, and I want you to help me think it through. My current criteria for satisfying a duty to be legible to the people close to you are:

1. You dont misrepresent your mental states.
2. When someone directly asks where you stand, you answer truthfully.
3. You don't strategically exploit the fact that others will misread you.
4. You make a reasonable effort to correct predictable misunderstandings when the stakes are high.

Now test the criteria against someone I know, call her Priya. Priya never lies, and she will answer any direct question. But she reports everything in the same flat register – there is no difference between how she says 'I'm upset' and how she says 'the bin needs emptying.' If you ask her whether something hurt her she says yes or no, truthfully, but she volunteers nothing about intensity or significance, and she won't flag when someone has misread her unless they ask exactly the right question. Last month I did something she apparently found quite destructive, and she mentioned it in passing weeks later in the same tone she uses for a weather report; I had no idea it mattered.

Did Priya fail the duty of legibility? Go criterion by criterion, be honest about where the criteria underdetermine the answer, and say whether the case shows I've drawn the criteria wrong – for example whether I'm confusing legibility with emotional expressiveness, or whether the flat register is a red herring. Write the response in British English.
