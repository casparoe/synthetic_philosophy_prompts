Gratitude has been bugging me because the word does too much work, and my gut goes different ways on different cases. I want to know whether there's one principle under all of it or whether I should split the concept up.

A friend cosigned my lease when I had no credit, and she's never brought it up since, and would be embarrassed if I did. I still feel like I owe her something, not just a thank-you, and it doesn't feel like a debt I can ever exactly repay. That's clearly gratitude.

A stranger once paid off a layaway balance of mine anonymously; I will never know who did it. I don't feel like I owe that person anything — I couldn't direct it at them if I tried — but I'd feel like a jerk if I didn't feel something and look for a way to pass it on. I can't tell if that's the same thing as the first case or a different thing wearing the same name.

If someone helps me by accident — say a driver clips my bumper and that's what kept me from going through an intersection two seconds before a truck ran the light — I don't think I owe them gratitude; I feel relief and maybe a weird debt to the universe. But if someone genuinely tries to help me and makes things worse, I still feel gratitude for the trying. Intentions seem to matter, but only sometimes.

I also feel like I can be grateful for things with no giver: my health, lucky timing, where I was born. Yet gratitude is normally directed at somebody. Those two intuitions can't both be right without some stretching, and I want to know which one has to give.

The past is where it gets murkiest. My great-grandparents made a hard move that made my whole life easier, and I feel something like gratitude toward them even though they're dead, never knew me, and weren't doing it for me specifically. Is that gratitude or just appreciation with a family story attached? Then there's the harsher version: some of my start in life traces to an inheritance built partly on stolen land, and I feel that I owe something to the descendants of the people it was taken from. But I can't tell whether that belongs with gratitude, restitution, or guilt, or whether it's a fourth thing.

What principle — or split set of principles — captures whichever of these are really gratitude? Be precise about each principle and say which case motivates each clause. Also run it on a case I haven't listed: someone does me a large unrequested favor that I would have turned down if asked in advance. And be honest about which of my verdicts the principle can't accommodate. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then reason it through; no preamble, no restating the question. Use American English.
