I am a high school student helping to put together my school’s philosophy club magazine. For an issue on what happens when we call something true, I would like you to write a satirical piece of about 600 words in the form of a product-recall notice issued by a fictional company, “James & Co.,” for its product “The Truth.” The voice should be calm, bureaucratic, and deadpan, like a real recall notice. The target should be William James’s account of truth as a process of verification rather than a static property. The humor should come from actually understanding the theory: truths that “happen” to ideas, beliefs that work for a while and then break down under new experience, the difference between verification and verifiability, and James’s concession that “expedient” means expedient in the long run and on the whole. The reader should also feel the force of the standard objection—that James is making truth change whenever a belief becomes inconvenient—without the narrator ever stating that objection outright or winking at the reader.

Please build the piece around the following passage from William James’s *The Meaning of Truth*:

“The truth of an idea is not a stagnant property inherent in it. Truth HAPPENS to an idea. It BECOMES true, is MADE true by events. Its verity IS in fact an event, a process, the process namely of its verifying itself, its veriFICATION. Its validity is the process of its validATION.”

The recall notice should quote or paraphrase this passage in a way that fits the genre, and the parody should turn on what the passage actually says rather than on a distorted version of it. It is fine to include a small-print note from a “Peirce estate” or similar device distinguishing James’s product from an earlier, stricter version of pragmatism, but only if the distinction is drawn accurately.

After the piece, add a short afterword of about 150 words in which you separate what is settled in the passage from what is genuinely contested among scholars who study pragmatism—especially the question of whether James is denying that truth depends on reality or merely describing the process by which we discover and hold true beliefs. Do not present contested interpretations as the consensus.
