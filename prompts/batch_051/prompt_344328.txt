I've been going back and forth between Niall Ferguson and Shashi Tharoor on the British Raj: Ferguson's balance-sheet claim that the empire delivered law, railways, and free trade, versus Tharoor's case that the Raj deindustrialized India, worsened famines through callous policy, and that Britain still owes reparations. What struck me is how much Tharoor's economics sounds like the old drain-of-wealth critique, and it got me thinking about what somebody from that earlier generation would have done with this debate.

So here is my question. What would Dadabhai Naoroji — the Parsi intellectual, early Congress president, and Liberal MP in the 1890s, who spent decades arguning that British rule was draining India's wealth and causing its poverty — make of the Ferguson–Tharoor argument, including the reparations claim? He obviously never read either of them, and the postcolonial balance-sheet genre and the modern reparations movement only came together after he died. He also wasn't a straightforward anti-imperialist: he framed his critique as a betrayal of British standards rather than as an argument to break with Britain. I'm not looking for fabricated quotations, just an educated reconstruction of where his actual positions point.

Give me exactly five points Naoroji would press in this debate, as a numbered list, each point a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Make at least a couple of them places where he'd refuse to fully side with either Ferguson or Tharoor.
