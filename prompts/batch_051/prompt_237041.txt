Many public-sector hiring panels work from a qualifying threshold rather than a full ranking: once an applicant clears the posted requirements, the panel may treat that applicant as acceptable and select the first such person interviewed, even though a later applicant might well have been stronger. The same agency, when purchasing office software, scores every bid exhaustively, imposes a strict ranking, and awards the contract to the top-ranked option no matter how small the margin. What theory of rational choice does the hiring practice presuppose? Does it presuppose that, above the threshold, further differences in candidate quality carry no rational weight, or only that the cost of continued search and finer comparison outweighs the expected gain? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say whether the two practices can be reconciled within a single account of rationality, and state what the agency would have to alter in its hiring if it held that maximizing expected utility is the only defensible standard of choice.
