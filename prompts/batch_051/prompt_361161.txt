Imagine John Stuart Mill somehow got his hands on the modern debate over epistocracy — roughly, the idea that political power should be weighted by political knowledge or competence instead of strict one-person-one-vote, the sort of thing Jason Brennan defends nowadays. Mill had his own proposal for plural voting, where more educated citizens get extra votes. So the question is: what would Mill actually make of modern epistocracy?

I don't want the lazy version where you just say he'd be an epistocrat because he was an elitist. I want you to work through the places where his own commitments would put real pressure on the modern proposals, and the places where he'd part company with Brennan. Specifically think about:

- Mill was against simply excluding whole classes from the franchise, which is why he wanted universal suffrage with weighted votes rather than a restricted electorate. Would he accept a modern "competence screening" test, or would he worry that its measures of competence just encode class and race?
- Mill cared a lot about the developmental effect of participation — that voting itself teaches people public-spiritedness. Does that cut against Brennan's purely instrumental case for epistocracy, and how would Mill try to keep both the developmental story and the weighted votes?
- Mill treated the vote as a trust exercised for others' benefit rather than a private right. How does that shape what he'd say about epistocratic vetoes, or about who gets to judge competence?
- Where would Mill and Brennan most likely disagree about what "competence" even means?

So give me a considered prediction that (a) seperates the main mechanisms on offer in modern epistocracy — weighted votes, vetoes, competence tests, restricted suffrage, whatever — (b) says which of them Mill would likely endorse and why, and (c) identifies the specific point where you think Mill would break hardest with the modern case.

Also, at the end, tell me how confident you are in the overall prediction, and single out the one sub-prediction you're least sure about. No need for citations or reading lists — I just want the reasoning.
