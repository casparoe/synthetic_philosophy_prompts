I am drawn to expected utility theory because its axioms seem individually unassailable and it yields a definite verdict in every case, but the theory appears to command me to pay Pascal's mugger: for any finite cost I incur by complying and any nonzero subjective probability I attach to the stranger's promise, there is some promised payoff large enough that the expected value of paying swamps the sure loss of refusing. My intuition is firm that paying would be irrational, and I do not think that intuition is mere risk aversion or a failure of nerve. Present each side at full strength, then give me a negotiated verdict: what would have to be true for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what would have to be true for the theory to be amended or abandoned, and whether there is a reading of "rational" on which both survive. In doing so, address at least bounded utility, discounting or thresholding very small probabilities, and assigning lower credence to more complex promises, and say what each move costs.
