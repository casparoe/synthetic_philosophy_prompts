I spent my career as a molecular biologist, and since retiring I have been reading philosophy of biology. Before I present my views to a reading group, I want an audit of whether the commitments I have landed on actually hang together. Here they are:

(1) DNA literally carries semantic information: a gene sequence is about a product or phenotype, and saying so is not a metaphor.
(2) The information also has a combinatorial side: a sequence has a bit-content fixed by its length and base composition, independent of what the sequence does.
(3) Semantic information requires an interpretive or decoding system; where there is no decoder, there is only chemistry, not information.
(4) Yet an unexpressed gene still carries its information even when nothing is currently interpreting it.
(5) The central dogma is right: information flows from DNA to RNA to protein, and not in reverse.
(6) I also accept as plain molecular fact that reverse transcriptase copies RNA into DNA in retroviruses.
(7) DNA is rightly called a code because the codon–amino-acid assignments are arbitrary; many other assignments were chemically possible.
(8) But the code was not designed or chosen by any agent; it was fixed early in the history of life and passed down by descent.

I cannot tell which of these pull against which, and I would rather not commit in advance to any particular revision. Please audit the set: identify the pairs or small subsets that are actually in tension, rank the tensions by severity, and for each say whether it is genuine or only apparent. For each tension, state what would have to give to remove it and what the revision would cost. Exclude pairs that are merely different in emphasis.

Return the audit as a JSON object with this schema:
- "tensions": a list whose entries each contain "rank" (integer, 1 for the most severe), "commitments" (a list of the numbers involved), "severity" (a number from 0 to 10, with 10 meaning outright contradiction), "status" (either "genuine" or "apparent"), "point_of_conflict" (one sentence saying what actually pulls), "cheapest_fix" (which commitment changes and how), and "cost_of_fix" (what is sacrificed by that revision).
- "overall_assessment": two or three sentences naming the single revision that would do the most cleanup, if any.
