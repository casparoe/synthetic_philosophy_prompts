I need a thought experiment for an essay on impact measures for low-impact AI. The essay asks whether the instruction to avoid side effects can be specified for a general-purpose agent without first deciding, in value-laden terms, which changes to the world count as side effects at all. Construct a case in which a value-neutral impact measure—defined only over the physical configuration of a home and the set of future tasks a generic agent could still perform, with no weighting for privacy, sentiment, or history—rates an action as the lowest-impact option, while an ordinary adult would judge that same action to be the most serious side effect in the scenario. The point of the exercise is to test whether the measure's verdict falls out of its rule or is smuggled in by the choice of what counts as a state, so do not stipulate the verdicts: the scenario must generate them. Constraints: a single domestic setting, one robot and one human, a mundane assigned goal such as fetching the car keys, and no exotic world models—the robot's impact model genuinely contains only object positions, reachable configurations, and a distribution over future tidying-and-fetching tasks.

Give me exactly five numbered items, each no more than two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. For item 1, give the scenario in two sentences. Item 2 should state what the measure computes, in qualitative terms, and why it ranks the offending action lowest. Item 3 should state what ordinary values say and why they diverge. Item 4 should identify the one feature of the measure doing the work—its state space, its goal distribution, or its baseline—and say what verdict the measure would give if that feature were swapped. Item 5 should give the strongest reply a defender of value-neutral impact measures could make, and the answer a critic should give to it.
