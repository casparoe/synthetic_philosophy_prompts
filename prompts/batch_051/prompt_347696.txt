Ok so I've been trying to get my views on what counts as alive into one place, and I want someone to check whether the set actually hangs together. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Viruses are not alive. I don't care that they evolve; evolving isn't enough for me.

(2) The alive/not-alive boundary is not some sharp joint in nature. It's more of a spectrum, and exactly where you draw the line is partly a pragmatic call.

(3) If you forced me to pick a single criterion, I'd pick metabolism. Something is alive to the extent that it's a bounded, self-maintaining, far-from-equilibrium system that keeps itself going.

(4) This is why a mule is obviously alive even though it can't reproduce, and why a crystal isn't alive. I think reproduction is a red herring.

(5) Fire is not alive, full stop. Any view that can't rule out fire is definetly broken as far as I'm concerned.

(6) But I also don't want to be a gradualist about viruses: they're just on the not-alive side, not 'a little bit alive.'

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. For each pair in tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest fix would be, and what the fix costs. Also flag the pairs that only look like they conflict and say why they don't. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
