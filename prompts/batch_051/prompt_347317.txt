Say I've been reading about ant colonies and I keep coming back to the idea that the colony behaves smarter then any of its members. Individual ants look like they're mostly just following simple rules (dropping pheromones, touching antennae, carrying stuff), but the colony as a whole builds bridges, farms fungus, maintains a steady temperature, selects nest sites, reallocates workers after damage, that sort of thing. This is basically the old Aunt Hillary / ant-fugue thought: there's no mind to be found in any individual ant, so if there is a mind, it would have to belong to the colony as a whole.

I don't want to just argue about this in the abstract. I want to know how you'd go about testing whether an ant colony has a mind, as opposed to just being a large heap of simple ants whose behaviour happens to aggregate into something that looks clever. So: design the test or tests you'd run. What result would favour the "colony-mind" reading, and what result would favour the "just ants doing simple things" reading? And then be clear about what your tests would and wouldn't settle. Could a clean result actually settle the question, or is there a leftover bit that no experiment reaches -- and if so, is that leftover a limit of the experiments or a defect in the idea of a "group mind" itself?

Please write your answer in British English.
