I am writing up a position on truth for a graduate seminar and would like a consistency check before I present it. My commitments are as follows. (1) I accept a deflationary account: truth is not a substantive or explanatory property, and the schema 'p' is true if and only if p captures everything essential about truth. (2) I think there are genuine truth-value gaps: a sentence applying a vague predicate to a borderline case expresses no determinate proposition and is neither true nor false. (3) I think sentences with empty fictional names are false rather than gappy: 'Sherlock Holmes lives on Baker Street' is simply false. (4) I think truth is a genuine norm of assertion, doing real normative work: one should assert only what is true, and this explains why certain assertions are defective. (5) I am an abductive scientific realist: the best explanation of the success of mature science is that those theories are approximately true.

I can tell that some of these pull against each other, but I am not confident which, and I suspect at least one apparent clash dissolves on closer inspection. Please go through the pairs, distinguish genuine tensions from merely apparent ones, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest repair would be and what it costs. Finally, tell me which single change would relieve the most strain while preserving as much of the view as possible.
