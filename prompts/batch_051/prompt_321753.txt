I want a working definition of humour — not of laughter or amusement as such, but of what makes something humorous. I'm aware of the familiar one-line theories (superiority, incongruity, relief, benign violation), but I don't want a survey of them; I want a single definition that does better than any of them alone, tested against the following cases. It should clearly count as humour: a well-constructed pun; a toddler's laughter at peekaboo; and the involuntary laugh at a pompous official's harmless pratfall. It should clearly count as not humour: a small child's laughter from being tickled; and the nervous giggle of someone just told their flight is delayed until morning. On each of the following, I want a definite verdict either way, even if you have to defend a surprising one: a joke that amuses only the person who thought of it and is never shared with anyone; a flat, punchline-free observation about office meetings that gets a laugh simply because it is true; someone retelling their own embarrassing blunder years afterward and laughing at it; the quiet satisfaction of solving an elegant puzzle that involves no butt of a joke, no victim, and no discharge of social tension; a joke told primarily to comfort someone that nonetheless makes them laugh; and the same joke delivered for the fifth time in one evening, now to groans. Run your proposed definition against every case, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise the definition or defend the verdict. Bonus if the account also explains why humour feels like it comes in degrees.
