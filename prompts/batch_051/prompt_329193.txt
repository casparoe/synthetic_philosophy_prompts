we've been reading heidegger on equipment this week — ready-to-hand and present-at-hand and how a tool kind of disappears while you're using it and only shows up as a thing when it stops working. and it's making me look at software design differently. when my phone's autocorrect works fine i type straight through it, it basically doesn't exist for me, and it only becomes this annoying object when it changes a word or makes a bad correction. but apps also do that on purpose: the "are you sure you want to delete this file?" dialog interrupts me mid-action and makes me stare at a choice even though nothing broke. and error screens are totally inconsistent — sometimes they dump a raw stack trace like the app is suddenly showing all its insides, and sometimes they just say "something went wrong" and hide everything. so here's what i'm trying to figure out. what view of the ready-to-hand/present-at-hand distinction does this whole design practice presuppose, if any? does it actually presuppose that equipment only becomes visible when it breaks, or is breakdown just one trick designers use to flip the switch? and if you held the opposite view — that you can notice a tool while it's still working fine — what would the practice concretely have to change?
