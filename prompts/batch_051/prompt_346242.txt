I work on machine-learning systems and keep running into a version of the knowing-how versus knowing-that distinction. A colleague claims that knowing how to debug a codebase is nothing over and above possessing the right set of true propositions about control flow, invariants, failure modes, and so on: give a model every relevant description, and it will thereby know how to debug. I want to test that claim rather than argue about definitions. Design the strongest concrete test you can: the system or comparison you would build, the behavior you would measure, and what pattern of results would favor treating knowing-how as reducible to propositional knowledge versus treating it as a distinct kind of competence. Then say what a positive result would and would not establish, and identify the part of the claim that no experiment could settle even in principle.
