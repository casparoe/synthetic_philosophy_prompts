You are a professional philosopher who works on value theory, vagueness, and the formal structure of 'better than.' I run a reading group on spectrum arguments for the intransitivity of 'better than,' and I want you to write a decision tree that sorts a person into a position on the question based on how they answer concrete questions about cases — not on which label they already like. Spend some time brainstorming the logical space before you write, but your final answer should start with the tree itself: no preface, no 'here's a tree.'

The tree should start from a spectrum like this: one life has a very long stretch of barely noticeable chronic pain, another has a very short stretch of agonizing pain, and in between there's a long sequence of lives where each adjacent pair differs only by a tiny amount — a little less pain and a little more time, or the reverse depending on which way you run it. The branching questions should be things like: Is each adjacent pair really 'not worse'? Is there a fact of the matter about the first step where the sequence flips from 'not worse' to 'worse'? Do 'not worse than' and 'at least as good as' always come apart? Does 'better than' in the overall comparison mean the same thing as 'better than' at the adjacent steps? Does choice need a transitive ranking, or can you choose on the basis of a nontransitive relation plus some covering notion? Phrase every node so someone can answer it without knowing the labels.

I want the leaves to include at least these positions, and gloss them in the tree so readers dont need to know the jargon: the transitivity-denier who accepts that betterness can be nontransitive and that each adjacent step is genuinely not worse while the endpoints are still ranked; the epistemicist/supervaluationist who keeps transitivity and says there is a sharp cutoff but we cant know where it is; the parity/incomparability view that says adjacent lives are on a par or incomparable, so calling each step 'not worse' is already too strong; the contextualist who says 'better than' is context-sensitive, so the spectrum quietly shifts the relation as you move along it; and a deflationary view that says the puzzle comes from forcing pairwise verdicts on a multidimensional comparison. Add any other leaves the logical space demands, and mark unoccupied combinations with a note on whether they're unoccupied because they're incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Aim for around seven to ten leaves. Also flag any pairs of positions the tree can't actually separate with the questions it asks, and say what extra case would separate them. After the tree, give a short routing key in a table and the diagnosis of empty cells. But the very first sentence of your answer should be the top node of the tree.
