The board of a small foundation I advise has been going back and forth about how a granting institution should handle proposals whose case rests on a tiny probability of an enormously large benefit. The discussion keeps returning to the general problem that expected-value reasoning, taken literally, can be dominated by negligible-probability outcomes carrying huge valuations—the pattern often discussed under the label expected value fanaticism. I want to arrive at a worked-through view of the available responses before our next meeting, and a ranked assessment would help me think more clearly than an unstructured survey would.

The responses I have collected so far are these: (1) bound the value function, so that total value has a finite upper limit and no tail outcome can push expected value upward without limit; (2) set a de minimis probability threshold, treating probabilities below some stipulated cutoff as zero for purposes of decision-making; (3) impose a prior epistemic penalty on extraordinary value claims, discounting hypotheses according to their complexity or according to how far the value they promise per unit of cost departs from observed base rates; (4) abandon expected-value maximization in favor of a different decision rule, such as maximin, satisficing, or a rule requiring both a tolerable worst case and a sufficiently high expectation; (5) use imprecise probabilities, representing the chance of the extraordinary payoff as an interval or a set of admissible credences and retaining only those actions that look acceptable across the entire set.

Please sort these five responses into three tiers—responses you would retain as central fixes, responses you would accept only under stated qualifications, and responses you would mostly reject—and order the items within each tier from most to least defensible. For each response, give a sentence or two on what it buys against fanaticism and a sentence or two on what it costs in abandoned or distorted ordinary expected-value reasoning. If a response should be accepted only conditionally, specify the condition that would move it up or down a tier.
