From a Q&A site's terms of service: "Reputation points are non-transferable and may not be bought, sold, or bartered." The site's whole quality system runs on these points — they gate editing tools, moderator elections, and how high answers rank. For the past two years I've been paying a retired expert in my field to draft answers, which I lightly edit and post under my own account, so my score now tracks his judgment rather than mine, and the score has got me elected a moderator. The clause was added after people where caught selling accounts and running upvote rings; the admins clearly meant to stop points changing hands, not to stop someone paying for the expertise behind a post. Am I breaching the clause? I don't so much need a yes or no as a map of how the different approaches to interpretation — plain words, the purpose behind the rule, what the drafters would have said if asked about ghostwriting, what a reasonable new user reading it today would take it to prohibit — each handle this, and whether any non-gerrymandered reading reaches me.
