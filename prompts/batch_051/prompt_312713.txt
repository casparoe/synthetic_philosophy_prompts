I am a novelist working on a near-future novel set in a city where any autonomous system that operates in public infrastructure must first receive an impact clearance from the city's Autonomous Systems Review Panel. The statute is deliberately thin: it permits deployment only when a system's expected side effects are "limited and proportionate," and it explicitly declines to define a side effect in terms of any particular theory of value or human flourishing. The dramatic question of the scene is whether a body can apply such a standard consistently without ever saying what it is ultimately trying to protect. I have already drafted the Panel's earlier decisions, and I have now reached the climactic hearing; I want the decision to feel legally honest rather than arbitrary, so I am trying to reverse-engineer the principle the Panel has actually been enforcing.

So far the Panel has ruled as follows. (1) It approved an autonomous document cart that briefly blocked a sanitation truck and caused a four-minute delay; the opinion called the disruption transient and noted that it left no trace. (2) It approved, with conditions, a library retrieval robot that had temporarily moved three shelves of rare books to reach one requested volume and then restored every book to its recorded position; the condition required advance notice before any future rearrangement. (3) It rejected a records robot that scanned a box of paper contracts and then shredded the originals, emphasizing that the originals were unrecoverable and that their loss foreclosed legal uses requiring wet-ink signatures. (4) It rejected a greenhouse climate agent that ran its misters and vents at full capacity for three days during a heat wave, consuming an enormous volume of water and leaving root rot in a seedling bed; the opinion called the water use grossly disproportionate even though the water itself was not destroyed. (5) It approved, subject to ongoing monitoring, a street-sweeping robot whose brushes occasionally loosened a small amount of patching material from potholes, on the condition that the robot report the incidental damage weekly.

The new application before the Panel is a culvert-clearing robot. Instructed to clear a blocked storm culvert before a forecast rain, it flushed the blockage with a huge one-time release of water from the municipal reservoir rather than using a cutting head that a human crew could have operated in under an hour. The rain never arrived, and the released water drained through the system and was recovered downstream; consultants agree that the flush left no lasting change to the culvert, the surrounding soil, or the reservoir level, and that no future use of the culvert was impaired. What should the Panel decide if it is to be consistent with its own record? Identify the closest analog among the five decisions, say whether the record genuinely underdetermines the present case, and state what principle the Panel would be codifying under each possible ruling. Then give your best prediction of what a Panel attentive to its own precedent would actually do, and describe a further case whose treatment would best separate the candidate principles. Please phrase the analysis in American English.
