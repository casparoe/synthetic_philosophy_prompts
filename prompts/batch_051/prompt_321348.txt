I help run a small travel forum, and every few weeks someone reopens the same fight: tipping in the US, queueing in Britain, jaywalking in a city where everyone crosses against the light, haggling in a night market, shoes off at the door. The fight is always about whether a visitor actually ought to follow the local everyday rules, or whether it's just politeness, or just strategy so locals don't hate you. I want to make a "what do you actually think" quiz for the forum: a decision tree that sorts a person into a view based only on their answers to concrete cases, not on the label they'd pick for themselves. It's for people with no philosophy training, so every question should be a situation they can picture and every branch should be the specific claim that splits it.

Here's the angle I care about. I don't want the tree built by starting from four named positions and then writing questions to match people to them. I want it built from scratch: work out the questions, think through which combinations of answers are possible, then locate the real positions in that space and name the empty combinations with a guess at why nobody is there (incoherent? just unattractive? just unexplored?). The first question should be a tipping case: a tourist from a country where tipping isn't practice eats at an American diner and leaves nothing. Then branch into queue-jumping in a place with a strong queuing norm, jaywalking down an empty street at night where locals do it, and haggling with a vendor who expects it. The leaves should include at least a "when in Rome" view, a view that these conventions have no moral force at all, a view that what matters is only whether a specific person is harmed or treated unfairly, and a view that following the rules is about treating locals as equals. Also flag the spots where two views can't actually be separated by any question you tried, rather than papering over it. Aim for maybe seven questions and ten leaves max, and give each leaf a one-sentence description of its most awkward commitment so people can see what they're signing up for.
