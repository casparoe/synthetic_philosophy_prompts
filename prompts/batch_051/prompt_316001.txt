I work as a senior policy analyst at a mid-size public utilities commission. We see maybe twenty generation-related proposals a year — utilities asking to keep an old nuclear unit open, developers pitching solar-plus-storage, legislators sending us bills to comment on — and every single one is framed by its sponsor as a necessary step in the energy transition. I get about a day per proposal to write the assessment the commissioners actually read before they vote, and the commissioners are smart but not technical; they want a clean recommendation and the two or three reasons behind it. The stakes are high enough that whichever way we rule, one side accuses us of climate negligence and the other accuses us of wrecking the grid, so I want something I can point to and say "here is the test, this proposal failed it on step three."

My staff keeps relitigating the same argument. One camp says we should score proposals on lifecycle carbon per megawatt-hour and be done. Another says that ignores reliability, and that a gas peaker running 200 hours a year is not the same climate problem as a coal plant running baseload. People invoke Germany and France as slogans — "we don't want to end up like Germany" or "France proves nuclear is the answer" — without ever actually working through what those two countries did differently. I want you to turn the Germany-vs-France comparison into a reusable decision procedure, not a one-off memo: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it has to hand the decision to a person because its a values call rather than a fact. It should not be nuclear-only; it should handle gas peakers, storage, efficiency spending, whatever lands on my desk. Then run the procedure on three cases: a proposal to extend an existing nuclear unit for eight years past its scheduled retirement; a proposal to build new open-cycle gas peakers to back up a growing wind fleet; and a proposal for a big rooftop-solar-plus-battery rebate program. I suspect at least one of those will come out differently than my commission would prefer, and I want the procedure to tell me that honestly. Finally, tell me where the procedure just does not decide — the places where the France/Germany comparison gives no clean answer and I should stop pretending it does.
