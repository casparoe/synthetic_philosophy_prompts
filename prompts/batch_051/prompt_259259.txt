I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and for most of this quarter I have been reporting a feature on theologies of translation — specifically, the question of whether a translated sacred text can be inspired in anything like the way its source text is said to be. I have finished the interviews and the reading, and before I build the piece around my own position, I want to put my working commitments through one more round of scrutiny. Here is the set I find myself holding:

(1) Inspiration attaches primarily to what a text says — its message and theological content — rather than to the exact sequence of words, so a faithful translation can be as genuinely inspired as the original even though it cannot preserve wordplay, idiom, and other language-bound features.

(2) Translation is nonetheless always an interpretive act: theological commitments can enter through individual rendering choices, and some meaning simply does not survive the move between languages.

(3) The early church's treatment of the Septuagint shows that a translation can take on authoritative standing of its own: communities built doctrine on the translated wording even where it parted ways with the Hebrew.

(4) Long communal use can confer authority on a translation that is not reducible to how accurately it tracks its source — a text becomes scripture for a people through being read, taught, and liturgically repeated, not only through philological fidelity.

(5) KJV-onlyism is wrong when it claims one early modern English translation is uniquely inspired, but it is pointing at a real problem: without a community-stabilized wording, a text can be bent to whatever an interpreter wants it to say.

(6) If a translation that diverges from its source can still bear this kind of authority, I seem to have detached authority from the original author's intended meaning — and I cannot see what then excludes inspired paraphrase, expansion, or commentary from the same status.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture: which pairs of commitments are in real tension and which only look as if they are? Please rank the tensions by severity, say what each would cost to resolve, and identify the single least costly revision that would make the whole set coherent. I'll handle the sourcing and attribution for the piece myself.
