I am a journalist who covers philosophy and the history of ideas, and I am planning a long feature on a dispute in Hume scholarship that has run for decades without either side quite displacing the other: the standoff between the so-called New Humeans and the older regularity reading of Hume on causation. I have persuaded two representative scholars—one who reads Hume as a causal realist, holding that Hume allows real necessary connections in nature but takes them to be forever beyond our comprehension, and one who reads Hume as a deflationist, holding that for Hume the only legitimate content of necessity is constant conjunction plus the mind's projection of a felt determination—to take part in an adversarial collaboration rather than another exchange of rebuttals. The deliverable is a single document they would both sign. I need you to draft it.

Please structure the document in four parts. First, a numbered list of claims about Hume's texts and about causation that both parties can accept without hedging; aim for formulations that are neither vacuous nor slanted, and be explicit about which apparent disagreements you judge to be merely verbal and therefore set aside. Second, a table of residual disagreements, with each side's position written in wording that side would accept and the disputed point stated neutrally. Third, a crux section: the sub-questions such that, if they were settled, at least one side would have to move; I expect the cruxes to include whether the Treatise and the first Enquiry are best read as claiming that the hidden springs of nature are unknowable-but-real or as claiming that there is nothing there to know, what work the mind's tendency to spread itself on external objects is doing in the account, and whether Hume has the resources—perhaps through something like a relative idea of an unknown cause—to mean more than the two definitions of cause say. Fourth, for each crux, the concrete textual passages and philosophical considerations both sides agree bear on it, with each side's prediction about what that evidence will support recorded in advance. Do not turn this into a verdict on who wins; the point is that both parties could put their names to every line except the entries in the disagreement table, and even those should be worded so each recognizes his or her own view.
