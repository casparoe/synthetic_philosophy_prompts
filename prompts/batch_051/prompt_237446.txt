In a discussion group I attend, the question of what adult children owe their parents comes up repeatedly, and one participant has settled on a way of framing it that clearly convinces him. He says that parents build up a kind of moral credit through the years of care, the money spent, and the opportunities forgone, and that adult children are therefore debtors, with a standing obligation to repay through financial support, regular contact, and a measure of deference to parental wishes. The debt analogy is doing real argumentative work for him: he infers from it that the obligation has a determinate amount, that it is owed to a specific creditor whether or not the relationship currently feels close, that it persists independently of affection, and that a child who neglects it has defaulted. Another member of the group counters with a line she attributes to Jane English: that what grown children owe their parents is not a debt but the kind of thing friends owe one another, which exists only while the relationship itself is alive and is not best understood as repayment for past sacrifices.

I would like you to audit the debt analogy rather than adjudicate the particular argument. Please give a systematic mapping between the two domains: what corresponds to the creditor, the debtor, the principal, the interest, the repayment schedule, and the default. Then identify where the analogy breaks down, and distinguish the disanalogies that matter for the conclusions it is being used to support from those that are merely superficial. Consider what the analogy quietly imports from the domain of debt -- for example, that the arrangement was entered into voluntarily, that the obligations are fixed and calculable, and that the parties are independent bargainers -- and assess whether those features hold in the parent-child case. Finally, say which of the original conclusions, if any, survive once the disanalogies are on the table, whether they can be supported on some other ground, and whether there is a better analogy than debt for what adult children owe their parents. Please answer in British English.
