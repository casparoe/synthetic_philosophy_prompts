I'm a PhD student in theoretical computer science, working on proof theory and the structure of derivation systems. My thinking is built out of directed graphs, paths, well-foundedness, roots, cycles, axioms, and derivations. My epistemology is the standard survey-course picture: the regress argument says that if a belief needs support from another belief, then justification must either stop somewhere, go in a circle, or descend indefinitely, and none of these seems acceptable; foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism are the three attempts to make one of those options acceptable. I want that debate rendered in my vocabulary, and I want the rendering to be honest about what does not carry over.

Here is the target vocabulary. Consider a directed graph whose nodes are beliefs, or propositions believed, and whose edges point from a belief to the beliefs that support or justify it. I can ask whether a node lies in a strongly connected component, whether every support path is finite and terminates in nodes with no outgoing edges, whether a node has a self-loop, whether a node lies on an infinite path, and whether an infinite path eventually repeats. From proof theory I also have axioms, understood as underived nodes from which finite derivation trees grow; rules of inference; derivations as finite, rooted trees; and cyclic or coinductive derivations, in which a derivation may loop or run forever and yet be accepted as legitimate rather than treated as a failure. What I do not natively have is any notion of experiential givenness, immediate apprehension, the content of perception, or beliefs being justified by something that is not a belief.

Please translate the three positions into this vocabulary one by one. For each, state the cleanest structural claim you can find: foundationalism as every support path terminating in base nodes that are either self-justifying or justified by something outside the graph; coherentism as rejecting terminal nodes and locating justification in cycles or strongly connected components; infinitism as requiring infinite, non-repeating support chains rather than cycles. Then give me the residue report: for each position, say what translated cleanly, what had to be distorted to fit, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. In particular, I want to know whether the difference between coherentism and infinitism becomes, in my terms, just the difference between eventually periodic and non-well-founded support chains, and whether that really is the philosophical dispute or an artifact of my modeling choices.

Finally, give me a verdict on the residue. Is it a defect in my target vocabulary, a sign that these positions have been described less precisely than epistemologists pretend, or a sign that the regress argument itself already smuggles in a graph-shaped — perhaps even tree-shaped — picture of support that not every epistemologist is committed to? Do not simply say my vocabulary is limited; say exactly which philosophical claims would be invisible to me, and whether an agent who thought purely in my vocabulary would be led to misjudge the debate.

I would also like you to state how confident you are in each of the main claims, and to single out the one point in your answer you are least sure about.
