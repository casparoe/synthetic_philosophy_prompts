Please defend the following position. Pascal's claim that justice without force is powerless, and force without justice is tyrannical, is best read as a constraint on institutional design rather than as cynicism about morality: enforceability is a necessary condition of a norm's being authoritative in practice, but it is never a substitute for justice. Steelman the strongest objection—that this collapses legitimacy into whatever happens to be enforced—and explain precisely why your reading does not end there. Along the way, keep separate three things: whether a rule is just in the abstract, whether it is authoritative for a particular agent, and whether it will in fact be obeyed. Then test the account against one concrete case: a rule that is widely violated despite being just, or a rule that is widely obeyed despite being unjust, and say what the account implies about our obligations under each.
