Here's an asymmetry in the way we treat consent that I can't get to sit right. If someone signs a contract with a gun to their head, courts won't enforce it and nobody thinks it was real agreement. But if someone clicks "I agree" to a fifty-page take-it-or-leave-it contract of adhesion they haven't read, for a service they basically need, the law treats that click as binding consent. Both people were under pressure and neither knew the terms they were agreeing to. Is there a principled difference between being coerced by a gun and being constrained by the ordinary power of a software company to set terms, or is the clickwrap case mostly a practical carve-out we maintain because modern commerce would break without it? Give me the strongest justification for the asymmetry you can and an honest verdict on whether the line should stay where it is.
