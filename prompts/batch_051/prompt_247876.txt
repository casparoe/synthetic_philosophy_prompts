I am pulled toward the adaptationist program because of its systematic power: it is a tidy discovery procedure — atomize an organism into traits, assume each was shaped by natural selection, and ask what it is for — and it has a real track record of testable predictions and surprising confirmations. But I cannot shake the conviction that this is Panglossian in just the way the spandrels critique charged: organisms are not cleanly decomposable into traits, selection operates on whole phenotypes under developmental, architectural, and historical constraints, and any given feature may be a by-product, a result of drift, a pleiotropic side-effect, or a structure later co-opted for a new use; asked too early, 'what is it for?' manufactures just-so stories rather than explanations. I do not want you to tell me which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the conviction to be the thing I give up, what it would take for the program to be the thing that is amended or abandoned, and whether there is a reading of adaptationism on which both survive — for instance, adaptationism as a research heuristic rather than as a claim about how often traits are adaptations. Say what evidence or considerations would justify each move.
