I am an undergraduate taking a module called Science, Evolution and Human Nature, and one of the assessed pieces is a short pastiche: we have to invent a manuscript in evolutionary psychology and then write the referee report on it for an imaginary journal. I would like you to produce that report as a model answer for me to work from.

Please write a mock peer-review report of roughly 700 words, in British English (spelling and punctuation). The manuscript under review, as I am imagining it, is titled something like The Interaction-Repair Module: An Evolved Adaptation for Post-Transgression Affiliation in Ancestral Foraging Bands, and it argues that the universal human tendency to apologise after trivial accidental transgressions — bumping into a stranger, brushing past someone in a queue, arriving a few minutes late — is a genetically canalised cognitive module shaped by Pleistocene selection for repairing cooperative relationships. Its evidence consists largely of self-report questionnaires administered to undergraduates, from which it generalises to the species.

The report should be in the voice of a conscientious, increasingly strained referee for a fictional journal, and it should be funny because it is methodologically exact rather than because the referee is rude. The objections should emerge through the manuscript's details rather than being named outright: the paper can accommodate any observation because a mismatch clause absorbs apparent counterevidence; the ancestral environment is reconstructed ad hoc from the very behaviour it is meant to explain; the argument runs backwards from the trait being present to the trait having been selected for; no independent evidence is offered on heritability, ancestral fitness effects, or cross-cultural ubiquity; and the theory could with equal ease have predicted the opposite of what is observed. Write it so a specialist in evolutionary psychology would recognise each complaint as fair and laugh rather than wince, while a lay reader could still follow it. Include a short summary of the manuscript, a section of numbered major concerns, a recommendation, and at least one comment that sounds generous but is in fact devastating. Remain entirely in the register of a genuine review, keep the irony deadpan throughout, and do not wink at the fact that the piece is a parody.
