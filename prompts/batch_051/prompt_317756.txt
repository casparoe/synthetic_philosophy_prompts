Before I read another argument that reaches for Gödel's incompleteness theorems to settle a question outside mathematics, I want the standard such an argument has to meet. Write the desiderata for any legitimate application of the incompleteness theorems outside their home domain of formal arithmetic: the requirements an account must satisfy before it may conclude anything about minds, machines, physics, law, or social systems from the fact that some formal system is incomplete.

For each requirement, give the consideration or the stock misapplication that motivates it, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely preferences of particular camps. Be explicit about at least the following, treating them as candidate requirements to defend or refine rather than assume:

- The scope condition. The theorems concern formal systems that are recursively axiomatizable (their axioms can be enumerated by an algorithm), consistent (or ω-consistent in some early formulations), and able to represent enough arithmetic — roughly, to encode basic facts about addition and multiplication — and they concern provability inside a specified system. A would-be exporter must show that the target system meets or instantiates these conditions, not merely gesture at "formal systems" or "rules."
- The completeness flank. Standard first-order logic is complete: every semantic validity has a proof. Several restricted theories are also complete and decidable. Explain what this rules out and why it matters for any sweeping claim about formalism or deductive method as such.
- The consistency burden. The first theorem yields an undecidable sentence given consistency; the second theorem's force depends on a careful reading of "cannot prove its own consistency," including the strength and consistency assumptions involved. Say what happens to the exported conclusion when those assumptions are weakened, strengthened, or shifted to a stronger background system.
- The provability/truth distinction. That a sentence is unprovable in a system is not that it is untrue, not that it is unknowable by us, and not that no stronger system can prove it. Likewise, a system's inability to derive its own consistency statement is not a demonstration that the system is unreliable. A legitimate application must keep these apart or argue explicitly for collapsing them.
- The mechanism gap. The theorems limit the deductive reach of formalisms. They do not by themselves characterize what a finite physical device, a trained network, a person, or a legal process can do, because those things are not formal systems of the relevant kind standing in the same relation to a fixed arithmetic. State what must be shown to cross this gap.
- The negativity of the result. Incompleteness is, in the first instance, a nonexistence result: there is no complete, consistent, recursively axiomatized theory of the relevant arithmetic. A successful export must explain how a negative limit of that sort yields a positive claim about the target domain, rather than leaving the connection implicit.

Then briefly run the stock moves against your list: the claim that the theorems refute computationalism about the mind, the claim that they show AI cannot be reliable or complete, the claim that law or physics is shown not to be a formal system, and the claim that they establish truths permanently inaccessible to any human or machine. Say which requirements each move satisfies or fails, state whether the list is jointly satisfiable by any non-trivial outside application, and identify the single requirement that does most of the excluding.
