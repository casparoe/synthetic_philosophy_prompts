I'm on the review board of a small volunteer marine-rescue outfit in a coastal town, and after our last big storm the board is split badly enough that the chair asked ot draft a written opinion to circulate before Thursday's meeting. I'd be grateful for a draft I can edit.

The case is this. Our only rescue RIB is rated for twelve people total, including the coxswain. During the flooding it picked up eleven people from a submerged causeway and was full. As it turned to head back, two more people were spotted clinging to a piling maybe forty meters away: a woman and her adult son. The coxswain judged that taking two more in heavy chop risked swamping the boat, shouted that he'd come back for them, and returned after offloading. The woman survived with hypothermia; the son slipped under before the boat got back. Our written policy says a boat at capacity does not take on additional survivors from the water, and the coxswain followed it exactly. Half the board wants to clear him and leave the policy alone; the other half wants to suspend him and rewrite it. What I keep getting stuck on is whether "we were full" is a real moral reason for leaving someone in the water, or only a physical fact that gets its force from a first-come, first-served assumption nobody on the board has actually defended. I can't tell if the people already in the boat have a claim to their seats that competes with the two people in the water, or if the only thing that matters is the expected number alive at the end.

Please write the memo for me. Lead with the bottom line: a clear recommendation on the coxswain and on whether the policy should be amended. Then give the reasoning, and take the strongest argument for the losing side seriously rather than knocking it down. Max 500 words.
