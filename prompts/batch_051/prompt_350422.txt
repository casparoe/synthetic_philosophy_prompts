I run an MA seminar where we hit Lakatos's research programmes in week six. The students know a bit of Popper and Kuhn but still say a theory was 'falsified' as if one failed prediction settles the matter. I'd like a short sorting quiz to open the session with — 'what is your actual view of how science changes?' — eight questions max, each a concrete mini-case with two or three options (a false prediction survived by tweaking an auxiliary hypothesis, a programme that absorbs anomalies for decades, a crucial experiment that convinces almost nobody, a scientist switching programmes for reasons that look like taste, and so on). Build it from the answer patterns rather than from the labels: first the questions, then the space of possible combinations, then the actual positions located in that space, with any empty combinations diagnosed as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. The leaves I'd like on it are dogmatic falsificationism, Popper-style falsificationism, Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes, Kuhn's paradigms, and Feyerabend. Flag at the end any two leaves your questions can't seperate. If you think the Popper-style leaf and the dogmatic one collapse in practice, or that Feyerabend can't be reached by any real pattern of answers, say so and adjust the leaves rather than forcing him in. More generally, if the task as I've set it is off somehow, tell me plainly instead of handing over a bad quiz. British English, please.
