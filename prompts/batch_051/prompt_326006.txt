I am a retired physical chemist, and after a career spent around instruments and experiments, I have finally had time to read Hume carefully. In An Enquiry concerning Human Understanding, Section VII, Part I, he writes:

"When we look about us towards external objects, and consider the operation of causes, we are never able, in a single instance, to discover any power or necessary connexion; any quality, which binds the effect to the cause, and renders the one an infallible consequence of the other. We only find, that the one does actually, in fact, follow the other."

I have been trying to turn this into a sharp distinction I have been calling "bare succession" versus "nexus-talk." By bare succession I mean the purely observational record: this event, then that event, with no observed tie between them. By nexus-talk I mean the terms scientists and ordinary people constantly use that seem to name the missing tie—force, power, mechanism, necessitation, efficacy. My thesis is that nexus-talk is not descriptive at all but is a predictive instrument: it compresses past regularities into a word that prepares us for what comes next, and the "necessity" in it is just our acquired expectation projected onto the world. I suspect this is more than a mere restatement of Hume because it explains why scientific practice does not collapse into pure phenomenalism: nexus-talk earns its keep by guiding experiment, model repair, and the design of interventions.

Before I go further with this, I would like an honest assessment. Is my distinction already just the standard regularity reading under another name? Does the quoted passage actually support the claim that necessity is only in the mind, or does it leave room for the New Hume view that there is a real necessary connection in nature that we simply cannot directly observe? And what are the three strongest objections to my thesis that nexus-talk is purely instrumental? Please be candid about whether this is a useful distinction or merely a tidy phrase.
