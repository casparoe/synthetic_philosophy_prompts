Construct a two-player finite normal-form game, presented as a payoff matrix with the row player's payoff written first in each cell, that realizes a clean divergence between two iterated-elimination procedures for strictly dominated strategies. In the pure-only procedure, at each round a player may delete only its pure strategies that are strictly dominated by one of its own surviving pure strategies, judged against the other player's surviving strategies. In the mixed-inclusive procedure, at each round a player may delete any pure strategy that is strictly dominated by a mixed strategy over its own currently surviving strategies, again judged against the other player's surviving strategies. Both procedures repeat until nothing further is deletable.

The game you construct must satisfy all of the following:

1. Each player has at most three pure strategies, and all payoffs are nonnegative integers no greater than 10.
2. In the original game, no pure strategy of either player is weakly dominated by another pure strategy; consequently the pure-only procedure deletes nothing from the full game.
3. One of the player's strategies is strictly dominated by a 50/50 mixture of two of its owner's other strategies, while being strictly dominated by no pure strategy.
4. The mixed-inclusive procedure then continues past the deletion in (3): after that first deletion, at least one strategy of the other player also becomes strictly dominated, so the mixed-inclusive procedure runs for at least two rounds and leaves strictly fewer surviving strategies than the pure-only procedure, which leaves all strategies in place.

Once you have found the payoffs, show that the case works by giving: the exact mixed strategy in (3) and the dominance inequalities it must satisfy; the verification that no pure strategy dominates the strategy in (3); the elimination order under the pure-only procedure and under the mixed-inclusive procedure, round by round, with the status of every surviving strategy; an explanation of which structural feature produces the divergence, using the fact that in a finite two-player game a strategy is strictly dominated by some mixed strategy if and only if it is not a best response to any mixed strategy of the opponent over currently surviving strategies; a statement of exactly which inequalities would have to hold for the mixed-inclusive procedure to collapse back into the pure-only procedure if that feature were removed; and a response from someone who refuses to delete mixed-dominated strategies, saying whether their complaint is about the mathematics or about the interpretation of mixed strategies.

The case is for teaching the difference between pure-strategy and mixed-strategy dominance elimination. The payoffs must generate the verdicts through the dominance definitions; a game that merely stipulates the divergence without the inequalities holding is not a solution.
