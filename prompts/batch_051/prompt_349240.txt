ok so im putting together a one page "where i stand" on animal minds for a reading group and want someone to stress test it before i present. heres the set as it currently stands:

1. i take morgans canon seriously, not just as a slogan. if a behavior can be accounted for by some lower psychological faculty, we shouldnt reach for a fancier one.
2. i think its fine to attribute something like beliefs and desires to honeybees, on the strength of flexible navigation: shortcuts, detours, the waggle dance, stuff like that.
3. i think octopuses are probably conscious, or at least we should treat them as such, even though their last common ancestor with us was some simple wormy thing rather than a complex brain-bearer.
4. i'm generally suspicious of parsimony arguments in cognitive ethology. simpler often just means less like us or more mechanistic, and evolution has no built in preference for minimal minds.
5. i lean toward thinking over-attribution is the field's bigger sin than under-attribution; if i had to err, i'd rather miss a real mind than invent a fake one.

go through these and tell me which commitments are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and which single revision would clean up the most. rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and tell me which pairs i could defend together with a straight face and which id have to do real work to keep.
