Quick refresher, since I want the reasoning not a summary. Locke's considered view is that personal identity over time is a matter of the same consciousness reaching backward through memory: what makes me now the same person as the boy who did something in the past is that I can remember that boy's thoughts and actions from the inside, as my own. He died before Thomas Reid hit this with the brave officer case. In that case, a man is flogged as a boy for stealing apples from an orchard; as a young officer he still remembers the flogging; as an old general he remembers capturing the enemy standard as a young officer but has completely lost the flogging from memory. So the officer's memory overlaps with the boy's, and the general's with the officer's, but the general's doesn't overlap with the boy's. Memory is nontransitive; identity is supposed to be transitive; so identity can't simply be memory.

Now here's a case neither of them could have imagined. Neurotechnology can copy a person's episodic memories — the first-person, lived, from-the-inside recollections of their own life, not skills or general knowledge — into another person's mind. The recipient recalls the donor's past as if they had lived it, with the same feeling of it being their own, while keeping all their own original memories. The donor stays alive and keeps their memories too. By a naive application of Locke's criterion, the recipient's consciousness now reaches into the donor's past. But then the recipient is the donor, while the donor is also the donor, and the recipient is also the recipient. That's branching identity, and its also the transitivity problem Reid spotted, except now the two memory chains run through two bodies at once instead of one fading chain.

What would Locke's most defensible considered response be? Try to reconstruct it rather than invent a better neo-Lockean view. Work through the options: tightening what same consciousness means so merely having the memories isn't enough; leaning on his person/man distinction and the forensic, accountability side of personhood; insisting the copied states aren't real memories because they lack the right causal ancestry; maybe something else you see in his apparatus. For each, say what it saves and what it quietly gives up. Then commit: what would Locke himself most plausibly have said if he had first read Reid and then been shown this case? And where does the brave officer style of worry bite hardest in the copy case — where does a reply that works for one case fail for the other?
