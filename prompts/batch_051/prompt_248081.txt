I am putting together a small anthology of satirical writing about the administrative machinery of the welfare state, and I would like you to contribute a piece in a particular genre. Please write a deadpan parody of a government form: specifically, a supplementary questionnaire issued by a fictional central-government department (something like the Department for Work and Assurance) that a claimant must complete before their claim for a means-tested benefit can be assessed. Aim for around 700 words, and include numbered questions with multiple-choice options, short guidance notes for the claimant, and a declaration to be signed. You may invent programme names, thresholds, and statistics; what matters is that the form's logic should be recognisable.

Your target should be the moralised distinction between the deserving and the undeserving poor as it is built into contemporary systems of conditionality, workfare, and benefit-fraud enforcement. The satire should exaggerate real features of that practice rather than inventing straw men: for instance, the demand that claimants perform need in the approved idiom and document it in humiliating detail; the asymmetry between the evidence demanded of claimants and the evidence demanded of the state; the concentration of resources on pursuing a small minority of fraudsters while support is made conditional on conduct; and the conversion of structural unemployment into individual moral failure. At least one question should be constructed so that every available answer can be taken as evidence of either fraud or insufficient effort, with the guidance notes doing the work of explaining this without ever saying it aloud.

Keep the irony deadpan and legible: a reader should be able to tell what is being ironised without any wink from the author, and the humour should come at the expense of the assessing institution and its categories, not of the people subjected to them. Questions that are only a slight, almost plausible exaggeration of real practice will be more effective than outright absurdity.
