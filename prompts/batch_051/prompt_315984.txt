In my philosophy of mind course we spent a week on machine translation, and my professor summarized Hofstadter's critique of statistical translation in a way I have been replaying ever since. As she told it, Hofstadter's charge is that a system like Google Translate deals in empty formal patterns rather than meaning: it calculates which words tend to occur together, and for that reason it can produce fluent output on flat, literal text but defeats itself the moment a sentence depends on a pun, an image, irony, or the particular cultural sense the author intended. She illustrated this with a joke that turned on a double meaning; the machine, she said, would hand back a grammatically impeccable translation that quietly kept only one reading and thereby lost the joke. So far I was with her. But she then presented the deeper moral as follows: real translation requires the translator to reconstruct something like the author's subjective mental imagery, and a system whose only grip on a sentence is statistical will forever be confined to shallow paraphrase. And from this she drew the further implication that Hofstadter's considered view is that no purely computational system, however sophisticated it became, could translate literary prose with genuine understanding.

I left the lecture uncertain. I had always understood Hofstadter as someone who thinks minds, whatever else is true of them, arise from physical and broadly computational processes, so the final implication seemed hard to square with that. On the other hand, my picture of him may itself be the simplified one, and the professor may be faithfully reporting a distinction between computation in the abstract and the kind of statistical mimicry he attacks. Could you help me audit her summary? In particular, I would like you to separate what Hofstadter clearly does claim from what is a common overstatement or conflation, and to flag anything he would explicitly resist or deny. Then tell me whether the final conclusion she attributes to him — the in-principle impossibility claim about computational translation — is something his actual critique commits him to, or whether it is an extra step she has added.
