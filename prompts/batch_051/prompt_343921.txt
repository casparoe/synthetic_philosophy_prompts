I've been drafting a kind of philosophical profile of my views about betting and risk because my friends keep saying the set can't all be true, but nobody can tell me exactly which ones clash, and honestly i can't tell anymore. For context, I'm mostly interested in repeated favorable bets where winnings get reinvested, not one-off gambles. Here are the commitments I keep going back and forth on.

(1) I'm an expected-utility maximizer: the rational act in a risky choice is the one with the highest probability-weighted average utility.
(2) I think the Kelly rule is right for repeated favorable bets — stake the fraction that maximizes expected log wealth — because in a multiplicative setup that's the policy with the highest long-run growth rate, i.e. the largest geometric mean multiplier per round. The arithmetic mean of outcomes misleads you when wealth compounds.
(3) But I don't think rationality picks out a particular utility function over money. How concave your utility curve is, and so how risk-averse you are, is a matter of taste, not correctness.
(4) Yet I also want to say that betting more than full Kelly is irrational, not just different taste. The growth rate is lower than it could be, and somewhere above Kelly it turns negative, after which your wealth converges to zero with probability one in the long run. That feels like a theorem about rational requirement, not a preference.
(5) I've worked through the expected-utility math for the standard power-utility family, and it only tells you to bet exactly Kelly in the one knife-edge case where utility is logarithmic. More concave than log gives less than Kelly; less concave than log gives more than Kelly. So (1) plus (3) seems to imply that overbetting is sometimes just what a less risk-averse but perfectly rational person should do, which seems to collide with (4).
(6) And I've never felt any pull toward the idea that logarithmic utility is objectively special. It just looks like one curve among many, and nobody has given me a reason it's privileged.

I'm not asking for a lecture on Kelly. I want an audit of this list: which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension and which only look that way, order the real tensions by severity, say what each would force me to give up and what the cheapest fix would be, and then tell me which single revision cleans up the most. At the very end, put \answer{...} with a short phrase stating that single revision.
