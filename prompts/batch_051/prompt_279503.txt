I am a PhD student writing on the absurdist and contemporary analytic debate about meaning in life, and I need a working definition of a meaningful life — the property at issue in that debate, as distinct from happiness, moral worth, or cosmic purpose — that gets the following six cases right. (1) A research physician whose discovery prevents a great deal of suffering and who finds the work absorbing. (2) A person who spends forty years watching television and eating, with no relationships, projects, or interests beyond the screen. (3) Camus's Sisyphus, rolling a boulder up a hill forever, fully lucid about the futility, and someone we are to imagine happy. (4) A woman who has lived her adult life in an experience machine, believing she is a celebrated novelist with deep friendships, none of which reaches anyone outside the machine. (5) A political activist who devotes her life to a just cause that is defeated, and who, looking back, would choose the same life again. (6) A philanthropist who is defrauded: she gives everything to what she believes is a hospital, funds nothing real, and dies believing she did great good. I take (1) to be clearly meaningful and (2) clearly not, and I have no settled view about (3) through (6). Propose the definition, run it through all six cases, tell me where it surprises you, and say which of your verdicts is least secure.
