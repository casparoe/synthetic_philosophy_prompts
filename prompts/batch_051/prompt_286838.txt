I'm a control engineer — process control, embedded systems, some robotics — and the vocabulary I trust is the one I use every day: setpoints, error signals, feedback loops, PID control, disturbance rejection, internal models, model predictive control, homeostasis and the regulation of internal state. The vocabulary I do not trust but keep running into is the one philosophers and some biologists use for gradations of sentience and moral status: phenomenal consciousness, valence, having a point of view, intrinsic moral standing, and so on.

I want you to do two things.

First, a translation. Take the main positions in the debate over whether sentience and moral status come in degrees — for example, (a) sentience is all-or-nothing though its physical basis is graded, (b) sentience itself is graded and moral status tracks degree of sentience, (c) sentience is graded but moral status cuts in only past a threshold of minimal welfare — and restate each as faithfully as you can in control-theoretic vocabulary. What is the setpoint? What is the error signal? What is the difference between a one-loop regulator, a layered homeostatic system, and a system with an internal model and predictive control? Is a thermostat a degenerate case of the same quantity, or does it lack the quantity entirely? Add any other position you think is central that my rough map omits.

Second, the part I care about more: the residue report. For each position, say which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression at all in the vocabulary of regulation and internal models. I especially want to know whether valence — felt badness and felt goodness — is genuinely captured by signed error signals and their prediction, or whether saying so smuggles in a first-person character that has no engineering counterpart. Then give me your honest verdict about the residue: is it a defect in my vocabulary, meaning I am missing concepts I should build, or is it a sign that the graded-sentience debate is leaning on notions it has not earned? Do not force a translation just to be agreeable: if a claim cannot be said in my vocabulary, say so, and do not present a fudged version as the same claim.

If the whole task is confused — for instance, if carrying it out properly already assumes functionalism, or assumes that felt experience is the kind of thing that could be a control-theoretic quantity — tell me plainly before doing the rest, and then do the nearest salvageable version or explain why there is not one.

End with a single line beginning "Summary:" that states the bottom line of the residue report in one sentence.
