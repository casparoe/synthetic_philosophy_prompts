I've been trying to get clear on the omnipotence paradox, and a friend who has read much more philosophy than I have gave me this summary of it:

"The paradox is usually put like this. If God is omnipotent, then He can do anything. So He must be able to create a stone too heavy for Him to lift. But if He can create such a stone, then there is at least one thing He cannot do, namely lift the stone; and if He cannot create it, then there is likewise something He cannot do. Either way, there is something God cannot do, so an omnipotent being cannot exist. The standard reply is to redefine 'omnipotence' as the power to do anything that is logically possible. But this just changes the subject, because a God who is limited by logic is constrained by something outside Himself, which is not omnipotence at all. That is why the paradox is still widely regarded as a serious problem for classical theism."
