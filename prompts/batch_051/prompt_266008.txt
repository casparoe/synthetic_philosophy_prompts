Ok, here's the assignment I want you to do. Write the joint statement that a committed methodological individualist and a committed methodological holist in social science could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Don't give me a diagnosis of why they've been talking past each other or a verdict on who's right — give me the document they'd actually co-author. Assume neither is a charicature: the individualist accepts that social structures, norms, and institutions are real in some sense and can figure in good explanations; the holist isn't claiming society is literally an agent with its own mind above individual people. What they're fighting over is what makes a social explanation a good one.

Format it like this:

1. Agreed Claims. A numbered list of substantive claims both sides sign. Avoid the mushy "both are right, it depends" formulations. Include at least (i) what a social explanation is answerable to — a rate, a pattern, a concrete event — (ii) what ontological status groups, norms, and institutions get on the shared view, and (iii) what counts as an acceptable mechanism or causal story.

2. Disagreements table. For each row: the issue stated neutrally; the individualist's own formulation of their view, worded so a real individualist would say "yes, that's exactly what I mean"; the holist's formulation, same test; and a final column where you say whether you think this row is a genuine disagreement or at least partly terminological.

3. Cruxes and tests. List the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, describe a concrete case or test both sides agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance.

Make sure you wrestle with these specific issues, though feel free to add others: (a) whether macro-level regularities can be autonomous in the sense of supporting counterfactuals that micro-level facts don't by themselves support; (b) whether an acceptable explanation needs microfoundations or merely needs to be compatible with the micro-level facts; (c) the familiar argument that the same aggregate outcome could have arisen from quite different individual psychologies — does that show the macro-level explanation is doing autonomous work, or just that individual-level detail was left out; and (d) what to make of claims like "the institution remembers" or "the group changed its mind" — metaphor, shorthand, or literally true in some sense.
