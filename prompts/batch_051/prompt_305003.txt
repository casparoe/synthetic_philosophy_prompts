I've been asked to help a small foundation figure out what to do with a couple years of theory money, and I keep flip-flopping on what's actually load-bearing. Their premise is that most alignment work has the order backwards: it treats getting the right answer as the goal and interpretability as a bolt-on after the fact. They want to fund conceptual work on making AI outputs legible by construction instead — training models to be understandable, not merely correct, even if that costs some capability. They can support maybe five researchers for two years, mostly philosophers and theoretically-minded ML people, and they definitly don't want another survey of the field; they want a defended research agenda with actual bets.

So here's what I'd like from you. What are the main open conceptual questions in building systems that are legible by construction rather than explained post hoc, and which two or three should the foundation prioritize? I care less about the list than the reasoning: for each candidate, why it's upstream of other work, whether it's tractable with mostly conceptual or small-scale experimental tools rather than expensive training runs, whether it's already crowded or weirdly neglected, and what evidence over the next couple years would bump it up or down. Please also say how you're trading importance against tractability — don't hand me the most fundamental question if nobody can make progress on it. I'd also like you to engage with two worries I have. First, if you train models to sound legible, you might just get models that are good at producing calm, human-approved explanations while the real basis of their decisions stays opaque — so maybe the fidelity of self-explanations has to come before anything else. Second, there's some level of capability loss where the whole legibility-first project stops being worth it, and I don't know whether thats a question to settle up front or something each subproject should assume a stance on.
