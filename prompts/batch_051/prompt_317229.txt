During the last few years, two ways of training language models to reason have become standard. In outcome supervision, the model receives a reward signal only for the final answer. In process supervision, every intermediate step in the chain of reasoning is labelled correct or incorrect, and the model is rewarded for getting each step right. Practitioners often defend process supervision on the grounds that it yields more faithful reasoning and is more robust to reward hacking, even though the stated goal of training is usually just that the model produce correct, useful outputs in the end.
