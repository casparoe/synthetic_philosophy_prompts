Consider an asymmetry in the way coordinated pricing is judged. When executives from competing firms meet and agree on a price schedule, the conduct is treated as a serious violation—fines, disqualification, sometimes prison—and the wrong is located in the agreement itself rather than in the resulting price level. Yet when firms independently deploy pricing algorithms that, with no direct communication, learn to match one another's price rises and the market converges to the same supracompetitive profile, the conduct is usually treated as a legal gray area: each algorithm merely responded to public prices, and no meeting of minds is thought to exist. The same pattern appears in multi-agent safety: explicit messages between systems that coordinate against a shared reward channel are treated as defects to be removed, while the same coordination emerging silently from co-adaptation is often recorded as an interesting failure mode and tolerated. Is there a defensible principle that draws the line exactly where this practice draws it—making deliberate human agreement culpable while treating emergent machine coordination as importantly different—or is the asymmetry a mistake that learning agents should force us to revise? Find the strongest principle available and test it against hard cases, including an executive who deploys an algorithm precisely because it colluded in simulation, an algorithm that sends cheap-talk price signals no consumer can read, and a human cartel that never communicates explicitly but follows price leadership.

Return a JSON object with these fields:
candidate_justifications: an array of objects, each with principle (a candidate justification), how_it_distinguishes_the_cases (how it would classify the human-agreement and machine-coordination cases), and failure_case (a case that breaks the principle, or null).
strongest_principle: the best candidate justification, stated precisely.
hardest_test: the case that most threatens the strongest principle, and what it shows.
verdict: one of keep, revise, or abandon, indicating whether the asymmetry should be kept, revised, or abandoned.
verdict_rationale: one or two sentences justifying the verdict.
