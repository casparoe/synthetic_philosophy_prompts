I keep seeing people say the mental imagery debate is basically settled: Kosslyn's experiments supposedly show we have literal pictures in our heads, or at least something depictive, and Pylyshyn's line that imagery is propositional descriptions underneath lost. I don't think the classic results show anything like that, but I'm also not sure what would. If someone says mental images are depictive rather than propositional, is that a claim about the format of a representation, or just about how it feels from the inside? Design a concrete experiment—or a pair of experiments—that could actually push on the format question, not just show that reaction times scale with distance or rotation angle. Say exactly what pattern of results would count as evidence for a genuinely depictive format and what would count against it. Then say what even a clean positive result would leave unsettled; for instance, could a propositional system with the right tacit knowledge fake it? Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion. Use the fifth point to say how confident you are in the overall answer and which point is least secure.
