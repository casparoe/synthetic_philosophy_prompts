I am a high school student, and I have been trying to make sense of a difference I noticed between how a Danish exchange student who stayed with us and my American relatives talk about government. When her family back home received a letter from the tax authority, she told me their first reaction was that it was probably correct and that any error would be an honest one; when my relatives get a letter from a government agency here, their first reaction is that it is probably mistaken or hiding something and needs to be checked line by line. I do not think this is only about those two agencies. My Danish friend treats government roughly the way I treat the public library: as a service run by people who are basically competent and well-meaning, where the sensible starting assumption is trust unless something specific goes wrong. Many adults I know here treat government more like a used-car salesman: the sensible starting assumption is that the institution is inefficient, self-interested, or deceptive until it proves otherwise. Each side, moreover, seems to regard the other side's starting assumption as obviously irrational — to my friend, the American attitude looks like free-floating paranoia that makes everyday cooperation harder; to my relatives, the Danish attitude looks like gullibility that invites abuse.

I am not asking which country's government is actually more competent or honest. What I am trying to identify is the underlying difference itself: the difference in what a person takes to be the reasonable default attitude toward an institution before evidence about that particular institution comes in, and the related fact that people on each side treat their own default as plain common sense and the other side's default as a bias. Is there an established name for this kind of default posture toward government, or toward institutions generally? And is there a name for the disagreement about which posture is the more reasonable one? If the closest established terms only partly match, I would like to know which comes closest, exactly how the phenomenon I am describing differs from it, and how the pieces fit together; if no standard term fits, I would like you to propose one and mark its boundaries. Please begin your answer by stating in the very first sentence what you take the best label — or labels — to be, or whether you think no established label fits; then give your reasoning, without any preamble or restatement of my question. Also, please separate what is empirically settled about these differences between countries from what remains genuinely contested among people who study the topic, and do not present a contested interpretation as if it were the agreed view.
