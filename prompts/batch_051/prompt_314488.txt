The two positions were basically: (1) the value is to be frugal — a first-person duty of self-restraint, something you owe whether or not it changes anyone else's behavior; and (2) the value is to promote frugality — what matters is the total waste and overconsumption avoided, and your own frugality is just one instrument among others for increasing that total. Our instructor kept pushing us to steelman the other side, and honestly both sides had better moves then I expected.

Could you write me a dialogue between these two views: a deontologically-minded student who holds (1) and a consequentialist-minded student who holds (2)? A couple moves I'd love to see in the mix if they fit (but don't force them): the deontologist worries that treating your own frugality as just one lever among many quietly erodes the stable motive that keeps you frugal when no one is watching; the consequentialist worries that a purely first-person duty leaves no one responsible for the overall level of frugality in a group. Seeding the setting is fine, but keep the arguments front and center.

At the end, add a short "where this bends" note: for each character, name the one consideration most likely to make them revise their position, or the step in their reasoning most likely to fail. I'm less interested in who wins than in seeing where each view gives way.
