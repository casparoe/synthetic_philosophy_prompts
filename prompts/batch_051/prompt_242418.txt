A friend in an evolutionary-psychology lab keeps reaching for the same image whenever I push back on his claims about specialized modules for cheater detection or mate choice: the mind is a Swiss Army knife, not a general-purpose computer. I get the work its doing for him. It's the base metaphor for massive modularity—the view that the mind is mostly or entirely a set of domain-specific adaptations, each shaped by natural selection for some ancestral problem, with no domain-general reasoning engine sitting on top. And I know Fodor's original modularity picture was deliberately narrower: modules were input systems (vision, maybe language), and central cognition was exactly the part he thought could not be modular. So the knife is being used to push past Fodor, not just restate him.

I want to actually audit the metaphor instead of just vibing with it or dismissing it. What corresponds to what, exactly? Is a blade a module? Is the whole knife the mind, or the knife plus the hand? Is the manufacturer natural selection, and is the user the person, the organism, a homunculus, or nothing? And where does domain-general thought go—is there a corkscrew for that, or does the knife just have no slot for it? The real question is which features of a Swiss Army knife the analogy quietly imports into the target: a fixed number of tools, tools that can't share parts or recombine, a designer with foresight, no development or learning, each tool good at one job and only one. Which of those disanalogies actually matter for whether massive modularity follows? Put differently: does the conclusion that cognition is all modules and there's no domain-general processor survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the knife doing the arguing?

Give me the whole thing as a JSON object with these fields:
- bottom_line: one sentence, the strongest claim the Swiss Army knife analogy is actually entitled to support
- correspondence_table: array of objects with keys knife_thing, mind_thing, fit
- fatal_disanalogies: array of objects with keys disanalogy, why_it_matters
- harmless_disanalogies: array of objects with keys disanalogy, why_it_doesnt_matter
- surviving_conclusions: array of strings, conclusions that survive the audit
- unsupported_conclusions: array of strings, conclusions that the analogy was being used to license but that don't survive
- better_analogy: string or null, with a one-sentence justification
