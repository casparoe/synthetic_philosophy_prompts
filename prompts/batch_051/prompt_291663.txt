I'm a novelist, and in the background of the book I'm writing I keep circling the way labels attach to people and change them. My research has led me to Ian Hacking's idea that kinds of people are unlike kinds of rock: a classification of rocks does nothing to the rocks, but a classification of people can alter how the people understand themselves and behave, and that alteration loops back and reshapes the classification — what Hacking calls a looping effect, and what lies behind his distinction between interactive kinds and indifferent kinds. I want a working definition of a looping effect, or equivalently of what makes a kind of person interactive rather than indifferent, sharp enough to handle the following cases: (1) mineral samples classified by crystal structure; (2) the cases Hacking wrote about involving what was then called multiple personality disorder, where the diagnosis's visibility appeared to feed back into how the condition presented and how often it was diagnosed; (3) autism spectrum disorder, which has a strong neurodevelopmental literature behind it but whose diagnostic boundaries have shifted repeatedly and whose diagnosed population now contains a self-advocacy movement that treats the category as an identity; (4) hypertension, where receiving the diagnosis leads people to change their diet and take medication, so the measured distribution among the classified changes even though the underlying vascular facts do not; (5) 'gifted child' as used by a school system, where the label alters instruction, expectations, and the child's self-conception; (6) seasonal affective disorder, where a patient community has formed around the diagnosis and winter low mood is now widely self-ascribed. I regard 1, 2, and 5 as relatively easy — mineral samples indifferent, the multiple-personality cases looping, gifted labeling looping — while 3, 4, and 6 feel genuinely hard to me for different reasons. Please state the definition in your first sentence, without preamble or restating the question, then run it through each case in turn, tell me which of your settled verdicts is least secure, and say whether the cases force the interactive/indifferent distinction to be a matter of degree rather than a clean binary.
