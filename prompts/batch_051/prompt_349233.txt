I have come to treat two properties as jointly criterial for counting something as a conversational implicature in Grice's sense. The first is calculability: a hearer should be able to reconstruct the inference from the literal content of the utterance, the context, and the assumption that the speaker is following the Cooperative Principle and its maxims. The second is cancellability: the speaker should be able to continue by explicitly denying the implicated content without self-contradiction. I have also been letting these two properties do real theoretical work. They are what I use to keep conversational implicature distinct from entailment, presupposition, and conventional implicature, and to say that a piece of content was communicated without being part of the sentence's stable meaning.

I want to know what would count against this position — not the standard objections recited for completeness, but a serious map of where the view is actually exposed. Which observations, constructed cases, or theoretical results should most move me, and which should I be able to shrug off?

Please cover at least the following, and for each say whether it is decisive, seriously damaging, or merely awkward for the view:
(a) apparent conversational implicatures that resist cancellation, or whose explicit denial speakers judge to be contradictory or self-undermining in the way that denying an entailment is, rather than merely unusual;
(b) inferences that are calculable from the maxims or from general rational principles but that a Gricean would still not want to classify as conversational implicatures;
(c) embedded implicatures — scalar or otherwise — that arise under negation, conditionals, or questions, where the assumption that implicature is a post-compositional, utterance-level phenomenon looks strained;
(d) the worry that the Cooperative Principle and the maxims are flexible enough to license almost any inference after the fact, making calculability too cheap to be explanatory;
(e) evidence from acquisition, processing, or cross-linguistic variation that bears on whether calculability and cancellability track a natural distinction rather than a convenient taxonomy.

For each, I want you to say whether a committed Gricean would concede that it counts against them or would instead reclassify the case, and whether the reclassification is principled or ad hoc. I also want you to seriously consider that the position has been insulated: that any troublesome inference can be relabeled as conventional, presuppositional, an explicature (a pragmatically enriched part of what is said), or as something else. If that is right, tell me whether the view has thereby become unfalsifiable, and whether that is a fatal problem or an acceptable price for a taxonomy with wide coverage. Rank the defeaters from the ones that should most move me to the ones that should least move me, and be clear about which pressures are live rather than merely logically possible.

For calibration: I would personally find a case most persuasive if a speaker explicitly denies the would-be implicature and ordinary hearers judge the continuation to be as unacceptable as denying an entailment. Tell me how much weight I should give to that kind of judgment, and whether such cases, if they exist, actually track the conversational/conventional boundary.
