I keep seeing the Nicaraguan Sign Language case brought up in arguments about innateness as if its the slam-dunk: deaf kids in Managua were thrown together in the 1980s with only rough home-sign pidgins, and the younger cohort somehow turned that into a fully grammatical language, so input alone can't be doing the work. I've internalized something like the poverty of the stimulus argument from the usual rationalist hits, and I half-believe the NSL story, but I've never seen anyone actually do the mapping between the two, and I want the forced version.

Take the episode and run it through PoS in both directions. Concretely: what exactly was the "stimulus" the second cohort got, and which features of what they produced genuinely went beyond it? For this to count as a poverty-of-stimulus case, which parts of the story are load-bearing — the noisiness of the input, the absence of certain grammatical markers, the fact that the older kids weren't consistent? The PoS argument I got taught is about normal acquisition: kids converge on rules of the adult grammar that are underdetermined by the evidence they hear, especially because they don't get negative evidence. The NSL thing looks like a different beast — its about a new system getting created, not about converging on an existing one. So tell me whether this is really the same argument, or two different arguments that both get filed under "something must be innate" and nobody notices the difference.

Then run it the other way: what does the episode do to PoS? Does it actually confirm the nativist claim, or does it mostly show that children regularize noisy, variable input in ways a domain-general learner could pull off? I'm especially interested in the first cohort: if they were already constructing structure bit by bit, then the second cohort's input was richer than the word "pidgin" lets on, and the across-the-board leap shrinks. I'm not after a debunk — I want to know which parts of the nativist reading survive even against a hostile reading, and where people are just asserting that input couldn't have been enough.

And end with the honest part: what would we need to know about the actual signing environment and timeline that we probably don't, and how much should that uncertainty discount anyone citing this as the settled proof?
