I keep chewing on a parallel between two things that don't usually get compared, and I can't tell whether it's deep or just a seductive metaphor. On one side, the chain-of-thought faithfulness debate worries that when a model says "first I do X, then Y, so the answer is Z," that explanation may be a fluent post-hoc story rather than a report of the process that actually produced the answer. On the other side, split-brain patients sometimes confabulate: ask the speaking hemisphere why the left hand just did something, and it supplies a smooth causal story that couldn't have been the real reason, since the hemisphere doing the talking didn't have access to the hemisphere that issued the command. Both cases look like the same structure to me: a verbal system observes an output and narrates a plausible causal history without access to the real mechanism.

Am I onto something, or does this fall apart on inspection? My own hunch is that chain-of-thought is generated before the final answer, so it can be causally upstream rather than a retrospective gloss, but I'm not sure that actually breaks the analogy.
