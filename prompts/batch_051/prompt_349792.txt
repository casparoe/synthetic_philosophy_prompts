Here's my opening. Two flatmates, Ana and Bo, one copy of a book, and three possible outcomes: Ana reads it, Bo reads it, or nobody reads it. Ana is a bit prudish: her favorite outcome is that nobody reads it, but if one of them is going to read it she'd rather read it herself than have Bo read it, because she thinks it would mess with Bo. Bo is an enthusiast: his favorite outcome is that Ana reads it (he has his reasons), and he'd rather read it himself than have it go unread.

Now I'd have thought each person should at least decide for themselves whether they read the book. Ana prefers nobody-reads to Ana-reads, so her right to decide for herself gives us nobody-reads beats Ana-reads. Bo prefers Bo-reads to nobody-reads, so his right gives us Bo-reads beats nobody-reads. And both of them prefer Ana-reads to Bo-reads, so Pareto says Ana-reads beats Bo-reads. Put those together and you get a cycle: Ana-reads beats Bo-reads beats nobody-reads beats Ana-reads. So which link do you break — and why shouldn't I just keep the personal-sphere rights and tell Pareto to get lost when they conflict?
