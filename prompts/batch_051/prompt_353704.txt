ok so in my youth group we've been having the same arguement since like october and it's making me nuts. it's the faith-and-works thing between Paul and James. one side basically lives on this line from the Epistle of James (chapter 2 verse 24, King James Version): "Ye see then how that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only." the other side says Paul is equally clear that a person is justified by faith and not by doing works of the law, and that if justification worked like a paycheck then people could boast. everyone agrees both sentences are in the Bible, everyone agrees the words mean what the words say, and yet nobody has moved in months. my working theory is that we aren't actually disagreeing about the verses but about what kind of question the verses are answering — like Paul is asking how someone gets into the covenant people in the first place and James is asking how you can tell a real faith from a dead one, and the word "justified" just gets stretched across both. but it could also be that we're fighting over whether "works" means the same thing for both of them, or over whether a statement like James 2:24 is supposed to be read as a definition or as a test. what i want is a diagnosis, not a win for one side: is this a genuine substantive disagreement, a mostly verbal one, two different questions under one heading, or a fight about different standards of evidence? and given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a distinction, a better translation, a different question — or should i just expect us to go in circles forever?
