I've spent too long in the rationalist/EA blog comment sections, and there's a configuration that keeps showing up in sweatshop debates that none of the usual words seem to carve quite right. Suppose someone takes a garment factory job in Dhaka. Their next-best option is worse: harder work, lower pay, more risk. The employer could double the wage and still make plenty; the foreign buyer could pay a few cents more per shirt and never notice. The exchange is voluntary and leaves both sides better off than no deal at all. Same shape shows up in kidney-selling and landlord-tenant arguments. The defender says: it's mutually beneficial, and boycotts or bans just hurt the people you claim to care about. The critic says: c'mon, they're still being exploited, the terms are grossly unequal. Both sound right, and they aren't exactly contradicting eachother. I want to know if there's an established term for this exact thing: exploitation that's voluntary and Pareto-improving relative to no deal, where the wrong (if there is one) isn't coercion, isn't fraud, and isn't making anyone worse off than their fallback. If there is a term, what distinguishes it from ordinary unfairness, plain unequal bargaining power, and coercive exploitation? If the term is contested or doesn't exist, what's the closest concept and where does it break? I care about the boundaries more than the label. End with \answer{...} containing the best name for the configuration, or 'no established name' plus your coinage.
