While reading Daniel Dennett's essay Quining Qualia, I got stuck on the section where he introduces two coffee tasters, Chase and Sanborn. Dennett has both men say they no longer enjoy the coffee they once liked. He gives Chase this story: the coffee tastes exactly the same as it always did, but what changed is that Chase now has more refined standards and judges the same taste more harshly. Sanborn gets the opposite story: his standards have not changed, but the coffee no longer tastes to him the way it used to. Dennett then argues that no possible test, behavioral or physiological or introspective, could force us to choose between saying the quale changed and saying only the judgment of it changed, because any result can be redescribed as belonging to one side or the other.

My friend took that passage and turned it into a formal argument, and I cannot find the exit. The argument goes like this. (1) If qualia exist, then in any case where someone reports that their experience has changed, there must be a determinate fact about whether the qualitative property itself changed or only the person's standards or judgments changed. (2) In the Chase and Sanborn case, every possible observation we could make is equally consistent with the quale-changed story and the standards-changed story. (3) If two hypotheses are consistent with every possible observation, then there is no fact of the matter which one is true. (4) Therefore, in the Chase and Sanborn case, there is no fact of the matter whether the qualia changed or the standards changed. (5) Therefore qualia do not exist, because qualia were supposed to be precisely what would make such a fact determinate. My friend says this same two-step can be run on any proposed quale, since a change in judgment can always be substituted for a change in the quality itself. I would like to keep qualia, and step (3) looks especially suspicious to me, but I am not sure I can reject it without also giving up other unobservable things I believe in. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
