A friend in a philosophy of language course summarized the later Wittgenstein for me as follows:

"Wittgenstein's decisive break with the Tractatus is that he replaced the picture theory with a doctrine of language games. Once you see that meaning is just what a word does in the game—the way a chess piece's identity is what it can do on the board—the old idea that words stand for objects drops away. And because all concepts are held together only by family resemblances, no word has necessary and sufficient conditions for its application. It follows that there are no real definitions, and questions like 'What is knowledge?' or 'What is art?' are pseudo-problems generated by a craving for an essence. Wittgenstein's positive advice is therefore to stop theorizing and just describe ordinary usage case by case. His discussion of games is meant to show, for example, that there is literally nothing all games share."

Some of this strikes me as too strong, but I cannot say exactly where the summary turns Wittgenstein into something he is not. Go through it sentence by sentence and tell me what is accurate, what is a simplification, and what Wittgenstein himself would reject or insist on qualifying. For each questionable claim, give the corrected version and say whether the general moral—that philosophy should replace explanation with description of usage—still follows. Bear in mind the difference between the early and later Wittgenstein, but do not recount the Tractatus in full.
