I keep going back and forth on a line in Aristotle's Physics, Book II, Part 1, and I'd like a careful walkthrough before I teach it. The passage is:

"As an indication of this Antiphon points out that if you planted a bed and the rotting wood acquired the power of sending up a shoot, it would not be a bed that would come up, but wood-which shows that the arrangement in accordance with the rules of the art is merely an incidental attribute, whereas the real nature is the other, which, further, persists continuously through the process of making."

Here is my problem. Aristotle has just distinguished things that exist by nature from artifacts by saying natural things have an internal principle of motion and rest, and artifacts don't — except incidentally insofar as they're made of stone or wood or whatever. Then Antiphon shows up with something like: if you planted a bed and the wood started growing, you'd get a tree, not a bed. Which seems to suggest the wood, the matter, is the real nature, and the shape imposed by the carpenter is just an arrangement. Aristotle reports this, then immediately turns around and says the form is nature more than the matter, because a thing is what it is when it has attained fulfillment rather than when it exists potentially. But the bed-sprouting example seems to cut the other way: the persisting thing, the thing with the power to grow, is the wood. So why isn't Antiphon's argument decisive? Where exactly does Aristotle think it goes wrong, or what does he think it shows? And how does the turn to form here connect back to the later argument that natural processes happen for the sake of something — I can see how form could be the end, but the bed example seems to make form look like just a shape imposed from outside, which is exactly the kind of thing he says natural form is not. So maybe walk through the passage sentence by sentence and unpack the matter/form relation Aristotle is trying to establish, and what it commits him to about how final causes operate in organisms as opposed to artifacts.
