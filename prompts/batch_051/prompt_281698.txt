So my friend and I got into this long fight about photography and I keep turning it over. He was arguing for the transparency view, the one that says a photograph literally lets you see through to the thing photographed, so looking at a snapshot of your grandmother is seeing her, not just a representation of her, the way a mirror or a window lets you see her. I kept objecting that this is nonsense: a photograph is a flat colored surface made after the fact, and my visual system is responding to the print or the screen, not to a woman who isn't there anymore. He said I was just assuming a cramped account of seeing. We went in circles for an hour and neither of us gave ground.

What I want to know is whether this is even the kind of claim that can be tested, and if so how. Imagine you could run an experiment comparing how people look at photographs, windows, mirrors, and photorealistic paintings: eye tracking, reaction times, that weird thing where people point at where they take themselves to be seeing, maybe fMRI of face or scene areas. If the photo condition pattern-matched the mirror condition, would that definately show photographs are transparent? If it pattern-matched the painting condition, would that show they aren't? Or would either result just be a fact about processing, with the philosophical claim left standing either way? Concretely, how would you design the best test of the transparency claim, what outcome would favor each side, and what would the test still leave open?
