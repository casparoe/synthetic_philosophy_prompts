I cover science and ideas for a general-audience outlet, so I repeatedly have to assess insecticide and pest-control proposals — public-health vector control, agricultural seed treatments, urban baiting — in which the human benefit is quantified and the insects are written off as pests. I want a reusable decision procedure, grounded in animal ethics and the philosophy of mind, for determining whether and how insect interests should enter the moral evaluation of such a measure: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure returns a determinate verdict, where it hands the decision to a human because the remaining question is one of values, and where it says insect interests count but are dominated by human interests. Make it usable by a journalist and an editor with no formal ethics training in under an hour per case, and be explicit about which steps are real procedure and which are judgment dressed up as procedure. The procedure has to handle the cases that lazy reporting trips over: uncertainty about whether a given insect group is sentient, the difference between nociception and felt pain, very large numbers of insects with very small per-insect stakes, whether deaths caused by spraying count differently from deaths allowed by not spraying, and human interests that run from preventing epidemic disease to protecting a cosmetic lawn. Then run it on three cases — indoor residual spraying against malaria-carrying mosquitoes, a neonicotinoid seed treatment at landscape scale, and a homeowner's ant bait in a kitchen — and make one of them yield an answer a cost-benefit-minded reader will not like; say which one and why the procedure insists.

Present the procedure itself first as a table with exactly these columns: Step; Question to ask; Sufficient answer; Next step; Fact or value call; How this step can misfire. Then a short prose section applying it to the three cases. End with a JSON object with exactly these fields: principles_imported, steps, case_verdicts, unresolved_value_calls, hardest_case, sharpest_disagreement_with_standard_practice.
