I have been using a distinction I want checked. I call the first variable epistemic load: how many, and how sharply formulated, are the propositions a member is expected to assent to. I call the second practical load: how much of daily life the tradition regulates through ritual, diet, dress, fasting, or role-specific obligations. I think ordinary talk of 'religion' runs these two together, and that most definitional disputes about the word come from that conflation. On the two-axis picture, Confucianism is low on epistemic load and high on practical load: one can be an exemplary Confucian by performing rites and fulfilling relational roles while remaining agnostic or vague about supernatural or metaphysical claims. A certain stripped-down secular humanism sits low on practical load but carries a nontrivial epistemic load of its own, since it asks members to accept a picture of human flourishing and the unavailability of the divine. Evangelical Protestantism is high on both axes, and a fully practiced Theravada Buddhism is also high on both, though the propositions it loads onto members are mostly about suffering, impermanence, and liberation rather than a creator deity. I would also place an observant Marxist or a committed member of a twelve-step program somewhere on the plane.

I want to know whether this distinction is coherent and whether it is already standard under another name. My stronger claim is that the reason 'Is Confucianism a religion?' and 'Is Buddhism a religion?' keep producing sterile arguments is that the category 'religion' flattens two independent variables; once you keep them apart, the yes/no question dissolves into 'which axis do you want the word to track?' Is anything actually being dissolved there, or have I replaced one contested folk word with two coordinates that are themselves contested to assign? And am I smuggling in a Protestant assumption that belief and practice can be separated in the first place? Where exactly does the two-axis model break down if one actually tries to apply it across traditions?
