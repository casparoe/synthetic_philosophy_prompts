I'm an undergraduate computer science major, but this term I'm taking an upper-level metaphysics course, and we've just finished the unit on Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition. My native framework is typed functional programming — Haskell or Standard ML: types and terms, arrow types, currying, higher-order functions, partial application, application and beta-reduction, and evaluation to normal form. I have a standing hunch that this debate is really about function application, but I want to know what breaks under the translation.

The problem, as the course presents it, is this. A proposition or state of affairs is supposed to be more than an aggregate of its constituents: Socrates and the property of being wise, taken as a mere pair or set, are not yet the proposition that Socrates is wise. Something must unify them. Bradley's objection is that a relation invoked to do the unifying is itself just another constituent, so unless the relation is already doing its relating, we need a further relation to relate it to its terms, and so on; but if the relation just relates, the tie has been taken as primitive and unexplained. Frege's reply, as we read it, is that the regress comes from treating every constituent as if it were the same kind of thing: objects are complete (saturated), whereas concepts and relations are incomplete (unsaturated) and essentially take objects as arguments, so the unity is already in the nature of the incomplete constituent rather than in an added item.

Please restate this debate — the initial problem, Bradley's objection, and Frege's reply — in the vocabulary of typed functional programming, as faithfully as you can. Make your modeling decisions explicit: are objects terms of ground type? are relations curried functions returning Bool? is the unified proposition the closed term, its normal form, or its type? Do not quietly build the philosophical solution into your chosen constructs and then announce that it has translated; flag every place where you had to choose.

Then give me the residue report: what maps cleanly, what maps only with distortion, and what has no counterpart in the programming vocabulary at all. I especially want you to address these sticking points rather than skip them. First, application is a primitive of the language and of its semantics, so is the framework dissolving Bradley's regress or just relocating it into the metalanguage by refusing to analyze the tie further? Second, a closed term like wise(socrates) evaluates to a truth value, and a truth value is as saturated as any object, so where did the proposition — the thing with content, capable of being true or false — go? If you introduce a distinct proposition type to carry it, say whether that is analysis or relabeling. Third, does the syntax and parse tree of wise(socrates) account for unity, or is that still a mere aggregate of symbols? Fourth, does currying genuinely model a relation's relating of two terms, or does it replace a relation with a chain of one-place unsaturated items whose intermediate stages, such as loves(socrates), have no obvious correlate in the metaphysics?

Finally, tell me what you think the residue shows: whether the programming vocabulary is missing something the metaphysics needs, whether the philosophical dispute is partly an artifact of treating all constituents as one ontological sort, or some mix. Throughout, separate what is settled in this literature from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
