Write a short research proposal, roughly 1,500 words, for a graduate workshop on decision-making under radical uncertainty. The proposal should address whether an agent who takes the simulation argument seriously should sharply discount the expected value of influencing the far future, relative to what longtermist reasoning would otherwise recommend. Treat longtermist reasoning, for purposes of the proposal, as the view that extremely low probabilities of astronomically large future value can legitimately dominate present decisions.

In the proposal, state the question as a determinate thesis about decision-making under uncertainty, not as a vague unease. Separate at least three readings of the dampening claim: that a simulated far future has no moral weight; that simulation reasoning redirects probability mass away from a single base-level future toward an unknown branching set of simulator-dependent futures, thereby undermining the estimates on which fanatical reasoning depends; and that acting inside a simulation to shape its distant future is like acting inside a process whose continuation and stopping conditions are controlled by hidden designers. Explain why the first reading is too quick, and why the second and third readings raise distinct problems for expected-value fanaticism rather than a single objection.

Then lay out the approach you would use to make progress. The approach may be argumentative, decision-theoretic, or model-based, but it must specify in advance what would count as success and what would count as failure. Say precisely what a successful defense of the dampening thesis would need to show, what it would mean for the simulation argument to leave longtermist fanaticism untouched, and which parts of the dispute are likely to survive any project of this size. Do not include a bibliography or request citations; refer to positions by name and indicate in one sentence where references would be added later. Finally, discuss a realistic scope for one semester and identify the point at which the project would need to be divided into two papers.
