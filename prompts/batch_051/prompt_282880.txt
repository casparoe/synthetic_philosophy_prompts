I'm writing a short statement of where I stand on religion and political justification for a political philosophy reading group, and I'd like a consistency audit before I circulate it. My commitments are these:

(1) When the law coercively restricts or burdens people, it must rest on reasons that all reasonable citizens can accept; a law therefore cannot be based solely on reasons internal to one religious tradition.
(2) A legislator may be moved in conscience by religious conviction when voting for such a law, provided there are also sound nonreligious reasons for the law that all reasonable citizens could accept.
(3) In public debate about coercive laws, citizens may introduce religious arguments, as long as suitable public reasons are supplied in due course.
(4) Public justification fails when the reasons an official offers publicly are not the reasons that actually moved them; a secular rationale assembled after the fact for a conclusion reached on religious grounds is not genuine justification.
(5) These demands bind judges and legislators in their official roles more strictly than ordinary citizens, and I do not take them to govern how citizens speak to one another in churches, families, or voluntary associations.

Please audit the set rather than smooth it over: go through the commitments and tell me which specific pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be; rank the genuine tensions by severity and say, for each, which commitment would have to give; then recommend the single cheapest revision and what it costs. Structure your reply as three sections, in this order: Genuine Tensions, Ranked (about 250 words); Apparent Tensions That Dissolve (about 150 words); Cheapest Repair (about 150 words).
