Please explain the conceptual difference between amplifying a piece of propaganda and reproducing it for the sake of critique, without treating the distinction as merely a matter of the reproducer's intention or of whether the audience is actually harmed. Consider a platform that displays a propaganda video alongside a fact-check overlay: defenders say this inoculates viewers, while critics say it launders the content to a wider audience. First say what would have to be true for the distinction between amplification and critique to do real work rather than simply track whether we approve of the outcome. Then distinguish three claims that might be held about such reproduction: that it predictably causes harm, that it is morally impermissible for individuals, and that platforms may be required to suppress it. Explain whether one can coherently accept the first claim while rejecting the second or third, and on what grounds. Finally, give the most plausible account of the criteria that separate critique from amplification once intent, framing, and audience effect are all weighed.
