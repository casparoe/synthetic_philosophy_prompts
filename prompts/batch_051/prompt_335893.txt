I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy or economics, and I am building a multi-agent simulation of an economy. Each agent privately holds local information about production conditions that is partly inarticulate — the kind of thing that shows up in habits, on-the-spot judgments, and local context rather than in a report the agent could file. I need to choose the coordination mechanism: the protocol that decides what gets produced, which inputs are used, and how output is distributed. I have been reading about the socialist calculation debate — roughly, Mises's claim that rational economic calculation requires actual market prices for the means of production, Hayek's extension of that point to dispersed and tacit knowledge, and Lange's reply that a central planning board could simulate a market by announcing parametric prices and adjusting them in response to shortages and surpluses. I want to turn that debate into an engineering specification rather than read it as intellectual history.

Please write the desiderata: the list of requirements that any proposed coordination mechanism must satisfy in order to count as solving the allocation-and-calculation problem those authors were arguing about. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp (Hayekian, Langean, or otherwise). Phrase each requirement in terms I can act on as an engineer: what information the mechanism must be able to read, what it must be able to compute from that information, what incentives it must give agents to reveal truthfully and then act on the result, and how it must behave as conditions change.

Make sure the list addresses at least the following pressure points: the dispersion of knowledge that cannot be articulated as a report; the need for a common unit in which heterogeneous inputs and outputs can be compared; the difference between prices formed by actual exchange and parametric prices announced by a planning board; the incentives facing enterprise managers; the treatment of capital goods and time; and adaptation to changing technology and preferences.

Then identify which subsets of these requirements cannot be jointly satisfied by any single mechanism, and explain why each tension is real rather than merely apparent. I am especially interested in two: (a) using all locally held, partly tacit information while also centralizing computation in one node; and (b) having a central board set prices by trial and error while still giving managers hard budget constraints. Tell me which single requirement does the most work to eliminate candidate mechanisms.

Finally, briefly score three candidates against the list — fully centralized planning, Lange-style trial-and-error market socialism, and an actual competitive market with private ownership — but keep the scoring short; the criteria are what I am after.

Do not include citations, a reading list, or references to specific books or articles. If you use a technical term, define it in one clause. Avoid relying on material I would need to look up.
