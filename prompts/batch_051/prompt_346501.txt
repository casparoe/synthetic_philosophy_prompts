I'm on the faith-and-curriculum committee at a small Christian liberal arts college. A biology professor (a practicing member of our own tradition) has proposed a required evolution module for the intro course, and it includes the fossil evidence that predation, parasitism, and bodily pain in animals predate humans by hundreds of millions of years — which seems to put animal suffering before the Fall — plus a unit on parasitoid wasps that lay eggs in still-living caterpillars. Some of us think this plainly contradicts our statement of faith, which treats creation as good and death as something that comes into the world through human sin. Others reply that the statement was never meant too address animal theodicy, and that forcing students to choose between the fossil record and our doctrine will do more damage than teaching the science straight. Draft the memo we'll vote on next Thursday: should we approve the module, approve it with conditions, or send it back, and what should we actually put in writing? Give us a concrete recommendation and spend real time on the strongest consideration on the side your recommendation goes against.
