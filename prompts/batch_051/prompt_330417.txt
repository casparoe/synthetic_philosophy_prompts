I am presenting a paper next month to a philosophy department whose faculty mostly work in formal ethics and consequentialist theory. The paper defends a Taurek-style thesis: in a choice between saving one person and saving five strangers, when all other things are equal, the fact that the larger group contains more people is not itself a reason to save the five, and morality may permit saving the one. My argument roughly is that pairwise equal regard rules out aggregating the separate claims of different individuals into a group claim; each person can complain from her own point of view, and the one person’s complaint is no weaker than any single person’s among the five. Prepare me for the question period. What will this audience object to, in their terms, which objections have real force, what should I concede up front, and what is the one reply that must land for the paper to survive? I am less worried about broad anti-consequentialist objections than about people who accept aggregation elsewhere but reject it here. Please write it as continuous prose, as in an email, with no headings, bullets, bold text, or tables.
