I am a doctoral student in philosophy of science, and I have been asked to open the final session of a graduate seminar on the interpretation of probability. Over the term we have worked through the three main families of position: frequentism, on which a probability is the limit of a relative frequency in a suitable repeatable sequence of trials; subjectivism, on which it is an agent's coherent degree of belief, exhibited in the odds the agent would accept; and propensity interpretations, on which it is an objective tendency of a chance setup to produce a given outcome on a particular kind of trial. I would like to begin the session with a short piece of verse that re-stages the debate rather than name-drops, and I would like it to stand or fall on whether a philosopher of probability would find it accurate.

Could you write a villanelle with two refrains, one voiced by the frequentist and one by the subjectivist? The frequentist's refrain should assert, in effect, that the probability just is the long-run relative frequency, and should keep demanding an appropriate reference class or collective; the subjectivist's refrain should answer, in effect, by asking what odds the thinker would accept, since a probability that no mind uses is inert. In the tercets between the refrains, please keep returning to an unrepeatable or single-case event — this coin, this patient, this storm — because the single case is the pressure point on which both views strain. The form should do the philosophical work: the villanelle's insistence on returning lines should dramatize the frequentist's commitment to repetition, while the subjectivist's refrain should keep intruding to remind the reader that even the long run is a fact someone must believe in and act upon.

In the final quatrain, when the two refrains finally meet in the closing couplet, I want their juxtaposition to open onto the propensity view without resolving into either of them: the reader should feel the unmet need for something objective yet genuinely about the single case — neither a limit in an imagined sequence nor a merely private degree of belief. The poem need not mention any philosopher by name, but someone who knows the debate should be able to tell which refrain belongs to which view and why they end up side by side without settling the disagreement.
