My brother and I have been going back and forth about whether people are ever really morally responsible for the things they do. He's been reading some philosopher—Galen Strawson, I think?—and has an argument he calls the basic argument: roughly, to be truly responsible for an action you'd have to be responsible for the character or frame of mind that produced it, and to be responsible for that you'd have to be responsible for whatever made you that way, and so on backward, but you obviously can't be responsible for how you started out, so the whole chain of responsibility never gets going. Now he goes around saying nobody is ever actually praiseworthy or blameworthy, not even a torturer or someone who spends their whole life helping refugees.

I dont know if he's right, but it seems to me part of the problem is that nobody in these arguments ever stops to define what "morally responsible" means in the first place. So I want a working definition of moral responsibility that gets these cases right, in the ordinary sense of "she's morally responsible for that": a healthy adult who steals a phone from a store; a four-year-old who draws on the walls; a man with a brain tumor pressing on his frontal lobes who suddenly attacks a nurse; a person forced at gunpoint to drive a getaway car; an adult who commits a similar assault after being horrifically abused as a child; and an ordinary person who lies on his own taxes because money's been tight for years. I'm confident the first is a clear yes and the four-year-old and brain-tumor cases are clear no's, but the other three I keep flipping on. Please give me the definition, run it through all six cases, tell me where it surprises you or where you'd want to revise it, and say whether you think my brother's argument survives a sensible definition or just depends on using the phrase in an inflated way. No headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables; just continuous prose like an email, and write in American English.
