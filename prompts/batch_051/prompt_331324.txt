Two people in my reading group have argued for months about whether the Scientific Revolution — and behind it, the Middle Ages and the Renaissance — are real joints in the history of ideas or mostly retrospective inventions. One is a periodization realist: she thinks the label picks out a genuine, concentrated shift in how people did natural philosophy, even if the edges are fuzzy. The other is a constructivist: he thinks it's a Whiggish story we impose later, full of continuities (medieval predecessors, earlier twelfth-century renaissances) that don't fit the clean break. Draft the joint statement they should have produced: what both can sign, the remaining disagreements in wording each side would accept as fair, and the cruxes. I'd guess the real cruxes are whether a period has to be experienced by the people living through it and whether the label is doing real explanatory work or just filing-cabinet work. For each crux, give a concrete kind of evidence or case both would agree bears on it, and what each side predicts. If part of their fight is mostly about words, say so and untangle it. Continuous prose, like a joint letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
