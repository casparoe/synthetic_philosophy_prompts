Consider the following clause from the code of ethics of a professional mediation association, drafted in the early 1990s, before machine-learning decision aids were in use:

"A mediator shall not make a recommendation unless the mediator can state the reasons supporting it. A mediator shall not offer as a reason any consideration that the mediator does not sincerely hold to be a reason."

A commercial mediator is handling a dispute in which the parties are far apart. After a full day of negotiation, the mediator runs the parties' position statements, the evidentiary summaries, and the mediator's own case notes through a proprietary machine-learning tool that returns a recommended settlement amount and a confidence score but no explanation of its grounds. The mediator has used the tool many times, has found its recommendations sensible, and now believes, on that basis, that the amount is a fair resolution. The parties ask why the mediator is recommending that amount.

Suppose the mediator says: "I recommend this amount because, in my assessment, it is the fairest resolution of the dispute." In fact, the mediator has no independent route to the figure; the mediator's actual basis is the tool's output plus past experience of its reliability.

Suppose instead the mediator says: "I recommend this amount because a tool I have found reliable recommends it, though I cannot tell you why it recommends it."

How should the clause apply to each answer? Work through what the words of the clause, its evident purpose, what the drafters would likely have said if asked, and what a reasonable party hearing the answer would take it to mean would each yield. Is there a version of the clause that would condemn the first answer without also forbidding legitimate reliance on reliable but opaque instruments — and if there is not, what does that show about what the clause was protecting: the sincerity of the mediator, or the legibility of the recommendation to the parties? Does the mediator here have a duty to be legible beyond a duty not to deceive?
