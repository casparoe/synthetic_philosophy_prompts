You're an expert on collective decision-making, and I'd like you to think this through carefully before drafting. I'm on the three-person review panel for my department's small-grants competition. The stated rule is that a proposal gets funded only if its strong on both significance and feasibility. We all read the same proposal and our individual judgments are perfectly consistent:

- Marta finds it significant and feasible, so she votes to fund.
- Jonas finds it significant but not feasible, so he votes not to fund.
- Priya finds it not significant but feasible, so she votes not to fund.

Now here's the bind. If we aggregate criterion by criterion, significance passes 2–1 and feasibility passes 2–1, so by our own rule the proposal clears both bars. But if we vote on the bottom line, fund-or-don't, it's 1–2 against funding. The chair asked me to draft a short memo to circulate before Friday's meeting recommending how the panel should resolve this. Please write that memo. It has to land on an actual procedure we could adopt (not just a way to settle this one proposal), and it should honestly engage the strongest argument for whichever procedure you reject. Also give us a short note on whether and how either procedure could be gamed by strategic voting, since a couple of us are already suspicious of that. Start by laying out what each option treats as "the committee's judgment," then think through which one we should commit to.
