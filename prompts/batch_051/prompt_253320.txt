I am genuinely torn about objective Bayesianism. On one side I am drawn to the maximum entropy principle because it appears to be the only rule that turns sparse constraints -- a known mean, a symmetry, an interval bound -- into a uniquely rational prior, rather than licensing every prior equally. On the other side I have a firm conviction that which prior it is rational to adopt cannot depend on how the very same possibilities are described; and yet, for continuous parameters, the maximum entropy recipe is not invariant under reparameterisation: a density that is uniform over a coin's bias is not uniform over the log-odds of that same bias, so the rule gives conflicting answers depending on the variable used to state the problem. I do not want a verdict about which side wins. I want to know what the parameterisation objection actually shows, and whether it should lead me to abandon the principle, to amend it -- for example by treating the problem's description as itself part of the evidence -- or to reinterpret the notion of a rational prior so that both my attraction to the principle and my conviction survive, and what would justify each of these moves. Please write the answer in British English.
