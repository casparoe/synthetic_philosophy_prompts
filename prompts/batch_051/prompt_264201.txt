I keep detecting what looks like a single mechanism in two bodies of thought that are never discussed together, and I cannot tell whether the resemblance is structural or merely suggestive.

One side is Douglas Hofstadter's Copycat work on analogy and fluid concepts. The program solves letter-string analogy puzzles—of the kind abc is to abd as xyz is to what—and it does so by treating concepts such as successor, predecessor, leftmost, rightmost, and letter-category not as fixed definitions but as items that can slip under analogical pressure. Depending on how the new string resists the obvious reading, the program may let successor become predecessor, or rightmost become leftmost, or even let a doubled pair of letters behave as a single unit; the deformation is accepted only if it yields a better overall analogy. A temperature parameter controls how readily these slips occur, so the program tries small, local distortions before it will countenance larger ones.

The other side is common-law reasoning by analogy. A rule formulated in terms of a category—a statute that forbids vehicles in the park—gets extended to an electric scooter, a motorized wheelchair, or a ceremonial gun carriage, and the concept vehicle quietly changes its boundaries. This does not look like arbitrary redefinition, nor like mechanical deduction; it looks like the same operation as slippage. The judge deforms the category locally in the direction the new case demands, while trying to preserve the best overall fit among the text of the rule, its point, the pattern of prior cases, and the costs of the decision. Lawyers call slices of this distinguishing, reading in an exception, or following the spirit rather than the letter, but the underlying operation strikes me as concept-slippage under analogical pressure, with precedent and statutory purpose playing the role of temperature.

The parallel I want checked is this: copycat-style conceptual slippage and common-law analogical extension are the same kind of computation—context-driven, coherence-preserving deformation of a category, where the degree of distortion is set by analogical pressure against a cost for novelty. Is that a deep identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion?

I want to know which parts of this are load-bearing and which are just a nice redescription.
