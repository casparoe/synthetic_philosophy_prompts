I've been turning over this concept from the free will side of things, "sphexishness." You probably know the wasp example: a sphex paralyzes a cricket, drags it to the entrance of its burrow, leaves it outside while it does a quick check inside, comes back out, and drags the cricket in. If you move the cricket a few inches while the wasp is inside, it comes out, drags the cricket back to the entrance, and then does the check *again*. You can loop it like this indefinitely; the wasp will never once skip the check and just drag the cricket in. From outside it looks like caution, but its actually just a fixed action pattern — a subroutine with nobody home.

Write a short story, maybe 700 to 1200 words, built around that idea. But don't do the obvious "guy slowly realizes he's a sphex loop and it's hopeless" version. The story should come from inside someone or something that is partially aware of its own loops and is trying, with mixed results, to distinguish real flexibility from fancier sphex routines. Setting can be whatever you want — a person, an uploaded mind, a customer-service AI, a small town, a bureaucracy, an alien. The key beat I'm after: at some point the reader should experience the flip from "that was a choice I don't understand" to "oh. that was a subroutine." It's fine if the story itself has a slightly sphexish structure, just don't be to cute about it.
