Been reading two bodies of work in parallel and I keep flip-floping on whether they're actually the same argument or just rhyme. One is the deepfake/epistemic-backstop stuff from evidence law and media theory: once generative video is cheap, "the tape shows X" can't do much work by itself, because the tape is exactly the kind of thing that's now in question. The standard reply is that we still have authentication routes — chain of custody, signed capture, original devices, witnesses, institutional archives — and at some point the regress of "but couldn't that be forged too?" has to stop in something treated as reliable enough. People call this the epistemic backstop.

The other is Agrippa's trilemma and foundationalism. A belief needs a reason, the reason needs a reason, and you either go on forever, loop, or stop at a basic belief that's somehow justified without further reasons. Foundationalism says the regress has to stop in something self-supporting, something you don't have to justify by appeal to anything else.

The structural parallel feels strong to me: both start from a regress of pretty much the same form — "how do you know the recording isn't fake?" / "how do you know the belief is true?" — and both answer by positing a point where the demand for further backing is refused. And both get accused of arbitrariness, because once you're willing to exempt something from the regress you need a reason for the exemption, and that reason seems to restart the whole thing.

But here's where I get stuck. A foundational belief is supposed to be special in kind: self-evident, infallible, or at least not justified by other beliefs. An epistemic backstop isn't special in kind at all — a custody log or a signed file is perfectly fakeable, it's just more expensive to fake than its worth. So maybe the deepfake backstop is a purely pragmatic, cost-based stopping point, while foundationalism wants a justificatory stopping point, and the analogy only looks deep because both happen to involve stopping a regress. Is there actually a deep identity here — should foundationalism just be reframed as "all justification bottoms out in cost-asymmetric backstops"? Or does the pragmatic/epistemic distinction do all the work, making the parallel superficial?
