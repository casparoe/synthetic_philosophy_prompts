Last night I got into a long argument with a friend about the familiar puzzle in which you compare one person being tortured for a lifetime with an astronomically large number of people each getting a single speck of dust in the eye. My friend insisted that no number of specks can outweigh the torture: however many trivial nuisances you pile up, the severe harm remains worse, because there is something discontinuous, almost lexical, between genuine suffering and mere annoyance. I took the other side: if a speck is unpleasant at all, then enough well-defined increments of unpleasantness ought to match, and eventually exceed, the torture, and refusing to say so looks like scope insensitivity in reverse. We went back and forth for hours and ended up not knowing what, if anything, could settle it.

I now think part of our disagreement is empirical and part is not. Empirical question: do people actually have a stable, unit-consistent severity scale on which very small harms aggregate, or do their trade-off judgments shift with framing, magnitude, and whether the harms fall on one person or many? Part of the difficulty is that in the original puzzle the specks are scattered across different people, so any scale would have to be interpersonal, not just a single person's pain meter.

Here is the task. Treat the following as a claim to be tested, not a conclusion to defend: People's comparative judgments about severe and very small harms reflect an underlying severity scale robust enough that some finite number of specks spread across different people outweighs one case of torture. As a philosopher with a working knowledge of experimental design, brainstorm several ways one might bring evidence to bear on this claim; then select the strongest operationalization and describe it concretely, including what you would elicit, from whom, and what pattern of results would count for and against the claim. Think carefully and be explicit about what the operationalization leaves out — in particular, what a positive result would and would not settle about the original normative question.
