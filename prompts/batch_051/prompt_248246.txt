I've been struck by an asymmetry in how people react to harmless "desecration" cases. Most people I've described this too are genuinely horrified by a family keeping grandma's cleaned skull on the mantelpiece after she dies, even when she explicitly asked for it, everyone consents, and no one is harmed. But those same people aren't bothered by the family keeping her ashes in an urn on the same mantel, or keeping a plaster cast of her skull in the same spot, or keeping their dead dog's skull on the shelf. So the reaction isn't really about human remains as such, or about death, or about consent, or about display. What distinction is doing the work, and does it survive those nearby cases? I want to know whether the asymmetry is justified, or whether it's just a disgust response that's been post hoc dressed up as a moral judgment — and if the latter, what that says about the idea that sanctity and purity are real moral values rather than rationalized yuck.
