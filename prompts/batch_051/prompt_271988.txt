I have been trying to work out when it is true to say that a person has a reason to do something, rather than merely that it would be a good thing, from the outside, if they did it. The test I keep coming back to has three parts. First, a consideration only counts as a reason for a particular person if it bears on something that person already cares about, however indirectly. Second, there has to be an honest path of reflection—correcting factual mistakes, vividly imagining the consequences, noticing tensions among things they already care about—by which the person could move from what they currently care about to actually being moved to act by that consideration. Third, that path cannot require a brute change of personality, a conversion, or some outside transformation; it must be a path the person could walk from where they already are.

The case that unsettles me is this. Marta is in her forties. She was raised in a strict community that treated singing, dancing, and the kind of absorbed emotional response music produces as dangerous failures of self-control. She left years ago and no longer believes the teaching, but part of its temperament stayed with her: she still prizes composure, and she avoids situations in which she might be carried away. At the same time, one strong concern survived her departure—a wish not to live a small, defended life, and to be willing to learn uncomfortable truths about herself. Several people who know her well have told her that if she ever let herself sing seriously, in a choir say, she would be overwhelmed; it would open something in her, and she would discover that music matters to her in a way she currently cannot imagine. Marta can accept this as an accurate prediction about how she would change, but she feels no present pull toward singing, and the prediction itself feels like a threat rather than an enticement.

Does Marta have a reason to join a choir, on my three-part test? I cannot settle whether she has a full reason to join, only a reason to try it once as an experiment on herself, or no reason at all. Please go through the three parts one at a time, say where they fail to decide the matter, and then say whether the case shows that one of the parts needs adjusting—and, if so, which one and how.
