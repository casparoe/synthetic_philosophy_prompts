Two philosophers are producing an adversarial-collaboration document about the moral equality of combatants in just war theory. One defends the orthodox, symmetry view: uniformed combatants on opposing sides of a war are morally on a par when it comes to killing one another, regardless of the justice of their side's cause, because the battlefield is not simply ordinary interpersonal morality scaled up. The other defends the revisionist, asymmetry view: killing in war is governed by the same principles of liability and self-defense as killing between individuals, so a soldier fighting an unjust war typically lacks a moral right to kill the soldiers sent to oppose him, even when he is sincerely mistaken, coerced, or otherwise excused.

Draft the document that such a collaboration should produce. Begin with a numbered agreed-claims section and follow it with a disagreements table. The agreed claims should be substantive and specific, including what the two sides can both sign about the difference between the laws of war and the deep morality of war, about the distinctive responsibility of leaders as against ordinary soldiers, and about how deceived or coerced unjust combatants should be judged. In the disagreements table, state each remaining dispute in wording both sides would accept, and include, where a side insists on it, the phrasing that side would use for its own position. Then identify the cruxes: the sub-questions whose answers would move at least one side. For each crux, specify a concrete case, argument, or test that both sides accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each side predicts it shows. In developing the cruxes, make sure the document confronts the difference between an action's being justified and its being merely excused, and the question whether there can be role-relative permissions that exist in war but not in ordinary life. Mark any part of the dispute you judge to be merely verbal, and say why.
