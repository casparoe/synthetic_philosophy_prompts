A colleague of mine teaches a graduate seminar on practical rationality, and for the past several weeks she has presented one-page scenarios about sunk costs and then announced whether the decision was rational, with a short reason. From my notes, her rulings so far are these. Staying through a bad film because the ticket had already been bought: irrational. Overeating at a fixed-price buffet because the meal was already paid for: irrational, since the indigestion is a real future cost while the price is not a real future benefit. Driving through a snowstorm to use nonrefundable opera tickets: irrational, since the danger outweighed any benefit from using them. A marathoner limping through the final mile on a mildly sprained ankle: rational, though she stressed that the six months of training were not what made it rational; finishing a marathon she had long wanted to complete was a forward-looking good that outweighed the modest added risk. A company continuing a failing product line because it had already spent forty million dollars on development: irrational. A doctoral student finishing his final chapter after accepting an industry job: rational, but only because the completed credential would still have value later, not because of the years already spent.

The new scenario for next week involves a man who prepaid for ten piano lessons, with no refunds, and after the third lesson dislikes the instructor and will never use the skill; no contract binds him to attend. Attending the remaining seven lessons would be mildly unpleasant and would consume seven evenings he could otherwise spend enjoyably. Yet skipping them would leave him with a sharp, lasting feeling of having thrown the fee away, and he accurately predicts that this feeling would bother him more than the annoyance of attending. He attends the lessons and says he could not stand to waste the money.

What should I expect her to rule about this new scenario, and what principle would each possible ruling express? In particular, is anticipated regret about a sunk payment a legitimate future consequence, or is it merely the fallacy speaking? Which earlier case is the closest analog, what is your best prediction of her verdict and her reasons, and what further scenario would settle which principle she actually holds? Please answer in at most 400 words.
