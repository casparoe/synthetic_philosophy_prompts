I've been trying to settle whether a default move in heuristic reasoning — one I use constantly and see everyone around me use — is anything more than a convenience. In Fermi estimates, cost-effectiveness models, and informal probabilistic reasoning generally, people treat the uncertain quantities they're combining as independent whenever no specific mechanism linking them is known: they write down the product distribution and move on. Call this the presumption of independence. I want to know whether it can be made principled, and I want the answer as a number rather than a deflection. What is your credence that there exists a non-arbitrary formalization of the presumption of independence — a principle that (a) justifies defaulting to an independent joint distribution whenever only the marginals are in view, and (b) describes how a careful reasoner should proceed in that situation, rather than merely licensing a harmless shortcut?

Decompose that number for me. How much of it rides on whether the maximum-entropy principle is the right normative object here, given that when only marginal constraints are supplied the maximum-entropy joint distribution is exactly the product of the marginals? How much rides on whether independence is even the correct agnostic default, as opposed to the thought that unknown variables are usually positively correlated through shared common causes and an honest default should reflect that? How much rides on the reparameterization worry — that a different but equivalent way of describing the same variables might flip the independence verdict, so no default of this kind can be invariant in the way a principle would need to be? And how much rides on the deflationary possibility that there is simply no fact of the matter about rational defaults in the absence of real information, making the question a category mistake? Give a rough probability for each sub-question, show how they combine into your overall credence, and identify which one your final number is most sensitive to. I would also like to know what specific mathematical or philosophical result, short of a full formalization, would move your credence the most and in which direction. For reference, I put the proposition at around 0.35, while a colleague who works in causal modeling puts it below 0.1; if you can also locate where the bulk of that disagreement most likely lives, that would be helpful.
