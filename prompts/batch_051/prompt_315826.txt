For a handout in a comparative philosophy course, build a decision tree—or a short quiz with a scoring key—that sorts a respondent into one of several positions on karma understood as a theory of moral desert. Please construct the tree from the questions before the labels, and phrase each diagnostic question around a concrete case that someone can answer without already knowing the positions. Use at least these distinctions: whether desert attaches primarily to the agent's intention or to the act's outcome; whether the mechanism that delivers results is an impersonal law or material process, a divine administrator, or a social/psychological feedback loop; whether the account needs a person who persists across lives to make sense; and whether karma is backward-looking retribution or forward-looking correction. The leaves must include at least a Buddhist-style intention-centered view that must handle the no-self difficulty, a Jain-style view on which karma is a material residue, a theistic view on which a god apportions results, and a secularized view that drops rebirth. Aim for no more than five diagnostic questions. Please also flag any two leaves the tree cannot separate without adding a further commitment, and briefly diagnose the empty cells in the logical space—say whether each is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Begin with the instrument itself, with no preface, and put any commentary after it.
