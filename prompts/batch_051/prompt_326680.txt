I cover science and ideas, and I am working on a piece about what cheap deepfakes do to our standing evidence practices. I keep noticing a mismatch in one institution that I cannot resolve on my own. In many criminal courts, a video recording enjoys a special status: once some witness has said it fairly depicts the event, the recording is shown to the jury as the scene itself, while eyewitness testimony is treated as a fallible reconstruction from memory. When the two conflict, the burden tends to fall on the witness. For decades the recording functioned as an epistemic backstop — the evidence you check other evidence against, and which itself receives only a perfunctory check in the ordinary case. Deepfakes attack precisely that feature, and courts are responding by thickening authentication (metadata, hash verification, expert analysis) while leaving the underlying privilege largely intact.

What philosophy of evidence and perception does this practice presuppose? Does it strictly presuppose that cameras are passive transducers of light that deliver the world uninterpreted, or does it require only the weaker claim that recordings are harder to tamper with and less reconstructive than memory? Which rival epistemologies of testimony and perception does the practice quietly rule out, and is it consistent with a view on which a recording is just another authored representation? Reconstruct the implicit theory, keeping separate what the practice is committed to from what it merely fits with, and say where the practice is underdetermined by the available theories. Then tell me what would have to change in courtroom practice if the backstop were replaced by a thoroughgoing fallibilism in which no form of evidence is self-authenticating. Finally, tell me what would change your reconstruction: which fact about how judges and jurors actually treat recordings, or which technical argument about what verification can and cannot establish, would most lead you to withdraw or reverse it, and where in your reasoning I should push hardest.
