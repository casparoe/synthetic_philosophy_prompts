Every time the conversation turns to climate, meat, or voting, someone wheels out the same picture: one less flight, one less burger, one vote in a big election is a drop in the bucket, so it's not worth doing. The point they take from it is that individual action is basically theater. I get that its a metaphor, but I want the thing audited, not just waved off. First, give me the correspondence table: what is the bucket, what is a drop, what is the water level, what does overflowing correspond to, and what decides how big the bucket is. Then tell me where the mapping breaks. A bucket of water is continuous and anonymous; real collective harms sometimes have thresholds or tipping points, sometimes not. My one action might have a tiny chance of being exactly the one that tips things, or it might shift what other people do, and in a close election a single vote can literally be the margin. Which of those disanalogies actually matter for the 'no point' conclusion, and which are just cosmetic? Does the conclusion survive once the picture is removed, or was the bucket doing the argumentative work? And is there a better analogy, or do carbon, meat, and voting each need their own model of how one person's act relates to the harm?
