Retired chemist here, recently fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole. I've been reading the friendship model of what grown children owe their parents, and I've ended up with a pile of views I cant tell whether they hang togehter. Here's where I've landed:

(1) I don't think the parent-child relationship is best understood as a debt, where the kid owes the parents repayment for the sacrifices they made. If someone benefits you unasked-for and you never signed on for the arrangement, that alone doesn't create an obligation to pay them back.

(2) I do think what you owe your parents is a lot like what you owe close friends: it comes out of the relationship you actually have now, not off a ledger of past benefits.

(3) But I also think the parent-child tie is special in a way ordinary friendship isn't. If my closest friend and my mother each needed the same kind of help at the same time, and I could only help one, I think I should help my mother.

(4) I don't think abusive or neglectful parents have much of a claim on their adult children—and I think this even though those parents may have done the basic caretaking of feeding and housing them as kids.

(5) I think what you owe someone can change when they become much more vulnerable. An aging parent with dementia might have a claim on me that they didn't have back when they were fully independent and we were both busy.

(6) I'm suspicious of the idea that the mere biological fact of parenthood gives a special claim. If I learned I was switched at birth, I don't think the woman who raised me loses her claim, and I don't think I owe the biological stranger anything just for gametes.

What I'm after is an audit: which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs are only apparently in tension, and for the real ones, how severe is the strain, what would have to give, and what would it cost to fix? I'm not attached to any one of these in particular.
