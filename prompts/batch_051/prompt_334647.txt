I spent my career in physical chemistry, where you could usually tell whether a concept was doing real work by pointing at the experiment that would fail if the concept was wrong. Now that I'm retired and reading around in moral philosophy, I keep running into the word "commodification," and people lean on it as if it names a single, unified bad thing — the turning of something that shouldn't be bought and sold into a market good. But I've realized I can't give a defintion that actually sorts the cases the way I want them sorted.

Here is my list. Selling a kidney to a stranger on the open market, or selling your vote to the highest bidder, seems to me like clear commodification. Paying a neighbor's teenager to babysit for an evening clearly isn't, even though it's care for money. But these middle cases leave me stuck: a company paying a school board to put its name on the school gymnasium; a hurricane price gouger selling cases of bottled water at ten times the normal price; someone paid to stand in line for concert tickets; a surrogate paid above her medical costs to carry a pregnancy; parents paying an adoption agency for a private adoption; paying cash to a friend to help you move apartments; a plasma center that pays donors fifty dollars a visit; and buying carbon offsets so you can keep flying. For a few of these I have a gut verdict — I'd call price gouging commodification, and paying a friend to move probably not — but I couldn't defend either call without the definition I don't yet have.

Put on your professional-philosopher hat: you work on the ethics of markets and the limits of exchange. Think carefully. Maybe start by brainstorming three or four candidate definitions and pressure-testing them against the clear cases before settling on one. Then give me your best working definition of commodification, run it through every case on the list, say where it delivers a verdict you'd have to defend rather than one I flagged, and tell me which case you're least confident about at the end. If the concept turns out to be doing two different jobs, say so and give me both definitions.
