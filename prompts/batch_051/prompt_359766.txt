I help run the philosophy club at my high school, and we are about to begin a unit on whether objects persist through time by being wholly present at each moment or by having different temporal parts at different times — the debate between endurantism and perdurantism. For the first meeting I want a sorting activity that assigns members to a position on the basis of how they answer concrete cases, before any of the labels have been introduced. Please write me eight to ten questions with a scoring key.

Each question should present a short case and offer two or three answer options, none of which uses philosophical jargon. Please make the following cases mandatory: a wire that is straight on Monday and bent on Tuesday; a statue molded from a lump of clay that is later squashed, so that the lump survives while the statue is destroyed; a time-travel case in which one person is in two rooms at the same time and shakes hands with themselves; and a first-person case asking whether the person who sat in my chair yesterday is literally identical with me now. Add further cases as needed to reach at least eight questions, choosing ones that press on a distinct commitment rather than re-testing the same point — for instance, a road that is smooth at one end and potholed at the other, which invites the spatial-parts analogy that many perdurantists lean on.

The scoring key should map patterns of answers onto at least these leaves: endurantism, perdurantism understood as the worm view, and stage theory. Build the key from the logical space of answer combinations first — say which combinations are coherent, which are incoherent or unattractive, and which appear merely empty — and only then attach labels. Flag explicitly any pair of positions your questions cannot separate (for example, a presentist endurantist versus an endurantist who treats the past and future as equally real), and where two positions end up inseparable, describe the extra question or case that would pull them apart. Make sure the statue-and-lump case distinguishes the view that two objects coincide at one time from the view that the statue and the lump share a temporal part, since that is one of the few places those positions visibly come apart.

Audience constraints: the members are fifteen to eighteen years old and have no background in metaphysics. Every question should be answerable simply by considering the case, the scoring key should be readable aloud by a typical member, and there should be no question asking people to identify with a label. Keep the quiz self-contained, with no citations or suggestions for further reading, since we will use it live at the meeting.
