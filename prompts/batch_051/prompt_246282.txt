I signed a co-op teaching agreement at our small school, and it has this clause: “The teacher shall govern the child by the esteem which the child has for his reason, and not by fear of his rod; he shall never beat the child for what is not in the child’s power to understand, nor suffer the child to be idle, and he shall always answer the child’s questions as plainly as the child can bear.” We’re now in a dispute because my son’s “teacher” for Latin and arithmetic is a chatbot tutor I set up. The bot never beats him, never suffers him to be idle, always answers his questions, and my son respects it quite a lot. The other parents say the clause plainly requires a human tutor with actual authority, and that a program can’t “esteem” anything or “bear” an answer. I say the clause governs the treatment the child receives, and the bot treats him exactly as the rule says. How should the clause be read? I want what the words require, what the purpose of the clause was, and what the founders of the school would have said if they’d imagined a language-model tutor. Which approach should win here, and why?
