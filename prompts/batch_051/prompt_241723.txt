I have been working through Maxwell's demon after a long argument with a colleague about whether information is physical, and I have arrived at a way of stating the standard resolution that, if it holds, makes the usual wording of the second law too strong. I call it the correlation ledger view. The idea is that whenever some process lowers the thermodynamic entropy of one subsystem without doing work on it, the missing entropy is not destroyed; it is transferred into mutual information between that subsystem and whatever apparatus performed the sorting. In the familiar thought experiment, after the demon has opened and closed its trapdoor many times, the demon's memory is correlated with the positions of the molecules on each side. On my account, that mutual information is the entropy debt: the books balance even if no heat has flowed yet, because the correlation is still stored. Landauer erasure is only one way of discharging the debt as heat, not the unique moment at which physics demands payment.

Now consider a demon that never erases. Instead of resetting its memory, it writes each fresh bit into an unused cell of an indefinitely extendable tape, so that the tape merely grows as the demon sorts fast molecules left and slow molecules right. My claim is that this demon can cool one chamber and heat the other indefinitely without doing work and without any compensating heat flow, because the entropy decrease in the gas is continuously balanced by growing correlations with the tape rather than by any thermal cost. If that is right, then the second law, in the operational form that rules out cooling a reservoir while heating another at no cost, is false unless one adds a proviso about finite memory or eventual erasure. The true constraint would not be a ban on sorting, but a ban on sorting and then forgetting without payment.

I am aware this sounds like I am trying to resurrect a perpetual motion machine of the second kind, and I suspect the error is somewhere in treating unbounded memory as physically cheap. Please give me an honest assessment. Specifically: is the correlation ledger view just the Landauer-Bennett resolution restated, or is the added debt metaphor doing real work? If the tape is genuinely unbounded, where exactly does the required heat cost re-enter, if at all? Could an external observer who can read the tape exploit the situation to run a heat engine, and does that reveal the loophole? And is the strongest form of the second law best understood as a claim about finite automata rather than about heat?
