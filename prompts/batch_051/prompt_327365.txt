In my AI reading group we've been arguing for six months about whether embedded agency is a real problem or just a fancy name for an old engineering nuisance, and the argument has gone completely stale. The setup everyone agrees on is this: a real agent is part of the environment it's trying to model — its model of the world lives inside the world, so the map is inside the territory, and acting in the world can change which model of the world is accurate. The first camp says this breaks classical decision theory in principle: expected utility theory assumes a clean split between agent and environment, with a fixed utility function and a prior over states that don't include the agent itself as one of the things in the world. Once you delete that split, it's not obvious what it means for the agent to choose an action or update on evidence in a principled way, and patching things up by re-drawing the boundary feels like your assuming the very thing you were supposed to justify. The second camp says this is overblown: the clean split was always an idealization, nobody thinks real agents literally enumerate states and consequences before acting, and the right move is to pick a workable boundary and get on with it — embeddedness is an engineering constraint, not a crisis for the very idea of rational agency. Both sides grant the same facts about self-reference; the difference looks like it's about what a theory of rationality is for. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement's structure rather than a verdict on who's right. My working diagnosis is that they're answering two different questions under one heading: one side wants a formal account of an agent that can reason about its own code, the other wants bounded practical guidance with manageable error. Is that the right read of the stalemate, or is there a genuine formal disagreement underneath (like whether any consistent self-referential decision procedure can even exist)? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a theorem, a distinction, or a tacit agreement to stop asking the question?
