i keep tripping over the word 'unit' and I want a working definition that actually earns it. Here are the cases I want it to get right. A sheep is a unit when you count seven sheep. The number 1 in arithmetic is the unit you count with. An inch or a second is a unit of measure, even though it isn't a little object at all. A battalion is a unit, made of many soldiers, so a unit doesn't have to be indivisible. An apartment in a condominium is called a unit because it is one part of a larger building. And in algebra, a unit is an element with a multiplicative inverse (in the integers only 1 and -1), which doesn't sound much like counting or measuring. Then there's Euclid: in the Elements he defines the unit as something like the thing by which each existing thing gets called one, though I forget the exact wording. His unit is tied to oneness, but a battalion is one unit despite being many soldiers. I suspect the word does at least two jobs, one about being a single countable thing and one about being a conventional standard or component. Give me a definition, run it against each case, tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and revise it. And keep clear about what is settled about what Euclid meant versus what is a genuinely contested interpretation: please distinguish the two and dont present a contested reading as the consensus.
