In my intro class yesterday a student kept meeting every version of the problem of evil with "everything happens for a reason," and when I pressed her on what reason and whose reason she was invoking she just said it louder. I'd like to turn this into a handout. Lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan could actually be making — for instance, every event is caused; every event has a purpose; every event is part of God's plan; every evil is compensated later; no evil is gratuitous; we can't discern which evils are gratuitous; some apparent evils are necessary for greater goods — and for each say whether its trivially true, substantive but not really a theodicy, or a genuine theodicean commitment. I'm especially interested in the slide between an explanatory "reason" and a justifying one, since I think that's doing a lot of the work. Then tell me which readings actually do work against the evidential problem of evil and which ones just redescribe it or change the subject. I want the handout to disarm the "repeat it louder" move by showing that the interesting readings are exactly the ones that need an argument.
