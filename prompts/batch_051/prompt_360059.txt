For a position paper I am drafting on accuracy-first epistemology, I would like an internal consistency audit of my current commitments before I circulate it. I am not asking for citations or a survey of the literature; I want to know whether the package below hangs together.

Here is what I hold.

1. Veritism: accuracy, understood as closeness of a credence function to the truth-values of the relevant propositions, is the only fundamental epistemic value. Any other epistemic norm is genuine only if it is derived from accuracy.

2. Probabilism, justified by the accuracy-dominance argument: relative to an admissible measure of gradational inaccuracy, every credence function that violates the probability axioms is strictly dominated at every possible world by some probabilistically coherent credence function, and that dominance is what makes probabilistic incoherence irrational.

3. Conditionalization, justified by expected-accuracy maximization: the only rational way to revise one's beliefs in response to new evidence is to conditionalize, because conditionalization uniquely maximizes expected accuracy relative to one's current credences.

4. Gradational inaccuracy is measured by strictly proper scoring rules, where a scoring rule is strictly proper just in case each probability function expects itself to have the best score. No strictly proper rule is uniquely correct, however: the Brier score, the log score, and the other strictly proper scoring rules are all equally legitimate.

5. Subjectivism about priors: beyond the probability axioms, there are no rationality constraints on initial credences; any probabilistically coherent prior is permissible.

6. Epistemic conservatism, but only as a tie-breaker: when two options are tied in expected accuracy, it is rationally permissible to prefer the one that departs least from one's existing beliefs.

Please audit this position for joint consistency. Identify which commitments genuinely pull against one another and which apparent conflicts dissolve on closer inspection. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, propose the least costly revision for each, and then indicate which single revision would do the most to make the overall position coherent. The response should be written in American English.
