I run the pricing-analytics unit at a property-casualty insurer. Every quarter my analysts update our catastrophe-loss models by blending a prior over model parameters elicited from senior underwriters with the quarter's newly observed claims. Right now we use a fixed credibility-weighting rule — 80% prior, 20% data, the same for every region and line — and I want to replace it with a procedure that decides each quarter how far the posterior should move toward the data and, just as importantly, when the prior should barely move no matter how much data has piled up. I'm told the theory to rest this on is the Bayesian convergence literature: the merging-of-opinions results in which two agents with different priors who see the same evidence eventually come to agree, and the posterior-consistency results in which the posterior concentrates on the truth. My analysts have master's-level applied statistics but no measure theory, and our regulator will want a written rationale whenever we leave a prior nearly unchanged in the face of new data. Translate the convergence results into a reusable quarterly decision procedure. It should specify: the conditions that must hold before "the data will eventually wash out the prior" is something we are entitled to infer, with a concrete check our analysts can run against the prior, the model, and the data for each condition; how the procedure should handle failures of those conditions — non-identified models, misspecified likelihoods, non-exchangeable or regime-shifting claims, heavy tails, and priors that assign zero mass to regions the data point toward; and the places where judgment cannot be eliminated, explicitly marked. Work two or three cases in detail, and make at least one a case where the procedure requires us to act against our current fixed-weight formula — for example, a long stable stretch of data that the procedure says should not move the prior because the model is misspecified in a way the data cannot reveal. Commit to a definite procedure and defend each step; do not lay out alternatives and leave me to choose.
