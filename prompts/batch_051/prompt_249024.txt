Suppose a midsized city decides novelty is a component of well-being and that going without it is a public problem, so it introduces a novelty allowance. Every resident over 18 gets $50 a month in a restricted account, but the money can only be spent on activities the city's app classifies as new for you at the point of purchase. It compares the transaction against your spending history, location trails, and streaming and reading records, and the vendor only gets reimbursed if the app flags the sale as novel. Credits expire after 45 days, can't be transferred, and can't be cashed out; the city describes it as insurance against a boring life, not an entertainment subsidy. Walk me through what this does to how people actually spend their time, who captures the money, and where the sytem gets gamed first. What new businesses spring up around the approval rule? Does the city end up buying novelty, or just buying the appearance of novelty in people's records? And if the real problem is the fear of a boring life rather than the absence of new experiences, where does the scheme stop being fixable without junking it entirely?
