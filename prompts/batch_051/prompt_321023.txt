A cognitive science textbook I have been assigned summarizes the mental imagery debate between Stephen Kosslyn and Zenon Pylyshyn as follows:

"The debate between Kosslyn and Pylyshyn was about whether mental images are pictorial or propositional. Kosslyn argued they are pictorial: in his scanning experiments, subjects memorized a map and formed an image of it; when asked to scan from one landmark to another, their response times grew linearly with the imagined distance, just as if they were scanning a real map. That, he concluded, shows that the image itself preserves spatial relations the way a picture does. Pylyshyn replied that all this shows is that subjects know how long it takes to scan a real map and adjust their reaction times accordingly. On his view the underlying representation is propositional—that is, sentence-like descriptions in a language of thought—so imagery is in effect just verbal description. The dispute remained unresolved until neuroimaging showed that visual cortex is active during imagery and that some of its spatial layout is preserved; this convinced most cognitive scientists that Kosslyn was right. So the textbook concludes that mental images really are picture-like representations watched by an inner eye, and that Pylyshyn's position was refuted."

I find this summary suspiciously tidy, but I cannot say exactly where the distortion is. Is it a fair summary of the debate? Please audit it claim by claim. I especially want to know (a) whether Kosslyn's scanning results really establish that images have a picture-like spatial format, or only something weaker; (b) whether Pylyshyn's position is accurately described as "sentence-like" or "verbal"; (c) whether the neuroimaging findings settle the issue in the way the textbook suggests; and (d) whether "picture-like representations watched by an inner eye" is a faithful statement of what a depictive theorist like Kosslyn is actually committed to. If the summary conflates distinctions the two sides kept apart—for instance, depictive format versus literal picture, or propositional format versus verbal description—spell out those distinctions and say which side's position is distorted by each conflation.
