Consider a presidential republic with a written line of succession that runs from the president and vice-president through the senior members of the cabinet. Because these officials all normally work in the capital, a commission worries that a single attack could kill the entire political executive at once, and proposes the following constitutional amendment, called the Sealed Successor Law.

Under the amendment:

(1) At the beginning of each calendar month, an independent continuity commission draws one name from a pool consisting of cabinet members and the chairs of the five principal legislative committees. The result is committed to two sealed records, one deposited with the chief justice and one with the national archivist. The drawn official is not told that they are the survivor.

(2) In the same proceeding, the commission draws two further names from the same pool as decoys. The survivor and the two decoys are then sent, under an ordinary administrative cover, to three separate secured locations away from the capital. Each of the three is told only that they are a member of that month's sealed-successor pool and must comply with the same security, communications, and readiness protocol; none is told whether they are the survivor or a decoy.

(3) If the president, the vice-president, and the three next constitutional successors are all killed or permanently incapacitated in a single incident, the chief justice and the national archivist open their records jointly, and the named survivor is sworn in at their posted location.

(4) Until a general election is held, the successor serves as acting president with strictly caretaker powers: they may command the military and emergency services, but may not dismiss parliament, issue pardons, conclude treaties, or appoint judges. The acting president must call a general election within ninety days, may not stand in that election, and may not hold any elected or cabinet office for ten years afterward.

Treat this as a mechanism, not as an abstract good-to-bad proposal. Work backwards from the incentives it creates. What should we expect from the three officials sent away each month, from their cabinet colleagues, from party leaders, from the members of the continuity commission, from the chief justice, and from the archivist? How would each try to use, game, or protect themselves against the scheme, and how would a hostile intelligence service or a domestic conspirator try to defeat it? What second-order effects would it have on who is willing to seek high office, on how decisions are made inside the government, on the public legitimacy of a successor chosen in this way, and on the moral predicament of a successor who may never have consented to the role and had no time to prepare? Finally, distinguish the failure modes that could be repaired by amending the scheme, and say what the amendment would have to be, from the failure modes that seem inherent in any attempt to combine secrecy with continuity of government.
