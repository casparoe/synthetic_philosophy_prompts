So in my philosophy of language class we've been doing rigid designation and the necessary a posteriori, and I have a case I cant decide on. Our criteria were roughly: a singular term is rigid iff it picks out the same object in every possible world where that object exists, and it never picks out something else in worlds where that object doesn't. For natural kind terms, the version from lecture was: a general term K is rigid iff it denotes the same underlying kind across possible worlds; its extension in any world is whatever has the actual underlying nature that fixed the reference, not whatever satisfies the superficial properties people first used to identify samples. Water is the easy case: it picks out H2O everywhere, even if some possible world has a clear drinkable liquid that isn't H2O.

Now the borderline case is jade. Before people knew the chemistry, "jade" was used for hard, cool, typically greenish stones that carved well; then it turned out that the stuff going by "jade" was mainly two different minerals, nephrite and jadeite, with different crystal structures and different chemical formulas, plus some other lookalike minerals occasionally getting lumped in. So there wasn't a single underlying nature waiting to be discovered, the way there was with water.

So the criteria from my notes: (1) the term was introduced with an intention to track a single underlying kind; (2) reference was fixed by samples and superficial properties rather than by a definition; (3) if the original samples turn out to share one underlying nature, the term tracks that nature across worlds; (4) if the samples turn out to be sort of mixed, the term is ambiguous or empty, not flexible to the closest kind. Does "jade" qualify as a rigid designator? Go criterion by criterion and be honest about where it's genuinely indeterminate instead of just no. And then say whether this shows something about the criteria themselves: should we amend them so that natural kind terms are only rigid relative to a settled science, or is that overkill and jade just wasnt a natural kind term in the first place?
