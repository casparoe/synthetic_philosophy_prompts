My wife and I are going through IVF right now and the clinic offered us PGT, wich means we can screen embryos for a bunch of conditions before one is implanted into her. I always thought eugenics meant the state forcibly sterilizing people or the historical horrors. But now a genetic counselor is handing us a menu of risks and we're being asked whether we want to pay thousands extra to pick an embryo with, I dont know, a lower chance of early heart disease. I cant tell where the line is.

I also half-remember this argument from a bioethics class called procreative beneficence: that if you're doing IVF anyway, you're morally required to pick the embryo expected to have the best life. My brother-in-law is deaf and says the same principle means he should be allowed to deliberately pick a deaf embryo, because he doesn't think being deaf makes his life worse. So now I'm trying to figure out what 'eugenics' even means before I decide whether using this screening makes us eugenicists or whether that word is just a club people use to win arguments.

Here are the cases I want the definition nailed to:

- A state forcibly sterilizing people it considers unfit.
- My wife and I screening out an embryo that has Tay-Sachs.
- A couple doing IVF who pick a female embryo solely because they already have two sons and want a daughter.
- A deaf couple who deliberately select an embryo with congenital deafness.
- A sperm bank that advertises donors by height, IQ, and college major, and a couple who pick the highest-IQ one.
- A government that makes sickle-cell carrier screening free and strongly urges carrier couples to use IVF with embryo screening, but bans nothing.
- A woman who takes folic acid and stops drinking while trying to conceive.

Watch: is that last one supposed to be the obvious non-case? Because if eugenics means 'trying to improve your future kid,' then folic acid is eugenics and the word covers too much. And if it means 'state coercion,' then our embryo menu escapes but the word covers too little. I suspect the word does two jobs, one descriptive and one condemnatory. Give me a definition (or two if you think it's cleaner), run it against each case, and say which of your verdicts is least secure and whether you'd defend it anyway. Finally: by your definition, does the situation my wife and I are in make us eugenicists? Write in American English.
