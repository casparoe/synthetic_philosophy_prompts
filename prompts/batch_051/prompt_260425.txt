I keep noticing a pattern in debates about pausing frontier AI, and I'm hoping someone can tell me whether it has a name. Both sides often agree that what actually matters is dangerous capability, not any particular physical quantity. But the proposed trigger for a pause or regulation keeps hopping: parameter count, then training compute in FLOPs, then total chip shipments, and now sometimes inference-time compute or algorithmic efficiency. In each round the proxy is just whatever happened to be the bottleneck during the last big capability jump, so when progress comes through a different channel the proxy stops tracking danger—not because anyone games the measure, but because the thing worth measuring has shifted underneath it. I know it's adjacent to Goodhart's law and metric fixation, but it doesn't feel like either: the metric isn't being manipulated, and the trouble isn't bureaucratic laziness. It's more like aiming at a moving target through one fixed window. Is there an established concept for this exact pattern in AI governance or the philosophy of measurement? If there is, tell me how it differs from Goodhart's law and ordinary proxy drift; if there isn't, give it a name and say where its edges are. Don't just hand me a label—I want the boundary lines. And whatever you conclude, tell me what argument or evidence would most likely change your mind about whether this is a real phenomenon rather than standard Goodhart.
