I have been trying to reconstruct an argument and cannot find the weak step. It is the hole argument, and as I understand it, it runs as follows.

(1) Spacetime substantivalism says that spacetime points exist independently of the matter and metric fields defined on them, and that a physical situation is constituted by which fields occupy which points.
(2) Take any solution of general relativity—a manifold with a metric and matter fields—and choose an open region of spacetime, the hole, lying wholly to the future of some time-slice. Apply a smooth one-to-one mapping that is the identity everywhere outside the hole but not inside it, dragging the metric and matter fields along to new points.
(3) The result is a different model by substantivalist lights: inside the hole, the fields sit on different points, even though the two models are identical outside the hole.
(4) Because Einstein's field equations are invariant under such mappings, the dragged model is also a solution; and because every observable feature is dragged along as well, no observation could distinguish the two.
(5) The two models thus share the same state on the chosen time-slice and outside the hole, yet differ inside it. Given the laws and complete information on the slice, substantivalism still leaves two distinct possible futures, so general relativity comes out indeterministic.
(6) But the underdetermination seems to have been manufactured by a mere relabeling of spacetime points, and a philosophy of spacetime ought not to be convicted of indeterminism that cheaply.

Which step is the weak one, and what does rejecting it commit me to? I would like to keep both determinism and a robust realism about spacetime, but I suspect the argument forces me to reinterpret at least one of them, and I cannot see which reinterpretation is the least costly.
