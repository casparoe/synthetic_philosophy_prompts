i'm working on a longish piece about AI safety via debate — the proposal that you have two models argue it out, one defending an answer and the other cross-examining it, and a human judge picks the winner, on the theory that the judge only has to spot the better argument rather than know the truth in advance. The objection that keeps snagging me is obfuscated arguments: a debater that's smarter than the judge doesnt have to be right, it just has to be persuasive, and it can learn to push the judge's cognitive buttons instead of revealing anything. i've been trying to get at this by rewatching Thank You for Smoking, because Nick Naylor mostly doesn't lie — he reframes, deflects, makes the other side's position look absurd, and walks out of every scene having 'won' while the facts sit there untouched. I want to read the film through the AI-debate lens seriously, not as a simple morality tale about a bad man with a silver tongue.
