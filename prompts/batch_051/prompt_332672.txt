I keep noticing the same structure in very different places, and I want to know whether it has an established name — and, more than that, exactly how to carve it up and delimit it.

Take Escher's Drawing Hands. Each hand is drawn by the other: the one on the left exists because the right drew it, and the right exists because the left drew it. The picture preserves a sense of hierarchy — this hand draws, that hand is drawn — but the ordering flips as soon as you follow it. If you try to ground the drawing by asking which hand was really first, you never reach a bottom element; you only get the loop again. And if you erase one hand on the theory that it is the foundation, the other stops being a drawing hand at all — the object you were trying to explain disappears instead of being explained.

I see the same shape in constitutional review. The constitution is the highest law and creates the court; but the court says, with authority, what the constitution means, including which principles count as part of it. So the constitution grounds the court, and the court, in turn, grounds the constitution's content. The hierarchy is real at any given moment — one level does govern the other — but there is no layer that is simply before interpretation. Ask what makes the constitution binding in the first place, and the answer has to be stated from inside the legal order it was supposed to found.

A third instance comes from formal systems. Ask why a rule of inference is justified, and you get an argument; that argument itself uses rules of inference. Ask why those are justified, and the justification eventually invokes the very practice of reasoning the rules make possible. Keep pressing downward, toward the bare symbols, and you do not hit bedrock; you reach a point where the symbols' meaning presupposes the whole system of use, and the system of use presupposes meanings. There is a level — not the bottom in any usual sense — below which further reduction no longer explains the system but simply makes it stop being the system.

I know rough terms such as circularity, mutual dependence, self-reference, and bootstrapping, but those mostly name the loop, not the fact that the levels remain ordered and that there is a floor beyond which explanation turns into destruction of the subject matter. Is there an established concept for precisely this — a hierarchy whose levels also constitute one another, with a point past which questions cease to be answerable because the system evaporates? How is it different from a vicious circle or a merely circular definition? Where are its edges: when is a self-referential loop an instance of this, and when is it just a gimmick or a defect?

I am not looking for a label to drop in conversation; I am after the carving — what this phenomenon is, what it is not, which of the nearby concepts it matches and where each comparison breaks down, and, if no established term fits, a proposed term with an explicit definition and boundary cases. Please commit: take a definite position and defend it, even if that means saying the existing terms all miss the mark and coining one yourself.
