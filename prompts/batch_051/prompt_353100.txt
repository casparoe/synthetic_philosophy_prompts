A contact at one of the big AI labs sent me a draft policy that's apparently been doing the rounds between the labs. The proposal: any lab that wants to run a training run above a certain compute threshold (call it 10^26 FLOP) has to lodge a 'capabilities bond' — say 5 percent of the estimated training cost — in escrow before the run starts. The bond is returned only after an outside red team has had six months with the model and signs off that it couldn't elicit a capability on a published list of dangerous ones. The anti-race twist is that the interest on all the bonds goes into a common pot, and every lab that has stayed below the compute threshold for the past three years gets an equal share of the pot, whatever it's size. Labs that cross the threshold forfeit their share for that cycle. The authors pitch it as a fix for the multipolar trap where each lab trains slightly bigger models than it privately thinks is safe, because it assumes one of the others will otherwise get there first.
