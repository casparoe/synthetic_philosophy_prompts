Suppose a city with a large cultural-tourism sector establishes a Cultural Lineage Registry together with a mutation fund. Any business whose trade depends on a named traditional form—a dish, a musical style, a textile pattern, a festival performance—must register the chain of named individuals or communities through which the form reached the business, and must pay a fixed levy of five per cent of form-related turnover. The Registry maintains a canonical baseline record for each registered form. The levy is split according to how far the business's version departs from that baseline: the nearer the version is to the baseline, the larger the share paid to the named source chain, with any remainder going to the mutation fund. Separately, the mutation fund awards substantial annual prizes only to registered forms that have departed substantially from a baseline while remaining traceable to it. A form that no business registers receives neither royalties nor prizes.

Walk through the incentives this creates for the businesses, for the source communities, for the Registry panel that fixes baselines, and for tourists. Then characterise the kind of memes—units of cultural transmission that vary in their copying-fidelity, their rate of reproduction, and their persistence in archives and markets—that this selection environment would favour. Predict the first- and second-order effects on the forms themselves, identify the points at which the system would be gamed first, and say whether the resulting failure modes can be repaired without abandoning the mechanism.
