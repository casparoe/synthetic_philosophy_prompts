At dinner last week, my cousin and one of my friends got into an argument about a new policy at my high school. The school announced that students can be disciplined for things they post online outside of school, whenever the dean decides a post could harm the school's reputation. A few months on, no one has actually been punished under the policy.

My cousin argued that we haven't lost any real freedom, since the school hasn't actually stopped anyone from posting anything. Freedom, he said, is about whether someone interferes with you in fact; a rule that is never used doesn't interfere with anyone, and feeling watched is just unease, not unfreedom.

My friend disagreed. She said the problem is that the dean can now punish us on his own judgment, without any clear standard for what harms the school's reputation. We have already lost something, she said, because every student now has to anticipate how the dean will react and watch what they say to stay on his good side. Being free isn't only about whether someone actually steps in; it's about not living under another person's arbitrary power.

My cousin replied that, by her standard, any teacher or boss or parent with authority over us would make us unfree, since they can all punish us if they choose. Avoiding the dean's anger, he said, is just prudence — like not provoking someone stronger than you — not a loss of freedom.

My friend replied that there is a difference between an authority with clear, predictable rules and penalties, and an authority that can act on something as open-ended as "harming the school's reputation." Clear rules, she said, let you plan around them, so you never have to guess what will get you in trouble; open-ended power makes you depend on another person's good will, and that dependence is already a form of unfreedom even if the dean is lenient.

I kept changing my mind about who was right, and they never resolved it. Can you go through the argument step by step and say who is right about which specific point, and what, if anything, both of them are missing?
