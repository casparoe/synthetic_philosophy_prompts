I'm collecting arguments against pure time preference—discounting future wellbeing solely because it arrives later, not because future people may not exist or may be richer. So far everything I have is a fairness argument: (1) temporal position is morally arbitrary, like birthplace or sex; (2) a chooser behind a veil of ignorance who didn't know which generation they'd end up in wouldn't build in a penalty for being born later; (3) equal consideration of interests means a future person's equal pain counts as much as a present person's; (4) the neutrality point, that any moment when it arrives will be just as real and urgent to the person living it as the present is to me. I want one more argument against pure time preference that is not on that list and not a rewording or special case of any of them. In particular, I'd like it to not rely on fairness, equality, or impartiality at all—something about the structure of value over time, or rationality, or identity perhaps. Does such an argument exist? If so, give it and say briefly whether it's any good; if not, say why every good objection collapses into fairness.
