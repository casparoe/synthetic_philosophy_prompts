In Chapter III of A Treatise on Probability Keynes argues that not all probabilities are numerically measurable, and that some probability relations are not even comparable with one another. In Chapter VI he distinguishes the probability of a conclusion on given evidence from the weight of that evidence, which turns on how much relevant information the evidence contains rather than on how strongly it favours the conclusion. I hold that this part of Keynes's view is essentially right: uncertainty is not always representable by a single real-valued probability, and a rational agent can sometimes legitimately say that one hypothesis is more probable than another on the evidence while having no determinate numerical degrees or having no basis for comparing some pairs.

I want an honest stress-test of that position, not a standard summary of Keynes. What would have to be true for the denial of universal numerical measurability to be false? What formal results, decision-theoretic arguments, or actual practices would count most decisively against the claim that some rational uncertainties are non-numerical or non-comparable? Which parts of the position would a defender be most reluctant to concede, and which parts should they already concede are exposed? Distinguish defeaters that would be fatal from those that would merely be awkward. In particular: would the success of subjective expected utility theory, Dutch book arguments, or modern probabilistic elicitation methods show that Keynesian non-comparability is just a temporary computational limitation rather than a feature of the logical relation between evidence and hypothesis? Does the weight-of-evidence distinction collapse once one formalizes unknown information as a second-order probability distribution, or does it mark something those models still miss? And has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability—if someone says a probability is non-numerical only because no exact number has yet been elicited, is that still Keynes's position or a retreat from it? Rank the main threats from fatal to merely awkward, and say openly whether the position is well insulated and whether that insulation is a problem for a view of this kind.
