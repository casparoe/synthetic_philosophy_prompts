Here's one about the symbol grounding problem. Suppose an AI system ends up with symbols that are suposed to mean something. Five ways it could have gotten them:

(a) old-school symbolic AI: each symbol is defined only via other symbols, so the whole thing floats free of perception and action;
(b) LLM style: the system learns from huge amounts of text, but never sees, touches, or does anything in the world;
(c) robot style: the symbols are tied to camera, microphone, and motor data, so 'red' gets learned accross many episodes of seeing red things and using the word in real tasks;
(d) reward style: the symbols are anchored to the system's own reward predictions and drives, not to external objects;
(e) social style: the symbols get grounded through back-and-forth with other agents (joint tasks, pointing, corrections, negotiating word use) with only crude perception.

Rank these from most to least genuinely grounded, give each a score out of 10, and then say whether any one of them alone actually solves the symbol grounding problem or whether a real solution needs a combination. Defend the ranking.
