I've been trying, over the last few evenings, to put together a small giving and decision framework for myself, and I keep running into the fanaticism problem: as soon as I allow expected-value reasoning with even mildly unbounded payoffs and tiny probabilities, every spreadsheet ends up recommending I take some glorified Pascal's mugging seriously. I don't want to just grit my teeth and multiply tiny probabilities by astronomical utilities until I'm wiring money to a stranger with an elaborate story about how, unless I send them a tenner, they'll simulate and torture an astronomical number of copies of me, but I also don't want to quietly abandon expected value in the cases where it's actually doing real work (weird low-probability high-upside science bets, x-risk work, that sort of thing). The version I care about isn't the toy one where the mugger asks for £5 once; it's the one where the logic leaks into my actual giving decisions over years.

So I've collected a list of candidate patches, some from reading forums and some i invented myself. Tier them by how much you'd trust them to stop fanaticism without silently giving up on EV reasoning everywhere else — a rough S/A/B/C scale is fine — and add a sentence or two on why each one lands where it does. The candidates: (1) bounded utility functions, like log or arctan or a hard cap; (2) a flat probability floor, never acting on a probability below some epsilon no matter how large the payoff; (3) a description-length or complexity prior, so long contrived mugger stories get assigned astronomically small probability; (4) requiring that a bet beat the best concrete intervention you can actually write down, where concrete means you can specify the causal chain rather than just assert a conditional; (5) an evidence bar that scales with the claimed payoff, so that the ratio of evidence strength to utility stays bounded; (6) Kelly-style betting or maximising expected log-growth instead of raw expected value for anything repeatable. If you think the actual answer is some hybrid of these or a seventh option I've missed, include it in the tier list.
