I've been arguing with a colleague for weeks about the ontology of classical electrodynamics, and I've realized that what would genuinely help is an outsider's credence together with the reasoning behind it, not just one more substantive argument. Here is the version of the question we've converged on. Electromagnetism can be formulated in terms of the electric and magnetic fields, or in terms of a scalar and vector potential that determine those fields but are not unique: two potentials related by a gauge transformation give exactly the same fields and the same physical predictions. The claim in dispute is whether the gauge structure itself is physically real. Put as a proposition: the vector potential — or, more carefully, the connection of which any particular potential is a gauge-dependent representative — is a physically real feature of the world, rather than descriptive surplus whose only real content is gauge-invariant, such as the fields and loop integrals of the potential around closed paths. My credence in that proposition is about 0.35; hers is about 0.75.

I'd like your own number, but I care much more about the decomposition behind it. Please break the question into the sub-questions your credence actually depends on, attach a rough probability to each, and indicate how they combine into the overall number. In particular, say how much turns on: whether the empirical equivalence of gauge-related descriptions is itself a reason to deny realism about anything gauge-variant; whether the Aharonov-Bohm effect — in which an electron interference pattern appears to depend on the potential in a region where the magnetic field is zero — shows that the fields omit something physically real; whether locality, or the standard formulation of quantum electrodynamics, favors treating the potential or connection as more than a calculational convenience; and whether the right realistic candidate should be the gauge-dependent potential at all, as opposed to only the gauge-invariant holonomies. Then tell me which of these sub-questions most plausibly explains the gap between my 0.35 and her 0.75, and what experimental result or argument would move your own number the most.
