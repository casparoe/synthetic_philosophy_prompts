I keep hearing people say language models can't actually produce new ideas, they just interpolate between things in thier training set. I want to test a sharper version of that claim. Suppose I train a small model from scratch on a math corpus where subtraction is only ever defined when the result is non-negative, and negative numbers never appear even indirectly (no debts, no below-zero temperatures, no points left of the origin). Then I give it problems that seem to force negative numbers: a pair of equations whose only solution has a negative value, or the x-intercept of a line that crosses left of the origin. Flesh this out into the best test you can for whether the model makes a genuine conceptual leap beyond its training distribution. Be concrete about what the corpus must and must not contain, what probe tasks you'd use, what result would count as a leap, and what result would make you say it just recombined things it already had. Then say what this kind of test could never settle even in principle.
