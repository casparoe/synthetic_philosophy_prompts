I am arguing with the rest of my firm about a proposed rule for how we use language models, and I want a cold analysis of how the rule would actually behave in practice, not a verdict on whether it is a good idea in principle.

The proposal is this. Any staff member may use a language model to draft work for clients: technical memoranda, risk assessments, design documents, estimates, anything we would normally put our name on. But before a draft can be submitted, the responsible staff member must attach a one-page dependency map of the central reasoning, drawn without the model or the draft in view. The map must show which conclusions rest on which assumptions, which claims are doing the real work, and which part of the argument would collapse first if a particular assumption were changed. The map is submitted with the work. Then, every Friday, a random number generator selects one submitted piece from that week; its author has fifteen minutes, with no notes, to defend the work orally before two partners, reconstructing the reasoning and answering at least one targeted question about what the conclusion should be under a changed assumption. If the author cannot do this to the partners' satisfaction, the author loses access to language-model assistance for thirty days and the work is sent back for revision.

Walk me through what this policy would do. What incentives does it create for how people use the language model day to day, what gets delegated and what people quietly choose to do themselves, and how does that change over time as people adapt? Where is the first place it gets gamed, and how? What happens to the quality and kind of work the firm produces, to who accumulates status, and to the people who are already most dependent on these tools, in either direction? And step back: would the policy actually preserve anyone's understanding, or would it mostly create a new compliance ritual that tracks the appearance of understanding? Where does the proposal fail in a way that is fixable, and where does its failure reveal something about the underlying problem it is aimed at?

Please begin with your bottom line in the first sentence—the single most important thing your analysis shows—and then give your reasoning, without preamble and without restating the proposal.
