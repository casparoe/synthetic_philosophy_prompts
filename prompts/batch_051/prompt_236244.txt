Lately I follow a policy I'd like a verdict on. When I notice a belief was formed or rehearsed under conditions I don't trust -- sleep deprivation, anger, wanting a result, skimming a paper I already had a stake in -- I make myself go back over the first-order reasoning from scratch. If the argument still looks good on a slow re-read, I keep my confidence exactly where it was; I don't add an extra discount just becuase the conditions were bad. Last month this happened with a meta-analysis I read at 2 a.m. after a fight: it still seemed solid the next morning, so I didn't lower my confidence. My friend says this is epistemic akrasia -- I'm retaining a belief while accepting higher-order evidence that my process was unreliable -- and that higher-order evidence must count for something on its own. But if I've already re-checked the object-level reasons, what work would lowering my confidence do? Is my policy defensible, or am I just giving my old conclusions a free pass? How would I tell from the inside?
