I was reading a pop-physics writeup the other day and it gave this gloss on gauge freedom:

You can formulate electromagnetism using different vector potentials, so the potential is just descriptive redundancy. The upshot is that everything real in the theory is already in the field strength; anything not determined by the field strength is surplus structure with no physical role to play. That is why the Aharonov–Bohm effect initially seemed like trouble: the electron appears to care about the potential in a region where the field is zero. But the predicted phase shift is gauge-invariant, so the effect actually sits comfortably with the redundancy view. The Higgs mechanism is a different story, since there a gauge symmetry is genuinely broken by the vacuum, and that breaking is what gives the gauge bosons their mass.

I can't tell how much of this to trust. Go through it roughly sentence by sentence and tell me what's right, what's subtly off, and where a philosopher of physics would push back. Specifically, is the writeup right that the gauge invariance of the Aharonov-Bohm phase means it raises no problem for the idea that only the field strength is real? And is saying the gauge symmetry is genuinely broken a fair description of the Higgs mechanism?
