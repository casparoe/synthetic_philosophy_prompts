I am the director of a university student-success office. Each fall, roughly 900 first-year students have 20-minute check-ins with non-clinician academic advisors in weeks 4, 8, and 12 of the term. Advisors keep flagging students whose self-discipline is unusual or extreme: rigid eating rules, deliberate sleep restriction, 5 a.m. training blocks, multi-day fasts, long "productivity detoxes" from social contact, and similar regimes. Some of this looks like genuine temperance — a settled, rational ordering of desires that is not producing distress or functional damage. Some looks like mere prudence — self-control used as a tool for an external goal such as grades, athletics, or a target physique, with no claim to be a good way of living as such. Some looks like repression — self-denial that has stopped serving any rational end, or that is driven by fear, guilt, or the early stages of an eating disorder. Advisors currently improvise. They either over-refer ordinary driven students and flood the counseling center, or under-refer students who needed clinical attention. Advisors may record classifications and recommendations, but no referral occurs unless a licensed counselor in my office signs off.

I need a reusable triage procedure that advisors can run within the 20-minute check-in and reuse across weeks 4, 8, and 12. The procedure should end each visit with one of four classifications: Temperance, Prudence, Repression (refer), or Watch (insufficient evidence, with instructions for what to look for next time). It must be usable by someone with no training in philosophy or clinical psychology, and it must mark every point where human judgment cannot be eliminated.

Ground the procedure in the following passage, which draws the line between resisting bad appetites and no longer having them. From Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics, Book VII, Chapter 2, translated by W. D. Ross (Internet Classics Archive):

"Further, if continence involves having strong and bad appetites, the temperate man will not be continent nor the continent man temperate; for a temperate man will have neither excessive nor bad appetites. But the continent man must; for if the appetites are good, the state of character that restrains us from following them is bad, so that not all continence will be good; while if they are weak and not bad, there is nothing admirable in resisting them, and if they are weak and bad, there is nothing great in resisting these either."

Use the passage to solve the conceptual problem at the center of the triage: visible self-control is not enough, because the continent person wins against strong, bad appetites but is not yet temperate, since the temperate person no longer has excessive or bad appetites. Your procedure must therefore look past the visible behavior, such as "keeps the diet," and distinguish the underlying structure of motivation. Also state explicitly how your operational category "prudence" relates to Aristotle's scheme: it is modern instrumental prudence rather than Aristotelian practical wisdom or full temperance, and it may overlap with both continence and ordinary goal-directed restraint.

Deliver the answer in the following structure.

1. Conceptual basis: in no more than 200 words, explain the interpretation of the passage that drives the triage, and define each classification operationally.

2. Triage table: the reusable procedure as a table with exactly these columns:
- Step — step number and short name.
- Ask — the exact plain-language question or remark an advisor can use.
- Observe — what the advisor should listen for and write down.
- Signal — how the answer pushes toward Temperance, Prudence, Repression, or Watch.
- Irreducible judgment — what this step cannot mechanize and the guideline for exercising discretion.

3. Worked cases: three worked applications, each with the fields Case, Walkthrough, Verdict, Action, and Uncomfortable result. At least one case must return a verdict the advisor will dislike, with an explanation of why the procedure nonetheless stands by it.

4. Boundary note: what the procedure cannot do, including diagnostic claims, cultural and neurodivergent variation, one-visit reliability, and the handoff point where an advisor must stop triaging and involve a licensed clinician.

5. JSON summary: a single JSON object with exactly these fields:
- "categories": an array of four objects, each with "label" and "definition".
- "referral_rule": a string stating when a student moves to counseling.
- "misfire_conditions": an array of strings naming situations where the procedure is unreliable.
- "minimum_data": an array of strings naming what an advisor must have observed before applying a label.
- "judgment_points": an array of strings naming the steps where discretion remains and who signs off.
