Suppose tomorrow a demonstration emerges, accepted by nearly everyone who can follow it, that Leibniz was literally right: the actual world is not just one decent world among others, but the best of all possible worlds in his precise sense — there are possible worlds with less evil, but they contain disproportionately less good, so no world has a greater net surplus of good over evil than ours. The demonstration doesn’t reveal why any particular evil occurs, only that each one is part of an optimal whole. I want your best argued prediction of what happens over the next thirty years to philosophy of religion, moral motivation and activism, and everyday expressions of regret. I’m less interested in whether the demonstration is sound than in the effects of broad confidence in its conclusion. Lead with your bottom-line prediction in the first sentence and then reason it through, no preamble. Along the way, tell me which downstream effects you’re most confident about and which hinge on whether people actually revise their habits of complaint and protest when those habits get undercut.
