Draft the joint statement that a retributivist retentionist and a "procedural abolitionist" about the death penalty could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Set up their starting views like this. The retributivist thinks the worst murders deserve death, that that's just what fitting the punishment to the crime means, that deterrence is irrelevant to the justification either way, and that the executing-the-innocent problem is a real but fixable procedural failure. The procedural abolitionist grants that desert is a real consideration and that maybe in some impossible world with perfect certainty and perfect fairness an execution could be permissible, but she thinks every achievable system carries an irreducible risk of killing the innocent, and that risk plus the irrevocability is decisive against the practice in the real world.

Produce the document they'd publish, not a transcript of their arguments. Include: (1) a numbered section of claims both can sign, with none of them being vacuous — each one should actually constrain somebody; (2) a disagreements table, with each residual disagreement phrased so the relevant side would accept the wording for their own view, plus a column tagging wether the disagreement is normative, empirical, or about which level (ideal theory vs. institutional design) is doing the work.

Then list the cruxes. I suspect the main ones are something like these three. (i) Whether the abolitionist's objection really is all about error, or whether she'd still object in a stipulated certitude scenario — a convict whose guilt and desert are known with certainty. (ii) Whether the retributivist is committed to positive retribution (the state wrongs someone by not executing the worst murderers) or only negative retribution (death wouldn't exceed what's deserved, but the state is free to show mercy) — here use a concrete commutation case: a governor may commute a plainly guilty, maximally culpable murderer's sentence to life without parole, and both sides say whether anyone is wronged by that. (iii) Whether there's any exchange rate each would accept between executing the innocent and under-punishing the guilty, or whether that question rests on a category mistake — specify the concrete case or ledger-like setup both would agree bears on it. For each crux, write down in advance what each side predicts the other will answer and what would have to be true for that prediction to be wrong. If part of their dispute is just the retributivist doing ideal theory while the abolitionist is doing institutional design, say which part and re-state it in wording they'd both sign off on.
