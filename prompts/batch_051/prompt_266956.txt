I keep noticing what looks like the same fork in two different fields, and I want to know whether the resemblance is structural or merely verbal. In modal metaphysics, one side holds that "Dana might have become a judge" is true only if Dana herself exists in another possible world and is a judge there, so the same individual persists across worlds under transworld identity. The other side denies that any individual exists in more than one world: Dana has counterparts in other worlds, distinct individuals that resemble her in the respects relevant to the context, and the modal claim is true just in case some suitable counterpart is a judge. Programming languages draw a strikingly similar line when they distinguish identity from equality. An identity test asks whether two expressions pick out the very same object, while an equality test can return true of two distinct objects with matching structure. The mapping I have in mind is that transworld identity relates to counterpart theory as reference identity relates to structural equality, and that counterpart theory's insistence on qualitative resemblance rather than bare cross-world thisness is like structural equality's deliberate disregard of an object's address. If the mapping is sound, trade-offs from language design should transfer to modal metaphysics, for example, the way one needs identity to track mutation and aliasing but structural comparison to reason about data, which suggests modal discourse might need both notions. But counterpart relations are usually non-symmetric and non-transitive, whereas structural equality is supposed to be an equivalence, so the analogy may already have split. Is this a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion? If it is genuine, what does the programming side actually tell us about the metaphysics of modality, and where does the correspondence run out? Please answer in American English.
