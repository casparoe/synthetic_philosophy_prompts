I'm trying to draw up a complete inventory of the moves available to a New Hume / skeptical-realist reader of Hume, the kind of person who wants to hold that there's a real necessary connection in nature even though Hume says we can't form an idea of it. These are moves they can use against a regularity reader who leans hard on the passage where Hume says necessity exists in the mind and not in objects. So far I've got five:

(1) the epistemic/conceptual point: that line is about the idea of necessary connection, not about what exists, so it can be true while there's still a real tie out there that we can't conceive;
(2) the secret-powers point: Hume keeps writing about unknown powers and springs in bodies and minds, and talks as if they're perfectly real, just hidden from us;
(3) the relative-idea move: Hume allows a relative idea of unknown substrata or unknown qualities in other cases, and the New Humean transfers that to the real connexion between cause and effect;
(4) the two-definitions point: the two definitions of cause define the idea we have, not cause itself, so neither definition shuts down the possibility of a real relation behind the definition;
(5) the external-world parallel: with bodies, Hume denies we have an adequate idea of them but doesn't deny that they exist, and the New Humean says necessary connection is the same sort of case.

Some of these are cousins, but I'm keeping them seperate because they needle different parts of the regularity reading. What I want is a sixth move that is not on this list and not a restatement or special case of any of them, that does real work against a regularity reading, and that stays within the Treatise or the first Enquiry — no secondary literature, no proper names needed. Then tell me honestly whether it survives being pressed or whether it just collapses back into one of the five.
