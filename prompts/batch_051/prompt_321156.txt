I help run a small independent research outfit—maybe a dozen people—and any one of us can put something on the public blog, release a dataset, or send a draft to a reporter without asking anyone. That was mostly fine until last month, when two people unilaterally published a post arguing that a widely used method is unsafe, and half the group thinks it should never have gone public. Me and a friend argued about it for an hour—whether they were reckless or we're just sore that someone moved faster than the rest of us. I've since been reading about the unilateralist's curse: when any single member can act, the group tends to act on the judgment of its most optimistic member, even when most people think the act is a bad idea. The stakes vary from mildly embarrassing to genuinely dangerous, and this comes up a couple times a month. I want a reusable procedure we can actually adopt. Before a tired grad student hits publish, it should tell them which questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer, where they're forced to check with one other person, where they need the whole group or a designated reviewer to sign off, where the procedure says just go ahead, and where it should hand the call to a human because it's really a values question. Then run it on three cases: publishing that kind of "this method is unsafe" post, releasing a partially redacted dataset that could maybe be de-anonymized, and a member quietly contacting a regulator about suspected misconduct. Be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and tell me one plausible case where the procedure would stop someone from doing something I'd personally be glad they did.
