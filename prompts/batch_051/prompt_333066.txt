I am a PhD student in epistemology, and for a seminar project I am building a formal model of how chains of justification come to an end under adversarial questioning. The protocol works as follows. A challenger may ask "why?" of any reason a defender offers. The defender must then respond in one of three ways: (a) supply a new reason; (b) reinstate a reason already used earlier in the chain; or (c) mark the current reason as foundational and decline further challenge. Crucially, the protocol has a fixed horizon: after twenty such challenges, the exchange ends automatically, with or without foundational closure.

Please evaluate the three classical responses to the Münchhausen trilemma — infinite regress, circularity, and dogmatic termination — as stopping policies within this forced finite horizon. Note that the regress horn, once embedded in a finite protocol, collapses into the policy of always supplying a new reason until the horizon is reached, and then simply stopping. Rank the three policies from most to least acceptable, and score each from 0 to 10 on two axes: (i) epistemic defensibility, meaning whether the policy would tend to leave the defender with a justified belief, and (ii) dialectical integrity, meaning whether the policy is honest and fair toward the challenger. In comparing circularity and dogmatic termination, say something precise about whether a circular chain of, say, seven moves that returns to its starting point is any better or worse than a dogmatic stop at the first premise. Also address whether it matters that a regress is merely potential — a further answer is always available — rather than an actual completed infinite chain.

Present the ranking as an ordered list with the two scores given numerically for each horn, followed by a short paragraph of reasoning per horn. Keep the whole response to about 450 words.
