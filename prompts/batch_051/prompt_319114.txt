I have been working through the section of Hume's A Treatise of Human Nature on the obligation of promises (Book 3, Part 2, Section 5), and I find its deflationary account deeply attractive. It promises to dissolve a superficially mysterious moral power into an artificial convention sustained by common interest and the general expectation of fidelity, leaving no unexplained obligation conjured by an inward act at the moment of promising. The passage I keep returning to is his statement of the challenge: "If promises be natural and intelligible, there must be some act of the mind attending these words, I promise; and on this act of the mind must the obligation depend." He then surveys the inward acts that might accompany the words and concludes: "The act of the mind, exprest by a promise, is not a resolution to perform any thing: For that alone never imposes any obligation."

Yet I also have a firm intuition that the small cases do not dissolve so easily. If I tell a friend I will meet her at seven, or that I will return her call tonight, and then simply fail to do so — even when she has rearranged nothing around me, suffered no measurable loss, and the general practice of promising would survive this one breach — I still feel that I have wronged her and that an apology is owed. The wrongness feels as though it attaches to my having said I would, to the small reliability I held out to her, rather than to the usefulness of the surrounding convention. Punctuality and minor commitments seem, in my experience, to carry a gravity that the conventional account strains to explain.

I want a negotiated verdict rather than a one-sided ruling. Should I conclude that the intuition is a byproduct of internalizing a useful convention, and let its apparent intrinsic gravity go? Should I conclude that the account needs amendment — that there is, after all, something in the act of committing, or in the virtue of reliability, that Hume's convention cannot supply? Or is there a reading on which the convention explains the standing institution of promising while the intuition tracks a separate but compatible obligation of truthfulness or respect for persons? Please give me the strongest version of each resolution and say what would justify adopting it.
