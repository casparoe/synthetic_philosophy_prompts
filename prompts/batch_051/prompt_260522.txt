I have noticed that this last phenomenon—identity surviving mutation—is exactly the point at which my own reasoning tends to break down.

I would like you to translate a philosophical dispute into that vocabulary, and then tell me what does not survive the translation. The dispute concerns how objects persist through change. Endurantism holds that a persisting object is wholly present at each moment at which it exists: the very same object exists at different times and simply has different properties at those times. Perdurantism holds that a persisting object is spread through time the way a road is spread through space: it is a whole made of distinct temporal parts, one for each moment or interval, and what we loosely call the same object at two different times is really two different temporal parts of a single four-dimensional whole.

Please restate each position, as faithfully as you can, in the programming vocabulary I described above: references, objects, identity, state, values, copies, and mutation of fields. Go concept by concept, and for each one say what translates cleanly, what has to be bent to fit, and what cannot be expressed at all. Then give me a residue report: identify what was lost or distorted in the translation, and explain whether that residue is a limitation of the programming vocabulary, a sign that the philosophical disagreement is less substantive than it appears, or both.
