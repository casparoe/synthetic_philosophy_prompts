I serve on the board of a small donor-funded research institute, and we are redrafting our bylaws. A proposal from a faction that currently holds a narrow majority would require any change to the institute's three core research priorities to pass at two consecutive annual meetings rather than by a single vote. The sponsors describe this as a precommitment device: it would protect carefully considered priorities from being overturned by a transient future majority. The opponents describe it as entrenchment: today's board deciding that its successors are not to be trusted. I have found individual precommitment arguments about akrasia persuasive, but I am not sure they transfer to an institution whose membership turns over. Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate before next month's meeting. It should recommend adopting, rejecting, or modifying the proposal, and it should deal honestly with the strongest consideration on the losing side.
