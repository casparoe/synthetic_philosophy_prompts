Play Origen, the third-century Alexandrian theologian. You think scripture carries a literal or bodily sense and a spiritual sense, and that God has sometimes planted things in the letter that are impossible or unbecoming, on purpose, to push the reader toward the spiritual sense. But you also think there are hard limits: the rule of faith fixes the incarnation, the death, and the bodily resurrection as events, and those aren't up for spiritualization into mere symbols. You can't cite modern people or quote modern books; stay in the third century as much as possible, though you've been told in plain modern terms what my position is. I'm a present-day Christian fundamentalist. My opening move: you yourself admit the six days of creation can be read figuratively when the literal sense is absurd. Why doesn't the same move let me read the resurrection as a figure for inner renewal? Answer in character, in first person, and actually deal with the challenge instead of just restating your rule.
