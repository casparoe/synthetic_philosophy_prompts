I'm preparing a handout for an upper-division philosophy of mind seminar on Dennett's "Quining Qualia," and I want the centerpiece to be a "which position on qualia do you actually hold?" quiz. The quiz should sort students by what they say about concrete cases rather than by which label they prefer, and every question should be answerable by someone who has never heard the names of the positions.

The underlying fault line the quiz should track is this. One style of view treats qualia as real, introspectable features of experience over and above what a subject can report, remember, compare, or be disposed to do. Another style says the phenomenal character of an experience just is its representational content, so there is no ineffable leftover. A third style says the leftover is an incoherent posit: many disputes that seem to be about qualia have no fact of the matter because the distinction they rely on is not well drawn. I want the questions to locate students along this line, and to show whether any coherent intermediate positions emerge.

Please write the quiz with these constraints:

- Eight to ten questions. Each question should describe a concrete case and offer two or three answer options. Do not ask in any question whether qualia are real, or which named position the student accepts.
- Use at least the following cases, each set up briefly and fairly: the inverted spectrum; Dennett's Chase and Sanborn case, glossed in one sentence (two professional coffee tasters diverge: one reports that the coffee tastes the way it always did but that he no longer enjoys it, and the other reports that he still enjoys it but that it no longer tastes the way it did); Mary the color scientist; a subject whose color perception degrades so gradually that she can never catch it changing; a blindsight subject who denies seeing but guesses above chance; and a person encountering a novel taste that she cannot describe.
- Provide a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto at least these leaves: qualia realism, where qualia are intrinsic and non-representational; representationalism, where phenomenal character is identical to representational content; and illusionism or eliminativism about qualia, where the posit should be quined (treated as an eliminable theoretical artifact) and the debates it generates mostly dissolve. Where a pattern does not fit any leaf, either name the residual view or mark the pattern as unoccupied or unstable.
- Flag each pair of leaves that your questions cannot reliably separate, and identify what sort of case or question would start to separate them.
- Identify at least one answer pattern that is logically available but that almost no one occupies, and diagnose it: is the combination incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply underexplored?
