I have been trying to make up my mind about a principle in formal epistemology that I find both elegant and, in many cases, impossible to accept. The principle, usually called Reflection, says roughly this: let P be my current credence function and P_t the credence function I expect to have at some future time t. Then, for any proposition A and any value r, I ought to satisfy P(A | P_t(A) = r) = r. In words: my present degree of belief in A, conditional on my future self's believing A to degree r, should itself be r. The principle has a lot going for it. It gives a precise answer to the question of how much I should defer to my own future judgments, it explains why I should regard my future self as a learning process rather than a stranger whose opinions I can ignore, and it seems to follow naturally from the thought that my future credences, insofar as they depart from my present ones, will have been produced by updating on evidence I do not yet possess. I also have some sympathy for the claim that violating it exposes me to a kind of diachronic incoherence, though I have never been sure how much weight to give that argument.

But there is a case that leaves me stuck. I am reliably informed that I am about to be given a drug that will temporarily make me paranoid and overconfident about one specific proposition — say, that my neighbors are plotting against me. I now regard that proposition as very unlikely, and I have no new evidence that would change that. I know the drug will make me assign the proposition a credence of 0.95. Reflection appears to demand that my current credence in the proposition, conditional on its being true that my drugged future self believes it to degree 0.95, be 0.95. But I do not accept that, and I do not think I am being incoherent in refusing. The future high credence is evidence about my brain chemistry, not about my neighbors. I am not willing to surrender this intuition, and I am not willing to surrender the principle without seeing what it costs.

So I want a negotiated diagnosis. Should Reflection be restricted so that it only binds me with respect to future selves whose methods or reasoning I currently endorse? Does that restriction preserve the principle, or does it drain it of all content? Or should I instead revise my intuition about the drug case — for example, by arguing that I have misdescribed the event I am conditioning on, or that the drugged state breaks the continuity of the epistemic subject enough that Reflection no longer applies? Walk me through what each option costs, and tell me which revision — of the principle, of the intuition, or of the way the case is set up — is most defensible, and what would justify it.
