Please argue that the circularity worry about Woodward's interventionism—roughly, the view that X is a cause of Y just in case Y changes under a suitable intervention on X—is not dispelled by the usual move of characterizing interventions in noncausal terms. The conditions on a good intervention on X with respect to Y (it changes X, it does not influence Y except through a route running through X, and it is independent of any common cause of X and Y) still look causal through and through, and I want you to argue that spelling them out as counterfactual or independence conditions merely relocates the circularity, since which counterfactuals count as surgical depends on background causal structure. Press hardest on the cases where the official reply is weakest: X and Y sharing an unmeasured common cause, and X and Y in a feedback loop. Then tell me wether the residual circularity is benign—because interventionism is best read as a semantics or a test for causal claims rather than a reductive analysis—or wether it actually undermines the view.
