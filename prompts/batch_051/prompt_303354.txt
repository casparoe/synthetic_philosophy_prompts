Question: The heritability of adult height in a well-nourished population is about 0.8. What does this number tell us about the causes of individual differences in height in that population, and what, if anything, does it imply about how much average height would change if everyone's childhood nutrition improved?

Student answer: The 0.8 means that 80% of the variation in height in the population is caused by genetic differences, and the rest by the environment. So improving childhood nutrition would do relatively little, because most of the differences between people are already genetic and the trait is mostly fixed by genes once nutrition is adequate. Twin studies confirm this: identical twins are much more similar than fraternal twins, which shows genetic differences have a strong causal role and the environment only a small one. The estimate is population-specific, of course; in a population where nutrition varied a lot from kid to kid, heritability would be lower, which would mean the environment was more important there than it is here.
