I have been thinking about the following parallel, and I would like a careful judgment about whether it is deep or superficial.

In inverse reinforcement learning (IRL), we observe an agent's trajectories through a known environment and try to recover the reward function the agent is implicitly optimizing. The central difficulty is reward ambiguity: once arbitrary rewards are allowed, infinitely many reward functions make the observed behavior optimal — down to the degenerate reward function that assigns zero everywhere, which makes every policy equally "optimal" and therefore explains nothing. To get a useful answer, IRL methods quietly impose a bias: rewards are constrained to be linear in some features, or among the reward functions consistent with the observed behavior we choose the one under which the behavior is as uncommitted or maximally entropic as possible, and so on.

Now consider radical interpretation: an interpreter observes a speaker's utterances and nonlinguistic behavior and must simultaneously assign meanings to words, beliefs, and desires. The interpreter proceeds under something like a principle of charity or rationality: choose the assignment that makes the speaker come out as rational and largely in agreement with the interpreter. The same shape of underdetermination seems to appear: for any finite batch of observed behavior, indefinitely many assignments of beliefs and desires could rationalize it, and without something like charity the attribution problem appears hopelessly degenerate.

So the structural analogy I am seeing is:

- observed trajectories / policy ↔ observed utterances and behavior
- reward function ↔ desires (or the utility side of belief-desire psychology)
- environment dynamics ↔ the circumstances and facts the agent is responding to
- assumption that the agent optimizes reward ↔ assumption that the agent is rational and mostly right
- reward ambiguity ↔ indeterminacy of interpretation
- max-entropy or feature bias ↔ charity as an inductive bias

I cannot decide whether this is a genuine structural identity or just the fact that both are inverse problems from behavior to an unobserved internal quantity. Please adjudicate.
