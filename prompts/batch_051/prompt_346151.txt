After a long argument with a friend about whether alienation is central to Marx's critique of capitalism, I realized we kept using the word without distinguishing what actually does the alienating in the passage below. I would like help understanding the conceptual mechanics of this paragraph from the Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844, in the section on estranged labor:

"The object which labor produces – labor’s product – confronts it as something alien, as a power independent of the producer. The product of labor is labor which has been embodied in an object, which has become material: it is the objectification of labor. Labor’s realization is its objectification. Under these economic conditions this realization of labor appears as loss of realization for the workers; objectification as loss of the object and bondage to it; appropriation as estrangement, as alienation."

My question is about how the argument moves. Is Marx claiming that objectification necessarily becomes alienation under private property, or is he saying alienation is just objectification viewed from the worker's side, so that the contrast between objectification and alienation does no real work? I want to know whether the premise that labor's realization is its objectification by itself contains the alienation claim, or whether the phrase "under these economic conditions" is carrying an extra, non-obvious step.
