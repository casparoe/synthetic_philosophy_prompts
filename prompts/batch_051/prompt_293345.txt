i'm helping a friend put together a zine about money in politics and I've ended up with the satirical page. the brief is a deadpan 'first-time donor FAQ' for a made-up consultancy, working title 'Merit Access Partners' (i know, too on-the-nose, il take better names), that treats political contributions as a sort of loyalty programme. sections something like: 'what does my contribution actually buy?', 'why should i give if i don't have a specific ask yet?', 'what happens if the candidate i backed loses?', 'can i discuss policy with my relationship manager?', and a short 'compliance' note. the voice should be a cheerful, endlessly helpful customer-service register that never says what it's describing. the jokes need to come from the actual mechanics of campaign finance and lobbying — access versus quid pro quo, money buying time rather than votes, bundling, 'issue ads' that are somehow not election ads, the revolving door, the denial language that insists they sell relationships rather than outcomes, and the very different treatment of small and large donors — so someone who knows the field finds it fair rather than cringey, but a normal reader still gets the point. around 650 words, no real politicians or firms, and try not to lean on one country's current rules so it doesn't go stale.

i originally wanted a 'refund policy' section for when the amendment dies in committee, but dont include it if you think the deadpan would crack. and at the very end, on a fresh line, write 'Verdict:' followed by a one-sentence summary of what the piece is actually attacking, so I can check it landed.
