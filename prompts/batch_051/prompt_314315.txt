I'm a software engineer at a small company that builds a climate-risk dashboard for municipal water utilities and county planning offices. The product lets a user pull up a location and see projected changes in things like annual precipitation, extreme-heat days, and reservoir inflow under different emissions scenarios, drawn from the big public climate model ensembles. My job has quietly become deciding how much confidence we can honestly put behind each number we show. The trouble is that the models frequently disagree, and even when they agree they are not genuinely independent, because many of them share code, parameterizations, and historical training data; a tight-looking band can reflect duplicated assumptions rather than real confidence. At the same time, our clients want a single number for a bond application or an infrastructure-sizing decision, our product lead signs off on anything client-facing and wants quarterly releases, and most of the people making these calls are engineers and product managers with no climate-science or philosophy background. Right now the decisions get made ad hoc, usually by whoever is editing the display layer that week.

I would like a reusable decision procedure we can run on any proposed projection before it ships. It should specify the questions to ask and in what order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure should stop and say we should not show this, and where it should hand the decision to a human because the remaining question is a matter of values rather than facts. I assume there is serious academic thought about model ensembles, robustness, and decision-relevant uncertainty, perhaps in philosophy of science or a related field, but the procedure itself has to be usable by my teammates under deadline pressure, and I want it to be explicit about which steps are real judgment calls even if we dress them up as mechanical checks. Please write the whole thing as continuous prose, the way you would write a long email or an internal memo: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables. I will adapt the structure into our internal documentation myself.

Then run the procedure on two proposals we are currently facing. The first is a 2050 mean annual precipitation projection for a small river basin, where 24 of 30 global models point to a slight drying, but the six wettest models share a particular convection scheme and the client wants a single number for long-range water-rights planning. The second is a 2070 projection of the number of days per year above 100 degrees Fahrenheit for a city, where nearly all models agree the number will rise but the spread is enormous, and the client wants us to show the ensemble mean as the headline. I especially want you to show whether the procedure ever returns an answer we will not like, for example that a number our largest client has already asked us to publish should not be shown at all, and to say clearly what the procedure cannot settle on its own.
