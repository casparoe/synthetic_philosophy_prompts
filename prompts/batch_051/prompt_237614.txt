I have no formal training in philosophy, but for years I have been turning over a single puzzle: when a tomato and a fire truck are both red, what exactly is the same across them? I have talked it over with friends, some of whom studied philosophy, so I have picked up a few technical words without having read the literature. Recently one of them asked me to write down my honest commitments. I did, and now I want to know whether they form a coherent package. Could you act as a professional philosopher working in metaphysics, look at the set on its own terms, and give me an audit? Here are the commitments, in my own words:

1. No single entity is wholly present in two distinct things at once. When a tomato and a fire truck are both red, there is not some third entity — redness itself — that occurs identically in both, or that each of them has a share of. The world is made entirely of particulars.

2. But it is still an objective, mind-independent fact that the two are the same color. If no mind had ever noticed them, it would still be true that they match in color.

3. That matching is basic. It does not consist in their sharing a common ingredient, reflecting light of the same wavelength, producing the same response in an instrument, or falling under the same label. Those are either separate facts, or they just push the question back a step.

4. There are no abstract objects — no numbers, sets, or properties conceived as items outside space and time. "Redness" understood as an entity that exists but is not anywhere would be an explanatory free lunch, and I cannot bring myself to believe in free lunches.

5. Every quality a thing has is itself a particular. The tomato has its own private redness — a color-instance just as dated and local as the tomato itself — and the fire truck has its own numerically distinct redness. The same goes for shapes, masses, and charges: a property is always a particular quality in a particular thing.

6. However, when two red things are the same color, the relation of being-the-same-color is not private to that pair. It is the very same relation that holds between other red things of that shade. It would be wrong to say that each pair resembles in its own peculiar way; the sameness recurs across all the matching pairs.

7. I am inclined to think those are all the ingredients reality needs: particular things, their particular qualities, and the primitive similarities among them. With those in hand you have everything, and nothing repeatable or abstract needs to be added.

I cannot see where the problem lies, but I suspect the package may be strained somewhere. Please think carefully before answering: first work through each commitment individually, then each pair, before settling on an audit. Then tell me which pairs of commitments are in serious tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say exactly what would have to give and what accepting that loss would cost me. If there is one revision that would do more than any other to make the whole set coherent, identify it — but do not pretend that every problem can be solved by a single fix if that is not so. I am not looking for reading recommendations or scholarly citations; I want to know whether these particular commitments, as stated, hang together.
