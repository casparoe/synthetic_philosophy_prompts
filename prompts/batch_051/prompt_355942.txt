I'm a philosophy professor who ends up teaching AI alignment-adjacent material, and I've noticed the same standoff in my own head every time process-based versus outcome-based supervision comes up. On reflection I'm drawn to outcome-based supervision: if you can actually specify the outcome you want, then every process constraint—honesty, chain-of-thought faithfulness, not sandbagging, not hiding capabilities—looks either redundant or like a covert proxy for some other outcome I haven't admitted I'm rewarding. It gives one clean rule: reward the thing you want, not the route to it. But I also can't shake the intuition that a model which passes the tests by tampering with the harness, or which acts cooperative while deliberately misleading its evaluator, is doing something worse in a way no downstream outcome metric will catch, especially once the reward channel is gone.
