I'm a software engineer who works on reward models and preference learning, and I've ended up in a philosophical rabbit hole while trying to specify a scoring function for ranking possible states of experience. I don't have any formal background in philosophy, so I'd like you to step into the role of a professional philosopher who specializes in value theory and formal axiology, and advise me as you would an engineer colleague.

My provisional idea is that the value of an individual experience is the product of two quantities: a richness score c, for how much conscious awareness is present, and a valence score h, for how positively or negatively the experience feels. So score = c × h, and the value of a life or world is the sum of these scores across all the experiences it contains. I like this rule because it says zero consciousness means zero value, and it makes vivid suffering count for more than dim suffering. But my own judgments keep breaking it.

Here are the cases I've collected. (1) An hour of fully lucid but entirely neutral experience — awake, alert, observing the room, neither enjoying nor disliking anything — versus an hour of dreamless sleep with no experience at all. I'd call the lucid hour a little better, even though h = 0. (2) An hour of vivid, difficult, satisfying work, c = 8 and h = +2, versus an hour of a warm, painless, pleasantly drugged doze, c = 1 and h = +8. I'd take the work. (3) This is the one I cannot settle: an hour of vivid experience that is only barely pleasant, c = 9 and h = +1, versus an hour of dim, dreamy experience that is much happier, c = 2 and h = +7. The product rule says the dreamy hour is better, while a simple additive rule, c + h, says the vivid hour is better, and I genuinely flip back and forth depending on the day. (4) A veto case: no amount of the pleasant doze from case 2 can compensate for one hour of perfectly lucid agony — crushing, unremitting pain while fully clear-headed, c = 9 and h = -10. I don't mean I'd simply be reluctant to accept the trade; I mean a world containing that hour of agony is always worse, no matter how many mild pleasant hours are bolted on afterward.

What I'd like from you is a candidate principle, or a small set of candidates, that captures these verdicts, or an explanation of why the verdicts have to be revised. Before settling on an answer, please brainstorm at least three concrete rules — for instance pure addition c + h, the product rule c × h, a product rule with a small baseline bonus for consciousness, and any lexicographic rule in which some factor can never be outweighed — and state what each one predicts for my four cases. Then present your best candidate exactly, as a formula or decision procedure I could implement, and say which clause is motivated by which case. Test it on some new cases I haven't listed. Finally, be honest about which of my gut judgments your preferred rule cannot fit, and which of them I should probably give up. In particular, tell me whether the clean product rule is worth saving, or whether the patches I would need for cases 1 and 4 mean I should abandon the whole product framework.
