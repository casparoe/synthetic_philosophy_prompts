I am a novelist, fluent in the craft vocabulary working writers actually use — story bible, draft-to-draft continuity, retcon, canon versus apocrypha, the reader's implicit contract, diegetic versus non-diegetic information, unreliable narration, point-of-view filtering, and the difference between what a text states and what it licenses a reader to infer — but I have no training in formal semantics. A running dispute from the philosophy of language keeps surfacing in my reading: whether the name Sherlock Holmes refers to anything, and what makes a claim such as that Holmes lives at 221B Baker Street true, false, or neither when assessed against the stories. I want that dispute restated in my vocabulary, position by position, and then a report on what did not survive the translation.

Cover at least these positions: the view that truth in fiction is governed by an implicit modifier or operator, so that a claim is true in the story just when it follows from the stated contents of the story under that operator; the view that the name abbreviates a set of descriptions associated with the texts; the direct-reference view that the name does its usual semantic work but has no referent; the artifactualist view that Holmes is an abstract object brought into existence by the writing and thereafter available to be written about; and the pretense view that the relevant truth is about what an authorized game of make-believe prescribes that readers imagine. For each, tell me what translates cleanly into my terms, what can be made to fit only with distortion, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all.

Then give me the residue judgment: does what fails to translate indicate that my craft vocabulary is missing distinctions worth acquiring, or that some of the philosophical disagreement is less substantive than it looks once the formalism is set aside? Keep the translation honest; do not silently rebuild the philosophical positions out of my craft categories, because the points where the fit fails are precisely what I need to see. This is not a request for a continuity manual or writing advice — I want the translation and the residue report. My stake is concrete: I want to know whether questions about my own invented characters already have determinate answers apart from my later decisions, whether a retcon changes what was true in a story or changes which story is being told, and whether readers' interpolations are best understood as guesses about something that exists or as additions to something still under construction.
