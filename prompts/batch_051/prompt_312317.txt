I cover AI and the foundations of physics, and a parallel has been nagging me between the embedded agency idea in AI alignment and the old Heisenberg cut in quantum mechanics. In quantum mechanics you can push the cut around and end up with Wigner's friend and a regress of observers; in embedded agency the agent's hardware and its world-model are themselves part of the environment, and its actions alter the very thing it is trying to predict. I can't tell whether this is a real structural identity or just the word observer doing too much work. Could you seperate the mapping from the loose analogy? Specifically, what would have to be true on both sides for a result in one field to transfer to the other, and is there a concrete prediction the parallel makes that turns out false?
