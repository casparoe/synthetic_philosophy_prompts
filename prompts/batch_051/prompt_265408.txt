Define 'species' in a way that gets the following cases right, or else tell me which of my intuitive verdicts I should abandon. Lead with the definition in your first sentence as your bottom line—no preamble and no restating of the question—then run it through every case, say where it strains, and say whether it forces me to change any of the verdicts I started with.

The cases:

1. A lion and a tiger. I take these to be clearly different species, even though they can produce hybrid offspring, which are usually sterile but not always.
2. A domestic dog and a gray wolf. In everyday speech I treat these as different species, but I know some classifications treat the dog as a subspecies of the wolf because they interbreed freely and produce fertile offspring.
3. A human and a Neanderthal. Classically treated as distinct species, yet they evidently interbred and left fertile descendants in many modern human populations.
4. A ring species: the greenish warbler populations that encircle the Tibetan Plateau, where each adjacent population interbreeds with its neighbors, but the ends of the ring do not interbreed where their ranges meet.
5. Two populations of bacteria traditionally classified as distinct species that regularly exchange genes horizontally across species lines while remaining distinct ecological and genetic clusters.
6. Bdelloid rotifers, an ancient group of microscopic animals that appear to have reproduced without sex for tens of millions of years, yet are classified into hundreds of species.
7. A fossil lineage that changes gradually over a million years but is divided by paleontologists into several successive named species.
8. A mule, the sterile offspring of a horse and a donkey; I take it not to be a species at all.

My confident verdicts are 1 and 8. I lean toward distinct species in 2 and 3 but could be argued out of it. For 4 through 7 I genuinely don't know what the right answer is. Propose the definition, run it through all eight, tell me where it surprises you, and revise it or defend the surprise.
