I run a small giving circle—twelve of us, about $200,000 a year to move—and we've spent three meetings stuck on a version of the same fight. Half the group wants to put the money into marginal interventions: bednets, cash transfers, deworming, the kind of thing with a tight RCT and a cost-effectiveness number we can defend when a donor asks. The other half says that's like optimizing the last mile while the road is still dirt: they want to fund policy campaigns, movement-building, neglected infrastructure or R&D, norm change—stuff where the expected value is huge and also, frankly, made up. We've agreed to stop relitigating the top-level question and instead spend our next meeting deciding which unresolved questions actually determine how our checks should move between the two columns. I want you to give me a prioritized list of the cruxes: the open questions where, if we knew the answer tomorrow, at least some of the money would shift from one side to the other. I have some candidate cruxes below, but I dont want a survey—I want a defended ranking that says which of these (or any others) is most worth settling first for a group of our size, with the dependencies between them made explicit. Its fine if the answer is that some of my candidates are nearly decision-irrelevant for us; I want to know that too.

My candidate cruxes, in no particular order: (a) whether systemic-change work has returns that are mostly thresholded and delayed, so a small donor's marginal dollar is wasted unless it pools with others; (b) whether the cost-effectiveness estimates for direct interventions survive out-of-sample and include general-equilibrium effects, or whether we're buying a warm feeling of rigor; (c) how much of the "systemic" wins are zero-sum or value-laden enough that the two camps aren't even measuring the same thing; (d) whether a $200k marginal dollar is better thought of as buying outcomes or as buying information about what works.

Return a JSON object with this schema:

- bottom_line: a string, two or three sentences: how you'd split the $200k today between marginal and systemic (rough percentages are fine), and the single caveat that matters most for that split.
- criteria: an array of two to four objects, each with name and gloss, spelling out the criteria you used to rank the cruxes (e.g., decision relevance for a group our size, tractability, how many other cruxes depend on it, neglectedness).
- cruxes: an array of four to six objects, sorted by priority, each with:
  - rank: integer
  - name: short label
  - question: the unresolved question phrased as a yes/no or parameter question
  - stakes: what would move in our allocation depending on the answer
  - tractability: one of high/medium/low plus one sentence on what evidence or reasoning could settle it in the next few years
  - upstream_of: array of names of other cruxes that hinge on it, or []
  - why_this_rank: one sentence defending the position against your criteria
- would_flip_ranking: a string naming the evidence or argument that would most reorder the list, and how
- deprioritized: an array of one to three objects, each with name and reason, for cruxes (including any of mine) that matter less for a $200k group than they'd appear to, and what would have to be true for that to change
