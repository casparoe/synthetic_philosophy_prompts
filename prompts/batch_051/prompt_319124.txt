ok so here's where I've landed on infinity and I'd like a consistency audit before I present it at a reading group. I'll list the things I believe, and I genuinely don't know which one is the problem child, so don't just tell me everything is fine.

(a) I'm a Platonist about math: sets and numbers exist mind-independently, and the set of natural numbers is a completed, actually infinite totality. Aristotle's potential-only view always struck me as dodging the question.

(b) I hold the Cantorian line on size: for infinite sets, "same size" just means there's a bijection. So the naturals, the evens, and the primes are all the same size, and the reals are a larger infinity.

(c) Hilbert's hotel doesn't bother me. It's just a vivid way of showing that for infinite sets a proper subset needn't be smaller.

(d) But I also think "half as many" is doing real work in a way (b) can't capture. There's a legitimate, not-merely-psychological sense in which the evens are only half of the naturals. I'm not saying we should bring back the whole-greater-than-part rule across the board, but this half-ness feels like a real feature of the situation.

(e) I'm a finitist about the concrete world: there are no actual infinities in nature — no infinite past, no infinite number of physical things. My Platonism in (a) doesn't feel in tension with this because abstract objects aren't in nature.

(f) I also think ordinary continuities — space, time, the path of a moving body — are genuinely continuous, with no smallest bits, and that the real numbers are the right model of a continuum.
