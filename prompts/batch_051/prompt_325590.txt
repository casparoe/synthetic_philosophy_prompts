I'm a retired physicist—spent most of my career in cosmology and gravitation, so I still think in terms of reference frames, coordinate charts, diffeomorphism invariance, and the difference between a gauge choice and a physical statement. Lately I've been reading philosophy of history, and I keep having the same reaction to the debate about whether the past can be told from everywhere at once. Some people argue that a properly global history shouldn't be centered on any one region; others reply that every history is written from somewhere, and that the supposedly neutral global standpoint is really just a Eurocentric set of organizing categories—development, modernity, the nation-state, secular time—wearing the costume of universality. To me this whole wrangle sounds a lot like the distinction between choosing coordinates arbitrarily and claiming that one coordinate system is physically privileged, and also like the point in general relativity that no single chart covers the whole manifold, but that doesn't mean you can't say anything true about the whole manifold.

That's the translation I want someone to actually carry out, not just wave at. Restate the positions in this debate—the aspiration to a decentered global history, the objection that every would-be neutral standpoint smuggles in a Eurocentric perspective, and maybe the middle view that you can have a global account only as a patchwork of many centers—in the vocabulary I know: charts and atlases, diffeomorphism invariance, observables versus coordinate artifacts, gauge symmetries, coordinate singularities. Then give me the residue report: what survives the translation cleanly, what had to be bent to fit, and what has no counterpart in the physics vocabulary at all. In particular I want to know whether my hunch is right that alot of the debate is really about gauge—about which standpoint you choose and whether you mistake your choice for the structure of the past itself—or whether there is something in the historians' disagreement (power, institutions, whose categories get to count as universal) that physics can't express and that does real work. Be as precise as you can, and don't let the analogy do the work.
