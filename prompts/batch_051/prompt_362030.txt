Here's my reconstruction:

(1) Assume, as several serious cosmologies do, that the universe goes on indefinitely in a state where random thermal fluctuations keep occurring.

(2) Over an infinite future, any finite physical configuration that the laws permit will eventually occur by chance — including a human brain in exactly the neural state yours is in right now, with all its apparent memories.

(3) It will not just occur once; over an infinite future it occurs infinitely many times.

(4) For every such brain that is embedded in a normal world with a real past — an Earth, an evolutionary history, and so on — there will be vastly more occurrences of that same brain state appearing alone as a brief, isolated fluctuation.

(5) So, amongst all occurrences of your current brain state, the overwhelming majority are Boltzmann brains: short-lived fluctuations that pop into existence and then dissolve, with no reliable connection to a real external world.

(6) You have no empirical way to tell whether your own current occurrence is one of the ordinary embedded ones or one of the isolated ones, and you should assign equal prior probability to being each kind of occurrence.

(7) Therefore you are overwhelmingly likely to be a Boltzmann brain, and the beliefs you have about the external world are probably false.

And another part of me thinks the argument is actually valid once you grant the cosmology in (1), in which case the only escape is to reject that cosmology. Which is it?
