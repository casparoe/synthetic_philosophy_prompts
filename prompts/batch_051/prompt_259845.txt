I chair a small methods group at a public health agency that reviews draft clinical guidelines before they become official recommendations. The part I keep getting stuck on is the chain of reasons behind each “we recommend X because Y”: if I chase the becauses far enough they either go on forever, loop back on themselves, or just stop at something the authors treat as too obvious to defend. I gather this is the old Münchhausen trilemma, and I’d like the philosophy of it turned into something my team can actually use. We see around three guidelines a month, I’m the one who signs off, and I have maybe forty-five minutes per guideline; nobody on the team has philosophy training, and their not going to read any background theory. Please write a justification-audit procedure, a checklist or flowchart in words, that we can run on a single recommendation. For each step, say what question we’re asking, what counts as a good enough answer to move on, where the procedure stops and says revise or reject, and where it has to stop and hand the decision to a person because the stopping point is a value judgment rather than a fact. Then run the procedure on two or three worked examples, including one where it makes us reject a recommendation the whole panel thought was fine. And be honest about which steps of the procedure are just judgment wearing a checklist.
