I have a standing policy and I'm not sure if it's smart or just protecting my priors. When someone warns that some institution or project (a company, a research community, an AI system, whatever) is undergoing slow value drift — quietly coming to pursue different goals than the ones it was set up with, without any single deliberate change — I don't take the warning seriously unless they can point to a concrete before/after decision or give me the mechanism by which the values changed. Without that, 'drift' feels unfalsifiable to me, and it often seems to mean just 'I don't like what it's doing now.' A friend says this is exactly how people miss slow failures: you often can't point to a single changed decision because the whole point is that small changes compound, and my rule means I would only ever notice the flashy failure mode of a system actively seeking influence, never the quiet one. Is my policy defensible? If not, is there a restated version that is, and how would i tell a real drift warning from vibes from the inside? Keep the answer under 400 words.
