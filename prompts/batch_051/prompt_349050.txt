The Daoist ideal of wu-wei is standardly described as a kind of action that is spontaneous, unforced, and not governed by deliberate calculation. Yet the attempt to become spontaneous looks self-undermining: the effort to stop trying seems to be one more instance of the very trying it is supposed to eliminate. In a recent class we considered psychological research on ironic processes as a possible modern articulation of this puzzle. Subjects instructed to suppress a thought—for instance, a white bear—typically find the thought intruding with unusual frequency, and deliberate attempts to monitor and control the fluency of one's own performance often make the performance stiffer rather than smoother. If that research is sound, does it show only that wu-wei cannot be pursued as a direct goal, or does it show something stronger, namely that any form of deliberate self-cultivation aimed at wu-wei is likely to reinforce the very self-consciousness it seeks to remove? If wu-wei can still be cultivated, explain what kind of cultivation that would have to be, and how it avoids covertly reinstating the effortful monitoring it is meant to overcome. If it cannot, say whether the ideal should be abandoned or reinterpreted, and what would be lost.
