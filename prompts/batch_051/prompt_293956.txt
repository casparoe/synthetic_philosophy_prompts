Okay, so a county manager I know is floating this plan and I want to think through how it actually plays out. Any project over a certain size—say a warehouse, a road widening, even a big data center—would have to file a moral impact statement before getting a permit. The county sets a public schedule of moral weights per affected being: humans 1.0, dogs .05, pigs .02, chickens .01, honeybees .0001 per individual, ants .000001, and “AI systems above threshold X” at .005. The developer estimates the number of each kind of being whose expected welfare is changed, multiplies by the weight, sums, and if the total exceeds a cap they have to buy mitigation credits from approved conservation groups or pay into a fund. Walk me through the incentives here. What do developers do first—contest the weights, game the counting, redefine what counts as a being, or corner the mitigation market? Does the cap just become a cost of doing business for big projects? What happens when citizens realize the ant weight implies you can pave over a million ants for the price of one dog? And can the schedule be fixed by better science, or does it need a political consensus on graded moral status that the county doesn’t have?
