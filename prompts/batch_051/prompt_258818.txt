Me and my friend Dennis have been going back and forth for over a year about whether viruses are alive and whether there's really a fact of the matter about where life begins and ends. I'm the one who thinks life is a real thing with a real boundary: I basically accept the kind of definition astrobiologists use, something about a chemical system that keeps itself going and can evolve, and I think with enough work you can say where the line is. He thinks 'alive' is more like a word we use for things we feel like treating a certain way, and that nature doesn't actually draw the line, because any definition either leaves out something we want in or lets in something we want out. We call my side the boundary view and his the label view. We've decided to write the thing you'd get from an honest adversarial colaboration: the claims we can both sign, the exact places we still part ways on, and the cruxes that would actually move one of us. Write it as continuous prose, more like a letter we'd send to our friends, and please no headings, bullett points, bold text, or tables. Make sure you get in the stuff we genuinely agree on, the disagreements worded in a way each of us would accept for our own side, and for each crux a test or case both of us agree would actually bear on it, with each person's prediction written down in advance. Bring in the hard cases, like viruses, prions (those misfolded-protein things that spread), and lab-built cell-like things with stripped-down genomes, and if you think some of our fight was purely verbal, say which part and why. I don't want a verdict on who's right; I want the document both of us could put our names to.
