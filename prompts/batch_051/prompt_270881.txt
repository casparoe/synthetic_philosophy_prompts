Exam question: State the Barcan formula (BF) and the Converse Barcan formula (CBF) in a standard quantified modal language, and explain whether accepting both formulas commits one to the thesis that everything that exists exists necessarily.

Student answer: The Barcan formula is ∀x□Fx → □∀xFx, and the Converse Barcan formula is □∀xFx → ∀x□Fx. In constant-domain Kripke semantics both are valid. In variable-domain semantics, BF is valid just in case the domain never grows along the accessibility relation, while CBF is valid just in case it never shrinks; so a frame validating both must have a constant domain across all worlds accessible from any given world. A logic containing BF and CBF as theorems therefore forces a constant domain. But a constant domain is exactly the claim that the same objects fall within the range of the quantifiers at every world. Hence any object in the actual domain occurs in the domain of every possible world; and since to be in the domain of a world is to exist at that world, everything that actually exists exists necessarily. Accepting BF and CBF thus commits one to necessitism.
