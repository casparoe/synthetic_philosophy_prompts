Someone claims that a deterministic chaotic system — the Lorenz attractor, say — is unpredictable in a stronger sense than a merely imprecisely measured linear system. On this view, sensitive dependence on initial conditions and the fractal geometry of the strange attractor are features of the dynamics itself, so the unpredictability belongs to the world and not just to the observer. A critic replies that this is an illusion: because the governing equations fix the future given the present, all unpredictability is epistemic, consisting only in the amplification of finite measurement error, and calling a system chaotic merely records how fast that error grows.

Design the best test you can for deciding between these two readings, and say exactly what the test would and would not settle. Be concrete: what system or systems would you compare, what quantities would you measure or compute, what would you hold fixed, and which results would favor each side? Then give a detailed account of the residue. Even a decisive positive result would leave what open, and why? For example, would it settle whether the distant future of the system is objectively open or merely unknown, and whether a strange attractor is a real object in state space rather than a convenient summary of visited states? Be explicit about which part of the original claim your test operationalizes and which part it simply posits.
