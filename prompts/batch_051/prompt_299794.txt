I'm a high school student who copyedits for my school's newspaper, and after a semester of arguing with the other editors about style, I've realized my own sense of when something counts as "bad grammar" doesn't fit any rule I can state. My teacher also gave us an article on the prescriptivism–descriptivism debate, which made me notice how inconsistent my reactions are. Here are my verdicts on a list of constructed sentences. (1) "If I was you, I'd apologize" — I would change was to were; it reads as an error. (2) "Me and him went to the store" — I'd rewrite it as "He and I went," and I'd count the original as wrong. Strangely, though, I have no problem with "It was me," and I would not correct it to "It was I," which sounds stiff and unnatural to me. (3) "I have less books than last semester" — I'd fix it to fewer, but only mildly, and I wouldn't be surprised if the rule eventually disappears. (4) "Hopefully, the meeting will end early" — this seems perfectly fine to me, even though a usage guide I read calls it wrong. (5) "I literally died" to mean I was very surprised or embarrassed is fine — informal hyperbole, not bad grammar. (6) "If I had known, I would have went" — clearly wrong to me; "would have gone" is the only acceptable version. (7) "Each student should hand in their paper by Friday" — I let this stand, and I'd do the same with they used for a specific person who asked for that pronoun. (8) "I didn't see nothing" — I think this is grammatical in the dialect of some students I know, yet I'd still mark it wrong in a formal news article. (9) "Between you and I, the experiment failed" — wrong; I'd change it to "between you and me." (10) "Irregardless of the result, we'll proceed" — wrong even though I understand the speaker perfectly. (11) "I could care less about the rule" — this also strikes me as a mistake, since it seems to say the opposite of what the speaker means, even though everyone understands it.

I keep wanting to say that some of these are real errors and some are just harmless variation, but I can't say what separates the two groups without making arbitrary-sounding choices.
