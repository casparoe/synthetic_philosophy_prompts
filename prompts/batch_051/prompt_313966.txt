Me again. I'm a software engineer; never took a philosophy class, so be patient with the vocabulary. I keep going back and forth on something and I'd rather get one honest answer than a survey. Here is the thing. There's an argument I've seen in a few places — I think people call it divine hiddenness — that goes roughly like this: if God is perfectly loving, he'd want a relationship with every person capable of it; a person can't have a relationship with someone they don't believe exists; so a loving God wouldn't allow people to remain in the dark. But there are plenty of sincere people who have looked and come up empty. So either God isn't there, or he isn't perfectly loving. That argument has a clean logical structure and it matches my engineering brain: it says the evidence is a spec, and the world fails the test.

But then I read Pascal's Pensées — I know it's old and not exactly a proof — and he has this idea that God deliberately gives just enough light for people who want to find him and enough obscurity for people who dont, because if the evidence were overwhelming it would force belief, and forced belief isn't love or faith. And I find that intuition really hard to shake. It feels true even though I cant formalize it: a parent who wants a genuine relationship with an adult child sometimes hangs back rather than making their presence impossible to deny, because being wanted freely matters more than being known. So I've got a rigorous argument that says hiddenness is a bug, and a messy intuition that says it might be a feature. I dont want you to just tell me which side wins. I want you to tell me what I have to give up to go either way: if the argument is right, why exactly is my parent/child analogy misleading? If the intuition is right, which premise of the argument breaks, and what does that do to the rest of it? Is there a reading where both can be true — where hiddenness is both a real cost and a deliberate feature? And how would I tell the difference between a God who hides for a reason and a God who just isn't there?
