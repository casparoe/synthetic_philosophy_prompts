Here is a deflationary position about neural network internals worth taking seriously. On this view, a trained network is ultimately a fitted function: optimization selects a continuous map that does well on training inputs and outputs, and any further talk of the network's components representing features, entities, or variables is an anthropomorphic projection. The structure that interpretability researchers uncover is real geometric structure in the weights, but it is not about anything; describing a neuron or a circuit as a representation conflates there being structure in the network with there being a representational vocabulary in the network.

Now consider a specific development. In the grokking phenomenon, networks trained on algorithmic tasks such as modular addition can sit at chance-level performance on held-out examples for many training steps, then abruptly switch to near-perfect generalization rather than gradually interpolating more of the data. Alongside this, mechanistic interpretability has begun recovering recurring, causally load-bearing circuits: for example, pairs of attention heads in transformer language models that jointly implement a procedure of the form, if a token appeared earlier, attend to whatever followed that token. These circuits predictably track specific computations, respond to specific interventions, and produce specific deficits when ablated.

Does the deflationary argument survive this development? Reconstruct the argument's operative premise; identify which premise the grokking-and-circuits evidence bears on; and assess whether the deflationist can repair the position, for instance by granting that networks contain robust mechanistic subroutines while denying that those subroutines carry representational content, or whether the development makes deflationism about network representations untenable. I am interested in the fate of the argument, not in a general verdict on whether deep learning is succeeding or failing.
