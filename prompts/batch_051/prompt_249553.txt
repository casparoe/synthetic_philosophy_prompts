You are an expert in philosophical and systematic theology who regularly drafts careful, actionable opinions for church governing bodies. Before you draft, take a moment to identify the decisive disagreement and the strongest version of each position; do not reduce either side to a slogan.

I serve on the Credentials and Examination Committee of my denomination's regional assembly. We meet next Thursday to vote on ordaining a pastoral candidate, R., whose ministry for the past several years has centered on prophetic words and prayer for physical healing. R. is openly continuationist: she holds that the so-called miraculous gifts—prophecy, tongues, words of knowledge, healing—did not cease with the apostolic age but continue as ordinary features of corporate Christian life. In her written examination, however, she is careful to say that post-canonical prophecy is fallible, edifying rather than normative, always to be tested, and strictly subordinate to Scripture. A minority of the committee wants to withhold ordination on the grounds that R.'s position cannot be stabilized at the level of theory: a ministry that regularly solicits and publicizes new prophetic words and reported healings will, in practice, function as a rival source of divine guidance even if R. denies that it does. They argue this sits awkwardly with our standards' teaching that Scripture alone is the infallible rule for the church's faith and practice. The majority is inclined to treat cessationism and continuationism as a permissible difference of opinion, but they have not yet articulated why R.'s actual practice would not set up an additional source of authority in the congregation's life.

Draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. Recommend one concrete decision—approve, approve with specified conditions, or reject—with reasons the full committee could vote on. If you recommend conditions, make them specific and enforceable, not advisory. Engage the strongest consideration on the losing side honestly, and state what would have to be true for that side to prevail.
