I've been reading the Wealth of Nations chapter on colonies, and I have this homemade concept I want checked. Smith seems to argue two things at once: that the American colonies have actually been great for Britain as a market and an outlet for goods, but that the monopoly system makes that trade much less beneficial than it could be. Then he goes further and says Britain should just let the colonies go, that they cost more than they yield. I want to call this 'colonial lock-in': once a state has invested in controlling a territory, the domestic merchants who profit from the monopoly become a political lobby that keeps the arrangement going even when the state as a whole is losing out, and the state mistakes the private gains of that lobby for the public interest. It feels like a public choice explanation before public choice existed. Is that actually what Smith is arguing, or am I reading to much Mancur Olson back into him? And more importantly, does Smith's diagnosis work: if the monopoly profits go to a politically connected few while the costs are diffuse, why would he expect Britain to voluntarily give up the colonies? Maybe he was just being naive about politics. Give me the strongest version of my concept, tell me what it gets right about Smith, and where it breaks.
