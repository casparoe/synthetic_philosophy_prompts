im taking a philosophy of argument course this term, and ive gotten pretty convinced by the idea that adversarial argument plays a filtering role: people don't have to start out unbiased, because as long as they have incentives to attack each other's reasoning, bad arguments get weeded out and what survives is more likely to track the truth. i find that story genuinely attractive—it's general, it explains peer review, cross-examination, and legislative debate in one move, and it doesn't depend on anyone being intellectually virtuous up front. but my roommate is starting an intramural dialectic league where arguments get scored like a sport: you get points for extracting a concession, for leaving an objection unanswered past the timer, for having the audience vote that one of your analogies survived. and i have this strong intuition that this is going to select for bulldozing and slickness rather than reliability—that people will optimize for winning, not for getting things right. so im caught between the theory and the intuition. my question is where the pressure should go. is there a reading of the filter view where it simply doesn't apply to scored competition, because the incentives are different in kind? is my intuition tracking something real, or is it just a tribal reaction to debate-bro culture i find cringe? and what would each move require me to actually show, as opposed to just asserting? i want to avoid special pleading toward whichever side i already liked, because i can already feel myself doing that alot.
