A classicist I interviewed for a piece on why humans ritualize booze walked me into this, and I still cant find the exit. (1) Plato's defense of the supervised drinking party for older citizens is roughly this: wine, by loosening self-control, strips away the social mask and lets a person's underlying character show, so the city can test and even train character cheaply and safely. (2) But if that's what justifies the institution, then any institution that loosens self-control and exposes underlying character is justified by the same logic. (3) Truth drugs, stress interrogations, public shaming rituals—they all fit that description, since they too peel back the performed self and reveal the person underneath. (4) Yet no reasonable person thinks a state should run truth-serum dinners or ritual humiliations as civic education. (5) So either Plato's rationale for wine proves too much, or it wasn't the real rationale. I'd most like to keep (1), because it makes a baffling passage in the Laws look coherent. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
