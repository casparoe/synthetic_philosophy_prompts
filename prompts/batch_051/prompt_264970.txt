I'm a trustee of a small family foundation whose founding charter says we exist to "make our region richer." For years we've mostly dodged what that means by splitting money between poverty relief and economic-development grants, but this spring a one-time gift forces a real choice and the old split won't paper over the disagreement. Two proposals are on the table for the same dollar amount. The first would fund a regional food hub and wholesale market: the projections show it would grow the area's total income, output, and material abundance, but the gains splash around among people and firms already doing fine, and there's no evidnece it pulls anyone out of poverty. The second would send the same money as direct cash transfers to the 400 poorest households in the county, which has strong support in the outcome literature for improving health and school results but does almost nothing to grow the regional economy. The real fight on the board is whether "making the region richer" means building wealth and abundance as something worth wanting for its own sake, or whether wealth only has moral worth through what it buys. I need a short written opinion to circulate before the vote next week: it should recommend a concrete course of action with an amount and a time frame and take the losing side seriously rather than just noting it. Please write it as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion. Make the first item the precise action I should move at the meeting, and make one of the five items state and honestly answer the strongest thing said by the side you're recommending against. Don't give me a both-sides note; actually pick.
