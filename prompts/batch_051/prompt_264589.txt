I have noticed two different ways a thing's tendency can fail to show up, and I would like to know whether the distinction has a recognized name and, more importantly, how it should be drawn. Consider a glass packed in a crush-proof shell: if you strike it with a hammer it does not shatter, but we still call it fragile, because the packaging simply stands in the way of the effect. Now consider a live wire connected to a cut-off that detects contact and switches off the current at the instant something would touch it, so the touch produces no shock. Here we also want to say the wire was live before and after, but at the moment of contact something about the trigger seems to have canceled the very condition that the word live describes. In the first case an external factor blocks the manifestation; in the second the trigger itself seems to remove the tendency before it can manifest. Do these two patterns have names? In particular, is there a recognized distinction between an obstacle that merely screens off the effect and a trigger that temporarily undoes the underlying state? I would also like to know whether the distinction has clear boundary cases and whether it matters for analyzing such tendencies as what would happen under some test condition.
