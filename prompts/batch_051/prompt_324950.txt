Last month I got voted onto the board of my neighbourhood association mostly because I'm the person who complains about the flyer, and now I'm the one writing the monthly update. I keep catching myself writing things like "the board is currently reviewing options with respect to the proposed maintenance schedule" when the true sentence is "we still haven't decided when to fix the roof." Its made me go back to Orwell's idea that political language hands you a box of ready-made phrases that do the thinking for you, and I want to run a one-hour session on it for the other board members, who are not readers and mostly think I'm being picky about grammar. I need a plan for how to teach it, not the explanation itself: what has to be understood first and what it would be a mistake to start with, the one concrete example that should carry the whole hour and what it needs to have (and not have), the misconceptions they'll arrive with and form along the way--probably "this is just about shorter sentences" and "so we should ban jargon"--with the exact point in the hour where each should be headed off, and a single question I can ask at the end to tell whether they got the Orwellian point rather than just felt scolded. Also flag anything I should openly leave out at this level. Put this as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion.
