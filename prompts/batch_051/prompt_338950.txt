ok so I've been asked to write up a one-page position sheet on normative reasons for a grad seminar, and before I send it around I want to know whether the whole thing actually hangs together. Here's where I've ended up:

(1) I'm an internalist about reasons in roughly Bernard Williams' sense: a consideration is a reason for an agent only if there's a sound deliberative route from that agent's actual motivations to acting on it. If nothing in her current motivational set would get her to the action through good reasoning and full information, then it isn't a reason for her.

(2) I think reasons are facts, not mental states. The fact that the bridge is unsafe is a reason not to cross it even if you don't know it's unsafe; your belief that it's safe isn't itself a reason.

(3) I think a psychopath who is entirely indifferent to other people's suffering, and who has no motive that reflection would connect to refraining, still has a reason not to torture someone for fun.

(4) I think a severely depressed person who has lost all desire to get better still has a reason to take her antidepressants.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which are only apparantly in tension. For the real tensions, rank them by severity and say what the cheapest fix is for each, and what that fix costs. Then tell me which single revision would make the whole set hang together best, and be specific about which one has to go.
