I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about how thought experiments age. Take Searle's Chinese Room and make one change: the man never holds the whole rulebook at once. The program's steps are split across forty thousand file cards, each one just a conditional — if the current mark is such-and-such and you're holding card n, write this other mark and pull card m. A mechanical index and a scratchpad do the rest, and the man works through the conversation the way a relay computer would. Collectively the cards, index, and man implement exactly the same input-output function as the original room: same Chinese questions in, same fluent answers out. But no single physical object, and no single human, ever runs or sees the whole program at once, and the man cant see more than one card at a time.

Now apply the systems reply: understanding belongs to the whole system, not the man. Here the whole system is a swarm of inter-referential index cards and a monolingual clerk. Does the reply still happily attribute understanding to it? If yes, what stops you from attributing understanding to a very large library catalog plus a patient librarian, once the catalog is clever enough? If no, say exactly which property of the original room did the philosophical work, and at what point in the splitting-up it leaked out. Start with your bottom line, then argue. 300 words max.
