A popular article I read summarizes the philosophical upshot of quantum particle indistinguishability like this: "Elementary particles of the same species have exactly the same intrinsic properties, and in quantum mechanics swapping two of them leaves the physical state entirely unchanged. So there is no property at all by which one particle could be told apart from the other, and by Leibniz's principle of the identity of indiscernibles the two particles cannot be distinct individuals — the question of which one is which is meaningless. Quantum mechanics therefore shows that the classical idea of individual substances with their own identities breaks down at the fundamental level." I suspect the opening claims are roughly right but that the leap from "swapping changes nothing" to "there are no individuals" is too quick. Is this summary fair? Go through it sentence by sentence and separate what is accurate, what is a simplification, what a philosopher of physics would regard as wrong or contested, and whether the final claim about the breakdown of individuality follows from the accurate parts.
