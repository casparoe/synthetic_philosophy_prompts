Ok so after a whole evening of arguing with a friend about whether i and -i are actually different numbers or just two names for the same empty slot in x^2 + 1 = 0, I keep coming back to a pattern I have no word for. You've got two objects that the whole system treats as interchangeable—everything true of one seems true of the other once you swap them, and no arithmetic test can tell which one is which. But the moment you actually do math you have to pick one: you call it "the" root, you build the bigger field around it or plot it, and then all the later formulas quietly inherit that choice. And yet people who work on this stuff treat the swap itself as a real, nontrivial move, which seems weird if the two things are genuinely indistinguishable. Is there an established name for this situation, and does the arbitrary label actually do any mathematical work or is it pure notation? More than the name, I want the boundary: at what point does a "mere" choice of labels start sneaking real structure into the problem, and does it make sense to ask which root really deserves to be called i? Please keep clear which parts of this are settled and which are still fought over, and dont present one side as the consensus.
