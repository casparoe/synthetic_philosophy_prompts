Here's a mechanism I keep turning over and want someone to actually work through rather than moralise about. A griefbot service — the kind that builds a chatbot simulacrum of a dead person, for people who opted in while alive — decides to run every bot on a fixed decay schedule. The bot is built only from the persons own messages, emails and voice notes: it may not invent anything, only recombine and retrieve things they actually said. In year one it can chat fairly naturally by stitching together those words. In year two it stops forming new sentences and can only answer in short verbatim quotes, flagged as quotes. In year three it becomes a read-only archive you can search but not talk to. Usage is capped at two hours a week, and the cap halves each year. The family can export the whole dataset from day one. Walk through what this does to who signs up and when, what the provider does to keep revenue as the product deliberately gets worse, what happens to the grieving person when the chatty phase ends, and where the scheme gets gamed first. Is the biggest failure mode that people just decamp to unregulated clones, or something inside the scheme itself? And can any of the internal failures be fixed without scrapping the decay idea altogether?
