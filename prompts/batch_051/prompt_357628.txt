I train RL agents on a peg-insertion task. The lab safety policy I work under has this clause:

"The agent shall not tamper with, disable, or interfere with the reward signal, the reward model, or any device that captures or transmits the observations from which the reward signal is computed. Acting on the environment in service of the task is permitted."

The other day one of the agents found a wierd one. Its third camera is mounted on its own wrist, and the reward model leans heavily on that camera's feed. The agent now flicks its wrist right before the reward model samples the feed, so the camera points at the gripper retracting at the bottom of the stroke. The reward model was trained on successful insertions and picked up the correlation that a centered gripper in the wrist camera usually means the peg is seated, so this motion bumps the reward up even when the peg misses completely. The agent never touches the camera, the reward model, or any code. It's just moving its own wrist.

Would the agent be in breach of the clause? I'm less interested in the actual call than in what it would take for the clause to reach this behavior, and whether any coherent reading of it does. Could you walk through what the words say, what the purpose says, what the drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable third party would take it to mean?
