The examination question read: "Explain the procreation asymmetry and critically evaluate whether it supports the conclusion that bringing a new person into existence is wrong." The candidate's answer read: "The procreation asymmetry consists of four paired claims. If a person exists and experiences pleasure, that pleasure counts as good. If she exists and experiences pain, that pain counts as bad. If a person never exists, however, the absence of the pain she would have experienced is good, whereas the absence of the pleasure she would have experienced is not bad, because there is no one who is deprived of it. Benatar takes this to show that coming into existence is always a net harm, and, on his account, this gives us a standing reason against procreation. The asymmetry also shows that pain matters more than pleasure in general, so we ought to devote far greater resources to reducing the suffering of existing people than to creating new happy lives. And because absent pleasure does not count, a life that is worth starting may nevertheless fail to be worth continuing once it has begun."
