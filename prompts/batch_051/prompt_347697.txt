I've come round to the hinge of history hypothesis — the loose version, not a worked-out theory: that the next few centuries are disproportionately high-leverage for the long-run future, such that choices made by the next few generations has an unusually large expected impact compared with most centuries in the past. I know that's vague, but I want to treat it as a claim that could in principle be wrong.

Tell me what would count against it. Not the lazy points about it sounding self-important or every age thinking it's special — though I do want to know how you'd distinguish a genuine hinge from that. I mean the structural defeaters. Suppose somebody tried to test the hinge claim in earnest. What observations, historical patterns, formal arguments, or developments should lower my confidence? What does the hinge hypothesis predict about the world that its rivals don't? A world where hinges are real should presumably look different to a world where every age merely feels pivotal from the inside — what, concretely, would the difference be? And what would a check on earlier centuries find: could a literate observer in, say, the ninth century or the sixteenth have asserted a hinge thesis on the same evidence I've got, and how would we tell whether they were right? I'm also interested in selection effects — how much of my sense of exceptionalism is survivorship at the level of whole civilisations, and whether there's a base-rate argument a defender would have to accept rather than dismiss.

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which are live rather than merely logically possible. Mark which of them a committed hinge theorist would concede counts against the view, not just what it's critics say. Then be honest about whether the hypothesis has been insulated — e.g. by adding that hinges are rare, unpredictable, or visible only in retrospect — and whether that insulates it into unfalsifiability. If it has, is that a problem for a claim that's doing real work in present-day prioritisation? The thing that would move me most is a worked example of an earlier age that had the same subjective experience of standing at a hinge and turned out to be just another century. If you can supply that, or show why it can't be supplied, I'd take it seriously.
