I keep seeing people argue about complicity by pointing at one case — the getaway driver, the guard at a camp — and never state what a theory of complicity is actually supposed to do. So give me the full spec: exactly five requirements an adequate theory of complicity has to meet, each with the case or distinction that motivates it. Make sure the list covers the redundant accomplice whose act made no causal difference, the line between enabling wrongdoing and merely being present while it happens, and the difference between making a wrong more likely and making it more acceptable to do. For each requirement, say whether you'd treat it as negotiable, and then say which single requirement knocks out the most familiar ways of thinking about complicity. Give it as a numbered list of five items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion.
