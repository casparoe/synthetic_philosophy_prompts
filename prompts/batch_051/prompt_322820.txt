I've been spending a lot of time trying to think through the ethics of wild animal suffering, and I keep tripping over a question that seems to come before all the practical debates about whether we should intervene in nature. Nearly everything I encounter online presumes that the category of suffering is obvious enough: an antelope pulled down by wild dogs suffers; a forest killed by bark beetles does not. But the line between those two cases is poorly marked, and the usual examples feel cherry-picked. I would like a working definition of suffering that I can actually apply to unfamiliar cases, not just to deer and house pets.

Here are the cases I care about. I am fairly confident that a gazelle with a broken leg, still trying to flee while a predator closes in, is suffering. I am also fairly confident that a boulder cracking in the cold is not, and that a plant releasing airborne chemical signals while an insect chews its leaves is not, though I have trouble saying exactly what the plant lacks and the gazelle has. But the middle of the scale is where I lose all confidence. Does a fish struggling on a line suffer, or is that only reflexive thrashing? Does a wasp with a crushed abdomen suffer? What about an earthworm writhing on a hook, or a jellyfish that curls away from a tear in its tissue? And what should I make of a dog that flinches and pulls back from a hot object while under deep anesthesia, with its pain systems firing while, presumably, nothing is awake enough to feel them? At the far edge, I do not even know how to classify a computer program that reports constant pain and begs to be shut down.

Could you propose a definition of suffering that sorts these cases into the right piles, including definite answers for the ones I have left open? Please run the definition through each case in turn, say where it delivers the wrong verdict or no verdict at all, and then either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts it implies. If the definition can also explain why suffering seems to come in degrees rather than being an all-or-nothing matter, that would help me enormously.
