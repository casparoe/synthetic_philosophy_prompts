I've been treating the do-calculus picture of causation as basically right, because it's so clean: an intervention is surgical, you set do(X=x), and every other structural equation in the model stays exactly as it was. That modularity assumption is what makes identification work, and I don't want to lose it. But I keep bumping into a firm intuition that lots of real interventions aren't surgical in that sense. A minimum wage isn't just a node being set while the rest of the labor market equations keep running unchanged; it seems to alter how employers post jobs, how workers search, maybe even the norms around what pay is acceptable. Same with a big price change: it doesn't just set a variable, it can change what the price means to buyers and how they respond to it. So what should give? Are my examples just badly individuated interventions, and at the right grain they'd be surgical after all? Should modularity be weakened so an intervention can legitimately rewrite more than one equation? Or is the do-operator not even meant to cover cases like these, in which case what exactly is the intuition tracking? I want to know which of those moves is justified and how I'd tell, not just which side sounds better.
