I spent my career in physical chemistry, so the philosophy side is a retirement project, but lately I got pulled into debates about "pre-AGI bargains" — the idea that people could strike credible deals now about what happens after artificial general intelligence is built. I've seen a concrete proposal floating around, and I'd like someone to work through it as a mechanism rather than tell me whether it's a good idea.

The proposal is a Covenant Registry. Any person, lab, company, or government can deposit money now into an escrow contract and attach a public, machine-readable pledge of the form "if my organization comes to control an AGI-level system, I will [do X]." The escrow is returned with interest if no AGI-level system appears before a cuttoff date. If one does appear, a panel of registered signatories — selected by lot but weighted by the size of their escrow — decides for each pledge whether it was honored. A breach finding forfeits the breacher's escrow, which is distributed pro rata among the other signatories, and the finding is published. Non-signatories get nothing. You can update your pledge any time before AGI by posting new escrow, but the old pledge stays public. There's no cap on the number of pledges.

What I want to know is how this actually plays out. Who joins and who conspicuously doesn't? What kinds of promises get registered, and do they track real intentions at all? How is it gamed — tiny escrows to buy panel weight, fake pledges for reputation, pledges that are technically honored while doing nothing? What does the fact that whoever controls AGI can probably ignore the panel and make the escrowed money irrelevent do to the enforcement story? And which of the failure modes can be fixed without turning the thing into something else entirely?
