I recently retired from geochemistry and fell into epistemology, and one thing I keep noticing is that the interesting disagreements aren't about whether someone calls themselves a fallibilist or whatever — they're about what people say about lottery tickets and brains in vats. For the reading group I run, I want a "which theory of knowledge do you actually hold?" quiz that sorts people by their verdicts on cases, not by which label they like. The positions it should sort between are: Nozick-style sensitivity/tracking, a safety view (Sosa-ish), a closure-preserving fallibilist "ordinary knowledge" view, and a skeptical invariantism that takes the BIV argument seriously. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key. Use these cases or close variants: a one-in-a-million lottery ticket; the zebra that could be a cleverly disguised mule; "I have hands" vs the brain-in-a-vat possibility; fake barn country; a stopped clock that happens to read the right time; the grandmother case where relatives would hide her illness; and at least one or two more of your own that separate sensitivity from safety. Before writing the questions, lay out the logical space — which combinations of answers are coherent, which are empty, and whether they're empty because incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Flag any pair of positions the quiz cant cleanly seperate (I suspect safety and closure-based fallibilism will blur together, and that's fine to say). No question should just ask readers to pick a theory or name.
