I work as a software engineer and have no formal background in philosophy, so I will frame this as a mechanism to be analyzed rather than as a position to defend. A community adopts the following rule as its operationalization of the sola scriptura principle, roughly the idea that a canonical written document is the sole authority and that no human interpreter may settle what the document means. The document's text is treated as the only valid source of norms for the community. When a dispute arises about what the document requires, any member may submit a proposed reading together with the passages they cite as support. That proposal is registered as one of the community's accepted readings unless, within 30 days, another member files a counter-derivation and the platform validates it. A counter-derivation must be built entirely from direct quotations of the document and must state an explicit contradiction with the proposed reading. Validation is mechanical: the platform checks that the quoted passages really occur in the document and that the contradiction is stated, but it does not judge whether the quoted passages actually entail the stated contradiction, whether the supporting passages actually support the original reading, or whether a quoted sentence has more than one admissible reading. If no counter-derivation is validated within the window, the proposed reading is accepted. Conflicting accepted readings are published with equal status, and any officer of the community may act on whichever accepted reading they judge best; there is no procedure for choosing among them.

Treat this rule as a mechanism, not as a theological position, and analyze how it would actually behave. Cover at least: what the rule incentivizes among members who want particular outcomes; how members could game the proposal, the counter-derivation, and the publication process; where the line actually falls between the text-alone principle and the unacknowledged interpretive decisions made by whoever writes the code, chooses the canonical text, maintains the quote checker, or displays the rulings; who ends up deciding what the text means in practice and through which channel; and whether amending the procedure would fix the failures or merely relocate the interpreter while preserving the letter of the rule.
