Just had a long argument with my friend about climate policy and it went exactly nowhere, again. We both agree emissions have to fall fast, and we both agree carbon taxes have mostly been politically toxic in practice. But she's gone full degrowth—she says green growth is a fantasy because of rebound, Jevons, and the tight link between GDP and material/energy throughput. I kept saying the alternative to pricing carbon is command-and-control, which is slower and easier for industry to capture, and degrowth is politically dead on arrival anywhere that matters. She says a carbon tax is just a license to keep growing. I say she's smuggling in the value judgment that growth is bad in itself and dressing it up as an empirical claim; she says I'm doing the same with the claim that growth is necessary. My working theory is that under the policy talk we're really disagreeing about whether growth is something to manage or something to eliminate, and that's a value question neither of us knows how to argue about. What kind of disagreement is this actually—a factual one about decoupling and rebound magnitudes, a values one about growth, or a disagreement about what counts as realistic policy? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress? Be honest about how confident you are in the diagnosis and single out the point you're least sure of.
