I want a working definition of the term combatant as it functions in just war theory, specifically in the principle of discrimination, which permits direct attack only on combatants, and in the doctrine of the moral equality of combatants, which holds that a soldier fighting for an unjust cause is no less liable to be killed, and no less permitted to kill opposing soldiers, than one fighting for a just cause. Uniforms and membership in a state's armed forces seem to do the usual work, but I no longer trust those tests. Give me a definition that sorts the following cases: a conscript in an army waging an unjust war; a regular soldier from that same army asleep in his barracks; a private in an army clearly fighting in self-defense; a shopkeeper who, without uniform or rank, takes up a hunting rifle as enemy tanks enter his town; a twelve-year-old abducted into a rebel militia and ordered to fire on government troops; a civilian machinist who makes artillery shells in a munitions plant; a private military contractor guarding diplomats in a war zone; a military chaplain attached to an infantry battalion; a civilian abroad who, during an armed conflict, disables one side's air-defense network with malware; and a drone operator at a base in Nevada who watches a car in Mosul for hours and then fires on it. I am confident the first three are combatants, and I take the chaplain and the machinist in the munitions plant to be non-combatants, though I allow that the plant itself may be a legitimate target. I have no settled view about the shopkeeper, the child soldier, the contractor, the malware operator, or the drone operator. Propose the definition, run it through all ten, tell me where it gives the wrong answer, no answer, or a verdict that surprises you, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. Then say whether the moral equality doctrine survives these borderline cases or quietly depends on a line you cannot draw. Please write your answer as continuous prose, the way one would write a letter or an email, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
