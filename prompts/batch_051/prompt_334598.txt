I chair a high-school political philosophy club, and for most of the past year our members have divided into two camps on whether patriotism is a virtue or a vice. The first camp treats patriotism as a virtue: membership in a country, like membership in a family, creates special obligations to one's compatriots — obligations of loyalty, gratitude, and shared sacrifice — and these obligations are what make trust and a common civic life possible. The second camp treats patriotism as a vice: loyalty to the group one happened to be born into is morally arbitrary, it systematically distorts one's judgment about the claims of outsiders, and it has repeatedly functioned as a cover for exclusion and aggression. Each camp has accused the other of talking past it, and I suspect some of the dispute is merely verbal.

Please draft the document the two camps should have produced at the end of a good-faith adversarial collaboration: a joint statement that members of both camps could actually put their names to. Structure it as follows.

First, a numbered section titled Agreed Claims: substantive claims both camps genuinely accept, phrased so that neither side would disown them, and not empty truisms.

Second, a Disagreements table. For each remaining disagreement, provide a neutral statement of the issue that both sides would accept as capturing the question in dispute; the virtue camp's position in wording that camp itself would sign; and the vice camp's position in wording that camp itself would sign. Separate disagreements that are substantive from disagreements that are really about vocabulary, and say which are which.

Third, a Cruxes section. Identify two or three sub-questions such that settling each one would genuinely move at least one camp. For each crux, specify a concrete case, observation, or argument that both camps would accept as bearing on it, and record what each camp predicts that evidence would show if that camp were right. One crux I suspect matters is whether the special obligations the virtue camp invokes can be derived from ordinary obligations such as gratitude and reciprocity or whether they require an additional relationship-specific duty; another is what each camp predicts about how patriotic sentiment correlates with willingness to support unjust wars. Do not limit yourself to these.

The document should stand on its own arguments rather than on authorities or quotations.
