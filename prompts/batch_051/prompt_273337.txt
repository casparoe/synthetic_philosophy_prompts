I run a small link-aggregator site (a tiny, sad reddit, basically) and I'm trying to work out what our own terms of service actually commit us to enforcing. The clause, which I copy-pasted out of a template years ago and never thought about again, reads:

"You may not post content that is unlawful in the country where it is stored or hosted."

Here's the case bugging me. We're a US company, origin server in Virginia. A user in Berlin posts something that's completely legal in the US and completely illegal in Germany — the specific content doesn't matter, pick whatever Germany bans that the First Amendment would protect; the category is bigger than you'd think. The post lives on our box in Virginia. But we're behind a CDN, and the CDN decides to cache that page on an edge node in Frankfurt. A reader in Stuttgart requests the page and pulls the bytes from Frankfurt, not Virginia. Nobody chose any of this: the caching is automatic, the cache might get evicted an hour later, and the user likely has no idea it happened. But it is literally sitting on a disk in Germany, and Germany says that's a problem.

So is the post "stored or hosted" in Germany? It's on a German disk, but it's also on a Virginia disk, and tomorrow it might be Virginia-only again. I'm less interested in the practical lawyer answer than in the different readings: the plain words, the purpose the template author probably had, what the template author would of said if you'd asked them about CDNs (probably "what's a CDN?"), and what a reasonable user takes "stored or hosted" to mean. Walk me through each reading and say which one we should treat as binding — for the user and for us. Also: does "hosting" mean bytes on a disk anywhere, or does it need something more sustained or intentional than an edge cache? The point of the clause was presumably to keep us legal where we are; has it accidentally turned us into an enforcer of every country's law that happens to have one of our caches? If the words and the purpose come apart here, which should win, and why?
