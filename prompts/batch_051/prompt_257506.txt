Im on the trust and safety advisory board for a mid-size platform that hosts mostly long-form essays and podcast audio, and we keep getting cases where a creator hasnt broken any rule exactly but their presence is driving real off-platform harm. The executive team wants a reusable policy for when social sanction—downranking, demonetization, removal from recommendations, deplatforming—is justified. That seems to run into Mill. From John Stuart Mill, On Liberty, Chapter IV: "The acts of an individual may be hurtful to others, or wanting in due consideration for their welfare, without going the length of violating any of their constituted rights. The offender may then be justly punished by opinion, though not by law. As soon as any part of a person’s conduct affects prejudicially the interests of others, society has jurisdiction over it, and the question whether the general welfare will or will not be promoted by interfering with it, becomes open to discussion." Write me a decision procedure we can actually run in a weekly policy meeting for when to apply social sanction to a creator or a piece of content. It should say what counts as affecting others prejudicially in a platform context, where the procedure stops with no action, and where it hands the decision to a person because its a values call. Then run it on three hypotheticals: a former academic whose long essays argue that a marginalized group is biologically less rational; a podcaster who never harasses anyone directly but whose fans flood a small nonprofit's voicemail after each episode; and a user who posts embarrassing but true screenshots from another user's locked account. One of these should come out in a way my board wont like. And please keep the whole thing in American English.
