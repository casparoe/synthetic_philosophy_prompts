I'm not any kind of philosopher, just someone who follows the news too closely. For years I've noticed a thing that keeps happening, mostly in politics but also sometimes in hospitals and families, and I've never had a word for it. A person has to do something that is, by any normal standard, wrong -- lying to the public, ordering a strike that will kill someone, breaking a promise -- because if they don't do it, something much worse will happen, like more people die or the whole arrangement collapses. And the strange part is, afterwards we cant quite say they made a mistake, but we also don't want them to just shrug it off and feel fine. Actually if they sleep easily afterwards, we trust them less, not more. So we seem to want them to do it and also want it to cost them something, and the cost isn't only public backlash -- its more like the act leaves a mark on them even when it was the best thing available. Is there a name for this situation? More than a name, I want to get the outline right: how is it different from just 'choosing the lesser evil,' and from a plain dilemma where both choices are so bad that you honestly can't say which is right? Does the person end up with real guilt, and if they don't feel any, does the situation stop being what I'm describing or is it still there? If there is no established name, what would you call it, and what would you put just outside the edge of the word?
