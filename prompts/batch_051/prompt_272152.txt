I chair the weekly Capacity and Access Panel at a mid-sized acute-care hospital. Each week the panel makes eight to twelve decisions about requests for constrained resources: operating-theatre blocks, ICU and step-down beds, high-cost medicines with limited supply, and inpatient rehabilitation places. Each request must be decided and documented in twenty to thirty minutes. The panel includes experienced clinicians, a finance officer, and a patient and family representative, but no one with formal training in philosophy or health economics. Clinicians and families can appeal our decisions, and our documentation must show that the panel is following a consistent and defensible procedure rather than a week-by-week judgment call.

The board has instructed us to replace our current case-by-case practice with a reusable procedure grounded in two ideas our quality team keeps invoking: quality-adjusted life years and the value of a statistical life. Please write that procedure for us.

We currently split on several recurrent issues, and the procedure will need to handle them explicitly: whether a small chance of a large benefit should beat a near-certain moderate benefit, whether younger patients should receive any priority, and how to treat patients whose chronic disability lowers their baseline quality of life. The procedure should be operational rather than theoretical. Please provide a fixed sequence of tests or steps to run in order, with the information to record at each step; a way for non-specialists to reason with QALYs and statistical-life reasoning without pretending the numbers are precise; and the distinctions from the underlying theory that genuinely have to survive translation into ordinary clinical language. Please include worked applications to three contrasting requests, at least one of which leads to an outcome the panel will find difficult to accept. Be explicit about the cases the procedure cannot settle, where the panel must exercise judgment, and about the most likely ways the procedure would misfire if applied mechanically. We need an operating document, not a bibliography or a set of citations.
