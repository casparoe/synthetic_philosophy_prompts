Consider the following familiar anti-reductionist argument in the philosophy of science. For a higher-level kind to reduce to a lower level, instances of it would have to share a lower-level property, or cluster of properties, with which the kind could be identified. But many paradigmatic higher-level kinds are multiply realizable: believing, being in pain, being a recession, and being a predator are realized by such heterogeneous physical configurations that there is no unified lower-level description of them. From this the argument concludes that the special sciences cannot in principle be reduced and that their kinds are autonomous.

Large trained neural networks now appear to do something the argument did not anticipate. A single network with finitely many parameters takes raw low-level inputs — pixels, audio samples, word tokens — and reliably classifies or generates instances of exactly the kinds of categories the argument treats as multiply realized: faces, words, objects, emotional states. These systems are finite, compact, and driven by low-level inputs, yet they track categories the argument said no compact lower-level description could capture.

Does this development refute the multiple-realizability argument, force a repair to it, or leave it untouched? Argue for one of these three verdicts rather than surveying them. Be precise about which premise of the original argument the deep-learning case actually bears on, and say whether the original conclusion can be restated in a form that survives. Also indicate where your reasoning is most likely to fail. Finally, say what would change your mind: what result about these systems, or what account of reduction or explanation, would move you from your verdict to one of the other two?
