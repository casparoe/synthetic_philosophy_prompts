I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, so I've been thinking about mind uploading and gradual replacement in systems terms, and I've arrived at a principle I want checked rather than politely ignored. I call it the Runtime Continuity Criterion. It says that what makes a later mind the same person as an earlier mind is not the information content of the mind but whether the later mind is a causal continuation of the same running process. A transfer from one substrate to another preserves identity just in case it is done the way one migrates a live service: the new substrate is brought up alongside the old one, state is handed over incrementally, and there is no instant at which the computation as a whole is halted, described, and restarted from the description. If the procedure is snapshot-and-restore — record a complete state, halt the original, and later instantiate the saved state elsewhere — then the result is a replica and the original has died, even if the replica is behaviorally indistinguishable and even if the destruction of the original happens to come after the copy. Gradual replacement of neurons passes the criterion, because the process never fully halts; destructive uploading fails it, because between halt and restart there is a gap in which no ongoing causal activity binds the later system to the earlier one.

The part I can't settle myself is what counts as a fatal gap. If a person is cooled until neural activity stops and is later revived from the same substrate, my criterion seems to say they died and were replaced, which strikes me as wrong. If someone undergoes general anesthesia, their consciousness lapses but their brain keeps running; I assume that is fine, but then the criterion must say whether the relevant "process" is consciousness or underlying causal activity, and I don't know which choice is defensible. Give me an honest assessment: is the criterion coherent as stated? Is it already a standard position under another name? What are the three strongest objections, and is any of them fatal rather than merely uncomfortable?
