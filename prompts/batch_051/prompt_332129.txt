I keep hearing a distinction made between rumination and self-examination, but the boundary rarely survives hard cases. The word "rumination" seems to me to hover between naming a kind of attention — repetitive, past-directed, self-focused — and passing a verdict on its outcome — the thinking went nowhere or made things worse. The practical question I care about is when self-examination becomes harmful, but I want the definition to carry that weight rather than presuppose it. I want a working definition of rumination — not a list of symptoms, but a definition — tested against the seven cases below.

1. A chess player spends an hour after a loss reconstructing the game and can say in terms where the position turned; the analysis ends with a corrected line of play.
2. A person cannot stop rerunning a single regretted sentence from a conversation, at bedtime and on waking, for two weeks; it returns unbidden.
3. A patient in cognitive therapy was taught to fill in thought records and now does so fifteen or twenty times a day, mechanically, with diminishing belief that the exercise is doing anything.
4. A widow keeps a detailed journal every morning about the person she lost; after several months she feels worse and cannot say what the journaling is meant to achieve.
5. A meditator sits for half an hour noticing thoughts arise and pass, labeling each "thinking" without following it out.
6. Before a difficult conversation, a person walks through the likely turns of the exchange and settles on exact formulations to avoid repeating a previous misstep.
7. A depressed person pores over old messages to confirm the thought that they ruin every relationship, selecting only what fits that conclusion.

I am confident that 1 and 6 are not rumination and that 2 and 7 are; I have no settled view about 3, 4, and 5. State the definition, run it through all seven cases in order, flag any disagreements with my confident verdicts, and tell me how it sorts 3, 4, and 5. Then identify the case the definition handles worst, and say whether that is a reason to revise the definition or to revise my verdict in that case.
