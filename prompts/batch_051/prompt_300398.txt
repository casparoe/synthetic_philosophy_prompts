I am chairing an upcoming workshop session on consciousness in computational systems, and I have been asked to interview a philosopher who thinks the access–phenomenal distinction still does real work against global workspace theories. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond restating the distinction. At least one question should turn on the passage in Ned Block’s “On a Confusion about a Function of Consciousness” where he motivates the distinction by observing that a state can be access-conscious—available for reasoning and rational control of speech and action—without there being anything it is like to be in it, and that arguments about the function of consciousness often slide between the two notions. For that question, include a follow-up that anticipates his most likely reply. Also include questions on whether global workspace models are best read as theories of access consciousness only, on whether the distinction leaves us with two explanatory targets rather than one, and on whether attributions of phenomenal consciousness in nonhuman systems can ever be tested. Please write the questions in American English.
