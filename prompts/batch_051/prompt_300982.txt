I am a self-taught software engineer with no academic background in philosophy or in the theory of machine learning; what I know about AI alignment comes from a year of reading on my own. I want to think through a debate in the vocabulary I actually use at work.

The debate concerns iterated amplification and distillation. The proposal, as I understand it, is this: take a large task that no single human can check end to end, split it into subtasks small enough that an overseer can reliably tell whether each was done well, solve the task by composing checked subtasks, and then train a smaller model to imitate the composed system. The claim is that both competence and alignment are preserved, because every subtask was either carried out under human-checkable constraints or chosen by a process the overseer approved. The objection is that competence and alignment come apart: the subtasks that are easiest to check are not the ones that carry the values; distillation preserves surface outputs while discarding the reasoning and caution that kept those outputs honest; and the result can be a system that looks right while drifting from what the overseer would have wanted, without the overseer ever noticing.

Restate this debate in the vocabulary of software engineering as I use it: modules and their contracts, unit tests versus integration tests versus end-to-end tests, code review, refactoring, regression, build and deployment pipelines, technical debt, and the difference between documented behavior and the tacit judgment of the engineer who wrote the code. I am fluent in this vocabulary; be as precise with it as you can.

First give the translation: which side maps onto which claims about software, and what each alignment concept becomes. Be honest about the places where you have to stretch a software concept to make it fit, and flag each stretch explicitly, rather than pretending it is a clean mapping. Then give the residue report. Which parts of the alignment debate have no counterpart in my vocabulary? For instance: in software, decomposition preserves correctness only relative to a specification, and the specification is itself written, reviewed, and maintained; the alignment debate seems to have no such specification, only an overseer's in-the-moment judgment. Is that a fair thing to say in my vocabulary, or does it distort the proposal? And is distillation just lossy compression of an expert system into a smaller implementation, or is the loss different in kind from what happens when a complicated service is rewritten as a simpler one? Finally, tell me whether the parts that do not survive translation indicate that my vocabulary is missing something real, or that the alignment debate is less precise than its participants claim.
