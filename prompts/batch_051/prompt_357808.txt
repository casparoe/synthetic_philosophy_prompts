Ok so I keep coming back to this thing in Rousseau that I half remember. The way I understood it, he said that when people in a small republic actually argue with each other in person, the result isnt just a pile of private preferences. The talking itself can change what they want, and out of it comes something he called the general will, which is supposed to be what the whole community would want if everyone were aiming at the common good rather than their own advantage. And he was careful to distinguish that from the will of all, which is just everybodys private wishes added up.

Now heres the thing. His whole argument seems to lean on face to face debate: you have to sit in the same room, hear the other side, and maybe get talked out of your first impulse. Say we build an app that lets every citizen vote yes or no on every law in a few seconds. No meeting, no having to give reasons, and before you vote the feed shows you arguments it already knows you'll like. The app still counts everyones voice, and the result gets announced as the will of the people. But the deliberating part is gone.

Does Rousseau's distinction between the general will and the will of all survive that setup? Or does the app just produce the will of all every time while dressing it up as the general will? And if it dies, is there something that would fix it, like forcing people to listen to the other side first or making a small randomly chosen group deliberate before the vote? Or was the whole idea only workable in a face to face republic, so we should just drop it?
