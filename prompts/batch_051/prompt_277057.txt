I am drafting a view about weakness of will, and before I try to build a positive account out of the commitments I keep reaching for, I would like an audit of whether they can all be held together. Here is the set, stated as plainly as I can.

(1) Akrasia is real. A competent adult can act intentionally against their own all-things-considered judgment about what to do, and this is not the same as compulsion, ignorance, or a motor tic. The action remains something they did and could have refrained from doing.

(2) Intentional action always proceeds under the guise of the good: whenever someone acts intentionally, they represent the action as good in at least some respect, and that representation is part of what motivates it.

(3) The best descriptive account of the standard failures of self-control — the dieter who caves, the procrastinator, the smoker who has supposedly quit — is hyperbolic discounting. As the smaller-sooner reward comes close, its motivational weight inflates out of proportion to any shift in the agent's long-term assessment, so the agent's occurrent preference reverses while their reflective judgment of the options does not change.

(4) I am drawn to an intrapersonal reading of these reversals. Weakness of will is best located in a conflict between earlier and later temporal parts of the same person: the earlier part sides with the larger-later good, and the later part, under proximity-inflated discounting, sides with the smaller-sooner good.

(5) But I also want to insist that when the lapse actually occurs, it is the whole person, not a temporal part, who acts and is answerable. Intrapersonal description should not become an excuse.

(6) The reversal is a failure. The later preference is not simply a legitimate change of values, and the lapse is not just a miscalculation; the agent is weak in the pejorative sense.

(7) Precommitment devices — removing the dessert from the house, installing an app blocker — are, when adopted in advance, paradigms of rational self-governance rather than restrictions on the agent's freedom.

I would like you to work through this set and tell me whether it hangs together. For each pair or cluster in genuine tension, rank the tension by severity and say what would have to be revised to remove it. For each pair that only appears to conflict, say what distinction dissolves the appearance of conflict. Then recommend the single revision that would let me hold onto the largest part of the view at the lowest cost.
