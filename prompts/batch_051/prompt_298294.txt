I help run a philosophy club at my high school, and we have been stuck for several meetings on whether nihilism is livable because different members use the word to mean different things. We have finally separated three claims: moral nihilism, the view that nothing is genuinely right or wrong; existential nihilism, the view that a human life has no point or purpose given to it from outside; and mereological nihilism, the view that there are no composite objects, so that a table is just some simple things arranged as we would ordinarily take a table to be arranged rather than an additional object made out of them. Our working question is whether one person could consistently accept all three claims and still live a decent, recognizable human life rather than sliding into passivity or despair.

I would like you to write out the list of desiderata that an account must meet to count as an adequate defense of the claim that all three forms of nihilism are true and livable. For each requirement, give the consideration or case that motivates it, and say whether it is non-negotiable or only the preference of a particular camp. For example, some members of the club require that the account preserve most ordinary moral language roughly unchanged, while others would accept an error theory on which moral statements are systematically false but still useful for coordinating behavior; some think the account must explain how someone who believes life has no external point can still have a reason to get out of bed, while others think that asks the nihilist to answer a question she has already dismissed. I also want to know whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, and which single requirement does the most to rule out candidate accounts. Do not spend much time scoring existing named positions against the list; our advisor will do that with us later, and we are more interested in whether the list itself is coherent. Citations and a reading list are not needed for our discussion. End your response with a single line beginning "Summary:" that states in one sentence which requirement does the most to exclude candidate accounts and whether you take that requirement to be negotiable.
