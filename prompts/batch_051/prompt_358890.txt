Take Quinn's self-torturer: a dial controls a painful current in imperceptible steps, and the agent is paid for each one-step advance, so he prefers each setting n+1 to n when they are compared singly; yet at the top setting he would pay to return to setting 0. That yields a cycle of strict all-things-considered preferences: 0 ≺ 1 ≺ ... ≺ T, but T ≺ 0. What is your credence that there is a possible fully rational agent whose all-things-considered preferences form such a cycle — where the agent is fully informed, the options are held fixed, and the cycle is not just coarse-grained indifference, akrasia, or a failure to think diachronically? Decompose your estimate into at least three terms: (i) the probability that rationality permits at least one case of cyclic all-things-considered preference; (ii) the probability that, conditional on rational cycles being permitted, the self-torturer's stepwise pattern is best read as a genuine cycle rather than as vague or interval-valued preferences, shifting standards of comparison, or a series of separate choices; (iii) the probability that, conditional on the first two, the standard objections meant to rule out cycles outright — money pumps, Dutch books, representation theorems — do not defeat this kind of case. Then say which term you are least confident in and what evidence or argument would most move your overall credence.
