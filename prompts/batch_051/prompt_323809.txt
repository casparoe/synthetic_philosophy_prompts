I'm a novelist working on a book about how much of modern life is governed by fine print, and I keep circling the same question: when someone clicks an agree button without reading, did anything actually get agreed to? I want to run a session for a philosophy of law reading group I sometimes teach — about 14 people, 75 minutes — where the participants run into the problem rather then just argue about definitions. Design a game or structured activity around clickwrap adhesion contracts: students race to claim some small real stakes (snacks, a little extra credit), have to click through terms to get it, and later find out they consented to something they'd never knowingly accept. Give me the rules and incentives, a timeline, the debrief, and be honest about what the live activity can't capture about real adhesion contracts. I want them to leave genuinely unsure whether 'agreement' is the right word for what just happened to them.
