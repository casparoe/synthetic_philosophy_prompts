Consider a cultural pattern that is familiar in several otherwise meritocratic societies. Formal institutions in these societies celebrate high achievement: they award medals and national honors, universities give prizes to top students, and public stories explain prosperity as the fruit of individual initiative, talent, and hard work. Yet the same public culture also punishes people who behave as though the celebration were deserved. A newly promoted employee who mentions the promotion at a social gathering is mocked as a braggart; a scientist or athlete who says their success was earned is told they are getting above themselves; peers cut down the colleague who stands out. This informal cutting-down has a name in parts of the English-speaking world: the tall poppy syndrome. A related set of informal norms in the Nordic countries, often called the Law of Jante, tells people not to think they are better than others, not to think they are more special than anyone else, and not to put themselves forward.
