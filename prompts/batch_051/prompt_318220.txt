My freind walked me into this after our Nomic group tried to patch the amendment rule, and I can't find the exit. We play a Nomic-style game where the rules themselves tell you how to change the rules, and we've been assuming the amendment rule can be changed like any other rule. He says that assumption is incoherent, and here's his argument.

(1) An amendment is valid iff it was enacted by the procedure that the rules in force at the time prescribed for that kind of amendment.

(2) If the game is genuinely self-amending, then the rule prescribing those procedures—call it R—is itself amendable by the ordinary procedure.

(3) Suppose a player proposes changing R to a new rule R* that includes the sentence: any amendment enacted under R rather than R* is null and void. The proposal passes in full compliance with R.

(4) By (1) and (2), R* is now validly in force and is the amendment rule.

(5) But if R* is in force, then its content governs: amendments made under R are null and void. Since R* was made under R, R* declares its own enactment null and void.

(6) Either R*'s self-condemnation is authoritative, in which case R* is void and R never stopped being in force; or R* is authoritative despite its content, in which case the game contains a valid rule that says it isn't valid. Either way we get a contradiction or a kick back to R forever.

(7) Therefore a self-amending game cannot coherently allow its amendment rule to be amended. To stay coherent you'd need an untouchable amendment rule—but then the system isn't fully self-amending, and you've just relocated the problem.

I don't buy this, partly because our group has tweaked its own amendment rule and the game didn't collapse. I'd most like to keep (1), since validity has to be fixed by the rules at the time of the act, and (2), since otherwise self-amendment is fake. So where does the argument actually go wrong, and what do I have to give up to escape it? If one of the steps is the culprit, name it and say why it looked so plausible.
