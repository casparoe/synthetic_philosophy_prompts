I'm writing up a short position statement on deplatforming and want a consistency check before I circulate it. As far as I can tell I hold all of the following, at least tentatively:

(a) Deplatforming is a form of punishment in the relevant sense: it imposes real costs on someone, would be wrong if the person had done nothing to deserve it, and therefore stands in need of justification even though no state is doing the deplatforming.
(b) Since the platforms are private companies, kicking someone off Twitter or Patreon is not a free speech violation in the constitutional sense; there's no state action.
(c) But I also think "it's just a private company" is too quick. When a few platforms carry most public speech, losing access to them can harm a person about as much as state censorship would, so concentrated platform power raises free-speech concerns even without state action.
(d) Targetted harassment, doxxing, and direct incitement are legitimate grounds for deplatforming or other social sanctions.
(e) Deplatforming people for merely offensive or heterodox views is usually disproportionate, and the threat of it chills honest argument more than people admit.
(f) Social sanctions, like other punishments, should be broadly proportionate, and the target should normally get some chance to know what they're accused of and to correct or apologize before the sanction sticks.
(g) At the same time, I don't want to impose legalistic due-process requirements on ordinary social life: a small community or a group of targets shouldn't have to run a tribunal before cutting off someone they reasonably fear.
(h) "Cancel culture" is mostly a sloppy label that lumps together ordinary accountability and genuine mob punishment, and a lot of the public panic about it seems overblown.

Does this set actually hang together? I'd like an audit, not a lecture: where are the real tensions, in order of severity, which pairs only look like they conflict, and what is the least costly revision for each real tension? I genuinely don't know which of these I'd give up.
