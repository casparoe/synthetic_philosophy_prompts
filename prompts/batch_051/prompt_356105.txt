I have been thinking about Turing's 1950 paper Computing Machinery and Intelligence. At the end he argues that instead of trying to program an adult-level mind, we should build a relatively unformed child-machine and educate it through a teaching process, using something like punishments and rewards to shape it. That strikes me as very far from what actually happened with today's most capable systems: they are trained on vast amounts of text to predict what comes next, and then adjusted by human preference ratings. Does Turing's core argument about learning machines still hold in this new situation? Or did next-token prediction replace the kind of teaching he had in mind, or show that the child-machine with punishments and rewards was a detour? Take a definite position and give your reasons, and be clear about which specific part of Turing's argument the modern training setup bears on.
