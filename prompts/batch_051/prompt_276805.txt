Question (intro ethics midterm): "Explain Moore's account of the ideal in Principia Ethica, focusing on the role of beauty and friendship, and connect it to his view of right action. Reconstruct the argument; no secondary sources."

Student answer: "Moore argues that beauty and friendship are the two great intrinsic goods. In Principia Ethica he claims the most valuable things we know are certain states of consciousness: roughly, the enjoyment of loving relations with other people and the appreciation of beautiful things. He is not a hedonist, because these states involve more than pleasant feeling — aesthetic enjoyment includes an awareness of beauty, and personal affection includes an awareness of the good qualities in the person loved, and that awareness is itself part of the value. Since these are the things ultimately worth having, Moore's account of right action follows: an act is right if it produces the most beauty and friendship. Beauty and friendship are intrinsically good as such, so we have reason to preserve artworks, create beautiful surroundings, and maintain friendships even when they cost us. And because the two goods are independent, a friendship organized around a shared love of beauty would have a value equal to the sum of the value of the friendship plus the value of the aesthetic enjoyment."
