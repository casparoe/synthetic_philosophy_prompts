I teach epistemology and serve as faculty adviser to our student newspaper. The editorial board is currently split over whether to publish a story based on a video recording, and I would like your analysis before I meet with them.

The facts are these. The paper has obtained a short smartphone video, apparently filmed by an anonymous source at a public university reception, in which a senior administrator is heard making a demeaning remark about first-generation students. Several people can confirm the reception happened and that the administrator was present, but none of the people we have interviewed recalls the remark itself. The administrator denies having said it and suggests the video is a deepfake. A digital forensics report commissioned by the paper finds no evidence of facial manipulation, but it flags one anomaly in the audio track: a 350-millisecond discontinuity that the same report describes as consistent with an edited splice slipped into an otherwise authentic recording, and, equally, consistent with a compression artefact of a kind common on that model of phone. The board is split. One side argues that publishing an accusation about a named individual on evidence that might be fabricated is irresponsible and defamatory. The other argues that if a mere unproven claim of deepfakery is enough to kill a story, then any public figure can escape accountability for things they were recorded saying, and the paper will have adopted a standard under which no recording can ever be actionable.

I am not asking for a legal judgment or a journalistic how-to. I want to know how to think about the epistemic situation: what the board is really choosing between, given that the recording can be either authentic or fabricated and that the decision itself changes what future recordings can be used to establish. Please act as a philosopher of social epistemology and the ethics of belief. Begin by brainstorming the relevant considerations, then give me only a single JSON object with these fields:

- diagnosis: a short statement of the epistemic structure of the situation (at most four sentences).
- live_options: an array of two or three options, each an object with the fields label, position, strongest_reason, and main_risk.
- recommendation: an object with decision (one of publish, withhold, publish_with_conditions, or other), justification (a concrete statement of what the paper should do and why), and confidence (low, medium, or high, with a one-sentence explanation).
- general_principle: a transferable rule for deciding when an unverified but not discredited recording should be treated as evidence.
- flip_conditions: an array of conditions that, if satisfied, would reverse your recommendation.
- steelman_for_opposite: the strongest case for whichever of publish or withhold you do not recommend, stated in two or three sentences.

Nothing outside the JSON, please.
