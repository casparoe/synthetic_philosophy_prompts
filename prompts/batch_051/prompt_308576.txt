I'm in a community garden association, and our rulebook has two clauses that have started to fight each other. Clause 4 says any part of the rulebook can be amended by a two-thirds vote of the full membership at a general meeting. Clause 12 says the board is the final authority on interpreting what the rulebook means. A while back the membership got fed up because the board kept using clause 12 to shut down proposols before members could even vote on them, so at the last meeting someone moved to delete clause 12 and replace it with a disputes committee picked by lot. It passed 78 to 11, well over two-thirds.

Now the board refuses to recognize the vote. Their argument is that at the moment the amendment was passed, clause 12 was still in force, so it was the board's job to judge whether the amendment itself was valid — and they've judged that clause 4 was never meant to let the membership use the amendment process to take away the board's interpretive power. The membership says that if that argument works, the amendment power is fake: the board could always rule any attempt to limit the board invalid, and the membership could never change who gets the final say. It feels like the two clauses are holding each other up, and whichever one you look at first wins.

I can't tell who's right, and more importantly I don't know what we should do next. We're a private association, so no court is going to step in. Is there a principled way to say one side should win? And if there isn't, is every way out just one side grabbing power, or is there some other kind of move we're not seeing?
