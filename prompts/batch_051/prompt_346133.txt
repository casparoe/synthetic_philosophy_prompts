Write a decision tree that sorts people into positions on what would make a future genuinely good to live in, not merely tolerable or free of disaster. The instrument is for a group setting the long-term goals of a new city district, and it must sort people by the futures they would choose rather than by the labels they accept. Build from the logical space, not from a list of schools. Work with at least these splits: (i) whether the goodness of a life is a matter of felt experience from the inside or of what one actually does; (ii) whether difficulty, boredom, and failure are pure costs, useful instruments, or partly constitutive of a good life; (iii) whether the good is uniform across people; (iv) whether a good future must include real risk and possible loss. Each branch node must be a concrete choice between two described futures or policies, phrased so a layperson can answer cold—for example, a future of safe, immersive virtual worlds with voluntary exit; a future of demanding collective projects with frequent failure and visible achievement; a future of dense mutual obligation and low mobility. Locate named and unnamed positions as leaves; mark every combination of answers that no one defends, saying whether it is empty from incoherence, unattractiveness, or neglect; and flag any positions the tree cannot separate. End by stating the single question you would add to split the most crowded leaf.
