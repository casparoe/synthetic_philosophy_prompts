I keep running into this argument for why the Nordic model can't be exported, and I half-agree with the conclusion but something in the middle feels off. Here it is, as my friend gives it: (1) A generous welfare state funded by high taxes only survives politically if citizens trust that most strangers will keep working and not free-ride. (2) That kind of generalized trust is a cultural trait rooted in ethnic homogeneity, shared religion, and small stable communities — exactly the Nordic conditions. (3) So importing the taxes and benefits without the culture gives you the institutions without the trust that makes them work. (4) Trust can't be created quickly by policy; it's a slow byproduct of deeper social conditions. (5) Therefore exporting the Nordic model to a big, diverse country like the US is bound to fail, and the failure is one of principle, not just poor implementation. Is the argument valid, and are the premises true? If it's valid but unsound, which premise is weakest; if it's invalid, where's the gap? I can't decide whether the real issue is that 'trust' is doing two different jobs here, that the causality runs the other way too, or that (5) overstates what the premises support. Give me the strongest repaired version that still says something deflating about export-by-policy. And be explicit about which parts of your diagnosis are solid and which are speculative; if the argument is too sloppy to pin on one step, just say so rather than pretending it's crisp.
