I am a novelist, and metaphor is central to my craft. I keep running into two claims about metaphor that appear to pull in opposite directions. Davidson maintains that a metaphor has no meaning beyond what its words literally mean: understanding one is not a matter of extracting a hidden proposition, but of being prompted to notice likenesses and connections. Lakoff and Johnson instead treat metaphor as the surface of systematic, embodied conceptual mappings—for example, understanding argument through the logic of war—so that metaphorical language reflects something about cognition rather than being a merely decorative figure. Please explain the conceptual difference between these two positions, clarify whether they are genuine rivals or largely address different questions, and spell out what each position commits one to about whether a metaphor can be paraphrased without loss. In your answer, please state how confident you are and identify the point you are least certain about.
