In my school's philosophy club we have been trying to find a workable way to distinguish manipulation from legitimate persuasion, and the working idea we keep returning to is that manipulation is influence that depends partly on not being noticed as manipulation. Someone in the club proposed the following rule for a large online forum where students and adults debate school policies, and I want to understand how it would actually function rather than whether it is a good idea in the abstract.

The rule would require that every post arguing for a conclusion begin with a transparency audit containing three items: (1) the exact belief or policy the author is trying to move the reader toward; (2) a plain-language description of each emotional appeal the author knowingly uses—for example, writing, "I open with a story about a humiliated student in order to make you feel sympathy"; and (3) the strongest argument against the author's conclusion that the author considered but chose not to develop. The audit must also answer one question: would this post still persuade a reader who read the audit first and then reread the post while looking for the disclosed techniques? Posts without a complete audit are hidden until edited, and any logged-in reader may flag an audit as dishonest. After 25 flags, the post is hidden until the author revises the audit; authors do not lose posting privileges.

Walk through how this system would behave if it were actually adopted. I would like you to address, at minimum: the incentives it creates for authors, for readers who can flag posts, and for moderators; how a skilled and determined writer would game the audit without violating the letter of the rule; what the forum would likely look like after a year of this gaming; and any second-order effects—for example, on who participates, on the tone of arguments, and on how much readers trust posts that comply. Then say whether the worst failure modes can be fixed by adjusting the rule or whether each fix pushes the system back toward simply trusting moderators to decide what counts as manipulative. Please write your answer in American English.
