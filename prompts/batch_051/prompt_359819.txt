I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am putting together an interactive piece that will let readers discover which philosophical account of laws of nature they actually hold, rather than which label they recognize. I would like you to construct a decision tree or sorting quiz for this piece. The tree should open with a concrete, pre-theoretical question — something like whether a law of nature is better thought of as a compact record of how things happen or as something that makes things happen as they do — and should then proceed through branch-node questions that a curious non-specialist can answer honestly without already knowing the names of the positions. The leaves should include at least the following positions: a naive regularity view; a Mill–Ramsey–Lewis best-system Humeanism; an Armstrong-style necessitarian view on which laws are non-Humean relations among universals; a law-primitivist view of the sort associated with Maudlin, on which laws are fundamental entities that push the world along; and a dispositional-essentialist view on which laws flow from the causal natures of properties. Please also locate the combinations of answers that no major position currently occupies, and briefly say whether each empty cell is empty because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

The quiz should be built around diagnostic cases rather than definitions. I want you to make sure it uses, as probes, at least the following: (i) the generalization that no sphere of pure gold has a diameter greater than a thousand kilometers, and whether that could count as a law; (ii) the contrast between the accidental truth that every coin in someone's pocket is copper and the standing truth captured by Coulomb's law; (iii) how strongly a law supports a counterfactual judgment about whether a particular copper wire would conduct electricity if it were connected to a circuit; and (iv) the thought that two possible worlds could share their entire history of particular events and yet differ in their laws. Phrase each branch node as a short question or forced choice about one of these cases, not as a request for philosophical vocabulary.

You are a professional philosopher of science with a gift for clear exposition. Please think carefully about the design. Before committing to the final tree, briefly brainstorm the underlying logical space: which candidate questions separate which positions, which combinations of answers turn out to be populated and which do not, and what order of questions produces the largest separations earliest. Then present the finished instrument, and flag explicitly any two or more leaves that your tree cannot genuinely pull apart, along with a one-sentence explanation of where the residual difference lies. Do not pad the tree with bibliographic references; the instrument should stand entirely on its diagnostic structure.
