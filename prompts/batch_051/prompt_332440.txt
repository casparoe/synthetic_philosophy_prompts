I've been trying to reverse-engineer a friend's moral judgments about lying versus misleading, and I keep going in circles. Her verdicts so far:
- A used-car dealer, unprompted, tells a buyer the car had "one careful owner." True, but it was a rental car, and the buyer takes it to mean it wasn't. She says: wrong, but less bad than lying.
- A financial adviser, asked point-blank whether a client's retirement fund is safe, says "the portfolio is entirely in government bonds." True, but the government is weeks from a possible default. She says: exactly as bad as lying.
- A spouse, asked "did anything happen at the conference?", says "we had dinner and went to our rooms." True, but it omits everything else that happened. She says: exactly as bad as lying.

Now consider this new case. A stranger selling a camera online is asked directly, "Has it ever been dropped?" and answers, "It was serviced last month and everything checks out." That's true, but the camera was dropped badly just before the servicing and has worked only intermittently since. The seller doesn't know the buyer, and there's no ongoing relationship.

Does she say this is exactly as bad as lying, or less bad? What rules fit her three earlier verdicts, what does each rule predict here, and wich further case would best tell the strongest two rules apart? Please write your answer in British English.
