I cover science and ideas for a living, and once a month I sit in on the same argument between two critics, Leo and Mara, at a reading group I use as an excuse to overhear smart people fight. Its always the same fight about where the meaning of a message lives. Leo's position is that a message is its inner content: the thing it actually says, the claim or image or punchline you could write down afterward on a notecard. The frame around it, the fact that it's a poem rather than a boundary dispute or a joke rather than a confession, is just how you got to the content, not part of the content itself. Mara says that's like arguing the envelope isn't part of the mail. The same sentence can mean opposite things depending on the outer message, the implicit instructions about how to decode it, and on the frame message, the announcement that a message is even present and of what sort. Leo replies that those are preconditions for receiving a meaning, like needing light to read a page, and the light isn't part of the page's meaning. Mara then points to six words spoken in a eulogy versus the same six words in a roast, Leo says that just changes the message, and we're off again.

I have a working theory, and I want someone to check it. My theory is that this is a verbal impasse about the scope of the word meaning: Leo has quietly defined meaning as the inner message only, and Mara has defined it as the whole nested package of frame, outer, and inner messages, so they're not actually contradicting each other; they're answering different questions under one heading. But I'm not sure, because both of them think the other's claim is false, not just differently labeled. Diagnose the actual structure of this disagreement using what each side actually says. Is it verbal or substantive? Is there a hidden question about whether preconditions can be parts? Where does semantics stop and pragmatics begin, or is that the wrong way to cut it? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress here: a distinction, a new example, a reframed question, or just accepting the stalemate?
