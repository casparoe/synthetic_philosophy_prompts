I have been trying to work out when self-examination stops being useful and turns into the kind of rumination that worsens a low mood. The tests I keep using are these four: repetition — the examination runs in loops instead of arriving at a result; abstraction — it keeps asking why things are as they are rather than attending to what is happening now; passivity — it analyzes the mood as a problem without moving toward any concrete act; and fruitlessness — it changes nothing about how one behaves. Now consider a standard exercise in cognitive-behavioral therapy. After each bout of anxiety or sadness, the person writes down the situation, the thought that accompanied it, the evidence for and against that thought, and a more even-handed reading of what happened. This is plainly self-examination; it is deliberately repeated; and it is organized around causal and evidential questions rather than present-moment experience. But it is assigned as treatment and is meant to issue in changed behavior rather than in more analysis. Does the exercise count as harmful rumination by my four tests? Take the tests one at a time, and where a test gives no clear verdict say whether the test or the case is the problem. End by telling me whether the four tests need redrawing.
