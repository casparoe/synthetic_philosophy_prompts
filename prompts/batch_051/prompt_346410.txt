I sit on my university's research safety board, the one that handles studies that are legal but odd enough that someone has to sign of on them. We've got a case from a cognitive psych lab that says it's stumbled onto what they call an antimeme: a small visual pattern that, once a subject has seen it, they can't hold in mind for more than about thirty seconds. Subjects do fine on a recognition test right after exposure, but five minutes later they can't describe it, can't draw it, and often deny that the study even had a stimulus. The lab's own records have gone strange too: one consent form keeps getting filed blank, one protocol document keeps being overwritten with garbage. The PI wants permission to keep running the stimulus on healthy volunteers, and to publish the methods but not the pattern itself, which would stay locked in a safe.

Half the board thinks this is just a surprising memory effect and wants to approve it with routine safeguards. The other half thinks we're looking at an information hazard, a piece of content that erases its own paper trail and maybe our ability to oversee it, and that our committee can't meaningfully deliberate about a finding none of us can reliably remember. I need to circulate a decision memo before Thursday's meeting. Please draft it so it recommends approve, reject, or approve with conditions, gives reasons the researchers could actually respond to, and takes the strongest point on the losing side seriously rather than just brushing it off.
