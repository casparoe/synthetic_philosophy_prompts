I'm reading a recent book on the theory of the state, and its treatment of Max Weber's account of the state strikes me as too brisk. The author summarizes the position as follows:

"Weber defined the modern state as the institution that successfully claims, within a territory, the exclusive authority to exercise legitimate physical force. The police are the face of that monopoly for ordinary citizens: they are, in practice, the only legitimate armed force most people will ever encounter. From this it follows that a state cannot continue to be a state, in Weber's sense, unless it keeps its police properly funded and empowered. That is why 'defund the police' is coherent only as a modest budget reform, and why full abolition amounts to anarchism. To give up the police is to give up the state itself."

I'm not sure how much of this to trust. Please go through the passage carefully and separate (a) what is faithful to Weber's view, (b) what is a common but misleading simplification, and (c) what Weber's account simply does not license. I would also like a clear verdict on whether the claims about "defund the police" and abolition actually follow from the accurate parts of the summary — and, if they do not, what the accurate parts can and cannot support in debates about defunding or abolishing the police.
