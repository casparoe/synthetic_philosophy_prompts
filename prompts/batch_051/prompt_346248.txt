I chair my university's student conduct board (long story, I got roped in through the faculty senate and somehow chairing means I write the opinions nobody wants to be responsible for). We now have a case that's split the board. About a dozen students occupied the main administration building for fourteen hours to try to force divestment from a defense contractor in the university's endowment. They blocked entrances, refused to leave after warnings, and campus police cited them for trespassing; no property damage, they cleaned up afterwards. Their defense is that this was civil disobedience against a policy they consider gravely unjust, they're not contesting the facts, and before the action they published a statement saying they expected to accept punishment. But then in the hearing a couple of them argued that the anti-occupation rule is itself an instrument of the unjust investment policy, so the university's authority to punish this particular violation is compromised. The rest stuck with the "we accept whatever penalty comes" line. The board is split: half wants expulsion or at least a semester suspension because rules don't enforce themselves and exceptions create a defection incentive; the other half wants essentially no sanction because punishing conscientious protest against injustice feels like punishing people for actually caring about the community. My own rationalist-brain keeps trying to model it as a credibility game where letting people pick which rules bind them leads to unraveling, but the students aren't exactly free-riding, since they invited the penalty.

The possible sanctions on the table are expulsion, a one-semester suspension with a transcript notation, disciplinary probation plus a reflective essay, or no formal sanction with a warning. Draft the board's decision memo: a clear recommendation among those (or a variant), the reasoning for it, and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. The thing that's bugging me, and that I'd like the memo to address head-on, is whether a protester can simultaneously claim the special status of civil disobedience (which is usually taken to mean accepting the legal order's authority and accepting punishment) while also denying that the university has authority to punish them at all — or whether, once you deny the institution's authority, you're just doing ordinary rule-breaking with a moral justification and should be judged accordingly. Also: the university is a voluntary association with an exit option, unlike a state, so does that change whether these students had an obligation to follow the rule in the first place? Don't feel like you have to settle all of that cleanly, but the memo should speak to it enough that the rest of the board can actually use what you write.
