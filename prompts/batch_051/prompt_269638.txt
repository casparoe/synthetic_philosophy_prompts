I keep hearing 'ascetic' used for such different people that I no longer know what the word is picking out. I need a working defintion of asceticism for an essay I'm writing (or of 'voluntary simplicity', if you think that's the better term for what I'm after), and I want you to test it against these cases, because I think they're the ones that break any quick definition.

Here's my list:
- a fourth-century desert hermit who eats once a day and prays half the night
- a guy in San Francisco who owns about forty things and lives in a tiny apartment, but has a six-figure tech job and replaces his phone every year
- a single mother on minimum wage who genuinely can't afford much
- someone doing intermittent fasting purely for longevity
- an environmental activist who goes car-free and lives in a housing co-op mainly to shrink his carbon footprint
- a monk under a vow of poverty in a monastery that is itself wealthy and well-fed
- someone who does brutal cold-water plunges every morning just to train his willpower
- a miser who lives on lentils and never spends a cent, not out of any ideal, just because spending hurts
- a person who quits all social media, buys a dumbphone, and calls it 'digital asceticism'
- someone who gives away a big chunk of their income but otherwise lives a normal middle-class life

I suspect the word is doing two jobs at once: one about how little you consume, and one about renunciation being aimed at some further end (God, virtue, clarity, discipline, whatever). If you think that's right, give me the two senses and say which of my cases fall under which, and where the boundary does the most work. Then tell me which verdict you're least sure about, because I want to see where my own intuitions are doing hidden work.
