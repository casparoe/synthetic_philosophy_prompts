A friend and I recently spent a long evening arguing about a case from his work: after a product-safety failure that had been years in the making, every individual in the chain could truthfully say that they followed approved procedure or relied on someone else's sign-off, and no single person appears to have intended the harm. He insisted that only the specific human beings involved can be blameworthy, and that blaming the company is at best shorthand for distributing responsibility across them. I insisted that the corporation is a distinct agent with its own decision procedures, institutional memory, incentive structures, and culture, and that blaming it says something true that blaming the individuals cannot. By the end of the night neither of us had moved, so we agreed to stop relitigating the case and instead draft an adversarial-collaboration document we could both sign. Please write that document.

Write it from the perspective of the two positions, which you may call the corporate-agent view (a corporation can be a moral agent and an apt target of blame in its own right, not merely a legal or administrative target) and the individualist view (only natural persons are apt targets of moral blame, while corporate blame is a second-order device for coordinating our responses to them). The document should open with a numbered agreed-claims section that both sides can endorse without reservation, covering at least: how corporate conduct is realized through the actions of individuals; how firms remember, decide, and act across changes of personnel; why individual blame often runs out in complex organizations; and what non-moral functions corporate liability and public criticism serve. It should then include a disagreements table in which each remaining point is stated exactly as each side would accept for its own view, with any rows that look merely verbal flagged as such. After that, identify three or four genuine cruxes, meaning sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of us, and for each crux give a concrete case or test that both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about the case recorded in advance. Candidate cruxes we kept circling include, but need not be limited to: whether a group's decision procedure can constitute a genuine intention rather than a mere aggregation of individual intentions; whether the firm remains the same blameworthy party across wholesale turnover of its personnel; whether reactive attitudes such as moral indignation and resentment fit a corporation in anything beyond an extended or fictional sense; and whether the reparative work of blame, such as acknowledgment, apology, reform, and reconciliation, can be done by blaming the firm when no individual steps forward. Conclude with a short joint paragraph stating what the two of us would both commit to doing differently in practice. Do not include bibliographies or citations; we want a jointly-owned statement, not a literature review.
