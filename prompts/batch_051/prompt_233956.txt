Just spent two hours arguing with a friend about wether natural selection is a tautology, and I walked away with a distinction I've been using since and want pressure-tested. I call it tilt versus tally. Tally fitness is the actual reproductive output: how many surviving offspring a type left in this particular run of the population. Tilt fitness is the before-the-fact bias the environment gives a type's traits: not what happened, but how much more probable one outcome was than another before it happened. My claim is that the old objection (that survival of the fittest just says survivors survive) only lands when fitness means tally. Once fitness means tilt, the claim becomes substantive: the population's composition was pushed in a direction by something other than sampling luck. The price is that tilt is unobservable in a single history — you never see the bias, just one draw from it, so you end up estimating the bias from the very tallies you were trying not to lean on.

My running example: in a drought year, long-beaked birds leave more surviving offspring than short-beaked birds. On the tally view, that bigger number just is their fitness, so saying the fittest left more offspring is circular. On the tilt view, beak length raised the probability of the long-beaked type doing better across the range of conditions that year, and the actual counts are one realization of that bias. My friend says I've just reinvented the propensity interpretation of fitness and made it worse, because now natural selection is a claim about an invisible quantity that can never be caught being false in any particular case. I think tilt is a bit finer than propensity, because people use propensity sloppily as a single-run summary, whereas tilt forces you to commit to a rerun or counterfactual reading.

So: is tilt/tally a real distinction or just a metaphor with a new name? Has someone already worked this out beyond the propensity interpretation, and does my version add anything? And what are the three hardest problems for it — does the tautology objection just move inside the tilt concept instead of getting solved, and is any of the problems fatal rather than just uncomfortable?
