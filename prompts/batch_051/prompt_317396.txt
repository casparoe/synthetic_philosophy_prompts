Working through a scenario for a class on transitional justice. A country coming out of a long internal conflict set up an amnesty comission. The legislation says: 'Acts committed in the course of the armed conflict, on behalf of a party to the conflict, and in furtherance of a political objective shall be eligible for amnesty, provided they are proportionate to that objective; acts committed for personal gain are excluded.' That's the whole relevant clause. Now here's an applicant. For three years he worked as a data analyst for the military police. He built a scoring system that estimated, for each village, the probability that insurgents were hiding there, using census data, checkpoint records, call logs, and old informant reports. The military used the scores to pick villages for raids. At least two raids on high-scoring villages ended in killings of non-combatants, though the analyst never set foot in any village, never carried a weapon, and he insists the whole point was to make searches more precise and reduce indiscriminate violence. The legislation was drafted in 1993, by people thinking about soldiers, bombers, informers, torturers — not about someone whose entire participation was a predictive model. Does he fall under the clause? Argue it from the words, from the purpose of the clause, from what the drafters would have said if someone had described this kind of algorithmic targeting to them, and from what a reasonable reader would say the clause plainly covers; then say which approach should control and why. In particular, does the proportionality requirement attach to his own conduct in building the model, or to the raids that used its scores, and why? Finally, if he gets amnesty, is that a signal the clause needs amending, or is it just the law working as intended?
