A state historical society is embarrassed that its archive is mostly governors, college presidents, and well-off donors and is basically silent on everyone else. So it adopts a rule: any individual or family donating a collection of personal papers can do so only if the society also commissions, that same year, an oral-history interview with a randomly selected adult from the county's lowest-income census tract, and the interview goes into the archive on equal footing with the papers. Treat this as a mechanism, not a moral position. What does it incentivize among donors and would-be donors, among the archivists, and among the people whose names get drawn? How would people game it or route around it? What happens to the people the lottery never picks, and do the silences just move somewhere else? Are the failure modes fixable, or does each patch just create a new workaround? Answer in American English.
