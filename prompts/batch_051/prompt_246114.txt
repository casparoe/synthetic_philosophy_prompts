I am a philosopher of mind, and for some time I have been arguing with a colleague in computational neuroscience who insists that integrated information theory settles the metaphysical question of consciousness in a way that philosophers have needlessly complicated. He quotes Tononi at me. In Tononi's own summary of the theory, as given in his Scholarpedia article "Integrated Information Theory" (2015): "Based on the postulates, it permits in principle to derive, for any particular system of elements in a state, whether it has consciousness, how much, and which particular experience it is having." My colleague reads this as the strong claim that the quantity Φ, a system's integrated information, does not merely correlate with or predict consciousness but measures, perhaps even constitutes, its actual level: a system is conscious if and only if, and to the exact degree that, it forms an irreducibly integrated complex of mechanisms with Φ above the relevant threshold.

I would rather subject this to experimental constraint than to further polemic. Suppose we set aside the formal derivation and ask: how would one actually test, against real systems, the identity claim that consciousness is integrated information, as distinguished from the weaker claim that Φ tracks consciousness in the particular conditions—wakefulness, sleep, anesthesia, perhaps coma—where it has so far looked promising? Design the strongest concrete experiment or battery of observations you can, drawing on whatever populations or systems you think most probative (sleep stages, anesthesia, severe brain injury, locked-in and covertly conscious patients, dissociative states, split-brain patients, nonhuman animals, artificial networks, even simple logic gates). Tell me precisely what pattern of results would favor the strong identity reading of the passage, what pattern would favor the weaker correlate reading, and what no result of any such experiment could settle. In particular, since every test must operationalize consciousness through behavior, report, or task performance, I want an honest account of whether the residue that no test can reach is merely a practical limitation of measurement and verbal report, or whether it exposes an ambiguity in the identity claim itself—a confusion between "measuring consciousness" and "matching the measurement"—that makes "Φ is consciousness" and "Φ correlates with consciousness" impossible to adjudicate in principle.
