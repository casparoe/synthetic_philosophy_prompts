For a seminar I am taking on the ethics of war, I have been asked to write out my current commitments on the morality of soldiering before I begin drafting my term paper. They are the following:

1. Moral equality of combatants. In a conventional interstate war, soldiers on both sides possess the same moral permission to target and kill opposing combatants, and this permission does not depend on whether their side's war satisfies the jus ad bellum criteria.

2. Independence of jus in bello from jus ad bellum. Whether a particular act in war is permissible is settled by the in bello rules, and the answer does not shift once one learns that the actor's side is fighting an unjust war.

3. The architects of an unjust war are murderers. Political leaders who knowingly launch a war without a just cause commit murder on a massive scale. I hold this while continuing to affirm (1) and (2); I suspect the apparent tension here is merely apparent, because it concerns a different level of agency.

4. Liability as the ground of permissible killing. A person may permissibly be killed in war only if that person is morally liable to be killed, where liability arises from making a responsible contribution to an unjust threat.

5. Individual wrongdoing in unjust service. A combatant who enlists knowing—or being in a position to know—that their side's war is unjust does something seriously wrong merely in taking up arms for that cause, even if their subsequent conduct complies with the in bello rules.

6. Duress excuses conscripts. A conscript coerced into the ranks of an unjust army is not blameworthy for the bare fact of their participation.

7. Conscripts remain legitimate targets. That same conscript remains a legitimate target for opposing soldiers, who may kill them in self-defense; and this is the normal war permission attaching to their status as combatants, not merely a lesser-evil exception.

I find each of these individually plausible, but I have not yet worked out whether they form a coherent whole. Please audit this set: identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be; rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equal; for each tension, state what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would be; and then tell me which single revision would most improve the overall coherence, and what that revision would cost.
