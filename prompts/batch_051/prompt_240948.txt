I help lead worship at a small Reformed church. We've been wanting to add a short moment into the service, right before the sermon: a member reads a lament written by someone in the congregation (we've lost a lot of people to overdoses in our town, and folks want space to grieve), and then we sit in silence for a minute. Our pastor is against it. He pointed me to the Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 96: "We are not to make an image of God in any way, nor to worship him in any other manner than he has commanded in his Word." He says that means we may only do in worship what Scripture explicitly tells us to do, and Scripture never tells us to have a congregation member read a newly written lament. But I look around our service and we do plenty that Scripture never mentions: we meet in a rented school cafeteria, we project hymn lyrics onto a wall, we take an offering by passing a plate, we have a printed order of service. So is the pastor actually applying the catechism right, or is he just being inconsistent? What actually seperates a "manner of worship" from the stuff around it, and on which side would our lament fall? I want a principled answer, not just "let people grieve."
