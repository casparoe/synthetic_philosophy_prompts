I serve on the board of a regional housing trust that administers roughly 4,000 income-restricted rental units. We are weighing a staff proposal to replace our voucher system—eligible households currently choose among participating landlords, with rents negotiated within a published payment standard—with a centralized allocation platform in which an algorithm assigns applicants to units using need and suitability scores set by staff, and rents are fixed by a cost-plus formula reviewed only once a year. The question has stopped feeling abstract to me because I have lately been reading the socialist calculation debate: Hayek's objection that a central board cannot gather the dispersed local knowledge that market prices reveal, and Lange's reply that a board can approximate markets by adjusting administered prices through trial and error. Please draft the short written opinion I will submit before our next vote: recommend either retaining the voucher system or adopting the platform, include a concrete transition timeline and a date for reassessment, and give the strongest consideration on the losing side an honest answer rather than a caricature.
