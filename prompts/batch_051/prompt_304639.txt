Could you build me a tree (or 7–9 question quiz with a scoring key, whichever works better) that sorts people into positions on how religious authority and philosophical demonstration relate. Make every branch a concrete case: a literal verse conflicts with a demonstrated conclusion; an ordinary believer hears the philosophical reading and is distressed; the ruler asks whether philosophy should be taught publicly; a theologian insists philosophy's "God" and the revealed God are different; a student asks what to do when two interpreters disagree. Phrase the questions and options in ordinary language, keeping jargon like "double truth" and "fideism" out of the question wording. The leaves I'd like to include are at least: strong rationalism (philosophy arbitrates, scripture means what it must), harmonism (one truth, reached by demonstration for the few and by persuasion for the many), a reason-has-limits view where revelation wins on conflict, and a no-need-to-cohere view where a claim can be true for philosophy and false for faith. Build the tree from the logical space of answers, not from the names: lay out the questions and the combinations they allow, then locate these positions in that space. For combinations no position occupies, say whether theyre empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. And flag any positions your instrument cant really separate (im suspicious harmonism and strong rationalism blur once both allow allegorical reading). Dont worry about matching historical labels exactly; if a leaf corresponds to a view historians say nobody actually held, just say so.
