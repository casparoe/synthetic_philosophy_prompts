I've always taken van Fraassen's Reflection principle to be basically a formal endorsement of deferring to your future self: if my later credence in A is going to be x, then my current credence in A, conditional on that, should be x too. But tonight I'm getting drunk with friends, and I know from experience that drunk-me forms wildly overconfident beliefs — that I can definitely drive, that texting my ex is a great idea, that I'll finish the project by tomorrow. Does Reflection really require me to start updating toward what drunk-me will believe in a few hours? that seems like a reductio of the principle, not a demand of rationality. Or is the principle only supposed to apply when I regard my future self as at least as rational and well informed as I am now? And if that's the restriction, then what actual work is the principle doing — since it would just tell me to defer in exactly the cases where I already have independent reason to trust the guy I'll become?
