Write a dialogue between a Nash bargaining theorist and a Kalai–Smorodinsky bargaining theorist who are standing at the same whiteboard, each trying to talk the other out of their answer to one problem. The problem: disagreement point (0,0), feasible set is the convex hull of (0,0), (10,0), (6,8), and (0,10). The Nash guy says the deal is (6,8); the Kalai–Smorodinsky guy says it's (20/3, 20/3), which is about (6.67, 6.67). Make them actually argue, not just recite definitions. Both should be sharp and respond to what the other has just said — especially when one leans on independence of irrelevant alternatives and the other leans on monotonicity. They should use the geometry of this particular feasible set: why Nash lands on the kink, why the Kalai–Smorodinsky ray cuts the upper segment, and what each would have to grant for the other's view to feel forced. Dont let either one be a strawman, and neither has to win; they just have to genuinely engage. No citations, no reading list.
