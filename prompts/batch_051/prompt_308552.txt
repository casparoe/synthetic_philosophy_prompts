I'm a novelist, mid-revision on a book where the harbor temple's high priestess doubles as the magistrate for anything touching the sacred. I've got four of her earlier rulings drafted, but I'm starting to worry she's been inconsistant scene to scene and I've just been going with my gut. I want to pin down the rule she's actually running before I write her next ruling.

Her track record:

1. A widow pawned the little consecrated household god she'd inherited from her mother to buy bread after a bad harvest. The priestess said it was not sacrilege. She made the pawnbroker return the idol, made the widow undergo a purification, and paid the widow's debt out of the temple stores.

2. A drunk sailor urinated on the foot of the harbor statue of the goddess at noon, with half the fish market watching. He was too drunk to know where he was. She had him flogged and banished, and said the stone was still the stone.

3. A traveling player staged a comedy in which a masked actor playing the goddess wheedled and whined for offerings. She called it blasphemy rather than sacrilege, fined him, and banished him for a season, saying a blow struck through laughter still lands.

4. A carver mass-produced cheap copies of the temple's sacred mask and sold them to foreign sailors from a stall outside the precinct. Nobody in the city much cared except the temple mask-carvers, who disliked the competition. She ruled it vulgar and misleading but not an offence against the sacred, so long as each copy was stamped as a copy.

The new case I can't settle: a woman whose only child died of fever takes her dead mother's consecrated household god, the same kind of idol the widow pawned, and smashes it in her own courtyard at night, then feeds the pieces to her pigs. She tells the court she knew exactly what she was doing: if the goddess let her son die, she says, she owes the goddess nothing. No one witnessed it, and she refuses purification.

What I want is the verdict that keeps the priestess most consistent with her own track record, which earlier case is the closest analog, and an honest statement of what principle she'd be adopting either way. I suspect her precedents underdetermine it, so lay out the rival rules that fit what she's already done, what each one predicts for the new case, and one further made-up case that would cleanly separate the remaining contenders.

For the response, give me a JSON object with these fields:

- candidate_rules: a list of objects, each with "nickname", "statement" (one sentence), "fits" (which of the four earlier rulings it explains), "clashes_with" (which earlier ruling it struggles with, if any), and "prediction_for_new_case" (the finding plus sacrilege, blasphemy, or no offence).
- recommended_verdict: your best call, with the finding and a one-sentence reason.
- closest_precedent: which earlier case is most analogous and why.
- adopted_principle: the principle she'd be binding herself to with that recommended verdict.
- further_test_case: a short made-up case and what it would show.

If some of that really wants to be prose, keep the JSON fields but make the values short paragraphs.
