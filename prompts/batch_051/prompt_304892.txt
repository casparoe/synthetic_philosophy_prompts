I spent most of my career as a research physiologist studying adaptation to training—how repeated, structured stress on a system produces specific, and only occasionally general, improvements in capacity. Since retiring, I have been reading philosophy and have become interested in a debate I gather runs roughly as follows. One side holds that rationality, like a martial art, can be trained through deliberate practice of standardized exercises—calibration drills, probability problems, argument-mapping, and similar forms—and that the resulting improvements transfer broadly to real-world reasoning. The other side holds that such training produces mainly narrow, task-specific gains: one gets better at the forms, and perhaps at closely neighboring tasks, but the promised far transfer to messy, time-pressured, high-stakes judgment is largely unsupported. I am not fluent enough in the philosophical vocabulary of this debate to push it much further, but I am very much at home in the vocabulary of exercise physiology and motor learning: specificity versus generality of adaptation, central versus peripheral determinants of performance, general physical preparedness, periodization, plateaus and overtraining, near versus far transfer, closed versus open skills (stable, repeatable drills versus skills performed in variable, unpredictable conditions), contextual interference, deliberate practice, and retention.

I would like you to restate this debate systematically in that vocabulary. Take each main claim on both sides and translate it. For example, what in physiology corresponds to the claim that a battery of canonical exercises can build a general capacity for reasoning, or to the skeptical claim that performance gains remain bound to the training context, or to the complaint that certain drills are too artificial to prepare someone for contested, real-world reasoning? Then give me the residue report. Specifically: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion or under strain, and which have no expression at all in the target vocabulary? Finally, say whether the residue is a defect of the physiology framework—places where it cannot see the phenomenon—or whether it reveals that the underlying philosophical claim was less crisp than it looked, leaning on the martial-art metaphor to do work it cannot carry. Please cap your response at 900 words.
