You're a professional philosopher of music—ontology of musical works is your thing. I'm putting together a handout for an aesthetics reading group, and I need your help catching a gap in it. The handout lists objections to the type/token ontology: the view that a musical work is an abstract type or repeatable pattern, and that performances are its concrete tokens. The objections I already have are these:

1. Creation. If works are abstract types, they exist outside time and can't be brought into being, but we routinely say composers create their works, sometimes at pretty precise dates.

2. Individuation. The view never says how close a performance has to come to the type in order to count as a token. If every detail has to match, almost no actual performance is a token; if only some details have to match, the boundary looks arbitrary.

3. Independent composition. Two composers working in different musical contexts could independently arrive at the same pitch-and-rhythm structure, and we'd want to distinguish the resulting works; the type view seems forced to identify them.

4. Performance means. A bare sound structure doesn't encode the instrument or medium the composer wrote for, so the view can't respect the thought that a work-for-flute and a distinct work-for-violin sharing a note sequence stay two works rather than collpase into one.

5. Works with no performances. A piece made directly on tape or as a fixed digital file has no performance tokens, so the view either demotes it to a mere object or has to find tokens where there are none.

I have a nagging sense I'm forgetting a sixth objection that has a genuinely different structure. What is it? Don't just reword or specialize any of these—in particular, don't give me another version of the individuation worry or another temporal objection about abstract objects. Say what this missing objection adds that my five don't, and give me your honest read on whether it's as damaging as (1) and (3) or more of a nuisance. Before committing, maybe brainstorm two or three candidates and pick the one that's most independent of my list.
