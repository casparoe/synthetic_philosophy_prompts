I've been going back and forth about something in the autonomous weapons debate and I'd like help pinning down the philosophy behind it. In the arms-control discussion people talk about meaningful human control over the use of force, and they usually distinguish between a human being in the loop, approving each specific engagement, being on the loop, monitoring and able to intervene, and being out of the loop. The strange thing is that actual military practice seems to mix these in ways that don't obviously fit a single principle. For offensive strikes, say a drone firing a missile at a suspected militant, there is normally a requirement that a human operator authorize the specific lethal action, often after watching live video. The machine can find and track a target, but a person has to make the final call. At the same time, navies have for decades used close-in weapons systems like Phalanx that, once switched on, automatically detect incoming missiles and fire at them, because the engagement window is too short for any per-shot human decision. The human involvement happened earlier: choosing the rules, setting the system's firing arcs and thresholds, positioning and arming it.

What view of meaningful human control does this combination of practices presuppose? Does it presuppose that control can legitimately live upstream, in the design, parameters, and rules of engagement rather than in moment-by-moment approval? If so, what would stop that same upstream control from justifying a pre-authorized autonomous offensive system? If not, the defensive systems seem to violate the principle. Or maybe the practice really presupposes a moral asymmetry between defensive and offensive force, or between shooting down objects like missiles and killing persons like militants. Does that asymmetry actually do the philosophical work, and how does it square with the fact that offensive counter-battery fire against an enemy artillery position also targets an object but still generally gets routed through a human decision? I'm especially interested in what the practice strictly presupposes versus what it merely happens to be compatible with, because I suspect there are several rival theories that could all license the same two practices.
