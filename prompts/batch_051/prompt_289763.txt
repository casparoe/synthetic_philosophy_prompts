For an article I am writing on the physics and philosophy of time, I have been reading popular treatments of special relativity, and one book gives the following account of what it calls the Rietdijk–Putnam argument. I would like to know whether this account is fair before I rely on it.

The book's summary, as I understand it: Special relativity shows that simultaneity is relative. Two observers moving relative to one another slice spacetime differently, so an event one observer classifies as happening now can lie in another observer's future or past. Since no inertial frame is physically privileged, there is no objective, universe-wide now dividing the past from the future. Now take any event you think of as lying in your future — say, a supernova in Andromeda that, in your frame, occurs tomorrow. There is some possible observer, moving fast enough in the right direction, for whom that supernova is occurring at this very moment. Because that observer's frame is just as valid as yours, the event must already be real. It follows that all events, past and future, are equally real; the future is already fixed; and the flow of time, with its special present moment, is an illusion. The book presents this as a decisive refutation of the common-sense view that only the present exists.

I am inclined to accept the physics, but the philosophical conclusions strike me as stronger than the physics alone can support. I would like a committed audit of this account, not a survey of positions. Please organize your answer as follows: (1) What the summary gets right (about 25 percent of your response); (2) Where it goes wrong — the subtle misstatements, conflations, or overstatements (about 40 percent); (3) The strongest reply available to someone defending the common-sense view (about 20 percent); and (4) Your bottom-line verdict on whether the argument as summarized refutes that view, and how much of the book's larger claim about a fixed future and an illusory flow of time survives correction (about 15 percent). I would rather you take a definite position and defend it than lay out the options and leave me to choose.
