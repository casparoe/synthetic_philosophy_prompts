A friend offered me this capsule of the Dennett–Caruso exchange: “Dennett defends retributive punishment on the grounds that determinism poses no threat to moral responsibility, because people act from their own beliefs and desires; once someone has the relevant capacities, they genuinely deserve blame and punishment when they misuse them. Caruso replies that science shows crime is caused by genes, environment, and prior circumstances, so no one is ever truly deserving; punishment is therefore legitimate only as a public-health measure—quarantining or rehabilitating dangerous people to protect society—never as payback.” I suspect this is mostly faithful but not entirely. Go through it piece by piece and say which parts each author would accept, which are simplifications or distortions, and whether the conclusion “never as payback” follows from anything Caruso actually argues. In particular, say whether the summary trades on a slide between “caused” and “not responsible,” and whether it unfairly turns Caruso’s rejection of basic desert into a denial of all responsibility.
