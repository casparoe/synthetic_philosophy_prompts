I'm working on a novel where a secondary character is a "certified epistemic wellness coach" who repackages C. S. Peirce's four methods of fixing belief as a suite of retreats and apps, and I keep returning to Peirce's "The Fixation of Belief" for material. This passage gets right at the man for me: "Few persons care to study logic, because everybody conceives himself to be proficient enough in the art of reasoning already. But I observe that this satisfaction is limited to one's own ratiocination, and does not extend to that of other men." I'd like you to write an Onion-style news article, headline plus 400–500 words, reporting that a local man has built a "tenacity pod" in his garage—essentially a soundproofed information environment that keeps his beliefs maximally fixed by screening out anything that might irritate his doubt. The jokes should land for someone who actually knows Peirce: the method of tenacity's collision with the social impulse, the comfort of belief as a calm state, maybe a neighbor trying to upsell him on the method of authority, or an a-priori meetup that disbands because they can't agree on the self-evident starting points. Keep it deadpan, satirize the practice rather than any real person, and include at least one quote from a concerned fallibilist neighbor who has actually read Peirce and thinks the whole thing is a category error. A typo or two is fine, but the Peircean details need to be right enough that a specialist would nod rather than wince.
