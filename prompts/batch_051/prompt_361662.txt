I'm a PhD student in philosophy and psychology, and the prof running this year's upper-level moral psychology seminar let me draft one final-exam question. We spent three weeks on the WEIRD folk-morality literature (ultimatum game offers across societies, third-party punishment, variation in how much intent matters for blame) and then two weeks on whether that kind of cross-cultural variation in moral judgment threatens moral objectivity. I want an essay question that cant be answered by just reciting 'different cultures disagree, therefore morality is relative.' It should force students to (i) say exactly what evidential role the variation is supposed to play in a debunking argument against objective moral truths, (ii) assess whether the actual WEIRD findings supply what that role requires, and (iii) take seriously the measurement-invariance worry that games normed on WEIRD samples may not measure the same thing in other societies. Please write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme on a 20-point scale. The marking scheme should list the specific moves that separate an A from a B answer and name the misunderstanding behind each lost mark — for example, treating disagreement as by itself a refutation of objectivity, or treating behavior in an economic game as a transparent readout of someone's moral principles. The question should be doable in 90 minutes and self-contained enough that a student who knows the seminar readings but not some particular niche study can still get full marks.
