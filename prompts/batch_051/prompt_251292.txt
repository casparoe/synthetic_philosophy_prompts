I'm a PhD student in music cognition and I keep getting tangled up in the "sad music" puzzle. I've been reading the classic debate between appearance emotionalism and persona theories—how an instrumental piece can be sad even though nobody in the situation is occurrently sad. Here's a variant I haven't seen anyone work through. Start with the standard sad-music case: a slow descending minor melody with appoggiaturas. Now suppose the same sequence is produced by a neural network trained only on nonhuman sounds—rainfall, traffic noise, whale calls—with no human speech, no human music, no emotion labels. Let's stipulate that expert listeners reliably describe the output as sad in the musical sense, not just "it makes me sad." Does this break the resemblance-to-human-expressive-behavior story, or can that story still accommodate it? And what does a persona theory say when the composer/generator has no body, no intentions, no gesture repertoire? I want to know whether the existing analyses can absorb the case without gerrymandering, or whether the case exposes a hidden assumption. Work through the main options.
