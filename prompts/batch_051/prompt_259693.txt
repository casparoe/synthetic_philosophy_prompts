I've come to accept the standard modern exorcism of Maxwell's demon: the demon cannot systematically convert heat into work without an offsetting cost elsewhere, because the information it gathers about molecular positions must be physically encoded, and erasing that record to close the cycle dissipates at least the Landauer bound, so the second law holds for the composite system of gas, demon, and memory. I want to know how exposed this position really is. What would have to be true for the demon to win after all? Work through the load-bearing assumptions — that logically irreversible operations carry a thermodynamic cost, that measurement necessarily leaves a physical record, that Shannon information and thermodynamic entropy are connected in the way the exorcism requires, that erasure rather than acquisition is the bottleneck — and say what the world or a well-designed experiment would have to look like for each to fail. Distinguish the live escape routes from the merely logically possible ones, say which objections a defender of the exorcism would concede as genuinely awkward, and give an honest assessment of whether the position has been insulated into unfalsifiability because any apparent violation can always be redescribed as hidden memory. If so, is that insulation a defect, or is it what a principled limit on possible machines should look like?
