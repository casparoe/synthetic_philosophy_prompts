I'm a novelist, and I keep circling back to Borges's "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius" in relation to the Twin Earth thought experiment. In Twin Earth, the idea is that what's in your head is identical but the meaning of "water" differs because the external stuff is different — meaning isn't in the head. But Tlön seems to be a world where the external stuff is, if anything, more in the head than ours: the inhabitents hold Berkeleyan idealism, they treat the world as a series of mental acts, and one of their languages has no nouns. So here's what I want worked out. Read Borges's story through the lens of semantic externalism, but not as a neat illustration. Does Tlön actually support externalism — showing that a changed metaphysics changes meaning — or does it undercut it, because Tlön's reality is presented as encyclopedically manufactured, a fiction that leaks into the world? Especially the ending, where Tlönic objects start appearing in our world and people become more Tlönic. If a world can be authored, what happens to the externalist's claim that meanings are anchored in a world we don't make? Also, the language without nouns: could a Twin Earth style argument even get off the ground there, or is the no-noun language a reductio of the idea that meaning depends on anything external? I want you to be honest about where the reading breaks down, not just where it fits.
