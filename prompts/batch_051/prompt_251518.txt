I have been trying to think through whether the ordinary freedom to quit a job already contains something like a right to strike, and I keep going in circles on the following argument. (1) An individual worker is normally free to quit, even when quitting imposes serious costs on an employer, just as an employer is normally free to replace a worker who leaves. (2) A strike, whatever else it involves, is at bottom a group of workers doing together what each is individually free to do: withholding their labor. (3) If each of several people is free to do something, then their doing it together by prior agreement does not by itself make the act wrong. (4) Therefore, a strike cannot be wrong merely because it is coordinated or because it hurts the employer; if it is wrong, it must be wrong for some further reason, such as violence, breaking a contract, or endangering third parties. I suspect step 2 or step 3 is quietly changing the subject, but I cannot make the objection precise. Is the argument valid? If not, exactly which step fails, and on what reading of it? If it is valid, what exactly does it establish?
