I've been loosing sleep over this ever since a conversation with a friend about whether an AI could ever actually feel pain, and I'd like to see the sharpest version of the back-and-forth rather than another summary. The specific corner I'm stuck in is epiphenomenalism about conscious experience: the view that the felt, what-it's-like qualities of pain, color, and so on are caused by physical processes in the brain but have no causal effects of their own — like a whistle on a steam engine, produced by the machine but not driving any of the wheels. The puzzle is what people call the paradox of phenomenal judgment. My conviction that I'm conscious, or that a particular pain feels a certain way, is itself a physical event in my brain; if epiphenomenalism is true, that conviction has to be caused entirely by other physical events, and the felt quality I'm supposedly reporting on didn't cause my report about it. That seems to cut off the one piece of evidence that made consciousness seem undeniable in the first place — the fact that I can just tell, from the inside, that there is something it's like. But maybe that's too quick: the same physical causes that produce the feeling could also produce the judgment, keeping the two in sync without the feeling doing any work. I can't decide whether this saves the view or quietly gives the game away.
