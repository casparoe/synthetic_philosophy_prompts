In the Zhuangzi, in the chapter conventionally known as Qi wu lun and rendered under titles such as The Adjustment of Controversies or On the Equality of Things, there are two adjacent reflections on dreaming that seem to me to pull in opposite directions. Earlier in the chapter, the text observes that the person in the middle of a dream does not know that he is dreaming, and it then speaks of what is often translated as the great awakening: an awakening after which we will know that this present life was itself a great dream. The passage appears to hold out a standpoint—not the one we currently occupy—from which the dream-like character of ordinary experience can be recognised for what it is. Yet the chapter closes with the butterfly passage: Zhuang Zhou dreams he is a butterfly, fluttering about; on waking, he cannot decide whether he was Zhuang Zhou who dreamt he was a butterfly, or whether he is a butterfly now dreaming that he is Zhuang Zhou.

I am not asking for a general introduction to Zhuangzi, nor for a bibliography. I am after a precise account of the relation between these two passages. On its face, the first seems to promise that there is a final epistemic state in which the dream-status of this life becomes known, while the second seems to leave the distinction between dreaming and waking permanently undecidable from inside experience. Can these be reconciled, and at what cost? To answer this well, I would expect a discussion of which of the following readings, or which combination of them, is most faithful to the text: the great awakening is not something we can reach while still living; the butterfly passage is itself an example of the ordinary waking that fails to settle anything, and so points toward the need for a higher awakening; the butterfly passage deliberately subverts the earlier apparent confidence; or the two passages use waking and dreaming in different senses that the text never explicitly distinguishes. Please lay out the strongest versions of the main options, say what each has to assume, and give a verdict on which reconciliation is the least strained and which would amount to special pleading.
