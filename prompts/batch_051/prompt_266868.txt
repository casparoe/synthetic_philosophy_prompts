Write a dialogue between a Dutch book advocate and a critic of Dutch book arguments for probabilism. The advocate's position: any degrees of belief that violate the probability axioms are irrational, because somebody holding them can be offered a set of bets, each one fair by that person's own lights, that together guarantee a net loss. The critic's position: that at most reveals something about an idealized betting psychology, and the argument secretly assumes you can't refuse bets or fail to value a bundle the way you value its parts. Make them fight mainly over that last assumption—the package principle, the idea that if each bet in a bundle looks fair to you individually, the bundle should look acceptable too. Both should make sharp, non-obvious moves, respond to what the other actually said, and concede minor points without giving ground. No name-dropping or citations. Write exactly five exchanges, numbered 1 through 5, each speaker getting one or two sentences per exchange, with no introduction or conclusion.
