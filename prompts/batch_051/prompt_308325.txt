Suppose that within roughly the next several decades a costly, safety-reducing competitive race to build transformative AI occurs between two or more actors—labs, firms, or states—that are at least roughly instrumentally rational and that share an interest in avoiding the race's costs. Fearon's rationalist account of war holds that rational actors should normally prefer a bargain to fighting, so war requires one of three mechanisms: private information with incentives to misrepresent, commitment problems, or issue indivisibilities. What is your credence that, conditional on such an AI race occurring, at least one of these three mechanisms (or a close structural analog) plays an ineliminable role in the best explanation of the race, as against an explanation that rests instead on bounded rationality or systematic miscalculation, organizational or agency pathologies, or some factor outside this framing? Do not answer with "it depends." Give a number and a decomposition: state your probability for each candidate explanatory factor, say how you handle overlap or joint operation, show how the terms combine into the final credence, and identify the single term to which the final number is most sensitive along with the evidence about AI development or strategic behavior that would move it most. If you think the war–race analogy makes the question ill-posed, or that "best explanation" is too indeterminate to price, say so plainly and answer the nearest well-posed version instead. End your answer with a line of the form \answer{0.XX} giving your final credence.
