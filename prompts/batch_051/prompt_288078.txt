I work as a software engineer on a consumer LLM assistant, and one feature of our alignment pipeline keeps bothering me. We run RLHF in the usual way: several thousand contractors compare pairs of model outputs, their judgments are pooled into a single reward model, and that one reward model then steers the same assistant for every user. When contractors disagree, the disagreement is treated as noise to be averaged out, so a contractor whose values are in the minority simply gets outvoted and leaves no trace on the model's behavior. The design seems to take for granted that the proper target for an aligned model is a kind of majority-rule aggregation of all raters' preferences, with each rater weighted equally. I want to know whether that assumption is actually forced on us and what it commits us to. Reconstruct the implicit theory of whose values such a system should serve, carefully distinguishing what the pipeline strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with. Then say where the design is philosophically underdetermined, and what would concretely have to change in the pipeline if we instead wanted the assistant to remain neutral between users' conflicting values, or to prioritize the users who are currently worst served rather than maximize overall preference satisfaction.
