ok so ive been trying to settle a debate with myself about whether the effort part of doing something hard matters on its own, or whether its just the price you pay for the result. i came up with a test and i want someone to tell me if its any good.

I call it the Pill Test. Pick a hard thing you care about doing: training for a marathon, learning a hard piece on piano, building your own table, whatever. Now imagine that right before you get there someone offers you a pill. The pill gives you the finished outcome — the fitness, the skill, the medal or whatever, people treating you like you earned it, and your own memories of all the struggle, down to the soreness and the days you wanted to quit. The only thing the pill cant give you is the struggle actually having happened. After you take it your life is exactly as good as it wouldve been if youd done the work, except the doing never occurred.

My rule is this: if youd take the pill, then the effort was just a cost you were paying for the outcome. If youd refuse the pill, then you think the actual doing was worth something in itself, over and above everything it got you.

I think if people are honest most of them would take the pill, which would mean effort is almost never valuable for its own sake. But I also think the people who refuse arent really valuing effort either — theyre valuing not having fake memories, or wanting to be the real cause of the thing, or something like that. So effort still comes out worthless.

Is the test actually fair? Does it measure whether someone values effort for its own sake, or is it rigged from the start? Could someone value effort for itself and still take the pill? And is my move of explaining away everyone who refuses it too cheap? What is the best case that the Pill Test is really measuring something else entirely, like wanting to be the author of your life or caring about the truth? Basically I want to know if ive actually shown something about effort, or just built a thought experiment that flatters a view i already had.
