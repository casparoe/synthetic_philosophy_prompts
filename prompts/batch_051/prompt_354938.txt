I live in a small co-op building that kind of wears its strictness as a point of pride. Our house rules, adopted in 1985, include this clause:

"For the quiet enjoyment of all residents, no resident shall play any musical instrument or operate any sound-producing device between 11:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m. if the sound is plainly audible in another unit."

A new resident has one of those electronic drum kits and practices at midnight with headphones on. There is no drum sound in the hallway, but the kick pedal sends a low thump through the floor, and the woman below says she feels it more than she hears it, but it still keeps her up. The board is genuinely stuck, because the rule was written when practicing an instrument basically meant making noise you could hear with your ears.

Could you work through what the rule requires here? Specifically, walk through what the words say, what the purpose of the clause suggests, what the 1985 drafters would probably have said if asked, and what a reasonable resident reading the rule today would expect. Then tell me which approach should win and why, and whether this building is better off as a tight community that enforces the text literally or a loose one that follows the spirit of the rule when technology creates a case the rule didn't anticipate. End with \answer{violation} or \answer{no violation}.
