In mechanistic anomaly detection there's a real question about what "same mechanism" means. Two candidate answers: (a) the parts-and-arrangement criterion—same mechanism iff the same components are causally wired together in the same topology; (b) the difference-making criterion—same mechanism iff it has the same intervention signature, i.e. when you intervene on the upstream variables, the downstream variables change in the same way. These mostly agree, but I'm interested in where they come apart, and specifically in the hard direction: a case where the parts-and-arrangement criterion says same mechanism but the difference-making criterion says different mechanism. (The easy direction is just multiple realizability—a digital thermometer and a mercury thermometer—so don't give me that.)
