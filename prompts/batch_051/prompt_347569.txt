I have retired from a career in the natural sciences and have lately been working through Frankfurt's account of bullshit. As I read him, the liar must know the truth well enough to conceal it, whereas the bullshitter is simply indifferent to whether what he says is true, because his real aim is to shape the impression he leaves. I have been trying to apply this to large language models. When a model confidently emits a falsehood, it matches one half of the account: the generation process is sensitive to statistical plausibility rather than truth. But the other half is missing, since no agent is behind the output presenting himself as trying to get things right. So I have coined a term I would like assessed: 'agentless bullshit.' By it I mean text with the outward marks of bullshit — fluent confidence, indifference to contrary evidence, smooth handling of challenge — produced by a system that takes no stance toward the truth at all. For example, a model that cites a nonexistent study in support of a claim and, when challenged, smoothly substitutes another nonexistent study is neither lying (it knows no truth to conceal) nor erring (no truth-aiming process was in play), yet it reproduces the bullshitter's surface perfectly. My question is whether this is a coherent fourth category alongside lying, error, and bullshit. Does the idea already go by some name I should know? And what are the three strongest objections to it — with a judgment as to whether any is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable?
