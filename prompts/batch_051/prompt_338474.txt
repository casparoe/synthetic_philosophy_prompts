I've been reading through the free-will material that circulates in rationalist forums — the threads on whether "could have done otherwise" matters, the compatibilism-versus-hard-determinism debates, and the recurring accusation that the whole fight is over the word "free." In one of those discussions, a friend who is normally the most careful reader in our group gave me his summary of Galen Strawson's Basic Argument, and I can't tell whether it is fair. His summary, roughly as he put it:

"Strawson's Basic Argument is that you can't be morally responsible for what you do because you didn't choose the way you are. Your actions flow from your character, and your character was produced by your genes, upbringing, and circumstances, so nothing about you is ultimately self-authored. He's careful to note that the argument does not depend on determinism, since adding objective randomness wouldn't make you the author of yourself either. The upshot is that nobody is ever truly deserving of praise, blame, or punishment; and because our practices of holding people accountable and punishing them presuppose exactly this desert, those practices are unjustified and ought to be abandoned."

I'm inclined to think the final step is overreach and that the summary is quietly dropping a distinction, but I can't articulate it well enough to trust myself, and I don't want my disagreement to be motivated reasoning. Please audit this summary for me. Which parts would a sympathetic and accurate reader of Strawson accept? Which parts are subtly wrong, overstated, or a conflation of two different claims? Is there a distinction at the center of the Basic Argument that the summary is missing, and does the practical conclusion about accountability and punishment follow from the accurate parts? I'm not looking for a reading list or citations; I just want a careful accuracy audit of the summary itself.
