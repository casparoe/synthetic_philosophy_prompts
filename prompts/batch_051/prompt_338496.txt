I’m a high school student writing a paper for a biology class on animal behavior, and I’ve been trying to pin down what I actually think about when it’s legitimate to attribute cognition to an animal. The standard rule we learned is Morgan’s canon, which I understand roughly as follows: don’t explain an animal’s behavior by a higher psychological capacity if a lower one would do. But when I apply that rule to my own intuitive judgments, I get a mess, so I want to know what principle, if any, actually captures them.

Here are my verdicts. A dog standing at the back door and whining at its usual evening time is, I think, wanting to go out; I don’t feel any need to reduce this to a learned association between a full bladder and the door. A crow that bends a piece of wire into a hook to lift a bucket from a tube seems to me to be problem-solving, though I stop short of saying it had a plan before acting. An octopus that removes an unfamiliar lid from a jar to reach a crab seems to me to be genuinely dealing with an obstacle, not just mechanically repeating a reinforced prying motion. A parrot that says want nut only when nuts are present seems to me to be requesting, not just emitting a sound that has been rewarded in the past. But when a jumping spider takes a detour around a gap to reach prey it can see, I don’t want to say it planned the route, even though the detour was not previously rewarded. And I’m genuinely torn about a dog that picks up its leash and drops it at my feet when I’ve been in a bad mood: I want to say it is trying to comfort me, but that feels like a much bigger claim than the ones I made about the crow and the parrot.

Is there a single account of when to attribute desire, intention, problem-solving, or understanding that delivers all of these verdicts, or at least most of them? If not, which of my verdicts should I overturn? I’d especially like to know whether the octopus and spider cases can be kept together, and what the resulting principle would say about a new case: a cleaner wrasse that seems to reconcile with a client fish after a conflict. Please state the principle precisely, tie each clause to one of my cases, and say explicitly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate. Also, please tell me how confident you are in the account you give, and what point you are least sure of.
