i'd like the joint statement that a generalist and a particularist about conspiracy theories would produce in a good-faith adversarial collaboration. By generalist i mean the view that there is a standing epistemic defect in conspiracy theories as a class—not that every one of them is false, but that the category itself gives a rational person a defeasible reason to lower their confidence, maybe because these accounts are typically unfalsifiable or come out of crippled epistemologies. By particularist i mean the view that each conspiracy theory has to be judged on its own evidence, and "conspiracy theory" is mostly a pejorative social label rather than an epistemically useful kind.

I want the document itself, not commentary on it—begin with the piece, no preface.

The document should have three parts. First, a numbered agreed-claims section. Make it substantive rather than a pile of truisms: include at least one claim about a real, documented conspiracy of the Watergate or Tuskegee sort, and at least one claim about what makes belief in a conspiracy epistemically bad in a particular case. Second, a disagreements table: each row gives the generalist's position and the particularist's position, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording, plus a one-line note on what the disagreement actually turns on. Third, a cruxes section: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, give a concrete case, test, or kind of evidence both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts.

I suspect two of the cruxes will be (a) whether there's a non-pejorative definition of "conspiracy theory" that tracks an epistemically relevant kind, and (b) what the right reference class is for judging the track record of official versus unofficial accounts. Handle the definitional crux carefull: if part of their dispute is merely verbal, say exactly which part and why.
