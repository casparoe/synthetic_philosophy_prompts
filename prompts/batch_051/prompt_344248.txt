I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and I am working on a piece about whether self-deception survives close analysis or collapses into something more ordinary. The strict problem is this: deceiving someone normally involves a deceiver who knows the truth and a target who holds a false belief, so when one mind plays both roles, the description seems to require that the person believe a proposition and its negation at the same moment. A satisfying account should either show why that apparent contradiction is only apparent, or concede that strict self-deception is impossible while explaining why the everyday phenomenon is still real in a weaker but important sense.

Here are five candidate ways through the puzzle, stated neutrally. The intentionalist account holds that self-deception is a genuine act of lying to oneself, made possible because the mind is divided enough that one part keeps the truth from another. The compartmentalization account holds that the person holds the two incompatible beliefs in mutually insulated cognitive subsystems that do not exchange information. The deflationary account holds that the phenomenon is real but misdescribed: it is motivated, one-sided processing of evidence that produces a false belief without any simultaneous knowledge of the truth. The temporal account holds that the two states are never simultaneous, because the person first knows the unwelcome truth and later brings herself to believe the opposite through motivated handling of the evidence. The social-performance account holds that what we call self-deception is really the management of a public self-presentation, and the paradoxical reading arises because we mistake that performance for a private inner lie.

Rank these five, from the account that best preserves a substantive notion of self-deception — one that does not quietly reduce it to ordinary bias, error, or impression management — to the account that does the least explanatory work. Give me exactly five numbered items, in rank order, one or two sentences per item, saying why each account lands where it does. No introduction and no conclusion.
