I do philanthropic advising for people who've sold a company or come into money, and the same fight keeps happening in every client meeting: one part of the family says you'd be rational to use the money for your own happiness and security, and the other part says you'd be just as rational to use it where it does the most good overall, and then they stare at me like I'm supposed to settle it. I know this is basically Sidgwick's dualism of practical reason, but I can't turn the theory into something I can actually run in a ninety-minute meeting. Write me a reusable decision procedure for when a donor can justifiably follow self-interest and when the impartial, do-the-most-good point of view should win, including what to do when they demand different things. It should be a checklist a non-philosopher can follow in order: the questions to ask, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure should stop with a no, and where it should hand the decision back as an unreduced values call. Then run it on three cases: a founder deciding whether to sell a company he knows will be run worse for society after the sale; a donor who wants to give mostly to their alma mater's new gym rather than where the money would do the most good; and a family that wants to keep enough wealth that their kids never have to work. Be honest about where a Sidgwick-based procedure is going to misfire or run out of road, especially where both sides really are requirements of practical reason and the procedure is just relabeling the conflict.
