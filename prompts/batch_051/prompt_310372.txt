Here's an embarrassing one from a philosophy professor. I teach the moral status debates—capacity to suffer, preferences, being a subject of a life, all that—but my gut verdicts on concrete cases still won't line up behind a single principle. Maybe you can do the reflective-equilibrium work for me.

Here's where my gut lands:

- Dog: I'd wade into a cold pond and miss office hours to save one, and I'd also say failing to would be wrong.
- Salmon: I eat them and don't feel guilty, but the way they thrash on a line bothers me, and I'd support stricter stunning rules even though I wouldn't march for salmon.
- Octopus: watching one work out a puzzle in a tank, it felt wrong to treat it as a lower animal; I'd put it near the dog, even though it's an invertebrate and I can't read its face.
- Honeybee: I swat it at a picnic without much guilt, but the idea of somebody pulling the wings off one for fun makes me a little sick, and I can't say why the second matters if the first doesn't.
- Shrimp: I've dropped them into boiling water without a second thought, but if they turn out to feel pain I'd be horrified; I genuinely don't know.
- Ant: stepping on one doesn't register at all.
- Thermostat: it "wants" 68 degrees, but obviously nothing—except I'm not sure how to say the bee differs from it in kind rather than degree.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? The live candidates I keep reaching for are (a) capacity to suffer, (b) having preferences or a point of view, (c) being a unified self with a life that goes better or worse. Run my cases through each, defend the best one or a hybrid with clear clauses, and tell me what it says about the cases I keep going back and forth on: a lobster, a venus flytrap, and a lab-grown human brain organoid that shows seizure-like electrical bursts but, so far as anyone can tell, no integrated consciousness. Then say honestly which of my verdicts has to go.

Give me exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no introduction and no conclusion: (1) the principle stated precisely, (2) which of my verdicts it captures and which it forces me to abandon, (3) the lobster, (4) the organoid, (5) the flytrap and the thermostat together, and wether the bee-to-thermostat gap is a difference in kind or just degree.
