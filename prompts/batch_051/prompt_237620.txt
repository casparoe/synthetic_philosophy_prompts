1. its only fair to bind someone to a rule if they had a genuine chance to find out it existed and what it required.
2. courts are basically right to say ignorance of the law is no excuse, and i dont think a legal system could work without that as the default.
3. some modern offences feel unjust in exactly this way: think of someone filling in a pond on their own land without knowing they needed a permit from an agency theyd never heard of.
4. most of the unfairness would go away if the state published every rule in plain English, kept it in one seperate searchable register, and gave people a short grace period after new rules came in.
5. in genuinely borderline cases where even careful people couldnt tell what the law required, its still fine for the person whose case loses to carry the cost, because some dispute has to be decided and someone has to end up stuck with the outcome.
6. whether someone "couldnt have known" should be judged by what a reasonable person in their situation would have known, not by what they actually knew.
