For the closing meeting of a small reading group on Buridan's ass and the possibility of choosing between exactly equal alternatives, I have been asked to open the discussion with a poem instead of a paper. The problem became vivid for me earlier this year, when I had to choose between two offers that remained indistinguishable after weeks of scrutiny, and I noticed that every piece of advice I received quietly imported a reason I did not actually possess. I want the poem to carry the argument in its form, not decorate the argument with verse.

Write a strict villanelle. The two refrains should name the two equal alternatives — the traditional two heaps of hay are acceptable, or they may be transposed to two indistinguishable offers, envelopes, or paths — and the refrains should be metrically and semantically interchangeable enough that swapping them would leave the poem nearly unchanged. The form must do structural work: the refrains alternate as the closing lines of the tercets and finally stand side by side as the last two lines, so that the oscillation between the two alternatives and the shrinking space left to the chooser are one and the same movement. Observe the full refrain and rhyme scheme exactly (nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain); do not merely approximate the form.

In substance, the poem should work through the dialectic rather than name-drop it, at a level a philosopher of action or a decision theorist would recognize: the opening demand that a choice be backed by a reason; the hidden-preference answer, which discovers a difference only after the fact; the uncaused-will answer, which breaks the tie without any further determining reason and thereby raises the worry that the choice has become opaque to the chooser; and the indifference answer, which treats selecting among tied options as unproblematic but leaves open whether that selection was a deed or merely an event. The closing couplet should not pretend that the last of these dissolves the puzzle. It should show, in the verse itself, why each move either smuggles in a difference or relocates the difficulty to a new place. Do not assert what Buridan himself held, and write so that a specialist recognizes the positions while a stranger to the problem still finds it a good poem.

After the poem, give a single JSON object with exactly these fields and nothing else:

- "title": the poem's title, as a string.
- "form_notes": a string saying which received features of the villanelle were kept and whether any were deliberately bent.
- "refrain_a": the first refrain line exactly as it appears.
- "refrain_b": the second refrain line exactly as it appears.
- "interchangeability": one sentence explaining what makes the two refrains function as equal alternatives in sound and sense.
- "argument_map": an array of objects, one per stanza in order, each with keys "stanza" (a string such as "tercet 1" or "couplet"), "surface_meaning" (what the stanza says), "philosophical_move" (which move in the dialectic it makes), and "formal_role" (how the stanza's position, rhyme, or relation to the refrains carries the move).
- "commitments": an array of strings, no more than six, each a declarative claim about choice, preference, or agency to which the poem seems committed.
- "risk_notes": an array of strings, each identifying one place where a specialist might object that a contested move has been treated as settled, and saying how the poem signals its status.
