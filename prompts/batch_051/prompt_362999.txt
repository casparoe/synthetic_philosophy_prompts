Here's a tricky one I'd like you to try. Construct a thought experiment in which a mens rea requirement ends up making the defendent's punishment worse than a strict-liability alternative would have—the opposite of the usual direction, since we normally think of strict liability as the harsh option and mens rea as the thing that protects defendants. The scenario should be realistic: a workplace, a product, a regulatory matter, not a trolley. Keep it to one paragraph. The mechanism can't be that the mens rea statute is drafted badly or that the prosecutor is being vindictive; it should fall out of the ordinary logic of charging and proof. Also, don't cheat by making the defendant secretly blameworthy in a way the strict-liability rule would miss—that would just make the mens rea outcome the correct one. After you give me the scenario, walk through what each rule says about the case and why, and tell me which feature of the case is doing the work. Then, briefly, say what the case is for, what happens if you remove that feature, and how a defender of mens rea requirements would attack it.
