Draft the joint statement that a committed Nozickian entitlement theorist and a committed advocate of patterned, end-state distributive principles (call the latter the "pattern theorist") would co-sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration centered on the Wilt Chamberlain example.

Open the statement with a short fixing of the example so the document is self-contained: a distribution is stipulated just, by whatever standard the two sides can accept for the sake of the argument; fans freely pay a small additional amount to watch Wilt Chamberlain play; Wilt ends up much richer than anyone else, with no coercion, fraud, or broken promise.

Then produce the following sections.

1. A numbered list of agreed claims. These must be substantive, not vacuous. Address at least: what the example's setup stipulates and what features the initial and resulting distributions have; what the entitlement theory is committed to and what it is not; what a patterned or end-state principle requires and why the Wilt case is supposed to put pressure on it; and what the example, even on its most favorable reading, does not by itself establish. State plainly where the force of the argument is meant to come from—the inference from a just starting holding and voluntary transfers to a just resulting holding.

2. A table of residual disagreements. For each row, phrase the disagreement twice: the wording the entitlement theorist would accept for their own side, and the wording the pattern theorist would accept for theirs. Neither side may be a caricature. Pay close attention to how the two sides use "voluntary," "just acquisition," "pattern," and "choice," and flag any place where a disagreement partly dissolves once the term is fixed.

3. A crux section. For each genuine disagreement, identify the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side. For each crux, give a concrete case, extension, or piece of evidence that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about that case. Include as cruxes only those both parties would accept as genuine; consider questions such as whether the voluntariness of a transfer is sufficient to transmit the justice of the initial holding to the resulting one, whether a distribution's failing to match any forbidden pattern matters independently of how it arose, and how the example's force changes once the starting distribution itself arose from past injustice.

4. A closing note identifying any parts of the dispute the collaboration concluded were merely verbal, with the paraphrase under which the apparent disagreement disappears.

The document should read as a text both parties can sign without reservation; where they cannot sign together, the table should make the residual disagreement exact enough that one could say in advance which results would move whom.
