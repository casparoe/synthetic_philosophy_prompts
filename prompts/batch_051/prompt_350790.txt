Ok here's a cleaned-up Taurek-style rescue case: you have one dose of a life-saving drug. A needs the whole dose to live; B, C, D, E, and F each need one fifth, and if you split the drug among them A dies. Everyone is a stranger, no special obligations, no downstream effects, all otherwise healthy, and you have to act now. What's your actual credence that giving the whole dose to A and letting the five die is morally permissible (all-things-considered, not just excusable)? I dont want "it depends on your moral theory" — give me a number and decompose it. How much rides on whether harms can be aggregated across persons, how much on whether the separateness of persons actually blocks "five deaths is worse than one," how much on whether "permissible" is even the right verdict in a case where somebody dies either way, and how much on whether you should instead give each of the six an equal chance — and whether that means a fair coin between A and the five or a weighted lottery with one-sixth each. Then say which sub-question would move the number most if you learned you were wrong about it, and what your new credence would be.
