Locke thought personal identity consists in sameness of consciousness, not sameness of soul or body — hence his prince-and-cobbler case, where a prince's consciousness put into a cobbler's body makes the resulting person the prince. Now feed him Parfit's teletransporter: it scans your exact brain state, destroys the original, and builds a perfect duplicate on Mars who wakes up with all the same apparent memories and dispositions. Would Locke count the Mars person as you? The tricky bit is whether his sameness-of-consciousness criterion requires the very consciousness to be genuinely extended backward into your past, or whether identical memory-content does the work. And would he be forced, like Parfit, to separate identity from what matters in survival, or can he keep them fused? Lay out his options and say which you think he'd take.
