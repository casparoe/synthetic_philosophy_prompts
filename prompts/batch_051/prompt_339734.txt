I'm a retired physicist — fair warning, I keep reaching for conserved quantities — and I've got a homegrown idea I'm calling the conservation of desert. The claim is that desert is never created or destroyed, only transfered or converted. If a worker deserves her wages, the desert doesn't spring into being on payday; it's transferred from the people her work benefits. If a criminal deserves punishment, the desert flows from the wrongdoer to the wronged party or the community. Luck looks like the obvious refutation: two farmers do the same work, one field floods and the other doesn't — hasn't desert just been created for one and destroyed for the other? I want to say no: their desert was identical all along, and what changed is only what it can be redeemed for. I know this might just be Nozick's entitlement theory wearing a physics costume, and I know Rawls says talents are morally arbitrary. Is the conservation principle a real principle or just a metaphor? What does it get right, and where does it break hardest? And whatever you conclude, tell me what argument or counterexample would change your mind, and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
