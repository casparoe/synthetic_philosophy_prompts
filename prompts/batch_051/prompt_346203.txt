Please draft the joint statement that a principled defender of tight social norms and a principled defender of loose social norms could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on how much rule-following a society should demand. The tight camp holds that a well-ordered society requires widely shared, reliably enforced norms and that deviance is presumptively costly; the loose camp holds that society should tolerate a wide range of deviance and enforce only a small core of norms, because individual discretion and cultural variety are themselves goods and heavy enforcement breeds rigidity and exclusion. Format the deliverable as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. It should include: (1) substantive claims that both camps can accept about what social norms do, the costs of both under- and over-enforcement, and the conditions under which each side concedes the other has a point; (2) a table of the residual disagreements, each stated precisely enough that the relevant camp's own adherents would accept the wording, with no vacuous or slanted formulations; and (3) between three and five cruxes, each a sub-question such that settling it would genuinely move at least one camp, together with, for each crux, a concrete observation, natural experiment, intervention, or argument that both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Go beyond the obvious point that the two camps assign different weights to the same costs: consider, for example, whether tight norms are best explained as solutions to coordination problems or as markers of group membership, whether tightness is a justified response to external threat or merely an intelligible one, and whether enforcement should target conduct only or also the attitudes expressed by nonconformity. If you judge that part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why. Keep the entire statement to no more than 700 words.
