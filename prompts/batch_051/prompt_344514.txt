I have been reading about higher-order evidence and epistemic akrasia, and I have compressed the debate into an argument that now proves far too much. I want to know which step to reject and what rejecting it costs.

Suppose I hold a first-order body of evidence E, some testimony, for a proposition p, and suppose E on its own supports believing p to degree 0.8, which is in fact my credence. I then receive higher-order evidence H: a reliable test tells me that my handling of just this kind of testimony was very likely distorted by a factor that systematically inflates confidence. H says nothing directly about p or about the witnesses; it bears only on my assessment of E. Now consider the two possible responses. (1) If I lower my credence in p, say to 0.55, then my credence no longer reflects what my first-order evidence E supports. E still supports 0.8, since H added no new witness and contradicted nothing the witnesses said, so my belief now floats loose from the evidence I have for p. (2) But epistemic akrasia just is the state of believing p while believing that one's evidence does not support p to that degree, and epistemic akrasia is always irrational. (3) Therefore lowering my credence in response to higher-order evidence is irrational. (4) Suppose instead I keep my credence at 0.8. Then I am ignoring H, and H is plainly relevant to p: evidence that my belief-forming process was unreliable is evidence that my belief may well have misfired, and a rational agent may not simply ignore relevant evidence she possesses. (5) Therefore refusing to lower my credence in response to higher-order evidence is also irrational. (6) So every available response to higher-order evidence is irrational.

That conclusion cannot be right, given that higher-order evidence is a routine feature of scientific and everyday reasoning. Where does this argument go wrong, and what alternative am I committing myself to once I block it? I would most like to keep both the enkratic requirement that my beliefs fit my evidence and the principle that evidence about my own reliability must be able to move me, but I cannot see how both survive together.
