My insurer denied coverage for treatment of a chronic, disabling condition on the ground that my diagnostic findings are within normal limits and no underlying pathology has been identified; the insurer concludes that, absent an identified disease, I cannot be unhealthy. My physician and the patient advocate assisting me both reply that health is more than the absence of disease. I can see that this sentence is being asked to block the insurer's inference, but I cannot tell exactly what it asserts.

Please take 'health is more than the absence of disease' and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making, from the nearly trivial to the radical. For each reading, say whether it is platitudinous, defensible but controversial, or false as stated, and what would have to be true for it to hold. Then identify which reading or readings would actually support the conclusion that a person with persistent symptoms and functional limitations but no identified pathology may still be unhealthy and entitled to medical care, and say whether that conclusion follows from the reading alone or requires further normative premises. I have been reading around the distinction between naturalist and normativist accounts of health and disease, so please indicate where the various readings fall with respect to that distinction, but without organizing the answer as a bibliography or assuming I can consult the literature.
