For a paper I am writing, I have come up with a distinction that I want checked before I commit to it. I distinguish two kinds of curiosity, which I call itch curiosity and pull curiosity. Itch curiosity is the uncomfortable awareness of a gap in one's understanding: the knowledge is wanted because it closes the gap and stops the discomfort. Pull curiosity is being drawn to a subject because the subject itself seems worth understanding: not knowing feels like a loss rather than an irritation, and the knowledge is wanted as an end rather than as relief. I want to defend two claims. First, these are genuinely different attitudes and not two descriptions of the same thing. Second, only pull curiosity can support the familiar idea that knowledge is worth having for its own sake, because itch curiosity treats knowledge as medicine for an itch, which makes the knowledge only instrumentally valuable. One piece of evidence I keep coming back to is that when I am itch-curious I would accept any correct answer that closed the gap, but when I am pull-curious a correct summary that answers my question yet leaves the subject thin feels unsatisfying. I also suspect that schools mostly reward the itch variety, which is why learning for its own sake sounds hollow to my classmates. Is my distinction coherent, does it mark a real divide, and does it already exist under another name? And is my second claim valid, or does it lean too hard on the itch metaphor — for instance, could relief from an uncomfortable epistemic gap be intrinsically valuable, or could knowledge still be valued for itself even when the motive was merely to close a gap? Tell me honestly what my view gets right and where it breaks.
