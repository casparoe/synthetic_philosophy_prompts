Nietzsche's essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life splits the historical sense into monumental, antiquarian and critical modes, and a lot of the argument takes for granted that forgetting is the default and remembering is the hard, active part: you need monuments, archives, festivals and school curricula to keep the past alive. Now suppose that assumption has quietly reversed. We live with cheap, comprehensive, searchable memory, where most things never really disapear and the hard part is not recalling the past but deciding what to forget, ignore or let lapse. Take each of the three modes and say whether that reversal strengthens or undercuts it. Then say what's left of the overall argument: does it survive, does it need repair, or was it never really about the cost of storing the past at all? If it needs repair, give the repaired version and say which of the original assumptions the repair has to replace.
