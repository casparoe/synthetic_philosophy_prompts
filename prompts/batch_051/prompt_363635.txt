I am a software engineer and I sit on my company's data practices committee; I have no formal background in philosophy. Our note-taking application logs fine-grained behavioural telemetry—typing rhythm, pause length, cursor movement, and edit-and-delete patterns—and a product team now proposes to use that telemetry to infer each user's moment-to-moment hesitation, cognitive load, and attention state. These inferences would drive in-app nudges and would also be sold as aggregated "focus analytics" to a partner that builds productivity tools. Users would not be told that inferences are being made, and the processing would rest on telemetry collected for the stated purpose of "improving the product". Our posted data principle says that a user's data "belongs to the user". The product lead argues that this cannot reach inferences our models construct from lawfully collected telemetry, since users never contributed those facts and the inferences are the company's own creation. Draft the committee's short decision memo: should we approve the proposal, reject it, or approve it with conditions? Recommend a concrete course of action we could implement—including any notice, consent, or deletion requirements and a review timeframe—and engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the side you ultimately reject. Please write the memo in British English.
