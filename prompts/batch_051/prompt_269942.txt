Lately I've been convinced that the conceivability of zombies — beings physically exactly like us but with no inner experience — is actual evidence against physicalism. A friend tells me that's just confusing what I can imagine with the way things could be, and that conceivability is more about the limits of my picture-making than about possibility.

I don't want an essay defending or attacking the argument. Instead, treat the conceivability claim as something you could in principle bring data to. Design the best concrete test you can of whether this kind of conceivability tracks a real gap between physical facts and experience, or whether it's just an artifact of how we imagine minds. Be specific: what would you measure or compare, what pattern of results would count for each side, and what would a positive result still leave open. And be honest about what would change your mind, and where your confidence in that test is most likely misplaced.
