I keep trying to get clear on the ethics of small stuff — being ten minutes late, saying 'I'll read it by Friday' and then not doing it, RSVPing and bailing, the friend who says 'we should get lunch sometime' and never means it, breaking a trivial promise because something better came up. Before I read any theory of it, I want a list of what an adequate theory has to cover.

So: write the desiderata for a theory of the ethics of small commitments. By 'small commitments' I mean promises, self-imposed deadlines, appointments, and similar low-stakes undertakings, as opposed to vows, contracts, and serious promises where a lot turns on it. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say whether it's non-negotiable or just one camp's prejudice. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. Keep the focus on the criteria; you can briefly score any existing view you know at the end, but the list is the point.

In particular, I want the theory to take a stand (or explain why it doesn't need to) on at least these: whether punctuality and promising have the same underlying structure or different ones; whether the wrong of being late is the other person's lost time or the implicit message about who matters; whether 'I'll try' and 'I'll do it' are commitments of the same type or different; whether an indefinite 'let's get lunch sometime' is a real commitment at all; and whether a hundred tiny breaches can add up to something serious or each stays negligible. My hunch is that some of these force incompatible desiderata, and I'd like to know which.
