I teach an upper-level philosophy of biology seminar and need a decision tree that sorts someone into a species concept on the basis of what they say about concrete cases, not by which label they prefer. The leaves to include are the biological, phylogenetic, ecological, evolutionary, and morphological (phenetic) concepts; you may add a genotypic-cluster leaf—introduced by its defining feature, clusters in trait or genotype space with few intermediates—if the tree can earn it. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from the list of names: work out the separating questions first, note which combinations of answers are occupied, and for the unoccupied ones say whether nobody is there because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and only then locate the named positions. The cases I most want used as diagnostics are: a ring species whose terminal forms do not interbreed although genes still flow around the ring; an ancient asexual lineage; bacteria with occasional horizontal gene transfer but stable ecological separation; a gradually evolving fossil lineage that paleontologists divide into chronospecies; and a narrow, stable hybrid zone between two otherwise distinct clusters. Aim for around eight decision points, each answerable from the case description alone and without technical vocabulary. Flag any two concepts the tree cannot separate using these cases, and say briefly whether that reflects a genuine overlap between the views or only a shortage of discriminating cases.
