I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, and I keep running into this analogy among illusionist-leaning colleagues. They compare the meta-problem of consciousness to a perceptual illusion. The meta-problem, as I'll understand it here, is the problem of explaining why we have such powerful intuitions that consciousness poses a hard problem in the first place — why phenomenal experience seems to resist physical and functional explanation. The Müller-Lyer illusion seems analogous: explaining why two equal lines look unequal is a purely psychological task, and completing that task does not require positing any real length difference; it shows the appearance is misleading. On the suggested analogy, explaining why consciousness seems to outstrip physical explanation is likewise a purely cognitive task, and completing it would show that the appearance of an explanatory gap is misleading. The hard problem, the suggestion continues, would then be diagnosed as a pseudo-problem rather than solved.

I'd like an audit of how far this analogy actually carries. Specifically: (1) lay out the mapping from the Müller-Lyer case to the consciousness case as explicitly as you can, including a table of correspondences if useful; (2) identify where the mapping comes apart, paying special attention to features of the illusion case that get quietly imported into the consciousness case — for instance, the fact that we have an independent way to measure the lines, or the fact that the appearance is of a property that can be shown to be misrepresented; (3) separate the disanalogies that matter for the intended illusionist conclusion from those that don't; and (4) say whether that conclusion — that explaining the meta-problem debunks the hard problem — survives intact, survives only in a weakened form, or fails once the disanalogies are on the table. If you think a better analogy would do different work, sketch it.

Please also indicate how confident you are in your overall assessment and identify the specific point you are least sure about.
