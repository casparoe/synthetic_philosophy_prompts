Draft the joint statement that a committed compatibilist about free will and a committed libertarian incompatibilist (the kind who thinks free will is real but incompatible with determinism) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want the whole thing built around this passage, from Hume's An Enquiry concerning Human Understanding, Section VIII, "Of Liberty and Necessity":

"Our idea, therefore, of necessity and causation arises entirely from the uniformity, observable in the operations of nature; where similar objects are constantly conjoined together, and the mind is determined by custom to infer the one from the appearance of the other. These two circumstances form the whole of that necessity, which we ascribe to matter. Beyond the constant conjunction of similar objects, and the consequent inference from one to the other, we have no notion of any necessity, or connexion."

The passage is the load-bearing part for me. I think Hume is doing more here than saying the two sides just talk past each other: he's claiming that "necessity," properly analyzed, is only regular conjunction plus our habit of inference, and that this analysis is supposed to leave room for the only kind of liberty worth caring about. A compatibilist can sign that and say it dissolves the traditional problem; a libertarian incompatibilist can sign the psychology and still say it doesn't touch whether we could have done otherwise in the robust sense that matters for moral responsibility. So I want the joint statement to make that the crux.

Format it as: a numbered "agreed claims" section (claims both camps accept, including claims about what Hume is and isn't committed to, and which parts of the standard debate are verbal rather than substantive), followed by a disagreements table with columns for the sub-question, the compatibilist formulation, the libertarian incompatibilist formulation, and what each side predicts the best arguments or cases would show on that sub-question. As you go, clearly seperate what is settled among people who work on this — the standard meanings of the positions, what the passage says — from what is genuinely live and contested, and don't make either position sound like the field's consensus. Every agreed claim should be non-vacuous and acceptable to both sides without wincing.
