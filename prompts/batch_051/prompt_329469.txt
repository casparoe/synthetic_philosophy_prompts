Well this was basically my evening: long argument with a friend about Keynes on risk vs uncertainty. She claimed once you put a distribution over the unknown you've collapsed uncertainty into risk, end of story. I said no, the move changes the problem, and we ended up going in circles around Ellsberg's urn. So I want you to actually run a variant for me.

Start with the usual Ellsberg setup: an urn has 90 balls, 30 red and the other 60 black or yellow in unknown proportions. A bet on red is a known 1/3 chance; a bet on black has a probability anywhere from 0 to 2/3, and people tend to prefer the red bet.

Now the variation. The experimenter doesn't just quietly fill the black/yellow part. She spins a spinner with 61 equally likely positions, numbered 0 to 60. She then puts that many black balls in the urn and makes the remaining 60 minus that number yellow. You know all this but don't see the spinner result or the inside of the urn.

First, do the actual probability calculation: given the spinner process, what is the chance a blind draw from the urn is black, and is there still anything ambiguous left in the mathematically natural sense?

Second, work out what happens to the standard Ellsberg behavior. Would an expected-utility maximizer still prefer red to black? Would the usual ambiguity-averse Ellsberg chooser still prefer red? Does the classic pattern survive this known second-order process, or does it disappear?

Third, the part my friend would care about: if knowing the spinner kills the preference, does that vindicate her view that Keynesian uncertainty was just missing information about a higher-order distribution all along? Or does it only show that adding an objective higher-order distribution changes the problem? Then push it: suppose you know she used a 61-position spinner to choose the number of black balls, but you dont know whether the positions are equally weighted. Does the red-over-black preference come back? Is there still a single probability of drawing black, and if not, what exactly is the residual that the first spinner had destroyed?
