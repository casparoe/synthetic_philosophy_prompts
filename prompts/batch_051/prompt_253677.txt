Please draft the document that a committed internalist about normative reasons and a committed externalist about normative reasons could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Take internalism about reasons to be the thesis—classically associated with Bernard Williams—that a consideration is a reason for an agent only if sound deliberation from that agent's existing motivations could move her toward acting on it; take externalism about reasons to be the denial of that thesis: at least some normative reasons obtain independently of the agent's present motivational set. The document should begin with a numbered list of claims both camps affirm—for instance, about what normative reasons are not (mere explanatory causes, mere desires, all-things-considered oughts) and about the phenomena a theory of reasons must accommodate. It should then state their remaining disagreement precisely, in wording each side would accept for its own position, and flag any portion of the dispute that appears merely verbal. Finally, it should present a table of cruxes—sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side. For each crux, supply a concrete case or argument that both sides agree bears on it, such as an agent who, after faultless deliberation by her own lights, is left motivationally cold by a consideration that others treat as reason-giving, and record each side's prediction about the case in advance. Format the result as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
