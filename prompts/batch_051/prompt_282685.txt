An argument I put together for a study group has left me with a conclusion I can't accept and no obvious way out; I'd like a diagnosis.

(1) The veil of ignorance gets its force from hiding particular facts about the chooser — class, talents, race, sex, and conception of the good — so that the principles selected cannot be tuned to the chooser's own position.
(2) Behind the veil, the parties have no basis for assigning probabilities to the positions they might occupy, so they choose by maximin: possible basic structures are ranked by the share of primary goods held by the worst-off representative person.
(3) This is what yields the difference principle.
(4) The underlying rationale — do not gamble with a life you may end up living — seems not to contain anything specific to the choice of fundamental principles. It looks like it applies whenever one person sets a rule and another person bears the serious downside of that rule.
(5) Many ordinary decisions of the basic structure have exactly this shape: approving a drug with a rare lethal side effect, fixing a bridge's safety margin, permitting an industrial process that occasionally maims a worker. In each case, the people deciding are not the people who will draw the bad lottery ticket.
(6) If those rules were chosen behind the veil by the same maximin reasoning, any rule whose worst-case for the person it falls on is catastrophic would be rejected, since you might be that person.
(7) So a society run on veil-style reasoning would reject nearly every medicine, bridge, railway, and factory — all of them impose some small risk of catastrophe on someone.
(8) Since Rawls plainly does not intend the difference principle to ban modern risk-bearing institutions, and since we are not prepared to treat every such institution as unjust, some step in the extension must fail — but I can't find a step I'm willing to reject without losing the original veil-to-difference-principle argument.

Which step do you reject, and what exactly does rejecting it cost? I would most like to keep (1)–(3); I'd sooner give up the extension to risk than the difference principle, but I'm worried that the rationale blocking the extension also undermines the original maximin move. If I have to pay, tell me which payment is real and which is a false choice.
