My flatmate and I had a long argument last night about whether consciousness is 'all a user illusion', and I'm still turning it over. His summary of the illusionist line (Dennett, Frankish, that crowd) went roughly like this. Consciousness as we ordinarily think of it doesn't exist: there are no qualia, no raw feels, nothing it is actually like to have a red afterimage or a pain. Introspection tricks us into believing otherwise — the brain produces the judgement that we're having rich private experiences, but the experiences themselves are never actually there. It's all like a desktop interface: evolution gave us a simplified dashboard of the world, and we mistake the icons for inner mental stuff. So when you look at a red apple, nothing anywhere is actually red; there's just a label attached to a representation, plus a false report that a sensation occurred. And since neither brain science nor careful introspection ever turns up these qualia, the honest conclusion is that conscious experience is like the luminiferous ether — something to stop believing in.

But something about this feels off to me, and I can't put my finger on where, because he has clearly read more of the actual illusionists than I have. Is his version fair to them? I know they say the word 'consciousness' doesn't just disappear, but I don't know whether that's them softening the blow or marking a real distinction. Walk me through it: which bits would a careful illusionist sign up to, which bits are a simplification or a step too far, and does the ether conclusion follow from the accurate parts? And acually, am I even right to think the last bit is where it goes wrong, or is the problem earlier?
