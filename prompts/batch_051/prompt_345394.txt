I'm a novelist, and Tuesday night two writers in my group got into it over a manuscript we'd read. The author, Marcus, had built a science-fantasy world where legal standing is assigned by a "sentience index": a thermostat sits at zero, a honeybee a little above it, a dog much higher, a person higher still. Under his system you couldn't destroy a bee for no reason, and even a sophisticated AI got fractional standing. Lena thought that was basically right. Ravi called it a category mistake.

(1) Lena's opening claim was that the sliding scale is the only honest way to think about it. In nature there's no sharp line between a nervous system that feels and one that doesn't — a bee has some learning and pain behavior, a jellyfish less, a thermostat none. If the underlying capacities come on gradually, she said, treating the beings as gradually mattering isn't a gimmick, it's just following the world.

(2) Ravi said he wasn't denying gradients of complexity or of well-being. A dog can be hurt in more ways and to a greater depth than a bee. But to be hurt at all there first has to be a point of view. Something either is a subject of experience or it isn't; below that line there's nothing it's like to be the thing, and assigning a bee or a thermostat a little bit of standing mistakes a fuzzy border for a fuzzy fact. The thermostat gets zero, not one-billionth.

(3) Lena shot back that he can't locate the line. Is a fly over it? A sea slug? A two-neuron system? If evolution installed no flag and nobody can find the boundary, then the difference between zero and one really does pass through middle cases — and that's all a gradualist is saying. His sharp threshold, she said, sounds less like a reading of the science and more like a metaphysical article of faith.

(4) Ravi said she was confusing uncertainty about a boundary with degrees of the property. We can be unsure whether a shrimp is below the line without concluding that the shrimp is a little bit of a subject; uncertainty just means we don't know whether it is one at all. And caution under uncertainty is different from owing something. He might avoid squashing an ant because he isn't sure it doesn't feel, but that isn't ant rights.

(5) Lena said that was drawing a cliff at an arbitrary spot and then calling it metaphysics. If caution does all the practical work — the less sure you are, the more careful you are — then he's already living with a gradient while insisting the world has a cliff. He wants the sliding treatment but won't pay for it.

(6) Ravi said a gradient of caution is fine; what he won't do is say the thermostat is a tiny bit wronged. "A little bit of a subject," he said, is as incoherent as a little bit pregnant. Once there's an interior at all, harms can vary in degree; before that, you've got only handles, no one home.

They asked me to referee and I honestly couldnt. Please go through their exchange move by move: for each step, say who's right about what, where they're talking past each other, and which concession each should make. Then say which overall position survives the exchange with less damage — especially on whether sentience itself can be graded, or whether gradation only makes sense for the interests of beings already past a threshold.
