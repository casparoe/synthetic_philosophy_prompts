I've been following a long-running series of case writeups by a moral philosopher on a forum I read, and I want to reverse-engineer the decision procedure behind her verdicts rather than relitigate the cases themselves. I'm after the latent rule that best compresses her track record, with the usual caveat that a finite list of verdicts underdetermines a rule.

Over several months she has given the following rulings:

- Trolley switch: permissible for a bystander to divert a runaway trolley away from five workers onto a side track where it will kill one worker. Her gloss: the one's death is a side effect of redirecting the trolley.
- Footbridge: impermissible to push a large man onto the tracks so that his body stops the trolley and saves the five. Her gloss: the plan uses his body to stop the trolley.
- Strategic bomber: permissible to bomb a munitions factory in a just war while foreseeing that nearby civilians will be killed. Her gloss: the civilian deaths are foreseen but not the aim.
- Terror bomber: impermissible to bomb a civilian population as a way of breaking enemy morale and ending the war. Her gloss: the civilian deaths are the means to the morale effect.
- Cancerous uterus: permissible for a doctor to remove a pregnant woman's cancerous uterus to save her life, foreseeing that the fetus will die. Her gloss: the fetal death is a side effect of removing the diseased organ.
- Craniotomy: impermissible for a doctor to crush a fetus's skull to save the mother in obstructed labor. Her gloss: the fetal death is the means of the rescue.
- Palliative morphine: permissible to give escalating doses of morphine for terminal pain, foreseeing that it will depress respiration and slightly hasten death. Her gloss: the intention is pain relief, with death merely foreseen.
- Lethal bolus: impermissible to give a deliberately lethal injection whose only purpose is to end a patient's life. Her gloss: death is aimed at.

Next week she is going to discuss the loop case. A runaway trolley is headed toward five workers. A bystander can throw a switch and divert it onto a side track, but the side track curves back and rejoins the main line at a point before where the five are standing, so an unobstructed trolley sent down the side track would still come around and kill them. The only thing that would stop the trolley is a heavy man standing on the side track: the collision kills him, and the impact stops the trolley before it re-enters the main line. The five are saved only if the trolley strikes him.

Please act as a professional moral philosopher and think carefully before committing. First, lay out the candidate principles you are considering and test each against her eight earlier rulings. Which verdict should I predict from her on the loop case? Which principle is she most plausibly running—the classic doctrine of double effect, a redirecting-vs-introducing-a-threat principle, a doing/allowing distinction, something Kantian about using people merely as means, or some hybrid? Which of those candidate rules fit all eight earlier rulings, and which rulings rule out which candidates? If the record underdetermines her rule even with the loop case, give your best prediction with reasons, and specify a further case that would settle the remaining ambiguity.
