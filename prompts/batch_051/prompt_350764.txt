I'm starting a seminar paper that needs to settle a three-sided question about temperance: is it a distinct virtue, is it just prudence applied to bodily pleasures under a more flattering name, or is what gets called temperance often just repression with a good reputation? Before I read the primary texts, I want the evaluative framework fixed, because otherwise I'll end up assessing each author against whichever criteria flatter the view I already hold. So my request is this: write the desiderata. What would an adequate theory of temperance have to deliver in order to settle whether the thing labeled temperance is a virtue, a kind of prudence, a form of repression, or some fourth option?

I want a full requirements list, not a definition. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it. At minimum the account has to handle: the difference between the self-controlled person who still wants the second slice but declines it and the temperate person who no longer has the struggle; the person who quit sugar months ago and whose cravings have gone quiet, and whether that is temperance or merely a new habit; the anorectic whose restraint around food is exacting but pathological, so the theory must not count them as temperate; the person who moderates pleasures purely instrumentally because overindulgence damages health or reputation, and whether that prudential self-management is virtue; and the ascetic who takes pride in wanting nothing, which can look like temperance from the outside. I also want the distinctions marked that any adequate theory must respect—for example, between changing a desire and overriding it, between wanting something and endorsing the want, between appetite and fear, between a settled disposition and one successful act of resistance, and between a desire that has been silenced and a desire that is absent. Tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely one camp's preferences, and identify the single requirement that does the most work in excluding candidates. Skip the scorecard comparing theories; I will apply the list myself.
