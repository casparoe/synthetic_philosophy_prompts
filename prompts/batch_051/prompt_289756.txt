I'm a retired wildlife biologist and I sit on the board of a small family foundation that funds animal welfare grants. We've got about $300k for this fall's cycle, and two proposals made it to the final round; we can only fund one. The first would send a mobile vet team into a few districts to treat street dogs with chronically painful but non-fatal conditions — tumors, mange, broken teeth, infected wounds, that sort of thing. It would reach a few hundred animals, but the suffering it prevents is severe and verifiable. The second would run an enrichment and socialization program at a well-run city shelter: better kennels, play groups, training, extra staff hours. Thousands of healthy dogs a year would be made happier, but they're already fed, sheltered, and not in acute distress. Our scoring rubric treats a unit of suffering prevented and a unit of happiness added as interchangable, so the shelter proposal wins on numbers, and most of the board thinks that settles it. I've been reading philosophy since I retired, and I keep hitting the idea that removing the bad is more urgent then adding the good, that the two don't just trade one-for-one. I'd like to circulate a short written opinion before our Thursday meeting: it should recommend concretely which proposal to fund and on what timeline, and it should give the honest strongest case for the losing side rather than a strawman. Please write it as a memo the board can act on, in plain language and without jargon.
