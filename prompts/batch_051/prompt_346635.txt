I'm a novelist by trade — mostly historical fiction — so the question of when a legend turns into history is one I live inside rather than study. Take the usual trio. Troy is the encouraging case: a real city was found more or less where the story said, so now everyone treats the whole epic as half-confirmed even though the war itself stays beyond archaeological reach. Arthur is the discouraging one: he starts as a name in a battle list and then accumulates a court, a round table, a grail, a moral order, none of it contemporary. And the Exodus sits somewhere else again: a foundational story that no Egyptian record corroborates, but a real movement of people wouldn't necessarily have left the kind of trace we'd hope to find.

Here's the parallel I cant decide about, and it may just be a novelist flattering their own trade: the process by which a legend hardens into "what actually happened" looks to me exactly like the process by which the backstory of a long-running fictional series hardens into canon. I mean the way a detail added in a later tie-in novel, or a retcon, or even a widely shared fan reading, gets picked up by later writers, harmonized with the earlier material, and eventually appears on wikis as what "really" happened in the story. In both cases you seem to have layered retellings, each one trimming contradictions and adding motives; the most coherent and satisfying version out-competing the earliest one; later readers unable to pry the early kernel from the later invention; and some institution — a priesthood, a canon board, a fandom — deciding what counts as the stable text. I'm not asking for a reading list. I want to know whether this is a real identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion.

Walk me through the mapping properly: what would have to be true on both sides for results to transfer, and can you name something the parallel predicts that turns out false? I know history has an independent world out there and a novel doesn't, but I'm not sure its doing as much work as it should, since the world only answers back through more texts, digs, and archives. Please write in British English, and at the end tell me how confident you are in what you've said and single out the point you're least sure of.
