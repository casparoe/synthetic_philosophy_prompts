I cover climate science for a mid-size outlet, and we've got a panel of five editors who vote yea or nay on whether a complicated story clears the bar. It's simple majority rule. Last week our data editor pitched expanding the panel to fifteen, and his argument has been bugging me. He says each of us is individually right more than half the time on these calls, and once you have a bunch of people like that, the chance that a majority of them is right shoots up toward certainty as you add more voters — he even sketched the curve on a napkin. So bigger panel, no brainer.

Our investigations editor says he's out of his mind. Her point is that all five of us read the same press release and the same two or three wire stories before we vote, so when a source is misleading we all make the same mistake. Adding ten more editors with the same blind spot just gives you a louder version of the same error, and probably a more confident one, because everyone sees everyone else nodding. She also points out that we just hired a junior editor who's still wrong about as often as he's right, and the napkin math seems to assume everyone's above even odds.

They've asked me to settle whether we expand, and I honestly dont know. Under what conditions is the data editor's math actually a good model of our newsroom, and under what conditions is the investigations editor right? Is there a way to structure the panel — who's on it, what they read beforehand, how we vote — so that the math and her worry can both be true? And what do I do about the junior editor?
