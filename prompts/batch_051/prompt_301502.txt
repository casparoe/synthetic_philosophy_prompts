I am preparing a handout for a seminar on the conceptual foundations of AI alignment, specifically the part of the course where we ask what it would take to recover a person's preferences from their behavior in the way that inverse reinforcement learning is supposed to. The students know the basic setup — an observer sees choices and trajectories and tries to infer the reward function or preference ordering behind them — but I want them to think hard about the standard of adequacy before we look at any particular method.

Write the list of requirements that a theory of preference inference from behavior must meet to count as an adequate account of what someone actually wants, together with the case or consideration behind each requirement. Among the candidates you might weigh: it should reproduce the observed choices without merely overfitting them; it should distinguish preferences from beliefs about the available options and from feasibility constraints, so that behavior caused by false belief or limited ability is not misattributed to taste; it should say how it handles noisy, habitual, or time-inconsistent choices; it should specify whether its target is actual preference, informed preference, or the person's endorsed all-things-considered preference; it should say how much weight to give to explicit self-reports that conflict with behavior; and it should yield something that transfers to novel choice sets rather than just imitating past choices. Mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as a commitment specific to a recognizable camp (revealed-preference economics, cognitive science, AI alignment, and so on), and say which single requirement does the most work in excluding naive accounts. The section I most want is the consistency check: it is often observed that large classes of reward functions are indistinguishable from the same behavior, and the problem only worsens once bounded rationality, context-dependent preference construction, and strategic self-presentation are allowed. Address whether one can simultaneously have uniqueness of the inferred reward, fidelity to all observed behavior, and robustness to noise and gaming; if not, say which requirement a defensible theory should relax first and what that relaxation buys. Skip scoring specific algorithms; I will do the scorecard of existing methods in class.
