I'm a novelist, and a friend roped me into writing a one-page "philosophical profile" on sincerity and transparency for their reading group — the question is roughly whether there's a duty to be legible to other people. I'd like to know if my actual views hang together before I present them. Here's where I've landed: (1) in my fiction, a character's actions should make their inner state reconstructible — if a reader can't tell why a character did something, that's a craft failure unless the opacity itself is the point; (2) in real life, people have no general duty to make themselves legible to others, and being hard to read isn't a moral fault; (3) sincerity and transparency are different virtues, and only sincerity — saying what you believe when you speak — is generally owed, not transparency about your reasons and feelings; (4) in close relationships there is a duty to be transparent about feelings that bear on the relationship, and making a partner guess why you're upset is wrong; (5) it's manipulative to withhold your reasons or feelings from someone who needs that information to decide wether to trust you or stay; (6) demanding that someone articulate their feelings before they've worked them out is coercive, and "I don't know yet" is a complete answer; (7) an author owes readers no explanation of their intentions outside the book — the work has to do its own work. Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it. For each real tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest fix would be, then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Also say how confident you are in the audit and which point you're least sure about.
