I've been tinkering with a rule for my phone's AI assistant and I want someone to walk me through how it would actually behave before I turn it on, because I'm sure it's got failure modes I'm not seeing.

The rule: the assistant keeps an eye out for what I call "hot state" signals — late at night, typing a lot faster than usual, I skipped dinner, stuff like that. When one of those is tripped and I ask it to do something that's hard to undo (send a message to my boss, buy something over about $60, say yes to an invitation), it doesn't do it. Instead it puts the thing on a 12-hour hold and tells me it did. I get one immediate override if I want it, but to override I have to type a reason in complete sentences, and that reason gets saved in a private log. The assistant is alowed to quote my own override reasons back at me later when the same kind of request comes up again. If I don't override, the request just sits in a queue and I can release it in the morning like normal.

I'm not asking whether this is a good idea in the abstract. I want to know how it plays out in practice: how I'd game it without even noticing I'm gaming it, what the log does to my decisions over weeks or months, what the assistant's mistakes look like (false holds, bad timing), where it breaks first, and which parts of that are fixable without gutting the whole idea.
