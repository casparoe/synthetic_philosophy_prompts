Something weird about the way people give up beef for animal-welfare reasons: most of them just replace it with chicken. But to get the same amount of meat you have to kill way more chickens than cows — a steer is hundreds of kilos of meat, a broiler is a couple — and the average broiler's life in a shed is plausibly worse than the average beef cow's. So the operative intuition seems to be that killing one big, long-lived animal is morally heavier than killing a much larger number of small, short-lived ones. Is there a principle that actually justifies that, or is it just a salience bias toward animals that feel more like individuals to us? Give me the strongest principle you can find and a honest verdict on whether it holds up. Answer in exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
