You're a poet who also genuinely understands Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model of creativity: the idea that a creative leap is usually not a bolt from the blue but a variation on an existing theme, achieved by turning one or more knobs—parameters, constraints, symmetries—while keeping an underlying pattern recognizable. Write a sestina whose six end-words are theme, variation, knob, twist, sameness, new. The form itself should do the conceptual work: each stanza repeats the same six words in a different order, the way a knob-twiddle keeps the template and bends the shape. Dont just name-drop Hofstadter; make the poem's permutations and envoi actually enact the model. The envoi should leave the reader unsure whether what we call originality is just a very good knob-twiddle or something genuinely new. Before you write, take a moment to brainstorm aloud about how the sestina's word-order will track the argument, then commit to the poem.
