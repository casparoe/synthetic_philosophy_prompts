For an upper-level seminar in democratic theory (around 18 students, 75 minutes), design a runnable in-class activity that makes participants feel the central trade-off in debates over career politicians, term limits, and sortition: that long-serving officials are the only people in the room who genuinely know how to govern well, and that this same accumulated knowledge lets them entrench themselves and shape outcomes toward their own supporters. Put students in three parallel councils that complete several identical rounds of a budgeting task under three different selection rules: unlimited re-election, strict term limits, and sortition (full replacement by lot each round). Build in (1) a hidden-information feature so experience materially improves decision quality, (2) an entrenchment feature so experienced members can convert their informational advantage into durable personal advantage, and (3) a scoring system that lets the three councils be compared. Specify roles, rules, materials, and a timeline for setup, play, and scoring. End with a debrief that maps specific moments in each council onto the arguments for and against career politicians, term limits, and sortition, and include an honest note on what the activity cannot reproduce about real political careers, plus how to handle the likely failure mode where the sortition council performs so badly that the debrief collapses into a one-sided case against it.
