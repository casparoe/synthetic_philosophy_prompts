I help run a tiny climate nonprofit and last month we got a letter saying our policy emails to city councilmembers count as lobbying under a new state rule, so I'm suddenly a registered lobbyist whether I like it or not. The rule works like cap-and-trade for access: each registered lobbyist gets 40 hours a quarter of contact time with any legislator, you can sell unused hours on a state-run exchange for whatever the market will pay, and every trade is posted publicly in real time. Meeting with voters, journalists, or anyone who isnt registered is unlimited and unlogged. I dont need the yes-it's-good or no-it's-bad version so much as the mechanics: does access get more or less concentrated, what sets the price of an hour, who becomes a seller and who becomes a buyer, how do big firms and tiny shops like mine behave differently, where does it get gamed first, and can the failure modes be fixed without just scrapping it? Also, what should a reporter be watching for in that public trade feed?
