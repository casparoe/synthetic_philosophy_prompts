In econ class we ran the dollar auction, where a dollar gets auctioned off but both the highest and second-highest bidder have to pay there final bids (only the highest bidder actually gets the dollar). The teacher said it shows the sunk cost fallacy: people keep throwing good money after bad, and the bidding goes way past a dollar. But here's what's bugging me. If you know the rules going in, can't you just use backward induction and see that once you enter, you could get dragged into an escalation spiral where both people are bidding just to not lose the money they already put in? So wouldn't the rational move be to never bid at all, and the only people who get caught are the ones who didn't think ahead? I guess I'm asking whether the dollar auction is really evidence about irrationality, or just a game that separates people who've heard of game theory from people who haven't. I feel like I'm missing something about what counts as rational once you're already in the middle of it, but I can't put my finger on it.
