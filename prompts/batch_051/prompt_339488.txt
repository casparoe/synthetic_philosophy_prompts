Construct a thought experiment that cleanly separates an effort-based account of desert from a contribution-based account of desert, and do it in the less familiar direction. Usually the harder worker has also contributed more, so the two accounts travel together; I want a case in which they come apart. The case should be one in which the person who works harder is, in the outcome, responsible for less of what others value, while the person who works with less effort is responsible for more of what others value. The effort-based account should judge that the harder worker deserves the greater reward, and the contribution-based account should judge that the less hardworking worker deserves the greater reward. Constraints: no differences in native talent between the agents; give them the same role, tools, and information; no fraud, negligence, or sabotage; an ordinary workplace rather than a science-fiction scenario; and the divergence should not turn on anyone’s cruel or unusual bad luck—ordinary market fluctuation should be sufficient. Specify the reward at issue concretely, and keep the scenario to one paragraph.

After the scenario, do the following. State each account precisely as the case ascribes it, and show why each gives the verdict it does. Identify the single feature of the case that is doing the real work, and say what happens to the two verdicts if that feature is removed. Finish by giving the strongest objection a defender of the contribution-based account would raise against the effort-based verdict, and be explicit about whether that objection succeeds and why.
