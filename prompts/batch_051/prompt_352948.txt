You are a professional philosopher of science who works on causation, explanation, and the epistemology of experiment. I have written down a set of working commitments about the interventionist account of causation, and I would like a careful consistency audit before I present them. Treat this as a diagnostic task rather than an invitation to write a general essay. Before ranking anything, sketch for yourself how each commitment might be motivated and what exactly it commits me to; then work through the commitments pair by pair. Do not flag every possible friction: some apparent tensions should dissolve once the commitments are read carefully, and part of the job is to say which ones those are. For each genuine tension, rank it by how severe it is, explain what would have to give, and state the cheapest revision and what it costs. At the end, tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole package coherent. Do not conclude that everything is fine or that everything conflicts.

Here are the commitments.

(a) The central thesis of the interventionist account is correct: a relation between X and Y is a causal relation just in case, and because, it is invariant under some appropriate range of interventions on X with respect to Y.

(b) Interventions must be characterized partly in causal terms. I is an intervention on X with respect to Y only if I causes X, does not affect Y except through X, and is not correlated with other causes of Y except through variables upstream of X.

(c) The resulting circularity is not a serious problem, because the interventionist account is not meant as a reductive definition of causation in non-causal terms; its point is to illuminate the connections among causation, manipulation, control, and experimental practice.

(d) Randomized experiments can discover causal structure without antecedently knowing that structure: randomization of the treatment assignment gives us probabilistic independence from confounders even when we cannot list or measure them.

(e) Causal claims can also be tested without literal interventions, by checking whether a relationship remains invariant across many different background conditions. A partial correlation that is stable across naturally varying circumstances is good evidence for a causal connection.

(f) The interventionist account does not by itself capture singular or actual causation — whether this token event caused that token outcome — and such judgments require counterfactual dependence along the actual sequence of events, which is a different kind of question.

(g) Soft interventions — changing the parameters or causal form of a mechanism rather than forcing a variable to a fixed value — are often more informative in complex systems, and they are legitimate interventions in roughly the same sense as hard interventions.

Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which are only apparently so, and how should I rank and repair the genuine ones?
