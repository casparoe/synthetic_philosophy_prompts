I keep running into some version of the arc-of-history-bends-toward-justice argument at the end of panels I cover, and it always sounds a little to clean, but I can't find the exact place where it goes wrong — or whether it does. Let me formalize the version I hear most often.

(1) Every major institutional injustice that is now widely recognized as an injustice — chattel slavery, serfdom, the old legal subordination of women, colonial rule, apartheid, Jim Crow — was, at its height, defended by plenty of smart people as natural or necessary, and then collapsed or was substantially dismantled within a few generations of coming under sustained moral attack.

(2) So the historical record shows a one-way pattern: institutions built on one group's domination of another start out looking natural and end up looking indefensible, and the direction of the change is always toward what we now regard as the more egalitarian side.

(3) The best explanation of this pattern isn't economic or technological but moral-psychological: given enough time and enough exposure to the testimony of the dominated, people tend to withdraw their allegiance from arrangements that can only be justified by denying that the dominated are full persons.

(4) If that mechanism is real, we should expect it to keep operating on currently contested institutions — mass incarceration, say, or sweatshop labor — and therefore expect those to be dismantled eventually too, with reversals along the way.

(5) Therefore history has a direction, and it points toward justice.

Maybe the argument is fine and my discomfort is just cynicism. But my suspicion is that (2) is doing something sneaky, because it measures the direction of history by our current standards, which would make the argument look exactly as persuasive to a future generation whose standards we'd find monstrous. Does that objection land on a particular step, and is it fatal? And if the argument is valid but a premise is false, tell me which one and what kind of evidence — historical, psychological, whatever — would test it without waiting a few hundred years.
