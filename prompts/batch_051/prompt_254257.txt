I want a working definition of what makes an observation count as evidence for a universal generalization, specifically the raven case. The setup, in case it helps: the claim all ravens are black is logically equivalent to the claim all non-black things are non-ravens, so by the usual story a white shoe ends up confirming all ravens are black, which feels nuts or at least weird. I dont want the history, I want a definition that actually works.

Here are my cases:

- I see a wild raven in good light and it's black. I'm sure this confirms.
- I see a white shoe in my closet. No idea. This is the paradox case.
- I spot a raven with distinctly pink wing feathers. I'm sure this disconfirms.
- I see a taxidermied raven painted black in a museum diorama. I'd say it doesnt confirm at all.
- I get a blurry trail-cam photo that could be a black raven or a black crow. Maybe weak evidence?
- An acquaintance who lies about half the time tells me they saw a black raven this morning. I lean no, but something has shifted.
- I see a black raven in a zoo. I flip-floped on this one: it's a real raven, but it got there through human selection.
- I read in a field guide that every reported raven sighting has been of a black raven. I don't know if that confirms the generalization or just pushes the question back a step.

Propose a definition, run it through all eight, tell me where it gives a verdict you find surprising or no verdict at all, and then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict.
