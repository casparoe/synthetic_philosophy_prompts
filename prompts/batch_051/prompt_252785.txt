I'm not a philosopher or anything, but Ive been trying to get a handle on what self-control actually is, mostly because I quit a few things over the years and noticed the times it worked felt really different from the times it didn't, even when the outer behavior was identical. So I want to know if there's a single principle behind my gut calls, or if some of my calls just have to go. Here's what I think about a bunch of cases.

1. My buddy keeps a candy jar on his desk and never touches it. He says he just doesn't want candy and never did. I don't think he's showing self-control. Nothing's being controlled; he just has different wiring.

2. My aunt quit smoking by renting a cabin in the woods for a month with no cigarettes and no car to go buy any. She quit for good. I admire it, but it doesn't feel like she exercised self-control—she just made the thing impossible. It feels like a trick, not a virtue.

3. A guy is on a diet and white-knuckles it all day. He's miserable, he thinks about cake constantly, but he never eats it. I think that's self-control if anything is. The fact that it hurts makes it more clearly self-control. Though a friend told me that's not self-control, it's repression, because the craving is still running his life. I don't buy it, but it nags me.

4. A woman signs up to run with a friend every morning because she knows she'll be too embarrassed to skip. In the moment she never fights the urge to stay in bed; the friend does that work. I want to call this self-control, or something just as good, but it's different from the white-knuckler.

5. A guy gives his best friend the password to the account he uses to order junk food, and tells the friend not to give it back for a month no matter what. When he's craving pizza at 2 a.m. there's nothing left for him to do. That seems like self-control to me, maybe the smartest kind, because he controls himself at the one time he actually has leverage.

6. A woman stays faithful to her husband only because she's terrified of getting caught. I don't fault the behavior, but I don't call it temperance. Shed cheat tomorrow if she knew she'd get away with it. That's just fear, or caution.

7. A woman keeps to a strict diet by having turned herself into "a healthy eater." When someone orders a family-size pizza she doesn't wrestle with it; she just feels that's not the kind of person she is. I lean toward saying this is better than self-control, not just self-control. But she's not struggling either, just like the candy jar guy, so maybe I've got a problem.

8. A man quit drinking after a doctor told him one more binge could kill him. He still desperately wants a drink every day but doesn't take one. I count that as real self-control, but it's self-control from fear, and that sits differently with me than someone who quit because he didn't want to be a drinker anymore.

The two cases I can't reconcile are the aunt in the cabin and the guy with the password: both offload the struggle to something outside themselves, but I'm convinced the password guy showed self-control and the aunt somehow didn't. The only difference I can find is that the password guy set up a rule aimed at his future self, while the aunt just removed herself from a situation. But I worry I'm rationalizing to keep a verdict I like.

So my question: is there one principle that captures all these, or close? If no, which of my verdicts should I drop, and why? And what does that principle say about two I'm genuinely stuck on:

A. A teenager hands his phone to his parents every night because he knows he'll scroll until 3 a.m. otherwise. By the time the urge hits, the phone is gone. Is that self-control, or does it stop being self-control because the parents are the ones holding the willpower?

B. A woman has been running for a year after forcing herself at first, and now it's fully automatic. She doesn't want to skip a workout, but she never deliberately built a "runner" identity; it just slowly grew on her. Is the automatic running self-control now, or did the self-control phase end once she stopped having to try, and if so what is the right name for what she's doing?
