You are a professional epistemologist specializing in formal epistemology, counterfactual theories of knowledge, and the epistemic closure debate. I want your honest assessment of a concept I have been calling inferential tracking erosion. The core claim is this: on a Nozick-style tracking account, knowledge is not closed under known entailment in a simple all-or-nothing way; I want to push further and say closure fails gradually. Each step of competent deduction from a known premise transfers only part of the premise’s sensitivity and adherence to the conclusion, because the closest worlds in which the conclusion is false need not be worlds in which the agent’s method would abandon the conclusion. So even ordinary, non-skeptical chains of inference can end in a proposition that is true, believed, validly derived, and still not known. For example, a long derivation from a known perceptual premise to an abstract theoretical conclusion may be impeccable as logic and yet fail to track the truth of the conclusion.
