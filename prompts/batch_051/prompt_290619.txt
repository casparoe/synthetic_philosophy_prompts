I've been reading a lot of Judge Okafor's rulings on statistical evidence, and they've got me confused in a useful way. As near as I can reconstruct it, her track record runs something like this:

- Hit-and-run bus case (civil, preponderance): the only link between the defendant's bus company and the accident was that the company operated about 80% of the buses on that road at that hour. She ruled for the defendant.
- Gatecrasher case (civil, preponderance): 1,000 people got into a show but only 300 paid; the promoter sued one attendee, with no other evidence that he personally skipped the ticket. She ruled for the attendee.
- Prison yard case (criminal, beyond a reasonable doubt): 99 of 100 prisoners in a yard took part in beating a guard; the state charged one prisoner and offered nothing but the odds. She acquitted.
- Gas station drive-off (civil, preponderance): the cashier testified she was roughly 85% sure the defendant was the one who left without paying, from a decent look at his face. She ruled for the gas station.
