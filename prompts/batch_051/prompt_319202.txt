Novelist here, so I spend an unseemly amount of time trying to get down on paper what it's actually like inside a person's head. But there's something about my own experience that keeps tripping me up, and I don't know whether it has a name.

Take looking out at the harbour. It doesn't feel like I'm looking at a picture my brain has assembled; it just feels like the harbour is right there, given, pressing in on me directly. Same thing with the self. My "self" feels like such an undivided, un-loseable thing, even though I've read enough about the brain to believe it's built up out of processes underneath. And here's the odd bit: learning that changes nothing. The construction never shows its seams. It stays like a window I look through but can never catch a glimpse of. After all these years, I could no more spot the "model" than I could spot my own eyes without a miror.

So my question is: is there a name for that quality — the way experience hides its own scaffolding so the model gets taken for the thing itself? I'm not just after the label, though. I want to know what the term actually covers. Does the same thing apply to perception and to the sense of being a self, or am I sloppy to lump them together seperately? Are there experiences where it fails — a thought you catch half-formed, a pain, a dream — and do those count as a hole in the phenomenon or just a different sort? And how is this different from the boring fact that I can't directly watch my neurons fire?

Could you structure the answer into four named sections, in this order:
1. "The phenomenon" — pin down what I'm describing, in about 120 words.
2. "Nearest existing names" — the closest terms you know, with a sentence or two each on where they match and where they fall short (roughly 200 words).
3. "Where the edges are" — whether it's one thing or several, and the boundary cases I should use to test that (roughly 200 words).
4. "Your verdict or coinage" — either the best-fitting name and why, or, if nothing fits, a term you'd invent and how you'd define it (roughly 120 words).
