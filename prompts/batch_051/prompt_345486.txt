In discussions of artificial intelligence and the long-run future, I keep meeting the term 'singleton' used as though its extension were obvious, even though I suspect different people draw the line in different places. Roughly, people seem to mean a durable ordering of the world in which the largest decisions are ultimately settled by one controlling agency, an agency also capable of preventing any serious internal or external challenge to its position. That sketch is too loose to settle anything, so I would like a working definition of 'singleton' in this future-studies sense, precise enough to test against concrete cases.

Here are the cases I want you to run the definition against. (1) A single world state with one legislature and one executive, a monopoly on large-scale armed force, and the constitutional power to override any regional or local law, even though its constitution reserves many ordinary matters to regions and guarantees individual rights. (2) A hegemonic state that no coalition of others could defeat, that can set the terms of trade and occasionally replace foreign governments, but that claims no formal jurisdiction over much of the world. (3) Several highly capable artificial intelligences whose goals are permanently and verifiably aligned with one another; they coordinate through markets, contracts, and shared protocols, with no overseer above them, and they are built so that war among them is impossible. (4) A decentralized world of human communities with no human or machine ruler, bound together only by a single constitutional algorithm whose rulings are final and in practice cannot be resisted. (5) The present United Nations system, in which major powers can and sometimes do ignore international decisions when it suits them. (6) A far future in which humanity occupies many star systems too distant to interact, each governed internally, with no system able to influence another. (7) A single artificial intelligence that controls all large-scale production and communication but holds no public office, and is obeyed only because no one has a workable alternative.

I have deliberately chosen a mix: a few strike me as clearly inside the term, a few as clearly outside, and the rest as genuinely borderline, and I want to see which way your definition pushes them. Please state your proposed definition, run it through each of these seven cases, say where it classifies correctly, where it gets the wrong answer, and where it gives no answer at all, and then either revise the definition or defend the surprising classifications. As you go, please separate what is settled among people who work on this topic from what is still genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus. I would like the whole thing in continuous prose, like a careful email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
