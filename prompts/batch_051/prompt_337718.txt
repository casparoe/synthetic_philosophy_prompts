I am laying out a two-year conceptual research agenda around a single question: under what conditions, if any, do the psychological differences that moral foundations theory documents between the political left and right tell us anything about which side is more likely to be morally correct, or about how much confidence either side should place in its own moral and political judgments? I have a background in moral psychology and normative ethics, and I do not plan to collect original data; I want to spend the two years where the conceptual bottleneck actually is rather than on another empirical replication or a survey of the field.

The candidate open questions I keep returning to are these: whether the standard measures of the care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, and liberty foundations are valid enough that observed left-right differences can really be attributed to differences in those foundations rather than to something else, such as group identity or threat sensitivity; whether the findings hold outside WEIRD samples and whether the liberal-conservative axis picks out a stable contrast at all; whether ideology shapes the relevant moral intuitions rather than the reverse; whether descriptive findings can be given normative weight without bridging the is-ought gap through some account of which of these foundations a sound morality should weight more heavily; and whether the much-discussed finding that each side understands the other side's moral reasons asymmetrically has the epistemic consequences for humility or confidence that people have drawn from it.

I would like you to rank exactly five questions for me, in order of priority for this project, replacing as many of my candidates as you think should be replaced. Rank them by how much settling the question would change the answer to my underlying question, how many other questions depend on it, how tractable it is for someone doing conceptual work without new data, and how neglected it is relative to the other questions. Please give the answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences saying what the question is, why it deserves that rank, and what result in the first year of work would most move it up or down, with no introduction or conclusion.
