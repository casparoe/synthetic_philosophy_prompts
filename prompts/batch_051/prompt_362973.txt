One thing I keep tripping over in rationalist circles is how often "what if everyone did that?" gets treated as a single test when it's really at least three different tests wearing one trench coat. There's Kant's version (can the maxim be conceived, or at least willed, as universal law), Hare's prescriptivist version (universalizability is already built into the logic of "ought," so it's a consistency constraint and not secretly about consequences), and something like Singer's generalization argument (which looks much closer to rule consequentialism: would everyone doing it have bad results). So the claim I want to stress-test is this: when ordinary people deploy the "what if everyone did that?" move, they are mostly running a free-rider/defection-detection heuristic or a crude rule-consequentialist generalization, not anything distinctively Kantian or Hare-like.

I want you to design an experiment, or a small battery of experiments, that could pull these readings apart emprically rather than letting the phrase stay blurry. I'm imagining a vignette study that crosses things like: does universal performance produce a practical contradiction, does universal performance have bad consequences, and does the agent's action ride free on others not doing it. Give me concrete cases for the cells, including at least one case where universalizing produces a contradiction in conception but not bad consequences, and one where it produces bad consequences with no contradiction in conception, so the usual confound is broken. The design should sort people by what they say about cases, not by asking them which philosopher they are.

Tell me the pattern of judgments each hypothesis predicts, what result would make you abandon each reading, and which comparison would carry the most weight. Then be blunt about what even the cleanest dataset could never settle, for example whether the folk test is a post hoc rationalization, whether it tracks the real cognitive process versus just the explicit justification, and whether any empirical result licenses the normative conclusion that "what if everyone did that?" is the right test. Also, I'm not sure the three positions are even fully separable once you pressure them, so say where they bleed into each other. And please write the answer in American English.
