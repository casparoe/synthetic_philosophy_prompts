I've been going down a rabbit hole on the rationalist explanations for war, specifically the Fearon framework, because it keeps getting used in arguments about AI development races. I'm a software engineer so I'm sure I'm mangling the theory, but here's my understanding. The standard list of rationalist mechanisms -- reasons why even roughly rational actors end up in a costly fight instead of some deal they'd all prefer -- has three entries: private information with incentives to misrepresent (neither side can credibly reveal how strong or determined it is, so they fail to find the deal), commitment problems (they could agree on terms but can't credibly promise to not grab a bigger share later, especially if the balance of power is shifting), and issue indivisibility (the thing at stake just can't be divided). Applied to AI races that gives you labs sitting on private information about their own progress while having every reason to bluff or sandbag to scare off rivals, safety agreements being unenforceable because whichever lab pulls ahead later would want to defect, and AGI or decisive advantage being treated as a winner-take-all prize that can't be carved up. The indivisibility one always feels kind of weak to me because you can usually invent side payments or equity or access deals, but I'll leave it on the list.

What I want: I'm compiling a list of genuinely distinct rationalist mechanisms that could push AI labs or states into an actual race or conflict. Suppose the Fearon three are the standard ones and my list already has everything obvious. Give me one more mechanism that is not on the list and isn't a rewording, special case, or combination of the three -- something with a different structure underneath. It should apply specifically to AI development races, or at least you should show how it cashes out there. Explain it in plain terms, then say what it adds that the Fearon three dont cover, and whether you think its actually a good explanation or just a clever redescription. I'm less after a survey of what people have already said and more interested in whether a clean fourth mechanism is even possible, and if so what it would look like.
