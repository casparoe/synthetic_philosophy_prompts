I serve on a working group on distributive justice, and I have been asked to circulate a short statement of my own settled views so the group can pressure-test them. Before I send it, I would like an audit of whether the package actually hangs together; I am not interested in which of these positions is currently fashionable, only in whether they jointly commit me to more than I realize.

My commitments are as follows. (1) The currency of egalitarian justice should be capabilities rather than resources or welfare: what matters is people's real freedom to achieve basic functionings such as being adequately nourished, mobile, educated, and able to take part in public life, and resources are an imperfect proxy for this because people differ in how efficiently they convert resources into functionings — a wheelchair user and an able-bodied person given the same cash transfer will not end up with the same mobility. (2) I am a responsibility-sensitive egalitarian, close to luck egalitarianism: inequalities traceable to unchosen circumstances, or brute luck, are unjust and call for compensation, whereas inequalities traceable to people's responsible choices, or option luck, may permissibly stand. (3) Accordingly, I think that freely chosen idleness, imprudent wagers, and expensive tastes do not create claims against others; when someone with the same option set as everyone else chooses to work less and thereby ends up with a thinner set of real options, there is no distributive injustice to repair. (4) I nonetheless hold that there is an unconditional social floor of basic capabilities — secure housing, necessary healthcare, enough income to appear in public without shame — below which no one should be allowed to fall, regardless of how they came to be without those capabilities. (5) I also hold that a person who becomes paraplegic in a skiing accident they knowingly and voluntarily risked is entitled, as a matter of justice, to the accommodations and aids that restore their mobility; the mere fact that the injury issued from a chosen risk should not, by itself, justify withholding them. (6) Finally, I believe the metric should be assessed in terms of real options rather than achieved outcomes or subjective satisfaction: equality of capabilities does not require making everyone actually function in the same way, only securing for each person the genuine freedom to function.

Please go through these positions and tell me where the package strains. For each pair that is genuinely in tension, rank the tension by severity, explain what each side of the tension is doing in my overall view, and state the cheapest repair and what it would cost me. For any pair that only appears to be in tension, explain why the appearance dissolves. Then tell me which single revision of the set would do the most work toward making the whole thing coherent.
