I've been refereeing a running argument between two colleagues for about a year now. One is a preference utilitarian who thinks Harsanyi's aggregation theorem is about as close to a proof of utilitarianism as social choice ever gets; the other thinks it's a formal result that quietly assumes the interpersonal comparability of utility it's supposed to launder. They've finally agreed to coauthor an adversarial-collaboration document instead of another e-mail chain. Draft the statement they should produce. For their purposes, take the theorem to say: if every individual's preferences and the social observer's preferences each satisfy the von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms, and social preference respects Pareto indifference (if everyone is indifferent between two prospects, so is the observer), then social utility must be an affine function of individual utilities—a weighted sum, up to a constant. The document should include (i) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign about what the theorem proves and what it leaves open, (ii) a disagreements table where each row states one point of divergence in neutral wording, followed by the wording each side would accept for its own view, and (iii) two or three cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side—each with a test or consideration both agree bears on it and each side's prediction written down in advance. Push them past the usual talking points: get into whether the Pareto condition is doing moral work or mere consistency work, what the vNM cardinalization does and doesn't give you, and whether the observer imagining themselves in each person's shoes helps or just relocates the problem. If some of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why.
