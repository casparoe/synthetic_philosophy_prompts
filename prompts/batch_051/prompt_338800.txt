Copyright law in most jurisdictions protects a work for the author's life plus several decades, and protection does not lapse merely because the rightsholder has disappeared. The source code of a game whose studio closed twenty years ago, or of business software whose publisher was dissolved with no traceable successor, can still be covered by copyright: copying, patching or preserving it would infringe unless an exception applies, even where there is no owner to license the work and no one obviously being deprived of revenue. Some legal systems have added orphan works arrangements that permit specific uses after a diligent search for the owner, but these arrangements generally do not treat the work as abandoned and do not transfer the underlying ownership.
