I work in compliance at a mid-sized logistics company, and every couple of weeks I have to write an incident review after something goes wrong — a bribe paid by a local agent, a safety rule ignored at a depot, a pricing algorithm that makes discriminatory offers. Each review has to conclude with either "individual failure" or "company failure," and the box it lands in decides whether someone gets fired or we change a policy. Our head of legal has a rule of thumb: call it a company failure only when we can't pin it on any particular person. That feels backwards to me, because some of the worst things this company has done weren't decided by anyone in particular, and other times one manager gets scapegoated for a system that was built to push them into exactly that choice.

Can you write me a reusable decision procedure for making those attributions? It has to be something a compliance analyst with no philosophy background can run in about half a day, and the division head signs off on the result, so it can't be a seminar. I want the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with "individual," where it stops with "company," and where it has to stop and say "this is a judgement call, a human being decides." Then run it on three cases sitting on my desk: (1) a branch manager authorized a small bribe to keep a permit on schedule after regional KPIs tied his bonus to impossible delivery deadlines; (2) a pricing model we deployed kept steering worse terms to certain postal codes and nobody checked it; (3) an engineer disabled a fatigue alarm at a depot, against written policy, because the line was short-staffed. I suspect your procedure will say the company is on the hook in more of these than my boss wants to hear, and if so, say it.

Also, if the whole request rests on a confused premise — if "the company failed" is just legal fiction and blame can only ever attach to individual people, or if there's no principled line between the two boxes at all — tell me straight instead of giving me a procedure. I'd rather be told I asked the wrong question than get a flowchart that papers over it. And wherever the underlying ideas are genuinely contested or your just making a practical simplification, flag it. I can live with judgement calls; I can't live with fake rigor.
