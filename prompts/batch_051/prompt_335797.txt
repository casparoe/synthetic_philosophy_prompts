I keep seeing epistemologists go back and forth on whether knowledge is 'luminous' — the idea that if you know p, you're in a position to know that you know p. The anti-luminosity side insists there will always be borderline cases where you know something but can't reliably tell that you know it. But the debate is usually conducted as pure armchair metaphysics, when at least part of what's at stake sounds like a claim about actual knowers. So: design the best empirical test you can for the dispute between luminosity and anti-luminosity (or the KK principle, if you think that's the better version to test). Be concrete about the task or manipulation, what you would measure, and what pattern of results would count for or against each side. Then say honestly what a clean positive result would still fail to settle — for example, whether subjects are really in the knowledge state rather than just reporting a feeling of confidence, or whether a normative principle can be tested empirically at all. Dont just propose a questionnaire where people rate there own certainty; if you use self-report, you have to say why it's evidence and not just a story they tell.
