I spent my career in atmospheric chemistry and have recently been reading philosophy of science, and Milton Friedman’s 1953 essay The Methodology of Positive Economics has left me somewhat provoked. Friedman is remembered for the claim that the realism of a model’s assumptions is irrelevant so long as the model yields sufficiently accurate predictions, and the section of the essay in which he asks whether a hypothesis can be tested by the realism of its assumptions has become a touchstone for the view some call the F-twist. I want a decision tree for classifying positions on the question: What, if anything, does a false assumption contribute to a successful scientific model?

Please build the tree from diagnostic questions first, before labels. Each branch should be a claim a working scientist could answer without already knowing the philosophical taxonomy: for example, whether any false assumption that is not causally negligible automatically taints the model; whether predictive success licenses any claim of resemblance between model and world; whether the false premises are supposed to be eliminated by taking limits, averaged out, or never meant literally; and whether there is a stable difference between idealization, abstraction, and approximation.

The leaves should include at least these positions: Friedman-style predictive instrumentalism; Galilean idealization/abstraction-realism; approximation-to-truth realism; a view on which all false assumptions are epistemic defects to be minimized; and a deflationary “models are mere calculation devices” view. Label any leaf you think is unattributed or probably unoccupied. Also flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate rather than papering over the difference. Use a concrete case from atmospheric science, such as modeling the dry adiabatic lapse rate while ignoring entrainment and turbulent mixing, to illustrate the branches where useful.

Audience: a mixed discussion group of physical scientists and philosophers meeting for a single session. Keep the tree to a one-page flowchart or a numbered list with branch labels, and give one sentence per leaf on its most awkward commitment. Please be plain about how confident you are in the classification and name the one point you are least sure of.
