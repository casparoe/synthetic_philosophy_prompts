I keep noticing a parallel I cannot decide about, between performance legitimacy and a problem in machine learning. The thesis of performance legitimacy holds that a regime can be justified by delivering prosperity, stability, and order, even in the absence of democratic or liberal sources of legitimacy. Structurally, this looks to me like inferring the quality of a hidden process from an output metric that the process itself controls: citizens update confidence in a regime from outcomes the regime can partly produce, select, and publicize. That is the shape of Goodhart's law and reward hacking, where optimizing a measured variable makes it worse as evidence for the underlying target. If the parallel is real, legitimacy-by-performance is a bet on a biased measurement and should inherit the corresponding pathologies: the more legitimacy depends on a metric, the stronger the incentive to game it, and the less diagnostic recent performance becomes. I can also see disanalogies — legitimacy is not literally an inference, citizens are not a single model being updated, and prosperity is not merely a signal but something valued for itself. I want a verdict on whether the parallel is deep or superficial. Which parts of the two structures genuinely correspond, which come apart, and does anything transfer from Goodhart's law and reward hacking to the normative question of whether performance can justify authoritarian rule?
