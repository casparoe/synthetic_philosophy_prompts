Hey, I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on temporal phenomenology, so I keep running into Augustine's Confessions XI and have enough of my own reading of it to be suspicious but not enough to be confident. A friend of mine who does semi-popular philosophy sent me this paragraph that he uses in an intro piece, and I'd like to know how much of it to trust before I tell him to publish it or push back. His summary is:

Augustine's main move in Book XI is to use the question what God was doing before creation to argue that time itself is something created. His famous line about not knowing what time is until someone asks just shows the concept is paradoxical. He then argues that only the present exists, while the past and future are completely unreal. Because past and future are unreal, all talk about them is really talk about present mental states: memory of the past, expectation of the future. Therefore time is just a distention of the mind. That means time is purely subjective — a projection of the mind onto the world, not a feature of the external world itself. One obvious problem is that if the past and future don't exist at all, then Augustine can't make sense of how we measure time, or how events are ordered, so his whole analysis collapses into saying that time is an illusion, which is just implausible.

Something in that gloss seems compressed to me — especially the jump from past and future only exist via the present to time is purely subjective, and the claim that Augustine can't account for measurement, since I remember there being a whole discussion of how we measure the length of something like a psalm. Could you separate what's fair to Augustine, what's a subtle misread, and whether the objection at the end actually lands?
