Two members of a philosophy-of-psychiatry working group—one a causal realist about psychiatric classification, the other a Hacking-style looping theorist—have argued for months and have now agreed to write an adversarial collaboration document. Take attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder as their running case. The realist holds that ADHD picks out a mostly stable causal structure in attention, impulse control, and activity regulation that exists independently of the label, and that any feedback from the label to the labeled is a secondary layer obscuring a real underlying kind. The looping theorist holds that once the classification circulates, people are sorted under it, reinterpret their behavior in its terms, adjust their self-descriptions and habits, and are seen differently by clinicians and institutions; the category then changes in response to the behavior it helped produce, so the kind itself is a moving target. Both accept that some feedback between classification and classified exists; what they dispute is its depth, its measurability, and whether it undermines or merely complicates realism about the category.

Draft the document they should produce. It must contain:

1. A numbered list of at least six substantive claims both can sign—claims about the classification, the classified people, clinicians, institutions, and the feedback process—phrased so that neither side finds them vacuous or slanted.
2. A precise statement of the residual disagreements, each side's position worded so that the other would concede it is fairly represented, with purely verbal disputes separated out and either dissolved or labeled as merely verbal.
3. The cruxes: the sub-questions whose settlement would actually move at least one side. Address at least these three: whether a stable causal core is visible across cultures and in the historical record before the label existed; whether the loop is closed (the category changes because of the behavior it helped produce) or merely one-way (the label changes behavior but the category stays fixed); and whether the label shapes core symptoms or only surface presentation, help-seeking, and reported distress.
4. For each crux, a concrete empirical or argumentative test both sides would agree bears on it, and each side's advance prediction about what the test would find, stated precisely enough that a later observer could tell who was surprised.

The philosophical work is in the drafting: find formulations that are neither empty truisms nor disguised advocacy, use language neither side would reject as unfair, and avoid collapsing the disagreement into a difference of emphasis.
