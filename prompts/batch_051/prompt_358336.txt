I'm on the town council of a smallish city. We have a law from 1963 that says any shop reselling used goods has to pay a yearly permit fee and keep a paper log of every item's serial number. As far as anyone can tell, it has never once been enforced: thrift stores, pawnshops, and consignment places have run for decades without the fee or logs, and our records show zero citations. Now a big chain resale store wants to open, and a few council members want to dust this law off specifically to slow them down. Their argument is that the law was never repealed, so its still law, and we're free to begin enforcing it any time. Something feels off to me but I can't articulate why. Is it legitimate to start enforcing a rule after 60 years of total non-enforcement? Does the fact that everyone ignored it for that long change what we should do, or is that just sentiment? How should I vote?
