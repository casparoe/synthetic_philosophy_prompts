I spent thirty years in a vision lab, and like everyone I worked with I assumed perception and cognition were seperate modules — the Muller-Lyer illusion seemed to have settled that, since knowing the lines are equal never makes them look equal. Since I retired I've been reading the philosophy of perception, and the cognitive penetrability debate is turning out to be a lot less settled than my textbooks let on. I want to put together a which-position-do-you-actually-hold quiz for a meetup of retired scientists who don't know the jargon, so it has to sort people by what they say about concrete cases, never by which label they prefer. Could you write it? Eight to ten questions, each one built around a concrete case: Muller-Lyer, a radiologist who after years of training just sees a tumor, Russian speakers distinguishing lighter and darker blue, a black-and-white banana that looks faintly yellow, the hill-slope effect when your tired or carrying a backpack, the dress photo, maybe something with fear and spiders. Two or three answer options per case. Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the main positions: strict impenetrability of early vision, penetration only through attention or late judgment, full constructivism, an enactivist or action-oriented line where the perception/cognition divide dissolves, and the deflationary view that the whole question is badly posed. I think the crux is forcing people to separate perceptual experience, perceptual judgment, and attention — write the options so those actually come apart. And flag any pairs of positions the quiz can't separate, and say whether that's because one of them is unattractive, incoherent, or just underdeveloped.
