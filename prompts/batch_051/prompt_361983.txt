In my algorithmic fairness course we have been working with three candidate conditions on a fair decision rule. Demographic parity: the proportion of people selected must be equal across groups. Equalized odds: the false-positive rate and the false-negative rate must be equal across groups. Calibration: at each risk score, the probability of the predicted outcome must be the same regardless of group membership.

We were then given this case. A college uses a risk score to decide which students receive proactive academic support, where the outcome to be predicted is whether a student would otherwise fail a required course. Students from high-poverty high schools fail at much higher base rates than students from well-resourced high schools. The score is calibrated, but the college sets different score thresholds by group so that the share of students flagged for support is equal across the two groups. The rule therefore satisfies demographic parity and calibration, while the group-specific thresholds make the false-positive and false-negative rates unequal, so equalized odds is violated.

Is this decision rule fair? Evaluate it against each of the three criteria. Then say whether the case shows that at least one of the three conditions is not a necessary condition of fairness, or whether it instead shows that no imperfect algorithm can satisfy all three when base rates differ. Which condition, if any, would you revise or give up, and what should the college do instead?
