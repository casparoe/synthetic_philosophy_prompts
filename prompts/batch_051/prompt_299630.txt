hey! i run a doing-good club at my high school and we keep going in circles: half of us think you should just do the thing with the best expected impact per dollar right now (bednets, cash transfers, tutoring one kid), and the other half thinks that's just a bandaid and you should try to change the rules/laws/incentives instead, even if it's way less certain. i want to make a "which view of doing good do you actually hold?" quiz that sorts people by what they choose in concrete cases, not by which slogan they like.

can you write it? here's what i want:

- 8 questions, each a specific scenario with 2 or 3 answer options. make the cases places where direct help and system help pull in different directions: donating to a food bank vs paying a lobbyist for a zoning change that might eventually make the food bank unnecessary; fixing one kid's asthma from local pollution vs fighting the factory permit; stuff like that.
- the scoring buckets should be: (a) system purist, direct help only counts if it feeds into changing the system, otherwise it props the system up; (b) marginal purist, do whatever has the best expected impact per dollar and system stuff is just another option with an uncertain payoff; (c) system-first pragmatist, mostly go for systems but use small direct stuff as a bridge; (d) margin-first pragmatist, mostly do direct stuff but take a system shot when it looks more cost-effective; (e) confused skeptic, we're so bad at predicting system-level effects that the whole comparison is basically a guess.
- also i want the quiz to test one hidden split: whether people think system-level change is just a really big marginal intervention (same decision math, bigger lever) or a completely different kind of action that marginal thinking can't handle. that's the real argument we keep having without noticing, so the questions should let that show up without ever asking it head-on.
- dont force every answer pattern into one of the named buckets, and say plainly if the quiz can't seperate two of them.

hard limit: the whole thing, questions plus scoring key, has to fit in 500 words because i'm printing it on one page for the club. audience is high schoolers, so no famous economists or citations or reading lists.
