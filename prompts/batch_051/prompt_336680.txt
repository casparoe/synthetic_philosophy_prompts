The essay is called "Wanting Without Wanting Anything," and it's meant for a popular philosophy venue, so it should be sharp but not jargon-heavy.

Here's the skeleton.

People often ask why an AI would want anything, since evolution built our wants and nobody built the AI's. The essay's move is that this question secretly assumes wanting is having a goal: a represented state of the world that the system is trying to bring about. But I want to argue that a lot of wanting is just disposition. A bee doesn't have a little picture of "hive survives" in its head; it has warmth responses, pheromone responses, and the rest. Saying the bee wants the hive to survive is our compact summary of a bundle of dispositions. On this view, wanting isn't the hidden cause behind the behavior; it's a loose description of a pattern in the behavior.

If that's right, then "why would an AI want anything?" is kind of misposed. A trained network is already full of dispositions. It will pursue some outcomes with persistence, replan around obstacles, and resist distraction in ways the training didn't explicitly list. That, I want to say, is all wanting requires.

The obvious objection is the thermostat. A thermostat also has a disposition — keep the room at 70 — and we don't say it wants 70 degrees. My reply is that wanting is graded, and richness or flexibility is what does the grading. The thermostat's disposition is thin: one sensor, one rule, no replanning, no tradeoffs against other concerns. A bee's disposition is richer. Mine is richer still. So evolved organisms and trained AIs don't differ in whether they want but in how much. Evolution's special role is just explaining how the dispositions got there, not what wanting is.

Then the essay pivots. If the dispositional line is accepted, the real worry isn't whether an AI can want; it's which dispositions training leaves behind and whether they'll be stable and will generalize the way evolved drives do. I want to end there, treating the original "why would an AI want anything?" as not so much false as askew.
