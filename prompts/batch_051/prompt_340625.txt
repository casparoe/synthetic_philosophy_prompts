Tell me where this argument goes wrong, or whether it's exactly right. A friend reconstructs Harry Frankfurt's essay On Bullshit like this. (1) Lying requires intentionally asserting something you believe is false. (2) A language model has no beliefs or intentions, so (3) a hallucination can't be a lie. (4) Frankfurt's distinction is that the liar cares about truth enough to hide it, while the bullshitter is indifferent to whether what he says is true. (5) A hallucinating LLM is indifferent to truth in exactly that sense. So (6) LLM hallucinations are bullshit, not lies and not mere errors. I keep wanting to say a calculator that prints 2+2=5 is just malfunctioning, not bullshitting. But maybe Frankfurt's point is that calling something bullshit is about the lack of a certain relation to truth, not about having a self. Which step, if any, fails? If none fails, are you stuck with saying the calculator bullshits?
