You are a professional philosopher who specializes in property, intellectual property, and the ethics of abandonment. Think carefully, and start by brainstorming a little before you commit to a verdict — I want a well-reasoned, criterion-by-criterion answer, not a quick reaction.

I've been trying to decide when a creative work, especially abandoned software, is morally fair game to use, preserve, or revive without the owner's permission. Here's the list of criteria I've been using for calling something genuinely abandoned:

1. You make a good-faith effort to identify and contact the rights holder, and either you can't find them, or they don't respond after a reasonable attempt.
2. The work has no active commercial life: nobody is selling it, licensing it, maintaining it, or even keeping a store page alive for it.
3. There is no reasonable prospect that your use would damage the owner's actual or reasonably foreseeable interests — no killing a future remaster, no undermining a live market.
4. The use is respectful and non-commercial-ish: credit is given where possible, you don't claim ownership, and you're not profiting off someone else's abandoned labor.

Now here's the borderline case that's been bugging me. In 2009, a tiny studio released a niche fantasy MMORPG. In 2012 they got bought by a big publisher — call it Labyrinth Entertainment. Labyrinth shut the servers down in 2014. On paper the publisher still owns all the copyrights, but since then: no re-release, no remaster, no licensing deals, no source code release, and emails to thier licensing department go unanswered for years. The trademark has lapsed. A group of fans reverse-engineers the server from old client files and starts a free shard: no subscription, no cash shop, no ads, and they credit the original developers prominently. Several of the original devs say privately that they don't mind. But Labyrinth's legal team did send one boilerplate takedown to a different fan revival back in 2015, then never followed up or responded again.

Does the fan shard count as using an abandoned work under my four criteria? Go through each criterion and say where it comes down, and where the criteria don't settle things. And tell me if this case shows that any of my criteria is wrong, or needs to be softened or tightened.
