I'm the grad student rep on my university's research ethics board and I've been asked to draft the decision memo for a case we're voting on Thursday. The applicant is a political science PhD student doing fieldwork on local governance. Her plan involves paying small cash 'sitting allowances' to village chiefs and junior local-government staff for the time they spend in interviews and for convening community members; the money goes to them personally, not to any institution, and the amounts are roughly what local NGOs pay. The university's anti-bribery policy says no payments to government officials, full stop, but our guidance also allows 'nominal customary gifts' — which is precisely the fight. Half the board thinks this is a bribe with extra steps, and that approving it would make the ethics committee complicit in corruption. The other half thinks this imposes a Western anti-corruption frame on a reciprocity norm, and that clientelism isn't automatically corruption; refusing to pay would just make the research impossible and insult people who are doing us a favor. I have to write the short opinion recommending approve, approve with conditions, or reject, with reasoning that engages the strongest argument on the other side, and a concrete plan if there are conditions. I keep flip-flopping — its genuinely hard to see where the line is between enabling access and buying it.
