You are an expert in the philosophy of cognitive science and AI, and you're especially good at checking whether an old problem survives a new technology. Please think carefully, and before you commit to a view, briefly brainstorm two or three ways of reading the question and say which one you're going to answer.

The frame problem, in its original logic-based AI form, is roughly this. An agent that represents the world with explicit axioms can be told that moving the glass from the table to the sink causes the glass to be at the sink. But unless the agent also has a big list of frame axioms—axioms that say what doesn't change—it won't know that moving the glass didn't change the color of the walls, shift the table, knock a sponge into the sink, and so on. Spelling out all the non-effects of every action isn't just tedious; it blows up combinatorially. Philosophers later generalized the problem into a question about relevance: how does a finite reasoner update its picture of the world after something changes without having to check every one of its beliefs against the change?

Now for the development. A modern LLM trained to predict text over a huge corpus has no explicit world model, doesn't perform deductive closure over a belief set, and has no frame axioms anywhere in it. You can say "I moved the glass to the sink, then turned on the tap," and it continues in a way that tracks what normally stays the same and what normally needs updating—at least in ordinary cases. It seems to know that the walls aren't now wet and the table hasn't disappeared, without anyone having to tell it.

So does the frame problem survive this development? I don't want a list of LLM abilities and failures. I want you to work through the original argument and say where exactly the new development bites. Does it bite the assumption that the agent is a symbol-crunching theorem-prover over an explicit belief base, so an LLM simply doesn't satisfy the setup? Or does the problem come back in a different costume—in what gets put into the context window, in the fact that the LLM has only distributional typicality rather than any guarantee of relevance, or somewhere else? If you think the frame problem is solved, say precisely what solves it. If you think it has been dodged or renamed, say what is still missing, and describe a kind of case where the LLM's strategy would predictably fail even though a completed (if impossible) set of frame axioms would handle it. Be explicit about whether you're talking about the narrow AI version of the problem or the wider philosophical one.
