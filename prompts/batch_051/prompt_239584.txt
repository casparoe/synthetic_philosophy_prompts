I was a bench chemist for thirty-odd years and I've been reading philosophy of science since I retired. Feyerabend has gotten under my skin. I used to assume there was a thing called the scientific method — conjecture, test, revise — even if nobody in my lab actually worked that way. Now I cant tell you what separates science from pseudoscience without sounding like a commitee chairman.

I want a working definition of "scientific method." If you think that phrase is unsalvageable and I should define "science" or a "scientific practice" instead, say so and define that — but I need it to do real classificatory work on the cases below. For each case I want a verdict: yes, no, or "this definition gives no answer." I'd rather you tell me your definition is silent on a case than twist it to force a verdict.

Spare me the literature review; I'm asking for your definition, not a history of the term.

Cases:

1. A physical chemist measuring a reaction rate constant under controlled conditions and fitting the data to a kinetic model.

2. An astrologer writing daily horoscope columns and never checking whether the predictions land.

3. A homeopathic "proving" where volunteers record symptoms after taking a remedy diluted past the point where any molecule of the original substance remains — but the protocol is blinded and placebo-controlled.

4. String theory in its present state: mathematically constrained but with no realistic near-term empirical test.

5. A climate modeling group running an ensemble to project regional temperature ranges fifty years out.

6. Field geologists reconstructing a one-off past event, like an asteroid impact ending the age of dinosaurs.

7. A psychoanalyst building a theory from a handful of case histories.

8. A tech company A/B-testing two website banners to see which one gets more clicks.

9. A phrenologist in the 1840s measuring skull bumps and running statistical correlations with personality traits.

My gut says yes to 1, 5, and 6; no to 2 and 9; and I genuinely don't know on 3, 4, 7, and 8. Give me a definition that earns those calls or gives me a good reason to abandon them. Run the definition through every case, mark where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and then either revise it or defend the surprising verdict. If you end up thinking the term has no real unity — just a family of practices with a good PR team — then say that, but only after showing me which cases force you there.

Last thing: tell me which of your final verdicts is the least secure and why.

And keep the whole thing in American English.
