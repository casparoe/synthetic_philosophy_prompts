I am a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, and I have been asked to run an undergraduate seminar session on how mechanical rigidity, behavioral flexibility, and free will relate to one another. The conceptual thread is sphexishness. The wasp Sphex performs a fairly elaborate provisioning routine: it stings a cricket, drags it to the entrance of its burrow, leaves it on the threshold while it goes inside to check the burrow, then comes back out and drags the cricket in; if an experimenter moves the cricket a short distance away while the wasp is inside, the wasp will bring the cricket back to the threshold and go inside to check again, and this can be made to repeat for many cycles. The usual moral is that the wasp is smart enough to run a complex, goal-directed-looking routine but not smart enough to notice when the routine has stopped making progress: its flexibility is tightly bounded. I want students to feel the contrast between that bounded rigidity and the open-ended revisability we associate with free action, and to come away seeing flexibility as a matter of degree, built out of higher-order capacities rather than as an exemption from causality.

Please design a runnable 80-minute activity for about 15 undergraduates that stages this contrast rather than lecturing about it. I have in mind something like a two-round game in which one partner plays a literally rule-bound wasp algorithm and the other partner is the experimenter who keeps perturbing the input, followed by a redesign round in which players may amend the algorithm and then face new perturbations; but I am open to a better structure if you have one. Give me concrete roles, rules, materials, timings, what the facilitator should watch for, and a debrief that maps particular moments in the activity onto the conceptual claims. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time it is run and how to handle it, and be explicit about which feature of sphexishness and free will the activity cannot reproduce and must be supplied by the facilitator. Do not name sphexishness or free will until the debrief.
