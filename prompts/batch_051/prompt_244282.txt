Freud in The Future of an Illusion describes religion as a universal obsessional neurosis, likening religious ritual to the obsessive-compulsive defenses of a neurotic child and tracing both to an unresolved relation to the father. I have a friend who uses this analogy constantly to argue that religion is something to be outgrown, like a neurosis, rather than engaged with intellectually. I'd like you to audit it. Give me the correspondence table — what maps onto the symptom, the underlying conflict, the analyst, the treatment, the resistance — then tell me where the mapping breaks. What does the neurosis picture smuggle in about religion's social function, its content, and its origin? Does the conclusion (religion would or should dissolve as people mature) survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or is the analogy doing all the work?
