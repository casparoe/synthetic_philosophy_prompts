Write a dialogue between two philosophers at a workshop on metaphor. The first, Dana, defends a Davidsonian view: literal meaning is the only meaning a sentence has, and metaphors work causally by prompting hearers to notice comparisons, images, and affective responses, none of which constitutes an additional propositional content. The second, Elena, defends conceptual metaphor theory: metaphorical expressions are systematic because they are governed by stable cross-domain mappings in conceptual structure, and these mappings are genuinely part of what competent speakers grasp. The assigned example is this: a film critic says that a particular film's visual style 'treats the city as a character,' and a class of viewers converges on claims such as that the city is portrayed as having moods, memories, and a distinct point of view, even though the film never states any such thing. Dana must explain, without attributing metaphorical meaning, how that convergence arises and why some paraphrases feel apt while others do not. Elena must explain, without allowing that just any association goes, exactly what the mapping is, whether it makes the city-character claims true or false of the film, and how her view handles novel metaphors. Each should address the other's strongest objections rather than scoring debater's points; in particular, Dana should press on the empirical and semantic status of the alleged mapping, and Elena should press on open-endedness, cognitive variability, and metaphors that resist paraphrase. Give each speaker at least two substantial turns, and do not force a resolution. Begin the response with the dialogue itself, with no preface or commentary.
