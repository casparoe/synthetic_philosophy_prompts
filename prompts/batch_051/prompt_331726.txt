I am drafting a novel set in a contemporary Pentecostal congregation, and the central figure is a mature believer who has spent decades defending — and at times ministering in — the continuation of the spiritual gifts: prophecy, speaking in tongues, words of knowledge, and above all healing. Her eventual loss of confidence has to feel earned, so the skepticism she meets must be the strongest version of the position, not a strawman. Treat continuationism as the claim that the New Testament's so-called sign gifts did not cease with the apostolic age but continue today as genuine acts of the Holy Spirit, so that at least some instances of prophecy, tongues, words of knowledge, and healing are not wholly natural, psychological, or fraudulent. Tell me what would count against that claim. I am not asking for a survey of the cessationist–continuationist debate; I am asking for a map of the position's exposure as a thoughtful defender would draw it. What would the world have to look like for continuationism to be false? Which observations about the distribution and character of reported healings and fulfilled prophecies would be decisive rather than merely awkward? Which exegetical findings about the usual cessationist proof-texts, or about the function of the gifts in the early church, would a continuationist have to take seriously? Which historical and sociological patterns — the apparent post-apostolic decline, the recurrence of similar ecstatic and healing phenomena across unrelated traditions, the rate of false or vague prophecy — genuinely press on the view, and which would its defenders dismiss as question-begging? Rank the defeaters from fatal to survivable, distinguish what a thoughtful continuationist would concede counts against her from what she can reasonably set aside, and say whether the view, as usually held, has been insulated against evidence — and whether, for a theological claim of this kind, that insulation is a flaw or defensible. I also need to know what continuationism commits its holder to predicting about the world where cessationism would predict otherwise; the novel depends on the two positions having genuine empirical texture rather than merely rhetorical texture.
