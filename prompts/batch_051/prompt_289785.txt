I'm a PhD student in information science, with a philosopher on my committee who keeps asking the annoying version of every question, and I'm writing a chapter on whether information about a person can be literal property. I need a working definition of the term "ownership of personal data" — or the broader relation "this information belongs to person P" — to do any real work in the chapter. I do not mean "what does the GDPR or US law say"; I mean a conceptual account, one a court could ignore but an ethicist couldn't.

I've been reading people who carve the space into volunteered data, observed data, inferred data, and relational data (data that is always about more than one person, like a genome or a message log). Please use those distinctions only as scaffolding, not as the answer, and run a proposed definition through these cases in order:

(1) the text of an email I compose and send to a friend — to me this feels clearly mine;
(2) the fact, noticed by a stranger on a bus, that I'm 5'11" and have a visible scar — clearly not mine;
(3) the continuous glucose readings from a sensor stuck to my body and auto-uploaded to the manufacturer's cloud — I genuinely don't know;
(4) the inference a supermarket draws about me from my purchases (e.g., that I'm probably pregnant), which I never recorded and never thought myself;
(5) my whole-genome sequence, given that about half of it is also my sibling's genome and my results leak information about them;
(6) the metadata of my texts — who I messaged, when, for how long — but not their contents;
(7) a stranger's photo of a street crowd in which my face is clearly visible.

Dont force each into a clean yes/no if the honest verdict is "partly," "split," or "depends on which strand of the concept you mean" — I'd rather have that.

Then tell me which two cases are hardest for your definition and whether the problem is the definition or the background idea that information about a person can be owned at all.

One more thing: could you say clearly where you're giving me something that's basically settled among people who work on this versus where you're taking a contested position? I keep reading people assert that you own your data like its a law of nature and it isnt, and I'd rather not reproduce that in a dissertation.
