Take the Achilles and the Tortoise puzzle where the tortoise accepts P and P implies Q but won't move to Q, and throwing in the conditional that if P and P implies Q then Q as an extra premise doesn't help. The standard lesson is that a rule of inference isn't another premise — something Frege-style modern logic is often credited with making explicit. Now vary the tortoise: he's not a flat-out skeptic about modus ponens. For any particular argument you actually put in front of him, he'll grant the step from P, P implies Q to Q for that very P and Q; he just won't grant the general statement that every argument of that form is valid. Does the original regress still get going somewhere, or does he now reason perfectly well and merely refuse to say the general thing?
