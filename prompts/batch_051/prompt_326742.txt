I have been defending a position in population ethics that I call lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism. The view says that below a threshold of pain intensity, pleasures and pains are commensurable: enough mild pleasure can outweigh a small amount of mild pain, and ordinary aggregative trade-offs are appropriate. But once a pain crosses the threshold—entering the range of severe, torturous, or otherwise deeply traumatic experience—its disvalue is lexically prior to positive hedonic value: no amount of pleasure, however large, can compensate for even one such pain. Above the threshold, outcomes are compared first by the minimization of above-threshold pain; below-threshold pains and pleasures matter only after that.

I want an honest map of what would count against this view. What would the world, or a defensible piece of moral mathematics, have to look like for the position to be false or unstable? Which assumptions does it stand or fall with? In particular, what could show that pleasure and pain are either everywhere commensurable or everywhere incommensurable, so that a threshold between two regimes cannot be maintained? What possible cases—including cases involving risky choices where above-threshold pain is uncertain, or cases involving very many people each experiencing below-threshold pain—would put pressure on the view? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones a committed defender would concede as genuine costs rather than dismiss as question-begging. I also want you to distinguish defeaters that are live possibilities from those that are merely logically possible but not credible.

If the position as I have stated it is confused—for example, if a lexical threshold between hedonic magnitudes is not well-defined, or if my framing of commensurability already assumes something contested—tell me so plainly instead of answering on its own terms. I am not asking for a bibliography or a survey of who holds which version; I want the structure of the view's exposure.

After the prose, provide a JSON object with the following schema:

- "position": a one-sentence restatement of the view.
- "core_commitments": an array of strings, each stating one claim the view must be committed to.
- "defeaters": an array of objects, each with fields:
  - "defeater": a string describing a possible observation, argument, formal result, or case that would count against the view.
  - "severity": one of "fatal", "serious", "awkward", or "merely_apparent".
  - "target": the core commitment it puts pressure on.
  - "defender_response": a string describing how a committed defender would likely respond, and whether they would concede it as a cost.
- "overall_exposure": one of "well_insulated", "moderately_exposed", or "highly_exposed".
- "assessment": a string saying whether that degree of insulation is a problem for the view and why.
