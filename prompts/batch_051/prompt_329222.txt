OK so at work we started this thing where design debates get scored. Two engineers get assigned sides of a technical call—rewrite the service vs patch it—and a panel scores them on argument quality, how well they handle objections, how far they can steelman the other side before answering, stuff like that. Scores feed a ladder, there's a season, people care about their rank; the actual decision still gets made later by the team lead, seperate from the score. I keep calling it a sport and people say no, a sport needs physical skill, this is just a game. The definition of sport I was taught was roughly: physical exertion/skill, organized competition under rules, judges or agreed scoring, and a winner decided by performance rather than by a committee deciding what's true. So my questions: is this debate ladder a sport in every sense that matters, or is the physical-skill criterion doing real work? And suppose we lean into it and treat it like a proper sport with rankings and winning as the whole point—would that make the actual reasoning better, or just train people to rack up points instead of tracking what's really a good engineering call? Go criterion by criterion, say where they underdetermine the verdict, and tell me whether the criteria themselves need fixing. Also say how confident you are in your conclusion and what point you're least sure about.
