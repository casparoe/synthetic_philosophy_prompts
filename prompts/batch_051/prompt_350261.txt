I'm on the board of a small charter school that's rewriting its academic honesty policy after a plagiarism scandal. Half the board wants a strict incentive system: automatic penalties, a public log of violations, maybe small rewards for students who report their own mistakes. They keep saying people respond to incentives, and I've gone down a rabbit hole reading about Han Feizi's Legalism, which is basically that view — a ruler shouldn't try to make people good, just make the payoffs clear. The other half says that's brittle and a little insulting: the only thing that actually works is building a culture where students don't want to cheat, through an honor code, teachers modeling honesty, and actually talking about virtue. They say a pure penalty system trains students to game the rules and falls apart the moment nobody's watching. Write me the short memo to circulate before Thursday's vote. Recommend a concrete policy — not a hand-wavey do-both compromise, but an actual plan with a bottom line up front. Take the strongest version of the losing side seriously, not a strawman, and keep the memo practical rather than academic. After the memo, add a note just to me saying how confident you are in the recommendation and which point you're least sure of.
