I cover physics and philosophy of science for a magazine, and last month I sat in on a workshop about gauge theories, mostly as a fly on the wall. During the coffee break two of the speakers, Maya and Eli, got into an argument I ended up taking pages of notes on. Maya said the vector potential is a classic case of surplus structure: two potentials connected by a gauge transformation describe the same physical situation, so only the electric and magnetic fields, and other gauge-invariant quantities, deserve to be called real. The potential itself is a bookkeeping device, like a choice of coordinates. Eli pushed back with Aharonov-Bohm: run an electron around a solenoid in a region where the magnetic field is zero everywhere along its path, and the interference pattern still shifts. E and B alone can't account for that, so the potential can't be merely redundant. Maya replied that the phase shift depends only on the line integral of the potential around a closed loop, and that loop integral is gauge-invariant; so the real object isn't the value of A at a point, which is still arbitrary, but the holonomy. Eli said sure, but you can't even write the local theory without the potential — charged matter couples through A, and if you insist on gauge-invariant quantities alone you're stuck with nonlocal descriptions. Maya said that's a point about convenient representation, not about what exists; coordinate systems are also indispensable to formulating the theory and nobody puts them in the ontology. Eli then went non-abelian: there, even the field strength transforms covariantly rather than invariantly, so the clean division between real fields and redundant potentials breaks down. Maya said that just moves the invariant content into traces and holonomies. Eli answered that then the alleged local ontology evaporates into nonlocal loops built out of the very connection she wanted to call fictitious.

Neither would budge, and eventually they asked me to settle it. I honestly couldn't, and I dont want to misstate the physics in the piece I'm writing. Can you adjudicate it point by point: who's right about which specific claims, where each overreaches, and what they're both missing? More than a scorecard, I want a workable answer to what "real" should amount to in a gauge theory.
