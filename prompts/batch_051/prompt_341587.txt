I run a continuing-education reading group that is closing out the term with Moritz Schlick's essay on the meaning of life. The view I want the poem to carry is his central one: that the demand for an external purpose gets its grip from work done as a means to an end, and that life's meaning is not found by discovering some further goal but by entering into activity done for its own sake — activity Schlick calls play, understood broadly enough to include absorbed, creative work that has become its own reward.

Please write a poem for this session: a Petrarchan sonnet in loose iambic pentameter, in the voice of a speaker who begins trapped in the "what is it for?" question and who, at the volta, stops searching for an external purpose and finds meaning in self-contained, absorbed activity.

Make the form do the philosophical work rather than merely illustrating it. The octave should be saturated with instrumental vocabulary — purposes, outcomes, means, ends, something still to be reached — and its syntax should keep pointing past each line toward a goal it never quite arrives at. The volta into the sestet should enact Schlick's turn: not an answer to the "what for?" question but a change of subject, in which the activity stops being for anything outside itself. The sestet should accordingly be self-contained: no instrumental constructions such as "in order to" or "for the sake of," and its rhymes and sentence-closings should settle into the lines themselves rather than pressing onward. The poem should not sermonize or pretend that grinding labor can simply be relabeled; it should keep the contrast between means-activity and self-rewarding activity sharp. Do not quote from Schlick's text.

The response should consist of the poem first, followed by a section titled "Commentary on the choices made" of roughly 200–250 words. In that section, explain how the poem's turn, rhyme scheme, syntax, and vocabulary track Schlick's distinction, and note any place where you had to depart from strict sonnet convention or from Schlick's emphasis for the poem to work.

Please write the response in American English.
