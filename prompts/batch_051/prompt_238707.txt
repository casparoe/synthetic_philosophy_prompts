I keep running into a move in my church that I don't have a word for, and I'd rather get the concept right than just have a name slapped on it. We're officially sola scriptura: the Bible is the only infallible authority, and no pastor, elder, or council has authority to bind anyone's conscience beyond what the text plainly says. Fine. But in practice, when a real dispute comes up — divorce and remarriage, women teaching, how much of the Old Testament law still applies — what happens is that the elders or the lead pastor settle it by giving their interpretation, and from then on that interpretation functions as if it were itself authoritative. People get corrected, taken out of ministry, or quietly sidelined for reading the text differently. And when anyone pushes back, the reply is always something like "we aren't claiming any authority; we're just saying what the text says." Then the same move repeats one level up: someone cites a commentary or a well-known preacher, and if challenged they say "I'm not following that teacher, I'm following the text; he just explains it faithfully."

I know there are nearby ideas — the regress of interpretation, the question of who decides what a text means, plain appeals to expertise, maybe authority versus persuasion. But I'm after the specific thing: an interpretation, offered by a particular person or body, which operates with effective authority in the community while the person offering it explicitly disavows having any authority, because they take themselves to be transparently reporting what the text already says. Is there an established name for exactly that? And if not, can you coin one and define it so that it's clearly distinct from (a) ordinary delegation of authority to a teacher, (b) trusting an expert's judgment, and (c) the general fact that a text needs a interpreter? I'd also want you to run it through a couple boundary cases — a pastor who openly says "the congregation called me to teach, so my reading carries weight," and a layperson who says "I'm persuaded by this commentary but it's just one fallible reading" — so I can see where the phenomenon starts and stops. The carving matters more than the name: I want to be able to tell whether my church is doing this particular thing or something else that just looks like it.
