For our final project in a translation seminar we have to predict what our professor would say about a choice we're stuck on, using the verdicts she's given all semester. Her track record, as best I've got it: she praised a version that threw out a pun that only worked in the source language and wrote a different pun that did the same emotional work; she marked down a version that kept a word for a local mourning custom in italics with no note, saying it just sat there like an obstacle; she approved, on the condition that we add a one-line note, a version that borrowed a term for a communal work party and glossed it on first use; and she rejected a version that used the dictionary equivalent for a neutral word about seasonal laborers, because in English the word landed as contemptuous. The new case is a word for a kind of street vendor that the novel uses both tenderly and with a slight class condescension. The class is split between borrowing it with a gloss, or rendering it with an English phrase that tries to get both the warmth and the condescension; a third camp just wants "hawker" since its the closest dictionary word. Predict her verdict, say which rule she's implicitly running (or which two she's torn between), identify the closest earlier case, and if the record underdetermines it, spell out what principle we'd be adopting with each choice. Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no introduction or conclusion, and make the last point the further translation case that would settle which rule she's actually running.
