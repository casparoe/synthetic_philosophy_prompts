Ok so in my philosophy club we've been going back and forth on whether the analytic/synthetic distinction is real or just a difference in how stubborn people are about certain sentences. "All bachelors are unmarried" really does feel true in a different way than "all bachelors are messy," but the Quine side of me thinks that's just entrenchment: we'd give up the second sentence way before the first, but in principle no sentence is totally immune to revision, it's all one web. Can you design a concrete test of whether there's a real distinction here and not just a difference of degree? Like, what experiment or observation could actually bear on it, what result would support each side, and then be honest abt the residue — what would even a clean positive result leave unsettled?
