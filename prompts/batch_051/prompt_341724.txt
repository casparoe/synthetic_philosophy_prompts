my epistemology reading group keeps going back and forth over whether we need to be able to justify our most basic certainties — that the external world exists, that these are my hands, that sort of thing. one member keeps leaning on the building picture: our beliefs are a structure, higher beliefs rest on lower ones, and if the bottom storey cant be given a non-circular justification the whole thing collapses into skepticism, so the fact that basic certainties lie beyond justification looks like a crisis. another member says that is exactly the picture Wittgenstein spends On Certainty trying to undo, and that these basic certainties are not foundations at all — they are more like hinges the inquiry turns on, or the riverbed the flowing water moves within. i want an audit of the building analogy against the hinge picture. give me the correspondence table first (what plays the foundations, the load-bearing walls, the upper storeys, the ground the building supposedly sits on?), then the places where the mapping breaks. i especially suspect the building picture quietly builds in a vertical image of justification — each belief held up by the one beneath it — and that the conclusion, no justified foundations means the structure collapses, might just be an artifact of the picture rather than an argument. which disanalogies actually matter for whether skepticism follows, and is there a better analogy that captures what the hinge and riverbed view is getting at?
