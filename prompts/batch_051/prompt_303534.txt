I get why that's supposed to be a problem for maximizing expected utility, but I honestly can't tell wether the proposed fixes are real solutions or just ways of changing the subject.

Here are the responses I keep seeing people throw around:

1. Bounded utility: put a hard ceiling on how good or bad any state of the world can be.
2. Probability thresholds: just ignore outcomes whose probability is below some tiny epsilon.
3. Complexity penalty: heavily discount hypotheses about the mugger that are too contrived or would require massive coincidence.
4. Just pay up: accept that expected utility genuinely says to pay the mugger, and that this is weird but correct given the premises.
5. Robustness across priors: instead of relying on one probability assignment, only take the action if it looks okay under many different reasonable priors, or use a maximin-like rule for the huge-stakes cases.
6. Rejecting the hidden calculus: deny that hypothetically simulated suffering should enter your utility function the same way ordinary, observable harms do.
