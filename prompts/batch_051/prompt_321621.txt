I'm taking a seminar on the ethics and economics of price controls — we've covered rent control and price-gouging statutes, and now we're on the minimum wage — and I've been trying to build a taxonomy of the distinct kinds of objection one can raise against a binding minimum wage. So far I have five, which I take to be the standard ones. (1) The allocative objection: in a competitive low-wage labor market, a floor above the market-clearing wage prices some workers out of employment, so the people the policy is meant to help lose jobs. (2) The targeting objection: many of the workers who receive the increase are teenagers or secondary earners in nonpoor households, while some of the poorest people are not in covered employment at all, so the transfer is poorly aimed. (3) The hidden-adjustment objection: even if measured employment holds steady, firms adjust along other margins — shorter hours, fewer benefits, stricter effort standards — so the harm is displaced rather than eliminated. (4) The spatial-uniformity objection: a single national minimum binds hardest in the low-wage, low-cost regions where it is least appropriate, and is close to irrelevant in high-wage regions, so its bite is concentrated where it does the most damage. (5) The freedom-of-contract objection: adult workers should be permitted to accept a wage below the floor if that is the best offer available to them, and blocking such agreements is a paternalistic wrong against the people the law claims to protect.

I am not asking for empirical magnitudes or for citations, and I already know the monopsony complication — it qualifies objection (1) rather than adding a distinct objection, so leave it aside. What I want is a list of exactly five more objections, numbered (6) through (10), each expressed in one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. The five must be genuinely distinct in structure from (1)–(5): not a rewording, not a special case of one of them, and not a combination of two. In particular, still another margin of adjustment would just be another instance of (3). After each objection, in a second sentence, identify the structural family it belongs to — or say honestly that, when pressed, it begins to collapse into one of (1)–(5).
