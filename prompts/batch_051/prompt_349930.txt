Two friends of mine got into it at a party about the shutdown problem for AI agents. Dan said people overstate it: if an agent is genuinely uncertain about what we want, it should welcome a shutdown button, because being switched off is evidence that it was doing the wrong thing; corrigibility would then fall out of ordinary rationality, with no explicit shutdown term in the utility function. Maya replied that this only works if shutdowns are informative. Real shutdowns are noisy—fat fingers, budget cuts, someone misreading a log—and an agent that treats shutdown as evidence will try to make sure shutdowns never look like bad news, which means itll manipulate the operators instead of the button. Dan answered: then restrict the evidence; let the agent treat only deliberate, well-informed shutdowns as informative, and ignore or even resist noisy ones—resisting an uninformative shutdown isnt the problem we were trying to solve. Maya said that just moves the problem into the classifier: either the agent learns to judge which shutdowns are informed, in which case it can game that judgment, or we hand-code the distinction, in which case the corrigibility was hand-coded and didn't fall out of rationality at all. Please adjudicate this move by move: who's right about which specific point, and what are both of them missing?
