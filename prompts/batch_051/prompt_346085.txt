Last night I got into a long argument with a friend who's into panpsychism, and I keep going back and forth about one thing. She kept saying that if the fundamental bits of matter already have some kind of proto-experiential interior, then there's no arbitrary point where consciousness suddenly shows up in bigger systems. I tried to push back with the William James point from The Principles of Psychology where he basically says you can pack a hundred tiny feelings together as tightly as you want and each one still stays shut inside itself, blinded to the others; you can't sum them into a single new feeling. We went around on this for a while, and her answer was that I was thinking of combination as if it were just physical assembly, like putting grains of sand in a bag. That's stayed with me. So here's my actual question: if all those micro-subjects are causally interacting with each other in the brain, why isn't that enough for them to jointly constitute one bigger subject, the same way atoms jointly constitute a molecule? Why isn't the combination problem just a request for a mechanism, which neuroscience could in principle fill in later, rather than a real conceptual objection to panpsychism?
