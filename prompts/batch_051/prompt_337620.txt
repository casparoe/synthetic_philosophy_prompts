We think a person who spends twenty years in a basement becoming a genuinely magnificent violinist, and then never plays for anyone, is a romantic figure, maybe even a hero. A person who spends the same twenty years becoming the world's best at a dead video game nobody else plays—or faster than anyone at memorizing phone books—strikes us as a bit tragic, like they wasted a life. Both mastered something extremely hard, with no audience and no benefit to anyone, and maybe with no interest in benefit at all. What's doing the work in the first judgment? Is there a principle that makes the violinist's excellence valuable but the phonebook champion's excellence not, or are we just prejudiced about which activities feel dignified? Give me the strongest justification for the asymmetry, and then your honest verdict on whether it survives.
