A colleague of mine works on verisimilitude, and her testbed consists of miniature worlds built from a handful of independent yes–no facts. In each case she records a verdict about which of two theories is closer to the whole truth, where a theory asserts some facts and stays silent on the rest. Here are three of her recent cases.

1. Truth: p, q, and r are all true. Theory X asserts that p and q are true, and is silent about r. Theory Y asserts that p is false, and is silent about q and r. Her verdict: X is closer to the truth.

2. Truth: p, q, r, s, and t are all true. Theory X asserts that p, q, r, and s are true, and that t is false. Theory Y asserts only that p is true, and is silent about q, r, s, and t. Her verdict: X is closer to the truth.

3. Truth: p, q, and r are all true. Theory X asserts that p and q are true, and that r is false. Theory Y asserts only that p is true, and is silent about q and r. Her verdict: Y is closer to the truth.

Now for the new case: the truth is that p, q, r, s, t, and u are all true. Theory X asserts that p, q, r, and s are true, and that t and u are false. Theory Y asserts only that p is true, and is silent about the rest.

What should she say about the new case, if she is being consistent with her earlier verdicts? Identify the plausible rules for ranking closeness to the truth that fit all three of her verdicts, and give at least two such rules that disagree about the new case, saying what each predicts. Then give your best prediction, with a reason, and state the simplest further comparison whose outcome would decide which rule she is really using.
