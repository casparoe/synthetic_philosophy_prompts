me and a friend spent a whole evening arguing about whether you can ever believe something on less than sufficient evidence. he kept coming back to Clifford's essay The Ethics of Belief, where Clifford says it is always wrong, for anyone, to believe anything without enough evidence, and he treated that as settled. i kept coming back to James's lecture The Will to Believe, where James says that when a decision is live, forced, and momentous, and the intellect can't decide it either way, the passional side of our nature may legitimately make the call. on their face those two just contradict. my friend says they can be recociled, because Clifford is talking about beliefs you form in matters you could investigate further and about letting your belief outrun the evidence that's already available, while James is talking about cases where suspending judgment is itself a risky choice, like having faith in another person or a religious commitment. i don't buy it, because James's whole point is that the evidence genuinely doesn't decide it and you still get to believe, and Clifford seemed to be saying exactly that no practical stake can make that okay. so what i want to know is: are these actually reconcilable without making one of them a special case that the other already took himself to be ruling out? walk through the most serious candidate reconciliations (different senses of belief, different evils being targeted, different contexts of inquiry vs. living) and say which ones are honest readings and which are special pleading. if the honest reading is that they just disagree, what exactly is the disagreement, and what would have to be true for each of them to be right?
