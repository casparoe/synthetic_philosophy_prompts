My civics textbook gives this definition: a strike is a temporary, collective stoppage of work by employees, intended to pressure an employer into meeting a demand. I am trying to apply that definition to two borderline cases. First, a data strike: the employees of a company that sells an analytics product keep doing all their ordinary tasks—coding, meetings, answering support tickets—but collectively refuse to enter their work into the shared database that generates the product clients pay for, so the company's output decays even though nobody stops working. Second, a sick-out: every teacher in a district reports sick on the same day, so classes cannot run; there is no picket line and no formal announcement, but the superintendent knows the absences are coordinated. Go through each of the four elements—temporary, collective, stoppage of work, employees pressuring an employer—for each case. Which of the two, if either, is a strike? And does either case show that one element is misstated—for example, that "stoppage of work" should really be something like "withdrawal of the labor the employer is paying for"?
