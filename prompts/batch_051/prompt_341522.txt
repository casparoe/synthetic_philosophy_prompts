My sister's just been diagnosed with ME/CFS and I've gone down a bit of a rabbit hole about whether it's a "real disease", since her GP clearly doesn't take it seriously. I was talking to my flatmate, who did a philosophy of medicine module last year, and she gave me this summary of the debate. She said it basically comes down to naturalism vs normativism about health. Naturalists, she said, think disease is an objective biological notion: an organ or system failing to do what it's designed to do, measured against normal species functioning, so whether something is a disease is a scientific question and your values don't enter into it. Normativists think the opposite: there's no scientific fact of the matter, because "disease" just picks out whatever conditions a society happens to disvalue, so whether ME/CFS counts is ultimately a social or political call. She then said both views are obviously in trouble, since naturalists can't explain why homosexuality was classified as a disease despite no malfunction, and normativists make disease weirdly relative to whoever's in power. Her conclusion was that the sensible position is clearly the middle ground, where you need both a biological dysfunction and a value judgement that the dysfunction is bad. This felt a bit too neat to me, and I suspect she's made one side into a straw man, but I don't know enough to say which bits are unfair. Can you go through her summary and tell me what a careful naturalist would accept and push back on, what a normativist would wince at, and whether her "middle ground follows" conclusion actually follows or whether she's smuggling in assumptions?
