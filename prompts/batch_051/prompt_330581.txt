I'm a retired physical chemist and I've been spending my retirement half-seriously trying to learn philosophy, lately through an online course on philosophy of mathematics. For the final assignment Ive drafted some notes for a short essay on what it means to understand a proof, and I'd be grateful for comments before I write the thing up. I'm mostly interested in whether the central idea is actually distinct, or whether it secretly collapses into the view I'm attacking, and where it's weakest.

The view I'm pushing against is the procedural-fluency view: that understanding a proof just is being able to reproduce it, apply it to new cases, and answer standard questions about it. My notes argue this misses something, but I want to avoid saying the missing thing is a private mental glow or an 'aha' feeling that happens to come along with proficiency. So my proposal is that understanding is a matter of representing the *dependence structure* of a proof: which steps are load-bearing, which assumptions protect which inferences, what would break if you weakened a hypothesis or reordered two subarguments, and how the conclusion is sensitive to those changes. The essay's main example is a student who can flawlessly reproduce the induction showing a degree-n polynomial over a field has at most n roots, versus another student who can't reproduce it from memory but can say that the induction bottoms out because polynomial division by x−r gives you a linear factor for each root, and can tell you where the argument would fail if the coefficient ring had zero divisors or if evaluation stopped behaving over a noncommutative ring. My intuition is that the second student understands the proof better even though she's less fluent; the first has procedural fluency without understanding. But here's my worry, and I want comments on it: is "representing dependence structure" just procedural fluency at one remove? After all, knowing what would break if you changed a step is itself a disposition to answer counterfactual questions, and a sufficiently good test battery could presumably measure it. A philosopher friend told me this sounds like behaviorism-vs-mentalism reheated, which is fair, but I think the dependence-structure idea is meant to be a third thing. I also wonder whether the essay should say anything about the phenomenology — the feeling of the proof 'clicking' — or whether thats exactly the private-mental-glow trap I should stay away from. Be blunt where this is muddled; I can take it.
