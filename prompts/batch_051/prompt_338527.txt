I teach a seminar on cultural evolution, and last week a student tried to settle a dispute by saying: "Memes are more than a metaphor." The context was this: another student had just argued that talk of memes is at best a loose analogy, because a joke, a dance step, or a piece of internet content is reshaped by every person who passes it on and is never copied with anything like genetic fidelity. The slogan was doing real dialectical work — it was meant to license moving from "culture is merely similar to genes" to "culture is the same kind of process," and to justify treating cultural variants as units of selection rather than as an illustrative figure of speech.

I want to hand the class a disambiguation of that one sentence. Lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making, from the nearly trivial to the very demanding. Carve the readings along at least these lines: whether the claim requires only variation plus selective retention, or additionally requires discrete, countable, faithfully copied variants; whether "replicator" is being used in the formal sense from the gene-culture debates (an entity that passes its structure largely intact through replication) or in some looser population-level sense; whether cumulative cultural adaptation must be explained without appeal to foresight or the intelligent design of variants; and whether the sentence claims only that the metaphor is useful or that memes exist as real entities with their own dynamics. For each reading, say in one or two sentences what would have to be true of cultural transmission for that reading to be true. Then give a verdict on the reading: platitude, substantive and defensible, genuinely contentious, probably false, or too underspecified to assess. Then say which of these readings would actually support the move the speaker used the slogan to make — from loose analogy to same-kind process — and whether the reading the speaker most likely intended is the one the argument actually needs.

Return the analysis as a JSON object with the following fields.

"slogan": the string being analyzed.

"readings": an array of objects, one per reading, each with:
- "id": a short identifier such as "R1".
- "label": a concise name for the reading.
- "precise_claim": the thesis the slogan would express on this reading, as one complete sentence.
- "conditions": one or two sentences on what would have to be true of cultural transmission for the reading to be true.
- "verdict": one of "platitude", "substantive-defensible", "contentious", "likely-false", or "underspecified".
- "supports_move": "yes", "no", or "partially".
- "note": one or two sentences justifying the verdict and the supports_move judgment.

"likely_intended_reading": the id and label of the reading the speaker probably meant, with a sentence explaining why.

"readings_the_move_needs": which reading or readings would actually carry the argumentative weight of moving from loose analogy to same-kind process, and whether the likely intended reading is among them.

"readings_left_unseparated": any pairs of readings that cannot be told apart without further empirical or theoretical commitments, and a sentence on what additional commitment would separate them.
