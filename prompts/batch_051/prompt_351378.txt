Last night I had a long argument with a friend about whether maximal moral goodness is really a desirable ideal. He distilled his side into the following argument, and although I feel as though I have been outmaneuvered, I cannot tell whether the argument is valid or exactly where it breaks. Here it is.

(1) A life of maximal moral goodness is one in which the agent never fails to do what she has decisive moral reason to do.

(2) Moral reasons are impartial reasons: they arise from giving equal weight to every person's interests, and they override any nonmoral reasons with which they conflict.

(3) Therefore, a maximally morally good agent makes every practical decision from the impartial point of view, and is prepared at any moment to abandon any particular attachment—a child, a marriage, a life's work—whenever impartial calculation says that abandoning it would make things go best overall.

(4) No human life lived that way can be a good life for the person living it. A life in which every particular commitment is perpetually hostage to impartial calculation lacks the wholeheartedness that makes a life worth choosing, for oneself or for someone one loves.

(5) So a life of maximal moral goodness is not a good life for the person living it.

(6) If a life of maximal moral goodness is not a good life for the person living it, then maximal moral goodness is not desirable as a personal ideal.

(7) Therefore, maximal moral goodness is not desirable as a personal ideal.

I suspect something happens between (2) and (3)—a slide from "morality counts everyone's interests equally" to "morality requires always deciding from the impartial point of view"—but my friend countered that once impartial reasons override all others, that slide is legitimate. Is the argument valid? If it is not, identify the exact step at which it fails and explain why. If a step is true only on a particular reading, give the strongest such reading and say whether the argument still goes through once that reading is made explicit. I want a precise diagnosis, not a survey of positions. Keep your answer to at most 500 words.
