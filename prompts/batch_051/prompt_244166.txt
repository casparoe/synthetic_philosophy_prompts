I'm trying to draft a joint statement for an adversarial collaboration between two researchers who keep arguing about what counts as the same mechanism when an anomaly detector flags something. They both work on a monitoring system for some industrial process, so their examples can be concrete. One of them, call her the structuralist, says two stretches of the process instantiate the same mechanism just in case they share the same causal structure: same variables, same arrows, same functional forms. On her view a change in parameter values, initial conditions, or the level of background noise isnt a change of mechanism until the causal graph itself changes. The other, call him the pragmatist, reckons that's the wrong grain. He says mechanism identity is partly interest-relative: whether a difference counts as a new mechanism depends on what the model is for, which interventions matter downstream, and how coarsely you've chosen to describe the system. So for him a mere parameter shift can be a genuine mechanism change, while a structural change that makes no difference to the interventions you care about can still be the same mechanism for practical purposes.

Write the document their collaboration would produce. Give me:
(1) a numbered list of claims both can sign, covering what anomaly detection is for, why causal structure is a useful notion, and which cases are clearly same or clearly different;
(2) the residual disagreement, put first in neutral wording and then in one sentence each side would accept as a fair statement of their own view;
(3) three cruxes, each framed as a sub-question with the property that settling it would move at least one of them;
(4) for each crux, a concrete case or test both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Format the first two parts as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. I'm also curious whether part of their fight is verbal rather than about the world; if you think so, say which claims hinge on an ambiguity in 'mechanism' or 'same', and what disambiguation would make them collapse.
