I've been trying to get clear on the ergodicity-economics distinction between time averages and ensemble averages, and I built a little argument that seems to prove something impossible, so I want help locating the bad step.

Suppose there's a bet you can take each year. In any given year it either multiplies your stake by 1.9 (a 90% gain) or by 0.45 (a 55% loss), each with probability one half, independantly from year to year. You start with $100 and reinvest everything each year.

Compare two setups.

(1) In a single year, the expected multiplier is (0.5)(1.9) + (0.5)(0.45) = 1.175, i.e., a 17.5% expected return.

(2) Now imagine 10,000 people each put $100 into the bet for exactly one year, independently, and then pool and split the total equally. By the law of large numbers, about half of them have their money multiplied by 1.9 and about half by 0.45, so the pot is roughly 5,000 × $190 + 5,000 × $45 = $1,175,000, and each person gets about $117.50 — essentially the expected value.

(3) Next, put a single person through the same bet year after year for 10,000 years instead. This is just the same 10,000 bets spread across time rather than spread across people. So by the same law-of-large-numbers reasoning, the arithmetic average of the realised yearly multipliers should converge to 1.175.

(4) Since the person's wealth is just the starting $100 multiplied by those yearly multipliers, their long-run yearly growth must therefore also converge to 17.5%, and they should end up at least as well off as the pooled group — in fact much better off, because of compounding.

(5) But that can't be right. For the compounding case the typical long-run growth per year is sqrt(1.9 × 0.45) = sqrt(0.855) ≈ 0.925, i.e., a loss of about 7.5% per year, and wealth converges to zero almost surely.

So the same bet looks both almost surely ruinous and almost surely profitable. Step (4) is my main suspect, but I can't articulate exactly what goes wrong in it. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails and why? And is the pooled group or the repeated single agent the right guide to whether someone should take this bet?

Also, tell me how confident you are in your diagnosis and which point you're least sure about.
