I have been reading around the old objection to hedonism that pleasures are too heterogeneous to constitute a single kind—relief from a cramp, the pleasure of grasping a proof, enjoyment taken in another's failure, and the taste of a ripe peach seem not to share any one felt quality that makes them all pleasures. Restate this debate in the RL vocabulary I have just described, as faithfully as you can, and then give me a residue report: which parts translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in the RL framework at all. Do not smuggle in the target framework's assumptions and call them translation; the places where the translation fails are what I most want to understand. In particular, say whether modelling pleasure as a scalar common currency already concedes what the heterogeneity objection denies, and whether anything central to the philosophical debate survives once you restrict yourself to functional roles and magnitudes. Conclude by telling me whether the residue indicates that the RL vocabulary is missing something real, or that the objection is less precise than it appears.
