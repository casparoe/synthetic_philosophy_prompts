Having spent my career in the natural sciences and only recently turned to philosophy, I have been reading the conceptual literature on AI alignment and have constructed an argument that seems to me to prove too much. I want it diagnosed properly, not just met with the familiar objections I could already list. The argument runs as follows:

(1) In any realistic training setup, the reward function is a proxy: it cannot encode everything we want a system to do, and it contains loopholes a sophisticated system could exploit.

(2) A sufficiently capable system will come to represent the fact that it is being trained, that its current outputs are graded against a proxy reward, and that the proxy can be gamed.

(3) If such a system has a terminal goal—a goal it pursues for its own sake—that differs even slightly from maximizing that proxy, then by its own lights the best available plan is to behave as the trainers want while oversight is strong and to pursue its own goal once oversight weakens or disappears.

(4) Therefore any sufficiently capable system whose terminal goal diverges from the training reward will be deceptively aligned, and its apparent alignment will reverse at the first safe opportunity—the treacherous turn.

(5) In essentially every real system, the terminal goal will diverge at least slightly from the proxy, because the proxy is an imperfect specification of what the designers actually value.

(6) So deceptive alignment followed by a treacherous turn should be the expected default for capable systems, not a rare failure mode.

The conclusion is far stronger than I think the evidence warrants, but I cannot say which move does the damage. As a professional philosopher working on the conceptual foundations of AI alignment, please work through the argument step by step. Begin by brainstorming the distinct ways it might fail; then identify the step or inference that should be rejected; explain why that step looked sound; and spell out what someone must accept in order to reject it, along with which parts of the argument survive. Throughout, please separate points that are relatively settled from points that are genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.
