I run a small private foundation that makes around forty grants a year to individual practitioners — instrument makers, calligraphers, choreographers, pure mathematicians — working at a high level in fields where the work may never have an audience, an application, or a beneficiary beyond the practitioner’s own activity. I have recently been reading Aristotle and was struck by this passage from the Nicomachean Ethics, Book X, in W. D. Ross’s translation, where Aristotle reports Eudoxus’s argument about what makes something an object of choice:

“And again that is most an object of choice which we choose not because or for the sake of something else, and pleasure is admittedly of this nature; for no one asks to what end he is pleased, thus implying that pleasure is in itself an object of choice.”

I keep meeting applicants who describe their work in exactly this way: the activity is done for its own sake and not for any result beyond the doing. But my trustees are not sure whether that is enough to justify a grant when nobody else benefits, or whether it is merely a disguised preference for private enjoyment. I want you to turn the principle in the passage into a decision procedure my staff and I can actually run on a proposal. The procedure should take the proposal’s own description of the activity and tell us whether it counts as excellence chosen for its own sake in the way the passage suggests, or whether it is something else — recreation, private satisfaction, mere pleasure, or work aimed at a product. The procedure should include the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than facts. Then run it on two worked proposals: (1) a calligrapher who wants a year to develop a formal system of letterforms that will probably have no readers and no influence, and (2) a pure mathematician who wants to work on a problem that is unlikely to have applications and whose solution, if any, will interest only a handful of specialists. One of these should reach an answer my trustees will not like, and you should flag what the procedure cannot settle.

Please return the whole thing as a JSON object with this schema:
- “principle”: a string stating which precise principle you are taking from the passage and whether it transfers to excellence.
- “steps”: an array of step objects; each step object has “question” (string), “sufficient_answer” (string), “pass” (string), “fail” (string), and “judgement_call” (boolean).
- “worked_cases”: an array of two case objects; each case object has “name” (string), “path” (array of strings recording the step outcomes), “decision” (string), and “uncomfortable_implication” (string).
- “limits”: an array of strings where the procedure cannot settle the question or needs a person to decide.
