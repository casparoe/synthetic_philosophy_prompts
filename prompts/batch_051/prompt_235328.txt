I'm a genetic counselor at a fertility clinic. Once a week or so a patient says something that turns on origin essentialism — whether a child who came from a different sperm, egg, or embryo would still be the same person. It comes up with embryo selection, after a selective reduction, with donor-gamete families, and with savior siblings conceived as a tissue match for an older child. I need a reusable procedure for deciding what to actually say in the room: when to gently correct the metaphysics, when to let a dubious claim stand because it's doing emotional work, and when to refer out to a therapist or ethicist. The philosophy I've tried to read isn't operationalized at all: origin essentialism says a person couldn't have come from different gametes, but some locate the essential origin in the zygote, some in the particular sperm-and-egg pair, and the tolerance view says small differences in origin would have been acceptable, so I can't tell which pieces are actually settled. Please turn that into a checklist I can run mid-session in my head, with each response sayable to a patient in plain language rather than as a philosophy lecture, flag the places where the metaphysics runs out and my values have to decide, and run it on three cases: a mother after a selective reduction says the surviving twin is also the one she lost; a donor-conceived teenager asks whether she'd be someone else if the clinic had used a different donor; and parents ask whether a future IVF cycle could give them the same baby as an embryo they chose not to transfer. Include at least one case where the procedure tells me to say something I won't want to say.
