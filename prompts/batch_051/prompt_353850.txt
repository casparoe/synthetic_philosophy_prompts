Epistemic status: been chewing on this for weeks and it won't resolve.

I keep finding myself committed to a view I don't fully endorse. The view is that what matters about a model's output is the output: accuracy, calibration, robustness, how gracefully it fails when it fails. On this view interpretability is purely instrumental—worth paying for only because it helps us debug, audit, and maybe prove safety properties. If two systems have the same output properties, the more legible one isn't epistemically better; it's just more pleasant to interact with.

But I can't shake the opposite intuition. Take two diagnostic models. One is 96% accurate and it's reasoning is a tangle of weights I can't inspect. The other is 93% accurate and I can read off the gears—what features it used, how its inference composed, where it could have gone wrong—and check the chain myself. Holding everything else equal, I don't just prefer the second for practical reasons like easier oversight. I have a strong sense that I know something when the legible model says the tumor is malignant that I don't know when the black box says it. It feels like an epistemic gap, not a comfort gap.

So the clash: the output-only theory says this intuition is a scaffolding bias—a useful heuristic from dealing with humans, where opacity correlates with deception or error, but not something that should become a terminal value. The intuition says there's a distinct epistemic good, something like understanding rather than being handed true answers, and a model whose reasoning I can't follow isn't a source of knowledge for me even when it's a source of truth.

I want the strongest steelman of each side and then an actual verdict, not a both-sides-are-kind-of-right hedge. Which should yield, under what conditions? Should I debunk the intuition as a prior that doesn't pay rent, or amend the theory so legibility is a good in its own right? If you amend it, say exactly what legibility contributes that accuracy doesn't, how that would cash out in a training objective, and at what accuracy cost I should still prefer the legible model. And tell me what would change your mind, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
