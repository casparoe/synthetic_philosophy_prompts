In one story in the Zhuangzi, after his wife dies, Zhuangzi is found beating on a basin and singing; when Huizi reproaches him, he replies that she has merely gone through another turn in a series of transformations—from lacking breath, form, and life, to acquiring them, and now losing them again—in the same way the four seasons proceed, and that further wailing would show he does not understand how things work. In another story, Zhuangzi happens upon a bare skull, which later appears to him in a dream and praises being dead as a release from rulers above, subjects below, and the work of the changing seasons, a rest that endures as long as heaven and earth. Offered the chance to have its body, family, and home restored, the skull refuses, saying it could not trade its condition for even a king's happiness. The first story treats death as a natural change to be met with equanimity; the second treats it as a positively enviable state. Are these two stories giving one coherent view of death, or do they pull in opposite directions?
