Was arguing with a freind last night about whether a good historical narrative actually explains an event or just flatters our hindsight bias. His line was that if a story ties everything together and makes the outcome seem inevitable, that's explanation full stop. I kept coming back with the standard narrative-fallacy point that after the fact you can always arrange the details so whatever happened looks like it had to happen. After he left I couldn't stop noticing a structural parallel with curve-fitting in statistics: in-sample fit versus out-of-sample prediction. A model with enough free parameters can fit any past data perfectly without telling you anything about the next point; a historical narrative seems to have that same property, where the free parameters are which facts get included as salient, where the story starts, what gets left out, etc. So the coherence of the story is just in-sample fit. Is this a real identity of structure or am I pattern-matching? What would have to be true of historical explanation for anything to transfer from overfitting — e.g., do historians have an analogue of held-out data or regularization, and would that even make sense for one-off events? And does the parallel predict something false about how good historians actually work?
