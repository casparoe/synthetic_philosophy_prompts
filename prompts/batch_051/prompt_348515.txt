I am a second-year undergraduate reading philosophy, politics and economics, and I am currently taking a module on judgment aggregation and democratic theory. I sit as a student representative on a university working group that is redrafting the voting rules of a three-member academic appeals panel, and I have been asked to prepare the document that an adversarial collaboration between the two factions on the group would produce. I would like you to draft that document.

The panel decides whether a student's degree classification should be changed. A change is made if and only if both of the following findings are made:

(F1) the examiners departed materially from the published marking criteria when grading the dissertation;
(F2) that departure affected the student's final classification.

The outcome, O, is therefore the conjunction of F1 and F2.

The first faction holds to a premise-based procedure: each member votes separately on F1 and on F2; the panel's decision follows from the majority's verdict on each premise; and those majority findings constitute the panel's published reasons. The second faction holds to a conclusion-based procedure: each member votes directly on O; the majority's verdict on O is authoritative; and the panel does not attempt to construct a single majority-endorsed set of grounds for its decision, though individual members may record their own reasons.

Both factions accept the force of the discursive dilemma. They agree that a circumstance such as the following is possible: Member A accepts F1, rejects F2, and therefore rejects O; Member B rejects F1, accepts F2, and therefore rejects O; Member C accepts F1 and F2, and therefore accepts O. In that circumstance, majority voting on each premise drives the panel to O, while majority voting on O drives the panel to not-O. Both factions also accept that the panel must publish its decisions, that its procedures must be workable in practice, and that no procedure should impose unreasonable demands on members.

Please draft the joint statement this adversarial collaboration should have produced. It should include:

(1) a numbered list of claims both factions can accept, phrased so as to be neither vacuous nor slanted;
(2) a table of the residual disagreements, each stated in wording that both factions would accept as an accurate statement of the two views;
(3) a section identifying the cruxes, that is, the two or three sub-questions whose resolution would most plausibly move at least one faction, and for each crux a concrete case or test that both factions would agree bears on it, with each faction's prediction recorded in advance;
(4) a closing paragraph that both factions could sign.

In drafting the statement, please attend to the possibility that some apparent disagreements are only verbal, for instance if the two factions mean different things by "the panel's reasons", but do not flatten substantive disagreements into terminology in order to make the document easier to sign. The document should be written in formal British English, and it should not rely on or cite any external sources; it should stand on the characterisation of the two positions and the panel's task alone.
