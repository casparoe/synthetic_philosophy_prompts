I am a high school student preparing a proposal for a national civics competition on the question of how to decide when a democracy is genuinely backsliding rather than simply being described that way by its partisan opponents. I want the proposal to take the form of a specific classification mechanism, and I would like it analyzed as a mechanism rather than defended as a moral position.

The mechanism I have in mind works as follows. Each year, an independent commission convenes a jury of two hundred citizens of the country being assessed, selected at random from the voter rolls and paid for two weeks of full-time service. A professional secretariat prepares an evidence file scoring the country on six indicators: whether election administration has been stripped of independence, whether courts are being packed or purged, whether public broadcasting and media licensing have been captured by the incumbent party, whether anti-corruption prosecutions have become one-sided, whether police treatment of protests has changed in law or practice, and whether the legislature can still meaningfully oversee the executive. The jury hears presentations from the government, from opposition parties, and from the secretariat, then votes by secret ballot. A country is formally classified as backsliding only if two consecutive annual juries each vote to classify by at least a two-thirds majority. The classification is published with the margin but without the jurors' names, and it expires automatically after two consecutive years without such a vote.

Please trace how this mechanism would actually behave. What incentives does it create for incumbent governments, opposition parties, private media, foreign governments and investors, and citizens selected for jury service? How could each of these actors game the system, and where is it likely to break first? Once the label carries real consequences, what second-order effects should we expect, such as preemptive changes before a jury convenes or campaigns to discredit the classification? Finally, could the most serious failure modes be fixed by adjusting the mechanism, or would the fixes tend to make the process easier to capture?
