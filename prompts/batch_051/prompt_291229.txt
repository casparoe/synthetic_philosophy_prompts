I'm working on a piece about why breaking an oath feels like a different kind of betrayal than just flaking on a promise, and I keep tripping over the same ancient example: the Greek gods swore by the river Styx, and that was the one oath even Zeus couldn't squirm out of — the story goes that a god who broke a Styx oath got laid flat for a year and then spent nine more barred from nectar and ambrosia and the council of the gods. Before I start calling philosophers for comment I want the full spec, not a survey. So: write the desiderata a theory of oaths and their binding force has to satisfy. What cases does it have to handle — the coerced oath, the oath to a government the person already considers illegitimate, the marriage vow, the professional oath, the courtroom oath from someone who doesn't believe in anything to swear by, the person who asks to "affirm" instead of swear, the informal "I swear on my life," and the god-level case where the enforcer is a river and the penalty is a decade of cosmic exile? What distinctions can't it collapse — between an oath that creates a new obligation and one that only strengthens an obligation that already existed, between "this oath was void from the start" and "this oath binds but should be broken," between the wrong of breaking faith and the ritual pollution the Greeks seem to have been tracking, between the act of swearing and the content of what's sworn? What has the theory got to say about why the ceremony matters — is swearing just costly signaling, is it a way of inviting a third party in as enforcer or witness, or does the Styx matter because it turns a private resolution into a public debt? Which empirical facts does it have to respect — that oath-taking shows up in unrelated cultures, that people treat sworn statements as weightier even with no legal penalty attached, that some people refuse to swear on principle and the law mostly lets them affirm instead, that oathbreakers get a distinct kind of untrustworthiness attached to them rather than just being called unreliable? Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's taste — "the oath has to be underwritten by an actual god" is obviously a taste, but I want the whole list sorted that way. And then the part I actually want for the piece: tell me which single requirement does the most to knock out the lazy versions of the theory, and whether any two of the genuine requirements can't be fully satisfied at once.
