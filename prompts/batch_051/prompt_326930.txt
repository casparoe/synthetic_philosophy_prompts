I've been trying to get clear on how much work the Solomonoff prior actually does in formalizing Occam's razor. The proposal, as I understand it, is that among the hypotheses consistent with the data we should prefer the one with the shortest description; more specifically, Solomonoff induction weights hypotheses by 2^{-K(h)}, where K is Kolmogorov complexity, thereby turning the informal advice "prefer simpler theories" into a precise prior. The objections I already have in hand are these: (1) the prior is defined only up to a constant that depends on the choice of universal Turing machine, so for any finite body of data, which hypothesis counts as simpler can be shifted almost arbitrarily; (2) K and the universal prior are uncomputable, so the scheme does not yield a usable inference procedure; (3) the weighting simply re-encodes the assumption that simpler hypotheses are more probable, so it presupposes Occam's razor rather than justifying it; (4) it penalizes long programs but not slow ones, so a short-but-intractable hypothesis competes on equal terms with a somewhat longer but tractable one; and (5) compression of the data is not the same as explanation of it, since a short program can reproduce the data while remaining a black-box or ad hoc encoder with none of the explanatory virtues Occam's razor is ordinarily meant to buy.

I would like one more objection to the broader project — the identification of Occam's razor with low Kolmogorov complexity or minimum description length — that is genuinely different in structure from all five above: not a rewording, a special case, or a combination of them.
