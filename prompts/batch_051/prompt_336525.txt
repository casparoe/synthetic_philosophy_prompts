In a reading group on Hume, people keep treating "causation is just constant conjunction" as the last word on the metaphysics of causation. I have seen it put to at least three different jobs: dismissing talk of causal powers in physics, diagnosing every appeal to "necessary connection" as a confusion, and ruling out the New Hume reading (the view that Hume himself accepted real necessary connections in nature while denying that we can know or conceive them). One sentence cannot do all three jobs in the same sense. I would like you to separate the precise claims that could be hiding in "causation is just constant conjunction." Present them as a menu: for each, state its content, classify it as an epistemological, metaphysical, semantic, or methodological claim, and give a verdict — platitude, substantive and defensible, or false. Then say which reading is being invoked, and which reading would have to be true, for each of the three uses I listed, and explain whether the New Hume reading undermines, or is undermined by, the slogan once the slogan is disambiguated. Finish by saying which of these readings, if any, a regularity theorist about causation is actually committed to, and whether the slogan fares better as a methodological maxim for scientific explanation than as a metaphysical thesis.
