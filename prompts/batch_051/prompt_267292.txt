I'm a software engineer (ML, mostly) and I've been arguing with coworkers about whether model hallucination is lying, bullshit, or just error. I tried to turn my view into a numbered argument, but now it looks like it proves two different things. I cant find the bad step, maybe there isn't one, but I'd like someone to go through it carefully. Please use American English.

(1) A lie is when someone intentionally says something they believe is false in order to get another person to believe it.
(2) An ordinary error is when someone says something false because they believe it's true.
(3) LLMs don't have beliefs or intentions in a literal sense, so a hallucination isn't a lie or an ordinary error.

(4) Frankfurt's point in On Bullshit is that lying and truth-telling both care about the truth, just in opposite directions: the truth-teller wants to convey it, the liar wants to conceal it. The bullshitter doesn't care either way; he's not oriented around the truth at all, only around what the talk does for him.
(5) When a model produces a hallucinated answer, the generation process also isn't oriented around the truth; it's oriented around producing plausible next tokens. Whatever checking happens is outside the model.
(6) So hallucinations fit Frankfurt's bullshit better than they fit lying or error.

(7) But Frankfurt is describing a human activity. The bullshitter is a person who could care about the truth and doesn't, usually because he's trying to get away with something, manage an impression, or speak to the occasion.
(8) A model isn't a person, doesn't have aims, and isn't trying to get away with anything. It isn't indifferent in the intentional way Frankfurt describes; it just has no relation to truth at all.
(9) So (5)-(6) give me that hallucinations are bullshit, and (7)-(8) give me that they're missing the thing that makes bullshit bullshit. Those can't both be right, so one of the steps has to break.

Walk me through the argument step by step, tell me whether it's valid, and if not, say exactly which premise or inference fails and on what reading. If the right move is that 'hallucination' is its own category and can't be collapsed into any of the three, just say that. Also tell me whether I'm relying on a version of Frankfurt's view that's looser than the real one.
