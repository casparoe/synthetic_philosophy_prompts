I've spent the semester in a reading group on the continuum hypothesis, half math PhD students and half philosophers, and we've arrived at a weird impasse. Its not that we disagree about the formalism: everyone accepts that Gödel showed CH can't be refuted from ZFC and Cohen showed it can't be proved from ZFC, so it's independent. The split is elsewhere. The Platonists in the room say there is still a fact about the cumulative hierarchy — the set-theoretic universe is one definite thing, and we just haven't found the right axiom to settle CH. The multiverse people say this mistakes the subject matter: talk of "the" universe of sets is a shorthand, and asking whether CH is true outright is like asking whether the parallel postulate is true without saying which geometry you're in. Two members have started interrupting each other with the same pair of examples every week, and nobody has moved since October.

My working theory is that the argument is not really about mathematics at all. It looks to me like a dispute about what it takes for a statement to be about something, or about what counts as evidence for a new axiom — one side treats new axioms almost like empirical hypotheses, judged by their consequences; the other treats them like explications that have to be intrinsically evident. But I worry I'm just re-describing the disagreement in fancier words. Diagnose it: is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one about the word "set," or two groups answering different questions under one heading? And tell me what would actually count as progress — a new theorem, a conceptual distinction, a clearer question — or whether we should expect none.
