I have been trying to figure out what I actually mean by self-control, and my judgments about particular cases seem to pull in different directions. Deleting social media apps and leaving my phone with my parents during exam week feels like self-control, even though by the time I would be tempted, the temptation has already been removed. Yet a classmate whose parents install blocking software on his computer seems to me not to be exercising self-control at all, even though the effect is similar. When a friend forces herself to stay under a strict calorie limit and then feels both proud and miserable, I want to say that is repression rather than self-control. When I bite my tongue after a teacher says something false about me because answering back would only make things worse, I call that self-control. When someone trains through pain to make the team, though, I hesitate: it might just be prudence about getting what she wants rather than self-control. And when another friend decides not to date during exam year despite wanting to, I cannot tell whether that is self-control, prudence, or fear. Is there one account of self-control, understood as a virtue as opposed to prudence or repression, that captures these verdicts, or close? Which of my verdicts does it force me to drop, and what does it say about a person who deliberately mocks their own interest in an expensive hobby until the desire dies out, or someone who uses a tool that fines them money whenever they skip studying?
