I am an undergraduate taking a course on Descartes and early modern epistemology. Over the past several weeks we have focused on the question of what exactly the cogito proves, and I have settled on the following four-part test. A proposition p counts as something the cogito actually establishes only if:

(1) p remains certain even when I suppose that an all-powerful deceiver is causing all of my thoughts and experiences;
(2) p can be known without any reliance on the senses or on claims about the external world;
(3) p is first-personal: it tells me that I am thinking or that I exist at the very moment I entertain it, rather than merely recording that some thinking is going on somewhere;
(4) p is strong enough to serve as a non-circular starting point from which Descartes can begin to rebuild knowledge.

Now consider the proposition "There is thinking occurring now." It seems to pass (1) and (2) easily: if a deceiver is at work, then at least the thinking involved in the deception is occurring, and no sensory premise is needed to know this. But the proposition says nothing about me; it would still be true if the demon, and not I, were doing all the thinking.

Treat this as a borderline case for the four-part test. Does "There is thinking occurring now" qualify as something the cogito proves? Please work through each criterion honestly and separately, identify the places where the criteria underdetermine the verdict, and then answer the further question: is criterion (3) doing genuine philosophical work, or is it quietly smuggling in the very "I" that the cogito was supposed to earn?
