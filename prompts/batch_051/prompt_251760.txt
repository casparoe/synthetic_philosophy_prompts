I recently read a short article explaining Nietzsche's essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life, and I would like an accuracy audit before I rely on it. The article summarized the essay roughly as follows.

Nietzsche's target is the modern habit of accumulating historical knowledge for its own sake, which he thinks drains the present of the courage to act. Against this, he insists that history is justified only insofar as it serves life, and he distinguishes three healthy ways it can do so. Monumental history studies the great figures and achievements of the past in order to show that greatness was once possible and can be possible again; it is meant for active, striving people, and its lesson is that we should take past exemplars as models to emulate in our own circumstances. Antiquarian history lovingly preserves the inherited past in its detail and gives a person or a people a stable sense of rootedness; its risk is that it can become indiscriminate and mummify the present by rejecting anything new. Critical history is for those oppressed by the past: it hauls inherited values before a tribunal, condemns what no longer deserves to survive, and thereby frees us for new beginnings. The author adds that Nietzsche regarded critical history as the most mature and most necessary of the three, since it alone actually liberates us from the past, and that the essay's ultimate lesson is to replace veneration of the past with critical scrutiny of it.

This all sounds plausible, but I suspect the account is a little too neat, especially the clean separation of the three modes and the claim that critical history is Nietzsche's preferred one. Could you tell me, point by point, what a careful reader of the essay would accept, what is defensible as a simplification, and what is distorted? In particular, where would a defender of Nietzsche's actual view object to this summary, and does the closing advice about replacing veneration with critical scrutiny follow from what precedes it? Also, does the summary leave out anything whose absence makes the overall picture misleading?
