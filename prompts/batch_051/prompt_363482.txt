I'm trying to pin down the concept of hope, and I want a working definition that does two things: it should separate hope from ordinary optimism on one side and from wishful thinking on the other, and it should let us say, without contradiction, that the same underlying state can be an epistemic vice in one case and a moral virtue in another. Here are the cases I need it to handle: a terminal patient who knows the statistics but continues planning her daughter's wedding; a gambler who sincerely believes he will beat the house because he feels lucky; an activist who expects most campaigns to fail and keeps organizing anyway; a person in an abusive relationship who keeps expecting her partner to change despite years of contrary evidence; a doctor who tells a dying patient there is always a reason for hope; and a severely depressed person who cannot form expectations about the future at all. I lean toward thinking the gambler is merely wishful and the activist genuinely hopes, but I'm not confident about the others. Propose the definition, run it through all six, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and then revise it or defend the surprising verdicts.
