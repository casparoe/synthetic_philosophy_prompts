I've been chewing on a principle I call the Continuity of Qualia. The core claim is that there's no sharp line between "the same experience with a new parameter" and "a genuinely new kind of experience": the only real difference is how far you've moved in the space of possible experiences and how coarsely you happen to carve it. So the question "could a brain interface ever give a human a truly novel colour?" is, I think, badly posed — novelty is a distance, not a kind. Teal is a new experience if you've only ever seen pure blue and pure green, but nobody treats it as a breakthrough. This would also mean a "forbidden colour" like reddish-green isn't a wall in colour space, just a region we can't currently reach with the knobs we know how to turn. Is this actually a view, or just a metaphor with no payoff? Has someone already worked it out under a better name? And what's the strongest counterexample — maybe an experience whose whole structure differs, like suddenly having echolocation rather than a new shade of something?
