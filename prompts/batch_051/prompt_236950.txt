Ok so I've been teaching the ring of Gyges bit from Republic II and I keep wanting to pressure-test the standard reading with a variation that isn't just the usual "the just man stays just because he's virtuous" move. Standard setup: Glaucon tells the story of a shepherd who finds a ring that makes him invisible, and argues that a just man and an unjust man, each given such a ring, would end up doing the same things, because what actually restrains people is the fear of being caught and the desire for a good reputation, not anything about justice itself. Now here's the variant I can't settle. Suppose the ring doesn't simply make you invisible; it transfers the visible and legal traces of whatever you do onto another person of you choice. Steal, and someone else is seen doing it, leaves the fingerprints, looks guilty to everyone, and ends up punished; you're not just unsuspected, you can actually play the person who "finds" the stolen goods and earns credit. The victim loses, the scapegoat loses, and the ring-bearer's expected costs are not just zero—they're negative, because he can cash in on the aftermath.

Run Glaucon's argument through for this scapegoat ring. Does the just man now do exactly what the unjust man does, and if so, which premise has to be sharpened (the original story only prevents the wearer's reputation from being damaged; it doesn't also let him actively improve it by looking like the hero)? Or do the two come apart, and if they do, where exactly—is it at the point of actually framing an innocent person, or already at the ordinary theft? And what would either answer tell us about whether Glaucon's justice-as-compromise account was ever right about the ordinary case, where the ring is just invisibility? I keep going back and forth: half the time I think the scapegoat feature is window dressing and the conclusion is untouched; the other half I think it's the version that finally makes Glaucon's view show its teeth, because the just man has to actively destroy an innocent person rather than just quietly help himself. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then reason it through, without preamble and without restating the question.
