I'm a science journalist profiling a theoretical biologist whose research concerns biological individuality: which living systems deserve to be treated as one organism, and under what criterion. In published papers and public talks she has ruled on a number of cases, but she has never, so far as I can find, stated her criterion in a single usable form. From that record: she treats a single human being as one individual; a Portuguese man o' war as one individual, even though it is a colony of specialized zooids; a honeybee colony as one individual, a superorganism, even though the bees are physically separate animals; and a coral colony as one individual, its polyps being connected clones that grew from a single settled larva. She has also said that a wolf pack is not one individual, that a school of anchovies is not one individual, and that a grove of separately grown trees is not one individual, despite each being coordinated, bounded, or interdependent in some loose way.

I now need to anticipate her verdict on a case she has not, as far as I know, ruled on: the human holobiont — the human body together with its resident microbial populations, which are mostly acquired from the environment after birth and are metabolically entangled with the host. Should I expect her to call the holobiont one individual, and if her record does not settle the matter, what verdict would consistency with her own precedent require?

I am less interested in a bare yes or no than in the reasoning behind it. Please work through: the candidate rules that fit the verdicts she has already given, and which distinct rules fit them; where those candidate rules diverge on the holobiont and what each predicts; your best guess about what she would say, with the reason for weighting one rule over the others; and the single further case I should ask her about — the one that would most sharply force her to reveal which rule she is actually running. The useful thing for me is an honest reading of her position, including the points at which her public record underdetermines her answer.
