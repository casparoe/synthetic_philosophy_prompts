So my city has this old ordinance, section 12-6(b) of the municipal code, passed in 1979 after a bunch of complaints about boom boxes, skateboards on sidewalks, and street vendors. It says:

"Section 12-6(b). It shall be unlawful for any person, while in or upon any public street, sidewalk, plaza, park, or other public place, to use any device, instrument, object, or machine in a manner that, in the judgment of any police officer, is reasonably likely to annoy, disturb, or alarm any other person."

I build little delivery bots as a hobby. Last week I ran one in the public square: it's shoebox-sized, moves at walking speed, stops about a meter from pedestrians, holds out a tray of wrapped mints with a sign saying "free mint," and doesn't make noise besides a faint motor whir. A cop wrote me a citation under 12-6(b), saying a couple of older people were "alarmed" by a machine rolling up to them.

I'm not really asking how to fight the ticket. I'm asking what the law can even reach. Under the plain wording, does this ordinance reach me? Under the purpose of the ordinance and what the 1979 city council would have said if you asked them about a polite mint robot, does it? And under what a reasonable person reading 12-6(b) would expect, does it? Then the bigger thing: is 12-6(b) precise enough to be a criminal law at all? Im thinking of void for vagueness. Is the ordinance void on its face or only as applied to me, and does the answer shift depending on which of those interpretive approaches you're using? Which approach should win here, and does the "in the judgment of any police officer" clause make the vagueness problem fatal?
