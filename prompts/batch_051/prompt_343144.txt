I just got back from a two-hour argument with a friend about separating the art from the artist, and we ended up stuck on an asymmetry that I think is the actual center of the debate. When a physicist who did something monstrous has a law named after him, no one says you can't use the equation. A bridge by a bad person still gets driven over, and a theorem by a bad person gets cited without anyone feeling dirty. We treat those as achievements you can detach from their maker, because a derivation is valid or its not. But when the bad person made a novel or a film or an album, suddenly people talk as if the wrongness got inside the work, and they cant consume it anymore without feeling like they're endorsing something. My friend started out insisting a poem should be judged only as a poem and ended up admitting there is a director whose movies she just wont rewatch. So she had one rule for equations and another for films. I want to know whether anything actually justifies that asymmetry. Give me the strongest principle you can for treating expressive works differently from technical or theoretical ones, something about how art invites you to inhabit a perspective or see through someone elses sensibility while using a result does not, and then actually stress-test it. Test it against a technical artifact that does encode a worldview, like an algorithm shaped by a bad view of the world, and an artwork that is nearly contentless, like a string quartet. If you think the asymmetry collapses, say which direction we should move: should we get more relaxed about art made by monsters, or more suspicious of the bridges, equations, and tools made by monsters?
