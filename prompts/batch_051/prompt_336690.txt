People are broadly fine with the idea that twelve randomly selected strangers can sit on a jury and decide whether someone goes to prison. But propose that a randomly selected chamber of a few hundred citizens should write and pass the country's laws, and nearly everyone treats it as a gimmick or a recipe for disaster. Both are ordinary people making serious, coercive decisions about strangers without being elected or trained for the job. Is there a principle that really distinguishes the two cases, or is our comfort with juries just familarity while our horror at a lottery legislature is snobbery? If you think the asymmetry is justified, defend it against the strongest nearby case you can think of where a jury is doing more than applying a law someone else wrote.
