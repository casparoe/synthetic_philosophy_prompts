You run an interdisciplinary working group on wild animal welfare and conservation, and a donor will fund a six-month focused project if you can produce a two-page proposal. The intended question is whether habitat destruction reduces wild animal suffering, and more fundamentally whether the net welfare of an ecosystem is the sort of thing that can be measured and used as a conservation target. Your half-formed suspicion is that the debate keeps running together three distinct issues: first, the causal issue of what a given habitat intervention does to the distribution of pain and pleasure among the sentient individuals in the habitat; second, the axiological issue of whether individual welfare states can be aggregated into a single net-welfare measure at all, and if so whether the right currency is total welfare, average welfare, or something closer to quality-adjusted welfare; and third, the normative-conservation issue of whether biodiversity and ecosystem integrity matter in themselves or only through their effects on welfare. Write the proposal so that it states the central question precisely, argues for which of these three issues the project should settle first and why that issue is upstream of the others, explains why the existing debate leaves that issue open rather than merely unsettled, describes the work plan over the six months, and specifies what a useful result would look like and what would count as the project failing. Do not include a bibliography or citations; I will supply those separately.
