I'm an undergraduate taking a seminar on the quest of the historical Jesus. We read Albert Schweitzer's *The Quest of the Historical Jesus*, and I was struck by his concluding argument that the nineteenth-century lives of Jesus were to a large degree self-portraits dressed in first-century clothing: liberal theologians found in Jesus the ethical teacher of their own post-Enlightenment religion, and the various reconstructions tended to turn Jesus into a mirror of the scholars producing them. We then moved on to the standard criteria of authenticity—dissimilarity, embarrassment, multiple attestation, coherence, and so on—and I assumed their whole purpose was to prevent exactly this kind of projection.

In my term paper I want to use the criteria to identify a minimal core of historically secure material. But I keep running into conflicts between them. For instance, Jesus's baptism by John seems to pass the criterion of embarrassment—why would the early church invent a story in which its Lord submits to a baptizer of repentance?—yet it might fail the criterion of dissimilarity, since John's apocalyptic milieu is precisely the early Jewish context out of which Jesus's own movement grew. If the criteria conflict in a case like this, is there a principled weighting that decides the matter, or are we just supposed to fall back on scholarly judgment? More generally, I want to know whether the criteria can actually settle who Jesus was, or whether they are simply a more technically disciplined version of the same projection Schweitzer diagnosed. If they can settle it, how should I rank them when they point in different directions? If they cannot, what is the honest conclusion for a student writing a historical paper?
