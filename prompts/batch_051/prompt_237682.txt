I am trying to work out whether my reactions to cases of loyalty, whistleblowing, and betrayal reflect any single principle. Here are my verdicts.

When a billing clerk at my hospital tells me in confidence that the hospital is billing insurers for procedures that were never performed, I would report it to the regulator. That strikes me as plainly right, even obligatory. When a different colleague tells me he has been pocketing twenty dollars a week from our shared coffee fund, I would tell him to stop, but I would not report him; silence feels fine. When my closest friend confesses that he embezzled a significant sum from a previous employer fifteen years ago and was never caught, I would not go to the police; here too silence feels acceptable. But when the same friend tells me he is now skimming money from the fund that pays for his disabled clients' care, I would report him, even though it would end the friendship and feel like a betrayal. When my sister tells me she is having an affair and asks me not to tell her husband—whom I also care about and who is about to co-sign a mortgage with her—I keep the secret, though it sits badly with me. And when I imagine an intelligence contractor leaking a classified program of warrantless surveillance of journalists and lawyers, I judge the leak justified despite being illegal.

The principle I keep groping toward is roughly: a confidence may be broken only to prevent serious, ongoing, nonconsensual harm to someone other than the confider. But that principle seems to demand reporting the coffee fund and telling my sister's husband, neither of which I want to do. Is there a single principle that captures all six verdicts, or nearly all of them? If so, state it precisely and say what it predicts about a case I cannot settle: my father tells me that decades ago he caused a fatal hit-and-run that was never solved, and there is no reason to think he would offend again. If no principle captures all of them, which of my verdicts should I give up, and does your preferred principle then compel me to reconsider others?
