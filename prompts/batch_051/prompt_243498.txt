I have been trying to get clear on what separates triage from the broader category of health-care rationing, and I have settled on a working list of five features that I think are individually necessary and jointly sufficient for calling an allocation decision an instance of triage. The features are: the scarcity is acute and at least partly unexpected rather than a chronic shortfall; decisions are made case by case, under time pressure, and with incomplete information; the only permissible grounds are clinical urgency and expected medical benefit from the scarce resource; no patient group is categorically excluded before the decision point; and the allocation is revisable as the resource situation improves.

A case I encountered seems to test this list. A hospital system in a well-resourced country faces a predictable winter shortage of intensive-care beds every year. Rather than wait for a crisis, it adopts a standing protocol: during the winter surge, patients with a clinical frailty score above a specified threshold, or with advanced dementia, will not be admitted to the ICU even if they present with otherwise treatable respiratory failure, because their expected survival benefit is small and each bed-day spent on them is a bed-day denied to a patient with a reversible condition. The protocol is fixed in advance, applied by a triage officer who follows the rule rather than exercising bedside clinical judgment, and it remains in force year after year.

I cannot decide whether this is triage or something else, such as rationing, categorical exclusion, or simply a QALY-maximizing admission policy. Work through my five features one by one and tell me where the case passes and where it fails. More importantly, tell me whether the case shows that my features need revision: is it a mistake to require that triage be acute rather than chronic, or that it be case by case? And if the hospital is doing something wrong, is it wrong because it violates the features of triage, or because the features themselves let too much through?
