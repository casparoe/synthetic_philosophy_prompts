I'm in Year 12 and I'm the student rep on our school's opportunities and inclusion committee. Next week we have to decide what to do about the Scholars' Programme, which is an enrichment stream for about the top ten per cent of each year — extra seminars, a trip, and a named mentor. It's genuinely good for the kids on it, but its deliberately selective, and a lot of parents and staff say it's unfair that a school already full of advantages piles more advantages on the students who probably need them least. The proposal on the table is not to open it up, and not to redistribute the money to the struggling students. It is to scrap the programme outright: the seminar budget would go back into the academy trust's reserve, and the students who would have been on it just lose the seminars, the trip, and the mentor. The only thing anyone claims gets better is that the school no longer has this visibly elite track — in other words, the place becomes more equal. Some members of the committee think that's a good enough reason on its own: the gap is what's wrong, and we should be willing to level things down even if no one is made better off in any other way. Others say that's absurd, and a change that makes some students worse off and no student better off can't be an improvement.

Ive been asked to circulate a short written opinion before the meeting so we can actually vote: scrap the programme, keep it as it is, or reform it (and if reform, which part should we change first). I keep flip-flopping because I do think the selective programme is unfair, but I also can't get past the worry that removing it just to level the school down helps nobody. Could you draft the memo? It should give a bottom line we can put into a policy motion, and it should take the strongest argument on the losing side seriously rather than just swatting it away.
