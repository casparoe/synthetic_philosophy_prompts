In an argument about English past tense the other day, my friend kept insisting English has a rule: add -ed. I pointed at go/went, buy/bought, sing/sang, and she just said "the exception proves the rule — suppletion like went is what shows the rule is real, because everyone treats it as the irregular one." I've heard the same sentence used about the old claim that sound change is exceptionless. I need someone to actually lay out the different precise things "the exception proves the rule" could mean — prove as in test, as in confirm, exception as in a genuine counterexample versus an explicitly listed deviation — and tell me which readings are platitudes, which are substantive, which are just false, and which one(s) would actually give my friend (or the sound-change people) their conclusion. Does go/went, under any of those readings, come out as evidence for the -ed rule rather than evidence against it?
