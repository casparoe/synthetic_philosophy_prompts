Her framing is that logic is one of those unusual fields with a fixed subject matter and a body of results that actually accumulate: Aristotle got the first fragments, the Stoics and Leibniz anticipated bits of propositional reasoning, Boole and Frege made it rigorous, and the 20th century cleaned up with completeness and compactness. Here is the working definition of Whig history I keep using internally. A narrative is Whig to the extent that: (i) it runs backward from the present and treats earlier work mainly as steps toward today's endpoint; (ii) it evaluates past thinkers primarily by how much they anticipated current ideas, praising proto-X figures and quietly filing failed programs as detours; (iii) it makes the present endpoint feel like what the whole earlier development was really about; and (iv) it downplays what past actors took themselves to be doing. I want you to test the proposed course against each of the four criteria and then step back and ask whether the criteria themselves are fair.
