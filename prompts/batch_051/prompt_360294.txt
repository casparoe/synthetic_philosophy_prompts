For a seminar on the ethics of attachment, I am writing a paper on the phenomenon people gesture at when they say that someone has made an idol of a career, a relationship, a child, or a political cause. The question I am interested in is not merely why the person might have chosen the wrong object of devotion, but what it is to value something in the wrong way even when the object is, in itself, entirely legitimate. I have collected four analyses of this sort of misplaced worship, each stated in secular terms, and I want to know whether there is a fifth that does not collapse into them.

The four are as follows. An ordering analysis holds that the defect lies in the comparative ranking of loves: a lesser good has been placed above a greater one, as when recognition at work is loved more than friendship, without the object itself needing to be bad. A conditionalisation analysis holds that the person has treated a conditioned and finite good as if it were the unconditional horizon of value, an end that relativises every other claim, so the defect is a categorical mistake about the object's status. A load-bearing analysis locates the wrongness in a mismatch between object and role: the person has assigned to a finite particular a weight of meaning, security, or self-justification that its actual properties cannot carry. A screening analysis holds that the object is not really being attended to at all, because it is functioning as a proxy for something else — an idealised self, a lost person, a community — so the defect is a failure of attention to the actual object rather than an excess of attention to it.

I suspect these four cover most of the familiar secular translations of the idea, and I do not want to pad the paper with restatements of them. Could you supply a fifth analysis that is genuinely different in structure from each of the four — not a rewording, special case, or combination — set out what the wrongness consists in on that analysis, and offer an honest assessment of whether it contributes anything the first four lack? Please write the answer in British English.
