I am an undergraduate in a course on causation and explanation. My professor has a practice of giving us weekly vignettes and announcing her causal verdicts without yet stating her theory. I need to predict how she will rule on next week's vignette and to work out which account of causation best rationalizes her track record so far.

You are an expert philosopher of causation, equally at home with counterfactual and interventionist approaches to singular causation. Before you answer, brainstorm the plausible rules she might be running, then reason case by case.

Here are her verdicts so far. In every case she insisted that the outcome be described at an ordinary level of grain, exactly as worded below, and she has not allowed anyone to substitute a time-indexed or otherwise fine-grained outcome description.

1. A woman throws a brick through a window with no one else around. Verdict: the throw caused the window to break.
2. A toppling domino knocks over a second domino, which knocks over a third. Verdict: the first domino's toppling caused the third domino to fall.
3. A safety net is stretched in front of a window. A thrown rock hits the net, and the window never breaks. Verdict: the net's being there caused the window's remaining unbroken.
4. A housesitter promises to water a plant while its owner is away. She never waters it, and the plant dies. Verdict: the housesitter's failure to water the plant caused its death.
5. A crate is sliding toward a window. A guard is about to lower a barrier that would have stopped the crate, when a visitor stops him to ask for directions. The barrier is never lowered, and the crate smashes the window. Verdict: the visitor's interruption caused the window to break.

Next week's vignette is this. Mara and Noah each throw a stone at a security camera. Mara's stone arrives first and smashes the lens. Noah's stone is on target and would have smashed the lens a moment later had Mara's not done so; as things go, it passes through the space where the lens had been. The professor will ask, first, whether Mara's throw caused the lens to smash, and second, whether Noah's throw caused it. She has said she wants the ordinary coarse-grained question answered first — whether each throw caused the lens to smash — before anyone introduces fine-grained descriptions such as smashing at that moment or in that way.

Please work through the prompt as follows. Identify the candidate theories of causation that are consistent with the five earlier verdicts, stating explicitly what each theory must add, stretch, or qualify in order to handle the omission and absence cases. Identify which earlier vignette is the closest analog to the new one, and say whether that analog decides anything. Then say what each candidate theory predicts for Mara and for Noah in the new vignette, marking exactly where the theories come apart. Give your best prediction of the professor's verdicts, with reasons for preferring that reading of her track record. Finally, propose one further vignette that would most cleanly settle which of the still-live candidate theories she is actually running, and explain what each possible verdict on that further vignette would show.
