Ok so we were reading about Hegel in my philosophy class and from what I understand he had this weirdly positive view of war. Not like he liked bloodshed, but he thought war was good for a society because it shakes people out of their comfortable little private lives and reminds them they're part of something bigger, and that being willing to risk your life for the state keeps the state morally/spiritually alive. Which I guess made more sense in his time.

But Hegel died before nukes, drones, cyberwar, all that. So what I want to know is: if he could see modern war, would he still defend it? Specifically, would a nuclear standoff, where the whole point is that nobody actually fights because everyone loses, do the thing he thought war does for a society? And would he count drone warfare as real war at all, since one side's operators aren't in any danger and theres no shared risk or sacrifice? Or would he say those are just different enough that his old view doesn't apply? I'm not asking for what you'd say about war, I want you to reason from what Hegel actually thought and predict where he'd land.
