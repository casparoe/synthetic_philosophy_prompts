Herbert Simon, in A Behavioral Model of Rational Choice, writes: “Broadly stated, the task is to replace the global rationality of economic man with the kind of rational behavior that is compatible with the access to information and the computational capacities that are actually possessed by organisms, including man, in the kinds of environments in which such organisms exist.” I have been trying to operationalize that sentence in software terms, and it led me to a concept I want checked. I call it “compatibility debt”: a decision procedure and an environment are compatible when the procedure’s information and computation requirements fit the environment’s actual demands; as the environment drifts, the procedure can keep terminating with acceptable-looking outputs while its fit quietly degrades, and the debt is the gap between what the current environment would justify and what the stale procedure actually produces. On my reading, satisficing is a strategy for keeping compatibility debt low when search is expensive, but it only stays rational while aspiration levels update fast enough to track environmental change. Is this a legitimate extension of Simon’s point, or am I overloading one sentence into a different idea? What is the nearest existing concept, and where does the debt metaphor break?
