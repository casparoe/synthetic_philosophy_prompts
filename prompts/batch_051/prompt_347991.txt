I cant decide if Malthus is actually dead or just early. His argument was that population grows geometrically while the food supply grows arithmetically, so famine or disease or war will always come along and knock the numbers back down. When he wrote that, farming was mostly plows and manure. But since then synthetic fertilizer has let us grow way more food on the same land, and the countries that got rich started having fewer kids, not more. It seems like those were the two moving parts in his argument, and both ended up changing, one from technology and one from people just acting differently when life is better. Does Malthus's argument survive all that? Or does it need repair, like the same underlying logic just moves over to some other resource we're using up? Or is it actually dead as an argument about how things work, even if he happened to describe one specific period? Don't just tell me he was right in spirit—tell me which part actually breaks, if any. At the very end, give me a one-word verdict in \answer{...}, either survives, needs repair, or dead.
