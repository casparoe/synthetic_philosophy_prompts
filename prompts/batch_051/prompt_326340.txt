For my German class we’ve been reading extracts from Eckermann’s Conversations with Goethe, and there’s this bit where Goethe talks about political upheaval. I don’t have the exact wording, but he basically says he hates violent revolutions because they ruin as much as they gain, and he blames both the people who provoke them and the people who carry them out. Then he calls himself a moderate liberal rather than a democrat (I think it’s from around 1824, but I can’t remember exactly).

A bunch of people in my class say he would obviously hate Just Stop Oil blocking motorways or activists glueing themselves to paintings, because he hated disruption and wasn’t even a proper democrat. But others say that’s missing the point, becuase he only hated violent revolution, and these protests are nonviolent, plus he did think the mass of ordinary people had decent instincts and that reform should be gradual, not that nothing should ever be pressured.

What would Goethe actually have made of modern climate protest tactics like road-blocking or museum stunts, if he was around now? I don’t mean whether he’d be a green party member or whatever, I mean whether he’d see them as the kind of revolutionary violence he hated, or as unfair, or as the useful pressure that makes gradual reform happen.

Also, please separate what the text actually locks down from what scholars genuinely disagree about, and don’t present your own guess as the obvious reading if it’s contested.
