During my working life as a scientist I learned to distrust a distinction until it could be made to do explanatory work, and the philosophical contrast between indicative and counterfactual conditionals seems to me exactly such a distinction. I would like your credence in a precise claim about it.

Consider a murder case in which the butler and the gardener are the only plausible suspects, and the evidence points strongly, but not conclusively, to the butler. Take two sentences:

Indicative: If the butler didn't do it, the gardener did.

Counterfactual: If the butler hadn't done it, the gardener would have.

The indicative is naturally accepted, since somebody did the deed and the gardener is the only live alternative; the counterfactual is naturally resisted, since the nearest course of history in which the butler abstains may be one in which there was no murder at all. The question is what this contrast shows.

Please assign a single credence to the following proposition: the indicative/counterfactual contrast is a semantic contrast — the two forms have different truth conditions, so they express different kinds of proposition rather than one kind of proposition arrived at from different epistemic postures. For the proposition to be true, the difference must remain after fully accounting for what the speaker knows or assumes; it cannot be fully explained away as pragmatics.

Decompose your number into at least the following four terms, with a rough probability and one sentence of justification each:

1. The probability that indicative conditionals have truth conditions at all, as opposed to being devices for expressing conditional probability, or for recording acceptance of the consequent after hypothetically adding the antecedent to one's stock of beliefs, with no hidden proposition to be true or false.

2. Conditional on indicatives having truth conditions, the probability that they are not merely material conditionals, but require a contextually restricted set of worlds or situations over which their truth is evaluated.

3. Given a contextual-domain account of indicatives, the probability that counterfactuals require something over and above that machinery — an ordering of relative similarity among worlds, an intervention or causal structure, or another revision mechanism — rather than being the same operator with the domain restricted in a different way.

4. The residual probability that the apparent contrast between the two forms can be accounted for entirely pragmatically, leaving no semantic gap.

Then tell me how the terms combine to produce your single credence, which term you are least confident in, and which term, if its probability were halved, would move your final number the most. Finally, state what evidence or formal result would move that most sensitive term for you.
