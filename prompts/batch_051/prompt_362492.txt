I have been working through the "ergodicity economics" argument that time averages and ensemble averages can come apart for multiplicative processes, and it has left me unable to resolve a conflict between a principle I find compelling and an intuition I cannot shake. The principle is expected-value maximization in its simplest form: when a gamble has positive expected wealth, take it, and choose so as to maximize expected wealth. It is linear, requires no arbitrary curvature in a utility function, gives a definite verdict on every gamble, and is vindicated by the law of large numbers for additive gambles repeated often enough. The intuition is brought out by a concrete case. Consider a gamble, repeated indefinitely, in which I win 50% with probability one half and lose 40% with probability one half, and I stake my entire bankroll each round. Each round has positive expected value, a 5% gain, and the ensemble-average wealth across possible histories grows as (1.05)^n without bound. Yet the typical history is ruin: the per-round time-average growth factor is the square root of (1.5 × 0.6), about 0.949, so wealth along almost every path decays toward zero while a tiny set of outlier histories dominates the mean. My firm intuition is that a rule which, followed faithfully, destroys the agent who follows it with probability approaching one cannot be the right rule for an agent who lives exactly one history. Both pull me, and I do not know which should yield.

I am not asking you to tell me which side wins. I want a negotiated verdict: what would it take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what would it take for the principle to be the thing that goes, and whether there is a stable reading on which both survive. Would it be justified to debunk the intuition as an artifact, say a scope confusion, an irrational demand for certainty, or a failure to notice that the ruinous strategy is only one strategy among many and not an indictment of the criterion itself? Or is the principle simply too narrow for multiplicative dynamics, so that it should be amended to maximize the time-average growth rate, which amounts to maximizing expected log wealth, or restricted to one-shot and additive settings? And is there a compatibility reading on which expected-value maximization is the right norm for a choice evaluated across an ensemble or under full diversification, while growth-rate maximization is the right norm for a single undiversified path, with the apparent conflict being an artifact of sliding between the two frames? Say what would justify each move and what each move costs, rather than only picking one.

If I have set this up as a clash when it is not one, for example if the clean principle I stated was never the full theory and the real question is just which utility function to have, or if "right rule" is doing two different jobs in my two paragraphs, tell me plainly and reframe rather than politely walking through a false dilemma.
