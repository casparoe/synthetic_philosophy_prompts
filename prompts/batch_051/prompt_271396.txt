I'm a backend engineer on a customer data platform, and I've never taken a philosophy class, so bear with me if I mangle this. We're reworking our ingestion layer so that when a record arrives we validate it against a schema that separates "hard" constraints (violation means the record isn't really an X at all) from "soft" constraints (violation means it's a weird X, or just an X with bad data). We've been fighting for weeks about where the boundary goes. Every time someone proposes relaxing a hard rule — e.g., letting a customer have no contact method — someone else says "but then it's not a customer, that's what customer means." A data scientist on the team pointed me to Quine's essay "Two Dogmas of Empiricism," and this bit has been stuck in my head:

> Thus from the point of view of the doctrine of meaning it makes no sense to say of the actual individual, who is at once a man and a biped, that his rationality is essential and his two-leggedness accidental or vice versa. Things had essences, for Aristotle, but only linguistic forms have meanings. Meaning is what essence becomes when it is divorced from the object of reference and wedded to the word.

I read him as saying that whether a feature is "essential" isn't a fact about the object itself; its an artifact of which word we happened to pick, and the same object can be essential-rational under "man" and accidental-under "biped." If that's right, a lot of our hard constraints are just temporary business rules that snuck into the definition. But I don't actually know how to translate that into a schema review.

Here are five hard constraints currently in our draft schema. Please actually rank them from 1 (most defensible as a genuine semantic/definitional constraint) to 5 (least defensible, basically a business rule in definition's clothing), and give a one- or two-sentence reason for each placement. Don't just summarize Quine; I want the ranking and the applied reasoning.

- A session must have a start timestamp; if the field is missing, reject the record as "not a session."
- A customer must have made at least one purchase; a record with zero purchases should be labeled a "prospect," not a "customer."
- A customer must have at least one contact method, either an email or a phone number.
- A payment must have an amount that is zero or positive.
- A customer must be at least 18 years old because our terms of service require it.
