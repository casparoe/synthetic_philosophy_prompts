I am a doctoral student in philosophy, working on mid-century existentialism and the objections pressed against it, and for the past year I have been mediating between two members of my reading group whose positions have hardened into two fixed camps. One defends a broadly Sartrean position: for human beings, existence precedes essence in the sense that no nature, function, or defining concept is fixed in advance of a person's existing and choosing; a human being is nothing other than what they make of themselves, and is for that reason radically free and responsible for what they become. The other, the critic, does not claim that this position is false; they claim that it is incoherent. On their reading, the declaration that there is no human essence is itself an essential claim about the human being, and the assertion of a universal and inescapable freedom reinstates, under another name, the fixed human nature the position claims to deny.

The two of them have agreed to an adversarial collaboration, and I want you to draft the document they should be able to sign. The deliverable is the document itself, not a narrative of how they produced it.

Structure it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. The agreed claims should be non-vacuous. Include at least the following: what both sides accept about the slogan's intended contrast, including the contrast with made artefacts whose design and function precede their instances; what the Sartrean position is not asserting; and which observations or arguments would not settle the dispute. Avoid formulations that are agreed only because they are empty.

The disagreements table must have the following columns:

- Crux: a short label for the sub-question.
- Neutral statement: the point of disagreement phrased so that neither side can object that it is loaded.
- Existentialist wording: the claim expressed in terms the existentialist will sign as an accurate statement of their own view.
- Critic wording: the same ground expressed in terms the critic will sign as an accurate statement of why they regard the position as incoherent.
- Classification: substantive or verbal, with a one-sentence justification.
- Shared test or case: an argument, example, or body of evidence both sides accept as bearing on the crux; this may concern the semantics of essence terms, the role of antecedent constraints on choice, phenomena of self-deception, or what each side would have to give up.
- Existentialist prediction: what the existentialist predicts the test or case would show, recorded in advance.
- Critic prediction: what the critic predicts the test or case would show, recorded in advance.

After the table, add a brief closing section stating which disagreements the collaboration treats as verbal, which remain substantive, and whether there is any crux on which both sides agree some conceivable evidence or argument would move them. Write the entire answer in British English, and do not include citations or bibliographic references.
