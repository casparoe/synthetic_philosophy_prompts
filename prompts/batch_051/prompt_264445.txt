Give me a working definition of "cause," as used in statements like "X caused Y," that correctly sorts the six cases below, most of which are omissions. By "cause" I mean the ordinary word, not a technical term from law or statistics. I am confident that the motorist in (a) and the parent in (d) caused the deaths, and that the woman in (b) did not. I lean toward calling the lifeguard in (c) a cause but could not say exactly what separates the lifeguard from the neighbor in (e). I have no settled view about (e) and (f).

(a) A motorist runs a red light and strikes a pedestrian, who dies from the impact.
(b) A woman in one country knows about a famine-relief charity but donates nothing; a particular child dies of malnutrition.
(c) An on-duty lifeguard, reading a magazine instead of watching the water, fails to notice a swimmer in distress, and the swimmer drowns.
(d) A parent does not feed her infant for two days, and the infant dies of dehydration.
(e) A neighbor watches from his window as an unsupervised toddler wanders into a busy street; he does nothing, and a car hits the toddler.
(f) A voter stays home on election day; in a two-candidate plurality election, the candidate she would have voted for loses by exactly one vote.

Propose the definition, run it through all six cases in order, say where it yields no clear verdict or gives an answer that surprises you, and then either revise it or defend the surprising verdict. Do not rest with a bare counterfactual test unless you can also explain why ordinary speakers resist saying the voter's non-vote caused the defeat; if your definition appeals to duties, roles, or background expectations, say what generates those norms.

Format: exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Item 1 should be your definition; items 2 through 5 should apply it to the cases in order, combining cases if necessary. Use your final sentence to say which of your verdicts you are least confident in and why. Write in American English.
