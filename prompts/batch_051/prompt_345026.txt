Before I draft it, give me the full list of requirements a demarcation criterion has to satisfy. I want the cases any criterion has to get right — include at least a few contested ones where informed people genuinely split (psychoanalysis, string theory, cold fusion, acupuncture, climate economics, maybe evolutionary psychology) — and for each case, two sentences on what the criterion has to deliver and why it's a requirement rather than my private intuition. Then flag which requirements are non-negotiable and which are really just tastes of particular camps (Popperian, Lakatosian, Bayesian, etc.). The part I most want is the tension map: which pairs of requirements can't be jointly satisfied — e.g. a criterion strict enough to keep out astrology and loose enough to tolerate the ad hoc patching actual research programs go through — and which single requirement knocks out the biggest share of the popular criteria (falsifiability, verifiability, predictive track record, puzzle-solving). Don't waste space scoring every contender against the whole list; spend it on the requirements themselves and the conflicts.
