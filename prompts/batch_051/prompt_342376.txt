Can you make me one of those 'which position do you actually hold?' quizzes, but for the ethics of historical fiction and biopics? I run a little film-and-philosophy blog and I want something that sorts readers into views about what we owe the real dead when we dramatise them. The quiz should be eight to ten questions, each a concrete case (a screenwriter invents a love affair for a real historical person; a series uses a recently dead actress's likeness for a degrading scene; a biopic builds a sympathetic backstory for a mass murderer from family photos; a novelist turns someone's actual teenage diaries into plot; that kind of thing) with two or three answer options. It should not ask readers to pick a label or agree with a slogan — the idea is to sort people by what they actually say about the cases.

I'd like the scoring key to map answer patterns onto at least these leaves: a fidelity view (what matters is sticking to what's documented), a respect or moral-rights-of-the-dead view (the dead still have claims about dignity and privacy), an artistic licence view (the dead are gone, so beyond not defaming the living the artist owes them next to nothing), and a stewardship or descendants view (the real obligation is to living communities, survivors and collective memory). If you think another leaf is genuinely separate and defensible, add it with a one-line note on why it needs its own leaf.

Build the quiz from the distinctions first rather than from the names: work out which questions actually seperate the positions, then locate the named views in that space. If some answer combinations are possible but nobody holds them, say briefly whether that's because the combination is incoherent, just unattractive, or unexplored. And if two leaves can't be told apart by any question, flag that instead of pretending the quiz splits them.

I care most about two distinctions. One is truthfulness versus respect: someone who thinks we owe the dead accuracy and someone who thinks we owe them protection can disagree on nearly every interesting case, so don't let your questions merge them. The other is whether the duty attaches to the dead themselves or only to the living people around them — the stewardship view should come apart from the moral-rights view, not just be a fuzzy version of it. Make sure each question actually forces a choice, and avoid options where every position would pick the same answer.

Oh, and write the quiz itself in American English.
