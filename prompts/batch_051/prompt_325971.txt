Here's a Nozick grandma case with a knob on it, and I want the thing actually worked through rather than gestured at. Quick setup for anyone hazy: I visit my grandmother, she's sitting up, chatting, looks perfectly healthy, so I form the belief that she's well. The standard case adds that if she were gravely ill the family would hide it and I'd still form the same belief, so my belief doesn't track the truth; Nozick-syle sensitivity says I don't know she's well, which a lot of people find like a reductio. I'm interested in whether that reductio survives once the cover-up gets chancy.

Modified version. There are three states: W (well), M (mildly ill but can fake it), and S (gravely ill, family has to run a cover story). I start with priors P(W)=0.85, P(M)=0.1, P(S)=0.05, and these priors are common knowledge. The family runs the same cover story in M and S: they tell me she's fine, and she looks the same as in W. The cover holds with probability c_M in M and c_S in S, with c_M greater than c_S. If the cover fails on a visit, I get a signal plainly incompatible with W, so I know the state is not W. Failures are independent across visits. I can make one visit or two. I believe she's well only if my posterior P(W | what I observed) is at least k.

What I want: for the settings below, say what happens to Nozick-style sensitivity and to safety, and whether closure under known entailment does anything weird. Then answer the question: as the knobs turn, does the tracking account get closer to the ordinary intuition that I know she's well, or further away? Does the original counterexample to sensitivity survive when cover-ups are chancy, or does it stop being a counterexample?

Run at least these settings:

- c_M=0.7, c_S=0.4, one visit, k=0.95
