I sit on the residents' management committee of a small housing co-operative in a university town, and I have been asked to draft the committee's written decision in a dispute that has become far more heated than the facts seem to warrant. The building has one shared laundry room with two machines. A long-standing, unspoken practice governs access: residents reserve their place by leaving their laundry basket in a line in the corridor outside, and no one stays with the machines because the cycle times are predictable. A new resident, an overseas postdoctoral researcher who moved in two months ago, has repeatedly ignored the baskets and used the first machine to become free. A long-standing resident has filed a formal complaint asking the committee to reprimand him and to adopt the basket-queue as a written rule. The new resident's response is that an unattended basket is not a queue; that he was never told about the practice and never agreed to it; and that in the buildings where he has lived previously, waiting meant being physically present. Committee members are split: some see a clear case of queue-jumping by someone who refuses to respect local custom, others think we cannot discipline someone for breaching a convention he neither knew nor accepted, and a third group worries that if we rule on this we will be pressured to enforce every unwritten norm in the building.

Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate before next week's meeting. It should recommend one concrete course of action—reprimand, adopt a written queueing rule, drop the complaint, mediate, or some combination—with the reasons for it and with an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. In setting out the recommendation you should also articulate the general principle the committee should apply when a newcomer or visitor declines to follow a local everyday convention they did not choose and may not have known about, since whatever we decide here will be treated as precedent for other disputes about noise, shared kitchen spaces, and the building's informal routines.
