Give me a working definition of "enhancement"—the enhancement side of the therapy/enhancement distinction—that handles the following cases. (1) Surgical repair of a congenital heart defect in an infant. (2) Measles vaccination for a healthy child. (3) A stimulant prescribed to a person diagnosed with ADHD. (4) The same stimulant taken, without any diagnosis, by a student wanting to concentrate for longer than usual. (5) LASIK surgery that corrects vision to 20/20. (6) A contact lens worn by someone with normal vision to achieve 20/10 acuity. (7) Growth hormone given to a child who is unusually short but otherwise healthy and has no detectable endocrine deficiency. (8) A beta-blocker taken by a concert pianist before an audition to prevent tremor. (9) A drug given shortly after a traumatic event that prevents the memory from becoming disabling in a person otherwise likely to develop PTSD. (10) A side-effect-free pill that permanently reduces the sleep a person needs from eight hours to five.

I regard (1) and (3) as clearly therapy and (10) as clearly enhancement, and I have no settled view about most of the others. I suspect the word is doing two jobs: a descriptive one about moving a person beyond normal or statistically typical functioning, and an evaluative one about unfairness, cheating, inauthenticity, or the proper limits of medicine. If you agree, give me both definitions, run all ten cases through each, and say which cases change their classification. If a case forces you to revise a definition, say so and re-run the affected cases. Finally, tell me which of your verdicts is least secure and what would have to be true for you to change it.
