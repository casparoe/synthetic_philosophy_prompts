I’ve been trying to work out what idolatry actually amounts to when I look at particular cases, and I want to know what principle, if any, my gut is tracking. Here are my verdicts.

1. My aunt kept my uncle’s ashes on the mantel for fifteen years and talks to the box every morning; she wouldn’t scatter them because that would be “losing him again.” That doesn’t strike me as idolatry. It seems like a healthy way to keep loving someone.

2. A colleague’s wife was the person who “held the holidays together”—cooked, hosted, remembered everyone. After their daughter died she stopped doing any of that, and he looked after her dutifully, but he told me he missed “his actual wife.” That feels like a wrong way to love someone. He loves a role, not her.

3. My neighbor treats the flag as literally sacred and would fight someone over burning one. The same man votes against veterans benefits and says homeless vets should be grateful to live in “the freest country on earth.” That is idolatry to me: he worships the sign while ignoring what it points to.

4. A chef I know refuses to update a traditional dish, and she’ll say a shortcut version that wins blind tastings is delicious but “not coq au vin.” I dont think that’s fetishism. It’s fidelity to a practice.

5. An environmentalist I know gets moved by the idea of untouched Wilderness, and he fights wind turbines in a degraded gravel lot, but he can’t name the birds in his own garden and has never bothered with the polluted creek down the road. That feels like valuing the image of nature more than nature.

Is there one principle that delivers all five of those verdicts, and if not, which ones do I have to revise? And what does it say about the case I keep flip-flopping on: someone who treats their wedding ring as irreplaceable and says the marriage “doesn’t feel real” without it?

Please write the answer in British English.
