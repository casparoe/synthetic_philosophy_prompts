I run a small bakery with my sister. Two years ago we started making tiny cost-cutting changes one at a time: slightly cheaper chocolate, a bit less butter in the croissants, marginally worse flour, that sort of thing. Each change on its own felt like an obvious win — the drop in quality was barely noticeable and the savings were real, so the new state genuinely looked like an improvement on the previous one. But now we've stacked enough of them that the pastries are clearly worse then when we started, regulars have drifted away, and my sister wants to undo almost all of it. Was each individual step a mistake? If every step was an improvement on the one before, how can the end be worse than the beginning? I can't point to the exact change where we should of stopped, but not stopping was clearly wrong. I'm not after a confident-sounding theory: if my framing is confused, or if the honest answer is that my step-by-step way of comparing options is broken somehow, I'd rather you say that plainly.
