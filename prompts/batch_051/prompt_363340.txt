I chair the Rules and Charter Committee of my high school's Student Senate, and I need to bring a decision memorandum to the committee at our next meeting. Our Student Body Constitution has a genuine gap, and the Senate will decide, on our committee's recommendation, who may act as the student government's chief executive until the gap is repaired. In practical terms, we have to decide who governs when the written rules run out.

The constitution creates a President and a Vice President, both elected each spring, with terms beginning at the start of the fall semester. Its only succession language says that the Vice President performs the President's duties whenever the President is absent or otherwise unable to serve. It says nothing about an invalidated election, nothing about a vacancy in the office of President, and nothing about who acts when there is no President at all. This spring, the presidential election was voided by the Principal, who certifies all elections, after one campaign was found to have distributed altered virtual ballots through a school directory; the Vice President's election was not challenged. The outgoing President's term ends by the constitution's fixed date at the end of the school year, and no new presidential election can be held until after the summer. When the fall term begins, there will be no President and no President-elect.

The committee is divided among four positions. The outgoing President argues she should remain as Acting President on a caretaker basis, even though the text fixes the end of her term, because continuity requires that some authorized person stay in the office to keep basic operations running. The Vice President argues that her valid election is the only surviving democratic mandate, and that a vacancy is simply the limiting case of the President being unable to serve, so the succession language covers it. The Speaker of the Senate argues that the Senate, as the only remaining elected body with a full slate, should exercise presidential powers collectively through the Speaker until an election is held, because that is the least overreaching solution. The Principal argues that when student self-government fails, the administration is the ordinary backstop and may run student government minimally until the Senate elects a President. Whatever we recommend, the committee has already agreed that any interim holder will exercise caretaker powers only: no binding commitments that outlast the interim period, no spending from reserves, no changes to election rules, and a duty to yield power as soon as a President is certified.

Draft the memorandum the committee will vote on. It should recommend one of these positions, or a clear hybrid, with the reasoning; it should state the principle we should use for future constitutional gaps rather than improvising each time; it should engage the strongest argument against the recommendation in its strongest form; and it should end with a motion-ready sentence the committee can adopt. Please write the memorandum in American English.
