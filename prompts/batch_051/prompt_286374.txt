Reading Bernard Williams's Makropulos stuff sent me down a rabbit hole about whether an eternal life—specifically the heaven kind, not just biological immortality—would eventually turn into a kind of spiritual cabin fever. And there's a move I keep stubbing my toe on, and I want to know whether it has a name. Someone objects in a concrete way: after you've heard every piece of music a few thousand times, or had every conversation you could possibly have with every other person, wouldn't endless worship, or even an endless series of projects, start to feel less like bliss and more like a job you're not allowed to quit? The defender then says I'm picturing it from a mortal point of view: in heaven your desires would be continually renewed, or you'd relate to time differently, or each repetition would hit with the freshness of a first time, so satiation never accumulates. Fair enough. But when I ask why the same trick can't be used to make an eternal torment acceptable—just say the inhabitants get desires that fit their situacion—the defender says no, because that state is bad and heaven is good. And at that point it seems like the goodness of the state is being read off the very desires the state is stipulated to give you, which lets any state be made to come out good.

It isn't quite an ad hoc rescue, because the transformed-psychology move is there from the start, not invented when the objection bites. It isn't quite a no true Scotsman, because the definition of heaven stays fixed; it's the person inside it whose psychology keeps getting adjusted. I'm after the exact concept, if there is one, in the immortality or eternal bliss literature. If there's no name, I'd be happy with a coinage, but I mean a labeled definition with boundary cases, not just a word. I'd want it marked off from plain unfalsifiability, from ad hoc hypothesis-saving, from no true Scotsman, and from the vaguer appeal that you can't judge the next life from this one. I keep feeling the defender is doing something a little more respectable than those, and I can't say exactly what. And if the whole description is off—if I've caricatured the position or if no actual debate has this shape—tell me that plainly instead of trying to salvage it.
