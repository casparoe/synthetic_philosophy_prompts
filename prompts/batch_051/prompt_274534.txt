I'm in an ethics seminar and we've been on Taurek all week, and our prof assigned us to write the "joint statement" for an adversarial collaboration between a Taurek-style anti-aggregationist and a straight utilitarian about whether the numbers count in rescue cases. Not a dialog or a debate transcript—I mean the actual document the two camps would sign after hashing out what they actually agree on before they start disagreeing. The setup is the drug case: you have one dose and you can save one stranger or five strangers, nobody has a special claim on you, and the five aren't tied to the one in any way. The Taurek side says it isn't obviously required to save the five, because the one's loss is not smaller than any given one of the five's losses, and you can't just add up losses seperately across people like they were a single big loss. The utilitarian side says that's crazy and five deaths are plainly worse than one. 

Write the statement like this: first a numbered list of claims both sides can sign without wincing, and I don't want empty ones like "both parties value life"—I want substantive common ground about the case, about what's at stake, about what makes this different from ordinary risk. Then a disagreement table where each row states one point of difference in wording each side would accept for their own side, not a caricature they'd reject. Then a short list of crux sub-questions—the kind where if one side turned out to be right, the other side would have to move—and for each crux, what each side predicts the evidence or argument would show. Somewhere in there deal with the obvious pressure points: separateness of persons, whether it's fine to aggregate harms but not deaths, whether the utilitarian is trading one person's total loss against five people's partial gains, and whether this whole fight is about fundamental values or just about decision procedure. I know Taurek's view gets accused of being anti-aggregationist in a way that sounds like it just refuses to count, so make sure an actual Taurekian would sign the document as fair. And no citations or reading lists—just the document.
