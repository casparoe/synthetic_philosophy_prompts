I run a workshop for early-career tech reporters — about twenty people, one 75-minute block — and I want them to feel in their gut the problem of extraterritorial jurisdiction over online speech: whose law governs when a post is protected in Berlin and criminal in Ankara, or the other way around? I want a role-play, not a lecture, and I want the point to show up in what happens rather than in a speech from me afterwards. Split them into roles: a platform trust-and-safety team, two governments with incompatible speech laws, maybe a couple of user groups threatening to leave. Have each group reason under real conflicting legal demands about one specific piece of content. Give me the scenario — the post, the two legal regimes, the platform's stated rules — plus how you'd run the 75 minutes, what each role is told, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the actual structure: no single sovereign, platforms effectively writing global speech law, the right to be forgotten colliding with American free-speach norms, and the fact that taking something down in one country can hide it everywhere. Add a simple mechanic so their decisions accumulate into public backlash or regulator pressure, and tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, plus wich feature of the real situation no table-top exercise can reproduce no matter how well it goes. Keep it runnable with paper and a whiteboard.
