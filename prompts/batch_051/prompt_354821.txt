Draft the joint statement that a committed defender of the principle of alternative possibilities and a committed defender of Frankfurt-style compatibilism could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. By a Frankfurt case, the parties mean the familiar structure in which Jones has decided to perform some act, Black can detect a prior sign that Jones is about to waver and can intervene to force the act, but Jones performs it on his own and Black never intervenes. The statement should open with a numbered list of claims both sides accept about such cases and about what they would establish if the standard no-alternative reading succeeds. It should then present a disagreements table giving each side's precise residual position in wording its own side would endorse, separating genuine disagreements from merely terminological ones. Finally, it should isolate the cruxes that would actually move at least one side, including at least the following: whether Black's reliable prior sign already presupposes determinism in the actual sequence, whether the flickers of freedom that remain in many Frankfurt cases are responsibility-relevant alternatives, and whether Jones's responsibility is better explained by an actual-sequence account of responsibility or by the availability of alternatives. For each crux, specify a concrete variant of the Frankfurt case or a targeted argument that both sides agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction about what it will show.
