Suppose a learned society's constitution has a single amendment clause, Article XI. Article XI provides that every other article may be amended by a majority of the votes cast at a general meeting. Article XI itself may be amended only by a two-thirds majority of the entire membership, after the proposed text has been circulated for one full year. Article XI also declares that any proposal to amend the procedure for amending articles, any proposal to alter the electorate or voting threshold for a constitutional amendment, and any proposal to change the classification of a provision as part of Article XI is to be decided under the two-thirds procedure rather than the ordinary majority procedure.

Treat this as a mechanism rather than as an exercise in drafting. Walk through what happens under the following maneuvers: a faction first uses the ordinary majority procedure to add a new article that directly sets the threshold for amending Article XI at a majority; a faction bundles a long-demanded reform with a small change to Article XI so that a vote for the bundle is a vote for both; a faction uses the ordinary procedure to redefine the entire membership or the circulation period in a way that makes the two-thirds hurdle easier to meet; and a faction moves to repeal Article XI outright and leave no amendment clause at all. For each, say whether the letter of the provision permits it, how the assembly will actually behave, and whether the self-reference in the clause produces a stable constitution, a deadlock, or a route for capture. Can any meta-rule about priority of old and new provisions fix the failures you identify, or does the problem simply re-appear in the meta-rule?
