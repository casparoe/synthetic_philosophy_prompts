was at a dinner party this weekend where someone was holding forth about why luxury goods arent actually irrational. his argument was that a loudly expensive watch is just the handicap principle imported from animal signaling: a peacocks tail is a burden, and its honesty comes precisely from being a burden, so the fact that a watch costs a fortune and is honestly kind of impractical is exactly why it works as an honest signal of wealth. the waste is the whole point, he kept saying, and from there he concluded that conspicuous consumption is a rational honest signaling strategy and that trying to tax it or roll your eyes at it is just missing the function of the behavior. i want to figure out how far this analogy actually carries. lay out what corresponds to what between peacock tails and luxury watches, where the mapping breaks, and which disanalogies matter for his conclusion. i have a vague sense that a tail is honest because its cost is condition-dependent in a way a price tag isnt, since you can go into debt for the watch or buy a counterfeit, while a sickly peacock apparently cant fake a magnificent tail, but id like the full audit. also: is the handicap part doing real work in the human case, or is conspicuous consumption better understood as a different kind of signal altogether, and does the fact that the audience and what is being advertised differ change the verdict?
