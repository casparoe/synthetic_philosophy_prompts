I've been rereading some of the interactive proof stuff from complexity theory — the result that a randomized, polynomial-time verifier, by interrogating an all-powerful but completely untrustworthy prover, can verify exactly the languages in PSPACE, which is wildly more than the verifier could decide on its own. And it struck me that this is structurally what Socrates claims about the elenchus in the Meno: a bounded inquirer (Socrates, the slave boy) plus question-and-answer with a respondent who is no expert can produce conclusions that outrun what either party could have demonstrated alone — the boy is brought to double the square, but only through interrogation.

So here's the mapping I keep wanting to make. The verifier is the Socratic questioner; the prover is the interlocutor; the completeness condition (any true instance has some prover strategy that convinces the verifier) corresponds to the Meno claim that there's a line of questioning that can draw out knowledge the answerer didn't know they had; and the soundness condition (no prover, even a lying one, can convince the verifier of a false instance) corresponds to the elenchus as a test — a false answer should be exposed by further questioning. On that reading, IP = PSPACE would predict that Socratic dialogue is not just a way to check answers but a dramatically more powerful route for a bounded reasoner to reach truths than solo deduction — and maybe even that a deceptive interlocutor cannot systematically mislead a competant questioner.

I genuinely can't tell whether this is a real structural isomorphism or just a seductive metaphor. Is there an actual mapping here, and if so, where does it come apart? I'd especially like to know what the parallel predicts that turns out false — for instance, is the power of interactive proofs bought entirely by the verifier's randomness and the algebra of arithmetization, neither of which has any Socratic analogue? And when you give your verdict, please also say what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would flip your assessment of the parallel, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
