At a meetup last weekend two people got into a long argument about the careenium/simmballs thought experiment—the one where a little world of balls bumps along by local rules, but at a higher level you can see patterns that look like symbols, and the question is whether those symbols can ever push the balls around or are just a compressed description. I didn't take notes, but the exchange went roughly like this.

A: "In the original careenium, symbols don't push. They're patterns realized by the balls. Saying a symbol pushes a ball is like saying a center of mass pushes a molecule. The high-level description is real as information, but it has no downward causal power."

B: "But if you add a detector that recognizes the pattern and then moves balls, the symbol is part of a control loop. The detector is made of balls, but what it responds to is the pattern. So the symbol can push substrate via the detector. That isn't spooky."

A: "The detector just responds to local ball configurations. If you rewound the micro story, the balls would do the same thing. You've added a coupling, not a new force. The symbol is still just a matching condition."

B: "If the detector accepts a hundred different tokens that all mean 'nudge left,' the balls that move depend on the token's meaning, not its micro details. In the interventionist sense, the symbol is the causal variable. Nobody says temperature doesn't cause the furnace to turn on just because a strip of metal does the actual switching."

A: "That's a different careenium. You've built in a semantic transducer designed by an outsider. The interesting version is whether patterns can have effects without a designer coding their meaning into a detector. You answered by adding the designer."

B: "Maybe the original careenium already has those feedback loops, just messier. Semantics is always partly in the system's structure. If the structure includes feedback from higher-level patterns, there's no bright line between pattern and cause."

A: "Then you've made the question engineering: does the transducer exist? If it does, the transducer pushes; the symobl is our label for its trigger condition. That seems to concede the point."

B: "I don't concede. The transducer is part of the symbol's implementation. When a thermostat turns on a furnace, the temperature causes it in the ordinary sense, and the metal strip is just the implementation. Same with the symbol."

A: "Thermostats are semantic devices designed by us. The careenium was meant to ask whether patterns can have effects without a designer building in their meaning. You keep answering with a designer."

B: "Maybe the original setup already contains bounded detectors. If we define 'symbol' as a coarse-grained pattern that effects downstream states, then the answer is empirical: does the system contain feedback loops? If yes, symbols push. If no, they don't. The debate is no longer metaphysical."

I honestly couldn't tell who got the better of it, and they asked me to referee.
