Suppose David Hume were shown the contemporary debate between so-called Humeans and anti-Humeans about laws of nature, and in particular four positions he never lived to see. The first is the bare regularity theory: laws are simply true, exceptionless universal generalizations over matters of fact, with no further metaphysical machinery. The second is David Lewis's Best System Account: laws are the generalizations that appear as axioms or theorems in whichever true deductive systematization of all particular fact best balances simplicity and strength over the entire mosaic of local matters of fact. The third is David Armstrong's anti-Humean view: a law is a second-order relation of nomic necessitation between universals, something in the world that binds properties together and in that sense governs their instances. The fourth is the primitivist view often associated with Tim Maudlin: laws are fundamental and unanalyzable, not reducible to regularities, systems, or anything else. Assume Hume is also shown the doctrine of Humean supervenience named after him: everything about the world supervenes with nomic necessity on the spatiotemporal distribution of perfectly natural point-local properties, and there are no necessary connections between distinct existences.

What would Hume's considered reaction be? Specifically: would he regard Lewis's Best System Account as a faithful development of his own position, or would he see the appeal to simplicity, strength, and naturalness as smuggling in a mind-dependent or a priori standard that his empiricism cannot license? Which of the four positions comes closest to preserving his claim that necessary connexion is never observed in objects but is instead projected onto the world by the mind, and does any of them preserve it fully? How would he answer the anti-Humean objection that a regularity or a system of regularities cannot account for the governance of nature or the explanatory force of laws, and that something more robust is required? And would he accept the label Humeanism as applied to the Best System Account and to Humean supervenience, or would he identify respects in which those doctrines assert more than he ever did and ought not to bear his name? Weigh the candidate readings and give a reasoned verdict on where Hume belongs in the contemporary map: what should Humean mean if it is to be a fair description of Hume's own view?
