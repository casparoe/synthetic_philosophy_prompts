I am a high school student, and I volunteer at a small farm animal sanctuary. The other volunteers and I are split over what should be done with the animals' byproducts. Some of us eat the unfertilized eggs the rescued hens lay, spread the cows' manure on the vegetable garden, and wear secondhand wool and leather, because none of this increases the demand for animal farming and all of it would otherwise go to waste. Others say this still treats animals as resources and keeps alive the habit of exploitation that the sanctuary is supposed to oppose; a free product, they argue, is not automatically a non-exploitative one. I would like a working definition of exploitation that gets the following cases right: (1) buying a factory-farmed chicken breast; (2) watching wild birds through binoculars; (3) eating a hamburger a friend is about to throw away; (4) eating a deer killed by a car; (5) eating the unfertilized eggs of a rescued hen; (6) spreading rescued cows' manure on a vegetable garden; (7) knitting with wool sheared from a rescued sheep for its health; and (8) buying a leather jacket at a thrift store. Please run the definition through all eight cases, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. I suspect the word does at least two jobs, one about causing harm or demand and one about treating a being as a thing, but I want to see whether a single definition can handle the hard cases.
