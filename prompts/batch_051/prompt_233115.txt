In the foundations of statistical mechanics there is a long-running dispute about the source of the thermodynamic arrow of time. The participants agree on the reversible microscopic dynamics and on the fact that entropy increases in one temporal direction and not the other, but they divide over what explains that fact. One group holds that the arrow is completely explained by a contingency: the universe began in an extremely low-entropy state, and all later asymmetries, including those of records and interventions, derive from that initial condition plus dynamics. A second group holds that the thermodynamic arrow is not a feature of the world's fundamental constitution at all, but an artifact of coarse-graining: the apparent rise of entropy tracks the macrostates we choose to treat as relevant, and a different choice of variables would give no monotonically increasing quantity. A third group holds that any such reduction to initial conditions, typicality, or coarse-graining is circular, because the concepts used to state those reductions — preparation, record, intervention, gradient, even the direction of the entropy change itself — already presuppose a temporal orientation, so the thermodynamic arrow is objective and not merely epistemic. The three camps appeal to the same formalism, the same fluctuation relations, and the same cosmological observations, and the dispute remains unresolved.

My working theory is that this is not a disagreement about any hidden empirical outcome, nor about the mathematics, but about what counts as an explanation of a temporal asymmetry: the first side wants the asymmetry to follow from a dynamical plus historical condition, the second wants it shown to be perspective-relative, and the third wants any explanatory apparatus to pass a circularity test that the other sides regard as too demanding. Is that right? Or is there a real empirical or formal question underneath, for example about whether the past hypothesis is itself independently confirmable, or about whether typicality reasoning can be given a non-question-begging foundation? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of positions, and an honest answer about whether anything — a new fluctuation theorem, a cosmological observation, a formal result about coarse-graining — could actually end the disagreement.
