I'm a reporter on the ideas and public-health beat, and I've been tracking state senator Dana Ortiz's votes on sex-work bills for three sessions now. She's the swing vote on the health comittee, and there's a new bill coming up next month that I genuinely can't call.

Her record, as best I can put it together:

(1) SB 114, which struck the state law against selling sex. She voted yes, and her floor statement was basically: the seller is the one calling us for help while we're busy booking her.

(2) SB 209, the safe-report bill: if you call 911 to report a violent crime against a sex worker, you can't then be charged with prostitution or purchasing sex in connection with that call. She voted yes.

(3) AB 387, a brothel-licensing bill. It would have classified the workers as independent contractors, so owners wouldn't owe workers' comp or have to cover injuries, and it exempted the brothels from county health inspections. She voted no, and said she wasnt opposed to "some hypothetical safe brothel" — just to this one.

(4) HB 77, which would have published the names and mugshots of convicted sex buyers online. She voted no; she called it a privacy bomb that would land on spouses and kids and didn't make anyone safer.

Now comes SB 501. It would license brothels only if they're incorporated as worker-owned cooperatives: everyone who works there has to be a voting member, workers are employees with workers' comp, the county runs the health and safety inspections, no outside owner can take a share, and it's capped at six workers per house. Selling sex stays decriminalized either way.

I can tell at least two stories about what principle explains her first four votes, and I think they point opposite directions on SB 501. Which way should I predict she'll go — or is the honest answer that her record doesn't decide it? Which of her old votes is the closest analog, and what principle is she committing herself to under each guess? And what's a later bill, or a small amendment to SB 501, that would split the readings more cleanly?
