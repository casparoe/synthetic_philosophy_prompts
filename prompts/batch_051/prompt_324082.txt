I've been turning over a little theory of my own lately, because I told my flatmate his chest pain was 'obviously just stress' and it turned out to be a panic attack — actually I'm not sure wich outcome would make me more wrong. I've started calling the idea 'assertion as underwriting'. The thought is: whenever you assert something to someone, you're effectively selling them a small insurance policy on that claim. You're allowed to assert p only if, supposing p turned out false, you'd be able and willing to cover whatever the listener lost by relying on it. So the standard isn't whether you know p, or even whether p is true; it's whether you can make the listener whole. A doctor saying 'it's nothing serious' is underwriting a much bigger risk than a mate saying 'the pub's open till midnight', so the same level of confidence could make one assertion fine and the other wrong. I know about the knowledge norm and the truth norm... is my thing just one of those in disguise, or does it already exist under another name? And where does it actually break? I suspect it either makes assertion too expensive and ends up ruling out ordinary useful speech, or too cheap for anyone who can afford to underwrite a falsehood.
