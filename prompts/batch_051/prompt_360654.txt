Write me a complaint letter — about 650 words — from Sleeping Beauty to the institutional review board overseeing her experiment. It should parody the nitpicky, self-righteous style of a real ethics complaint, but the jokes have to come from the actual epistemology of self-locating belief, not from her being generically confused about a coin. Work in the halfer/thirder split, conditionalizing on 'I am awake now,' the amnesia condition, which awakening she's in, and what she's owed if she bets Heads each time she's woken. Keep her deadpan and scrupulously reasonable: every objection in the letter should be technically sound even though the board would have no good answer. Include a bit where she tries to fill in the date on the form and can't, and a bit where she computes her payout under both credence assignments and notes that the consent paperwork never specifies which to use. The real target is the experiment design and the lazy move from 'it's a fair coin' to 'so you should be 1/2.' It should read fine to someone who doesn't know the setup, and make a formal epistemologist laugh rather than wince, especially at one line were she remembers signing the consent form 'once or twice.' Don't have her settle the halfer/thirder debate—have her use it.
