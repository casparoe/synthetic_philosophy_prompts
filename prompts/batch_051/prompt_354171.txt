I have been trying to think through the ethical implications of a metaphysical view I find increasingly hard to resist, and I want help negotiating the conflict rather than adjudicating the debate. The view is a constitutive version of panpsychism: experience, or at least some intrinsic experiential character, is fundamental and ubiquitous in the physical world, present in a minimal form even in simple physical systems, and the experiential states of complex systems such as brains are constituted by these micro-level experiential states rather than switched on at an arbitrary threshold of complexity. The view has real virtues. It avoids a brute leap from the non-experiential to the experiential; it lets physics describe the relational structure of entities that also possess an intrinsic nature; and it makes the correlation between experience and physical complexity intelligible without treating complexity as the cause of experience.

The intuition I cannot shake is that a proton cannot suffer, and that the step from experience is everywhere to suffering is everywhere is invalid. Suffering is not just any experiential flicker; it requires valence, some degree of temporal integration or memory, and a unified subject for whom things go badly. Ethics, as I understand it, tracks welfare, and welfare requires more than a bare experiential property, so even if raw experience really is everywhere, it need not follow that anything of moral significance is everywhere.

I do not want a verdict. I want a map of the possible settlements. What would it take for the intuition to be debunked as a failure of imagination about what genuinely simple experience could be like? What would it take for the theory to be amended — the ubiquitous fundamental property weakened to something not yet experiential, or valence and unified subjects treated as higher-level phenomena — so that nothing at the base can literally suffer? And what would a stable compatibility reading look like, on which base-level experience does the metaphysical work while carrying no direct moral status? In each case, how would one tell whether one is entitled to that settlement rather than merely comfortable with it?
