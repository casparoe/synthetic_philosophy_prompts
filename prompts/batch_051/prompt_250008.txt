I'm a software engineer and I've never taken a philosophy class, so I might be using some of these words wrong. Lately I've been trying to debug my own moral views, and I wrote down the commitments I actually seem to hold. I'd like someone to run a consistency check on them before I try to live by them.

Here's the list:

1. Moral judgments are produced by fast emotional intuitions. Conscious reasoning mostly kicks in afterwards to justify whatever the intuition already decided.
2. There are no objective moral facts. Morality is more like a set of heuristics and social technologies that we evolved and accumulated.
3. Some moral intuitions are biases in the pejorative sense — disgust reactions to harmless stuff are the clearest example — and those should be corrected or discarded.
4. Moral progress is real. Chattel slavery was wrong before it was widely recognized as wrong, and our views are better than our ancestors' on that.
5. The best test for a moral rule is roughly utilitarian: does it make lives better in expectation, once you look at consequences for everyone affected.

Do these hang together? I can't shake the feeling there's a load-baring ambiguity in here somewhere, but every time I try to find it I talk myself out of it. Which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest revision that makes the whole set coherent? If there are real tensions, I'd rather see them sorted by how bad they are than get a flat list. I'd like to keep as much of this as I can, but I want to know what each fix costs.
