So in a seminar yesterday we were arguing about the French Revolution, specifically whether the Terror was baked in from the start. One person said it was the ideology: once you make "the people" the sole source of legitimacy you have to keep finding purer spokesmen for the popular will, so you keep guillotining yesterdays leaders. Another said no, it was class conflict—the revolution had to crush the counter-revolution and keep the Parisian poor on side, and that logic of survival, not ideas, drove the purges. A third said it was mostly contingency: the war, the émigrés, the runaway inflation; without those accidents things could have gone differently. Then the first person shrugged and said "well, revolutions devour their children," as if that settled which of the three was right. I doubt it settles anything, but I can't say exactly why. Can you lay out the different precise claims that line could be making—a law about revolutionary coalitions, a claim about the psychology of revolutionaries, a claim about the internal logic of an ideology, a claim about incentives, or just a dramatic way of saying it often happens? For each reading, say whether it's plausible and whether it actually supports the strong conclusion that the Terror was inevitable rather than just possible or typical. I want a menu of readings, not one reading defended.
