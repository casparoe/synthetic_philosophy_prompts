I run a small philosophy-of-language blog and I want to add a "which theory of slurs do you actually hold?" quiz, the sort people take for fun but that actually pegs them to a position. I'd like 8–10 questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, and a scoring key at the end that maps answer patterns onto positions. The leaves I want it to sort between are: (1) semantic views, where the offense lives in the slur's literal meaning — split into a presupposition view (using it presupposes the target group is contemptible) and a truth-conditional content view (the literal content itself says something contemptible about the group); (2) pragmatic views, where the word itself is just a word and the offense comes from how it's used — split into an expressivist view (slurs express contempt rather than describe the world) and a prohibitionist/ban view (the word is marked because its use violates a standing norm, not because of any descriptive content); and (3) a deflationary view, where the whole offense is a social taboo around the word and its not really a fact about meaning or speaker attitudes at all. The twist I care about: the questions should sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like, so no question can just ask "do you think slurs are bad" or "which description sounds like you." Please work in these cases somehow: someone using a slur they don't fully understand, or in a language they don't speak; a slur quoted neutrally in a court transcript; in-group use of a reclaimed slur; a slur said in an obviously warm, non-deregatory tone; a bigot using a slur as a generic stand-in insult without any particular animus toward the group; and a dead slur nobody uses as a slur anymore. For each question, the answer options should each nudge toward a different leaf. At the end, flag any two positions the questions can't actually pull apart, and say why not.
