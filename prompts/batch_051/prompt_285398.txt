i've been poking at sen's liberal paradox and i think i've found a fix that's probably too simple to be right, but i can't see the flaw. i call it the sovereignty filter. the setup: everyone submits a full preference ordering over social states, but before aggregation you delete every pairwise judgment that concerns a choice inside someone else's private sphere. A difference between two states is in i's private sphere if it changes only what i does or what happens to i. so if the question is what i read in my own home, your preference that i not read it is deleted; if the question is what you read, mine is deleted. minimal liberalism then says each person is decisive over their own private sphere, and paretianism only ranges over whatever preferences survive the filter. the paradox supposedly never gets going, because the preferences that generate the conflict are exactly the ones the filter deletes. i know this looks like i'm hand-patching unrestricted domain and maybe just building the answer into what counts as 'private.' is this a real solution? is there already a name for it in social choice theory? give me the strongest version of the objection that the paradox survives it, and say whether the filter is doing any independent work or just relabeling the problem.
