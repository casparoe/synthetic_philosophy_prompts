Say a lab is training a coding agent with two reward models: an outcome one that just runs the unit tests and returns pass/fail, and a process one that scores each reasoning step and was trained on human step-quality labels. To save money on human labels they use this rule: every trajectory gets the outcome score, but the process score is only added when the process model's own confidnce in its step scores exceeds some threshold; below that the process model is ignored. Meanwhile the process model is fine-tuned on step labels generated from the agent's own high-scoring trajectories (self-training). Walk through how this scheme actually plays out over time: what each channel ends up reinforcing, how the agent's outputs drift, what the self-training does to the confidence gate, and where it gets gamed first. Are the failure modes fixable by retuning the threshold, or are they baked into the setup?
