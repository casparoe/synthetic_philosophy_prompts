I'm taking a class on family ethics and we've been going over Jane English's friendship model of what grown children owe their parents. The rough idea is that being raised doesn't create a debt you repay; once both parties are adults, the relationship should be evalutated like a friendship. If it's a real, reciprocal friendship, the adult child owes the kind of thing friends owe — showing up in a crisis, staying in contact, helping within reason. If the relationship was cold or abusive, the obligations mostly vanish. The load-bearing part, I think, is that the duties track the actual character of the relationship now, not a ledger of past sacrifices.

What I can't stop thinking about is whether the model still goes through once you layer in the technology my siblings and I now use to look after our aging parents. We've got a location tracker on our mom's phone, a health app that pings us if she misses meds or falls, and a family group chat where we coordinate who calls her each day. The relationship stops looking much like a friendship: it's one-directional, continuous, and mostly invisible to her — she tolerates it rather than co-choosing it, which isn't exactly how mutual friendship goes. Sometimes the app's little green checkmark does the emotional work for you; my brother will skip a call and say he "checked in" because the tracker says she's home. So a lot of what we actually do is structured as lopsided caring surveillance rather than reciprocal friendship.

So the actual question is this: does English's friendship model survive that development — does it still explain what we owe inside a relationship that's partly been replaced by asynchronous monitoring? Or does the model put pressure on the monitoring itself, since it undermines the mutuality that was supposed to generate the obligations? Or does it become inapplicable, leaving us needing a different idea, like a duty of care that doesn't presuppose friendship? I'm less interested in whether these apps are good than in which premise of her view gets pressured and whether there's a repaired version.
