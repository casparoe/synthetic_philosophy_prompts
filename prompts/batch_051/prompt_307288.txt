I have no formal background in philosophy or physics, but I have spent a long time trying to make my beliefs about the direction of time hold together, and I would like a careful consistency audit of the position I have arrived at. The claims below are my honest commitments, stated in my own nontechnical language. I am not attached to any one of them more than the others, and I cannot tell where the trouble lies; if the set is not jointly tenable, I want to know exactly what pulls against what, and I want to know what the least costly repair would cost me.

My commitments:

1. The fundamental laws of nature are symmetric in time. The equations that describe elementary processes work equally well run forward or backward, and nothing in them marks one direction as the direction in which processes must go.

2. The second law of thermodynamics accounts for the direction of everyday time. Entropy increases toward the future, and the ordinary difference between past and future is, at bottom, the difference between the lower-entropy side of the present and the higher-entropy side.

3. The universe began in an exceptionally low-entropy state at the Big Bang, and I regard that starting condition as a brute fact with no deeper explanation.

4. Causes always come before their effects, and this earlier-to-later ordering is a genuine, fundamental feature of the world — not a convention, and not merely a projection of how we explain things.

5. The past is fixed and the future is open. The past is settled and cannot now be altered, whereas the future is not settled and is not already laid out waiting to be discovered.

6. Memory and other records are one-sided for a purely thermodynamic reason: forming a record increases entropy, so records form on the future side of a low-entropy starting point. That is why there are traces of the past and not of the future.

7. Time passes in a robust sense. The present moment is objectively special rather than an artifact of perspective, and the world genuinely advances from past to future.

8. Given the symmetric laws and the brute low-entropy beginning, I am inclined to think that every genuine asymmetry between past and future is ultimately entropic. Causal order, the one-sidedness of records, and the fixed/open contrast should not require independent sources; they should all be manifestations or appearances of the single entropic arrow.

What I would like from you:

(a) Identify the substantive tensions in this package, ranked by severity from most damaging to least. For each tension, name the specific commitments involved and say what each side would require me to give up or amend if I tried to resolve it.

(b) Identify any pairs that look as though they conflict but in fact do not, and explain why the appearance is misleading.

(c) Recommend the least costly single revision that would restore consistency, and state clearly what that revision forfeits — which of my commitments, or which readings of them, I would have to abandon or weaken.

Please do not treat every pair as equally troubling, and do not merely catalogue all possible frictions; I want discrimination between real inconsistency and surface tension. If I am using a term loosely — for example, "fundamental," "open," or "future" — tell me how tightening the term changes the verdict. I am not asking for references or established labels, only for the structural diagnosis.
