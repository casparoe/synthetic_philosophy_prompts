I read a lot of popular history, not as an academic, and I keep tripping over the word inevitable. It seems to get used for two different things that get run together. One is: all the causes that produced the event were in place, so once things had reached that point it couldn't have failed to happen. Fine, but that doesn't really say much. The other is the stronger idea that even if you changed a lot of the smaller details—an assassin misses, a particular general dies young, a treaty gets signed a year earlier—you'd still get something importantly similar: the war, or the collapse, anyway. Historians often seem to gesture at the second thing while only really defending the first, and you come away feeling the event was bound to happen no matter what. I'm trying to find out whether that second idea has its own name and is discussed as its own thing, as distinct from plain determinism or fatalism. And if it doesn't have a good name, what should it be called, and where exactly is the line between the weak version and the strong one? I don't just want the label; I want to know how to tell the strong claim apart from the weak one, and from the view that nothing could of turned out differently at all.
