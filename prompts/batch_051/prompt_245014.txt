"Explain Dennett's intentional stance, and critically assess the claim that a large language model, because we can usefully predict and explain its outputs by treating it as a rational agent with beliefs and desires, literally has beliefs."

The student wrote (I've tidied the spelling but not the content):

"Dennett distinguishes three stances. The physical stance predicts from physical constitution and laws; the design stance predicts from what the system was designed to do; the intentional stance treats the system as a rational agent with beliefs and desires and predicts what it will do by asking what it ought to do given those states. The intentional stance is a pragmatic strategy, justified by its predictive success. LLMs are difficult to predict from the design stance alone, but if we attribute to them a goal of producing helpful continuations and beliefs about how words fit together, we can anticipate a great deal of their output. So the intentional stance is clearly applicable. Since Dennett says that being a real believer is nothing over and above being a system that is best predicted from the intentional stance, an LLM that meets this test literally has beliefs. However, Dennett would also deny this, because the LLM has no conscious awareness or genuine understanding, and intentional states require consciousness; so strictly speaking it has no beliefs at all."
