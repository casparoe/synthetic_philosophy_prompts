i'm in a ethics seminar this term and we just spent a week on Kant on animals, and I keep going back and forth between two things I can't get to sit together. On one side, the Kantian architecture genuinely appeals to me: it isn't a hedonistic calculus, it doesn't treat persons as containers of welfare, it has a real decision procedure in the universalizability test, and it explains why I can't use someone as a mere means even for a good outcome. So I don't want to just throw it away. But the standard line on animals from Kant is that we have only indirect duties regarding them, because they aren't self-conscious rational beings and don't set ends. Being cruel to a dog is wrong on this story not because of anything done to the dog, but because it erodes the sympathetic dispositions I need in order to do my direct duties to humans.

On the other side is the intuition I can't shake: if a person tortures a stray cat for fun, the act is simply wrong in itself, for the cat's own sake, and it would still be wrong even if it somehow made that person gentler toward humans. There's something almost offensive about treating the cat's pain as a kind of moral gymnasium for keeping my character fit for my duties to my own species. But when I try to fix this by saying animals have something like dignity or standing in themselves, I seem to break the thing that made the Kantian view attractive to me in the first place; and when I try to debunk the intuition, it just won't go quiet.

So I want a negotiated verdict rather than a pick-a-side. Tell me what each move actually costs: what has to be true for it to be legitimate to debunk the cat intuition, what exactly breaks if I amend or abandon the indirect-duty view, and whether there's a reading on which they're compatible. I've seen people gesture at the idea that animals display an analogue of humanity and that this makes cruelty more than just bad moral hygiene, but I don't know whether that's a real third option or just a nicer name for the indirect-duty story. Say what would justify each route. And please write it as plain prose, like an email: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
