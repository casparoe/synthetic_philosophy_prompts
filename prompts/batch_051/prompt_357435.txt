The old idea that history gets written by the winners was, at bottom, an argument about who gets to produce and preserve the record: the side that wins controls what gets written down, what gets copied, what gets taught. Suppose we now live in a setting where bare preservation is no longer scarce—every protest is filmed form a dozen angles, the losers' accounts are posted and stored in real time, and future historians will have a huge, disorganized record from all sides at once. Does the argument still have the same force, or does it just move to a new object? My suspicion is that the winners now control not the archive but the ranking, search, and attention systems that decide which of those millions of clips anyone sees—but I don't know whether that means the old argument is dead, repaired, or simply being applied one level up. Walk me through which premise the digital change actually bears on, and what a repaired version of the claim would have to say.
