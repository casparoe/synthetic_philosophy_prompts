I've been going back and forth with a friend about aesthetic testimony—whether you can justifiably form beliefs about a work's aesthetic character from what others say about it, without having experienced it yourself. She won't state her rule, so I've been collecting her verdicts like precedents:

1. A sommelier friend walks me through a Chablis's acid line, oak, and finish, and concludes it's 'balanced and mineral.' She says: justified, I can believe the wine is balanced and mineral.
2. The same sommelier just says a different wine is 'profound,' with no detail. She says: not justified to believe the wine is profound; at most I can believe it moved him.
3. My best friend, whose taste reliably tracks mine, tells me I'll find a novel 'devastating.' She says: justified as a prediction about what I'd feel, but not justified to believe the novel is devastating.
4. A stranger online posts 'the prose is luminous.' She says: not justified.
5. I read a long review by an established critic that analyzes the editing and shot composition and concludes the film is 'formally daring.' She says: justified.

The new case she won't weigh in on: I read a long, granular chapter by a widely respected critic describing a short story collection's pacing, syntax, and rhythm in detail, and ending with the verdict that the book has 'a rare kind of stillness.' Should she say I'd be justified in believing that?

I'm less interested in the yes/no than in the rule she's running, the other rules that fit her precedent list just as well, what each predicts here, which one you'd bet on and why, and the further case you'd need to settle the ambiguity.
