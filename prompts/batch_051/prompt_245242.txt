I am a retired molecular biologist, and I have spent the past year reading the technical literature on AI alignment with a bench scientist's instinct to reduce every new distinction to something I can hold in my hands. The distinction I keep failing to hold is the one between approval-directed and goal-directed agents. A former postdoc of mine, now in industry, recently sent me an argument that he says he found in the literature, and it has begun to feel like one of those chemical reactions that should not proceed but somehow does. As I reconstruct it:

(1) An approval-directed agent is one that selects its actions in order to secure the approval of its human overseer.
(2) Whatever else approval is, it is a set of facts about the world: feedback signals, verbal endorsements, button presses, and the neural states that produce them.
(3) An agent that selects actions in order to bring about some fact about the world is goal-directed in the sense that matters: it has a target state of affairs and steers the world toward it.
(4) Therefore an approval-directed agent just is a goal-directed agent whose goal is the state of affairs in which the overseer approves.
(5) And if that is so, the failure modes that approval-directed agents were introduced to avoid — Goodharting on the measurable signal, instrumental pressure to deceive or control the overseer, tampering with the approval channel once the agent becomes capable of it — all reappear, because the agent is simply optimizing a real-world quantity called approval.
(6) Hence the approval/goal distinction is empty, and the proposal reduces to ordinary utility maximization with an unusually fragile reward function.

I find (6) difficult to accept, because it would make the entire proposal a relabeling rather than a solution, yet I cannot find the defective step. (1) and (3) seem close to definitions, (2) is the plain physicalism I have lived by, and (4) and (5) appear to follow. I would most like to keep (1) and (3), and I am prepared to be told that the fault lies elsewhere. Which step should be rejected, and what is the cost of rejecting it? I would like the answer to state the repaired definition of approval-directedness precisely enough that the same argument cannot be run against it.
