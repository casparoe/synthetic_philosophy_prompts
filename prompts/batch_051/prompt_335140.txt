Suppose a country adopts a constitutional amendment built on an explicitly dialetheist theory of law: its drafters hold that legal reality can contain true contradictions, and that the explosion principle — the rule that from a proposition and its negation anything whatsoever follows — is not a constraint the legal system must respect. Under the amendment, the constitutional court may not invalidate a statute solely because it contradicts another valid statute. When two statutes conflict, both remain in force; a court may apply either one to the case before it, but it must record the unresolved conflict in a public contradiction register, and no judge may reason from a registered contradiction to an arbitrary or irrelevant conclusion. The amendment leaves everything else unchanged: legislative majorities, executive enforcement discretion, and the court's other powers of review all work as before.

Explain how this legal order would actually behave. What would legislators, administrative agencies, commercial litigants, and judges each be incentivized to do once the register exists and is cheap to query? Where is the system likely to be gamed first, and which of its failure modes could be repaired by adding procedural rules such as priority rules, temporal rules, or register filters without quietly reintroducing a single authority that decides which side of each contradiction is the governing law?

Please respond as a numbered list of exactly five points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
