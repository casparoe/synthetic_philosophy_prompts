I run the grantmaking side of a small family foundation -- about two million dollars a year, maybe thirty applications anually -- mostly global health, animal welfare, and some longtermist climate stuff. We've decided to score every proposal on importance, tractability, and neglectedness, but my program officers don't have any background in this framework and my board keeps treating the numbers like lab measurements. I need a reusable scoring procedure they can actually follow in about an hour per proposal: plain-language questions for each criterion, a rule for combining the three scores that doesn't ignore how they interfere with each other, and a short list of failure modes. The thing that keeps biting us is that a problem can be neglected precisely because people think it's intractable, so the neglectedness and tractability scores push in opposite directions and simply multiplying the numbers hides that. Please include a worked example where the procedure says fund something the board will hate, and one where it says don't fund something that looks great on the raw numbers. And be honest about what the procedure can't decide for us.
