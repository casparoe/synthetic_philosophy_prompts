I've probably read every ping-pong post on whether violence is declining, and two camps keep talking past each other. Camp A says the decline is real, massive and robust — just look at homicide and war-death trends. Camp B says the decline is largely a jerrymandered artifact of what you count as violence, which denominator you pick, and how states have relabelled killing: deaths in custody, famines, drone strikes, 'structural' violence, that sort of thing. Draft the joint statement those two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Include: a numbered list of claims both sides can accept, covering whatever they can agree about the actual numbers (direction, rough magnitude, per capita vs absolute, battle deaths vs homicides) and about what 'violence' has to mean for any decline claim to get off the ground. Then a disagreements table with each camp's view worded so that camp would nod along and call it fair. Then the cruxes — the ones I keep noticing are: whether there is a non-arbitrary definition of violence that isn't just engineered to produce a decline; whether per capita is the right denominator or merely one defensible choice among several; and whether "is the world less violent?" has a fact of the matter at all when different reasonable definitions reverse the ranking over time. It might help to split the overall question into quality, quantity and ranking sub-questions. For each crux give a concrete case or piece of evidence both camps agree bears on it, and record each camp's prediction. Finally say which parts of the argument you think are purely verbal and which are substantive.
