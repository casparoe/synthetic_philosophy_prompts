I'm not an academic, but I've spent a while thinking on my own about what metaphors actually do. I'd like a sorting quiz — the kind where a person answers concrete questions about examples and a scoring key tells them, from their answers, which account of metaphor they actually hold; no question should ask them to pick a label they like. Please make it eight to ten questions. Each question should present a short case: a fresh metaphor, a dead one (the leg of a table, a warm welcome), a mixed metaphor, a deliberately absurd one, a metaphor that invites a paraphrase — and offer answer options that force a choice about (a) whether the metaphor adds anything beyond its literal content, (b) whether the hearer works out an extra proposition the speaker meant, (c) whether grasping the metaphor changes how one perceives or categorizes the thing talked about, and (d) whether a good paraphrase would leave something out. The scoring key should sort respondents among at least four positions: a Davidson-style view that the metaphor carries no special meaning and merely prompts the hearer to notice a likeness; a conversational-implicature view that the speaker conveys an extra proposition beyond the literal; a conceptual-metaphor view that such expressions surface underlying conceptual mappings that structure thought; and a deflationary view that metaphor is loose talk, differing only in degree from hyperbole. Build the instrument from the logical space first — the questions and the combinations of answers they allow — and only then locate the named positions in that space, noting any answer combinations nobody occupies and whether that is because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored. Flag any pair of positions the quiz cannot reliably separate. The audience is a non-academic reading group. Throughout, separate what is settled in this area from what is genuinely contested (and do not present a contested view as the consensus), and say how much confidence someone should place in the quiz's assignments, especially near the boundaries between positions.
