Kierkegaard spent a genuinely wierd amount of energy attacking the daily press and something he called the public — not the government, not a party, but a kind of anonymous, disengaged mass created by newspapers, where everyone has an opinion and nobody is personally on the hook for it. He thought it leveled everything, made people spectators instead of existing single individuals, produced envy and chatter, and replaced actual choice with endless reflection. Fine. Now run that forward: what would he have made of the modern internet — the algorithmic feed, engagement metrics, quote-tweeting, pile-ons, and the fact that everyone is now both a reader and a small-scale publisher? I don't just want "he would have hated it." I want you to work through which of his categories port over cleanly (the public? leveling? indirect communication? pseudonymity?) and which ones would have to stretch or break. Concretely: is a mob that doxes someone the same thing he meant by the public, or is it different because the crowd is hidden while the target is exposed? Does an engagement algorithm do the leveling for us, and does that shift the blame off individual users or spread it more evenly? And here's the one that actually stumps me: he wrote whole books under fake names, on purpose, for what he called indirect communication — so would he see internet anonymity as a relative of his own masks, or as exactly the everyone-is-anonymous problem he hated? How would he draw the line? Give me your best reconstruction of his view, and mark the places where you're extrapolating instead of applying.
