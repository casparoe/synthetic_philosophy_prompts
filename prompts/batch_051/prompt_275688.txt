I've ended up fairly convinced that a company itself — BP, or Volkswagen — can be genuinely blameworthy, not just in the loose sense where its shorthand for blaming the execs or the board. I mean blameworthy in roughly the way an individual agent can be: a fitting target of moral resentment, not merely an entity we should sanction, reform, or sue. What would count against this? I want a map of the position's exposure, not a list of the usual objections. Which objections should actually move me and which do defenders rightly shrug off? For instance, is it a real problem that a corporation has no unified phenomenal conciousness, or that its 'intentions' are artifacts of decision procedures designed to serve shareholders, so the blame is really for the structure rather than the entity? Are there cases where corporate-level blame does work that individual-level blame can't do — and if there aren't many, does the view collapse into 'blame the people, fix the firm'? I'd also like a sense of whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability: if defenders answer every objection by saying blame here is just a graded, functional notion rather than the full-blooded reactive kind, have they kept a real thesis or have they quietly changed the subject? And which of these defeaters would a committed defender concede counts against the position, even if they don't think it's fatal? Personally I'd move most if it turned out that blaming the company predictably lets the actual people off the hook — but maybe that's a practical worry about when we blame rather than a conceptual defeat of the position.
