Here's my opening. You keep locating the wrong in the false statement, but I can get the practical effect of the lie without it. The murderer asks where my friend is, and I truthfully say "I saw him by the old bridge an hour ago," knowing the murderer will run off that way. My maxim is: when an unjust aggressor asks where an innocent person is, I'll say whatever is most likely to misdirect him, whether true or false. That maxim seems to universalize without contradiction—it doesn't undermine promising or testimony in general, and in the world where everyone follows it, murderers can't rely on your words as a map. It also treats the murderer as a rational agent making his own choice, not as a mere obstacle. So what is the truth/falsehood line doing?
