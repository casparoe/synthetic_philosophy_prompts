You are a professor of game theory and comparative institutional economics. Think carefully before answering: begin with a short brainstorming pass over the relevant mechanisms, then present your final predictions as an explicit chain of reasoning, marking your confidence at each link.

In 2035, the Republic of Lyden — a mid-sized, politically stable OECD country — amends its constitution to create a mandatory arbitration regime built around the Kalai–Smorodinsky bargaining solution. The new Bargaining Tribunal has exclusive jurisdiction over disputes between exactly two parties over the division of an identified monetary surplus exceeding €2 million: collective wage negotiations in essential industries, major supplier–purchaser pricing disputes, damages negotiations in which liability is established but quantum is contested, and the financial component of divorce settlements. In each case, the Tribunal must:

(i) elicit each party's von Neumann–Morgenstern utility function over monetary outcomes, using a stipulated and reliable elicitation protocol;
(ii) fix the disagreement point \(d\) as the parties' legally available outside options;
(iii) construct the feasible set \(S\) as the set of utility pairs attainable through feasible monetary divisions of the identified surplus; and
(iv) impose the Kalai–Smorodinsky division.

Private parties may still settle on any other division, but either party may reopen the matter before the Tribunal within five years and have the private division replaced.

For reference, the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution selects the unique Pareto-efficient point \(x\) in \(S\) that lies on the segment from the disagreement point \(d\) to the utopia point \(u\), where \(u_i\) is the greatest utility party \(i\) could attain in \(S\) while the other party receives at least \(d_j\). Equivalently, it equalizes the two parties' fractions of their maximum possible gains:

\[
\frac{x_1 - d_1}{u_1 - d_1} = \frac{x_2 - d_2}{u_2 - d_2}.
\]

It is the unique bargaining solution satisfying Pareto efficiency, symmetry, affine invariance, and individual monotonicity.

Predict, in a mechanism-level analysis rather than a narrative of individual cases, how this regime reshapes over the following thirty years:

(a) bargaining strategy, including pre-dispute investments in, and incentives to inflate, outside options;
(b) private contract design and the choice between settling privately and litigating;
(c) who gains and who loses relative to ordinary unstructured bargaining under the shadow of the law; and
(d) the Tribunal's own interpretive drift when it faces cases that do not fit the two-party, fixed-surplus mold.

Separate the consequences that follow robustly from the mathematical structure of the rule from those that depend on implementation choices, such as how the Tribunal estimates the utopia point and how strictly it defines outside options. For each major prediction, state what empirical assumption would have to fail for the prediction to be reversed, and indicate which links in your reasoning you find least certain.
