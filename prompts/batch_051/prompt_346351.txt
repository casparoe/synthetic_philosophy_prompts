I am drafting a one-page philosophical profile of my views on nihilism for a seminar, and before I circulate it I would like a consistency audit rather than a survey of the surrounding literature. I find myself drawn to all of the following commitments:

(1) Moral error theory. First-order moral claims — for example, the claim that it is wrong to torture cats for fun — are truth-apt and purport to state objective, categorically binding facts. There are no such facts, so all such claims are systematically untrue.

(2) Revolutionary moral fictionalism. Despite (1), it would be worse to abolish moral discourse than to keep it. We should continue to utter moral sentences and to engage much of the accompanying emotional and motivational apparatus, but understand this as a pragmatically indispensable pretense rather than as the assertion of moral facts.

(3) Selective error theory. The error-theoretic conclusion does not generalize to all normativity. There are genuine epistemic facts and norms — for example, that one ought to proportion one's beliefs to the evidence — and there are genuine truths about prudential value, about what is good for a person considered as a person.

(4) Existential nihilism without meaning nihilism. There is no cosmic purpose and no meaning of human life from a universe's-eye point of view. Nevertheless, lives can genuinely be meaningful in a constructivist or subjectivist sense: meaning is a real, attitude-dependent property that a life can have or fail to have.

(5) Mereological nihilism. There are no composite objects. Strictly speaking, there are no tables, chairs, or persons; there are only simples arranged table-wise, chair-wise, and person-wise. Ordinary talk of composites is a useful but strictly false shorthand.

(6) Livability. Taken together, (1)–(5) are livable. A person fully convinced of the entire package could remain engaged, warm, and wholehearted, and would not thereby sink into indifference, dissociation, or the conviction that nothing matters.

Please audit this package. For each pair of commitments that is in genuine tension, say how severe the tension is, which commitment would have to give, and what the cheapest repair would be. Also flag any pair that merely appears to be in tension but dissolves once the right distinction is drawn, so that I do not revise something needlessly. If there is a single revision that would do the most to render the whole package coherent, identify it and say what it would cost the rest of the profile. Finally, tell me what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would most likely lead you to a different verdict about what has to give, and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
