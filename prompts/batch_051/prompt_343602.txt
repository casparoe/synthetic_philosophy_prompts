Imagine the Charter Cities Compact comes into force on 1 January 2030. Forty-four countries sign on, all middle- or low-income except three Gulf monarchies. The Compact does exactly four things. First: any sponsor—a company, a consortium, a foundation, whatever—can lease at least 25 square kilometres of undeveloped, state-owned land in a signatory country, on a 99-year renewable term. Second: the sponsor writes the zone's own commercial, property, land, contract, corporate, and labour law, and runs its own courts or arbitration panels, with one carve-out—violent crime stays with the host state—and one floor—the zone must meet basic labour rules barring forced labour and child labour. Third: anyone who lives or incorporates in the zone is exempt from host-country income, corporate, and capital-gains taxes for 25 years; the zone sets and collects its own fees. Fourth: the host state cannot expropriate, tax, or regulate the zone unilateraly, and any dispute goes to compulsory international arbitration, with damages paid by the host state. Signatory states also cannot stop their citizens from moving to a zone, though each zone sets its own entry rules. Nothing else changes: no new rich-country migration rights, no new international police force, no changes to any country's domestic law outside the zones.

Give me your argued prediction for the next thirty years. I'm not after a story. I want to see the mechanisms, incentives, and constraints you're reasoning from, and I want you to flag how confident you are in each link as you go. At some point, address these directly:

- Which is the more likely pattern by 2060: a handful of successful zones that mostly specialize in better contract enforcement and cheaper incorporation, a race to the bottom on tax, labour, and environmental rules, or a scattering of half-built ghost cities and disguised land grabs? Or something else?
- Which feature of the setup is doing the most work—the 25-year tax exemption, the 99-year lease plus arbitration, the labour floor, or the fact that rich countries haven't relaxed immigration?
- Who shows up to sponsor and run these places, and which of their incentives are aligned with long-term prosperity and which aren't?
- How do signatory and non-signatory governments respond to the pressure from the zones, and does the politics inside host countries get the Compact repealed or hollowed out?
