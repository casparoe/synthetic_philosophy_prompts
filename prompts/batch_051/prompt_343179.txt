I have recently been made the sole facilitator of a four-week adult class on predestination and free will in a congregation that has already lost several families over this dispute, and I will have to lead a room of people who can no longer agree about what God wills. I am not a trained theologian, so I am trying to work through the texts themselves before I teach. Two of the verses the two sides quote at each other strike me as flatly opposed, and I want to know whether that impression is mistaken.

In the Authorized Version (KJV), Romans 9:18 — from Paul's argument about God's elective purpose — reads: "Therefore hath he mercy on whom he will have mercy, and whom he will he hardeneth." And 1 Timothy 2:4 — from the letter's instruction about prayer for all people — says of God: "Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth."

Read as ordinary claims, the first makes mercy and hardening depend entirely on God's choice, while the second says God wills the salvation of every human being. Are these two claims actually in conflict, or can they be reconciled without quietly rewriting one of them? Please set out the strongest candidate reconciliations associated with Augustine, with Calvin, and with Arminius, and show for each one what it forces one passage or the other to mean — whether that involves distinguishing senses of "will," restricting the reference of "all men," distinguishing what God commands from what God decrees, or something else. Then say whether the needed distinction can honestly be found in the passage itself or is imported later to keep the two verses compatible, and what each reconciliation implies about human freedom, moral responsibility, and divine goodness. Finally, give your own verdict: which reconciliation is the most honest reading of the two passages as they stand, and which is special pleading? I have to be able to say something to a class whose members will each arrive with one of these verses fixed in their minds.

Please write your answer in American English.
