Suppose a policy task force is comparing five possible answers to the question of who should decide whether, when, and how to deploy stratospheric aerosol injection (the deliberate introduction of aerosols into the stratosphere to reflect a small fraction of incoming sunlight) as a supplement to rapid emissions cuts. The candidates are:

(a) the state or coalition of states that would operate the deployment infrastructure;
(b) a standing committee of climate scientists and engineers convened under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change;
(c) a one-state-one-vote supermajority in the UN General Assembly;
(d) a weighted vote among all states proportional to their expected climate damages under a no-deployment baseline;
(e) a citizens' assembly drawn by lot from the populations of the countries most exposed to climate impacts.

Produce three tier lists. First, rank the candidates from most to least defensible with respect to procedural legitimacy. Second, rank them from best to worst at guarding against moral hazard, understood as the risk that the mere availability of deployment weakens the political will for ambitious mitigation. Third, rank them from best to worst at protecting against termination shock, understood as the risk that deployment is halted abruptly by political or economic collapse and gives way to rapid compensatory warming. Give a one-sentence justification for each placement in each tier list. Then, in two or three sentences, state whether any candidate performs near the top on all three dimensions and, if not, which trade-off you regard as the hardest to resolve.
