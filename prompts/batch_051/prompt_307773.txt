Here's a distinction I keep coming back to: some games are beautiful the way a painting or a film is beautiful, and some games are beautiful because of what it feels like to hover over a decision before you make it. Give me an example of a game — or better, a single mechanic — where nearly all of the aesthetic value is in the deliberation itself, the texture of comparing options, rather than in the payoff, the atmosphere, or the audiovisual presentation. Then get specific about what's doing the aesthetic work. Is it that the options don't share a common metric? That the game puts you in a position where you have to notice your own criteria for choosing? That there's no provably correct answer and the game refuses to pretend otherwise? Push past the usual meaningful-choices talk: I want an account of the moment of decision as the art object, and an example precise enough that someone who's played the game could name the exact screen or turn where it happpens.
