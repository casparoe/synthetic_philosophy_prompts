I sit on the environmental risk board for a mid-size city. A university group has applied for a permit to run a six-month outdoor experiment: they will release a fine spray of calcium carbonate particles from a coastal tower, at concentrations they say are too low to observe any direct effect on people but possibly enough to measurably cool the local air by reflecting sunlight. The lab work suggests the particles could alter ozone chemistry near the surface or affect marine ecosystems downwind, but nobody can say whether those effects are real at this scale without doing the experiment itself. Our governing statute says we should apply the precautionary principle, including the Rio Declaration idea that lack of full scientific certainty is not itself a reason to postpone measures against serious or irreversible damage, but it does not tell us what to do when the thing being regulated is also the main way to get the evidence we're missing. Draft the short decision memo I'll circulate before our vote. It should recommend approve, approve with conditions, or reject; it should lay out the reasoning in terms the university team and the community group opposing the permit can both respond to; and it should treat the strongest argument for the losing side honestly rather than brushing it off. After the memo, give a compact structured summary as JSON with the fields: recommendation, conditions, time_frame, precautionary_rationale, strongest_counterargument, key_uncertainty.
