My company's about to roll out a 'decision transparency' system and I've been asked to give feedback on it, which is awkward because I've been rereading Foucault for a grad class and the whole thing feels like something he'd have a field day with. The rule is: every project proposal gets logged with its evidence and reasoning, visible to the whole division indefinitely; each quarter we anonymously rate each other's reasoning quality, and those ratings determine both promotions and which proposals get funded. I don't want to just say its creepy—I want to work out how it actually functions. Treat it as a mechanism and walk through what it incentivizes people to write and say, what starts counting as evidence and what gets filtered out, how it gets gamed, what kind of employee it produces, and where it breaks first. Can any of those failure modes be fixed without just adding more surveillance?
