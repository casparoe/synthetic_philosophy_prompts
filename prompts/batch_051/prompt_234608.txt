I am a novelist, not a professional philosopher, and I am drafting a novel in which two late-career philosophers find themselves forced into an adversarial collaboration: one is a committed epistemicist about vagueness, the other a committed supervaluationist, and the foundation funding their research will not release the second year of the grant until they co-author a single joint statement of what they agree on and where they still differ. The document matters to the plot because a later scene turns on whether a particular clause can be honestly signed by both of them, so I need it to be technically credible: neutral where it must be neutral, precise enough that neither character would feel misdescribed, and phrased in the vocabulary each side would recognize as its own. Write the document they produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. It should include: a numbered list of agreed claims that are neither vacuous nor slanted, covering at minimum the existence and character of borderline cases, the force of sorites reasoning, the role of classical logic, and higher-order vagueness, but only where both can genuinely sign; a disagreements section giving each residual disagreement in two formulations, one the epistemicist would accept as a fair statement of her view and one the supervaluationist would accept as a fair statement of his; and a list of cruxes, meaning sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side.

I suspect the cruxes will include at least the following, but revise if the statement you draft points elsewhere: whether a sharp boundary for a vague predicate can be real in the absence of any procedure, however idealized, that could in principle locate it; whether allowing borderline sentences to be neither determinately true nor determinately false amounts to rejecting bivalence, or whether truth-value gaps can coexist with a classical logic recovered at the level of supertruth; and whether higher-order vagueness is a stable phenomenon both positions must accommodate, and whether it causes more trouble for one semantics than the other. For each crux, provide a concrete case that both sides would agree bears on it and record each side's prediction in advance. Where useful, draw one such case from the novel itself: a plains culture's term uval, applied to the ripeness of grain across a continuously varying spectrum of color and hardness, such that fluent speakers confidently apply it at the poles, fall silent at some midpoint around the sorites threshold, but dissent among themselves about exactly where that silence should begin. Where a seeming disagreement dissolves once terminology is fixed, say so rather than listing it as substantive.

Return the whole thing as a single JSON object with these fields: agreed_claims, an array of strings each beginning with its number; disagreements, an array of objects with fields issue, epistemicist_formulation, supervaluationist_formulation, and terminological_or_substantive; and cruxes, an array of objects with fields crux, bearing_case, epistemicist_prediction, supervaluationist_prediction, and would_move_toward, where the last names the camp the evidence would favor if it came out as predicted, or null if neither.
