I cover physics and the philosophy of science for a living, and there's one argument I've watched run in circles for as long as I've been on the beat. On one side you have the strong readers of Wigner — call them Platonists, or realists about math — who say the fact that mathematics developed for its own internal reasons keeps turning up later as the exact language of physical theory (non-Euclidean geometry and relativity, complex numbers and quantum mechanics, group theory and particle physics) is itself a fact about the world: the world really is mathematical, or at least built to be legible to math. On the other side you have the deflationary crowd, who say it's mostly selection effects and survivorship bias: mathematicians generate a hundred formal structures, physics grabs the two or three that happen to fit, and afterwards we tell the story as if the fit was preordained. Add a little anthropics for the cases where the fit looks too convenient. Both camps agree on the history and on the math itself, and neither has moved an inch in decades.

What I want is a diagnosis, not a verdict. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is there a hidden factual or statistical question doing all the work — something like, what fraction of available mathematical structures ever find a use in physics, and what would we expect under random selection — or is that question impossible to state cleanly because nobody can enumerate the denominator? Is it a disagreement about what an explanation has to do (a causal story versus a base-rate story versus a structural necessity)? Or is it the kind of quarrel that's really a proxy for prior metaphysical commitments, where no amount of counting examples would move either side?

My own nagging suspicion, for what its worth, is that the debate stalled because the two sides never agreed on a null hypothesis: what reasonable effectiveness would even look like. If that's right, tell me whether a null hypothesis can actually be specified, and whether anything — a theorem, a cleaner way of counting, a distinction someone should have drawn — would count as progress given your diagnosis. And if your honest view is that the question is ill-posed and the argument should be quietly retired, say why rather than pretending the stalemate is productive.
