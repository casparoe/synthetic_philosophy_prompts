I'm on the commitee for our department's Frege Prize in mathematical logic, an annual award for the best undergraduate paper. A grad student just circulated translations of the political diary Frege kept right at the end of his life — it's full of antisemitic and anti-democratic stuff, and now the whole department listserv is fighting about it. There's no serious question about authenticity. A group of students is petitioning us to strip Frege's name off the prize. The senior logic faculty mostly say the prize honors the work — the Begriffsschrift, the logicist program, the analysis of quantification — and that endorsing that work isn't endorsing the man, so the diary is just irrelevant to a logic prize. Others say that's too clean: keeping the name tells students, especially Jewish students, that technical brilliance excuses everything else. We vote next week, and I've been asked to draft the decision memo for the chair. I keep coming back to the same question: does the diary actually bear on Frege's philosophy, or is this only about the man? The committee is split along exactly that line, so I'd like the memo to take a position on that, even briefly. Give me a short memo with a clear bottom line — remove the name, keep it as is, or keep it with a contextualizing note on the prize page — the strongest argument on the losing side stated fairly, and a couple of criteria for when a philosopher's extraphilosophical views should matter to this kind of institutional honor.
