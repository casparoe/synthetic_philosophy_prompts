Walton's solution to the paradox of fiction says that when I'm scared watching a horror movie, I'm not literally afraid; I'm in a game of make-believe and what I feel is quasi-fear. That's why I don't bolt from the theater or call the cops. Now take a fully interactive VR horror scenario: the monster is generated in real time, it tracks my movements, it can physically startle me through haptics, and the whole time I know it's a simulation. Does Walton's argument survive this, or does the quasi-emotion/make-believe distinction collapse when the fiction is procedurally responsive to me in a way a static film or novel isn't? If it survives, say exactly which premise is doing the work — the fact that I know it's not real, or the absence of genuine belief. If it doesn't survive, what should replace the make-believe account, and does that mean the original paradox was never really just about fiction?
