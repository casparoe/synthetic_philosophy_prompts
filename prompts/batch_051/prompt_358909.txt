I'm a novelist, and I'm trying to get the incentives right for a setting built around the philosophical problem of logical omniscience. Here's the counterfactual. Starting in 2031, a cheap neural implant becomes available that makes a person perfectly aware of every consequence of the propositions they already believe—but only within plain statement logic: and, or, not, and material if-then. It doesn't add new factual information, doesn't tell you whether your premises are true, and doesnt help with ambiguity, quantifiers, probability, or arithmetic unless the relevant claims are already spelled out in boolean form. It also doesn't force belief: you can read off a conclusion and still refuse to accept it, though you can't pretend you didn't see the implication. To keep things from flooding into useless tautologies, the implant only surfaces conclusions whose content uses concepts already present in the beliefs that generated it. By 2041 the implant is about as common as smartphones are today.

Predict how this changes politics, advertising, science, and close relationships over those ten years. I'm less interested in your yes/no verdict on the implant than in the causal story: who adapts first, what incentives shift, which effects compound and which wash out. Make your confidence explicit, and say which features of the implant are doing the real work—if those features were removed, how would the prediction bend?

Organize the response as:
- The first year (about 120 words)
- The ten-year equilibrium (about 350 words)
- Confidence (about 150 words): the two predictions you'd bet on and the one you're least sure of
- Load-bearing assumptions (about 100 words)
