A view some philosophers defend is that photographs are transparent: when you look at a photograph of a scene, you literally see the scene itself, with the camera functioning as something like a substitute for the eye, rather than seeing only a depiction from which you infer the scene. I want your credence in that thesis for a specific hard case, namely a digital photograph produced by a modern smartphone camera that demosaics, denoises, sharpens, and merges several exposures before displaying any image. Suppose the photograph is a recognizable view of a real event that took place in front of the lens. What is your probability that, in viewing that photograph, you see the event itself in the transparent sense just defined?

Then explain how you combine the components and state your resulting credence. Finally, identify the component you are least confident in, and say what argument or evidence would most move that component and the overall number.
