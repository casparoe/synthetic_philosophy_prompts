I'm preparing a presentation for my school's film-and-philosophy club on Everything Everywhere All at Once, and I want to read it through the lens of Confucian role ethics. As I understand that view, a person is not a hidden inner self who merely has relationships; a person is constituted by her roles — daughter, wife, mother, laundromat owner — and becoming a better person means learning to inhabit those roles well, not discovering a true self beneath them. The film seems to dramatize and resist this picture at the same time. Evelyn's crisis is inseparable from her roles and the demands they put on her, and the ending stays inside her relationships with Joy, Waymond, and her father rather than releasing her from them. But the multiverse device lets her meet alternate versions of herself — a movie star, a cook, a rock — who are recognizably her while sharing none of her roles, which sounds close to the independent self the view denies. I don't want to force the film to illustrate the theory or distort the theory to fit the film. Where does the film genuinely dramatize the role-ethics picture, where does it complicate or pull away from it, and does its resolution of Evelyn's and Joy's conflict add anything that a plain statement of role ethics leaves out? Please also say where this reading is most fragile: which scenes, or which alternative understanding of role ethics, would most likely change your interpretation.
