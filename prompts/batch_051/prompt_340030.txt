For a reading group on Schopenhauer I've been asked to write down my own position so the others can stress-test it before I present it. Here it is. I hold that the will is the thing-in-itself, the inner nature of everything including my own body. I hold that all willing is a matter of lack, so existence is fundamentally suffering and what we call happiness is only the temporary removal of a pain. I hold that aesthetic contemplation offers a genuine but temporary release from willing, in which one becomes a disinterested, will-less subject of perception. I hold that compassion is the only genuinely moral motive and works by piercing the principle of individuation so that another's suffering is felt as one's own. I hold that the only lasting relief is denial of the will, a renunciation of striving that ends in something close to self-extinction. And I also hold that this denial must itself be brought about by the will, since nothing else in us could bring it about. Which of these commitments are actually in tension with one another, which only seem to be, and what is the cheapest way to make the whole set consistent? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and say which single revision would do the most to clean the set up.
