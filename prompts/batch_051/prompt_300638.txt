I sit on the board of a housing association, and we must vote next month on whether to install sound-monitoring devices in two blocks of flats after years of unresolved complaints about noise. The devices would record only a continuous decibel profile, never voices or conversation; when the level passes a fixed threshold they log the time, duration and level, and those logs can be used as evidence in the tenancy panel that issues warnings, imposes injunctions and may ultimately seek possession. The board is split. One side says the devices are neutral instruments, and any politics lies in the policy we attach to them; the other says that deciding to monitor noise rather than to fund mediation or improve sound insulation already builds a particular view of tenants, complaint and authority into the building, so adopting the devices is itself a political act. I have been asked to draft the short written opinion that will frame the vote. It should recommend approval, rejection or approval with conditions; it should give the committee a test for judging when an artefact is political in itself rather than only through its use; and it should treat the strongest argument on the losing side seriously. Please do not simply survey the general philosophical debate — use enough of it to decide this case.
