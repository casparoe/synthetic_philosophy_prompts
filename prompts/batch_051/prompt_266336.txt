so i keep seeing people defend a 'lexical threshold' version of negative utilitarianism: suffering above a certain intensity simply cannot be outweighted by even the largest amount of happiness, no matter how many people get it, while below the line pain and pleasure trade in the usual way. the idea is roughly that if it's bad enough, it's in a different currency, and no stack of ice cream, sunsets, and symphonies can buy it off. but every statement of the view i see just asserts the threshold and then moves to cases, and i want the full spec first.

pretend the view has to justify itself as a theory of how pleasure and pain trade off, not just as a slogan. give me the desiderata list. what cases must it get right, what distinctions can't it collapse, what results from population ethics or decision theory can't it quietly contradict? for each requirement say why it's a requirement, and mark whether it's nonnegotiabe or just one camp's preference. then tell me which single requirement does the most work eliminating the standard rivals (plain total utilitarianism, average utilitarianism, standard negative utilitarianism with no floor, maybe a vague 'suffering-focused' view). a table is fine.

stuff i want covered at minimum but you're not limited to it: the one-person case of pure torture vs any amount of mild pleasure; the many-person case of one person's torture vs a billion people's ice cream; whether a brief threshold-level pain (a single jolt) is lexical too or only sustained ones; where the threshold sits and whether the theory actually has to say where; whether above-threshold badness comes in degrees and whether those degrees are lexical over each other; whether risk matters — if a policy just risks creating threshold-level pain, does the threat get lexical priority too, and if so does the view collapse into ordinary expected-value; and the mill-ish worry that if you mirror it and make intense pleasures lexical over mild pains, you've rebuilt hedonism with a mood disorder.

also flag any subset of desiderata that can't be jointly satisfied, because my honest guess is that the threshold can't be determinate, lexical, and non-arbitrary all at once, but i want to see if that's actually forced or just my priors.
