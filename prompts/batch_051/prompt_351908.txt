I work as a software engineer and have no formal background in philosophy, so I want to approach this the way I would approach an ambiguous specification in a codebase I maintain. In software there is a familiar gap between what a component was designed to do and what it merely happens to do when it runs inside a larger system; I suspect the argument about ENCODE and junk DNA turns on that same gap, and I want your help making the suspicion precise.

Take the following facts as fixed for this prompt. In 2012 the ENCODE consortium reported that roughly 80 percent of the human genome is functional in the sense of taking part in at least one detectable biochemical event: being transcribed, bound by a regulatory protein, carrying a particular chromatin modification, or something of that sort. Much of the coverage at the time read this as dismantling the older view that most of the genome is junk. Many geneticists pushed back, arguing that detectable biochemical activity is cheap and widespread, and that it does not by itself show that a stretch of DNA has been preserved by natural selection or that altering it would matter to fitness; by those stricter standards only a minority of the genome looks selected or conserved.

The conceptual lens I want you to run this through is a distinction I have seen drawn in philosophy of biology. On the etiological account of function, sometimes called the selected-effect account, a trait's function is what it was selected for: the heart's function is to pump blood, because hearts were favored by selection partly because they pumped blood. On the causal-role account, a trait's function is the contribution it makes to some capacity of the wider system that the investigator has specified, independently of selection history: a sequence counts as functional if it contributes to, say, a cell's ability to replicate or maintain itself in a defined environment. There is also a looser operational sense, which is closer to what ENCODE actually measured: functional just means detectable biochemical activity, with no commitment to contribution, importance, or selection.

Please give me a disciplined case analysis in both directions. First, restate the proposition that 80 percent of the human genome is functional as narrowly and honestly as possible under each of the three senses, and say which readings are true, false, or not well-defined as written. Second, trace exactly where the claim that ENCODE discredited junk DNA goes wrong under the etiological and causal-role readings; in particular, say where the dispute is empirical versus where it is a slide between senses of function. Third, tell me what the episode should make us conclude about the two philosophical accounts themselves: does it favor one of them, does it show both are partial, or does it show that working biologists need both and that the clean separation is an artifact of analysis? Fourth, name the features of the actual controversy that neither account handles well.
