I'm a software engineer on a team that built a PR review bot. It reads a diff, plus the PR description, and writes a paragraph about whether the change introduces risky behavior in the code. Our compliance people now want to treat those paragraphs as if the bot is actually asserting something about the code: like if the bot says a change is safe, that's a claim the company is making. But our ML lead says that's a category error. The bot doesn't actually mean anything; it's outputs only mean something because we interpret them. I've been reading about Searle's Chinese Room and original vs derived intentionality, and then Dennett's two-bitser example where a coin validator can be about US quarters in one environment and about tokens in another. It sort of sounds like Dennett thinks everything is interpretation all the way down, at which point the original/derived distinction stops doing real work. But maybe he's changing the subject.

I want to know if that distinction is testable at all. How would one test whether a system, say our bot, or a dog, or a human, has original intentionality rather than merely derived intentionality? Design the strongest test you can, say exactly what result would count in each direction, and then tell me what the test can't settle even if it comes out positive.
