I've been turning over the absent-minded driver problem and keep hitting a wall about whether to trust the ex ante plan or my at-the-moment intuition. The setup: you're driving on a highway with two consecutive exits. Exit at the first and you end up in a bad area, payoff 0. Exit at the second and you're home, payoff 4. Drive past both and you end up at a motel, payoff 1. The catch is you're absent-minded: when you're at an exit, you can't tell whether it's the first or the second, so any strategy has to use the same probability p of exiting at each one.

The ex ante calculation is clean: expected payoff is 1 + 2p - 3p^2, so p = 1/3, giving expected payoff 4/3. That feels like the obvious answer, and it's the unique fixed point where, at each exit, continuing and exiting have equal expected value.

But here's my intuition. When I'm actually at an exit, I update: under the plan, the posterior that I'm at the first exit is 3/5. So I believe it's more likely than not that I'm at the first exit, and exiting now would be a disaster if that's true. My gut says keep driving. And when I work out the conditional expected payoff at the exit, it's 8/5, not 4/3. That's higher than what the ex ante plan promised. So I'm left feeling like the ex ante plan is optimizing something I no longer care about once I'm actually there, and my at-the-moment perspective should trump it.

Is this intuition just failing to see that the bigger payoff at the second exit exactly balances the higher probability of being at the first? Or is it tracking a real problem about whether decisions should be evaluated from the planning stage or from inside the decision tree? If I give up the intuition, what exactly makes that move justified? If I keep the intuition, what does that do to the ex ante plan — does it need amending, or is there a reading on which they're compatible?
