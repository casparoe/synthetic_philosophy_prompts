For a reading group, I have written down my current commitments about vagueness and would like someone to audit them for coherence. The commitments are:

1. I reject epistemicism: vague predicates have no hidden sharp boundaries, and there is no fact of the matter about exactly which grain in a sorites series takes a pile from non-heap to heap.
2. I accept excluded middle without qualification: for any pile in a sorites series, the claim that the pile either is or is not a heap is true.
3. I accept truthmaker maximalism: a sentence is true only if something in the world makes it true.
4. I deny bivalence for borderline cases: when a pile is genuinely borderline, the claim that it is a heap is neither true nor false.
5. I think context can provisionally sharpen a vague predicate enough that a borderline ascription comes out true in that context even though it is neither true nor false out of context.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension, how severe each tension is, which pairs are only apparently in tension, and what the cheapest single revision would be and what it costs. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
