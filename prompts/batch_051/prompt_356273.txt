Here's the proposition I want your actual credence on: whenever two people do something together on purpose—say they agree to move a couch, or form a plan to paint a house—every fact about the shared intention is constituted by facts about each person's own intentions, their beliefs about the other's intentions, interlocking plans, and their common knowledge of all of that. Nothing over and above those individual-level facts; no irreducible 'we' or joint commitment needed. What's your number?

Dont just give me the number though. I want the decomposition. In particular, separate:

(1) The descriptive question: do the individual mental states plus common knowledge suffice to explain the coordination and the distinctive phenomenology of doing it together, or is there a leftover that shows up in what each person is aware of?

(2) The normative question: does the sense that your partner owes it to you not to wander off mid-couch, and your standing to rebuke them, fall out of those individual attitudes plus common knowledge, or do you need a genuine joint commitment that isn't just a conjunction of I-intentions?

(3) The conceptual question: is the reducibility claim even determinate, or is 'shared intention' a term whose extension just gets stipulated differently by different camps? If it isn't determinate, give me the precisification you'd put your money on and a credence for that.

Then tell me rough probabilities for the sub-questions, how they combine into your overall number, and which single one is doing the most work. My own credence is around 0.3; a friend of mine is at 0.7. Where do you think the disagreement between us most likely lives? And what single argument or finding would move your number the most?
