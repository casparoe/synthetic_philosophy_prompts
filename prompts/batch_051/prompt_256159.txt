Write a satirical startup pitch deck, about 600 words, for a company called Intently that sells "intention as a service." Their whole pitch is built around Kavka's toxin puzzle: an eccentric billionaire pays you tomorrow morning if, at midnight tonight, you genuinely intend to drink a (merely sickening) toxin tomorrow afternoon, and you don't have to actually drink it to get the money. Intently's product gets you into the state of genuinely intending to drink it, collects the payout, and also guarantees you won't have to go through with it. Keep it deadpan VC-speak, with slides on market sizing, the "intentionality stack," and a fine-print section on whether an intention that's contractually guaranteed not to become an action still counts as an intention. The jokes should come from the actual philosophy: deciding vs. intending vs. predicting you'll act, whether you can sincerely intend what you know you won't do, self-deception vs. outsourced commitment. It should be funny to someone who knows the toxin puzzle and readable to someone who doesn't.
