Please give me a working definition of "excellence" — the kind of excellence at issue when people ask whether it is good to be excellent even if no one benefits — and test it against the following cases: a surgeon who reliably performs nearly miraculous, life-saving operations; a pianist of extraordinary technique who plays only alone in a locked room and is never heard, recorded, or known; a naturally gifted student who does no work and easily passes but never produces anything outstanding; a con artist who is exceptionally skilled at reading people and winning their trust; a world-record holder in an obscure sport with only a handful of serious competitors; a diligent but untalented craftsperson who tries hard and consistently produces mediocre work; and a person who, through immense preparation, performs a single astonishing feat and never does anything comparable again. I am fairly confident the surgeon counts as excellent and the diligent but mediocre craftsperson does not, and I cannot settle the rest. Propose the definition, run it through all seven cases, say which of your classifications you are least confident about, and revise or defend the definition where the cases push back.
