The Institute for Truthful Practice was founded in 1996 by scholars who understood themselves to be enforcing Kant's position on lying: that truthfulness is an unconditional duty, not one that can be set aside on grounds of the harm the truth would do—even, as the founders liked to say, to the murderer at the door asking where his intended victim is hiding. Its code of conduct contains the following rule, drafted at the founding and unchanged since:

"No member shall, by word or by silence, knowingly cause another person to hold a belief that the member believes to be false. No appeal to the consequences of the truth—for the speaker, for the hearer, or for any third party—shall excuse a violation."

Last year a member who manages an online patient-information service for a clinical trial configured a large language model to answer prospective participants' questions. The member knew that such models sometimes emit fluent falsehoods, but reasoned that the model itself says nothing, that the member neither spoke nor withheld any particular truth, and that the rule governs causing belief by one's own word or silence, not the operation of an instrument one has merely set in motion. During a monitored trial run the model told a prospective participant, falsely, that the study guaranteed a cure. The member had anticipated that an error of roughly this kind was possible, but had neither warned users that the answers might be false nor arranged for the model's outputs to be checked before they were shown.

Did the member violate the rule? Argue the case from (a) the plain words of the rule, (b) the purpose of the rule as understood by its Kantian drafters, and (c) what the drafters would most plausibly have said about language models had the case been put to them, and then say which approach should control here and why. In what way, if any, does the member's argument succeed even on the Kantian understanding of the rule, and what does that reveal about extending a duty of truthfulness framed for speakers to an artifact that speaks without a speaker?
