I have been reading, in a scattered and unsystematic way, about how societies come to abandon long-standing practices such as slavery, dueling, foot-binding, trial by ordeal, and public executions, and the same unsatisfying pattern keeps reappearing. One family of explanations says these practices end because people are gradually persuaded by moral and humane arguments; on this view, the stated reasons of reformers are the real motors of change. A second family says that is naive and that the arguments are mostly a story told after the fact: the practices persist exactly as long as they serve the interests of powerful groups, and they collapse only when the material or political incentives shift. A third family says both are missing the mechanism, because many of these practices are held in place not by conviction or profit but by a code of honor; such codes can reverse quickly, so that a practice goes from a point of pride to a source of shame without anyone having been argued into a new belief and without the balance sheet having changed.

The dispute between these three positions has apparently been running for a long time, and each side can produce its own favorite examples but never dislodges the others. I am less interested in being told which side is right than in understanding why the disagreement is so sticky. My own suspicion is that the three sides are not simply offering rival causes of the very same thing: the word because seems to be doing different work when one says a practice ended because of an argument, because of an interest, or because of a shift in its honor. But I cannot tell whether that makes the disagreement verbal, substantive, or something in between, such as a difference in which cases count as data or in what each side would accept as evidence that the practice ended for one kind of reason rather than another. Could you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, and say what would count as genuine progress given that diagnosis, or explain why the impasse is built into the question?
