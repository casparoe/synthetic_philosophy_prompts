I am a high school student who has been reading Kant, and I came across this passage in his essay "Idea for a Universal History from a Cosmopolitan Point of View" (1784), in Lewis White Beck's translation: "All natural capacities of a creature are destined to evolve completely to their natural end." A friend then built the following argument on it, and I find the conclusion absurd but cannot find the faulty step. (1) If a creature has a natural capacity, nature has destined that capacity to develop completely. (2) Human beings have natural capacities for reason and for conflict, since both appear in every known society. (3) Whatever nature has destined to develop completely belongs to nature's purpose for that creature. (4) Therefore conflict, cruelty, and war belong to nature's purpose for humanity just as much as reason does. (5) What belongs to nature's purpose should in some sense be affirmed as part of the completed end of the species. (6) Therefore the wars and atrocities of history should in some sense be affirmed. I would much rather keep the denial of (6) than keep (1), but I need to know which premise to reject and what rejecting it costs. Where does this argument go wrong, if it does? Answer as a numbered list with exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
