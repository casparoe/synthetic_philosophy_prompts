W. E. B. Du Bois defended the view that art is never politically innocent — that every artwork already does persuasive political work — and that Black artists should stop pretending otherwise and deliberately direct that work toward racial uplift. Suppose a private foundation institutionalizes this as a funding rule: every applicant submits (1) a sample of the proposed work and (2) a public statement of the specific political effect the finished work is intended to have; a jury scores the application 50% on craft and 50% on the demonstrable fit between sample and stated effect, and the statements are kept in a searchable public archive. Analyze this as a mechanism, not by asking whether Du Bois was right. What does it select for, in artists and in projects, over successive funding rounds? In particular, how does it treat a formally ambitious work whose likely effect is diffuse and delayed against a mediocre work with a legible, easily scored effect? How will applicants game the statement scoring, and how will the jury's behavior change once archived statements become a genre with conventions of their own? What second-order effects follow for the surrounding art world, including artists who reject Du Bois's premise? Then say which failure modes can be fixed without abandoning the Du Boisian core, and which are the core working as intended.
