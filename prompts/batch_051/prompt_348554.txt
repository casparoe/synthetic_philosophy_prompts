Just got back from a long argument with a friend about consciousness and he backed me into a corner; I need someone to find the exit. Here's the argument as best I can reconstruct it. (1) The meta-problem is just the question why we find it so overwhelmingly intuitive that there's a hard problem of consciousness — why conscious experience feels like it couldn't be physical. (2) There's a deflationary answer: introspection gives us a stripped-down model of our own states with no information about the underlying mechanisms, and we systematicly mistake that missing mechanistic detail for an ineffable, non-physical quality. (3) General principle: if an intuition is fully explained by a process that doesn't track the truth of what the intuition says, then the intuition gives no reason to believe what it says. (4) The process in (2) doesn't track whether consciousness is actually non-physical, so by (3), our dualist-leaning intuitions about consciousness give no reason to think anything non-physical is going on. (5) But (3) is general. Every intuition we have — logical, mathematical, inductive — is produced by some process, and for any one of them you can retell the story so the process doesn't track the relvant truth. So (3) debunks every intuition, including the intuition that (3) itself is a good principle, and that looks insane. I want to keep (1) and most of (2), and I don't see a principled place to stop the slide from (4) to (5). Which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
