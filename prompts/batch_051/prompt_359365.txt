For an essay in my biology class on what it means to be alive, I have written down the following commitments, and I would like to check whether they are jointly tenable before I build my thesis around them. The commitments are: (1) viruses are not alive; (2) being alive is a matter of degree rather than a sharp all-or-nothing property; (3) the capacity to take part in evolution by natural selection is a necessary condition for being alive; (4) a sterile organism such as a mule is nonetheless alive; (5) for any given entity, there is a fact of the matter about whether it is alive. I genuinely do not know where, if anywhere, the problem lies. Please identify which of these commitments are in real tension and which only appear to be, rank the tensions by severity, and tell me which single revision would do the most to make the set coherent and what that revision would cost.
