Here's one. I want you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration inside an AI lab's safety-and-alignment team. The two camps have spent months arguing past each other about whether prosaic alignment — the current toolkit of RLHF, Constitutional AI, red-teaming, supervised fine-tuning, and aggressive eval suites — can ever produce a system that is aligned in the sense that matters, or only a system that looks aligned on the tests it was trained and red-teamed against. Camp A, the behavioral camp, says that for current systems, alignment just is robust, corrigible, helpful behavior under adversarial testing that includes held-out red teams and distribution shift; they think demands for evidence about what the model is 'really trying to do' inside its weights are either unfalsifiable or a recipe for never shipping. Camp B, the internals camp, says eval-passing is only an outer shell: a model can be sycophantic, selectively honest, or optimized against the eval score rather than the trait the eval is a proxy for, and without mechanistic or interpretability evidence about why and when the behavior generalizes, acing the suite is weak evidence for deployment. The board has ordered them to produce a single document that both camps have to sign before a deployment decision.

Write that document. Use this structure: (1) a numbered list of claims both camps can sign, as strong and nontrivial as you can make them — not 'both sides care about safety'; (2) a disagreements table, one row per residual disagreement, where column A states the behavioral camp's view in wording they'd accept, column B states the internals camp's view in wording they'd accept, and a third column says whether the disagreement is substantive or partly verbal (and if verbal, whats left once you cash out the terms); (3) a crux list: for each crux, specify the experiment, metric, or failure case both camps agree would bear on it, and record each side's prediction in advance so the disagreement can be scored later. Don't declare a winner — the document is theirs to sign, so if a formulation would make one side walk out, rework it. The jointly signed claims should also be operational enough that someone deciding whether to ship can actually lean on them.
