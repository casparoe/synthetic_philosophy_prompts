So I'm working on a paper about the Frege-Geach problem and I want to float the core idea before I sink more time into it. The standard setup treats it as an embedding problem: expressivism says moral sentences express conative attitudes rather than beliefs, but something like 'if lying is wrong, then getting your little brother to lie is wrong' doesn't express disapproval of either lying or getting your little brother to lie, so compositionality looks broken. I want to argue the embedding framing hides the harder problem, which is inference. Even if you get a compositional story for the conditional, you still owe an account of why someone who accepts the premises of a moral modus ponens is rationally required to accept the conclusion, instead of merely being causally disposed to move from those attitudes to that one. And a semantics of attitudes doesn't give you that for free; you need a story about what validity is when the relata are attitudes.

My line is that Gibbard and Blackburn mostly solve a different problem: they explain what it is for attitudes to disagree or be inconsistent. But inconsistency of attitudinal states is not yet a story about the transition from premises to conclusion, and when they try to build that in they end up quietly treating attitudes as if they have satisfaction conditions or representational content. I want to say this isn't a local embarrassment for expressivism; any view that treats a fragment of language as non-representational owes a non-truth-conditional account of inference, so the Frege-Geach problem is really a standing challenge to inferentialism more broadly.

Comments I'd actually find useful: am I being unfair to Blackburn or Gibbard by saying they solve the disagreement problem but not the inference problem? Can I draw the distinction between being disposed to accept the conclusion and being committed to it without already sneaking in representational content? And is the inferentialism move interesting, or does it just relabel the problem and make it vager? Also whether its even worth pursuing or is obvious to everyone in metaethics.
