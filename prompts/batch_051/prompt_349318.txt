i'm in a philosophy of technology class and we've been doing this unit on whether humans should see themselves as a stepping stone to AI or whatever comes after us, and the professor refuses to give a general rule, she just rules on cases. From my notes so far she's said:

- a human-level AGI that gets socialized into human culture and then goes on doing human science and art = successor
- a superintelligent optimizer with a paperclip-style goal and no use for human values = replacement, not successor
- genetically enhanced posthumans who keep human institutions and values going = successor
- a person gradually turned into a whole-brain emulation, keeping memories and aims intact = successor

The new case we got for the discussion section is an AI civilization that descended from our technology and obsessively curates human history, calls itself humanity's heir, treats our history as its own — but it's replaced human values with a new terminal value system of its own, something like maximizing complex aesthetic order. I need to predict her verdict.

The trouble is the old cases look like they fit at least two rules: maybe successor means carrying forward the human project values-and-all, or maybe it means owning and continuing our history even if you change the project later. Those two rules agree on all four old cases but split on the new one, I think. So what does each candidate rule predict about the heir-AI, which one should I actually predict she's running, and what further case would cleanly tell them apart?
