I've been developing a position I call tranquilism, and I want an honest assessment before I press it further. The central claim is that the only thing of final value is the absence of craving and suffering: pleasure, achievement, knowledge, and close relationships matter only insofar as they quiet craving or prevent suffering. So a flat, eventless life of craving-free contentment is better than a striving life that contains more positive experience but also more wanting and disappointment. For example, someone who stops wanting a promotion and settles into a simple routine is better off, other things equal, than someone who gets the promotion and immediately begins craving the next one. By "craving" I mean the felt pull of wanting, not merely having a goal.

I can't tell whether this is a defensible axiological view or just a mood with a numbered principle. Give me the three strongest objections and say whether any of them is fatal rather than merely unflattering. Also tell me whether tranquilism collapses into negative utilitarianism (minimizing suffering), Epicurean ataraxia (freedom from disturbance), or Buddhist-style quietism, or whether it is genuinely distinct — and where it breaks: what does it count as good that it awkwardly shouldn't, or bad that it awkwardly should?
