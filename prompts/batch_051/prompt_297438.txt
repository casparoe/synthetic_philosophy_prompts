I've been turning something over since last weekend, and I'd rather have a careful analysis than a take, because my sister is a philosophy PhD student and she's already claimed victory. She told me I have a "moral testimony problem." Here's the background. About two years ago I became convinced that buying fast fashion is wrong. I can reproduce the whole case from memory: the suppressed wages, the water use, the microplastics, the clothes piling up in landfills, the way the ads manufacture urgency so you don't ask questions. But every single one of those arguments came from her—she sent me the readings, corrected my misunderstandings, told me which parts of the documentaries to pay attention to. And now I genuinely get angry when I see a haul video, and I've stopped buying the stuff. The catch: if she called me tomorrow and said she'd reconsidered and the whole critique was overblown, I honestly don't know weather I'd hold the line or fold.

Here are the criteria I've been using for what it takes to actually hold a moral view, as opposed to borrowing one from someone else:
(a) you can state the view and give reasons for it;
(b) the view moves you—it shows up in your feelings and your behavior;
(c) you can revise the view when new evidence or arguments come in; and
(d) the view is yours—you didn't just adopt it on authority or out of social pressure.

Now the borderline case is me. By my own list: (a) passes easily. (b) mostly passes, though I can't tell whether my anger is really mine or just me rehearsing her anger. (c) is shaky because the person I'd trust to tell me the supply chain has changed is, again, my sister. And (d) is an obvious failure—the origin of every reason I have is her testimony.

So do I actually hold the view that fast fashion is wrong, in any sense that matters? Or am I just an unusually fluent mouthpiece for her? If I do count, does criterion (d) need to be repaired or dropped? And if I don't count, what exactly am I missing, given that I already have the reasons and the feelings?
