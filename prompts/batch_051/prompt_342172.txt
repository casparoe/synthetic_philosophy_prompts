Please make a rigorous argument for the following position:

The most instructive reading of Sen's liberal paradox is neither that minimal liberty genuinely conflicts with the Pareto principle nor that the Pareto principle must simply be weakened. It is that rights should not be modeled as decisiveness — that is, as an individual's power to determine how society ranks a protected pair of social states. Once rights are represented instead as constraints on admissible outcomes (or equivalently for present purposes, as individual choices over personal actions rather than as preferences over complete social states), the apparent cycle is a diagnostic artifact of forcing rights into a preference-aggregation framework. The paradox is therefore best understood as an argument about the correct formal representation of rights, not as a demonstration of a substantive conflict between liberal and welfarist commitments.
