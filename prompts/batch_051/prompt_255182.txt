I find myself drawn to lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: the view that there is some level of suffering above which no amount of pleasure can compensate, while below that level pleasure and pain remain commensurable in the ordinary way. The view attracts me because it is structurally clean, it gives a definite answer in trade-off cases, and it captures a conviction I am not willing to surrender: that no quantity of happiness elsewhere can make a world containing torture acceptable. But I also hold, with equal firmness, that the badness of pain is continuous in intensity. A barely perceptible worsening of a pain cannot plausibly change it from a harm that enough pleasure could outweigh into a harm that no pleasure could outweigh. Yet the threshold seems to require exactly such a discontinuity, and any particular cutoff looks arbitrary: why there rather than slightly lower or slightly higher? I want to know which of these I should give up, and whether there is a third reading on which the theory and the intuition are compatible. Present the strongest version of each side, including the case for debunking the continuity intuition, the case for amending or abandoning the lexical threshold, and the case for reconciliation, and say what you would do.
