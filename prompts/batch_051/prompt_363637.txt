I've been chewing on a homemade idea I call the Chance Rule, and I want an honest take on it. Here it is: if an event has a genuine objective chance—the kind that's really out there in the world, like a fair coin's 50/50 or a lottery's one-in-ten-million—then once you know that number, your confidence should just be that number. Full stop. Your hunches, your past experience, even evidence about what actually ended up happening shouldn't move you, because the chance is what the world says the liklihood was, and everything else is a different kind of fact.

I know this sounds crazy in the bad cases. Suppose I flip a fair coin, and a friend I'd normally trust completely sees it land heads and tells me so. My rule still says I should be 50/50 on heads. Same if a reliable crystal ball showed me heads in advance. I actually think that's right: the chance was 1/2, and my friend's report (or the crystal ball) is evidence about the outcome, not about the chance. Mixing those two up is the mistake, not the rule.

So: does this already exist under a name? Is it wrong, and if so, where exactly does it break? And is there a patched version that keeps the core idea—that objective chances should rule your beliefs—without the crazy bits, or should I drop the whole thing?
