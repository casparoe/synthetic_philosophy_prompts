I have spent years reading rationalist forums, and I keep noticing that the word intelligence gets cashed out very differently depending on what problem the writer is trying to solve. I am compiling a taxonomy of the genuinely different kinds of proposal about what intelligence is, pitched one level of abstraction above the details. So far I have:

- Spearman's g: intelligence as the common factor that explains the positive manifold of correlations across cognitive test batteries.
- The Cattell–Horn–Carroll model: intelligence as a stratified set of broad abilities, such as fluid and crystallised reasoning, organised under a general factor.
- Gardner's theory of multiple intelligences: a rejection of a single general factor in favour of several relatively independent modalities.
- Sternberg's triarchic theory: a division into analytical, creative, and practical abilities.
- Turing's imitation game: an operational criterion on which a system counts as intelligent if its conversational behaviour is indistinguishable from a human's.
- Legg and Hutter's universal intelligence: expected performance across environments weighted by a Solomonoff-style prior over computable environments.
- The optimisation-power view associated with LessWrong and Yudkowsky: intelligence as efficient cross-domain optimisation, the ability to steer future outcomes toward a goal.

Give me one more proposal about the nature of intelligence that is not already on this list under another name, and that is not merely a special case or hybrid of two or more items on it. To make it harder, I am not looking for another factor, faculty, operational test, or formal measure. Say what the proposal is, what it captures that the listed accounts do not, and whether it is any good. Please write the answer in British English.
