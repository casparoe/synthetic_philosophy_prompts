Question: What, if anything, does the relativity of simultaneity in special relativity tell us about whether past, present, and future are equally real? Reconstruct the central argument (often associated with Rietdijk and Putnam) and assess what it establishes.

Student's answer: Special relativity tells us that simultaneity is frame-relative. If two events are spacelike separated, whether they are simultaneous depends on the inertial frame, and no experiment can isolate an absolute frame. Rietdijk and Putnam turned this against the idea of a cosmic "now." Consider two observers in relative motion who pass near one another: relative to one observer, a distant event already belongs to the past, while relative to the other it still belongs to the future. If reality were an observer-independent matter, these two verdicts could not both be true; yet physics provides no privileged frame with which to settle the dispute. It follows that reality cannot be confined to a single present. The only remaining option is the block universe, in which past, present, and future events all exist equally within a four-dimensional manifold; indeed, relativity's representation of events in a unified spacetime already pictures them that way. The felt flow of time is therefore an illusion. It arises because a conscious observer advances along a worldline and passes through the successive three-dimensional slices of the block.
