I hang out in econ-adjacent rationalist spaces, and there's this one move that keeps ending arguments before they start: someone says "unrealistic assumptions don't matter," maybe with a vague nod to Friedman or the phrase "F-twist," and then acts like anyone who criticizes a model's assumptions just doesn't get how modeling works. Most recently it came up in a thread about whether a labor-market model with zero transaction costs, no search frictions, and perfectly informed workers tells us anything about actual labor markets. The person's move was: criticizing assumptions is pointless by definition; the only thing that can count against a model is failed predictions. I'm pretty sure that one-liner packs a bunch of distinct claims, from obviously true to pretty radical, and I want a menu I can actually use the next time someone invokes it. Lay out the different precise theses "unrealistic assumptions don't matter" could amount to, and for each say whether it's a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or just false. Then say which reading(s) actually license the specific move from that thread — that assumptions are never legitimate targets of criticism and only predictive success matters. I suspect "assumption" is papering over several different things (approximately true simplifying idealizations, wildly false stylized facts, known-false tractability devices, auxiliary claims about the target system), and "don't matter" might mean don't matter for prediction, for explanation, for understanding mechanisms, or for policy. I dont know if those are the right cuts; feel free to redraw them.
