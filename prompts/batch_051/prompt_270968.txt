I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about why randomized controlled trials keep producing ethical fights — same protocol, different IRBs, different countries, totally different rulings. I just spent a week talking to an oncologist who runs adaptive platform trials and to a patient advocate who walked away from a trial because he felt like a data point. The oncologist kept telling me individual uncertainty is basically a fiction and what matters is whether the expert community is genuinely divided; the advocate kept saying a doctor who can't tell you which arm she'd want for her own kid shouldn't be allowed to randomize you at all.

Can you build me a sorting instrument for this debate — a decision tree or a short quiz with a scoring key — that takes a reader from a few concrete gut responses about trial scenarios to a named ethical position on when randomization is acceptable? The angle that matters for my piece is the divide between positions that rest the ethics on what's best for the enrolled patient and positions that rest it on what the medical community or future patients get out of the trial; but I suspect other distinctions (uncertainty, consent, scarcity/fairness) cut across that, and I'd like the tree to surface those rather than shove everything into my one axis. Please include as leaves at least the main recognizable options: something like personal equipoise or the uncertainty principle, Freedman-style clinical equipoise, the view that informed consent plus scientific soundness is enough and equipoise is a red herring, and the view that randomization in clinical care is never or almost never justified. Work through at least three scenarios I keep hearing about — a placebo-controlled trial for a condition that has an existing but lousy treatment, a sham-surgery trial for a procedure that's become common but unproven, and an adaptive platform trial where allocation ratios shift as data accrue. Phrase each branch node as a concrete case or a yes/no claim a non-philosopher can answer without already knowing which position it leads to. If two positions can't be seperated by any question you can think of, flag that rather than pretending they can. And I'd like you to commit: give me one definite tree with definite questions in a definite order, and tell me why those splits rather than some other set — not a menu of possible taxonomies.
