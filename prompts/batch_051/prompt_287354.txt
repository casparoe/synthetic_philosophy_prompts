For the past decade I've worked as a journalist covering science and ideas, and my beats have included information theory, machine translation, and the way authority gets attached to records and protocols. My working vocabulary comes from those fields: source message and channel, encoding and decoding, lossless versus lossy compression, checksums and error detection, semantic content versus bitstream, fidelity, authorized copies, and so on. A theologian I've been interviewing keeps pulling me back to a single question: can a translated text be inspired? Behind that question sit several distinct positions I want to keep straight. There is the ancient tradition that the Septuagint, the Greek rendering of the Hebrew scriptures, was produced under divine supervision and therefore carries its own authority rather than being merely a derivative text. There is the family of positions associated with King James Only movements, which treat a particular English translation as the divinely preserved or inspired form of the text for English speakers, not just one reliable translation among others. And there is the more minimal view that inspiration attaches only to the original autographs — the manuscripts as first written — so that every translation is at best a faithful derivative with no comparable authority.

Restate each of those positions, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually use — source coding, channel conditions, reconstruction at the receiver, lossy compression, checksums, authorized copies, and the like. I want the translation done honestly: don't smuggle theological assumptions into the engineering terms and present that as a translation, and tell me explicitly where my vocabulary forces a distortion of the original claim. Then give me the part I care about most, a residue report. Which claims translate cleanly? Which translate only if you stretch a term, and how? Which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? End with your verdict on what the residue means: is it a defect in my conceptual toolkit, or a sign that the theological claims are doing different work — or are less coherent — than they present themselves as doing? Throughout, flag the mapping or residue judgment you are least confident about, and say what consideration — a better reading of one of the positions, a different case, another vocabulary — would most likely change your overall assessment.
