I'm a junior and I'm the parliamentarian for my high school's student council, which basically means I'm the one who has to read our constitution out loud whenever there's a rules fight. Our student council constitution (written in 2019) has this clause:

'The Student Council is the instrument of the student body. Every question of general student concern shall therefore be settled by a direct vote of the whole student body, and the Council shall carry out the result of that vote without adding to it, taking away from it, or delaying it.'

Last month there was a vote on whether the school store should stop selling energy drinks before the end of the school day. It passed 67 to 33, but turnout was only about 55 percent. Afterwards we found out that the week before the vote, someone taped up a fake flyer with the school store's logo saying the store was about to start charging a $2 'caffeine fee' on every drink, and alot of yes voters are now saying they only voted yes because they thought they were voting against that fee, which was never real. There's also a rumor about double voting because the poll was just a google form, but nobody can prove it. The council is split: half says the clause is clear, the students voted, so we have to enact the ban or we'd be overruling the student body. The other half says this wasnt really 'the student body deciding' because people voted on a false picture of the world.

I have to give a ruling next week. My government teacher said this is basically the argument people have about populism and "the will of the people", so I want to get the reasoning right. Read the clause the way a court would read a law: what does it actually require here? I care less about the practical answer and more about what makes a vote count as the will of the students under a clause like this. Go through the text, the purpose of the clause, what the students who wrote it in 2019 would probably have said if someone had asked them about fake flyers and double voting, and what a reasonable student reading it today would expect. Which approach should win? And can a council ever say a vote was too misinformed to bind them without turning into a little elite that overrules the majority, and if there's a line, where is it?
