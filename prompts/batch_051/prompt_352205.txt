My friend who works at an anti-corruption NGO explained the field to me like this: "Corruption is the abuse of public office for private gain, and that standard is universal. So clientelism, vote-buying, and gift-giving to officials are all basically the same kind of thing wherever they happen, and the scholars who treat some of these as legitimate gifts or local norms are really just rationalizing corruption because they don't want to impose Western standards." I know she's condensing a big literature into a few sentences, but I can't tell where the gloss turns from roughly right to actually misleading. How fair is that summary as an account of how work on corruption and clientelism handles the gift/bribe distinction and the universalism debate? I'd like the accurate parts separated from the overstatements, and a sense of which bits reflect consensus, which are contested, and what the standard definition does and doesn't actually establish.
