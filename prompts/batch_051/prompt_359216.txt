i've been stuck on a kind of heideggerian view of tool use and i need someone to help me sort out whether the theory or the intuition has to give. the theory says our primary way of being in the world is practical absorption: when i'm hammering or typing, the tool isn't an object with properties that i'm representing, it practically withdraws into the task. the present-at-hand thing—a physical object with weight, materials, a mechanism—only shows up as such when the equipment breaks down. the systematic appeal is real: it explains skilled coping and expertise with a single basic relation, and it gives breakdown a deep epistemological role instead of treating it as a mere lapse of attention.

but i can't shake the intuition that this gets the ontology backwards. when the key sticks or the hammer head loosens, the properties i finally notice—the friction, the grain, the crack—don't seem to have come into being at the moment of breakdown. they were there all along, doing causal work, and in fact they're part of why the activity went smoothly until it didn't. so i want to say readiness-to-hand is a claim about where my attention lives, not a claim about what exists or what's fundamental; the world is full of determinate present-at-hand things the whole time, and breakdown merely reveals them.

so my question is whether the heideggerian is actually committed to denying that, or whether calling the present-at-hand derivative is doing a subtler job than i think. if the theory really does say the present-at-hand is constituted by breakdown, what would it take for the theory to go—can i keep the insight about absorption and just drop the metaphysical primacy claim? and on the other side, what would it take for my intuition to go—am i confusing causal independence with fundamentality, or treating introspection about a broken tool as evidence about how things were before i looked? give me the strongest version of each and tell me which one you'd actually give up, or whether there's a reading where both survive and the only thing that has to go is the metaphor of the object being conjured into existence by the breakdown.
