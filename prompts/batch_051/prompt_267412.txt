I'm a retired field ecologist — spent most of my career on social insects, so Darwin's discussion of sterile castes in the Origin always felt close to home — and I've recently gotten into philosophy through a reading group with some old colleagues. We've been working through the Descent of Man, and the convenor asked each of us to write up where we've landed on Darwin's treatment of morality and religion so the rest of the group can attack it. I'd like you to do that attack first, as a professional philosopher who knows Darwin's ethics well. Please think carefully, and maybe start by free-associating about how all my commitments interact before you write the actual audit.

Here is where I've landed:
(1) The moral sense — conscience, guilt, sympathy — is a product of natural selection, assembled from social instincts that were selected because groups with more dependable cooperators outlasted groups without them. That's basically Darwin's own story in the Descent.
(2) Because of (1), the content of our moral convictions is explained by what promoted fitness in ancestral social environments, not by tracking any independent moral facts. So I lean strongly toward anti-realism about morality, though I'm not a full-blown nihilist; I just don't believe there are mind-independent moral truths.
(3) Religious belief is the same phenomenon: once you have social instincts plus our overeager tendency to attribute agency and intention, you get spirits, gods, and moralized religion. So the near-universality of religious belief needs no supernatural object to explain it.
(4) Even so, I don't think Darwin's caution about a first cause was special pleading. He held that the question was beyond the reach of human intellect, and that still seems right to me: agnostic about a deistic first cause, atheist about personal gods.
(5) I'm a scientific realist through and through. Natural selection is a real process, science works, and our ordinary and scientific cognitive faculties are broadly reliable, especially once we add corrective machinery like experiments, peer review, and instruments.

Does this set hang together? I can't tell where the real stress points are — every pair looks fine to me, wich is probably a bad sign. I'd like you to go through the commitments and say which ones are genuinely in tension, ranked by severity, which tensions are only apparent and why they dissolve, and for each genuine tension what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs. Don't just tell me everything is fine, and don't tell me I have to abandon the whole thing; I need the sorting.
