For a workshop on knowledge, stakes, and practical reasoning, I have been asked to write down my current commitments so the other participants can audit whether they hang together. I would like to keep all of the following, and I honestly cannot tell where the real strain lies. Here is the setup, stated as precisely as I can:

1. Contextualism about knowledge ascriptions. Whether a claim of the form S knows that p is true depends on the ascriber's conversational context, and in particular on which alternative possibilities are being treated as live and relevant in that context.

2. Univocality. The word knows has a single semantic meaning, and there is no separate practical sense of knows alongside the epistemic sense.

3. Pragmatic encroachment. Whether a subject genuinely knows that p can depend on what is practically at stake for the subject, even when the subject's evidence, reliability, and belief are held fixed. I have in mind cases like the following: two people have the same evidence about whether a bank is open on Saturday; one has almost nothing riding on the answer, while the other needs to deposit a check before Monday to avoid a serious penalty. I want to say that the first can know while the second, despite the same evidence, may fail to know, and that this difference is explained by the difference in stakes rather than by anything about what an ascriber happens to be discussing.

4. The knowledge norm of practical reasoning. One may properly rely on p in practical deliberation only if one knows p; and if one knows p, one may properly rely on p in practical deliberation even when the stakes are high.

5. Fallibilism. Knowing is compatible with there being uneliminated error possibilities, provided those possibilities are not relevant in the circumstances. Knowledge does not require epistemic certainty.

Please audit this set rather than lecturing me on the topics. For every pair of commitments that is in genuine tension, describe the nature of the tension, rank the tensions in order of severity, and say what would have to be revised or given up in each case and at what cost. Then identify any pair that merely appears to be in tension and explain why the appearance is misleading. Conclude by stating which single revision to the list would do the most to restore coherence, and what that revision would sacrifice.
