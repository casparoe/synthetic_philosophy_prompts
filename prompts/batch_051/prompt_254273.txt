Alright, so I've got something that keeps bugging me and I can't tell if it's a known killer objection or just my own confusion. I've been trying to build priors for some small forecasting models at work, and the folk rule is: when you know almost nothing about an unknown quantity, spread your probability as evenly as possible. Fine. Suppose an unknown rate r has to lie between 0 and 1. The natural move is a flat distribution over r. Okay. But suppose I'd described the same unknown by its log-odds instead, the log of r/(1-r), which runs from minus infinity to plus infinity. Being "even-handed" about that variable gives, after I transform back, a distribution over r that piles up hard near 0 and 1. So two people both following the same "least committed possible" principle end up with incompatible priors, purely because one of them happened to work in probabilities and the other in log-odds. And I don't see what makes r more "natural" than log-odds, or odds, or any other monotone transformation.

The obvious patch is to say: don't spread evenly by hand, just pick the distribution with maximum entropy subject to whatever constraints you actually have. But that seems to just push the problem back. Differential entropy isn't invariant under stretching or compressing the axis, so maximizing it in r versus in log r can give different answers, and a choice about which scale to treat as flat is still lurking in the background. The choice of which constraints to encode matters too: given the same loose background information, constraining the mean hands me one curve and constraining, say, the mean of the log hands me another, and it's not obvious which of these is "the" information I had.

My question: is there a name for this specific problem, and is it the same thing or a seperate thing from the old worry that "equally likely" breaks down when there are infinitely many possibilities (I half-remember something about Bertrand and circles)? More than the name, I want the carving: do objective Bayesians have a principled reason to treat one parameterization as the privileged one to be flat over, or to regard some constraints rather than others as the right ones to feed into maximum entropy? And if the answer is ultimately "you just have to pick and be consistent," then what, if anything, is still objective about objective priors?
