My friend and I were argueing till 2 a.m. about whether our weekly bar trivia team is a waste of time. He's the instrumentalist: it's only worth doing because it keeps friendships alive, gives our brains a workout, and makes the workweek bearable; if it stopped doing those jobs, we'd be irrational to keep going. I'm the autotelic side: the game is worth doing for its own sake, and the friendship/cognitive/restorative stuff is a welcome byproduct, not the ground. We both know a bit of Huizinga and Pieper, and they kept getting dragged in: he says Huizinga's point that play is disinterested just means it stands outside ordinary life and isn't aimed at material gain, not that it has non-instrumental value; I say Pieper's idea that leisure is a receptive state of soul that isn't justified by work or utility is exactly what he's missing. If we'd run this as a proper adversarial collaboration, what's the document we'd have ended up with? I want a numbered list of claims both sides could actually sign (about what play and leisure are, about what for-its-own-sake value would have to mean, and about which instrumental benefits are real); then a seperate table of the exact residual disagreements, with each side's view worded so that each of us would say yes, that's what I mean; then the cruxes, each with a concrete case or test we'd both agree bears on it and what each side predicts. I suspect one crux is whether the fun of the game counts as part of its being worth doing for its own sake or as just another instrumental payoff, and another is how each of us treats a case where the friendship and stress-relief clearly stop. The point isn't to settle who's right, it's to find the non-vacuous common ground and make the rest of the disagreement exact.
