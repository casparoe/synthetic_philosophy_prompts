At a family lunch, two of my cousins got into an argument about where experience actually happens. One said there must be a place in the brain where everything comes together: sight, sound, and feeling arrive as separate streams, so the fact that we enjoy a single, seamless scene means the brain must assemble them on some kind of inner stage before we become aware of it. The other called this the theatre-in-the-head mistake. If there really were a screen inside the skull, something would have to watch it, and then that something would need its own screen to watch, and so on. Unity, he said, is not a place but an after-the-fact story the brain tells about what it has already done.

His cousin pushed back. She said she was not imagining a little person in the cinema; she only meant there must be some neural stage at which the separate bits get bound into the experience we can report. Without a point where it is all together, why would we report one coherent scene rather than a jumble of disconnected signals? He answered that the coherence could live in the report itself: saying 'I see a red ball' is one act, and there need be no single place, before the report, where red and ball are already united. The unity is in what the system can say and do, not in a hidden inner screening.

She came back that experiments exist in which a change is made just before someone reports what they saw, and the person reports only one version, as if the brain edited the footage before showing it. Editing, she said, implies at least a final cut, and a final cut implies somewhere it is shown. He replied that editing only means some processes win out over others: there can be many drafts, and the one that gains control of speech is the one we call conscious, with no audience ever watching the finished film. She said that still ducks the hard question, because whoever issues the report is the audience. He said 'whoever' here is the whole system, not a point inside it, and positing such a point explains nothing.

They asked me to referee, and I honestly could not. Please adjudicate the disagreement move by move: say which claims are sound, which are mistaken, what both of them are missing, and who is more right overall.
