Act as a philosopher of cognitive science with deep expertise in predictive processing and the free energy principle. Think through each step in order before giving your diagnosis.

I am a journalist who covers neuroscience and ideas, and I keep encountering a version of the following argument. It seems to prove too much, but I cannot identify the step I should reject.

(1) The free energy principle says that any system with a Markov blanket—a statistical boundary between its internal states and its environment—that maintains a far-from-equilibrium state over time must minimize variational free energy.

(2) This includes organisms, but it also seems to include candle flames, convection cells, and hurricanes.

(3) Variational free energy quantifies the mismatch between the system's internal states and the sensory states it registers, under a probabilistic model of the external causes of those sensory states.

(4) Minimizing that quantity is the same thing as performing Bayesian inference: the system revises its model to fit incoming sensory evidence and acts to bring sensory evidence in line with the model.

(5) If a system is performing Bayesian inference, then it has beliefs about the causes of its sensory states, in at least a minimal sense.

(6) Therefore, candle flames and hurricanes have beliefs.

(7) But they do not; belief, however minimal, requires something that these systems lack.

Where does this argument go wrong? Which step or steps should I reject, and what does rejecting them cost? I would most like to keep step (4) as a claim about organisms, but I want to know what I am paying for that.
