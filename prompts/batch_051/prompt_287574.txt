I am a student in a philosophy of science course, and we have been working through Goodman's grue example. Let "grue" mean the invented predicate that applies to an object just in case either the object is examined before some fixed future time t and is green, or it is not examined before t and is blue. The familiar puzzle is that, before t, the observed evidence supports "all emeralds are green" and "all emeralds are grue" equally well, yet the first projection feels clearly more reasonable than the second. A classmate has been arguing a specific version of the lesson: that the preference for green over grue is not contributed by the evidence, the environment, or the structure of the world, but is supplied entirely by the learner—by our perceptual similarity spaces, our prior linguistic entrenchment, or both. They think, in other words, that an induction system with no antecedent bias toward green would find the two projections genuinely symmetric and could be trained to project a grue-like predicate as readily as a green-like one.

I would like a rigorous analysis of how one could bring empirical evidence to bear on that claim, since it now looks partly like an empirical claim about learning and partly like a conceptual point about what evidence could ever show. Please propose the strongest concrete test you can. You may design a psycholinguistic experiment with human adults, a developmental study with children, a comparative study with non-human animals, or a learning-model simulation—whichever gives the claim its best chance to be confirmed or disconfirmed. Specify the training histories, the target predicates, and the measured outcome. Then state exactly what result would count in favor of the claim, what result would count against it, and why the two readings are hard to separate. Finally, be explicit about what the test would not settle even if it came out cleanly: in particular, whether a finding that many different learning systems converge on "green" would establish that green is more projectible than grue in a sense independent of the minds tested, or merely that the same bias is widespread. What residue is unreachable by any test because the very design of a prediction task must take some predicates for granted?
