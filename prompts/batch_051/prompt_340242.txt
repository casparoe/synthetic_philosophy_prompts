I'm putting together a short statement of my views on pain for a philosophy-of-mind discussion group made up mostly of friends from the rationalist-blog world, and I'd like someone to audit the position before I defend it in front of them. Here is the set of commitments I've settled into:

(1) I'm a physicalist about pain: pain states are brain states, and their phenomenal character involves no non-physical qualia. (2) I'm a representationalist about phenomenal character: what it's like to be in a pain state is fixed by that state's representational content, and the canonical content of pain is something like bodily damage of such-and-such a kind and severity occurring at such-and-such a location. (3) I think the badness of pain is exhausted by its unpleasantness, and unpleasantness is an evaluative-motivational dimension of the experience — roughly, the way the experience registers the damage as not-to-be-tolerated and urges avoidance — rather than an additional raw feel bolted onto the sensory qualities. (4) I take the standard reports of pain asymbolia at face value: patients say they feel a localised, intense pain with an ordinary sharp or burning or dull quality, yet deny that it is unpleasant or bothersome, and they don't withdraw or protect the affected part; I'm also inclined to call this state genuine pain rather than mere nociception. (5) I take the best explanation of asymbolia to be that the sensory-discriminative and affective-motivational components of pain can come apart, with the latter selectively lost. (6) I regard first-person reports as our best, though fallible, evidence about phenomenal character, even when they make trouble for a neat theory. (7) I'm inclined to think of the affective-motivational component as a matter of the pain's downstream role in attention, motivation, and action, not as an extra layer of representational content.

I can't tell which of these are doing real work and which are quietly pulling against the others. Which pairs genuinely conflict, and which only appear to? For the genuine tensions, rank them by severity and say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs; then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
