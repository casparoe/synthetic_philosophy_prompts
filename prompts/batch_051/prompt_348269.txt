Ive been turning over a parallel and I cant decide if its deep or just pattern-matching. The logic of Chesterton's fence -- dont tear down an institution until you know why it was put there -- seems to me to be exactly the logic good engineers apply to gnarly old code: nobody deletes the deprecated config flag or the test that fails for no visible reason until someone reconstructs what failure mode it was guarding against. And Burkes idea that inherited institutions contain wisdom no single generation can fully articulate sounds like the same thought with the git blame lost. On the code side, a guard either does a job or it doesnt, and there is a determinate fact about why it was added even if nobody remembers it. On the institution side, the reasons seem distributed across many people and partly made by ongoing interpretation -- maybe there never was a single reason. Is that difference fatal to the analogy, or is it exactly the thing both traditions were warning about?
