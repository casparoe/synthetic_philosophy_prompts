This year's final examination in my upper-level political philosophy seminar includes a section on political obligation and civil disobedience, and I need one essay question for it. Please draft a 90-minute question, a model answer of roughly 600 words, and a marking scheme worth 20 marks. The question should make it impossible to pass by reproducing a standard survey of the topic: it must require candidates to keep two questions distinct — whether there is a general, content-independent duty to obey the law, and whether a given act of civil disobedience is justified — and to explain why a justified act of civil disobedience is compatible with affirming a pro tanto (defeasible) duty to obey even the law one judges unjust, so that it need not collapse into the view that there is no duty to obey law at all (philosophical anarchism). It should also push them to give a substantive account of what the willingness to accept legal punishment does, beyond merely signalling sincerity, and to face the objection that the disobedient takes an unfair exemption from rules the rest of us obey. Present the marking scheme as a JSON object with the following fields: a "total_marks" field with value 20; a "must_hit_points" array containing exactly five entries, each with "label" (a short name for the required move), "marks" (how many of the 20 the move is worth), "full_credit_content" (one sentence stating what a top answer must say), and "common_mistake" (one sentence describing the specific error that typically loses these marks); and a "separates_A_from_B" field naming the moves that distinguish an A from a B answer.
