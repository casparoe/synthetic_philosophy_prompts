I write the one-sentence animal fact labels at a zoo — a few hundred a year, the ones screwed to the railing: "ducks lay eggs," "mosquitoes carry malaria," "peacocks have iridescent tails." Every season it's the same fight. Someone says the label is false because only females lay eggs or only a few mosquito species transmit the parasite; someone else says it's fine as a generic, it says something true about the kind even if most members don't do it; and we go in circles. I want a decision proceedure for when a bare-plural animal sentence is OK on a public label and when we have to rephrase ("female ducks lay eggs," "some mosquitoes..."), one a new label writer with no philosophy background can run in five minutes. It should give the questions in order and what counts as a good enough answer, say where the procedure stops with a no, and mark the steps that are really a communication or values call and not a fact. Then run it on "ducks lay eggs," "mosquitoes carry malaria," and "peacocks have iridescent tails," and make sure at least one comes out a way we wouldn't have liked. Say what the procedure can't settle.
