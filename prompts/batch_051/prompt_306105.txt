I've been reading Frederick Douglass's argument that the Constitution is actually anti-slavery and I tried to lay it out like a proof because I want to figure out if I actually buy it. Here's what I got:

(1) If the Constitution really protected slavery, the text would have to say so in a way a court could point to, because you can't enforce a protection the document only hints at.
(2) But the Constitution never says slave or slavery anywhere, and even the clauses proslavery people cite are written in dead legal language about persons, service, and labor, not about owning people.
(3) So as a matter of the text alone, the Constitution doesn't protect slavery; the proslavery reading has to shove slavery into the words retroactively.
(4) Douglass's claim was that his reading is just the plain-meaning reading, and the other side is the one doing the shoving.
(5) Therefore the Constitution is anti-slavery, and that whole it-was-a-compromise-with-slavery view is basically a misreading.

I keep trying to see where it breaks and I cant tell whether the slide is from not saying slave to not protecting slavery, or whether step (1) is the controversial one that does all the work. Which step, if any, actually fails? If none of them fail, am I allowed to reject the conclusion just because it feels wrong given that the founders clearly did compromise on slavery?
