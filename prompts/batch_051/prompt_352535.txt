I'm a novelist, about a third into a draft set in Florence in the 1300s, and I keep getting stuck on the same thing. My characters are merchants, notaries, a monk, and I honestly can't decide wether they should be speaking Latin or Italian — or whether that's even a real question, since the Italian they'd speak doesn't quite exist yet and Latin still runs everything official. Every time I bring it up, somone rolls out that quip, "A language is a dialect with an army and navy," and acts like that settles it. I'm sure it doesn't, but I can't say exactly what it does settle. Can you pull apart the distinct precise claims that line could be making — about mutual intelligibility, about standardization, about who holds political power, about naming and prestige — and for each tell me whether it's trivially true, substantive and defensible, or just false, and say which of those readings would actually answer the question of when Latin stopped being Latin and started being Italian? I'd like it as exactly five numbered readings, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
