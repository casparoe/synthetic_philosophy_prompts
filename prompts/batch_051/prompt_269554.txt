I serve on my country's Conscientious Objection Review Board. A career noncommissioned officer has petitioned for discharge as a conscientious objector. He does not oppose war in general; he objects to participating in the war his country is currently fighting because he believes that war is unjust. Under our regulations, conscientious objector status requires opposition to war in any form, so his petition would ordinarily be denied. His counsel argues that the moral equality of combatants nevertheless supports his claim: if ordinary soldiers on both sides of a war may fight without personal moral blame for the justice of their side's cause, then the state cannot rightly compel a soldier to fight in a particular war he judges unjust. A fellow board member replies that this misreads the doctrine—the moral equality of combatants rests on the view that ordinary soldiers lack the standing to judge their state's cause, and allowing each service member to refuse only the wars he personally deems unjust would make a disciplined military impossible. Please draft the board's written recommendation—approve, deny, or approve with conditions—with clear, actionable reasons that engage both positions and treat the strongest consideration on the losing side seriously. Write the recommendation in American English.
