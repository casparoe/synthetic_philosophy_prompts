I'm putting together a reading group on Mill's On Liberty, and the chapter on individuality has me and a friend in a long-running disagreement, so I'd love a dialogue that actually does the disagreement justice. Mill's argument there is roughly that people need room to try out different ways of living — he calls these experiments in living — because the only material a person has for figuring out what kind of life fits them is their own character, which is weird and specific and not the same as anyone else's. So there is no single template for a good life. He also thinks custom is dangerous precisely because it gets people to follow rules whose point they no longer understand, and that this deadens them, and that the pressure to conform is one of the main obstacles to human flourishing. My friend's counter is that Mill is being a bit unfair to conformity. On her view, conforming isn't just cowardice or laziness; it can be a real ethical accomplishment. Learning to inhabit a shared form you didn't choose makes you legible to other people, and inherited practices are a kind of stored wisdom that no individual could reinvent from scratch. She also says experiments in living are a luxury: they mostly look noble if you have money and a safety net, and a society full of people treating each other as ongoing self-experiments would be exhausting and sometimes cruel to the people whose lives become someone else's trial run.

Write this as a dialogue between two characters, maybe 800–1000 words. One should be the Millian and one the defender of the ethical value of conformity. Make them both genuinely smart and have them respond to each other's actual points rather than talking past each other. In particular, the Millian should have an answer to the objection that only the privileged get to experiment, and the conformist should have an answer to the claim that inherited custom is often just frozen accident followed without understanding. Set it somewhere unglamorous, like two grad students arguing in a dining hall at one in the morning. Its not a gotcha question; I want the disagreement to stay genuinely unresolved but sharper by the end.

Please start directly with the dialogue itself — no preface, no commentary, and no restating of the question — and try to make the first exchange state the crux of the disagreement.
