Van Fraassen's Reflection principle is one of those things i keep coming back to. The rough idea is that your current credence should treat your future credences as the opinions of an expert you're about to become: P_t(A | P_{t+k}(A) = r) = r. If tomorrow-me is going to be 0.8 that A, then today-me, conditional on learning that tomorrow-me will be 0.8, should already be 0.8. Its got a lot going for it. Conditionalization already says you can't expect your credence to move in any predictable direction; Reflection looks like the natural strengthening where you can't even foresee a future credence and call it wrong. It gives self-trust across time a tidy formal shape, and the usual defenses make it sound almost unavoidable.

But then there's the pill case, and I can't get around it. Suppose I know that in an hour I'm going to be given a drug that reliably makes people certain that the number of stars in the Milky Way is even, regardless of any evidence. I might even have watched it work on someone else. Here's the thing: I already know the drug will be administered, so learning that future-me will have credence 1 in even is learning something I already knew; it should leave me exactly where I am, somewhere around 1/2. Reflection says it should push me to 1. And that seems wrong: my future certainty there tracks a cause unrelated to the truth, and I know it.

So I feel pulled. Either Reflection needs an exception for future selves I already expect to be irrational, but I worry the exception either collapses the principle or turns it into the empty advice to defer exactly when your future self is worth deferring to. Or the pill intuition has to go, but giving it up means I should, right now, become certain the stars are even just because I foresee my own future certainty, and that feels less like a fix than a different failure mode. Or maybe Reflection was never a deference principle in the first place, and I've set up the collision wrong.

I don't want to be told which side wins. I want the strongest version of each resolution: carve out the exception and show it isn't vacuous, debunk the pill intuition hard enough that I'd genuinely bite that bullet, or find the reading on which Reflection and the intuition don't actually touch. Tell me which you'd endorse, and what evidence would make you switch.
