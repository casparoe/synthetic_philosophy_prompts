I've been working through the color phi phenomenon and Dennett's use of it against the idea of a determinate moment at which something becomes conscious, and I want your judgment about which of two things should give way. In the standard demonstration, two small lights are flashed in quick succession at nearby locations, one red and one green, and subjects report seeing a single spot that travels between the positions and changes color somewhere along the way. The trouble is that the later flash seems to shape how the earlier flash is experienced, which suggests either a backwards revision or an unnoticed delay. Two descriptions are available. On the Orwellian story, the subject first briefly experiences the red spot as stationary, after which the later information arrives and rewrites the record, so the subject's memory reports a moving spot that never entered experience as moving. On the Stalinesque story, nothing false is ever experienced: the second flash is held in a buffer, the two stimuli are spliced together, and the subject's experience is delayed just enough that what reaches experience from the start is the moving spot itself. Both stories fit all the data. The position I'm drawn to says there is no fact of the matter distinguishing them, because the distinction presupposes a privileged place and instant where content becomes conscious, and once that picture is dropped the question of which of these the subject really experienced has no determinate answer. And yet I have a firm intuition that in my own case there is, at any given moment, a determinate sequence of conscious contents, and that if I were the subject it would have to be true either that I briefly experienced a stationary red spot before it was overwritten or that I never did. The claim that there is no fact here sounds to me like denying that there is anything it is like to be me at a particular moment. So I'm asking you to adjudicate. Which should yield: the reading of the color phi data that declares the Orwellian-Stalinesque question empty, or the intuition that experience has a determinate temporal order? Or is there a reading on which both are right, and if so, what exactly is the intuition tracking? I want a committed response: take a definite position, argue for it, and say what should be debunked, amended, or reconciled, rather than laying out the options. Write in continuous prose, the way you'd write a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
