You're a philosopher of physics who has been asked to act as an honest broker between two colleagues whove been arguing for a year about whether spacetime is a substance. One is a substantivalist: on their view, space and time exist independently of matter and have their own points or regions. The other is a relationist: spacetime is nothing over and above the network of spatiotemporal relations among bodies and events. Draft the joint statement of their adversarial collaboration. Before writing the final version, brainstorm privately what each side can and cannot concede. Then produce a document with three parts. First, a numbered list of claims about motion, acceleration, inertia, and the content of Newtonian and relativistic physics that both parties can sign without gritting their teeth. Second, a disagreements table in which each residual dispute is stated in wording both sides would accept as fair to themselves. Third, the two or three real cruxes, where settling that subquestion would actually move at least one of them. For each crux, spell out a concrete scenario, thought experiment, or observation that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each predicts about it. At least one crux should engage the classic cases directly: Newton's bucket, the rotating globes, and Leibniz's static shift. Be careful to separate genuine disputes from disagreements that were mostly about words, and say which of their year of arguments youd classify as merely verbal.
