Last week I found out my insurance company has been buying a data broker's "inferences" about me—like a score for how likely I am to file a claim, built from purchases and where I drive. I mentioned it to two friends, and they got into a real argument about whether information about me is actually mine.

Priya said it obviously is: the data traces her life, choices, body. The clean fix is to treat personal data as property. Then companies would need consent and payment, same as for anything else they take from you.

Jonas said facts aren't the kind of thing anyone can own. The fact that you bought cold medicine on a Tuesday is just something that happened. And if you own facts about you, what about a reciept—a fact about both you and the store? Do you co-own it? He said we'd be putting property rights on public facts and turning ordinary life into a copyright violation.

Priya said fine, any single fact is just a fact, but a thousand data points about one person is like a portrait someone painted without asking. The brushstrokes are public but the painting isn't. And if mixing your labor with something grounds property, she's the one who mixed labor into the data by living her life, not the broker.

Jonas said that's exactly the problem: if it's property, it's sellable. The first thing you'd get is a "five bucks for your location history" button, and the people who sell first are the ones who can least afford not to. He said the right frame is privacy, not property—ban harmful uses, and treat control over your data as an inalienable right, not a commodity.

Priya said banning harmful uses is just property under another name. If a company can't use her data without her say, that's ownership in everything but the word.

Jonas said no—property you can alienate, but you can't validly sell the right not to be discriminated against. The difference between "you may use it only as I permit" and "you must not use it to harm me, period" isn't just a naming issue.

I honestly thought both of them landed some punches. Can you go through the exchange and say who's right about which specific point, and what both of them are missing?
