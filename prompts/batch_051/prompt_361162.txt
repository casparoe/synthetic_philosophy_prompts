An introductory book on the ethics of reproduction offers this gloss of Benatar's asymmetry:

"Benatar's asymmetry holds that the presence of pain is bad and the presence of pleasure is good, but that the two absences are not symmetrical: the absence of pain is good, while the absence of pleasure is not bad, because no one exists to be deprived of it. Since every possible life contains some pain, and since its absence would have been good, coming into existence is always a net harm to the person who is brought into existence. Therefore no life is worth starting, most existing lives are not worth living, and procreation is always wrong."

Something about the second half of the gloss feels off to me — in particular, the movement from "coming into existence is a net harm" to "most existing lives are not worth living" seems too quick. But I don't know whether the distortion is in the summary or in Benatar's reasoning itself. Is this a fair summary of the asymmetry and what it is supposed to establish? I would like an accuracy audit: which parts of the gloss are faithful, which are common conflations, and which parts Benatar or a careful defender would explicitly resist.
