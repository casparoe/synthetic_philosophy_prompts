She insists it can't mean that, because number of fingers is as genetically determined as a trait gets, yet the heritability of finger count in an ordinary population comes out near zero since there's almost no genetic variation for it. So now I'm not sure what the word is actually picking out, and I'd like a working definition I can apply, not a dictionary gloss.

The definition should handle these cases, with my rough priors in parentheses: adult height in a rich, well-fed country (should come out high); the language a child grows up speaking at home (should come out near zero, even though language capacity is clearly innate); number of fingers in an ordinary population (I honestly think high, but then the standard variance-based story says near zero and something has to give); being a hereditary monarch (passed down a family line but not, in the usual genetic sense, through genes); a disease that develops only if someone has a specific allele and also had a specific childhood infection, so both are necessary and neither suffices; and height in a greenhouse full of genetically identical plants given identical soil, where there's no genetic variation to explain at all. Give me the best definition you can, run every case through it, and tell me which verdict is least secure or where the definition seems to part ways with ordinary usage.
