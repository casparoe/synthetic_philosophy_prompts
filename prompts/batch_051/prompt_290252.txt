I'm drafting a statement of my views on the foundations of probabilistic reasoning for a reading group, and I'd like a consistency audit before I circulate it. My working commitments are these:

(1) Cox's theorem shows that probability is the unique logic of plausible reasoning: any numerical plausibility assignment that respects ordinary Boolean logic must obey the sum and product rules, so Bayesian inference is not one convention among others but the only coherent one.

(2) Probability is epistemic rather than physical: it is a relation between a hypothesis and a body of information, not a frequency or a fact about how chancy the world is. A deterministic world is therefore no obstacle to probabilistic reasoning.

(3) The correct probability for the next roll of a die, for an agent who knows only the sixfold physical symmetry, is 1/6 per face. This is a maximum-entropy assignment required by the agent's information, not an estimate of a hidden chance.

(4) An ideal reasoner is logically omniscient: it assigns probability 1 to every logical truth and 0 to every logical falsehood. Uncertainty is always uncertainty about empirical facts, never about logic or mathematics.

(5) Since real reasoners are not logically omniscient, we use tractable approximations; I take this to be an engineering limitation of implementations, not something that undermines the normative theory.

(6) Solomonoff induction supplies the right objective prior over computable hypotheses: shorter programs receive more prior probability, which turns Ockham's razor into a consequence of the probability framework rather than a further principle.

I can't put my finger on where this package might fail, and I don't have a preferred fix. Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, say what would have to give in each case, and identify which single revision would do the most to make the set coherent. Also say how confident you are in your assessment and single out the point you're least sure of.
