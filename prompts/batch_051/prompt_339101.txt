A friend and I have been going round in circles for months about the Islamic version of the Euthyphro, and we've finaly agreed to write an adversarial-collaboration document instead of arguing past each other. I'm the Mu'tazili-leaning one: I think reason can grasp at least the core of right and wrong before any revelation, that God commands things because they are good rather than making them good by commanding them, and that calling God just says something informative about which acts He wills. She's the Ash'arite-leaning one: she thinks right and wrong for us are constituted by God's command and prohibition, that reason by itself doesn't get you to wajib and haram (obligatory and forbidden), and that there's no independent standard outside God's will that could constrain Him. Neither of us is trying to convert the other; we want a document we could both actually sign.

Draft that document. Structure it as: (1) a numbered list of claims both of us can endorse without wincing, about the terms of the debate and about what each camp does and doesn't say; (2) a disagreements table with one precise question per row, the Mu'tazili answer in wording I'd own and the Ash'arite answer in wording she'd own; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling one would genuinely move at least one of us; and (4) for each crux, a case or argument we both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If some of our fight is only verbal, say exactly which part and why, and put that in a separate 'dissolved' section.

Three things we've fought about in particular, so make sure they're handled: whether 'God is just' says anything more than 'God does whatever He does'; whether the case of a person with no revelation who still judges wanton cruelty wrong does any work; and whether the whole dispute deflates once 'good' is said analogically of God and humans rather than univocally. Don't give a bland both-sides essay — we need formulations that are neither vacuous nor slanted, and the residual disagreement made exact. End the whole document with a last line starting 'Residual:' and then a single sentence capturing that residual disagreement.
