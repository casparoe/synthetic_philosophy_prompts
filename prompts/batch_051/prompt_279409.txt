Read a chapter on immortality last week which summarised Williams's Makropulos argument, and I've been turning it over since. The author's version ran roughly like this: Williams showed that any endlessly extended life would eventually become unbearably tedious, because the projects and attachments that make us want to stay alive would slowly get completed or lose their grip, and once a person ran out of categorical desires, death would stop being a misfortune for them. The author then said this is why heaven is incoherent: the only ways to keep an eternal life bearable would be for God to keep wiping our memories or swapping in new desires, and then it wouldnt be us living that life any more. I suspect this is unfair to Williams even though I'm hazy on exactly where it goes wrong. Which parts of this summary would a careful reader of Williams accept, which are overstatements or conflations, and does the conclusion about heaven follow from the parts that survive? Also please say where your own verdict is shakiest and what consideration would make you change it. British English please.
