I am preparing materials for a seminar on experimental philosophy and the side-effect effect often associated with Knobe's chairman vignette. The familiar finding is that participants are far more willing to say that a foreseen but disavowed side effect was brought about intentionally when the side effect is harmful than when it is helpful. The usual discussion moves too quickly from this asymmetry to the conclusion that the moral badness of the outcome is doing the work, so I would like some cases that force two candidate explanations to peel apart.

Please construct a pair of vignettes to the following specification. We will distinguish two candidate explanations of the asymmetry. The valence account says that the moral badness of the side effect drives the intentionality judgement: bad side effects attract attributions of intentionality because the observer is trying to blame the agent, while good side effects do not. The cost account says that what drives the judgement is the agent's own practical orientation toward the side effect: a side effect is judged intentional when the agent treats it as a cost or downside he is willing to accept in exchange for the main benefit, and this is standardly true of harms but not of helps. Build two realistic business cases, one paragraph each, in the style of the original chairman vignette, with an ordinary executive facing an ordinary commercial decision and no exotic features. In both cases the agent must foresee the side effect with certainty, and the side effect must remain a side effect rather than a goal. In Case 1 the side effect must be morally bad but not treated by the agent as a cost of the decision. In Case 2 the side effect must be morally good but treated by the agent as a cost of the decision. Then show that the two accounts make opposite predictions in each case: in Case 1 the valence account predicts an intentionality judgement and the cost account does not; in Case 2 the reverse. Commit to a definite design rather than canvassing alternatives, state plainly each account's verdict and the reasoning behind it, identify the precise feature of each case that does the work, and explain what would happen to the divergence if that feature were removed. Finally, say how a defender of the account that comes out badly in each case would try to block the case. Please write in British English.
