I recently read the passage in the Zhuangzi in which Zhuang Zhou dreams that he is a butterfly, fluttering about and enjoying himself, and on waking says he no longer knows whether he was Zhuang Zhou who dreamed of being a butterfly or is a butterfly now dreaming that it is Zhuang Zhou. The passage then remarks that between Zhuang Zhou and the butterfly there must be some distinction, and calls this the transformation of things. I do not see why this is treated as a deep philosophical insight. Waking experience is continuous, stable, and publicly shared; the dream was private, unstable, and came to an end when he woke. Moreover, from the waking perspective he can remember the dream, whereas the butterfly presumably had no reciprocal memory of his life as a man. Why, then, should the case remain open at all? Why is Zhuangzi's inability to decide regarded as profound rather than as a failure to apply the ordinary criteria that clearly distinguish waking from dreaming?
