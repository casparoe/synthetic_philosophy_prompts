I would like a careful account of a fault line in the theory of metaphor. On one side is Davidson's view that a metaphor has no meaning beyond its literal meaning: the words in a metaphor mean only what they mean literally, and whatever else the metaphor does is a matter of the effects it produces and the comparisons it prompts, not of semantic content. On the other side is the conceptual-metaphor tradition associated with Lakoff and Johnson, which treats metaphor as a matter of thought rather than language: systematic cross-domain mappings, such as understanding argument as war or time as money, structure our concepts and the way we reason about abstract domains. Please explain what each side has to mean by "meaning" if the two positions are to be in genuine disagreement, and assess whether the dispute is substantive or whether the two camps are largely talking past one another. I would especially like to know whether Davidson's view can consistently grant that metaphor shapes cognition and inference while denying that it has a special semantic content. Please distinguish what is settled among people who work on this from what remains genuinely contested, and avoid presenting either side's account of meaning as the field's consensus.
