Consider the claim that many simple computational systems are computationally irreducible: for a large and interesting class of systems and prediction questions, there is no shortcut that yields the answer substantially faster than running the system itself forward. The claim is often presented as a formal fact about computation, but it is also deployed to explain something conceptual — why some domains resist compact theories, why surprises cannot be engineered away, and why simulation is sometimes the only theory we can have.

Your task is to bring evidence to bear on the deeper version of this claim: that computational irreducibility is a robust feature of the systems themselves rather than an artifact of how we currently search for shortcuts or how we happen to measure "faster." Design the best concrete test you can, and be explicit about what a favorable or unfavorable result would and would not establish.

Organize the response as follows:

- **Precisification** (~150 words): State exactly which version of the claim you are testing. Say what a candidate "shortcut" must do to count as a shortcut, what "substantially faster" means in operational terms, and whether you are testing a universal claim, an existential claim about some class of systems, or a statistical claim about prevalence.

- **Test design** (~350 words): Specify a concrete protocol. Say which systems you would sample and how you would generate prediction questions for them. Describe how a search over shortcuts would be conducted, under what resource model speed-up is measured, and how you would avoid the obvious failure mode of treating "we could not find a shortcut" as evidence that no shortcut exists. Include controls or benchmarks that would separate genuine irreducibility from limitations of the search procedure.

- **Reading the results** (~250 words): Say what pattern of outcomes would lead you to accept the claim, what pattern would lead you to reject or substantially revise it, and what would be merely inconclusive. Distinguish evidence of absence from absence of evidence.

- **Residue** (~200 words): Identify what even a decisive computational result could not settle — in particular, the parts of the conceptual claim concerning explanation, surprise, and the limits of theory that no test of shortcut availability can reach, and why those parts remain outside the reach of this kind of evidence.
