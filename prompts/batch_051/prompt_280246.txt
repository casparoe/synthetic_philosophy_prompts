I am a retired physical chemist and a recent, amateur reader of philosophy of religion. What I want from you is not an analogy but a disciplined translation. The position to be translated is the mainstream Christian doctrine of the Incarnation: the claim that one person, Jesus Christ, exists in two complete natures, a divine nature and a human nature, and that these are united without being fused into a third kind of thing and without being separated into two subjects. Please work from the claim as I have stated it, rather than from the history of its formulation.

My own professional vocabulary is that of physical chemistry: components and phases, mixtures, solutions, colloids, emulsions, alloys, compounds, chemical potential, phase equilibria, degrees of freedom. I am fluent in these, so use them at a technical level and do not stop to explain them. My working intuition, for what it is worth, is that the doctrine sounds like a two-phase system within a single material: two distinct natures coexisting, each retaining its characteristic properties, neither dissolving into the other, yet jointly constituting one thing. I cannot yet tell whether that picture is genuinely illuminating or smuggles in a picture that the doctrine itself would reject.

Please take the role of a professional philosopher of religion who also has a competent working grasp of physical chemistry. Begin by setting out the candidate translations you are considering — mixture, compound, two-phase equilibrium, and any others — and say briefly why each seems promising or mistaken. Then carry out the translation as faithfully as you can, claim by claim. After that, deliver what I would call a residue report: which parts of the doctrine translate cleanly, which could be translated only with distortion, and which have no counterpart at all in the chemist's framework. Finally, tell me whether the untranslatable residue is best explained as a limitation of my home vocabulary, as an unclarity in the doctrine itself, or as evidence that my two-phase-system intuition is simply the wrong sort of picture. I am less interested in a vivid analogy than in learning precisely where it stops working.
