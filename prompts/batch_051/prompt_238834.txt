My friend and I keep going back and forth on whether meme is more than a metaphor. The old argument for taking it literally went like this: cultural variants—catchphrases, tunes, rituals, fashions—get copied from person to person with enough fidelity that they form lineages, and variants that are better at getting copied spread, so you get a genuinely Darwinian process. The standard objection is that this overstates copying fidelity: when humans pass something along they reconstruct the gist, adapt it, and blend it with what they already think, so there's no stable unit doing the replicating. That objection made sense when the only copying machinery we had was a human mind. But now a huge share of culture moves through channels where no human has to understand the item at all: copy-paste, algorithmic reposting, and language models that reproduce text almost verbatim or recombine it without anyone reading it first. Does the original Darwinian argument for memes survive this shift, or does it need to be replaced? Say exactly which premise the shift touches, and what, if anything, can be salvaged without pretending the old objections were never there.
