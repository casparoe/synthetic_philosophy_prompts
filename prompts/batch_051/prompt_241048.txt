An incorporated residential community founded in 1931 has a governing compact with a marriage clause drafted by members who said they were applying Bertrand Russell’s argument, in *Marriage and Morals*, that marriage matters to society chiefly through children. The clause reads:

“Marriage is a social institution whose importance to the community lies in the begetting and rearing of children, not in the private feelings of the parties. The community shall therefore recognise and solemnise as marriage only a union of one man and one woman that is not, by the deliberate choice or natural incapacity of the parties, closed to children. Any union closed to children is a private arrangement and enjoys no marital standing under this compact.”

Two applications are now before the trustees. Case one: two women seek recognition; one is pregnant through donor insemination, and the two intend to raise the child together. Case two: a man and woman aged seventy-one and sixty-nine seek recognition; they cannot have children and state that they intend a companionate, non-procreative marriage.

The trustees must rule on both cases. Give the readings required by the text, by the purpose of the clause, and by what the drafters would likely have said if asked, and then say which approach should control and why.
