I serve on a small statutory commission that advises the government on parental leave and early childhood policy. We are due to send the Minister a short memorandum recommending a single course of action on the following question. At present the country grants fourteen months of paid leave at eighty per cent of prior earnings, all of it transferable between the two parents, together with a cash-for-care allowance payable to families who keep a child at home rather than using subsidised childcare during the child's second and third years. The commission's own evidence is that over ninety per cent of the paid leave is taken by mothers, and that this pattern is associated with lower rates of return to work among mothers, flatter wage trajectories, and smaller pensions. The Government has put two proposals before us. Proposal A divides the leave: six months is reserved for each parent on a use-it-or-lose-it basis, two further months may be shared, and the cash-for-care allowance is converted into funding for expanded, fee-capped public childcare. Proposal B keeps a single transferable pool but raises the replacement rate to one hundred per cent and reserves only two months for the second parent, while allowing that reservation to be signed over to the other parent. Within the commission, one faction contends that Proposal A is the only serious way to change the default that channels care work to women, and that the dual-earner couple must become a dual-earner, dual-carer couple if the promise of equal opportunity is to be kept. The other faction contends that Proposal A is illiberal: a state that claims to be neutral among families' own arrangements has no business telling parents which of them must stay at home, and the proposal devalues care given in the home. The disagreement is as much about the state's proper stance towards family life as about the evidence. Both views are held by members I respect.

Please draft the commission's memorandum. It must take a definite position among Proposal A, Proposal B, or a specified amended version of them, give the reasoning for that decision, state honestly the strongest consideration against it and why it does not carry the day, and note any conditions or monitoring the commission should attach to its advice. Do not present a menu of options or rest with a balance of considerations; the Minister will act on whatever we recommend.
