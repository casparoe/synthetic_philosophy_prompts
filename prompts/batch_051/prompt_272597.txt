I'm a software engineer, and I serve on the rules committee of a small Protestant denomination — roughly three hundred adult members — that holds the text of Scripture as its only binding authority, with no pastor or council above it. We are considering a governance rule, and I want to reason through what it would actually do before we adopt it. Suppose that, starting next year, any adult member may propose an interpretation of a disputed passage, and that interpretation becomes binding on the whole denomination unless every other adult member files a formal objection within ninety days. Predict, with reasoning, how the denomination's teaching, governance, and membership look after thirty years: which interpretations come to dominate, who can object in practice, whether interpretive authority genuinely spreads to ordinary members or merely relocates, and where the rule gets gamed or abandoned. I want mechanisms and incentives, not a story: which outcomes follow from the rule itself, which depend on the denomination's size and social structure, and which could plausibly go either way. For each major claim, say how confident you are, identify the link in your argument you are least sure of, and tell me what would have to be true for your overall prediction to be wrong.
