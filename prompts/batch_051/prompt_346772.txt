In my tech ethics class we've been talking about Goodhart's law and specification gaming, and there's an asymmetry I keep noticing outside class. A researcher who inflates their citation metrics (h-index and such) by self-citing and trading citations with labmates gets treated like a fraud if anyone notices. But a company that pays an SEO consultant to reverse-engineer Google's algorithm and jump up the rankings is treated like it's just doing marketing, even when the page itself is no better, or is actually worse, than what it outranked. Both are gaming a proxy for quality—citations for scholarly impact, search rank for usefulness. Is there some principle that seperates these two, or is it mostly that one kind of metric-gaming got built into a normal industry while the other stayed taboo? If you defend the asymmetry, I want you to handle the case where the researcher and the SEO person are doing the exact same kind of manipulation, aimed at different numbers.
