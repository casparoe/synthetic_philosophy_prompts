Folks in the AI open-source debate often talk as if releasing model weights is just like publishing a text. The analogy does real work: if code is speech, then restricting frontier weights is censorship, and Mill's On Liberty gets recruited to say so. I want you to audit that analogy, but not via the usual harm principle quote. Here's the passage I actually want to lean on, from Chapter 1 of On Liberty: "The subject of this Essay is not the so-called Liberty of the Will, so unfortunately opposed to the misnamed doctrine of Philosophical Necessity; but Civil, or Social Liberty: the nature and limits of the power which can be legitimately exercised by society over the individual."

If we take that framing seriously, where do frontier model weights fall? Are they closer to thoughts, publications, or something like industrial equipment? Give me the correspondence table for the speech-to-weights analogy: what plays the role of the speaker, the text, the audience, the harm, the censor. Then tell me where the mapping breaks and whether the conclusion—that open-sourcing should be presumptively protected—survives once the disanalogies are on the table. American English please, and don't be polite where the analogy is doing the arguments work.
