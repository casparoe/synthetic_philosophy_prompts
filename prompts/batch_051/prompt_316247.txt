A friend of mine in our philosophy club has strong, settled views about which relationships are genuine love, and I have been collecting her verdicts as if they were case law. She says that loving someone for their honesty, patience, and humor is genuine love, though she objects to the way the person reduces it to a checklist of traits. She says someone whose partner loses exactly those traits to a long illness but whose love stays undiminished is, if anything, in the deepest kind of love, not a self-deceived one. She says a person who feels nothing for a near-duplicate who shares all the same listed traits is right not to, because love cannot transfer to a better copy. She says a person who can name no exceptional qualities in their partner and cites only years of shared difficulty is genuinely in love. And she says a person smitten after one conversation, who claims to just know while knowing almost nothing about the other person's actual life, is not in love, just infatuated. Next meeting we are discussing a person who enters a relationship purely by decision: no shared history and no list of admired qualities, but the decision is durable, sincere, and meant as a lasting commitment. What would she say about that case? I want the prediction, but more than that I want an account of which view of love is doing the work for her—the view that love answers to qualities, the view that love creates value through commitment, or the view that only shared history grounds love—and which further case would settle which view she actually holds.
