My ethics reading group has managed to get stuck on the is-ought gap and the naturalistic fallacy for months now, and I want someone to diagnose what kind of disagreement we're actually having rather than tell me who's right.

The split is this. One faction says the whole issue is a logical point: you cannot derive an ought from an is, end of story, and any attempt to ground ethics in human nature or pleasure or evolution is committing some version of the naturalistic fallacy. The other faction says that framing is way too crude, because Moore's actual target was not just about deriving normative conclusions from non-normative premises but something about the indefinability of good and the mistake of confusing different ethical questions.

I keep coming back to this passage from the Preface to Moore's Principia Ethica:

"I have tried in this book to distinguish clearly two kinds of question, which moral philosophers have always professed to answer, but which, as I have tried to shew, they have almost always confused both with one another and with other questions. These two questions may be expressed, the first in the form: What kind of things ought to exist for their own sakes? the second in the form: What kind of actions ought we to perform?"

My working hypothesis is that the is-ought debate has stalled because it isn't one debate at all: it's an argument about logical derivability fused with an argument about the definition of good fused with an argument about which question a moral theory is even answering. If I'm right, then both sides in my group are partly talking past each other, but neither is just confused.

What I want is a diagnosis, with reasons. Name the actual disagreement or disagreements you think are bundled together here, say which ones are verbal, which ones are substantive, and which ones turn on starting intuitions that can't be argued for. Then tell me, given that diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a distinction, a formal result, a change of question, or just agreeing that no progress should be expected. Be honest if my hypothesis is only half right.
