I run a small family foundation and make four or five grant decisions a year, each in the tens of thousands of dollars. I keep flip-flopping between funding the marginal interventions—bednets, deworming pills, cash transfers—where I can mostly understand the evidence per dollar, and funding systems-change work—governance reform, market-shaping, movement-building—where the evidence is much thinner but the upside if it works seems enormous. I've read enough of this debate to know the standard arguments on both sides, so I dont need another essay defending a camp. What I want is a reusable decision procedure I can actually run in about two hours per proposal, with no staff and nothing more formal than rough arithmetic. It should take a specific grant proposal and push toward fund, don't fund, or fund a smaller exploratory amount, using ordered questions or tests, and it should flag the exact points where I'm really just making a judgment call. Write it as plain continuous prose, like an email—no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables. Then run it on two or three invented proposals, including one systems-change proposal that the procedure says to fund even though I'd be nervous about it, and one marginal-looking proposal it says to reject, and say explicitly what the procedure cannot handle.
