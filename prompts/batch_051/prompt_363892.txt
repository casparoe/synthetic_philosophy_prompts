I am a PhD student in philosophy of language, and a friend at the law school has asked for help with an appeal. She was denied a research grant because, the funding body says, her project is not sufficiently philosophical; the guidelines never define that phrase, and everyone agrees the project sits somewhere between philosophy and political theory. I am torn between two ways of describing the committee's decision. On the first, there is a fact of the matter about where sufficiently philosophical cuts off, even if no one can identify the exact boundary. On the second, in the unclear zone there is no settled answer about whether the project counts until some standard is stipulated, so the committee's task is to choose a reasonable sharpening rather than to locate a hidden threshold. I need to know which picture is more defensible, whether the choice between them actually changes what the appeal should request, and how the appeal should be framed in light of the better picture. Please write this as a short, principled memo to my friend, not as generic advice about appeals.
