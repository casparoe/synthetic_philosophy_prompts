Write a dialogue between a defender of the self-sampling assumption (SSA) and a defender of the self-indication assumption (SIA), who run into each other at the coffee cart after a talk on anthropic reasoning. For context: SSA says, roughly, reason as if you're randomly drawn from all observers in your reference class; SIA says, roughly, reason as if you're randomly drawn from all possible observers, weighted by how many observers each hypothesis predicts. Have them actually tangle with each other. The SSA person pushes the doomsday argument—you should expect to be around the middle of humanity's timeline, so update toward earlier extinction—and complains that SIA licenses the presumptuous philosopher, who overwhelmingly prefers a theory predicting trillions of observers over an otherwise identical theory predicting millions. The SIA person pushes back that SSA's choice of reference class is arbitrary and that SIA is just conditioning on the fact that you exist at all. Neither should be a strawman: each one should land real points and make real concessions, and they should be responding to what the other actually said rather than giving alternating mini-essays. Don't have them suddenly agree or paper over the difference—leave it unresolved. Write the whole thing in American English.
