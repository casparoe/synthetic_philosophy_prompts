My political philosophy seminar did Aristotle’s Politics and we got stuck on the flute example in Book III (chapter 12 in the Jowett translation). Aristotle says something like: if two flute players are equal in their art, the better instrument should go to the superior player, not to the one with better birth, because better birth won’t make him play better. That seemed obviously right to us — distribution should track the relevant skill, not status. But then someone pushed it one step further and we couldnt find the stopping point.

Here’s the argument we ended up with:

1. For any scarce good that exists for the sake of an activity, it should go to the person with the greates relevant capacity to perform that activity — the best flute to the best flute player, scarce medical school places to the most promising future doctors, and so on.
2. If a person deserves a good in virtue of having such a capacity, then that capacity must itself be something they can plausibly be credited with.
3. But the capacities that make someone the best candidate — talent, focus, even the tendency to practice — are in large part the product of genes, early parenting, and other unchosen circumstances.
4. So the person cannot be credited with those capacities in the way desert requires.
5. So the person does not deserve the good that those capacities qualify them for.
6. But the same point applies to every capacity anyone ever has.
7. So no one ever deserves any competitive or merit-based good at all — which seems absurd, because it would make “she earned that spot” impossible to ever say.

I don’t believe (7). We tried to resist by saying the argument conflates “the best person for the flute” with “the person who deserves the flute,” but I’m not sure that’s fair because our intuition that she earned it is exactly what is doing the work. Where does this argument actually go wrong? If it’s valid, which premise is least costly to give up and what does that concession do to the ordinary idea of merit? Alternatively if there’s a hidden equivocation, I need to know exactly which term shifts meaning between steps and how a meritocrat could repair the argument.
