I'm designing a unit for an upper-level undergraduate course on the physics of information, cross-listed between philosophy and physics. The sixteen students have seen entropy, the second law, and basic probability, but not information theory. I want a ninety-minute unplugged activity that makes them feel, rather than hear from me, why Maxwell's demon does not actually break the second law. The target insight is that the demon can sort fast and slow molecules only by acquiring and storing information about them; at the end of a cycle that information is either still sitting in some physical record, in which case the cycle is not complete, or it has been erased, and erasure is itself a physical operation that dumps entropy back into the environment. Last year I tried a quick card-sorting version and the students gamed it by guessing rather than keeping records, so this year I want a design in which guessing is structurally punished and record-keeping is forced.

Please design a runnable game for this session, with a demon role, some mechanical stand-in for a trapdoor that separates hot tokens from cold tokens into different chambers, and a memory or ledger constraint: the demon may open the door only if a written record or physical marker of the token's type exists, and clearing that record costs something visible that stands for dissipation. Specify the setting, group size and grouping, materials, exact rules, number of rounds, scoring or incentives, and a minute-by-minute timeline for the ninety minutes. Make definite choices at every point, no options or variants, and briefly defend each design decision. The students should hit the problem through the rules and incentives before it is named in the debrief.

End with a debrief script that maps concrete moments in the game to the concepts: information-bearing physical states, the demon's memory as part of the system, the difference between finishing a cycle with a full ledger and finishing with an erased one, and why the second law survives. Also state frankly which part of the physics the activity cannot reproduce and must be supplied afterwards, such as the quantitative Landauer bound of kT ln 2 per erased bit and the idealization of reversible computation. After the prose plan, append a JSON object that summarizes the same design, with exactly these fields: title, learning_target, participants, duration_minutes, materials, roles (array of objects with fields role, task, constraint), timeline (array of objects with fields minutes, name, instructions, purpose), debrief_points (array of objects with fields moment, concept, question), predictable_failures (array of objects with fields failure, correction), missing_feature, committed_choices_rationale.
