I help run a small forecasting tournament that started as a friend-group thing, accidentally got prize money, and now has an actual rulebook. The clause that's causing trouble says:

"7.3. The first-prize winner shall be the entrant whose final submission achieves the highest score on the held-out evaluation set at the close of the tournament, as reflected on the public leaderboard. Entrants may submit up to three complete forecasts per day and will receive, for each submission, only the aggregate public score and their current rank. No entrant may access the held-out resolutions, attempt to manually label any of the withheld data, or coordinate with another entrant. The organizing committee may disqualify any entrant who attempts, in its sole judgment, to circumvent the scoring process."

We assumed "circumvent the scoring process" covered things like scraping the test set or colluding. The winner did neither. Instead they used the leaderboard as an oracle: the held-out set was forty historical questions whose resolutions we'd kept secret, and over the season they submitted forecasts holding all but one answer fixed, using the movement in their aggregate score to back out each hidden resolution one question at a time. By the end they'd reconstructed almost the whole answer key and submitted a final forecast tuned to it. On ordinary unseen questions they weren't much better than average, but the metric didn't care anymore—which I recognize from the usual Goodhart discussions, except now it's my rule and my prize money, and I can't tell whether the real failure was the rule, the metric, or us.

What I want is a serious application of the clause to a case its drafters didn't foresee. Read literally, did they circumvent the scoring process? They only used the interface we gave them, so text, purpose, and what-the-drafters-would-have-said might genuinely pull apart, and I'd also want the reasonable-entrant reading. Walk through each and tell me which should govern. Then say whether there is any version of this rule that bans score-as-oracle probing without also banning the kind of normal iterative improvement the tournament exists to reward. I care less about who gets the prize than about whether a rule can protect a measurand once the measurand has been made a target.
