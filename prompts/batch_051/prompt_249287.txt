I've been trying to sort out what I actually believe about how to do good in the world, and I've ended up with a cluster of commitments that I can't get into a single coherent frame. I'd like an audit of the set itself rather than a reading list or a general lecture on the surrounding debates. Here, roughly, is where I've landed.

First, I think choices about doing good should be made by comparing expected marginal impact: what happens, in expectation, if I devote an additional unit of money or effort to one option rather than to the best alternative. Second, I'm drawn to a systems picture according to which the largest opportunities lie in altering the structure of a system — its incentives, norms, and feedback loops — so that a small intervention at the right leverage point can produce a disproportionately large downstream change. Third, I'm in practice deeply suspicious of systemic-change interventions, because their effects are diffuse, delayed, and entangled with too many confounds to be measured well; as a result, I mostly trust direct, well-evaluated interventions such as distributing bednets or giving cash. Fourth, I think one should be cause-neutral, following the evidence rather than holding a prior allegiance to any particular cause or theory of change. Fifth, I think the highest priority should be addressing root causes rather than symptoms, and I worry that merely treating symptoms can sometimes entrench the underlying problem.

I want to know whether this is a jointly tenable position. Please go through the commitments and identify which pairs or triples genuinely pull against one another, which apparent tensions dissolve once the relevant terms are disambiguated, and what the cheapest way to restore consistency would be — including what that repair would cost. Order the tensions by severity. And please say how confident you are in the diagnosis overall, and single out the specific point in your analysis that you are least sure of.
