A friend tried to convince me of this and I can’t tell if it’s airtight or where the trick is.

(1) A trained model just is a function of its training data plus its architecture and training algorithm.
(2) So for a fixed model, the output on a prompt is fully determined by patterns in the training data, or by how the algorithm compresses and extrapolates from those patterns.
(3) An intuitive leap is a move to something genuinely new: an idea that wasn’t already there in the materials and rules you started with.
(4) A function of old materials and fixed rules can never produce anything genuinely new—it can only recombine, interpolate, or extrapolate from what it was given; even proving a theorem nobody has seen before just reveals what was already implied by the data and rules.
(5) Therefore a trained model can’t make an intuitive leap, however original its outputs look.

I suspect step 4 is doing too much work, but when I try to say why I end up sounding like I’m moving the goalposts about what “new” means. Is the argument valid? If not, exactly which step fails and what would the corrected step say?
