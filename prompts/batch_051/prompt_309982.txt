I'm teaching a unit on blasphemy, sacrilege, and offence against the sacred, and I keep coming back to Monty Python's Life of Brian. Everyone remembers the squabbling revolutionary factions and the crowd mishearing the sermon, but I want a careful reading rather than the usual take. Is the film actually blasphemous in the strict sense—contempt aimed at the sacred object itself—or does it mostly lampoon the machinery around religion: institutions, schisms, crowds that turn a nobody into a prophet? The film goes out of its way to make Brian not Jesus, just an ordinary man who keeps getting mistaken for the messiah; how does that shape the charges you can level at it? And read the crucifixion finale through the same lens: does it profane the cross, or does it do something else with it, maybe even something reverent in a backhanded way? I want an honest reading that says where the film stays on the safe side of the line and where it might not, because I don't want to screen it and realize my students got a too-neat "satire of institutions" conclusion.
