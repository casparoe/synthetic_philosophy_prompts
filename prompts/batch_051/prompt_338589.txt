I keep coming back to the Dutch book arguments and wondering how much work they actually do. Ramsey in Truth and Probability famously argued, roughly, that if your degrees of belief don't satisfy the axioms of probability, there's a system of bets you'd accept each on its own but that collectively guarantee you a loss, however the world turns out. I don't want a survey of the literature though—I want your actual credence that this kind of argument succeeds in showing that rational degrees of belief must obey the probability calculus. Give me a number, and decompose it: how much weight do you put on the claim that your betting dispositions reveal your degrees of belief, on the claim that vulnerability to sure loss is the right test of irrationality rather than just a pragmatic defect, on whether real agents have the kind of stable, unlimited betting behavior the argument presupposes, and on whether logical omniscience and infinite book are doing hidden work that we shouldn't accept. Then tell me which sub-question you're least confident about and what evidence would move you most.
