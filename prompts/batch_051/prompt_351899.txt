You are a philosopher of law specializing in the rule of law. Work through this carefully, and start by brainstorming several candidate principles before you settle on your final answer.

Consider an asymmetry in ordinary judgment about desuetude—statutes that remain formally valid but are never enforced. Two people are arrested. The first violated a minor ordinance that is well publicized and regularly enforced; most people call the arrest unremarkable. The second violated a minor ordinance that is still on the books but has not been enforced in living memory, while the conduct it bans is open, widespread, and tolerated and many residents do not realize it remains an offense; most people call the arrest unjust or at least disturbing. Yet on standard criteria of legal validity the two ordinances have the same status, and both defendants knowingly or unknowingly violated a law that was in force.

What, if anything, justifies treating the two arrests differently? Find the strongest principle you can. Candidates might include fair notice, settled expectations, implicit repeal by public practice, the rule-of-law requirement that law be capable of guiding conduct, or state estoppel. But the task is not just to list these: you need to say whether any of them, or some combination, actually does the justificatory work. Does the principle show only that the dormant law should be enforced with leniency, or that it has lost its claim to be enforced, or even that it has ceased to be law? And where does the principle meet its limits: how long and how visible must non-enforcement be, and would the same reasoning permit a powerful group to nullify a law it dislikes by ignoring it?

Test the principle against nearby cases before you commit: a law that is enforced only against a despised minority but ignored for everyone else; a law whose existence citizens could have discovered but which no one in practice has heard of; a law whose text forbids conduct that the enforcing agency has long publicly permitted. Then render an honest verdict: is the asymmetry in ordinary judgment justified, and should desuetude affect a statute's enforceability, its validity, or neither?

At the end, state how confident you are in your verdict and single out the point you are least sure of.
