Philonous replies that when Hylas conceives the tree, the tree is thereby present to his mind, so what Hylas has actually conceived is a tree that is being conceived, not one that exists unperceived. I am less interested in the exegetical question than in whether there is any sound general principle that determines when this style of argument succeeds and when it fails.

I believe there was such a determinate number, and that no one has ever known or thought about that number. This too seems coherent.

I want to say that any object of my thought is thereby an object of thought, and so cannot be an object that no one has ever thought about.

Fourth, I can think about a particular rock on a planet so remote that no finite mind will ever perceive it. I think it is coherent that such a rock exists unperceived by any finite mind.

Fifth, I cannot conceive of an object that no possible consciousness, not even a divine mind, ever perceives. If unperceived is defined as unperceived by all possible minds, that seems impossible to me, though perhaps only for theological or conceptual reasons.
