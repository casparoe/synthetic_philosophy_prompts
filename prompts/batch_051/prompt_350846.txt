Here's a mechanism a friend wants to write into a constitution, and I can't decide whether it's clever or mad. Each month, two people are drawn by lot and named the country's designated survivors for that month: one from the cabinet, one from the shadow cabinet of the largest opposition party. For that month, both are barred from attending cabinet meetings and from reading cabinet papers, and each gets the same short national security briefing as the prime minister. If all other cabinet members are killed during that month, the pair form a joint caretaker executive for four months with power only to pass a budget and call an election, and either can veto the other. If the month passes quietly, they cannot be sacked, reshuffled, or deselected for the following year, and each must publish a note within ten days saying what they would have objected to had they been in the room, written without having seen the internal papers. Walk through what this does to how cabinet meetings actually function, to who parties put on their front benches, to how the public reads those monthly notes, and to the way the scheme gets gamed before any real disaster has occurred. Can you patch the worst failure mode without gutting the point of the rule? Please answer in British English.
