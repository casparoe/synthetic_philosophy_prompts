I work as a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy. I keep running into a pair of complaints about work that seem related but won't resolve into one idea, and I want to know whether the distinction between them has an established name — and, more importantly, where its edges are.

The first complaint is about pay. Someone argues that a company exploits its workers because they produce far more value than they receive. The reply is that the wage is at or above the market rate and the workers consented. The argument stalls because one side compares pay to output, while the other compares pay to what an uncoerced exchange could command; both baselines sound legitimate, but the word "exploitation" is doing different work in each.

The second complaint is about authorship. In a previous job, my tasks were specified, scheduled, and reviewed so tightly that the finished program no longer felt like mine, though I wrote every line. The work was voluntary and well paid, so the first complaint did not apply, yet my coworkers and I still said something had been taken from us. The feeling recurs whenever a process divides work finely enough that no individual decides what the finished artifact is for.

Am I looking at one concept with two symptoms, or two distinct concepts sharing a vocabulary? If the latter, is there an established term for the difference — one that lets me say "this case is of the first type and that of the second" — and for the gray area between them? I care less about the label than about a criterion: for a concrete arrangement, what would I check to determine which complaint actually applies?
