ok so in my class we're doing meritocracy and desert and i keep coming back to the Varsity Blues scandal from 2019. the basic facts, so you don't need to look anything up: a bunch of rich parents paid a guy named Rick Singer to get their kids into selective colleges, mostly by faking athletic profiles and having someone else fix their SAT/ACT answers, and some coaches and test proctors were in on it. everyone agrees it was wrong, but here's what i keep getting stuck on: people treated it like an attack on meritocracy, while the normal admissions process already hands rich kids huge advantages — legacy admissions, private counselors, tutors, paid extracurriculars, donor preferences — and nobody seems to call that an attack on meritocracy. i want you to analyze the scandal through the lens of philosophical theories of desert and meritocracy, and run the analysis both directions. first, what does the theory say about each group and what they should have done: the parents, the fixer, the coaches and proctors, and the kids who may not even have known? try to separate wrongs that are violations of desert, someone receiving a benefit they didn't earn, from wrongs that are just rule-breaking like cheating on a test or lying on a form, and say whether those two really come apart in this case. also draw the line, if there is one, between what the parents did and legal advantages like legacy admissions or donor preference — if a consistent meritocrat is outraged at the first but fine with the second, what's the principle? second, say which features of the scandal a desert-based picture of meritocracy explains well and which it has to ignore or distort. third, turn it around: does the case mostly confirm meritocracy as a norm people really believe in, or does it expose meritocracy as a myth that exists to make inherited advantage look earned? im honestly torn, so i want the argument, not a both-sides answer.
