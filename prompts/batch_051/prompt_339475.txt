I am preparing an advanced undergraduate seminar in the philosophy of the social sciences, and I want to run a session on the claim, most prominently associated with Steven Pinker in The Better Angels of Our Nature, that human violence has declined over the long run. In past years the discussion has repeatedly stalled because participants use different working notions of both "violence" and "less." One student means deaths in interstate war, another means homicide rates, a third wants to include famine and structural deprivation, and a fourth insists that the only coherent question is whether any given individual is less likely to suffer violence, so per capita rates are all that matter — while someone else counters that the absolute number of people harmed is what morally matters, whatever the population denominator. I want to short-circuit that impasse next year, not by picking a side, but by making explicit what an adequate treatment of the question would have to accomplish.

Act as a professional philosopher of social science with expertise in conceptual analysis and measurement. Produce a memo giving the desiderata: the list of requirements that any adequate account, measure, or combination of measures of violence must meet in order for an answer to the question "Is the world becoming less violent?" to amount to more than a stipulation. For each requirement, give the case, comparison, or consideration that forces it, and identify whether it is non-negotiable for anyone serious or a contested preference of a particular camp. I especially want your treatment of the following: (a) how the account should fix the extension of "violence" — for example, whether it must draw and defend lines between direct interpersonal violence, organized political violence, and structural or institutional harm, and whether violence against nonhuman animals is in or out; (b) the per capita versus absolute-number dispute, including whether the two can be combined into a single trend claim and what should be said when they point in opposite directions; (c) whether the account must commit on whether there is a single fact of the matter here, or may legitimately conclude that the answer is index-relative or partly conventional; and (d) which of these requirements would most sharply narrow the range of defensible answers if enforced, and whether any two requirements are in tension and might not be jointly satisfiable.

Please think carefully and begin with a short brainstorming phase before presenting the final list. A table marking each requirement as non-negotiable or contested is acceptable, but the reasoning behind each requirement is what matters most; do not produce a bibliography or reading list.
