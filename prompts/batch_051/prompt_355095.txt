So I've been on a bit of a meditation kick lately, and after a particularly weird sit last week I've ended up with a theory I can't shake. I've been calling it the 'dashboard self', and the idea is this: the self isn't a thing or even a process, it's a display — like the little fuel gauge on a car, a summarized readout the brain produces so the organism can steer through social life. The readout includes a running 'I did that' label, but it's laggy; it gets stamped on actions just after the underlying machinery has already done its thing, which is why the feeling of being the author is so convincing but mostly confabulated. The central claim is that the self is the model the mind shows itself, not the thing doing the modeling, and its job is coordination, not truth. I know the Buddhists say something like this with anatta and Hume's line is that the self is just a bundle of perceptions, but I think my version adds the timing-lag bit and the whole 'this display is displayed to itself' puzzle. Honestly: is this just Hume with nicer trim? Does it secretly smuggle the self back in by asking who's reading the dashboard? And where does it break — if the self is a display, does it make sense for me to be prudently worried about my future self the way I am, or does that kind of concern turn out to be irrational? Give me the honest version: what it gets right, where it breaks, and whether I've just reinvented somebody's wheel.
