Analyze the familiar historical coincidence that John Adams and Thomas Jefferson both died on July 4, 1826, the fiftieth anniversary of the Declaration of Independence, and that James Monroe also died on July 4, five years later, in 1831. Treat it as a case study in the epistemology of anecdote, with the birthday paradox as the formal instrument. Make the mapping explicit: what corresponds to the people in the room, what corresponds to the shared birthday, and distinguish carefully between the probability that some calendar date would see several presidential deaths and the probability that this particular date, July 4, would see three. Then give an honest account of where the model breaks: the date's salience is noticed only in hindsight, July 4 is not a neutral date, death dates are not uniform across the calendar, and the anecdote is selected for retelling precisely because it is striking. Conclude by saying what the episode shows about the evidential weight of vivid coincidences, and whether the birthday paradox underwrites any stable epistemic principle about discounting anecdote.
