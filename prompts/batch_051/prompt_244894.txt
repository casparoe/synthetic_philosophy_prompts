Like most novelists, I keep a dictionary within reach, but I have noticed a mismatch between what I do with words and what the book I consult says about them. The dictionary lays a word out as a numbered list of senses, each sense a separate, stable item; a well-made sentence, by contrast, often depends on a word carrying several of those senses at once, or hovering between them rather than settling into one. That makes me suspect the numbered-sense entry quietly commits dictionary-makers to a particular theory of meaning. What theory of word meaning does this practice presuppose? Does it strictly presuppose that a word's meaning divides into discrete units that occur one at a time, or is it merely a practical inventory, compatible with the view that a word has a flexible semantic range that contexts specify? Reconstruct the implicit theory as precisely as you can, distinguish what the practice requires from what it merely suggests, and say what would change in how dictionaries are written, or in how a novelist should treat them, if a range-based view were right. If I have built the question on a false opposition between senses and range, say so plainly instead of working within it.
