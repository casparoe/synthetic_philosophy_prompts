Suppose a fictional 1973 export-control statute, the Fission-Relevant Technology Restriction Act, contains the following provision:

"No person shall, without prior authorization, communicate or make available to any foreign person any technical information, instrument, or article which, if used as directed or in a manner reasonably to be expected, would contribute materially to the design, development, production, or use of a nuclear explosive device."

In 2026, a lab releases the trained weights of a general-purpose foundation model on a public repository. The model was not designed for weapons work; it was trained on a broad corpus and instruction-tuned to refuse obvious misuse. But before release, red-team evaluations show that a moderately skilled engineer can jailbreak it, and that for a few hundred dollars of compute the model can produce dimensioned drawings, simulation code, and troubleshooting guidance that would materially shorten the path to a crude gun-type device for a state or group without an existing weapons design team. The release is accessible worldwide, including from states with active weapons programs, and the lab releases it knowing this.

The drafters of the 1973 provision plainly did not have open-weights foundation models in mind. Write an essay arguing for a definite interpretation of whether this release violates the provision. Weigh the plain-language reading, the evident purpose of the statute, what the drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable reader of the clause would understand it to cover. Treat the case as a stress test of the nuclear-weapons analogy for AI governance. As a reader of rationalist literature, I especially want you to make explicit the reference class you are assigning the released model to—published textbook, shippable machine tool, telephonable consultant, or something else—and to show how that choice drives the rest of the interpretation. Do not end on a non-committal note: commit to the most defensible interpretation and defend it. Also address whether any reading catches this release without also catching ordinary scientific publication a weapons program might use; if you think no coherent line is available, make that part of your verdict rather than a reason to retreat into ambiguity.
