After thirty-some years in solid-state physics I retired and started reading philosophy of science for fun, and I've somehow walked away with a pile of commitments about simplicity and induction that I'd like stress-tested before I try to write any of it down. Here they are, roughly as I'd defend them over coffee:

(i) Simplicity is truth-conducive. When two theories fit the data equally well, the simpler one is, all else equal, more likely to be true, not just easier to work with.

(ii) The best formal expression of this is Solomonoff induction: set the prior probability of a hypothesis to about 2^{-K(h)}, where K(h) is its Kolmogorov complexity — the length of the shortest program that generates the observations the hypothesis is meant to explain — so shorter programs start out more probable and Bayesian updating does the rest.

(iii) But I'm not a Bayesian in any thick sense. I don't think working scientists actually have precise degrees of belief, I doubt rationality requires them, and I think strict conditionalization is a toy model that badly misdescribes how evidence actually moves people.

(iv) I'm also bothered that Kolmogorov complexity is only defined relative to a choice of universal machine. The invariance theorem pins it down only up to an additive constant, and for the short hypotheses we actually compare, that constant can flip which one counts as simpler, so I worry the razor isn't tracking anything mind-independent.

(v) Even so, I think Occam's razor is justified in real scientific practice, and that its justification shouldn't have to settle the problem of induction, assume the universe is computable, or endorse computationalism about minds.

(vi) In a concrete case, if two empirically equivalent theories both look descriptively short, I'd say the one with the shorter description in our actual scientific vocabulary is probably right, but I'd refuse to put actual numbers on that 'probably.'

Do these hang together? I want an audit, not a lecture. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only seem to be (and why the appearance is misleading), and how would you rank the real tensions by severity? Then tell me the least costly revision that makes the set consistant, and what that revision gives up. I honestly can't tell where the load-bearing conflict is, so I need you to locate it rather than just shaking your head at the whole thing.
