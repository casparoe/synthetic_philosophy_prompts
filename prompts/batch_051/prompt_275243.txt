Lately I keep noticing the same deadlock in conversations about consciousness, and I can't figure out if there's a term for it. Someone gives a careful account of, say, seeing a red tomato: wavelengths, retinal cells, visual cortex, the signal getting routed until the person says "red" and reaches for it. The other person replies that all of that is just the plumbing—it doesn't explain what it feels like to see red. Fine, says the first person, so what would an explanation have to include to cover the feel? The second person says, you'd have to capture what it's like from the inside. First person tries: if I add a system that monitors its own visual states and reports on them, is that it? Second person: no, because whatever system you build is still just more plumbing being described from the outside; the inside feel is exactly the thing that can't be specified that way. At that point the demand looks impossible to satisfy by construction—every proposed answer gets waved off as "the easy part." And yet I can't shake the sense that the original complaint is pointing at something real, not just moving the goalposts. Is there an established name for this specific impasse, and where exactly is the line between its legitimate version and the version where it's rigged so no answer could ever count?
