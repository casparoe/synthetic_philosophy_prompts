I’ve been in a forum argument about whether the language you grow up speaking meaningfully constrains how you carve up experience, and I kept noticing people on both sides using "Whorfian" as a term of abuse without being able to say which precise version they were rejecting. That sent me back to the source, and I got stuck on the preface to Sapir’s 1921 book Language, where he writes:

"Its main purpose is to show what I conceive language to be, what is its variability in place and time, and what are its relations to other fundamental human interests—the problem of thought, the nature of the historical process, race, culture, art."

What strikes me is that he lists "the problem of thought" as one among several fundamental human interests, not a single research program. A century later, the Sapir–Whorf debate has collapsed into a narrower empirical question about whether language shapes cognition in specific domains, and everyone agrees the strong "language determines thought" version is false. But I'm trying to figure out where there's still live work to do. I have about three years of self-directed research time—some philosophy and cognitive science background, enough stats to read the psychology literature, but no linguistics PhD—and I don't want to spend it relitigating settled questions or chasing something untestable.

If you were building an agenda for that time, which two or three open questions in linguistic relativity would you put at the top, and why? I care less about the list than the reasoning. For each, I want to know: is it upstream of other debates, so that a solid answer would change or dissolve downstream claims in cognitive science, AI, or philosophy of mind? Is it tractable with current methods—cross-linguistic experiments, corpus work, computational models, or something else? And which questions that sound interesting would you deprioritize because they're underdetermined or the answer wouldn't change much either way? Also, what evidence over the next couple years would make you drop or promote an item?
