I have recently found myself responsible for the notebooks of my late great-uncle, who was an electrical engineer and a church organist. His pages mix ordinary English prose with musical scores, mathematical derivations, circuit diagrams, and several pages of a private shorthand he used for daily journaling. The archivist who has agreed to take the collection has asked me to separate the "written material" from the "graphic material that is not writing," and I have discovered that I cannot say where the line falls without circularity.

I would like you to propose a working definition of writing—or of a writing system, if you think the distinction matters—and test it against the following cases. I suspect that any definition which takes a phonemic alphabet as its model, as though the ideal script encodes exactly the phonemes of a language, is prejudging the question; I would therefore like the definition to say explicitly what a script must encode, and to treat the degree to which an alphabet is phonemic as one possibility rather than as the standard. The cases are as follows:

- a page of English in standard Latin-alphabet orthography;
- a page of Mandarin written in Chinese characters;
- a photograph of a street scene;
- a musical score in staff notation;
- a mathematical derivation in ordinary notation;
- a short emoji sequence that communicates a specific event—for example, a house, a flame, and a crying face, meant to convey that one's home has burned;
- a complete notation for a signed language that records the handshape, location, and movement of each sign and can record whole sentences;
- a system of knotted cords used by a bureaucratic state to keep census and tribute records.

I am confident that the first two cases are writing and that the photograph is not. I lean toward saying that the musical score is not writing, but I could not defend that verdict. The remaining cases strike me as genuinely unsettled. Please run the definition through each case, identify which of your verdicts is least secure, and either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. I am asking because I must make a defensible distinction in practice, not because I need a bibliography or a history of the scholarship. Please write the answer in American English.
