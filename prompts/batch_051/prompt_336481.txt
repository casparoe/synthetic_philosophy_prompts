I've been going down a Fermi paradox rabbit hole lately. I work in signal processing (telecom, not SETI) so the detection side — how you'd actually pull a faint signal out of noise — is pretty intuitive to me, but the strategic side is where I keep going in circles. I've ended up fairly convinced by the dark forest answer: the sky looks empty because broadcasting your location is suicidal. Any civilization that detects you is a potential existential threat, and the rational move is to strike first or, at minimum, stay absolutely silent, so the universe is full of quiet hunters and the silence is exactly what we should expect. I can already see the most obvious objection, which is that we've been leaking radio and TV for about a century and nothing has happened; my answer is that a century is nothing and our leakage is too weak to have gotten anywhere interesting. I'm not certain — I just think dark forest beats the other stories I've heard, like "life is just rare" or "everyone listens but nobody transmits," because those seem to need extra assumptions to explain why not a single civilization ever slips up.

So the actual ask: what would genuinely count against this view? Not cheap shots, but the evidence or arguments that should move me, ranked by how decisive they'd be. What would we have to actually observe — a signal, a pattern in survey data, something about interstellar weapon physics, or something about our own history — for dark forest to look worse than its rivals? Which objections would a serious dark forest defender concede as counting against them, and which would they shrug off as minor patches? I also want to know if I've quietly made the view unfalsifiable, and if I have, whether that's a problem for a claim like this or just the price of doing a one-off strategic argument about civilizations. Be honest about how exposed the standard story actually is, and if theres one thing that should tip me, say what it is.
