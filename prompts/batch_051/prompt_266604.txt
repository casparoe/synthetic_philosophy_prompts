I'm putting together a short statement of my position on whether companies can be blamed as companies, and I'd like an audit of it for internal consistency before I show it to anyone. I appear to hold all six of the following:

(a) A corporation can be morally blameworthy in its own right, so that corporate blame is not merely oblique blame of the human beings behind it.

(b) Blame in the strict sense is a reactive attitude — to blame is to resent or be indignant toward the blamed party — and it is fitting only toward an agent who could appropriately feel guilt in response.

(c) Corporations cannot feel anything, so they cannot feel guilt or remorse; I reject group minds.

(d) A corporation can make a judgment that belongs to it as a unit even when no member individually holds it — a board can adopt a policy that is the corporation's judgment though every director privately has reservations — and that is what makes corporate blame different from member blame.

(e) A company can be blameworthy even though any punishment of it (fines, restructuring, dissolution) predictably lands on innocent shareholders, employees, and customers, since blame and punishment come apart.

(f) Companies act only through the actions of individual persons; there is no extra corporate act above and beyond what the members do.

Tell me where the genuine tensions are and where the tension is only apparent. Order the genuine ones by how serious they are, specify what would have to be revised for each, and then tell me which one adjustment to my view would resolve the most at the least cost.
