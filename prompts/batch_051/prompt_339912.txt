I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm beginning work on a long-form essay about whether "national character" is a coherent notion and, if it is, when — if ever — it is acceptable to generalize about a people. The essay will sit somewhere between social ontology and cross-cultural psychology, and I'll have room to develop only two or three lines of argument seriously. The goal is not a survey of the literature but a piece that helps a thoughtful reader see which moves in this debate genuinely shift the ground and which are spent.

To indicate the kind of thread I'm weighing, I've been turning over: (1) whether cross-national differences in measured personality are stable and large enough relative to within-country variation to support more than the thinnest "average differences" claim, once you discount response styles and reference-group effects; (2) whether the object of inquiry should be population-level dispositions carried by institutions, norms, and coordination, so a country might count as, say, risk-averse as a collective even if few of its members are; (3) whether national-character talk is largely a self-fulfilling set of scripts — reputations that become causally real through expectations and behavior even when they begin as fictions; (4) the ethics of generalization: when a statistically true claim about a group can still be wrong to assert because it essentializes, stigmatizes, or licenses downstream treatment of individuals; and (5) whether the notion is too politically contaminated to retain even a sanitized descriptive use. I'd welcome threads I've missed.

Which of these strands — or additions of your own — matter most for my piece, and in what order? I'm less interested in a list than in a defended agenda: for each, why it is upstream of the others, how far its resolution depends on unsettled empirical work rather than conceptual analysis, and which contenders are overrated because settling them would change little downstream. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating the question.
