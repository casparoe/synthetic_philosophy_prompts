After a long argument with a friend last night, I want a clearer account of what we were actually disagreeing about. He argued that the Anthropocene, if it is to command scientific respect, must be a stratigraphic unit: an epoch whose beginning is fixed by a sharp, globally traceable marker in the rock record — such as the mid-twentieth-century signal from nuclear fallout, plastics, and the carbon spike of the Great Acceleration. I found myself resisting this, not because I thought his dates were wrong, but because I suspected we were using the same word for two different things: he seemed to be talking about a geological boundary, while I was talking about a historical and political period in which cumulative human activity became a driver of planetary change. The argument ended without a resolution, and I want a considered verdict on the underlying conceptual question.

Compare two ways of understanding the Anthropocene. On the first, stratigraphic, understanding, the question "when did it start?" is answered by identifying a suitable global boundary marker in the rock record, and the authority to decide belongs to communities of geologists working with formal chronostratigraphic criteria. On the second, historical-political, understanding, the question is answered by identifying when the human–Earth relationship changed in the relevant way, and the answer involves prolonged, uneven, and overdetermined processes such as agriculture, colonization, and industrialization, so the authority to decide is far less clear. When people disagree about the Anthropocene's start date, are they disagreeing about the evidence for one shared kind of fact, or are they disagreeing about which of these two concepts should control the question? I would like a committed response: take a definite position on which understanding should carry the authority in the dating debate — and on who, if anyone, is entitled to decide the matter — and defend that position against the alternative. In particular, address the concern that the stratigraphic understanding borrows its authority from a kind of precision that may be misplaced here, while the historical-political understanding threatens to make the start date merely a matter of the story one prefers.
