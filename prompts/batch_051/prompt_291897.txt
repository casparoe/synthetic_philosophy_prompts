Before I read any more proposals for extending consequentialist reasoning to infinite populations, I want the specification of what such a theory must get right. Write the desiderata for an adequate theory of infinite ethics. The task: to compare, rank, or responsibly decline to rank outcomes in which welfare is located at infinitely many points—infinitely many people, sentient beings, or moments of experience—when the ordinary additive total diverges or is undefined. Adequacy here means fit for normative decision-making: the theory must be able to guide, or give a principled refusal to guide, a consequentialist choosing among feasible outcomes, not merely describe a mathematical object.

Give me the requirements list, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. For each, mark it as non-negotiable or as the preference of a particular camp. Address at least these five: (1) what "better" and "equally good" can mean once sums diverge, and why leaving a live policy comparison undefined is a substantive defect unless argued for; (2) whether and how a change that helps finitely many individuals and harms none must register—the intuitive demand that finite improvements not vanish outright in an infinite sea; (3) whether the ranking may depend on the order in which the same individuals are enumerated, and what it would mean if it did; (4) how the theory handles welfare with no finite upper or lower bound, including mixed outcomes containing both infinitely good and infinitely bad components; (5) whether it must always identify a best available action, or may settle for the weaker deliverable of ruling out dominated actions while allowing that no option is maximal.

Then flag any subset of the desiderata that you suspect cannot all be satisfied together; I most want to know whether the list itself hides an impossibility. After that, briefly say which single requirement does most to exclude the standard moves—limit rules, temporal or spatial discounting, non-Archimedean valuations such as hyperreal or surreal numbers, and the view that all such comparisons are simply indeterminate or incomparable. Finally, be self-critical: tell me which requirement on your list rests on the most contestable premise, and what argument, counterexample, or impossibility result would lead you to demote it to optional, or to add a requirement you suspect I have omitted.
