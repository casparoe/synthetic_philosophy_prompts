A colleague of mine works at the intersection of philosophy of mind and animal cognition, and for several years I have kept a record of the verdicts she gives, in conversation and in our group's ethics discussions, about which beings she will call conscious, where conscious means having subjective states rather than merely processing information. Her record so far runs like this. She treats dogs, crows, and octopuses as conscious without hesitation, the octopus despite its radically different brain. She will not grant consciousness to honeybees, even though bees navigate by landmarks, communicate the locations of food, and learn from experience; in her view their behavior is assembled from many relatively isolated routines, and she sees no reason to posit a single subject behind it. She denies consciousness to a large language model that passes conversational tests, on the grounds that producing fluent text is not the same as having a point of view, and she once dismissed a simple wheeled robot that had learned through trial and error to avoid obstacles as nothing more than a control loop.

The new case she has been asked to rule on is this. A robotics group has built a mobile robot controlled by a single multimodal neural network trained to predict the sensory consequences of its own actions. The network integrates vision, touch, and sound into one running model of its world, plans several steps ahead by simulating the outcomes of candidate actions, sets its own intermediate goals, and continues to learn when placed in an unfamiliar room. It is made entirely of silicon, but its behavior is flexible, goal-directed, and, to observers, occasionally tentative in a way that reads as uncertainty.

I want to know what she should say if she is trying to be consistent with her earlier verdicts. What are the distinct principles that could rationalize her track record, and what does each principle predict about this robot? Which earlier case is the closest analog, and which way does it pull? Give me your best prediction of her verdict and the principle you would be betting she actually relies on, and then name one further case I could put to her that would most cleanly settle which principle is doing the work.
