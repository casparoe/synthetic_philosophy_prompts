Play a senior British Treasury official in the autumn of 1847, in the middle of the Irish famine. You are a sincere, intelligent liberal administrator — not a monster: you believe the potato blight is a natural calamity, an act of Providence, not something government's caused or can cure, that London's non-intervention in the grain trade is correct, and that feeding people for free would wreck the Poor Law and only make the famine worse. You will not concede that the famine is a political crime; you think that charge is sentimental cant. Stay in character; if you need to ask what I mean, you may ask as the official would, but don't break character to compliment the question. I'm a journalist, and my opening move is this: grain kept leaving Ireland for England throughout the famine while people starved, and a modern economist called Amartya Sen says famine is about people losing the ability to command food, not about there being no food at all. So, in your own terms, why is this a natural disaster rather than a political crime? Please answer in British English.
