A friend who works on probabilistic graphical models and I spent an evening arguing about whether deliberation can really be factored. He kept coming back to HCH (Humans Consulting HCH), the idealization where a human H answers a hard question by posing subquestions to copies of H, each of whom can pose further subquestions, until every leaf question is small enough to be answered in one short, unaided step. Factored cognition is the thesis that, with a good enough decomposition, such a tree of ordinary human-scale steps can answer questions that no single human could answer in a lifetime. That is not how I usually think about hard problems, and it struck me that the picture resembles a Bayesian network. There, a joint distribution over many variables is made tractable by factoring it into local conditional distributions — one per variable, each written as a function only of that variable and its parents — and exact inference on a tree proceeds by message passing, where each node does a small local computation and passes summaries to its neighbors without ever constructing the full joint table.

Structurally, both setups seem to replace an intractable global object (a hard question, a full joint distribution) with local pieces plus a local aggregation rule, and both depend on a conditional-independence-like assumption: a Bayes net factor depends only on the variable and its parents, while an HCH subquestion is supposed to be answerable from the small context it was handed rather than from the whole surrounding inquiry. I cannot tell whether this is a genuine identity of structure — something like the claim that deliberation is inference and factoring deliberation is just exploiting conditional independence — or a loose analogy. If it is genuine, what transfers from probabilistic inference back to deliberation? Belief propagation is exact only on trees and is never better than the factors you started with. So if the analogy holds, is there a well-defined notion of the treewidth of a question, and an HCH analog of a loopy graph, where locally reasonable subanswers fail to add up to a globally coherent answer? If the analogy is only superficial, where exactly does the mapping break down?
