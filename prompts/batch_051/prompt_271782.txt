Consider Davidson's thesis in radical interpretation and triangulation: what a speaker's words mean is not fixed independently of interpretation; it is what an interpreter, starting from observed behavior and constrained by the principle of charity, could recover by attributing to the speaker beliefs that are by and large true by the interpreter's lights. In the later triangulation version, the claim strengthens: a creature's utterances have determinate content only if the creature stands with another creature in a relation to a common external world, with the shared object fixed by where the two causal chains from object to each responder intersect. Meaning and thought are thus constitutively social and world-involving.

Now consider a large language model. The model is trained by next-token prediction over a corpus of human writing; its training loop involves no two creatures jointly attending to the objects the writing is about. Yet a competent speaker interacting with its output does something that looks like interpretation: over long stretches, she attributes to the model a stable position on indefinitely many topics and treats those attributions as conversationally usable, even though the model also produces confident falsehoods and has had no perceptual contact with most of what its output is about.

Does Davidson's radical-interpretation argument survive the existence of such systems? If it survives, show exactly why the absence of any interpreter during training, the absence of shared perception, and the model's falsehoods do not undermine the relevant premises. If it does not survive, identify which premise fails and give a repaired version — for example, a version replacing the triangulation of two perceiving creatures with a derived content grounded in the triangulations of the humans who produced the corpus, or a version replacing actual interpretation with interpretability in principle — and say what the repair gives up relative to the original. The question is about the fate of the argument, not about whether the model genuinely understands language. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
