i keep using a way of updating my beliefs that i dont have a word for, and i'd like to know whether it has an established name — but also where its edges are, because i won't be satisfied with just a label. Ordinary Bayesian conditioning handles the case where i learn something outright: i see the card is red, P(red) becomes 1, and everything else shifts by conditioning. But most of my evidence isn't like that. Last week i saw a shape by the dock and started at 60% duck / 40% log; squinting moved me to 90/10 without ever making me certain of either. When i then asked whether it would make noise if i whistled, i didn't recompute from scratch — i kept my 'if duck' and 'if log' judgments fixed and just reweighted the two possibilities. Same move when a doctor's voice sounds slightly off: my credence that the test was normal drops from .8 to .4, but my beliefs about what would follow given normal vs. abnormal stay put. The general pattern: fix all the conditional probabilities given each cell of a partition, change the probabilities of the cells themselves, and recompute everything else as a weighted average with the new weights. So: does this move have a name, and is it a special case of Bayesian conditioning or a real generalization of it? What exactly is the 'fix the conditional probabilities' part doing — is it part of the definition, or a seperate assumption that can fail? And if the evidence also changed what i thought a log would do (not just how likely it is a log), does that mean my partition was too coarse rather than that the rule broke? I'm trying to understand how to use this thing well and what counts as using it badly.
