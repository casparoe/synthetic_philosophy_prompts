I serve on my city's Planning Commission, and we are deadlocked on a land-use decision that keeps turning on a question none of us has been able to state cleanly. A large parcel near the new transit station is up for development, and two applications are before us. The first, called the Grove, would build roughly 1,100 compact apartments, most of them one-bedroom, and the developer's projections show it would draw about 2,800 new residents, many from outside the city. The city runs an annual resident satisfaction survey, and our staff estimates that the average satisfaction score would fall slightly, say from 7.4 to 7.1 out of 10, because of added strain on schools, street parking, and the parks. But the survey would also be answered by thousands more residents than it is today. The second proposal, Riverside Commons, would build about 300 larger homes aimed at current residents and their adult children who want to stay in the city. Under that plan, average satisfaction is projected to rise from 7.4 to 7.6, and total satisfaction in the city would go up, but by much less than under the Grove. Our code directs us to approve projects only when they serve the overall welfare of the city, but it does not say whether residents who would move here because of the project count as part of that welfare.

At our last hearing, two camps formed. One says the Grove is clearly better because, over the life of the buildings, tens of thousands more people would get to live decent, transit-accessible lives in a city they want to live in, and the fact that each of us already living here becomes slightly less satisfied does not erase that. The other camp says this gets the responsibility backwards: the city governs for the people who already live here, and knowingly lowering the average quality of life for the people we actually serve is a failure, even if multiplying more people by a lower score produces a bigger number.

Please draft the written decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's vote. It should recommend approving one of the two applications, or approving one with concrete conditions, give the decisive reasons for the recommendation, and engage honestly with the strongest version of the argument for the other camp rather than dismissing it.
