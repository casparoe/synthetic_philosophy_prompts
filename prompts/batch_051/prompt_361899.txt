I'm not an academic or anything, just been watching a lot of archaeology documentaries and going to museums. And I keep noticing the same move, and its starting to bug me. They dig up an object nobody can figure out a practical use for — like a hollowed-out bone tube or a little polished stone ball with no wear marks — and the caption says it was probly used in rituals. Then they find a room with thirty broken pots dumped in the corner and the voiceover calls it a ritual deposit. The pattern seems to be: "ritual" gets pulled out exactly when the evidence runs out. If they knew what the thing was for, they'd call it a tool or a storage pit; when they don't, it becomes ceremonial. And then the guess gets repeated in the next exhibit like it's a settled fact. Does this way of arguing have a name? I'm not just after a label, though. I'm trying to get clear on what the pattern actually is — how it's different from just being wrong, or from an ordinary educated guess, and where the boundary is between "this probably really was ritual" and "we have no idea, and saying ritual is just a fancier way of saying we have no idea." What would you call it if there's no established name, and how would you carve it up so the label doesn't do all the work?
