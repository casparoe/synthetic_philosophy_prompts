I am a PhD student in late antique history, and I have been watching two camps in the field argue past each other for some time. One treats the dissolution of the western Roman state as a genuine collapse: the loss of the imperial army, the central tax system, and the senatorial elite as functioning institutions, followed by material and demographic simplification. The other treats it as a renegotiation: barbarian kingdoms working within Roman administrative and legal frameworks, local elites adapting rather than falling, and the Mediterranean world remaining recognizably Roman into the sixth and seventh centuries. The same evidence, whether a palace, a law code, a coin hoard, or a bishop's letter, is read differently by each side.

Write the document a good-faith adversarial collaboration between these two positions would produce. I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign about the western empire between roughly 400 and 600 CE; (2) precise formulations of the remaining disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, the kind of evidence or argument both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Format the output as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. I suspect one crux is whether the fifth-century western state retained the capacity to collect and redistribute resources at scale, but I want the full set of cruxes, and I want them stated concretely enough that a historian of the period would recognize the positions.
