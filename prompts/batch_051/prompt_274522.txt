I keep returning to a resemblance I noticed between Lewis's triviality results for indicative conditionals and Arrow's impossibility theorem, and I cannot tell whether it is a genuine structural identity or a loose analogy dressed up in similar formal clothing.

Here is the parallel as I see it. On the Lewis side: start from the attractive thesis that the probability of an indicative conditional, P(A→C), should equal the conditional probability P(C|A). Lewis showed that if this equation is required to hold across a class of probability functions closed under conditionalization, then in the non-degenerate case where both P(A∧C) and P(A∧¬C) are positive the conditional degenerates. Applying the thesis to the probability functions obtained by conditioning on C and on ¬C gives P(A→C|C)=1 and P(A→C|¬C)=0; expanding P(A→C) over the partition {C,¬C} then yields P(A→C)=P(C), and since the thesis equates P(A→C) with P(C|A), it follows that P(C|A)=P(C). The antecedent ceases to do any probabilistic work: the two-place conditional collapses into a projection onto its consequent. On the Arrow side: start from the attractive package for a social welfare function over at least three alternatives — unrestricted domain, weak Pareto, and independence of irrelevant alternatives. Arrow showed that the only aggregation procedures satisfying all three are dictatorial; the social ordering collapses into a projection onto one voter's ordering and ignores everyone else.

The structural analogy I am struck by is this. In both cases, a function is supposed to track a relation across a whole domain: the conditional tracks the probabilistic dependence of the consequent on the antecedent, and the social welfare function tracks how individual pairwise rankings ground the social pairwise ranking. In both cases, an innocuous-looking locality constraint — the probability equation for conditionals, IIA for social welfare functions — says, roughly, that the value should be fixed by the relevant marginal or pairwise ingredients rather than by the rest of the global context. And in both cases, imposing that constraint together with closure or domain requirements across the whole space forces the function to discard most of its input and track only one component: the conditional ignores the antecedent and tracks only the consequent, while the social welfare function ignores every voter but one.
