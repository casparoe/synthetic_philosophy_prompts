I am drafting the statement of my views on moral testimony for a seminar, and I would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. My commitments are these. First, I am a pessimist in the strict sense: you cannot acquire moral knowledge by trusting another person's moral judgement, because moral knowledge requires a first-person grasp of the reasons that make the judgement true. Second, I think you can acquire moral knowledge from non-moral testimony, for example when someone describes the facts of an employment practice plainly and you then see for yourself that it is exploitative. Third, I think there can be moral expertise of a limited sort, and that a considered verdict from someone more reliable than me on a moral question should raise my confidence in that verdict at least somewhat. Fourth, I am a conciliationist about moral peer disagreement: when an equally competent and equally informed person reaches the opposite verdict after we have exchanged our reasoning, the fact of the disagreement itself should substantially lower my confidence in my own verdict. Fifth, I do not think the fourth commitment by itself undercuts moral knowledge in ordinary cases. Please go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which merely appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each genuine tension say what the least costly revision would be and what it would force me to give up. Do not recommend abandoning more of the package than necessary. Write the audit in British English, in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
