Ok so in class we're doing free will and my teacher is a compatibilist — he says determinism is no threat to free will, because a free action is just acting on your own desires without being forced, and you don't need some magical ability to have done otherwise. I tried to build a counterexample and now I've got an argument that seems to prove way too much, and I can't find the bad step.

(1) Suppose a neuroscientist secretly puts a chip in your brain that makes you genuinely and deeply want to rob a bank — it's not an urge you're fighting, it just feels like who you are now — and when you rob it, you're acting on that desire. Nobody's forcing you. On my teacher's compatibilism, you acted freely and are responsible.

(2) But that's absurd — the neuroscientist planted the desire, so you're not responsible for the robbery.

(3) So a compatibilist has to say that whether you're free or responsible depends on the history of how you got your desires, not just on acting on them in the moment.

(4) But in a deterministic world everyone got their desires from forces outside their control too — genes, parents, teachers, whatever — just slower and less on-purpose than the chip.

(5) So if history matters like (3) says, then in a deterministic world nobody is free or responsible.

(6) So compatibilism either makes the chipped bank robber fully responsible, or it makes everyone in a deterministic world not responsible. Both seem unacceptable.

Which premise is the weak one, and what do I lose by rejecting it? I realy don't want to give up (2) or (5). Please also say how confident you are in your answer and which point you're least sure about.
