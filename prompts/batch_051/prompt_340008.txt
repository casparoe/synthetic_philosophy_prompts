I am a novelist, and for several years I have served on the board of a small residential writers' colony. Each term the colony hosts about a dozen early-career writers, who share a common dormitory and a common workroom, and one distinguished visiting fellow, an older writer of considerable reputation, who is given the sole use of a small private cottage with an attached study. The cottage cannot sensibly be divided among the others. At our most recent meeting, several board members proposed that next term the cottage simply be locked and left unused, so that the distinguished fellow would write alongside everyone else in the common room. Nothing else would change: the early-career writers would receive no additional money, space, or time, and several of them have said that the cottage has never troubled them. The distinguished fellow, who is financially secure, has said she would accept the change without resentment. The proponents argued that the gap between her arrangement and everyone else's is itself a defect in the colony, and that closing the gap from the top would make the colony fairer even though it improves no individual writer's situation. Something in this reasoning unsettles me, but I cannot yet say what it is. Should I vote for the proposal? In your reply, please address the question in the following four named sections, in this order: "The case for the proposal" (roughly 150 words), "The case against the proposal" (roughly 200 words), "The principle that should decide the matter" (roughly 250 words), and "My recommendation" (roughly 150 words).
