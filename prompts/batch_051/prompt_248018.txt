I'm a PhD student in political theory working on judgment aggregation, and the discursive dilemma is driving me round the bend. The standard example: a three-judge panel has to decide whether a defendant is liable under a rule saying liability requires both A and B. Judge 1 finds A but not B, judge 2 finds B but not A, judge 3 finds both. So a majority finds A, a majority finds B, but a majority rejects liability — yet liability just is A and B. If I say "the group judges that P" (or believes, or holds; I don't much care about that destinction), I can't get a consistent read on whether the court judged A, judged B, and rejected A&B, or whether the group-level claim is something else entirely.

I'd like a working definition of "the group judges that P" that you then test against these cases: (a) a committee votes 5–0 to adopt a resolution endorsing a research proposal; (b) the discursive dilemma panel just described; (c) a hiring committee whose five members all privately think the candidate is the best on the shortlist, though there has never been a collective discussion or vote; (d) a corporation whose public ads and training materials say safety is its highest priority while an internal review shows a cost-cutting programme repeatedly overriding safety warnings; (e) a futures market where prices imply a 70 per cent chance the Fed will keep rates on hold, but most traders interviewed say they personally expect a cut and think the prices are distorted; (f) a queue at a bus stop, where each person individually believes a bus will come soon.

Give me one definite definition, run it against all six, flag where it seems to give the wrong answer or no answer, and then either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. I'd rather you commit to a particular definition and take the consequences than lay out three options and leave me to choose.
