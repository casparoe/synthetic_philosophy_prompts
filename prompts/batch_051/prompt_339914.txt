My friend has been working her way through a list of famines and classifying each one as either a natural disaster or a political crime. She hasn't said what rule she's using, and I've been trying to reverse engineer it.

Her verdicts so far:
- Irish potato famine, 1845-1852: natural disaster
- Bengal famine, 1943: political crime
- China's Great Leap Forward famine, 1959-1961: political crime
- Ethiopian famine, 1984-1985: political crime
- Dutch hunger winter, 1944-1945: political crime

I was reading Amartya Sen's Poverty and Famines at the same time, and there's a passage early on where he says something like famine isn't really about the total supply of food falling; it's about specific groups of people losing their ability to legally command food. He makes the point with Bengal: the overall food availability per person in 1943 wasn't dramatically lower than in some non-famine years, but rice prices spiked and wages collapsed, so certain groups like rural labourers couldn't buy what they needed. He also notes that Ireland kept exporting food during the potato famine, which complicates the idea that the Irish famine was just a natural production failure.

This has me stuck on two things. First, what should I predict she'll say about the Bangladesh famine of 1974? That year there was serious flooding that destroyed a lot of the rice crop, so there definately was a production shortfall. But there was also huge hoarding and speculation, prices went through the roof, and a lot of people on the ground said food was sitting in the country but not reaching people. Second, is she actually running something like Sen's entitlement approach, or is she running something else? And what further case would do the best job of telling the candidate rules apart?
