I grew up around Bible-study arguments and then kept noticing the same shape in constitutional debates and even office-policy fights, and I can't tell whether it has a name. It goes like this. A person or group insists they're going strictly by the text: "we don't add anything, we just do what it says." But the moment there's a real dispute about what the text says, they fall back on some unstated rule that isn't itself in the text — which translation counts, whether an explicit command is exhaustive or a permitted example is also binding, whether to read words as the original audience would have or as a lay person would now, which passage wins when two seem to conflict. That rule is doing most of the work, but the person using it experiences it as "just reading," not as a rule at all. And the denial matters: the whole appeal of the position is that there's no human authority deciding, so if you pointed at the rule and named it, the position would collapse. The sharpest version is when two sides reach opposite conclusions from the same text and each can spot the hidden rule in the other while insisting that on their own side its just what the words say. I've seen it in a church split over whether the New Testament permits instrumental music, and in an argument over a constitutional clause where both sides said the other was "reading things into it."

I'm not after "hypocrisy" or "bad hermeneutics." What I want to know is whether this specific configuration — a commitment to a text as sole authority, plus a non-textual decision rule that is doing the deciding and is denied or invisible — is a recognized thing with defined edges. If a term fits it, which one fits best, and what are its boundaries? If no term fits well, what would you call it? Either way, help me carve it: how is it different from the hermeneutic circle, from the regress of interpretation (where any application of a text requires choices by a reader), and from plain self-deception? Is it best thought of as a failure of the position, or as unavoidable structure that the position simply can't acknowledge without changing what it is? Finally, tell me how confident you are in your answer and flag the single point you're least sure about.
