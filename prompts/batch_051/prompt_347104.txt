I've been turning this over on my own and I want you to actually take a position, not just explain what a nudge is. Below is a list of ten policies. Score each one twice, from 0 to 10.

Score A is how much the policy actually takes away or bends your choices. 0 means pure information, 10 means flat-out banned.

Score B is how much it treats people like they cant be trusted to run their own lives. 0 means it treats you like an adult, 10 means it treats you like a kid.

The policies:

- Calorie counts printed next to each item on a restaurant menu.
- Graphic pictures of diseased lungs on cigarette packs.
- Employees are automatically enrolled in a retirement savings plan but can fill out a form to opt out.
- A tax of a few cents per ounce on sugary sodas.
- Banning artificial trans fats in restaurant food.
- A 24-hour waiting period before someone can buy a handgun.
- A organ donor system where you are a donor unless you file a form to opt out.
- Requiring payday lenders to make borrowers watch a short video about what the fees add up to before they sign.
- A school cafeteria that puts fruit at eye level and moves the cookies to a dim bottom shelf.
- A law that adults in cars have to wear seatbelts.

Then rank all ten from most okay for the government of a free country to least okay. What I actually care about is the mismatches: cases where the two scores and the ranking don't line up neatly. Something that scores high on both scales but you still think is fine, or low on both but you still think is not okay. Spend most of the answer on those mismatches and why they happen, not on defending the exact numbers. If any two of these feel like basically the same thing to you, say which ones and why they still end up in different spots.
