Total amateur here, so be patient but don't dumb it down. I keep meeting the line "history is just one damn thing after another" online, and last time it got deployed it bugged me. I was going back and forth with someone about whether a narrative account of the July crisis in 1914 — alliances, mobilization timetables, every move narrowing the next set of options — genuinely explains why WWI broke out. I said yes, and that demanding a proper covering-law explanation (showing the event falls under some general regularity) is too strict for history. He said no, a story is just story-telling, not explanation, and then he capped it off with "history is just one damn thing after another" and acted like that settled it.

Put on your professional philosopher of history and explanation hat. Think carefully, and start by brainstorming rather than committing to one reading. Becuase I suspect that sentence can stand for a bunch of quite different precise claims, from near-platitudes to wild metaphysical ones, and only some of them actually support his move. So lay out a menu of the distinct things "history is just one damn thing after another" could be claiming. For each reading, say whether it's trivial, substantive and defensible, or just false — and most importantly, say which readings would actually support his claim that my WWI narrative wasn't a real explanation, and which readings (if any) would cut against him. I don't want you to pick one reading and run with it.
