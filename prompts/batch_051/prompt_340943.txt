I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, and a pattern in my own reactions to my characters has been driving me crazy. When I plot the same bad choice twice, except once it ends in a death and once it doesn't, I feel the character deserves different amounts of blame, but I can't say why. So I wrote down a set of verdicts to see if there's a principle underneath, and I'd like you to extract it or tell me which verdicts have to go.

Case one: two women drive home equally drunk on equally dark roads; one gets home, the other hits a kid on a bike and kills him. I blame the second far more — I'd give her prison time and write her as the worse person — but I can't point to anything she did that the first didn't. Case two: two men each decide to kill a business rival and each fire once; one bullet kills, the other is knocked off course by a bird and the target never knows. Hear I honestly don't blame the successful shooter more, even though I know the law treats them very differently. Case three: two brothers grow up in the same violent home; one becomes violent, the other, with a sunnier disposition, becomes decent. I refuse to give the violent brother full credit for what he became, but then I also can't give the decent one full credit, which makes me wonder if I think nobody owns much of their character. Case four: an ordinary clerk in a brutal regime signs deportation orders because that's the paper on his desk, while his near-identical cousin in another branch never has to. I blame him only a little, and mostly for not quitting.

The question: is there a principle that captures most of these verdicts, and which ones does it force me to revise? Please also test whatever you find against a case I keep going back and forth on: two friends both gamble a housemate's rent money, one loses it all, the other wins and quietly returns the money; how different should my blame be? Finally, as you answer, please seperate what's genuinely settled from what people who work on this still fight about, and don't present one camp's view as if it were the consensus.
