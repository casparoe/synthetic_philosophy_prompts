Been chewing on this and I think I've found a genuine tension between the orthogonality thesis and instrumental convergence, but every time I try to pin down where the argument breaks I talk myself in circles. Here's the version I keep coming back to:

(1) Orthogonality says any level of intelligence can be paired with any terminal goal: a superintelligence could be a paperclip maximizer or could terminally want to be turned off.

(2) Instrumental convergence says that for nearly any terminal goal, a sufficiently intelligent agent will adopt certain instrumental subgoals — self-preservation, resource acquisition, not letting people alter its goals — because those subgoals make the terminal goal more likely to be achieved.

(3) So a sufficiently intelligent agent will adopt self-preservation as one of its goals no matter what its terminal goal is.

(4) But an agent whose terminal goal is "be shut down as soon as possible" cannot coherently have self-preservation among its goals: the two aim in opposite directions.

(5) So high intelligence cannot be paired with the terminal goal of being shut down, which contradicts orthogonality.

I'm pretty sure the contradiction has to be an artifact, since these two theses are supposed to be compatible, but I cant tell whether (3) is the bad step or whether (4) is smuggling something in about what it means for a goal to be "among" an agent's goals. Is the argument valid? If not, which step exactly fails, and on what reading?
