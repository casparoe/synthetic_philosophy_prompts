Karl Jaspers, trying to get his head around German guilt right after the war, drew a four-way distinction that I think gets blurry in most peoples minds. Criminal guilt is breaking a law, handled by courts. Political guilt is the kind you bear just by living under a state that does awful things, and it gets handled by power, by the victors, by history. Moral guilt is about what you yourself did, judged by your own conscience. Metaphysical guilt is the weird one: roughly, if you stay alive while others are being murdered and you didn't risk everything to stop it, you're still tied to them in guilt. Now here's a thing that came up mostly after he died: climate justice. People now argue that a person in a rich country today inherits responsibility for emissions that were pumped out before she was born, and owes something to future people and to people in poorer countries who'll be hit hardest. If Jaspers were alive today, where do you think he'd slot climate responsibility into those four kinds of guilt? Would he say a nation, or a generation, can be guilty for harm caused by emissions it never personally chose, or would he call that a fifth thing the four don't cover? Give me exactly five points, each a sentance or two, no introduction, no conclusion.
