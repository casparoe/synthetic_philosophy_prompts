Consider the following argument, which is meant to establish that for any body of partial evidence there is a uniquely rational prior probability distribution: the distribution that maximizes Shannon entropy subject to the constraints implied by that evidence.

(1) A rational prior should be maximally noncommittal. In particular, it should incorporate no information beyond what the agent's total evidence supports.

(2) In the complete absence of evidence, and relative to a finite set of mutually exclusive possibilities that the agent has no reason to privilege, the maximally noncommittal prior is the uniform distribution, since every nonuniform distribution would discriminate among possibilities without evidence.

(3) When the evidence consists in partial constraints — for example, the constraint that some quantity has a known expected value — the rational prior should remain as close to uniform as those constraints permit, departing from uniformity only where the evidence forces a departure.

(4) Shannon entropy measures how far a distribution is from maximal uncertainty: the uniform distribution uniquely maximizes it on a finite space, and each constraint lowers the achievable maximum. Maximizing Shannon entropy subject to the evidential constraints is therefore the correct way to find the distribution closest to uniform among those that respect the evidence.

(5) Under the standard constraints invoked by objective Bayesians, this maximization problem has a unique solution.

Therefore, for any body of evidence expressible as constraints of this kind, there is a uniquely rational prior, given by the maximum entropy principle.
