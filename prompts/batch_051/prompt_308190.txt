Here's what I think I think:

1. A job or a place in a program should go to the person who will do the thing best. Selecting on predicted performance isn't just efficient; it's fair to everyone downstream of the choice.
2. But no one deserves their raw talent or their early circumstances, and those are a big part of what makes someone "the best." So a selection system that simply tracks ability is partly tracking luck. Desert has to track only what a person is actually responsible for.
3. Which is why I think that when two candidates are roughly equally qualified, the nod should go to the one who had to work harder or overcome more to get to that same level.
4. But I don't want adversity treated as its own credential. I would not pick a less able candidate over a more able one just because the first one had a harder path—that feels patronizing and like it changes the subject from what the role is for.
5. I also think the right response to the luck in ability is to fix things upstream (schools, training, access) before people get to the application pile, not to redistribute positions downstream after the fact.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, and which only look like it? Order the strains from most to least serious instead of saying they're all equally bad, and tell me the cheapest way to make the set hold together and what it would cost.
