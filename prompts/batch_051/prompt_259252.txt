I need a working definition of "a boring life," because I keep using the phrase to mean something like "bad in virtue of lacking novelty" but I can't actually cash that out, and I want to know whether novelty is a component of well-being or just an instrumental cause of its other components. Here are the cases I want the definition to classify: a contented retiree with the same breakfast, garden, and tv schedule every day; a thrill-seeker who changes city, partner, and job every couple years because calm feels like dying; a monk repeating the same prayers and chores for decades; a severely depressed person for whom everything is flat and pointless; someone stuck in a literal Groundhog Day–style loop; a person whose life is objectively chaotic and varied but who hates every minute of it; and an anxious person who avoids all novelty because change feels unsafe. My intuitions: the monk and the contented retiree aren't boring even though an outside observer would get bored watching their days, the depressed person is boring in a different sense, and the anxious novelty-avoider is the case that actually tests the definition. Propose the definition, run it through all seven, tell me where it gives a verdict you didn't expect or would bite the bullet, and then say whether novelty comes out as a constituent of well-being, a cause of its other constituents, or just a noise term for the absence of something else.
