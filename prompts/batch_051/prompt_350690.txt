At work we've been argueing about a proposal from our safety people for the customer-support bot I'm on. The rule: the model can do all the read-only work it likes—drafting replies, searching internal docs, sketching multi-step plans—but any action that changes something outside itself (sending an email, editing a customer record, issuing a refund) needs a human sign-off first, and after every sign-off the model's entire working context is wiped and it only gets back the action log plus the outcomes so far. So it can't chain actions on its own or build up long-horizon agency; they're calling it tool mode. Treat this as a mechanism, not a position I'm endorsing: walk through what it actually does. What does it incentivize the engineers and the support agents to do, where does the first pressure to stretch the definition of "read-only" come from, and does the context wipe really stop long-range coordinated behaviour or just shift it into the action log, the prompts, and the human? Where does the scheme get gamed first, and is there a fix that doesn't gut it?
