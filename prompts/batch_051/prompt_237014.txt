Over the past year I have been taking classical guitar lessons, and a pattern in the instruction has begun to bother me enough that I want to pin it down. One of my teachers is a superb performer. When he demonstrates a difficult right-hand pattern and then explains what he is doing, his account is confident and vivid, but when I record his playing and slow it down, the motion he describes is not the motion visible in the recording. He is not lying; he seems genuinely to experience his own playing that way. My other teacher is a less accomplished player but a much better explainer. She gives a precise, step-by-step account of the same technique, and I can reproduce those steps slowly and repeat her description back to her accurately. Yet when I play the passage at tempo, following her instructions makes it fall apart, and the only times it comes out right are times when I stop following the steps and do something I could not then have described.

The strange thing is that an accurate description and the ability to perform seem to come apart in both directions: the skilled player possesses the doing but not a true account of the doing, while the skilled explainer possesses the account but not the doing. I have noticed the same thing in other people—a friend gives correct explanations of swimming technique but cannot swim well, and an athlete I know cannot describe how he keeps his balance but keeps it without effort. So the pattern does not seem to be an accident of my teachers.

Is there a name for this? I am less interested in a label than in getting the boundaries right. Is the two-way split one phenomenon seen from opposite directions, or two different failures that only look symmetrical? And how does it differ from the ordinary case of knowing something but being unable to put it into words, since what strikes me is not silence but a confidently false description? If no established term covers exactly this, can you define one and say where the phenomenon ends?
