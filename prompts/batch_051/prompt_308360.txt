My AI reading group has been going round in circles for a year on whether we've already got transformative AI. One side says obviously yes: systems like GPT-4 changed how a large chunk of knowledge work gets done, and if a technology shifts the tasks most people do at work, that's transformative by any honest standard. The other side says obviously no: transformative means the kind of change that shows up as a step change in growth or automation across the whole economy, not a chatbot that makes office workers a bit faster, and on that standard nothing has happened yet. The odd thing is everyone has read the same pieces and nobody moves. I've started to suspect this isn't really a disagreement about the future at all but something more like a verbal dispute about how much change 'transformative' has to involve, plus maybe a dispute about whether you can ever say a threshold like this has been crossed while you're still inside it. I'd like a diagnosis rather than a verdict: is this verbal, a hidden empirical disagreement about diffusion rates or about what current systems can actually do, or a difference in standards of evidence for saying a change is already under way? And given the diagnosis, what would actually move the debate forward, if anything? Please write the answer in American English.
