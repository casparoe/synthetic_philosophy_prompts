A friend and I have just spent a long argument about whether the apparent absence of technosignatures should lower our probability that technologically communicative extraterrestrial life exists at present. We kept coming back to the same five considerations, but we could not agree on how much weight each deserves. I would like an independent ordering. Please rank the following considerations in order of how much weight they should carry when setting a prior for the existence of such life now, from most to least, and give a one-sentence justification for each placement:

- The sheer number of potentially habitable planets and the opportunities they provide for life to arise.
- The absence of detected technosignatures, conditional on how long technosignatures would remain detectable.
- The number of difficult, still-unquantified transitions in Earth's evolutionary history.
- Observer-selection effects, including the fact that we necessarily find ourselves on a planet where life did arise.
- The probability that intelligence evolves once complex life exists.

Let the ranking reflect epistemic weight rather than ease of estimation.
