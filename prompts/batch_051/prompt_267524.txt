You are a professional metaphysician specializing in early analytic philosophy, the metaphysics of relations, and the unity of the proposition. I am drafting a paper on what explains why a proposition is a single unified representational whole rather than a mere heap of constituents, and I have arrived at a set of commitments that I would like you to audit before I commit to them. Please think carefully and systematically: begin by restating each commitment in your own words and tracing what it entails, and only then evaluate how the commitments interact. Do not simply lecture me on the topic; I need a diagnostic audit of this particular position.

For the paper I currently hold, or am strongly inclined to hold, all of the following:

(1) Propositions are structured complexes. The proposition that a is F has a and F-ness among its constituents.

(2) The proposition that a is F is not identical to the mereological sum, or set, of its constituents. A sum of a and F-ness is a mere heap, whereas the proposition is a single unified thing; this unity requires an explanation.

(3) The unity of the proposition is explained by a tie of predication that combines a and F-ness into one propositional whole.

(4) The tie is not a further constituent or term in the proposition, on a par with a and F-ness. It is a non-relational nexus: it combines the constituents without itself needing to be combined with anything by a further tie. This is what blocks Bradley's regress in the propositional case.

(5) Bradley's regress is a genuine and vicious problem for any attempt to explain a complex's unity by positing a relation that is itself one of the items whose unity with the rest must then be explained. Adding further relations can never turn a heap into a unity.

(6) In the world, what makes the proposition that a is F true is the fact, or state of affairs, of a's being F. Facts are unified complexes as well.

(7) The fact of a's being F is unified by instantiation: a and F-ness are bound together in the fact by the instantiation relation.

(8) Instantiation is a genuine dyadic relation, a universal in its own right, and it is a constituent of the fact that a is F.

(9) Truth is correspondence: the proposition that a is F is true just in case the fact that a is F exists, and the constituents of a true proposition stand in a one-to-one correspondence with the constituents of the fact that makes it true.
