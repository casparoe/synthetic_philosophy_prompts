I sit on the research oversight commitee for a consortium of five labs that share a funder and a building, and we're about to vote on a rules change. Right now each lab director can greenlight certain pilot experiments on their own judgment, as long as they sign off that expected benefit beats expected risk. At our last meeting it came out that all five directors agree that if each keeps this discretion, at least one of them will probably run a particular pilot—a small demonstration with real upside but a low-probability, high-severity failure mode—even though a majority of the directors would vote it down if it came to a single collective decision. This looks like the unilateralist's curse to me, but the committee is split on whether that's the right frame. The chair has asked me to draft a memo to the directors recommending what we should do: specifically whether to replace self-approval with a joint approval threshold, and if so what threshold and what criteria. Please write that memo. It should have a clear bottom line, give the reasoning, and deal honestly with the strongest argument for keeping the current self-approval rule. And no headings, bullets, bold, or tables—just continuous prose, the way you'd write an email.
