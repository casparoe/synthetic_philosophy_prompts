For my high school's European politics club (around 28 students, ages sixteen to seventeen, 80 minutes), design a structured activity that makes participants run into the tension between national sovereignty and EU-level collective decision-making. I want the students to discover for themselves that a rule everyone can see is collectively beneficial still leaves some national publics feeling overruled or alienated from the authority that made it. Include roles (for example national governments, ministers in the Council, MEPs, and domestic constituencies), voting rules that are simple but mirror something real about qualified majority voting and the ordinary legislative procedure, a timeline for setup, play, and debrief, and a scoring system that does not need real money. The debrief should map specific moments in the activity onto the difference between input and output legitimacy and onto the idea of ever-closer union. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and which part of the democratic deficit the simulation cannot reproduce, so I will have to supply that part myself as facilitator.
