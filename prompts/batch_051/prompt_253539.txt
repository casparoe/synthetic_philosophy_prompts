I've got a default rule for causal claims, and I'm worried its circular. I only take "X causes Y" seriously if I can picture a surgical intervention: some way of wiggling X while holding everything else nearby fixed, so that Y moves. And I want the connection to be stable across a whole family of wiggles, not just a one-off. If there's no way to manipulate X (history, the economy, what a model "intends"), or every knob that changes X also jerks a dozen other things, I downgrade it to "correlation" or "just a way of talking."

But here's the rub: to know my intervention is surgical — that it affects Y only through X — I already need causal knowledge of all the other paths it might touch. So am I testing for causation with one hand and smuggling causation in with the other? Is the rule just a fancy restatement of what I meant by cause, or is there a defensible version of it? Where exactly does it break, and when is it fine in practice even if it's circular in theory?
