Here is a reconstruction of Augustine's view of lying. A lie is an assertion the speaker believes to be false, made with the intention to deceive; for Augustine the deeper fault is a split between the speaker's belief and the speaker's words, so that the utterance becomes a snare for the hearer. No lie is made permissible by good consequences, though Augustine ranks lies by gravity, from lies about the faith down to lies told to save someone's life. Now consider this case. A priest hides a fugitive in the sacristy, a separate building within the church grounds. A soldier searching for the fugitive asks, "Has he entered the church?" The priest answers, "He has not entered the church" — true if "church" means the nave or the sanctuary, but the priest knows the soldier will understand "church" to mean the whole complex and will leave. Is the priest lying in Augustine's sense?
