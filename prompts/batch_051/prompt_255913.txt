Conveniently, it just became practical: there's a general election coming up and the committee has to decide what our official line should be in the local constituency. Normally we endorse a candidate and run get-out-the-vote, but this year its a mess. The incumbent, from one of the two big parties, is the only candidate with a realistic chance of beating the right-wing challenger, and she's mostly okay on student issues, but she recently voted for a bill that cuts maintenance grants and tightens student visa rules. One group on the committee wants to endorse her anyway as the lesser evil. Another group wants us to endorse a candidate from a small radical party whose platform matches union policy almost word for word but who's polling around 4%. A third group wants to endorse no one and run a 'none of the above' campaign, because in their view voting for a lesser evil just launders a bad system. Please draft the short decision memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend one concrete course of action, with reasons the committee could actually act on, and it has to engage the strongest consideration on the losing side honestly rather than just dismissing it. Please write it in British English.
