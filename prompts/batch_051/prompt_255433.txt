Before I rework the what-is-language week in my philosophy of language course, I want the actual requirements list, not another survey of definitions. Write the desiderata a theory of what a language is has to meet, with the case or consideration behind each. The non-starter for me: it can't rule out signed languages by definitional fiat — no privileging sound, no treating speech as the base and sign as a manual code for it. It should also get the weird cases right: homesign, where a deaf kid with no exposure to a sign language builds a systematic gesture system thats language-like but not a full language, and the way a full grammar can emerge within a generation or two when deaf kids are thrown together. Mark which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are just my hobbyhorses, and flag any that can't be jointly satisfied. Don't score the usual theories against the list; I'll do that in class.
