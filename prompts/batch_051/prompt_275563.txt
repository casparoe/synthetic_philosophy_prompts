I'm preparing a two-hour session for a philosophy reading group of intelligent non-specialists. Some have analytic training, some come from religious studies or art, but none have spent time with Buddhist philosophy. The session is on Nāgārjuna's tetralemma and Madhyamaka emptiness, and I want it to turn on a single passage: the verse in Mūlamadhyamakakārikā 18:8 where Nāgārjuna says, in the paraphrase I'll offer them, that the Buddha's teaching proceeds through the claims that everything is real, everything is unreal, everything is both real and unreal, and everything is neither real nor unreal. I don't want the session to become a survey of Buddhist doctrine or a history lesson. I want them to see the tetralemma as a piece of philosophical machinery rather than a piece of mysticism.

Plan the session's explanatory arc. Don't explain the view to me; plan how I should explain it. What understanding of assertion, truth, and negation has to be in place before the verse can do real work rather than sounding like wordplay? Which single concrete example should carry the whole session — an ordinary object, an event, a person, something else — and what features must it have and lack so that each of the four limbs can be applied to it without either trivializing the verse or inviting the audience to think Nāgārjuna is just refusing to answer? Map the misconceptions you expect: that the tetralemma is a four-valued logic, that emptiness is a fifth position or a metaphysical absolute, that the third limb is a contradiction rather than a conjunction, that the fourth limb is nihilism, that the verse describes stages of meditation, perhaps others. For each, say exactly where in the sequence I should head it off, and which one I should deliberately leave unresolved for the sake of the session's final move. Give me a precise check for understanding at the end that would distinguish someone who has learned the argumentative motion from someone who has merely learned the vocabulary, and say explicitly what I should refuse to explain at this level.
