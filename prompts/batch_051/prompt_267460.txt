You are a professor of early modern political philosophy with a side interest in contemporary libertarian readings of Locke. I am preparing a seminar on the Lockean proviso and want a thought experiment that makes the conceptual difference between two readings vivid. Please construct one to specification.

Construct a realistic scenario — one paragraph, set in a small pre-modern community — about an act of original acquisition of some natural resource. The scenario must satisfy these two conditions:
(1) On the sufficiency reading of Locke's proviso, on which acquisition is permissible only if what remains in common for others is as plentiful and as good as what was taken, the acquisition is clearly illegitimate.
(2) On a Nozick-style no-worsening reading, on which acquisition is permissible as long as no one's material circumstances are worsened relative to the relevant pre-acquisition baseline, the very same acquisition is clearly legitimate.

Constraints: no money, no wages, no state or legal enforcement, no coercion or fraud, no malicious acquirer, and no technology beyond what early modern Lockeans would recognize. The divergence should be generated by the structure of the case rather than stipulated.

Begin by briefly brainstorming two candidate cases and explain why you chose one over the other. Then present your chosen scenario. After it: (a) walk through what each reading says about the acquisition and why; (b) identify the single structural feature doing the work in separating the two readings; (c) say what happens when that feature is removed — does the case collapse into one where the two readings agree, and if so in which direction; and (d) give the strongest objection a defender of the sufficiency reading could press against the no-worsening reading's verdict in this case.
