Last night I argued with a friend about whether science really has a method, and he kept appealing to Feyerabend. His summary of the position ran as follows: every rule of method that has ever been proposed has been violated by real science at some point, so there is no rational method by which scientific claims are assessed; scientists choosing theories are no more rational than astrologers or faith healers; and the appearance of a superior track record is just institutional power and propaganda. He took the slogan that anything goes to mean that any procedure is literally as good as any other, and concluded that preferring physics to astrology is purely political. I suspect the summary is unfair, but I do not know the source well enough to say exactly where it goes wrong. Could you separate the parts a careful Feyerabend would accept from the parts that are distortions or overstatements, and say whether the conclusion about astrology survives once the distortions are corrected?
