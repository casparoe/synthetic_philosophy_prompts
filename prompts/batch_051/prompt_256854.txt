I keep running into a problem when I try to say what counts as one organism, and I'd like a diagnosis rather than a yes-or-no. The features people usually reach for, as I understand them, are roughly these:

1. One genome: the parts share a single genotype.
2. Functional integration: the parts depend on one another and can't do their job alone.
3. Shared reproductive fate: selection acts on the whole; the parts rise and fall together rather than being counted separately.
4. Boundedness and homeostasis: there's a single inside/outside boundary, and the whole maintains itself across it.
5. A bottlenecked life cycle: each generation starts from one cell or one propagule, which resets the parts so they develop together.

Now consider Pando, the quaking aspen clone in south-central Utah: on the order of 40,000+ above-ground stems, all one male genotype, connected by a single clonal root system across roughly 100 acres, and routinely described as one of the heaviest and oldest living things on Earth. But each stem looks and behaves like a tree: it photosynthesizes, flowers, and can die independently, and foresters count the stems as individual trees. The clone spreads vegetatively — new stems are sent up from the roots — rather than by seed, so there's no single-cell bottleneck between successive stems. And the root system has fragmented in places over time, so some same-genotype sections may no longer be physically connected to the rest even though nothing else about them changed.

This case seems to pull the criteria apart. Criterion 1 says one organism; criteria 2, 3, and 5 look like they say no, or at best "sort of"; criterion 4 cuts both ways, since each stem has its own bark and stomata while the clone has no single enclosing boundary. A lichen pulls the criteria in the opposite direction: one body, one name, one ecological role, but an obligate partnership between a fungus and a photobiont — at least two genomes, integrated enough that the partners typically can't survive alone in that habitat.

So my question isn't just "is Pando one organism?" Work through the criteria one by one, say where each gives a clear verdict and where it underdetermines the case, and then tell me what the pattern shows. Is "organism" tracking a single thing or several different things that come apart at the edges? Are the criteria wrong, or is the concept doing multiple jobs? I'm not attached to a particular verdict; I'm trying to figure out whether the question itself is well-posed.
