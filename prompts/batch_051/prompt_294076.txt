I'm drafting a conceptual chapter on "collapse" and want a consistency audit of a position I've come to defend. I hold: (a) "collapse" is too evaluative and teleological to function as an analytical category; (b) there are nonetheless abrupt, measurable declines in population, political integration, and administrative scale, and these declines are morally neutral descriptive facts; (c) the Classic Maya lowlands and Easter Island are genuine cases of such failure, and saying that Easter Island's decline was partly self-inflicted through deforestation does not moralize; (d) the Late Bronze Age Mediterranean, by contrast, is better treated as transformation and reorganization than as collapse; (e) "resilience" and "transformation" can carry values just as easily as "collapse," so neither should become the field's replacement vocabulary without scrutiny; (f) the field still needs a comparative category for the abrupt declines in (b), though it should be built from concrete measures rather than metaphors. Which of these are actually in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity and say, for each, what would have to give. Write your answer in American English.
