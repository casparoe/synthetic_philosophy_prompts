I keep going in circles on Hobbes and want someone to check this reconstruction before I build an essay on it. (1) On Hobbes's own account, injustice just is the breaking of a valid covenant, and in the state of nature any covenant made on mere trust, without a common power to compel performance, is void. (2) The original covenant that institutes a sovereign is itself made in the state of nature, since there is no sovereign yet. (3) It is an agreement of each person with each person, each giving up their right of self-government to the sovereign on the condition that the others do the same. (4) At the moment it is made, there is still no common power to give anyone assurance that the others will perform, so each person is only trusting everyone else to come through. (5) Therefore, by (1), the founding covenant is void. (6) So nobody actually transfer any right, and the sovereign can never rightfully come into existence by covenant. That seems to make Hobbes's whole social contract self-defeating. Is the argument valid? If not, say exactly which step fails and what the correct reading of Hobbes would be at that point; if it is valid, is the conclusion something Hobbes could accept without giving up the rest of his theory?
