I find myself pulled in two directions about causation, and I would like you to think through it with me as a philosopher who works on Hume and the metaphysics of causation. Please don't just vote for a side: start by steelmanning each position I describe, canvass the possible resolutions, then tell me which should give way, and under what future evidence or argument you would change your mind.

The position I keep finding compelling, on reflection, is a Humean regularity view of causation. It has the virtues I usually reward: it refuses to posit anything we cannot observe or measure; it explains our causal talk without invoking an occult tie between events; and it fits what we actually have epistemic access to, which is just patterns of succession and contiguity. On this picture, to say that A caused B is ultimately to say that A-type events are constantly conjoined with B-type events, and the felt inevitability of the connection is something the mind contributes — a habit of expectation built up from repeated experience. I can even explain away my own conviction that there is something more: I am a pattern-matching animal, and I project the feeling of forced inference back onto the world.

And yet. When I watch one billiard ball strike another, or snap a dry twig and see the pieces separate, the intuition will not stay explained away. I do not merely predict that the second ball will move; it seems to me that the impact makes it move, that there is a difference in the world between a mere lawful sequence and a genuine production, and that this difference is exactly what regularity leaves out. This is not a vague preference: it is one of the firmest convictions I have, firmer than many of the auxiliary assumptions I use in daily reasoning. I can say the words "the mind projects the necessity," and I can see the mechanism by which it might, but I cannot make the datum go away.

I know there is a third possibility, the reading sometimes called the New Hume: Hume did not deny that there are real causal powers or necessary connections in objects; he denied only that we can know or even form a coherent positive conception of them. On that reading, the regularity account is a story about our epistemic situation, not an inventory of reality, and my intuition might be tracking something real that is simply hidden from inquiry — a skeptical realism rather than a bare anti-realism about causes. I find this simultaneously attractive and suspicious: attractive because it would let me keep both the Humean epistemology and the intuition; suspicious because it may just be a face-saving move, a way to have the feeling without paying the metaphysical bill.

I am not asking for a bibliography or for which scholar carries the day. I want to know what each option actually costs. What would justify debunking the intuition as a projection artifact and dropping it? What would justify amending or abandoning the regularity view? What would it take for the skeptical-realist resolution to be a genuine third way rather than a restatement of the problem? Give me the strongest version of all three paths, tell me which way you would lean, and specify what new evidence or argument would move you off that lean.
