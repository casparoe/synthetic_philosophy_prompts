I have recently joined my hospital's clinical ethics committee, which is revising its guidance on how much weight to give to behavioural and neuroimaging evidence in disorders of consciousness. In the reading I have done, I keep running into two things that seem to be doing different jobs: the distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness, and global workspace theory's claim that a state is conscious just when its content is broadcast to a wide range of consuming systems, such as memory, planning, and verbal report. I would like you to construct a thought experiment in which these come apart in the direction I find harder to make sense of: a state that global workspace theory counts as access-conscious — broadcast and available for flexible report and control — but that is not phenomenally conscious, so that there is nothing it is like for the subject to be in that state. Constraints: make it a realistic case — a patient with a disorder of consciousness, a surgical patient, or a narrowly intelligent artificial system — not a philosophical zombie and not science-fictional; keep the scenario to one paragraph; and do not simply stipulate the absence of phenomenal consciousness in the set-up — it should fall out of the mechanisms you describe, in a way that is not question-begging against global workspace theory. The point of the case is to help the committee see whether a broadcast criterion could ever outrun subjective experience, so a case that assumes its conclusion is no help to me. After the scenario, walk through what global workspace theory says about the state and what someone who regards phenomenal consciousness as distinct and irreducible would say, and tell me which structural feature of the case is doing the work and what would happen if that feature were removed. Finally, commit yourself: say whether the case should be treated as a refutation of global workspace theory as a theory of consciousness, and give the strongest reply a global workspace theorist could make. I would prefer a definite position with reasons rather than a survey of options. Please write the response in American English.
