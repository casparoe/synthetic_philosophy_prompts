Please write an essay on a live dispute in the philosophy of mathematics and science. Some philosophers think that certain biological facts have irreducibly mathematical explanations. Two examples are commonly offered. First, periodical cicadas emerge in broods with 13- or 17-year life cycles, and one proposed explanation is that prime periods reduce the chance of a brood's emergence synchronising with the cycles of competing broods or predators. Second, honeybees build combs of hexagonal cells, and one proposed explanation is that a hexagonal tiling minimises the perimeter needed to partition the plane into equal-area cells, and so minimises the wax required for a given amount of storage. Proponents of the enhanced indispensability argument claim that, if our best explanations of such phenomena cite arithmetic or geometrical facts in a way that cannot be paraphrased away, then we are no less justified in believing in numbers and geometrical objects than in believing in the posits of successful empirical theories. Opponents reply that the mathematics is merely representing physical constraints, that the explanations can be restated without ontological commitment to mathematical objects, and that even a genuinely mathematical explanation would at most warrant commitment to the physical facts that make the mathematics applicable. Set out the strongest version of each side, then state and justify a clear verdict about what these cases actually show. You may conclude that the cicada and honeycomb cases differ in strength, but do not remain neutral between the positions. Write in continuous prose only: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables. Use British English spelling, punctuation, and conventions throughout.
