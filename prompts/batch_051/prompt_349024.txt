I've been reading about AI safety lately, and one idea that keeps coming up is "low-impact AI." The thought is that instead of trying to write down a full specification of human values, you just design the system to avoid having much impact on the world beyond the narrow task it's assigned. If the robot only changes a tiny bit of the world, the reasoning goes, then even a misspecified goal can't do much damage. The intuition makes sense to me, but I got confused about what "side effect" even means here.

Why can't you define it in a purely causal, value-free way? For example, you could run the agent in the actual world, then simulate the world as it would have gone if the agent had never been turned on, and penalize the agent for any difference between the two. Or you could use the medical notion: an effect is a side effect if the intervention causes it but it wasn't part of what the intervention was intended to do. Neither of those seems to require a full theory of value — just a counterfactual or a stated goal. I get that these would be computationally brutal, but I don't understand why people call the problem conceptual, as though you already need to know what matters before you can say what counts as a side effect. Am I missing somehting here?
