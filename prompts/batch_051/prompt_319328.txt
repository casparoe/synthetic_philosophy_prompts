After retiring from a career in the natural sciences, I have been reading my way through the philosophy of science and, increasingly, the history and historiography of science. Not surprisingly, this has surfaced a tension in my own thinking about how the past ought to be written, and I would like help working out which side to revise.

I am attracted to a view I keep encountering in different forms: no account can include everything, so every historical narrative selects; and because the past cannot simply hand the historian its own principles of selection, those principles must be supplied by the historian's present situation, interests, and categories. From this it seems to follow that the much-lamented Whig habit—arranging the past as progress toward, or failure to reach, the present—is not a distinct methodological error. It is just what selection looks like when the selected thread happens to be progress. Every age writes present-centered history; the difference between a Whiggish account and a respectable one is at most a matter of degree, and perhaps not an epistemic fault at all. The view appeals to me because it is simple, it unifies a wide range of cases, and it offers a tidy procedure for dissolving accusations of anachronism.

Yet I have a conviction I cannot shake. When I read past science reconstructed as a series of failed attempts to arrive at current theory, or when earlier investigators are scored by how closely they approximated what we now believe, something has gone wrong that is not merely a choice of emphasis. The people of the past are being used as props for a present-day debate rather than understood, and I find the result a worse history, not just a differently selected one. I remain convinced that there is a real difference between explaining past actors in their own terms and grading them by ours, even though I can no longer state the difference cleanly.

Walk me through what each option costs: surrendering the intuition as an artefact of disciplinary convention, amending or abandoning the selection view, or showing that the two are compatible after all. What should determine which move is right? Please also tell me which argument or consideration would change your mind about the verdict you reach, and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
