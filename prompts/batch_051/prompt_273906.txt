I've been reading about the idea that many psychiatric categories are not like the categories of ordinary medicine. On this view, when something like depression or ADHD gets a name, enters the diagnostic manual, and starts organizing clinical practice, the people who receive the label do not remain the same. They reinterpret their past in its terms, adjust their behavior, and are treated differently by others, so the classification changes the very thing it set out to describe. Ian Hacking calls this a looping effect: the people grouped under the label form an interactive kind rather than an indifferent one, and the category becomes a moving target, so the statistics gathered under an earlier version of it gradually go stale. I find this theory compelling because it explains why diagnostic boundaries drift, why some disorders seem to spread once they are named, and why receiving a diagnosis can be transformative rather than merely informative.

But I also have an intuition I cannot shake: that at least some conditions would be there, in roughly the same recognizable form, even if no one had ever named them. Schizophrenia is my clearest example. It seems to me that the voices, the disorganized thought, and the withdrawal from the world are not brought into being by the classification, and that the condition is as real as any medical disease even though no single biomarker has been found for it. If someone told me that what we call schizophrenia is a looping effect in the same sense as the way the diagnosis of multiple personality disorder once spread and then nearly disappeared, I would push back that this misses how severe and stable the condition is.

I don't want to be told which side wins. I want the strongest version of each side, and then a negotiated verdict: what would it take for my intuition to be the thing that gives way, what would it take for the theory to be the thing that gives way, and is there a reading on which psychiatric categories can loop back and reshape the people they classify without that making the underlying condition unreal or merely invented?
