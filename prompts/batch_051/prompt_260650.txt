I'm a retired physical chemist — spent my working life doing structure elucidation, NMR, mass spec, X-ray crystallography, that sort of thing. Since retiring I've been reading philosophy of physics, and a parallel keeps nagging me.

In my old field you rarely observed a molecule directly. You proposed a structure and accepted it when several methods — say a proton NMR spectrum, a high-resolution mass spectrum, an IR spectrum — all pointed at the same formula, and, just as important, no alternative structure was compatible with all the data. The data aren't independant in any clean probabilistic sense; every one of those instruments rests on the same quantum mechanics. But we still treated the coincidence of independent constraints as genuinely confirmatory.

Now I'm reading defences of string theory that seem to run the same inference. The theory hasn't been tested at the string scale, but defenders say it's (or is near enough) the unique mathematically consistent completion of the framework that already passed every test, that its parts fit the rest of physics in non-trivial ways, and that the programme has a strong track record. Some people now call this non-empirical theory confirmation.

My question is whether the resemblance is deep or just pattern-matching. If the chemistry inference is legitimate because the spectra are empirical constraints, does the physics case lose that entirely because its constraints are theoretical and mathematical? Or is that distinction shakier than I used to think, since even an NMR spectrum is theory-laden? What would need to be true on both sides for the confirmation to transfer, and can you name something the parallel would predict that turns out false?

Could you open with your verdict and then set out the mapping? Keep it under 600 words.
