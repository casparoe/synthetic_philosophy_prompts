I teach a non-credit weekly reading class at the local labor temple for people who work in the warehouses around here. Last week we read William Morris, "Useful Work versus Useless Toil," and I gave everybody a take-home question. I wrote the question myself and now I'm grading it, but I keep going back and forth on one answer. Below is the passage I gave them, then the question, then the answer. Can you grade the student's answer out of 10? I need the comments I'd actually write on the paper, point by point, including what the answer gets right and any subtle things it gets wrong. I don't want just a general impression.

The passage I gave them was:
"Thus worthy work carries with it the hope of pleasure in rest, the hope of pleasure in our using what it makes, and the hope of pleasure in our daily creative skill. All other work but this is worthless; it is slaves' work - mere toiling to live, that we may live to toil."

Question: Morris says that any work without the three hopes is worthless slaves' work. Using this passage, decide whether a worker who oversees automated palletizing machines in an Amazon-style warehouse could be doing worthy work. Show how Morris's three criteria apply, and say what you think his verdict would be.

Student's answer:
"Morris's three hopes are rest, product, and pleasure in the work itself. The warehouse overseer has rest because they have shifts, lunch breaks, and days off. They have product because the packages they help send out are genuinely useful to the customers who receive them. The problem is the third hope: watching screens and clearing jams isn't creative in the way making a chair by hand is. Since one of the three is missing, Morris would say this job is not worthy work. He would probably call it slave's work, because any modern paid job is just toiling to live. Maybe that's too extreme, but Morris seems pretty hostile to all industrial work."
