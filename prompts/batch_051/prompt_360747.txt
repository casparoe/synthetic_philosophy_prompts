So my friend and I just spent way too long arguing after I killed a moth in my kitchen, and I need someone to tell me if either of us was right. He got genuinely upset about it, saying I'd ended a life over nothing. I said if the moth counts for something, then my smart thermostat should count for a little too — it senses the room, pursues a goal, and learns our schedule. He called that a category error and said a thermostat just cant matter in the same sense as an animal, even a moth. But then neither of us could actually say what the difference is. The obvious candidate was pain, but we couldnt tell if the moth actually feels pain or is just running reflexes. The next candidate was having some kind of inner point of view, but neither of us could say how we'd know where that starts. By the end we wanted to hold two things at once: (1) the moth deserves at least a little consideration and the thermostat at most a different, non-moral kind, and (2) every feature we could name as the difference either the moth doesn't clearly have or the thermostat has in a bargain-basement version. Are those two positions actually compatible? Or is it more honest to say it's a smooth slope, with the thermostat just very far down it rather than off the scale? Is there a principled place to draw a line, or is the whole idea of a sharp line a mistake?
