Classic anti-reductionist arguments used to lean a lot on multiple realizability: the thought that pain (or believing it'll rain) could be realized by completely different physical goings-on in humans, octopuses, and Martians, so there's no one neural or chemical kind to reduce it to. That argument was always fought with hypothetical critters. But now we actual have a field that mass-produces this stuff. Train two neural nets on the same task with different architechtures or just different random seeds and you get different weights that don't line up one-to-one, yet the input-output behavior is more or less interchangeable, and nobody cares which one you deployed. Does the old multiple realizability argument survive this? I can see it going either way: maybe deep learning is the first real vindication of the argument, or maybe it shows that once multiple realizability gets this cheap, "same function, different realizer" stops tracking a scientifically useful kind and the argument looses its teeth. I want you to actually pin down which premise the deep-learning development bears on and say whether the argument needs repair, is strengthened, or is dead.
