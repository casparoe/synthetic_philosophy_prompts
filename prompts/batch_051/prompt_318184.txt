I got into a long argument with my housemate, who took exactly one political philosophy class and now talks like a seminar paper. We were going back and forth about whether a policy that just made the rich worse off, without helping anyone, could ever be worth doing, and she broke it down for me like this:

"Utilitarians just add up everyone's welfare, so they don't care at all about equality. Egalitarians say inequality itself is bad, which sounds nice, but then they're committed to saying leveling down—making the rich poorer with no benefit to the poor—is an improvement, which is obviously insane. Prioritarians are basically utilitarians who add a thumb on the scale for the worse off: they don't think inequality is bad in itself, only that a benefit matters more the worse off you are. But once you've said inequality isn't bad in itself, the only reason to prefer transfers from rich to poor is to maximize weighted welfare, so prioritarianism just collapses into utilitarianism with an extra parameter no one can justify. And since leveling down refutes egalitarianism and the prioritarian weight is arbitrary, the only view left standing is sufficientarianism, which says all that matters is whether people have enough."
