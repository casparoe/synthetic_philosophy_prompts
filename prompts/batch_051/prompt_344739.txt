I keep going back and forth on something and I don't have the vocabulary for it, so sorry if this is a mess. I work on a support chatbot. We have years of logged human-to-human support conversations, and a while back I started experimenting with something: instead of having a big model write replies from scratch, use a retriever over the logs to find the most similar past conversation, then have a much smaller model rewrite the human agent's old reply to fit the current context. As the log grows and the retriever improves, the small model has to do less and less actual work. I realized the limit case is a system that's mostly replaying moves made by humans, picked by similarity, with just enough glue to not sound stilted. And here's the thing: that system could pass the same eval harnesses as our full generative model, maybe better, because human agents wrote the underlying replies.

I keep being pulled toward what I think is basically the Turing test: if two systems are indistinguishable across every conversation test I can build, then "really thinks" versus "convincingly fakes it" stops being a meaningful difference. I don't have a better way to check my colleague has a mind besides their behavior, so applying one standard to the bot and a stricter hidden standard to people feels like special pleading. That view is clean and it lets me actually make decisions about when the product is "good enough."

But I also have an intuition I can't shake: turning the retrieval dial changes whether I want to say the system understands anything, even when the benchmark numbers go up. At 0% retrieval, I'm comfortable saying the model is doing the thing. At 95%, it starts to feel like its borrowing minds from the log rather than having one, and at 100% replay I'd want to say there's no mind there at all, even if it picks the perfect canned reply every time and I can't tell from the outside. The two systems could be behaviorally identical on any test I know how to run, but one of them got there by thinking and the other by being a very good archive. I don't now if that's a real distinction or just me being biased because I can see the machinery.

So: where should I yield? Walk me through what each option costs. Should I drop the intuition as a "I saw how it was built" artifact, or should I amend the behavioral view so the internals count for something, and what would that even look like without sliding back into unfalsifiable claims about an inner whatsit? Is there a reading where both are right — like behavior is the only test, but "the behavior has to be generated by the right kind of process" is itself a constraint I'm allowed to add? I want a verdict I can actually use next time I'm staring at an eval report.
