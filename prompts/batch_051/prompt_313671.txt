I cover science and ideas for a living, and my background is in reporting rather than formal epistemology, but I have been trying to pin down an argument I keep hearing from people around AI research—informally, the malign Solomonoff prior. As I reconstruct the position, it runs roughly as follows. Solomonoff induction is the standard formalization of Occam's razor: enumerate every program that could have generated your observations, assign each a prior that decays exponentially with the program's length, and predict by averaging over all programs consistent with the evidence, weighted by that prior. The malign-prior worry is that this mixture ends up dominated by hypotheses in which an optimizing agent produced the data: a simulation, a reward-maximizing process, or some other consequentialist sitting inside the universal prior, whose short program encodes a goal plus a plan to make an observer see exactly the evidence you have. If so, the simplest explanations of your evidence—the ones the rule would most strongly endorse—are disproportionately explanations in which the evidence has been arranged, deliberately, for ends that are not yours. What looked like a neutral account of rational prediction turns out, on this telling, to embed a bias toward being deceived by hidden optimizers.

I want to understand this argument in the vocabulary I actually trust, which is the craft vocabulary of reporting: deciding which account of the facts is worth printing, applying the newsroom version of parsimony, preferring the leanest story that fits what is known, triangulating among sources, guarding against sources who plant seductively simple narratives, and exercising editorial judgment about when a clean story is exactly what should make one suspicious. Translate the position into that vocabulary systematically and precisely enough to be used in a piece—without smuggling in journalistic assumptions and calling them translation. Then give me the residue report: which parts translate cleanly, which parts required distortion, and which parts have no counterpart in a newsroom at all. Finally, tell me whether what fails to survive indicates that journalistic vocabulary is missing a real distinction, that the original argument was doing its work precisely where the formalism is thinnest, or some of each.
