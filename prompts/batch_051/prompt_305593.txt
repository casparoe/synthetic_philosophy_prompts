I do ML compliance work at a company that sells a decision-support system to banks and hospitals, and I keep noticing something strange about how we handle explainability. When our model denies someone a loan or flags a suspicious lesion, the customer or the regulator usually requires an explanation of the decision, and what we hand over is a post-hoc artifact: SHAP feature attributions, a LIME approximation, a saliency heatmap, or a natural-language rationale that the model was fine-tuned to write. Everyone on the team knows these do not neccessarily describe the actual computation that produced the decision—the rationale can be completely at odds with what the internals are doing, which we mostly can't observe. But it passes the review, because the explanation reads as plausible to the person receiving it, and plausibility is treated as the thing that matters. We even score the explanations by asking human graders whether they find them satisfying, not whether they track the mechanism.

Meanwhile, if a doctor wrote down a diagnosis with a rationale and we later learned the rationale wasn't the real basis for the judgment, we'd call that a rationalization and a failure of professionalism. For models we seem fine with exactly that. I want to get clear on what this asymmetry commits us to. What theory of explanation, or of understanding, does the practice presuppose? Is it genuinely a pragmatic or communicative view—that an explanation's job is to make the outcome intelligible and actionable for the recipient rather than to mirror the causal process—or is it just a regulatory kludge that doesn't add up to a coherent epistemology? Where is the practice underdetermined, and what would have to change in how we build and evaluate these systems if a regulator or a hospital actually insisted that explanations be faithful to what the model did?

After the essay part, give me a JSON object with these fields: "practice" (one sentence restating the practice), "reconstructed_theory" (the implicit theory of explanation or understanding, in a sentence or two), "strict_presuppositions" (array of things the practice must assume), "merely_fits_with" (array of views it is compatible with but doesn't require), "where_underdetermined" (array of respects in which several theories would license the same practice), and "what_would_change_under_faithfulness_requirement" (array of concrete changes if explanations had to mirror the actual computation).
