I teach a graduate seminar in science studies, and for the first time I’ve been reading Ludwig Wittgenstein’s On Certainty alongside some work in hinge epistemology. I’m struck by the passage where Wittgenstein says that ordinary doubt and inquiry operate against a background of commitments that are not themselves raised as questions in that inquiry, like hinges on which a door turns but which themselves do not turn. For my next session I want to test how far this image travels. My working list of criteria for a hinge commitment is this: (1) it is not adopted on the basis of evidence, but is what makes the gathering and weighing of evidence possible within a domain; (2) its truth is not normally treated as an open question by competent practitioners, even though they can state it; (3) if a practitioner does raise it as an open question, the practice’s ordinary methods of testing and adjudication do not tell for or against it; (4) it does not function as a hypothesis whose failure would revise one local claim, but rather its withdrawal would leave an entire body of practice without its usual sense of warrant; and (5) it is held fast in practice, not merely asserted in theory.

The borderline case I want to use is the principle that no two human fingerprints are exactly alike—the uniqueness assumption that forensic friction-ridge examiners rely on when they present an identification as identifying a single source. This assumption has sometimes been defended by pointing to empirical studies, but examiners routinely treat it as a settled background condition of their practice, one that individual casework does not test; and when lawyers or statisticians ask for an error-rate for uniqueness itself, the field tends to say that question lies outside its methods. Does the fingerprint uniqueness assumption qualify as a hinge commitment on these criteria? Please go through the criteria honestly and say where the case strains the criteria; if it fails some, say whether that shows that the criteria need refining or that the case is better understood as an ordinary empirical generalization that the field has wrongly shielded from testing. And where your verdict is strongest, please say what argument or consideration would most likely change your mind about it.
