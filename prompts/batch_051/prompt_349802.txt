You are a professional philosopher of science, specialising in mechanistic explanation and causal reasoning. I am not an academic, but I read a good deal of popular science and medicine, and I have noticed that claims about "the same mechanism" are made to do very different work. One study will say that two species share the same mechanism for a behaviour; another will say that two drugs work by the same mechanism even though they bind to different receptors; a third will deny that two similar-looking behaviours share a mechanism at all. I have also seen the phrase "mechanistic anomaly detection" used for the attempt to tell effects apart when they look alike but are produced differently.

I would like a working definition of "the same mechanism": a criterion, or small set of criteria, for deciding when two systems, processes, or interventions count as having the same mechanism. Please start by brainstorming several candidate criteria before you commit, and then test your definition against each of the cases below, saying for each whether it gives the right verdict, the wrong verdict, or no verdict, and revise it if it fails. Write the answer in British English.

a. Two mechanical watches of the same make and model, ticking side by side. I regard these as the same mechanism.

b. A mechanical watch and a quartz watch, both keeping time. I regard these as not the same mechanism, despite doing the same job.

c. A bat's wing and a bird's wing, both producing powered flight. I genuinely do not know what to say here: the bone anatomy differs, but the aerodynamic logic is similar.

d. The same drug inhibiting the same receptor in two patients, where a downstream genetic difference makes the symptom improve a lot in one patient and only a little in the other. Is this the same mechanism of action?

e. A sorting algorithm running on two different computer processors. The computational procedure is identical, but the physical hardware is not.

f. A thermostat and a neuron that both use negative feedback to hold a variable stable. They seem to share a mechanism at some abstract level, but share no physical parts.
