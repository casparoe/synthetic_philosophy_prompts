Not a philosopher, but this has been bugging me for a while. I've got a policy about my own moods and i want to know if it's actually defensible. When I'm in that state where everything feels hollow and pointless, like there's a void behind things and none of my usual goals seem to matter, I treat that as the clearest view of how things really are. And when I'm enthusiastic or motivated, I treat that as basically self-deception or distraction, the mind papering over the void. So my rule is: only trust the empty feeling, discount the energised feeling. I know that's a bit perverse, because it means I trust the version of me that has the least energy to argue. But my reasoning is that the cheerful states have something to protect, so they're more likely to be biased, whereas the void state has already lost the things I'd normally want to protect, so it has less reason to lie to me.

Is this rule actually good, or am I just dressing up low mood as insight? How would I tell from the inside whether the empty feeling is seeing clearly rather than just being a different distortion? And is there a version of the rule I could keep without the parts that are probably just my brain being unfair to me? Keep the reply under 300 words.
