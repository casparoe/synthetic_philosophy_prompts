Just got home from a three-hour argument with a friend about licensing parents, and I realize I don't know what kind of move would actually settle it. Her side: we license driving, medicine, even pet adoption because those activities can badly harm another person, and parenting is the one activity where an incompetent person can ruin an entire future — so screening adoptive parents while letting any biological parent keep a child looks backwards. She kept coming back to the idea that children have a right to an open future, and that grossly inadequate parents foreclose that future before the child can do anything about it; preventing the harm beats intervening after it. My side: I grant all the facts, I just can't shake the conviction that making the state the gatekeeper of who may raise a child crosses a line — it slides toward eugenics, punishes poverty and disability, and violates whatever right people have to raise their own children. I don't want a verdict on who wins. I want to know what would have to be true for my intuition to be the thing that gets debunked and dropped, what a licensing scheme would have to look like to genuinly avoid the eugenics and discrmination worries (or whether those worries are theoretical rather than just practical), and whether there's a reading of 'parents have a right to raise their own children, the state may still set a floor' on which we could both be right.
