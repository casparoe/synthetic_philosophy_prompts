I'm a journalist working on a piece about how people estimate the durability of states and institutions, and I want a short poem that stages the collision between the inside view and the outside view. Act as a poet with a working knowledge of judgment and decision research. Write a sonnet in which the octave gives the inside view—an empire's advisers reasoning from the singular details of their own geography, institutions, and recent successes—and the volta at line 9 introduces the outside view: the reference class of comparable past states and the base rate of how long they actually lasted. Let the sestet show the base rate cutting that narrative down to size without pretending to name a date, and let the closing couplet state exactly what the outside view does and does not license. Before writing, think carefully about how the poem's images should encode the choice of reference class and what a base rate can and cannot tell a forecaster. The result should be faithful enough that a specialist in forecasting would recognize the argument, not merely the label, while a reader who has never heard of the outside view should still find it a good poem.
