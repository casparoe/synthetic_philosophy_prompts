so I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but I've ended up deep in this anyway. The book I'm writing is narrated in free indirect style -- most of my books are -- and the closer I get to the main character, the more I suspect the narration is quietly committing to what Ryle called the ghost in the machine: a private inner stage where the real thinking happens and the body just executes it. I don't want to be smuggling in a metaphysics I havent earned, but I also can't shake the feeling that the inner life is the very thing I'm trying to render, and that a dispositional account of mind flattens it.

Could you write a short dialogue between two characters, maybe in an empty college common room after a talk, where one is a Rylean about mind and the other defends the reality of occurrent inner experience? The thing I most want them to fight about is whether free indirect narration of a character's unvoiced thought -- the thing they would never say out loud -- commits a category mistake. Make both of them genuinely good; no strawmen, and have them actually answer each other's points rather than trade speeches. Let them get into the weeds: knowing-how versus knowing-that, occurrent episodes versus standing dispositions, masked dispositions that never actually get exercised, the visitor who sees all the colleges and still asks where the university is, and whether the defender of inner experience can turn the category-mistake charge back on the Rylean. It doesn't need to resolve -- I'd actually prefer a real impasse to a handshake. And if one of them says something I could steal for the novel, all the better, but dont force it.
