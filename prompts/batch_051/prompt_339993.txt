Next week I'm giving a colloquium talk at a philosophy department and defending a semicompatibilist line: the Consequence Argument may be sound, so determinism rules out alternative possibilities, but moral responsibility doesn't need alternative possibilities — what matters is how the action was actually produced, whether it answered to reasons I recognized as reasons and came from mechanisms that are properly mine. The audience includes two hard incompatibilists who think responsibility goes down with alternative possibilities, a cluster of Fischer-style compatibilists who share my conclusion but think I'm crazy to give away the Consequence Argument, and one libertarian who thinks Frankfurt cases were always a distraction. Walk me through the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each of those people would actually put them, with a short reply to each and where the reply is weakest, what I should concede, what I should hold, and the one question I should be praying no one asks.
