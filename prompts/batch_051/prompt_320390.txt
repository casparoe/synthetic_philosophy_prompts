A few of us at the meditation center I attend started a small group for people trying to think through the ethics behind Buddhist practice rather than just absorb the standard talks. We decided each person would write down what they actually believe, and then the rest of us would go through the list and test whether it holds together. I volunteered to go first, but reading over what I wrote, I realize I can no longer tell which of my commitments are doing the real work and which are just along for the ride. I would like someone to audit the list as a whole.

Here is what I wrote:

1. What ultimately matters in ethics is suffering and its relief. An action is right or wrong only in virtue of what it does to the amount of suffering in the world.
2. At the same time, the moral quality of an action is decided by the intention or state of mind behind it: an act that comes from greed, hatred, or confusion is wrong even if it happens to reduce suffering, and an act that comes from generosity, compassion, or clear seeing is right even if it happens to misfire badly.
3. Karma is best understood as a natural law running through intention: the states of mind you act from build habits, and those habits ripen as suffering or ease for you and for the people around you.
4. I should care about all sentient beings impartially. The suffering of a stranger or an animal weighs the same as my own or my family's, once I am seeing clearly.
5. There is no self beneath any of this: no owner of intentions, no recipient of karmic results, just a stream of causes and conditions.
6. Because of that, the only project I can genuinely take on is working on my own mind, and that has to come before trying to fix the world. An untrained mind cannot reliably calculate consequences, and compassionate-sounding actions from an unexamined state usually make things worse.
7. A person who has actually uprooted greed, hatred, and confusion is no longer bound by the same outer rules as everyone else. The identical act that would be a violation of the precepts for me could be blameless, even necessary, for them, because it could not come from an unwholesome root.

Please give me a committed audit of this list. I want to know which of these commitments are in direct conflict, which only appear to conflict and why the apparent conflict dissolves, how severe each real conflict is, and what single change to the list would leave the strongest position standing. I would rather be told I have to give one of them up than be offered a menu of possible readings.
