I am a retired physical chemist, and in the last few years I have been reading my way into moral philosophy. I have become preoccupied with a question about well-being: whether pleasures and pains belong on a single scale. I have worked myself towards a view I call the two-register account of hedonic exchange, and I would welcome an honest assessment of it; I find the view increasingly plausible, which is exactly why I distrust my own judgement.

The core of the view is that hedonic comparison proceeds in two registers rather than one. Beneath a threshold of intensity — the ordinary register of headaches, small comforts, frustrations and modest enjoyments — pleasures and pains are commensurable in the familiar way: a toothache can be made up for by a good dinner, a month of tedium offset by an afternoon of real delight, and several mild burdens can jointly outweigh a single mild benefit. Above the threshold sit what I call severe states: excruciating pain, panic, despair, and on the positive side rapture, ecstasy, and the deep relief that follows the ending of a great fear. My central claim is that no amount of below-threshold pleasure can compensate for even one episode of above-threshold pain, whereas above-threshold states can offset one another at fairly stable exchange rates. A unit of pain therefore has no single exchange rate; it has one rate beneath the threshold and another above it.

I am aware that this sounds like lexical threshold negative utilitarianism — the view that above a certain severity, averting pain takes strict priority over any amount of pleasure — and perhaps it just is that view with an unnecessary addition. What I want to know is whether the second exchange rate does any genuine work, whether the boundary between the registers can be drawn non-arbitrarily, and whether aggregation across the threshold causes the view to collapse from within. Please give the three or four strongest objections, indicate the nearest existing ideas by name if you can, and say whether any of the objections is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable. Please lead with your bottom line in your first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
