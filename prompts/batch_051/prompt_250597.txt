I've been going around in circles trying to define mental illness, and Szasz's line that the whole category is a myth keeps nagging at me. His point, roughly, was that real diseases have some bodily lesion or pathology you can point to, while what we call mental illnesses are just difficult stretches of life that we've medicalized. I don't buy that — schizophrenia, or a melancholic depression that keeps someone in bed for a month, sure look like more than a difficult stretch of life — but I realize I can't state a criterion that seperates them from ordinary unhappiness. So give me a working definition of mental illness (or mental disorder, if you think that's the better category) that gets these cases right: schizophrenia; a depression severe enough that someone can't work or get out of bed for a month; ordinary grief a month after losing a parent; ADHD; heroin addiction; severe social anxiety that keeps someone housebound; and antisocial personality disorder, where someone habitually deceives and exploits others and feels no guilt. I'm fairly confident about the first two and about ordinary grief, though I know the diagnostic manuals have blurred that line, and I genuinely don't know what to do with addiction or the antisocial personality disorder. Propose the definition, run it through all seven, and tell me where it surprises you or where you'd have to bite a bullet.
