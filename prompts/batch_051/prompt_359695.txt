I'm putting together a three-hour final exam for an upper-level seminar on Victorian political thought, and I want one essay question that seperates students who have actually read Mill's *The Subjection of Women* from those who just know the standard line about equality. It will be closed book, but students will get the following passage from Chapter 1 of Mill's *The Subjection of Women* (1869):

The object of this Essay is to explain as clearly as I am able, the grounds of an opinion which I have held from the very earliest period when I had formed any opinions at all on social or political matters, and which, instead of being weakened or modified, has been constantly growing stronger by the progress of reflection and the experience of life: That the principle which regulates the existing social relations between the two sexes—the legal subordination of one sex to the other—is wrong in itself, and now one of the chief hindrances to human improvement; and that it ought to be replaced by a principle of perfect equality, admitting no power or privilege on the one side, nor disability on the other.

The question should force them to interpret this passage rather than just rehearse Mill's general argument. In particular, I'd like it to get at Mill's treatment of appeals to 'nature' as a defence of women's subordination. I don't want the question to be answerable by saying "Mill says what we call nature is often custom." I want it to push on the relation between the opening claim that the subordination is wrong in itself and the later argument that we cannot know what women's "nature" is until they have been allowed the same conditions as men.

Please give me: (a) the exam question, (b) a model answer, and (c) a marking scheme. The marking scheme should identify the specific moves a first-class answer must make, and should name at least three identifiable student mistakes (e.g. confusing legal subordination with social custom, or treating Mill as if he denies any biological differences). Say what marks each mistake would lose. Total mark: 25.
