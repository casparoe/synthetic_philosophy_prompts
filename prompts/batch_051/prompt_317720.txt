I'm the student rep on my school district's accountability committee, and I got assigned to draft the memo we're voting on Thursday. The superintendant wants to cut chronic absenteeism, but instead of holding schools to their raw rate (which basically tracks poverty), she proposed tying part of each high school's discretionary budget to how much they lower their rate from the previous year. The pitch is that year-over-year improvement is fairer to schools with harder populations. The pushback is that any budget target will just get gamed: schools coding chronically absent kids as on independent study, principals pressuring teachers not to mark kids absent, that kind of thing. One member keeps bringing up Goodhart's law and says the improvement version is actually worse than the raw-rate version, because schools could sandbag their baseline year and then rack up easy gains. Another member says that's too cynical and the district should at least try something. I need the memo to recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and it has to engage the gaming worry seriously instead of just waving it off with "targets get gamed." Whichever way it lands, the memo should address the strongest version of the other side's argument, so the committee doesn't just split on vibes. If there's a version of this policy that could survive the gaming problem, the memo should say what it is and what guardrails it needs.
