Analyze the Zapruder film of the Kennedy assassination through the lens of the transparency thesis in the philosophy of photography: the view, usually associated with Kendall Walton, that photographs are transparent in the sense that when you look at one you literally see the photographed object or scene, not merely a depiction of it, because the image is produced by a mechanical, belief-independent causal process rather than by a person's representing it. I want a careful mapping of the film onto that thesis, not a summary of either. What exactly does the transparency thesis commit its holder to about someone watching the film — that they saw the fatal shot, in anything like the sense that a bystander in Dealey Plaza saw it? Where does the thesis do explanatory work for this case, and where does it strain? Work through at least the following: the camera's single vantage point and handheld instability, the lens and frame rate, the silence of the footage, the fact that it is an amateur home movie rather than evidence prepared for an inquiry, the way the film has circulated through stills and crops (including frame 313) and has been stabilized and enhanced, and the long-running interpretive disagreement about what the footage shows — for instance, the disagreement about the direction of the head movement after the final shot and what it implies about the number or direction of the shots. Run the analysis in both directions: what does the transparency thesis illuminate about the film's status as evidence, and what does this episode reveal about the thesis — does it show that seeing through a photograph can tolerate perspective, delay, and fallibility, or does it show that the thesis collapses into a claim about reliable photographic evidence and does no independent work? Be disciplined about marking the difference between the film showing the event and the viewer seeing the event, and say plainly where you end up.
