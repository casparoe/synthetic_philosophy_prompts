Before my reading group meets I need a working definition of 'sentience' I can actually defend, because I keep changing my mind every time I read a new paper. The cases I want it to sort out, ordered roughly from clear to not-clear: a healthy adult human; a dog; an octopus (about half a billion neurons, most of them in its arms); a honeybee (just under a million neurons); a jumping spider, which has a tiny brain but apparently does detour planning; C. elegans, a worm with 302 neurons that still curls away from noxious chemicals; an oyster; a Venus flytrap; a thermostat with a set point and a feedback loop; and an LLM prompted to output 'I am in pain.' Plus one wrench: a person in deep dreamless sleep — they aren't sentient at that instant, but I'd be appaled if the definition made them lose moral status. I'd be unhappy if the dog or the octopus came out non-sentient, and I'd be suspicious if the flytrap, thermostat, or LLM came out sentient. The bee, spider, worm, and oyster I genuinely have no settled view on: I want the definition to force an answer there, or to say clearly why the case is ill-posed. I also suspect 'sentient' is really two ideas glued together — the having of any experience at all, and the being the kind of thing whose experiences count morally. If you think that's right, give me the two definitions and run them through each of my cases, telling me which ones switch columns. If not, give me your single definition, run it through each case, and tell me how it handles the sleeping person without quietly slipping in a capacity/occurrence distinction.
