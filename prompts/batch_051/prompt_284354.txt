I've got an argument a friend sent me that seems to show Solomonoff induction doesn't actually ground Occam's razor, it just relocates the language-dependence problem into the choice of universal Turing machine. I can't tell where it goes wrong, or wether it even goes wrong. Quick background in case it's not obvious: Solomonoff induction fixes a universal Turing machine U, and assigns prior probability 2^{-|p|} to each prefix-free program p that generates a hypothesis's predictions on U. The invariance theorem for Kolmogorov complexity says that switching to another universal machine V changes all the complexities by at most some constant c depending only on U and V, so people say the prior is machine-independent up to a constant. My friend's argument:

(1) Pick U and define the Solomonoff prior the usual way.
(2) For any other universal machine V, there's a fixed c with |K_U(h) - K_V(h)| ≤ c for every hypothesis h.
(3) But V is up to you, and nothing in the invariance theorem caps how much extra information V can have built in. All the theorem requires is that V still be universal, so for any h* you care about, you can build V with h* already built into its machinery.
(4) In particular, build V like this: on any program starting with 1, simulate U on the rest of the bits (which guarantees universality); on the one-bit program 0, hardwire and run h*. Then K_V(h*) is at most 1, even if K_U(h*) was enormous.
(5) Under V, the program 0 has prior weight 1/2, so h* gets at least half the prior. And as long as the data so far are consistent with h*, h* keeps at least half the posterior no matter how much data you've seen, since the total weight of all programs consistent with the data can't exceed the total weight of all programs, which is at most 1.
(6) So for any computable hypothesis you want to promote, you can make it look like the simplest and best-supported hypothesis for any amount of data consistent with it, just by switching reference machines.
(7) Therefore Solomonoff induction hasn't solved the grue/bleen problem; it's just moved the arbitrary choice of language into an arbitrary choice of UTM, and the razor is no more objective than it was before.

Where should a defender of Solomonoff induction push back? If the right move is to reject a step, name it, say why it looked fine, and spell out what rejecting it commits you to. If you think (1)-(6) are all sound, then say what (7) tries to get that it doesn't earn, and whether a defender can accept (1)-(6) and still say Occam's razor is objective in a sense worth wanting.
