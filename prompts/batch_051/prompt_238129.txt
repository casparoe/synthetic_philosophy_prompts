Over the past several years I have followed the public dispute about removing or recontextualizing monuments to morally compromised historical figures — Confederate statues in the United States, the Rhodes Must Fall campaign, the toppling of Edward Colston's statue in Bristol, and streets and buildings named for slave traders and colonial officials elsewhere. What puzzles me is how little the two sides move despite how much factual ground they share. They largely agree about what the honored figures did and about the suffering those actions caused. What they disagree about seems to be something further back: what a monument is, and what it does.

Here is my working hypothesis, which you should test rather than accept. I suspect the standoff is really several disputes folded into one. One is about meaning: does a monument's significance reside in the intent of the people who erected it, in the convention governing the act of commemoration at the time, or in the social meaning it carries now? And does a standing monument assert that its subject is still worthy of honor, or only record that a community once thought so? A second is about collective identity: are present-day citizens the same collective agent that performed the honoring, and do they inherit responsibility for that endorsement? A third is about standpoint: is reappraising a past figure genuine moral progress, or an anachronistic projection of present standards backward? A fourth may be empirical and hidden: does removing or recontextualizing a monument actually do anything about the present-day harms its opponents cite, or does it merely move the site of conflict?

I would like a diagnosis of the structure of this impasse, not a survey of positions. Which of these candidate disagreements are doing the real work, which are red herrings, and which, if any, are merely verbal? Are the two sides in effect answering different questions under one heading — one asking what keeping a monument says now, the other asking whether it is permissible to dismantle a historical artifact? Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a distinction, an empirical finding, a procedural fix, a change of question, or an argument that convergence should not be expected. If your diagnosis implies no convergence, say so plainly.
