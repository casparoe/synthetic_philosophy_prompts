I serve on my university's Academic Misconduct Panel, which is due to decide a plagiarism case next week. Our regulations state that a finding of plagiarism requires two separate findings: first, that the submitted work reproduces another author's words or ideas without adequate attribution; second, that the reproduction was deliberate rather than accidental. The panel of three will vote on each finding separately before voting on the charge as a whole. The case concerns a doctoral student whose literature review contains several sentences very close to a published review article, along with an unattributed summary of a second paper. In our preliminary discussion we have split in a way that is making me question the procedure itself. One member accepts the first finding but not the second, because the student's notes suggest carelessness rather than intent. A second member accepts the second finding but not the first, because the wording is arguably too loose to count as reproduction. I am the third member, and I believe both findings are made out. So a majority accepts the first finding, a majority accepts the second finding, and yet a majority would reject the charge if we were to vote on it directly.

I was arguing about this with a friend last night, and she insisted the answer is obvious: the panel should simply vote on the charge, since that is the question we are actually asked, and any procedure that allows a finding of plagiarism when a majority of the panel would reject it is indefensible. I am not so sure. Issue-by-issue voting is what our regulations appear to require, and it stops each member from smuggling a dissenting view of the evidence into the verdict. I gather this is an instance of what the literature calls the discursive dilemma, but I need something the panel can act on rather than a survey of positions.

Please draft a short written memo for circulation to the other two panel members before our meeting on Thursday. It should recommend a concrete decision procedure for this case, with an actionable bottom line and a time frame; it should explain what the discursive dilemma shows about issue-by-issue voting; and it should give a fair and serious treatment of my friend's position, which I expect the second member to raise in the meeting. Please write the memo in British English.
