I spent forty years in physics, and "theory of everything" was always in the air — half in-joke, half dead serious. Now that I'm retired and have finally started reading philosophy of science, I keep meeting it again, usually doing the work of an argument-ender: physics is converging on a final theory, and when we have it we'll know, and doubting that is just contrarianism. But when I push people on what exact claim they're making, I get different answers: all four forces unified? every physical fact in principle derivable from one set of equations? no more genuine surprises, ever? something about the universe being mathematical? I'd like you to seperate the precise theses hiding in that phrase, say which are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, and which are actually false, and then tell me which reading really does the work when someone uses it to argue that physics could be finished and that we'd know it when we see it.
