Posting this here because I've watched this argument run in circles for years and I now suspect it's stuck for a structural reason rather than because anyone is failing to steelman the other side.

The debate is the old democratic-theory boundary problem: who gets to count as part of the demos in the first place? If you say everyone affected by a decision should get a say, you've just punted the question, because you have to decide who counts as "affected," how heavily, and by whose judgment. If you say everyone subject to the laws, you run into the fact that climate policy and migration law reach across borders, and "subject to the laws" has a threshold problem of its own. If you say the existing citizenry decides its own future boundaries, that's circular: the current in-group can keep itself in power by choosing who gets included later. And if you say some external principle picks the right demos, you've stopped letting democracy determine it's own preconditions. So every intuitive answer either begs the question, bootstraps circularly, or appeals to an outside authority that itself needs justifying.

What I find strange is that nobody moves. The arguments have been restated for decades with no new evidence, just sharper formulations. My working theory is that the camps aren't answering the same question. Some are answering a normative question about who has a legitimate claim to be heard. Some are answering a practical coordination question about which boundary rule is stable and implementable. Some are answering a conceptual question about what "the people" could even refer to. And the standard principles — all-affected, all-subjected, membership by consent — end up doing triple duty as answers to all three, so a counterexample to the normative reading gets deflected by flipping to the coordination reading, and nobody notices the switch.

So here's what I want: a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it substantive, with one principle genuinely more right? Is it mostly verbal, with the sides meaning different things by "belonging" or "affected"? Is there a hidden empirical claim about thresholds doing the work? Or is this an ill-posed question wearing a well-posed costume? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a new way of asking the boundary question, or an honest argument that no progress should be expected? Diagnose the structure of the disagreement itself; don't survey the positions. Hard cap: 600 words.
