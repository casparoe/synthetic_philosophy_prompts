I recently started grading for an introductory metaphysics course and need a second opinion on one exam answer before I return it. The exam question was: "Explain why Bradley's regress is thought to be a serious problem for relational accounts of predication, and assess whether Frege's notion of unsaturatedness dissolves the difficulty."

The student wrote:

"Bradley's regress arises for any analysis of a's being F into two independently existing items, the particular a and the universal F, joined by a relation of instantiation. If instantiation is a genuine relation, then a and F must be related to it in just the way they were originally supposed to be related to one another, so a further relation is required to link instantiation to a and F, and so on without end. The regress, which is in essence Plato's Third Man argument applied to instantiation, shows that any relational account of predication is caught in a vicious infinite regress and cannot explain the unity of a fact. Frege's response is to treat concepts as unsaturated: a concept contains a gap that an object fills. If the same kind of incompleteness is granted, in reverse orientation, to both concept and object, then the two can complete one another directly, no further relation is needed, and the regress is stopped. So Frege's solution succeeds, provided the copula is not treated as a relation."
