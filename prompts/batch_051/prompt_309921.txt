Here's the test I was taught for whether something outside your head is literally part of your cognitive system, not just a tool: (1) it's reliably available and you invoke it often, (2) you can get at the information easily when needed, (3) you more or less automatically endorse what you get from it, and (4) the information in it was consciously endorsed by you at some earlier point. Now consider someone with a stutter who runs an app that plays their own voice back to them with about a sixty-milisecond delay, and as long as it's running they speak fluently. They don't read anything off it, don't retrieve stored information, and can't say why it helps; it just does something in their speech loop. Is the app part of their cognitive system? Go through the criteria honestly; where the fit is bad, say whether that's because of the criteria or because of the case, and whether the test needs to be changed to cover real-time, non-memory cognition.
