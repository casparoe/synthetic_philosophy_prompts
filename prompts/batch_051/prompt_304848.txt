Okay so I spent last night arguing with a friend about moral luck, and I'm less sure than I was when we started. He kept saying there's no such thing because you can only be praised or blamed for what was under your control. I think that's wrong, but every time I tried to state what moral luck actually is, it fell apart on some case or another.

Can you give me a working definition of moral luck that handles these four cases? I want the definition to say something definite about each one, not just "it depends", and if one of your verdicts surprises you, tell me which one and why.

The cases:
1. A driver has one beer too many and swerves into a tree, hurting no one. Another driver has the same amount and briefly closes his eyes at the wrong moment, hitting and killing a pedestrian. Are they morally different?
2. Someone grows up in a house where honesty was constantly modeled and winds up honest. Another person with, as far as anyone can tell, the same starting temperament grows up surrounded by fraud and becomes a fraudster. Do we judge their characters differently?
3. Two people each put arsenic in someone's morning coffee with the same intentions. One victim gets an unexpected call and leaves before drinking; the other dies. Is the unsuccessful poisoner less culpable than the successful one?
4. A teller with a dishonest streak works at a bank with loose oversight and easy opportunities, and embezzles. Another teller with the same streak works at a bank where oversight makes it impossible, and never steals. Is the second one a better person?

I'd like your answer as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Make point 1 the definition, and use points 2–5 to run it through each case in turn, saying where it holds up, where it strains, and which case you're least confident about.
