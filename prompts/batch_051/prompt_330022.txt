I work at a start-up that uses a big language model to filter insurance claims. The regulator now wants us to be able to say why the model rejected someone, so a colleague built a second, much smaller model that reads the big model's internal activations and spits out a short English explanation of what the big model was doing. It predicts the big model's decisions quite well, but we don't understand the little model internally either; we just read the explanations it produces. One colleague says that's fine, because the smaller model is a translator into language we understand, and we don't need to open up the translator's brain. Another says it's a con: we've swapped one black box for another, and if the explainer is itself opaque, nothing has actually been explained. Who's right? Is there a genuine regress if we then try to explain the explainer, or can the chain legitimately stop somewhere? I'm after the conceptual issue, not a compliance answer.
