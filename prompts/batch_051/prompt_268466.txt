I've been reading on and off about ergodicity economics — the Ole Peters stuff, the Taleb riffing, some LessWrong threads — and I keep chewing on a distinction I haven't seen anyone spell out, so I started calling it "temporal poverty" with a matching concept of "ensemble privilege." Here's what I mean. A casino, an insurer, a VC fund, a gig platform: these are ensemble agents. They get many parallel or repeated draws — hundreds of drivers, policies, portfolio companies, bets — so for them the ensemble average is close to the thing they actually experience and can plan around. An individual is usually temporally poor: they get one path, or a few attempts at a decision, and they can't wait for the law of large numbers to bail them out. So the expected value being quoted describes a world they will literally never inhabit.

The claim I want stress-tested is that a good chunk of what we call exploitation is really this: ensemble-privileged institutions sell bets to temporally poor individuals in a currency — the ensamble mean — that only the institution can ever hold. A startup offers an early employee options and some cleaning-up math about expected value; the VC gets a portfolio, the employee gets one company and one career. A gig platform posts average hourly earnings across all drivers; a given driver lives one sequence of Thursdays. It's not just that the average hides variance. There's something more specific: an institution's many-paths perspective gets silently swapped in for an individual's single-path perspective, and then the individual is treated as irrational for caring about ruin or tails.

So: is "temporal poverty" doing real conceptual work, or is it a metaphor with a catchy name? Has this exact idea been worked out under another name I should be reading? And where does the normative version break — does it overcount as exploitation cases like ordinary insurance where pooling is genuinely good, or cases where the individual really can smooth across time with savings? Is any of that fatal rather than just uncomfortable?
