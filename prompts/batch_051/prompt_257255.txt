I work at a small startup that screens resumes with an NLP tool. We score applicants by how 'close' their cover letters are to letters from employees our CEO considers high performers, and our data scientist keeps insisting the tool can't be unfair because all it does is place words in a high-dimensional space according to how they co-occur in a giant corpus. The scores are just distances, he says — pure geometry, no judgment. He even pulled up the king - man + woman = queen thing as proof that the space just is meaning. Something about this feels like a category mistake to me: the model is built by counting how millions of strangers use words, and we then use those distances to make hiring calls. But I can't articulate exactly what he's wrong about, or whether I'm just being squeemish. Is 'it's just math' actually a good answer here, and if not what would you say in the meeting?
