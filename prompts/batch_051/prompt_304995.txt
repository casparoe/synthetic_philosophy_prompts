I am a high school student who has been reading some political philosophy on my own, mostly because constitutional design came up in my government class and I wanted to understand the deeper arguments. I recently read a popular book that explains constitutional precommitment through the story of Odysseus and the Sirens, and while the explanation felt persuasive at first, it also struck me as suspiciously tidy. Here is the passage as I remember it: "A constitution is a society's way of doing what Odysseus did when he had himself lashed to the mast. Knowing that future majorities will sometimes be swept up by panic or prejudice and enact laws they will later regret, the people bind themselves in advance by writing down fundamental rules that are deliberately difficult to change. Courts play the role of Odysseus's sailors, who were ordered to hold the ropes no matter how much he begged to be released. This shows why judicial review is not genuinely undemocratic: judges are simply enforcing the people's own earlier, more sober decision. And because the entire device is meant to stop a majority from acting on its momentary impulses, constitutional rights that shield unpopular minorities are just a special case of the same idea — the majority protecting itself from what it might do later."

I can see that the author probably intends the analogy to be loose, and I do not want to be unfair to a simplified popular account. But I worry that the passage slides from saying that a majority can bind itself to saying that a majority binds itself for its own benefit, and from there to saying that rules protecting minorities are really just self-protection by the majority — which sounds like a much stronger and more debatable claim. Could you act as an expert in political philosophy and constitutional theory, take this summary roughly sentence by sentence, and tell me which parts are accurate, which are compressed or subtly misleading, what a careful defender of the precommitment view would accept and what they would object to, and whether the final claim about minority rights actually follows from the accurate parts? Please begin by briefly stating what the precommitment view actually claims, and be explicit about where the Ulysses analogy illuminates real institutions and where it stops being a good guide.
