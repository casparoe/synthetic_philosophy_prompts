I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, but a lunch argument between two friends has been buzzing in my head and I need someone to referee it. Jan read a blog post about Stoicism and opened with the claim that negative emotions about things you can't control are a kind of loss-twice-over: the event costs you once, and then the grief or anger costs you again, so the rational move is to treat most emotions as noise and train yourself to drop them fast. Rob said that's like calling a smoke alarm 'fire plus extra noise' — the grief isn't a second, optional cost stacked on top of the loss; it's how the loss registers as mattering to you, and somebody who stopped feeling it wouldn't have gained anything, they'd have lost the ability to care. Jan came back that he was blurring two things the Stoics themselves kept apart: there's the involuntary first jolt — the pang when you hear bad news — which nobody thinks you can fully prevent, and then there's the assent, the judgment that the thing is genuinely terrible and the decision to keep replaying it. She said she's only against the second part; the jolt is just data. Rob said once she admits the jolt is unavoidable and feels bad, she's in trouble: either the jolt itself is a harm, in which case even the ideal life is full of harm and her view starts to leak, or the jolt isn't a harm, in which case she's drawn the line at the wrong place, because the suffering that actually matters — the part where you feel the loss — is downstream of it. He said the first-movement/assent distinction isn't neutral; it's just the conclusion dressed up as a premise. Jan said that's not a refutation, just relabeling: of course the line is where the work is, the claim is precisely that you can weaken the downstream part with practice, the same way an engineer can read an error log without panicking; the fact that the log printed is no argument for also panicking. Rob said she's using 'data' to smuggle in exactly the picture he's rejecting — data only matters to a system that already cares about some variable; if the jolt is neutral information, there's nothing to drop, and if it isn't neutral, she owes an account of what makes a jolt harmful while the full emotion is just noise.

I honestly couldn't referee it and their both still waiting for me to pick a side. Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about which specific point, where each of them overreaches, and who is more right overall?
