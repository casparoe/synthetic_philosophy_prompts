I've been trying to get my head round the idea that normally you shouldn't be punished unless you had a guilty mind — mens rea, I think its called — but that there are these "strict liability" offences where you get fined even if you didn't mean any harm and weren't careless. I've never studied law or philosophy, so I might be using the terms a bit loosely. The usual examples are things like a corner shop being done for selling booze or a vape to a kid who showed a convincing fake ID, or a business being fined for a spill it couldn't really have prevented. After going back and forth with my sister about it, I wrote down my own rule for when punishing without fault is OK. My conditions are: (1) the person freely chose to do the activity, and it isn't something they basically had to do to get by; (2) they stood to gain from it, so the fine can be seen as a cost of doing that thing rather than a punishment of a bad person; (3) the harm is a foreseeable, ordinary sort of risk of the activity rather than a freak accident; and (4) there are realistic steps they could have taken to lower the chance of the harm, so the rule nudges people toward taking them.

But then two cases came up and now I'm stuck. First: a woman runs a phone-repair stall and buys batteries from a wholesaler whose paperwork all checks out, except the certificates turn out to be forged and the batteries are counterfeits. One of them overheats while charging overnight in the shop, starts a fire and damages the flat above. She did everything a careful small trader would realistically do, but under my rule she'd probably still be fined: the work is optional, she makes her living from it, battery fires are a known risk, and I suppose she could have tested every single battery, though nobody actually does. Second: a bloke keeps two beehives in his back garden, mostly because he likes bees and gives the honey to neighbours. One hot day the colony swarms, and a child next door who's badly allergic to bee stings gets stung and ends up in hospital. Beekeeping is optional, he gets a bit of honey out of it, swarming is a known risk of bees — but it feels off to lump him in with the repairer, and the harm only happened because of the child's rare allergy.

So here's what I want: go through these two under my checklist, condition by condition, and say whether each qualifies for punishment without fault. And, more importantly, does either case show that one of my conditions is doing too much work, or that I'm missing a condition — something about whether the person can realistically insure against it or spread the cost, maybe? I keep flip-flopping between "if nobody was careless, why is anyone being punished?" and "the injured person shouldn't have to just absorb the loss either." Please finish with a short verdict in the format \answer{...} — something like "both qualify", "neither qualifies", "only the repairer qualifies", or "criteria need changing".
