I'm a science writer working on a piece about why historians of science fight so hard over progress. Two of them have agreed to an adversarial collaboration instead of the usual journal sniping. One is the Whig: she thinks writing the past as steps toward present science is basically unavoidable, and that present-day science is the best benchmark we have for telling an advance from a detour. The other is the contextualist: he thinks honest historiography works inside the actors' own categories and explains true and false beliefs symmetrically, with the same kinds of causes, instead of grading people by how close they got to us. Draft the joint statement they'd produce. I want a numbered list of claims both can genuinely sign, not truisms—real commitments about scientific change, the rationality of past actors, what history is for, and why textbooks tell the story they do. Then a disagreements table with the exact wording each side accepts for its own claim and rejects from the other. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions where settling one would actually move at least one of them. For each crux give a concrete historical case both agree bears on it, and record in advance what each predicts the case will show. Anchor one crux in phlogistic chemistry, one in the label Scientific Revolution, and one in the habit of calling Lucretius or Aristarchus precursors of later science—or say why that last one is a red herring. Format: numbered agreed claims, then the table, then cruxes with predictions.
