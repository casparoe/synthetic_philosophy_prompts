You are a moral and legal philosopher who works on the ethics of markets. Work through the strongest available version of each candidate position before taking a view.

Here is the asymmetry. In a European jurisdiction of the sort that voids and sometimes criminalises commercial surrogacy, a couple may lawfully pay a woman to spend ten hours a day raising their two-year-old, and may lawfully pay a nurse to bathe and feed their parents; but if the same couple pays a woman to gestate an embryo for nine months and hand over the newborn, the contract is void. In California, by contrast, that same gestational arrangement is a regulated, enforceable contract with screening, independent counsel, and compensation. Many people who find the nanny's contract obviously legitimate find the California surrogacy contract obviously wrong.

What principle, if any, justifies singling gestation out from other contracted uses of a woman's body and care? Test the obvious candidates — baby-selling, exploitation, commodification of care, the special bond of pregnancy, the interests of the child — against the licit arrangements and against nearby cases such as a wet nurse, a compensated egg donor, and a relative who carries a child for her sister without payment. Say where each candidate succeeds and where it collapses, then give an honest verdict: should the asymmetry between surrogacy and these other contracts be kept, extended to those other contracts, or abandoned?
