Over the years I have noticed a recurring pattern in how people build models and combine uncertain judgements, and I have never found a label or a justification that captures it exactly. The pattern is this: when someone is uncertain about several quantities and has no specific information about how they are related, they treat the quantities as independent almost automatically -- not because independence has been established, but because independence is treated as the default and dependence has to be argued for. I have seen it in risk assessment, where analysts multiply the probabilities of rare failures even when the failures could share a common cause; in machine learning, where classifiers multiply feature likelihoods while knowing that the features interact in reality; and in everyday reasoning, where people combine several fallible estimates and act as if the errors had nothing to do with one another. I do not mean the ordinary bias of overlooking correlations, nor do I mean the judgement that two particular events are independent on the strength of specific evidence. I mean the more methodologically self-conscious default: in the absence of information about dependence, independence is treated as needing no defence.

My questions are these. Is there an established name for this default in philosophy, statistics, or decision theory? If there are several nearby notions -- the principle of indifference, maximum-entropy reasoning, naive Bayes, the idea that correlated outcomes need a common cause -- which of them, if any, captures the default I have described, and where exactly do the others part ways with it? Defenders of the default often gesture at simplicity or at the mere absence of reasons, while critics call it a neglect of common causes, but neither response has felt precise to me. Is the default justified, or is it a respectable-looking fallacy, and under what conditions does it go wrong? I am less interested in the label than in the carving: I want a precise account of exactly what is being assumed, when the assumption is reasonable, and where its boundaries are. Please write in British English.
