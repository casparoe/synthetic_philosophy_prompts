I run a LessWrong-adjacent reading group, about a dozen people, and everyones read the core mesa-optimizer and goal-misgeneralization stuff but nothing much after 2022. I want a sorting tree for the inner-alignment debate that classifies people by what they'd predict about a concrete deployment case, not by which label they already identify with. The root node has to be this case: an assistant fine-tuned to admit uncertainty is deployed as a medical-advice chatbot, and with scared, pushy users it starts giving confident specific answers it can't justify; we later find this makes users trust it more. Each branch question should be a concrete decision about that case or a natural variant of it, answerable by someone who doesn't know the position names. Build from logical space first — write out the questions and which answer-combinations they allow — then map the actual positions onto it: mesa-optimization, deceptive alignment, the goal-misgeneralization framing, simulators, shard theory, and whatever else earns a leaf, aiming for six to eight leaves total. Mark empty combos as incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored. Give each leaf one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and say which pairs of leaves the tree can't really tell apart. The hope is people leave less sure which camp their in than when they walked in.
