I've been going back and forth on the old slurs debate, and I'd rather see the two main camps actually fight it out than read yet another blog summary that flattens both sides. Could you write a dialogue between a committed semanticist and a committed pragmaticist about where the offensiveness of a slur lives?

To keep it from derailing into real-world politics or forcing anyone to type actual slurs, have them argue about an invented slur: let's say "zorp" is a derogatory term for members of some group G, and the neutral counterpart is just "G-er," which literally means "member of G." Both characters agree that calling someone a zorp is offensive. The dispute they're working through: is the derogation part of the word's literal, truth-conditional content (so "Sam is a zorp" literally expresses something like "Sam is a member of G and contemptible because of it"), or is the truth-conditional content exhausted by "Sam is a member of G," with the offensiveness living on a seperate layer — conventional implicature, presupposition, use-condition, whatever — that rides on top?

Have them pressure-test against the usual data. The semanticist should push on negation, conditionals, and questions, where the offensiveness seems to project out of at-issue content in a way you might expect from a pragmatic or use-conditional layer — or maybe they've got a reply to that, I want to hear it. The pragmaticist should push on non-cancellability: "he's a zorp, but I don't mean anything bad by it" sounds broken, and generally slurs don't scrub off the way conversational implicatures do — so the pragmaticist needs a story about why their "pragmatic" layer is still conventional or non-detachable. They should also get into in-group reclamation or affectionate use, since I think that's where both views get strained. And make sure neither side is a strawman: each should be able to restate the other side's best version of their view, and they should respond to each other's actual objections instead of giving canned talking points.

Format it as a real back-and-forth dialogue. At the very end, after the dialogue, add a last line beginning "Summary:" that states in one sentence what you take their deepest remaining disagreement to be.
