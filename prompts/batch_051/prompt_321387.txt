In my philosophy class we have been reading the part of Friedrich Nietzsche's The Gay Science where he introduces the idea of the eternal recurrence. He imagines a demon appearing to you in your loneliest mood and telling you that the life you are now living will be lived again and again, exactly as it has been, with no new experiences but only the same sequence repeating forever. Nietzsche says that this thought, if it took possession of you, would either crush you or transform you into someone who could say yes to such an outcome. I understand the basic alternative, but I do not see what the eternity is actually doing in the argument. If I already hate my life, then of course I would hate to live it again; if I already love it, then I would gladly repeat it. The addition of endless repetition seems to leave my present judgment exactly where it was, so I do not see why Nietzsche treats the idea as something with its own distinctive weight. I am also unsure whether we are supposed to treat the demon's message as a claim about the actual structure of the universe or as an exaggerated thought experiment that tests our attitude toward our life. Could you help me work through what the eternal recurrence adds beyond a straightforward self-assessment, and whether the thought is meant to be believed or only entertained?
