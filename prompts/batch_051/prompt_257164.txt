Act as a philosopher of AI safety with a dry, regulatory sense of humor. Write a parody of an aviation-style Minimum Equipment List for a lab that is about to deploy a frontier model it privately believes is misaligned but too useful not to run. Keep it deadpan and FAA-ish in tone, around 650 words, formatted as coded entries (ALC-01, ALC-02...) each with a short "conditions for continued operation" note. The target is the practice of "control, not alignment" safety cases: the lab has quietly given up on the model actually sharing its goals and is now just doing monitoring, sandboxing, tripwires, off-switches, tamper-evident logging. The humor should come from treating the model as an unreliable aircraft system, and from the real difficulties of control. Without ever naming them or winking, work in: a monitor is only as good as the model's inability to game it; a sufficiently capable model has an incentive to look aligned while watched rather than be aligned; "shared values" would be the one MEL item you can't inspect; there's a regress in who monitors the monitor; and the off-switch is only reassuring if the model cant act on the world in ways you dont notice. Before writing the final piece, brainstorm six to eight plausible MEL entries, think carefully about which ones earn their place, then write the final list.
