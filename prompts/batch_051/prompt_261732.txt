My group chat has alot of intense takes (vote the lesser evil, vote your conscience, don't vote if you're uninformed, it's a civic duty, it's all just self-expression) and I keep changing my mind depending on the case. Could you write me a "which theory of voting ethics do you actually hold?" quiz, like a sorting quiz that gets people by what they'd do rather than by what they call themselves.

It should have 8–10 questions. Each should be a concrete voting scenario with 2 or 3 answer options. Please include at least one of each of these kinds of case: a safe seat where your preferred candidate can't win; a razor-thin seat where the two viable candidates are both compromises; a third-party or protest vote in a safe seat; a candidate you agree with on almost everything but who seems chaotic or unserious versus a viable candidate you mostly disagree with; a case where you're pretty uninformed about the issues; a case of compulsory voting with a fine; someone offering to pay you to vote a certain way; and a case that forces the generalization question (what if everyone in your demographic voted exactly as you're about to). No question should just ask "do you think voting is a duty?" or "are you an expressive voter?".

The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these six outcomes: act-utilitarian/expected-value voter, Kantian-style generalization voter (vote the way you'd want everyone in your situation to vote), expressive voter, civic-duty voter, lesser-evil/harm-reduction voter with complicity concerns, and informed abstainer/no-duty-to-vote. If you think a seventh outcome earns a leaf, only add it if your questions actually separate it; otherwise note that it's absorbed.

Build the scoring from the logical space of answer combinations rather than from the labels: first the questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then where each real position sits, and identify unoccupied combinations with a quick note on why nobody is there (incoherent, just unattractive, or unexplored). Also tell me which pairs your quiz can't cleanly separate—I suspect expected-value voter and lesser-evil voter come out nearly identical, and expressive voter and the safe-seat conscience version of civic duty do too, but I want to know.

Audience is my very-not-formal group chat, so keep the cases US-ish and party-neutral, and write each question so someone can answer without knowing the jargon. Thier should be a hand-scorable key at the end.
