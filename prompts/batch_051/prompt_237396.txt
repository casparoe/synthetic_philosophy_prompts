I'm putting together a small unit on experimental philosophy and I need a custom case to hand students who have already seen the standard Knobe CEO vignette too many times. You know the classic result: a chairman is told a new program will raise profits and will also either help or harm the environment; he says he doesn't care about the environment, he just wants profit; and people end up saying he intentionally harmed the environment but didn't intentionally help it. The familiar takeaway is that moral valence, or badness specifically, is doing the work in folk intentionality judgments.

Here's the job. Design a thought experiment — really a matched pair of vignettes, each a paragraph max, set in a workplace, school, or hospital, nothing science-fictional — in which that asymmetry inverts: the good side effect reads as intentional and the bad side effect reads as non-intentional. In both cases the agent has to knowingly bring the side effect about and explicitly say its not their aim, as in the standard case. Don't cheat by turning one agent into a saint and the other into a villain, or by just relabeling a bad thing as good; the flip should fall out of how the side effect enters the agent's practical reasoning. To make the debate tractable, assume there are two candidate explanations in play: a moral-valence account (bad side effects look intentional because of their perceived wrongness) and a practical-relevance account (side effects look intentional to the extent the agent treats them as part of the plan or uses them downstream). Build the pair so those two accounts come apart.

After you give the vignettes, walk me through what the moral-valence account would predict about your pair and what the practical-relevance account predicts, then tell me exactly which feature of the case is doing the work. Then say what happens when that feature is removed — does the case collapse back into a standard Knobe pattern? — and how a defender of the moral-valence account would attack or redescribe your case to save their view. I'm mostly after the diagnosis, not the vignettes themselves, so put your effort there.
