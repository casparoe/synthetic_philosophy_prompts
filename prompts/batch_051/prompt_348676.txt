I am trying to predict how a friend of mine will judge a new moral case, since her past verdicts are consistent but hard to reduce to a single principle. Her verdicts so far are:

- It is morally wrong for a family to cook and eat their dog after it has been killed by a car, even though no person is harmed and the dog would otherwise be cremated.
- It is morally wrong for two adult siblings to have a single, fully consensual sexual encounter using two reliable forms of contraception, with no power imbalance and no intention to repeat it.
- It is not morally wrong, though she finds it repulsive, for a performance artist to drink a glass of her own urine on stage.
- It is morally wrong to use a national flag as a rag for cleaning one's own toilet, even if no one ever learns of it.
- It is not morally wrong to eat a steak grown in a laboratory from one's own muscle cells.
- It is morally wrong to eat a meal made from a donated human placenta, even when the donor consents and all diners are informed.

The new case is this: a man loses his lower leg in an accident, keeps the leg, has a chef prepare it, and eats it at a private dinner with fully informed, consenting friends.

Please predict her verdict on the new case and explain. In particular, identify at least two candidate principles that fit her existing verdicts, say what each candidate predicts about the new case, give your best guess with reasons, and name a further case that would most cleanly separate the candidates. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence (your predicted verdict and the principle you identify), then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
