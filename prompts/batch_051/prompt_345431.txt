Me and my roommate got into it about Thomson's lamp after watching a video on supertasks. The version we were arguing about goes like this: a lamp starts on, and at the 1-minute mark it gets switched off, at 1.5 minutes switched back on, at 1.75 off again, and so on, with the interval halving each time. By the 2-minute mark, infinitely many switches have happened, but no switch happens exactly at 2 minutes. The usual question is whether the lamp is on or off at 2 minutes.

My roommate thinks the set-up is not just underdetermined but impossible, and he gave this step-by-step argument:

(1) If the lamp is on at exactly 2 minutes, then some switch before 2 minutes must have been the one that last turned it on.
(2) But every switch before 2 minutes has a later switch before 2 minutes (off-switches get followed by on-switches, and on-switches get followed by off-switches).
(3) So there is no last switch before 2 minutes.
(4) So the lamp cannot be on at 2 minutes.
(5) By the same reasoning, the lamp cannot be off at 2 minutes either.
(6) But at every time, a lamp is either on or off; that's just what those states mean.
(7) Therefore Thomson's lamp is contradictory, not merely mysterious.

He also pre-empts the usual "the rules just don't settle it" response. He says underdetermination is a fact about our description, but the lamp itself is a real object and has to be one or the other at every instant, so "the rules don't tell us" doesn't dissolve the problem.

I'm stuck. Is the argument valid? If not, can you say exactly which step fails and why, and also say whether his challenge to the underdetermination answer works?
