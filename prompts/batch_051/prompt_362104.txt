I'm hosting a film-and-philosophy night next month and somehow got volunteered to lead the discussion on Denis Villenueve's Arrival. The thing that's been nagging at me is the way the film structures Louise's choice: once she acquires the heptapod language, she sees her daughter Hannah's whole life before Hannah is conceived — the sweet early years, the illness, the death as a teenager — and she still goes ahead and has her, and has her with Ian, without telling him what she knows. Ian later experiences that as a betrayal and leaves. I want to run the discussion through Parfit's nonidentity problem and the 'wrongful life' debate, but I really don't want the movie treated as an illustration of the theory.

So the thing I'd like you to help me plan is a discussion that actually lets the film and the philosophy push on each other. Is Louise's choice structurally the same as Parfit's fourteen-year-old girl who has a child she knows will have a difficult life — where the alternative is a different child or none at all, so the child can't be said to be harmed by being brought into existence? Or does the film's twist, that Louise isn't just predicting Hannah's life but in some sense already remembering it, change the moral situation in a way the standard theory doesn't register? Also, where does the movie resist the philosophical reading: the film stay almost entirely inside Louise's experience, so does it rig the question of whether Hannah's life was worth living? And what would a fair-minded critic say about Ian's position — is he just the one Parfit's reasoning is supposed to dissolve, or does the film show the nonidentity problem as a live disagreement between two people who both love Hannah? I'd like a reading faithful to the actual structure of the nonidentity problem and to the film's details, and honest about the places where the film complicates the theory rather than just illustrating it.
