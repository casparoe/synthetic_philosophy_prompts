I've just had to actually walk away from something I'd sunk a couple of years and about four grand into, and the mental back-and-forth I did before quitting made me realise my views on sunk costs may not all hang together. I'd like an audit before I try to explain myself to the person I did the thing with.

Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) The time and money already put into something are in themselves no reason to keep going. A decision should be made on what continuing and quitting are expected to bring from now on, full stop.

(2) I'd defend walking out of a non-refundable theatre ticket if I'm exhausted and would rather be in bed. I don't owe it to the price of the ticket to stay.

(3) But I do think past investment can legitimately make me readier to keep going, because the time I've put in often makes the remaining work go faster or shows me I can finish it. That's about the future rather than about waste, so I don't regard it as honouring sunk costs.

(4) I also think there's something right about being the kind of person whose earlier commitments bind them: if I could be released from a plan by my later self any time the plan stopped looking best, then my present decisions would never settle anything. Maybe abandoning something I'd resolved on is different from never having resolved on it in the first place.

(5) And I think a string of well-reasoned quits can still mark a character flaw. I'd worry about someone — me, for instance — who starts things and then drops them whenever the expected value dips below the alternative, even if each individual quit made sense at the time. The pattern seems like an extra cost that the one-by-one decisions don't capture.

(6) Last one: my past investments partly constitute who I am. A novel I've written three hundred pages of isn't just an option on the next fifty pages; it's something I've become a certain kind of person in relation to. That sounds like a sunk cost dressed up as identity, but I can't tell whether it's a real reason or just nostalgia.

Could you go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look as if they are, and how severe each real tension is? I'd like them ordered by seriousness, with what would have to give for each, and some sense of which single change would tidy up the most. Also be explicit about how confident you are in your judgements and flag the point you're least sure of.
