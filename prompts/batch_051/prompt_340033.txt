I've been sat in far too many faculty meetings lately, and I want a bit of anonymous satire to send round the department. Could you write a deadpan mock set of minutes from a university appointments commitee that has wandered straight into the discursive dilemma without noticing? The committee has three members voting on a senior hire, and they've already agreed that the candidate should be appointed just in case the post is a strategic priority and the candidate meets the bar. They then vote on each question separately. The votes go like this: Anita thinks the post is a priority but the candidate is weak; Ben thinks the candidate is excellent but the post isn't a priority; Clara thinks both. So on each premise the majority says yes, but on the final motion to appoint the majority says no, because two different people are dissenting on two different grounds.

The minutes should be completely straight-faced: the committee should seem faintly proud of its procedure, never notice the inconsistency, and respond to the awkwardness by re-voting items, rescinding earlier motions, or 'clarifying' wording. The jokes should come from the actual aggregation theory — premise-based versus conclusion-based voting, path dependence, the way the inconsistency just reappears under any reordering — rather than generic meetings-are-tedious gags, so someone who works on social choice laughs for the right reasons and a normal reader still sees the absurdity. Don't have anyone in the minutes name the phenomenon or mention 'discursive dilemma'; the committee should never cotton on. Aim for 500-600 words, formatted as real minutes with numbered agenda items and at least one helpful footnote from the committee secretary.
