Write me the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on the value of felt negative emotion.

Camp A, the Constitutive camp: a life without felt sadness or grief would be missing something of value even if it felt better overall — felt badness is sometimes part of what it is to properly care about or understand a loss, not merely a cost attached to caring.

Camp B, the Instrumental camp: sadness and grief have only instrumental value — they flag the loss, focus attention, and motivate repair — so if you could keep the accurate appraisal of the loss and the motivation while deleting the felt badness, you should, and the resulting life would not be worse.

I don't want a survey of options or a referee's verdict. I want the actual document the collaboration would produce, drafted by someone willing to commit to specific formulations rather than hedging.

Include:

(1) A numbered list of about ten claims both camps can sign without weasel words. They should cover what grief and sadness typically do, the distinction between recognizing a loss as bad and feeling bad about it, and what both sides can jointly say about cases where the appraisal and the affect come apart. Keep them substantive.

(2) A disagreements table, at least five rows. Suggested rows: 'felt badness is...', 'the grief-suppression pill would...', 'a person with flawless loss-appraisal but no felt sadness has...', 'a person with all the sad affect but no object of grief has...', 'a life without felt sadness, viewed from inside, would be...'. For each row give the strongest wording each side would accept for its own position, not the strawman the other side prefers, and a one-line note on what would actually change that side's mind.

(3) A cruxes section: two or three subquestions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each, name a concrete case or intervention both sides agree would bear on it, and record each side's prediction in advance.

Spend almost no time on the ordinary functions of sadness — that part everyone already signs. Spend the effort on the exact place where the two views seperate, and say whether any of the residual fight is just about the word 'better' (felt well-being vs. having reasons) rather than a real disagreement.
