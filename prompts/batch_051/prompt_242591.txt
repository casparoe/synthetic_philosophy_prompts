I've been asked to moderate a series of public debates about whether religion makes people better, and the discussion keeps dissolving because the participants mean different things by "better" before they even disagree about the evidence. I want to force some discipline into the discussion by settling on an ordered list of candidate readings of "more moral," from best to worst as a target for empirical and causal claims, rather than letting the panelists slide between them while arguing.

Here are the candidates I want you to rank into tiers: (a) self-reported moral values, self-discipline, and the importance respondents say they attach to honesty; (b) behavior in anonymous, incentivized experiments, such as dictator games or honesty tasks that allow undetected cheating; (c) costly real-world prosocial acts, such as blood or organ donation, sustained volunteering, or reporting wrongdoing at personal risk; (d) stable civic dispositions toward strangers, such as generalized trust and cooperation in public-goods settings; and (e) harshness of moral judgment and willingness to punish wrongdoers. Rank these by how well each would allow a claim like "religious people are more moral" to mean something worth arguing about—that is, how well it resists self-deception and reputation management, how behaviorally real it is, how portable it is across cultural contexts, and how capable it is of revealing a disposition rather than merely a situation. For each candidate, give a one-sentence justification for its placement and identify the most serious weakness that kept it out of the tier above.

Then apply your ranking to two stylized findings. First: a study finds that religious and secular respondents differ reliably only on self-report measures, with no difference on anonymous behavioral measures. Second: a study finds the reverse—differences in anonymous one-shot games but no difference in long-term volunteering, donations, or trust in neighbors. Under your top tier, what conclusion about "being better" does each pattern actually support, and would your bottom tier lead someone to overstate it? Finally, name one plausible interpretation of "religion makes people better" that your ranking deliberately refuses to operationalize, and explain why you refused it.
