I have been working through Quine’s radical translation thought experiment, and I want to press on one part of it. A field linguist encounters a people whose language is entirely unrelated to any known language. On seeing a rabbit run by, the informant says Gavagai. The problem, as I understand it, is not merely whether the sentence means something like Lo, a rabbit or There is a rabbit, but what the term itself refers to. All the behavioral evidence available to the field linguist seems consistent with several rival referential schemes, because the same stimulations can be accommodated by adjusting the background apparatus of identity and individuation.

So far I have collected these standard candidates for the reference of the term Gavagai: (1) whole, enduring rabbits; (2) undetached rabbit parts, such as rabbit ears, paws, or rabbit-shaped volumes of matter; (3) rabbit stages, the brief temporal slices of a rabbit’s existence; (4) the rabbit fusion, a single scattered object composed of all rabbits; and (5) rabbithood, the universal or property that each rabbit instantiates. Quine’s point, as I take it, is that each of these can be fitted to the same totality of stimulus patterns if one compensates elsewhere in the translation manual, especially in how one translates the apparatus of individuation, identity, and predication.

I want to know whether there is a sixth candidate that is genuinely different from these five. By genuinely different I mean: not a mere rewording, not a special case of one of the five, and not a disjunctive or gerrymandered combination of them. For example, I would not count the set of all rabbits as new unless you can show it is doing something distinct from both the fusion and the extension of rabbithood, and I would not count a time-slice of an undetached rabbit part as new because it is just a combination of candidates 2 and 3. If there is a sixth candidate, describe it, explain why the field linguist could adopt it consistently with all the same behavioral evidence, and say whether it does something the standard five do not. If you think the list is already exhaustive or that every further candidate collapses into one of these, you should defend that claim directly and identify where the line falls between a genuinely new referential scheme and a mere variant.
