A friend of mine has what she insists is a coherent line on which systems genuinely have beliefs, as opposed to ones where we just find it useful to adopt the intentional stance for prediction. She is not doing the everything-with-a-thermostat-thinks move, and she is not a panpsychist either. Over the last few months I've been keeping a list of her verdicts:

- Thermostat: no beliefs.
- An image classifier that generalises to new photos and is right 99% of the time: no beliefs.
- A numerical weather model that out-predicted the Met Office on last week's storm: no beliefs.
- A dog: yes, it has beliefs.
- A macaque: yes.
- A base-model LLM, just next-token prediction, no RLHF, no tools, no persistent goals: no beliefs.
- A customer-service chatbot that has been RLHF'd to say things like I believe the refund was processed, but has no world model and no preferences beyond the conversation: no beliefs.

This week's case is a model-based RL agent trained in a small simulated gridworld. It has learnt a probabilistic causal model of its environment (where the food is, where predators tend to appear, which obstacles move), feeds that model into a planning algorithm, and picks multi-step actions to maximise expected reward. It can plan a path through a part of the environment it has never visited, via the model it has learnt. It has no language, no self-model beyond an (x, y) location variable, and no way to report its beliefs. If I had to bet, it isnt conscious.

What would she say? I keep going back and forth. The obvious rule is something like uses a stored causal model plus planning for goals, which would give a yes. But a rule about consciousness, or maybe about needing a self-model and not just a world-model, would give a no, and I don't think her earlier list separates those. What's your best guess about her verdict, which rule (or rules) shes actually running, which extra case would settle it, and what would change your mind, i.e. which new argument or case would flip you the other way? Keep the answer in British English, and dont send me a reading list; I want the analysis.
