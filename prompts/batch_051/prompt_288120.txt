Write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a frequentist and a subjective Bayesian about what probability claims mean. The deliverable is a single document both parties would sign: first a numbered list of claims both can accept (about the probability calculus, about calibration, about the sense in which long-run frequencies are relevant to rational belief), then a table of the remaining disagreements, phrased so each side would accept the wording of its own position. For each disagreement, give the crux—the sub-question such that settling it would actually move at least one side—and say what each side predicts an agreed test or case would show. Pay special attention to whether the dispute over single-case probabilities (the probability that this particular coin lands heads) is substantive or mostly verbal, and to the frequentist's reference class problem. Dont adjudicate from outside: the document itself has to be neutral enough that both parties would put their names to it.
