After a long argument with a friend about the paradox of phenomenal judgment, I keep returning to an analogy from software, and I cannot decide whether it is deep or superficial. If qualia are epiphenomenal, then my utterance that I am in pain is not caused by the pain itself but by the neural machinery that also causes the pain; so why should the utterance be about the pain rather than merely correlated with it? The structure I have in mind is a system that, from a single internal state, emits two outputs: a status record that is never read by any later instruction (the analog of the qualia), and a report that downstream processes do read and that states the content of that record (the analog of the phenomenal judgment). The report is not caused by the status record, and the status record never feeds back into the system; they share a cause, and the report is accurate because the same state drives both code paths. Is this an honest structural parallel to what the epiphenomenalist must claim about judgments, or does the software case smuggle in the guarantee of shared information that epiphenomenalism lacks? What exactly would have to be true on the software side for the report to count as knowledge of the unread record, and does that condition transfer to phenomenal judgment?
