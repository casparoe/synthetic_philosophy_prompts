Last week in my grad seminar I ended up defending — badly — the idea that there is such a thing as *the* rational prior. You know the standard subjectivist line: priors are just where you start, anything coherent goes, and eventually the evidence washes out the differences. But I've never been fully satisfied with that. If two people with exactly the same evidence assign very different priors, I want to say at least one of them is making an epistemic mistake, not merely expressing a taste. The objective Bayesian move, as I understand it, says: take all the priors compatible with your background constraints, pick the one that maximizes Shannon entropy — the most uninformative one — and treat that as the single rational prior. I know the obvious objections: the maxent prior is langauge-dependent (redescribe the same constraints and you can get a different prior), and it's unclear whether "rational" here means accuracy-tracking or something more pragmatic. But I keep feelng the pull: there should be a fact of the matter.

Here's what I'd like from you. Give me your actual credence in this claim: for a given hypothesis space and background constraints, there is a uniquely rational prior probability distribution, and it is the maximum-entropy distribution. Don't just give me a bare number — decompose the reasoning. I want at least these terms: the probability that there is a fact of the matter about rational priors at all (as against pure subjectivism), the probability that maximum entropy is the right criterion for pinning down the prior given that there is such a fact (as against, say, transformation-invariance or other symmetry rules), the probability that it actually yields unique, sensible priors in realistic continuous cases given that it is the right criterion, and any other term you think the number depends on. For each, give a number and a line of justification, then show how you combine them into the overall credence. Then tell me which term you are least confident in, and what evidence or argument would move that term the most.

One last thing: I'd like you to keep separated what is actually settled here from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this — please don't present a contested view as if it were the consensus. It's fine to be casual; I just want to see where your credence is strongest and weakest.
