I co-chair a working group at a public-policy research institute that is preparing an assessment of long-run trends in human violence. We keep returning to the same difficulty. Our statistical colleagues can produce two internally sound summaries of the past two centuries: the absolute number of violent deaths has grown during many periods, chiefly because the human population has grown, while the rate of violent death per 100,000 people has fallen substantially in the long run. One faction in the group takes the per-capita trend to show, straightforwardly, that the world has become less violent, on the grounds that what matters is the average person's probability of dying by violence. Another faction says the per-capita interpretation is not a discovery but a decision: to privilege "risk to any one person" over "aggregate human suffering" is to choose which quantity deserves to inherit the phrase "less violent." A third position, which I half believe, is that the sentence "the world has become less violent" has no determinate truth-value until a metric is stipulated, because the relevant quantity is not a natural feature of the world with a fixed correct scale.

I would like to know whether this disagreement can be brought under empirical control rather than settled by stipulation. How could one test the conceptual claim that a metric such as violent deaths per 100,000 people per year tracks something real about how violent the world is — a property that is there to be measured and that makes one summary correct — rather than merely being one admissible bookkeeping convention among others? Please design the strongest concrete test you can think of. Spell out what observations or measurements would be made, what result would favor each reading (including the "no fact of the matter" reading, if any evidence could favor it), and be explicit about what a positive outcome would and would not settle. For present purposes, bracket the prior question of how broadly "violence" should be defined; our disagreement is about what makes a summary of the underlying phenomenon correct. Please write the response in American English.
