I'm a junior on my school's Fine Arts Council, which doles out the arts budget and signs off on independent projects that can count toward the arts requirement, and I'm supposed to circulate a written opinion before Thursdays meeting, so I actually have to draft the thing. We're stuck on Maya T's application. She's a sophomore and she built a short interactive story game in Twine — the writing is decent but nothing special, the visuals are basically stick figures — about a person who has one week to decide whether to tell their best friend a piece of news that will hurt the friendship either way. Maya's whole argument is that the art isn't the text or the pictures; it's the way the game makes you live inside the choice. Every path leaves traces: people remember what you didn't say, the options you almost picked keep resurfacing, and the ending is less important then the feeling that your choices had a shape and a cost. Some council members say that's just a choose-your-own-adventure story with extra steps, and that a real artwork has to express the artist's specific vision rather than handing the player a menu. A couple others say that objection misunderstands games entirely, and that what matters is something like an aesthetics of agency, where the art is in the choosing itself rather than in what gets chosen. Half the council thinks it's obviously not art and the other half thinks we'd be blind to reject it, and our council hasn't defined what counts as art in years, so now we have to vote on Thursday.

Please draft the memo I'll send around. It should have a clear bottom line we could actually vote on — fund and recognize Maya's project, reject it, or approve it with conditions — plus reasons the losing side can respond to without feeling bulldozed. Give us criteria we can use for future game projects, like what separates an art game from a coding exercise or an interactive story with no artistic point, and take the strongest version of the "it's just a branching story and the player does the composing, not the artist" objection seriously, since I half-agree with it myself.
