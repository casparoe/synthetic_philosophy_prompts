Two coworkers of mine argued after a lunchtime seminar on Singer's "Famine, Affluence, and Morality." For background: in that article Singer defends roughly the principle that if you can prevent something bad without sacrificing anything of comparable moral importance, you ought to do it. He motivates it with a case in which a passerby sees a child drowning in a shallow pond and would obviously be required to wade in even at the cost of muddy clothes and being late, and he then treats distance and the presence of many other people who could help as morally irrelevant.

Their exchange, as best I can reconstruct it, went like this:

(1) She said the pond case is airtight: nobody thinks the cost to your clothes or the fact that the child is not yours are serious reasons to leave him, so if you accept that case you have already accepted that you should give far more to distant strangers than you currently do.

(2) He replied that the pond is disanalogous because there you are the only person in a position to act, the child is identifiable in front of you, and your action would directly and certainly save him, whereas in global poverty millions could give but do not, and any particular donation may be misdirected or lost.

(3) She answered that uniqueness is not morally important; it only changes the epistemology. If a well-run charity can save a life for a few thousand dollars, a donation buys a real probability of saving someone, and a one-in-a-hundred chance of saving one child is morally on a par with saving one child under correspondingly less favorable conditions.

(4) He said that move changes the nature of the duty. The pond case is a one-off rescue with a clear endpoint, but the principle she is now proposing turns every earned dollar into a potential rescue and permanently dissolves the line between duty and supererogation.

(5) She said that is not an objection to Singer; it is precisely his point. Singer is trying to show that the ordinary line between moral requirement and charity is drawn too high, not to preserve our peace of mind.

Please adjudicate the exchange move by move. Give exactly five numbered items, one per move, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. For each move, state which speaker has the better of it and rate the move from clearly mistaken to clearly right; at the fifth move, also say whether the open-endedness objection can be turned back against Singer or whether it simply restates his conclusion.
