I run a monthly reading group on decision-making under uncertainty. Its members are working professionals — mostly people from finance, public health, and urban planning — and we usually have fourteen to sixteen people and ninety minutes. We have been reading about ergodicity economics, and the distinction between time averages and ensemble averages has become personally urgent for me: I recently sat with a family member while a retirement projection highlighted an attractive average path even though most of the individual simulated paths failed. My group can now state the distinction, but they don't yet feel why it matters; they keep evaluating repeated choices as though the expected value across many people over one period were the right guide for one person over many periods. I want a single-session activity, using no more than a coin, a score sheet, and slips of paper, in which each participant manages a starting stake through repeated multiplicative bets. A payoff structure of the right kind is the mildly favorable-looking gamble where heads multiplies the stake by 1.5 and tails by 0.6: looked at over one round, it has a positive expected value, while over many rounds the typical individual's stake decays. Please arrange the game so that each participant watches their own wealth path while the room's average wealth is displayed alongside it, and let them notice the divergence themselves. The facilitator should not say the words ergodicity, time average, or ensemble average until the game is over.

Then provide exact rules, a non-monetary payoff scheme, a timeline for the ninety minutes, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the play to the time-average/ensemble-average distinction, the non-ergodicity of the process, and the gap between the arithmetic-mean growth of the group and the geometric-mean growth that governs a single participant's path. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — people wanting to quit, pool their stakes, or vary the fraction of their wealth they bet — and how I should handle each without giving away the lesson. Finally, be candid about which part of the ergodicity-economics point the activity cannot reproduce and what I will need to supply myself in the debrief.
