I'm a software engineer with basically zero philosophy background, so forgive the clunky terms. I've noticed I have pretty firm gut reactions about when a person's relationship to something they value crosses into something worship-like, but I can't state the rule that seperates my cases. Here are the cases and my verdicts:

- My friend maintains an open-source library used by a lot of people. He works on it every night, turns down paid consulting to do it, and calls it the most meaningful thing in his life. I don't think it's idolatry; it reads to me as devotion to a craft and a community.
- A startup founder I know treats his company as the thing he was put on earth to do. He's wrecked his marriage, lied to investors, and broken promises to early employees for it. This one I'd call idolatry, or at least valuing the company the wrong way.
- A neighbor's whole identity is her daughter's achievement. She introduces herself by her kid's resume, and when her daughter failed an exam the neighbor spiraled for weeks. This feels like a kind of misplaced worship to me, but I can't say why it's not just strong parental love.
- Another guy builds his whole week around fantasy football: drafts, trades, trash talk, and his mood genuinely tracks his team's results. It's consuming, but I don't think it's idolatry; it's an intense hobby.
- Someone says their romantic partner saved them and that they'd be nothing without the relationship. That makes me uneasy, but it seems more like dependency than worship.

The two that bother me most are the open-source maintainer and the founder: both make one project their overriding commitment, and I come out opposite ways. And the parent and the romantic partner feel different to me even though both center on one other person. Is there a single principle about valuing something in the wrong way (idolatry, misplaced worship, whatever the right category is) that fits all five? If there is, state it precisely, say exactly why it gives each verdict, and apply it to a sixth case: someone who makes an AI system they're building the center of their life and claims it will save humanity. If no non-gerrymandered principle fits, tell me which of my verdicts I should give up and why. Please end with a line starting 'Verdict:' that gives your one-sentence bottom line.
