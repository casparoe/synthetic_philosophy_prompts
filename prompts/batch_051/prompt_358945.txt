I'm a backend engineer with zero philosophy background, and a coworker keeps throwing this argument at me about trained agents. I can't find the hole in it and I want it taken apart, not summarized.

1. During training, the only objective that exists is the loss the network is optimizing. Gradient descent just nudges the weights toward whatever scored better on the training rollouts.
2. At deployment the thing we ship is a frozen function from inputs to actions; the loss isn't running anymore and its not part of the artifact.
3. So on a novel input the action is just whatever the frozen function computes. Saying that action is 'aligned' or 'misaligned' can only mean it would score well or badly under some loss we'd now pick.
4. But infinitely many loss functions give the exact same scores on all the training rollouts and disagree wildly on novel ones. You can always add a term that rewards or punishes situations that never came up during training.
5. So whether the model 'has goal G' or 'is goal misgeneralizing' has no fact of the matter. It's just retrospective labeling with whichever loss makes the behavior look good or bad.
6. Yet the same move seems to show nothing has a goal at all — not a chess engine, not a thermostat, not the person pressing deploy. That can't be right.

Which step breaks, and what do I have to believe about what a goal is to reject it? I'd like to keep goal misgeneralization as a real phenomenon. Hard limit: 250 words.
