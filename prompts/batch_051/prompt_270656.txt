I have been trying to work out, from my own reactions to particular situations, where I actually draw the line between expression that should be protected and expression that may be restricted because of what it does to people. I used to think the rule was simply that speech should be protected unless it directly causes concrete harm, but my verdicts keep pulling away from that. I would like to know whether there is a single principle that captures the pattern, or whether some of my verdicts have to be revised.

Here are the cases.

First, a man writes a long, true account of his former partner's private life and posts it publicly under her real name, including details she told him in confidence. The account is humiliating and serves no public interest. I think this should not be protected, and she should be able to have it taken down and to seek damages.

Second, a newspaper reports that a government minister has a progressive illness that will likely impair his judgment within a year, based on leaked medical records. I think the newspaper should be protected.

Third, a restaurant critic writes a harsh but honest review of a chef's new dish. The chef loses customers. I think the critic is clearly protected.

Fourth, a group of neighbors starts a social media campaign falsely claiming that a local shop sells expired food. The shop loses most of its customers. I think the neighbors should be liable for the losses.

Fifth, a comedian during a live show singles out a private person in the audience and mocks her facial scarring. I think it is cruel, but should remain legal.

Sixth, someone sells an online course claiming that a paste made from apricot kernels cures cancer and tells buyers that doctors are hiding the truth. I think this should be illegal.

Seventh, a platform hosts user reviews of doctors, and a coordinated campaign posts dozens of identical false reviews saying a surgeon is negligent. The surgeon's practice collapses. I think the platform should be required to remove the false reviews once it has been shown that they are false.

Eighth, a political leader gives a speech in which he never explicitly instructs violence. He names a journalist, says she is a threat to the nation, and says someone should deal with her; afterward his supporters send her death threats and one attacks her. I keep changing my mind about whether the leader should be held responsible, since he never gave a direct order.

Ninth, a person posts a nude photograph of an ex-partner online without consent. The photograph is real and was given voluntarily, but not meant to be shared. I think this should be illegal even though it is true.

Tenth, a company creates an AI-generated video of a public figure saying offensive things the person never actually said. The video is clearly labeled as synthetic but is widely shared. I am unsure: it feels less like expression than a counterfeit, yet no one is likely to believe it is real.

Is there a coherent account of the harm that expression can do that captures these verdicts, or nearly all of them? If not, which of my verdicts should I give up, and why? And what does the account you propose say about the two cases I cannot settle, the political leader and the labeled synthetic video?
