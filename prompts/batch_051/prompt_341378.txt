So here's the assignment. I did forty years in physical chemistry, retired a while back, and somewhere in the last year I fell down the philosophy of language hole. I've read enough Kripke and some Frege/Russell to know the two teams, and I want you to draft the one document I haven't been able to find: the joint statement a committed Millian direct-reference theorist about proper names and a committed descriptivist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Don't give me a verdict on who's right; give me the document thier collaboration would produce. Open with numbered claims both sides accept, including at least one substantive claim they had to earn rather than something vacuous. Then a disagreements table, with each side's position worded so that side would actually sign the wording. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would visibly move at least one camp. For each crux, give a concrete argument or case both sides agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance. If part of their fight is verbal, say exactly which part and why.

Use Neptune and Vulcan as the worked example rather than the usual Morning Star/Evening Star routine. Neptune was posited to explain anomalies in Uranus's orbit, and the name got attached to whatever was doing the perturbing once it was spotted; Vulcan was posited the same way for Mercury's anomalous perihelion advance and turned out not to exist. In my lab we used to do the same thing: we'd run a column and talk for weeks about "the impurity that elutes at 12.4 minutes" before we knew its structure, and only later swap in a name. I honestly can't tell whether that pattern is a point for the descriptivists, the Millians, or neither. Make the two camps wrestle over Neptune and Vulcan specifically, and show me the exact place where they part ways.
