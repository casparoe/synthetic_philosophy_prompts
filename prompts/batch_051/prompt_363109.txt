I've been watching a years-long argument on the rationalist-adjacent internet between what I'll call continuists and thresholdists about sentience and moral patienthood. Continuists think the property "there is something it is like to be this system" is graded all the way down: a honeybee has a small but real amount, a fruit fly less, and even a thermostat gets some vanishingly tiny nonzero trace because it has internal states and a feedback-regulated setpoint. Thresholdists think there's a hard requirement—something like unified subjectivity, or valence that can actually be felt as better or worse—below which there is literally zero patienthood, so bees are below the line and no finite number of sub-threshold systems generates a single unit of moral weight. The argument keeps circling the same two cases (bee brains, thermostats) without anyone writing down what they'd actually need to see to move.

My reading group finally talked them into an adversarial collaboration. Draft the document the two camps should produce and could both sign. I want: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section, nonvacuous rather than just "both sides care about welfare"; (2) a disagreements table where each real disagreement is stated in two wordings, each phrased so its own side would sign there column; (3) the cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case, measurement, or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Work through the honeybee, a room thermostat, an RL agent trained on a single numeric reward, and a human waking from propofol as cutting cases, and say where each lands or why it fails to discriminate. I suspect one crux is whether the property that excludes a thermostat can be specified without smuggling in a cutoff, and another is whether tiny weights aggregate continuously, so a big enough hive of bees would eventually outweigh a chicken. If you conclude that part of the dispute is only about the word "sentience" doing double duty, say which part and rewrite the joint wording so that part disappears. I'd rather have a committed, definite document than a survey of options: if a popular-sounding claim shouldn't actually be signed by one side, don't include it.
