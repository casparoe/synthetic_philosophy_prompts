Can an AI system be made to avoid unintended side effects without a full specification of human value? Consider two research programs for answering this. One side holds that side effects can be defined and measured in value-free, task-independent terms: choose a baseline, such as doing nothing, and penalize the agent, as an auxiliary penalty on top of its task reward, for how far its actions move the world away from that baseline. The other side holds that every such proposal is covertly value-laden: the choice of baseline (why doing nothing rather than some other default policy?) and the choice of which state variables count as changes (why the vase's position but not the dust's?) already amount to a partial specification of what matters, so an impact measure cannot be more value-neutral than the full value function it was supposed to replace.

Draft the joint statement that a committed representative of each side could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The statement should include: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides accept, including the minimal adequacy conditions an impact measure must meet and the technical roles played by baselines, state variables, and auxiliary penalties; (2) a disagreements table giving the exact formulation of each remaining disagreement, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own view; (3) the cruxes on which the dispute turns, stated in neutral language; and (4) for each crux, a concrete scenario both sides agree bears on it — for example, a scenario in which preventing an injury requires causing a large, transient disruption, or a scenario in which the agent fulfills its task while altering many task-irrelevant features of its environment — along with each side's prediction, recorded in advance, about how a well-designed impact measure would treat the scenario.
