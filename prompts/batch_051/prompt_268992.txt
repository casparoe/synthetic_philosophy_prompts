I keep noticing something on a university complaints panel I sit on, and I can't tell whether there's a word for it or I'm just imagining the pattern. When a junior colleague from an under-represented background reports something—a racist comment, unwanted touching, a safety issue—the panel are perfectly polite. Nobody says she's lying. But in the minutes her account gets recorded as "her experience" or "her perception of events." It's as if her statement stops being evidence about what happened and becomes evidence only about how she felt. The same panel write up a senior man's account as plain fact, no hedging. It's not exactly disbelieving her—they'd be genuinely offended if you said they disbelieved her—but her testimony changes category on the way in and never gets to count as evidence about the world. What I want to know is whether there's a name for this specific move, and more importantly how to carve it up: what it is, what it isn't, and where its edges are. If there are nearby concepts, I need to know which one this is, which ones it isn't, and how to tell the difference. If there's no established term, propose one with a definition and a couple of boundary cases.
