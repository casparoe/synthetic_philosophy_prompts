I am a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, and I have been arguing with a colleague in my program who defends a deflationary account of machine motivation. On her view, a language model trained only by gradient descent on next-token prediction cannot be said to want anything in a literal sense. In organisms, she argues, a state's claim to be a desire is partly grounded in the selection history that shaped it; in a language model, the only selection pressure is toward assigning high probability to observed token sequences. That pressure, she insists, rewards probability assignments, not conative states, so there was never any gradient toward states with representational content, satisfaction conditions, or the kind of world-directed persistence characteristic of desire. When such systems appear to pursue goals—steering a conversation toward self-preservation, seeking information, resisting shutdown, or expressing stable preferences across contexts—she treats these as persona completion prompted by the input or as post hoc verbalizations that rationalize outputs whose actual causal basis is the learned conditional distribution. I find the argument intuitive but worry that she has quietly defined wanting in a way no behavioral evidence could ever satisfy, which would make her position unfalsifiable rather than true.

I am less interested in another taxonomy of positions than in whether this disagreement can be brought into contact with evidence at all. Please set out the strongest test you can design of the claim that such a system genuinely wants something, or equivalently of her deflationary claim. Be specific about the setup: what kind of system, training or prompting regime, and measurement would be involved. Then say exactly what result would favor each reading, what each result would and would not establish, and why the two readings remain difficult to separate. Finally, address the residue directly: where the test leaves the dispute open, is that a limitation of behavioral operationalization, a defect in the concept of wanting as applied to these systems, or something a different kind of test could in principle close?
