I keep finding that I have firm periodization judgments about individual figures, but no rule that seems to generate them, and I want to know whether the labels are tracking something real or are just historiographical conveniences. My verdicts are as follows. Giotto, the early fourteenth-century Florentine painter: Renaissance, not medieval. Dante: medieval, not Renaissance, though I admit this feels forced. William of Ockham: medieval. Petrarch: Renaissance. Filippo Brunelleschi, the fifteenth-century Florentine architect: Renaissance. Leonardo da Vinci: Renaissance, even though the observational quality of his anatomical studies feels like a later phenomenon. Copernicus: Scientific Revolution, not Renaissance. Andreas Vesalius, the sixteenth-century anatomist: Scientific Revolution. Paracelsus, the sixteenth-century physician and alchemist: none of the three; at most I would call him early modern. Galileo: Scientific Revolution. Newton: Scientific Revolution, paradigmatically.

Two pairs strike me as hard to square. Giotto and Dante are near-contemporaries in early fourteenth-century Florence, both doing something plainly novel, yet I put one in the Renaissance and the other in the Middle Ages. And I put Vesalius in the Scientific Revolution on the strength of observation-based anatomy, but I put Leonardo's observation-based anatomical work in the Renaissance. So novelty cannot be my criterion, and empiricism cannot be either.

Is there a single principle, or a small set of candidate principles, that captures all or most of these verdicts without just re-describing each figure? State it precisely. Say what it predicts for a case I have not settled, namely William Harvey, the early seventeenth-century English physician, and his account of the circulation of the blood, and tell me which of my verdicts, if any, it cannot accommodate. Finally, on the strength of that principle, should the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution be treated as real divisions in the historical material, or as later impositions that happen to organize the material well in some places and badly in others?
