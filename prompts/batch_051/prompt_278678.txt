I have been reading the philosophical dispute over whether DNA is literally a code, and whether talk of genetic information is a precise claim or a metaphor that papers over distinctions. I want to stress-test that framework against a concrete scientific controversy, as a two-way case analysis.

The case is the ENCODE consortium's 2012 announcement that the large majority of the human genome — the headline figure was about 80 percent — is functional. The consortium used an operational definition of function as specified biochemical activity: transcription, protein binding, or a particular chromatin state. On that definition most of the genome counts as functional, and the result was widely reported as overturning the idea of junk DNA. Many evolutionary geneticists objected that this conflates activity with function. They argued that function in the relevant sense requires evidence of selection, that conservation data cannot accommodate 80 percent functional sequence, and that transcription or binding often reflects noise, repetitive elements, or transient interactions rather than an evolved instruction.

Using the distinctions you would draw in that debate, analyze the episode. Start by specifying what each side in the controversy treated as evidence, and what exact claim each side needed to establish. Then distinguish at least three senses in which DNA might be said to bear information: (1) Shannon-style correlation, where a sequence reduces uncertainty at a downstream receiver regardless of whether the receiver is an adaptation; (2) causal or instructional information, where the sequence reliably directs a specific outcome with the right kind of causal specificity; and (3) semantic or representational information, where the sequence is about a product or phenotype, with room for misrepresentation and error correction.

Apply those senses to the ENCODE dispute. Which of the consortium's functional claims survive under each sense, and which collapse? Does the biochemical-activity definition turn mere correlation into communication? Is the selection-based criticism too restrictive — can developmentally important but poorly conserved biochemical activity still be code-like, and under which sense? Finally, run the analysis in the other direction: which features of the ENCODE dispute does the code/information framework illuminate, which does it distort or leave out, and does the episode show that the language of code and information is a precise instrument, a misleading metaphor, or a cluster of distinct claims that the two sides were never actually using univocally?
