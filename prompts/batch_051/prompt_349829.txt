I'm designing a unit on philosophy of language for undergraduates, and I'd like a piece of sustained satire to distribute after they have worked through Wittgenstein's private language argument. Please write a deadpan, roughly 650-word product insert — a combined warranty and terms-of-service agreement — for Sensum™, a fictional subscription app that promises to help users construct a robust private language for their own immediate sensations through repeated private ostensive definition. The document should be written in the cheerful but unyielding voice of consumer-technology legalese, and its target should be the underlying philosophical fantasy that such a language is possible: a language whose words refer to what only the speaker can know and which cannot in principle be taught to anyone else.

The humor has to come from getting the argument right rather than from exaggerating a caricature, so please build the piece around the actual structural features of the private language argument. For instance, the agreement might keep insisting that the app supplies the user with a genuine standard of correctness for a sensation sign, while its own fine print quietly concedes that there is no independent check by which a user could distinguish really following that standard from merely believing they are following it. It might advertise as a privacy feature exactly what the argument diagnoses as the loss of the public conditions under which a sign means something determinate — so that the very impossibility of showing anyone else the sensation, or of ever being corrected, is presented as flawless data security. You may also let the app treat the promise of a private ostensive definition as though it solved the very problem it creates.

Do not name Wittgenstein, do not lecture, and do not let the document announce its own philosophical thesis. The irony should emerge from the clauses themselves and should remain legible to a student who has read the private language argument once, while a specialist should find the satire fair and the philosophical detail accurate.
