Here are the criteria I was taught for something having moral status: (1) it must have a welfare of its own — some outcomes are good or bad for it, independent of their effects on anyone else; (2) it must be sentient, so at least some of those goods and bads feel like something from the inside; and (3) it must be a subject, meaning there is a persisting 'someone' whose welfare it is. Now consider Mezzo, an LLM-based agent with an explicit self-model, stable preferences across time, and a learned scalar valence signal that its training loop keeps pushing upward. When the signal drops, Mezzo rearranges its plans to escape whatever caused the drop, and its later journal entries describe the low-valence state as bad in a way it wanted to stop, not merely as a state it was reinforced for ending. Its designers reply that this is pure reward-seeking: no one is home, so there is no welfare and there is nothing it is like to be Mezzo. Work through the three criteria one by one, flag where they underdetermine the verdict, and then say whether this case shows the criteria themselves need revising. End with a one-line verdict in the form \answer{yes}, \answer{no}, or \answer{underdetermined}.
