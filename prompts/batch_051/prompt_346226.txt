I've been going in circles on the Epicurean argument that death isn't bad for the person who dies, and I'm stuck between an argument I can't refute and an intuition I can't shake. The argument, as I understand it: there was an infinite stretch of time before I existed, and I don't regard that prenatal nonexistence as something that harmed me; there will be another infinite stretch after I die, and that's just more nonexistence. So if nonexistence before my life isn't bad for me, nonexistence after it shouldn't be either; the only difference is where the nonexistence sits relative to the life, and why should that matter? I find this compelling because it's got almost no moving parts, treats past and future symmetrically, and explains the fear of death as a sort of temporal illusion. But the intuition on the other side is as firm as any I have: if I were told I'd die within a year, I would take it as terrible news for me, not just for the people who would miss me, and I feel nothing remotely like that about all the time before I was born.

I've tried to talk myself out of the intuition by saying we care about losing goods we already had, not goods we never got, but that mostly restates the asymmetry instead of justifying it. I also tried to turn it into a choice: I'd obviously trade a year of my actual life for an extra year at the far end, but would I trade a year of prenatal nonexistence, whatever that would even mean, for one? I can't tell whether the thought experiment is coherent, and my hesitation there makes me suspect the asymmetry is doing more work than the argument lets on. So I want someone to walk me through what each move actually costs. Should I debunk the intuition and keep the symmetry argument, and if so, what would a successful debunking look like from the inside? Should I amend or restrict the argument to make room for this one asymmetry, even if that looks ad hoc? Or is there a third reading under which both the argument and the intuition can be right? I'm less interested in a verdict than in knowing exactly what I'm giving up either way.
