A constitutional convention has put forward the following secession mechanism for a multinational federation. To open an independence process, a region must file a petition signed by at least 8 percent of its registered electors within one year. If the petition qualifies, a drafting panel—half its members drawn from the region, half from the federation as a whole—sets the ballot language and publishes a preliminary terms-of-separation document covering debt, borders, citizenship, pensions, and defense. An independence referendum then requires 55 percent approval on turnout of at least 65 percent to pass. If it passes, a four-year transition period begins; at the two-year mark a confirmatory referendum must pass by a simple majority, and if that vote fails or turnout falls below 50 percent, the process ends and the region may not reapply for fifteen years. Treat this as a mechanism, not as a moral argument for or against secession: analyze what it incentivizes among separatist parties, unionist parties, the central government, ordinary voters, and outside states; how each stage could be gamed; what the mid-transition confirmatory vote does to the politics of negotiation; and where the scheme would fail first. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
