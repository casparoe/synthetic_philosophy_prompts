ok so i just got back from a long argument with a friend about 'right side of history' talk and he kept saying it's just whig history with better branding — every age secretly thinks it's the ending that all the earlier ages were fumbling toward. i half-agree, but i think the creepier part is that noticing this doesn't get you out: the minute i say we've outgrown the Victorians' self-congratulation, i'm writing a whig history of whig history, with me as the destination. id like a poem that does this instead of explaining it.

write a proper villanelle (19 lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain) in the voice of a comfortably progressive gentleman in 1850 who is certain that all of history was a long, clumsy preparation for people exactly like him. the two refrains should carry the two halves of the move. one should be his boast, roughly 'all of it led here, to us.' the other should be a thing he says about earlier ages — that they were drafts, half-blind gropings that only make sense in hindsight — which the reader slowly realizes is exactly what a later age will say about him. the refrains need to alternate and then meet in the last two lines so the form shows that 'we are the destination' and 'we are somebody else's stepping stone' are the same sentence said from different spots on the staircase. dont resolve it into 'history has no direction'; the point of the poem should be that the temptation snaps back the moment you say that. someone who knows the whig-history debate should recognize the idea rather than feel it's just name-dropped, and someone who doesn't should still find it a good poem.
