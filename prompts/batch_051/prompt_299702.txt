One of the elders at our church has to decide a dispute and I keep flip-flopping. Our denomination's 1934 basis of faith contains this clause: "The Holy Scripture of the Old and New Testaments, given by the inspiration of God, is the infallible Word of God and the only binding rule of faith and practice; every part of it is to be received as true, and no part is to be laid aside as merely human opinion or as mistaken in what it teaches."

The dispute is over a guy who teaches the adult Sunday school. He's been telling the class that Isaiah is best read as a layered book: chapters 1–39 as mostly the eighth-century prophet's preaching, chapters 40–55 as an anonymous prophetic voice speaking to the exiles in Babylon, and 56–66 as still later material from after the return. He doesn't deny any of it is revelation; he says the book is inspired, but that its truth was addressed to each layer's first audience, so the servant songs in 40–55 spoke first to sixth-century exiles and only later, typologically, to Christ. Some members say this breaches the basis of faith; he says the clause doesn't say anything about who wrote the books or when, and he isn't laying any part aside as merely human opinion.

The basis predates the current historical-critical stuff and it's drafters almost certainly assumed single authorship. How should the elder apply the clause? Work through what the words themselves require, what the purpose of the clause requires, and what the drafters would have said if this exact case had been put to them in 1934. Then say which of those readings should govern, and whether the Sunday school teacher is in breach.
