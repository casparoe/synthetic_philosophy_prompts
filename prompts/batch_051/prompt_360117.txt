I keep going back and forth about what to make of the gender-equality paradox, and I need a negotiated verdict rather than a winner. On one side I'm drawn to the social-construction account of gendered career choices: kids are steered from early on by stereotypes, teacher attention, parental expectations, and peer norms, and that story nicely explains the huge variation across countries and decades in how many women end up in engineering. On the other side I have a firm intuition that if that account were mostly true, thoroughly gender-egalitarian societies should show smaller sex differences in the fields men and women choose — yet Sweden and Norway show, if anything, larger STEM gaps and more occupational segregation than a lot of less egalitarian countries. I know the cross-country indices and the statistics is contested, and I'm not asking you to referee the data; assume the pattern is real at least in its crude form. I want to know what it would take for the social-construction view to be the thing that gets amended rather than abandoned, what it would take for my intuition that egalitarianism should shrink the gaps to be the thing that goes, and whether there's a reading of socially constructed or egalitarian on which both survive. And assuming the paradox holds, what does and doesn't follow about the justice of trying to steer kids toward or away from particular fields?
