I am not a scholar, but I have spent years reading about the duel in Britain and in the northern and southern United States between roughly 1700 and 1870, and I keep circling the same disagreement about why it ended. One story is moral: preachers, pamphleteers, and reformers argued that dueling was unchristian, irrational, and a poor way to settle a question of truthfulness, and in time the argument prevailed. The other story is that what changed was not anyone's moral verdict on dueling but the social machinery that made it mandatory. On this view, a gentleman in 1800 could not turn down a challenge without losing his standing, while a gentleman in 1860 could refuse and remain respectable, because honor itself had been rebuilt. Refusing could now look like self-command or Christian manliness rather than cowardice, and a man's reputation came to rest less on being dangerous and more on being reliable, solvent, and self-controlled. Dueling was not argued out of existence; it fell away because the honor code that backed it lost the power to shame anyone.

I would like to know whether this second claim can be tested rather than merely asserted. The claim, as I understand it, runs roughly: the decline of dueling was caused chiefly by a change in honor norms—what counted as disgraceful and who possessed the standing to inflict shame—not by persuasion that dueling is wrong in the way that murder or theft is wrong. The distinction feels real, but it blurs when I press on it: honor and morality were tangled in the minds of the people involved, stated reasons may have been retrospective, and a man who declined a fight may not have known which motive was doing the work.

Could you set out the strongest empirical test of that claim that could actually be run with ordinary documentary evidence from digitized archives—diaries, letters, newspapers, court records, court-martial files, club and regiment records, and similar material? Be concrete: what evidence would you collect, what comparisons would you make, and what pattern of results would count for the claim and what would count against it? Then be honest about the residue, the part of the claim that no test of this kind could settle and why. Please keep the answer under 900 words.
