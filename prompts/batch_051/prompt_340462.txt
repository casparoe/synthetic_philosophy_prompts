Ok so in crim law we learned the two-prong test for void for vagueness: a criminal statute is void if (1) it fails to give a person of ordinary intelligence fair notice of what is actually prohibited, and (2) it invites arbitrary or discriminatory enforcement because it leaves too much to the judgment of cops, prosecutors, or juries. Now consider a made-up city ordinance that makes it a misdemeanor to "operate an unmanned aircraft within 50 feet of any non-domestic animal in a public park in a manner that visibly disturbs it," where "visible disturbance includes, but is not limited to, the animal fleeing, vocalizing, or halting normal activity." Is this void for vagueness or not? Go through both prongs honestly. The part I keep getting stuck on is this: "visibly disturbs" feels objective-ish because it's anchored to observable animal behavior, but the "includes but is not limited to" plus the fact that a park ranger on the ground has to decide what counts as disturbed seems to hand back exactly the discretion the second prong is supposed to prevent. Does the two-prong test handle this case cleanly, or does the case expose a gap in the test itself? If you think it fails one prong but not the other, tell me whether that is because the ordinance is badly drafted or because the prong itself is doing less work than advertised.
