I'm on the emergency services policy review board for a mid-size city, and we're redoing our mass-rescue triage protocol after a bad call. A parking garage collapsed and the incident commander had time to clear one of two sections: one had a single trapped person, the other had five. He went for the five; all five made it, the single person died before another crew could get there. The current protocol just says to prioritize saving the greatest number of lives, and a big faction on the board thinks that's obviously right because going by the numbers saved four extra people. Another faction wants a lottery rule for these one-vs-many choices, so each trapped person gets an equal shot at being the one rescued; their argument is that each person stands to lose exactly one life, nobody suffers five deaths, and the five don't collectively outrank the one (a Taurek-style point, roughly). A third faction wants the protocol to stay silent and let comanders use judgment. I need you to draft the board's decision memo for Thursday: recommend one of these options or a modified version, spell out the exact rule we'd put in the protocol, and give a serious treatment of the strongest argument for the option you reject. Keep it something an incident commander can actually use in the middle of the night, not a philosophy seminar.
