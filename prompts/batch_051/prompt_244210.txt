A forecasting platform maintains a single community probability distribution over a finite partition of possible states. It accepts reports from a pool of approved forecasters. Unconditional reports, of the form P(E) = r, are incorporated by ordinary Bayesian conditioning. Conditional reports, of the form P(E | C) = q, are incorporated by a minimum-relative-entropy update: the platform replaces its current distribution with the distribution that satisfies the stated conditional constraint while minimizing KL divergence from the current distribution. This is the Judy Benjamin problem — the problem of revising a probability distribution on learning a conditional probability, where standard conditioning gives no answer — turned into a standing institutional rule; do not assume the platform has solved it. Forecasters are paid by a proper scoring rule. Unconditional forecasts are scored whenever the relevant event resolves. Conditional forecasts are scored only when C occurs; if C occurs, the forecaster's stated probability q is scored against whether E occurs. If C never occurs, the conditional report is never resolved and the forecaster neither gains nor loses from it. The platform's published distribution is used by outside parties for contingent planning, so moving it has real downstream effects.

I want an analysis of how this rule would actually behave in practice, not a verdict on whether it is a good idea in the abstract. What does the rule incentivize forecasters to do when deciding between an unconditional and a conditional report, and when choosing the conditioning event C? How do sequences of reports behave — is the update order-dependent, and what happens when a later conditional conflicts with an earlier one? What happens when C has near-zero prior probability, or when a forecaster has evidence bearing on C itself but submits only a conditional? Can the rule be Dutch-booked or otherwise driven to a distribution no individual forecaster endorses? Which of these failure modes are fixable — for example, by scoring conditional reports differently, requiring forecasters to report a marginal on C, or modeling the process by which the forecaster came to know the conditional — and what do the fixes cost in terms of the platform's ability to use conditional information at all?
