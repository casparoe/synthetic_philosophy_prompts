I spent my working life in the natural sciences, mostly in ecology and evolutionary biology, and I have recently begun reading the philosophy of social science. The idea I keep returning to is path dependence. As I understand it, the strong version says more than that earlier states causally constrain later ones; it says that a particular historical sequence can lock in an outcome that was not uniquely favoured by the current environment, so that a rerun of history under identical selection pressures could have settled into a different and equally stable arrangement. The weak version says only that history matters in explaining how a system reached its present state, which seems uncontroversial and, frankly, close to the ordinary historical causation I knew as a scientist.

What I have not found is a clear account of how one would test the strong claim empirically rather than illustrate it with anecdotes. In my old discipline we would ask what observation would separate two hypotheses and what each result would imply. So here is what I would like from you. Take some social or economic phenomenon, such as a technical standard, a legal doctrine, an urban layout, or an institutional rule, and set out the best empirical design you can for deciding between the strong path-dependence claim and the alternative: that the observed outcome is the one that would have predictably emerged anyway, given the same background conditions. You may use comparisons across cases, natural experiments, or simulation, but the design should be concrete enough that a positive result and a negative result are distinguishable. State precisely what pattern would count as evidence for lock-in, what pattern would count as evidence for convergence on the same outcome, and what the test would leave unresolved.

I would also like to know whether the residual is merely practical, a problem of missing counterfactual data, or whether something in the concept of path dependence resists operationalisation altogether. For instance, even a clean experimental rerun of history might show that a different outcome was possible without showing that the actual outcome was in any useful sense suboptimal or accidental. Be as exact as you can about which part of the claim is being tested and which part is being assumed. The response should be in British English.
