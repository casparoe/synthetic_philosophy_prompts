I am a retired sensory physiologist, and I have recently begun reading philosophy of mind with a group. I would like an audit of the position I have drifted into before I present it. I will list my commitments as evenly as I can, without indicating which ones I am most attached to.

1. Strong representationalism: the phenomenal character of an experience is exhausted by its representational content.
2. Evaluativism about pain: a pain is a sensory-evaluative state rather than a neutral sensory registration of tissue damage to which an unpleasant feeling is later added. Its unpleasantness is the representation of the damage as bad for the subject, and that evaluative content is what constitutes the hurting.
3. Pain asymbolia is a genuine clinical dissociation. The patients I have in mind can locate, classify, and describe noxious stimuli, and they use the vocabulary of pain, but they do not wince, withdraw, or complain, and they insist that the sensation does not bother them.
4. Those patients are genuinely in pain. What asymbolia removes is not the pain but its unpleasantness.
5. The asymbolic patient's state is not bad for them in the way ordinary pain is bad, precisely because it lacks unpleasantness.
6. The badness of ordinary pain is intrinsic to its unpleasantness. Pain is not merely instrumentally bad because it signals tissue damage or disrupts behavior; the hurting itself is the harm.
7. I take first-person reports at face value unless there is strong reason not to. I therefore accept both parts of the asymbolic report, the pain and the indifference.
8. I am inclined to treat pain as a single natural kind, so an adequate account should unify normal and asymbolic pain rather than dismiss the latter as a different state that merely shares a name.

I would like a pairwise diagnosis. Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, and which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, give the cheapest repair for each and what it would cost, and then say which single revision would do the most to make the set cohere.
