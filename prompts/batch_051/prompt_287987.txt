I help set process for a small custom-software company. We're debating a rule: before anyone starts building a feature, they have to write a plan covering dependencies, edge cases, and what order they'd do the work in. Then the plan gets sealed until the feature ships, and only then do we compare the plan to what actually happened. The thinking is that you get the benefits of planning—having actually worked through the problem—without the rigidity, because nobody can keep checking the plan day to day. Walk me through what this actually does to how people build. Will the plans turn into theater once everyone knows they're sealed? What do we learn, or fool ourselves into thinking we've learned, by comparing plan and outcome at the end? Where does it get gamed first, and is a variant where a teammate reads your plan but you can't any better then just sealing it? Also, what does this rule quietly assume about what planning is for, and is that assumption sound? End by saying how confident you are in the whole picture and which specific point you're least sure of.
