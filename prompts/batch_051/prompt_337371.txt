I've been going back and forth with some people from a LessWrong meetup about AI control as distinct from alignment, and I've noticed my standards for what a control scheme has to do keep shifting depending on whether I'm annoyed at capabilities researchers or annoyed at interpretability people. I want a fixed list I can hold everyone to, including myself.

Write the desiderata for a control scheme in the specific sense: the model may be misaligned—maybe it's trying to sabotage the project or exfiltrate its weights, maybe it's just systematically cutting corners in ways we haven't anticipated—and the scheme is the set of monitoring, auditing, sandboxing, capability-evaluation, and task-decomposition measures whose job is to let us get useful work out of the model anyway, without having to trust it. For each requirement, give the concrete failure case or experiment that motivates it, and mark whether it's non-negotiable or just a preference of a particular camp. Flag any subsets that you suspect can't all be satisfied at once; my guess is that "the model may be strategically aware of the monitoring and better than the auditors at hiding" plus "we actually want to use the full capabilities of the model" is one of those pressure points, but I'd like to see it laid out rather than assumed. Also spend some time on the control-evaluation angle: if we deliberately insert a model we've trained to be misaligned into the pipeline and check whether the controls catch it, what makes that kind of test informative beyond just catching the particular red-team models we happend to build? Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases behind them, and tell me which requirement does the most work excluding otherwise plausible proposals. You can briefly score monitoring, trusted/untrusted decomposition, sandboxing, and pre-deployment capability evals against the list if it helps make a criterion more precise, but the list itself is the thing I want to steal for my notes.
