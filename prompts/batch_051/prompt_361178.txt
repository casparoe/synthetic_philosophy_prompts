Hey, I've been kicking around an essay idea about metacognition and I'd appreciate some comments before I take it further.

The rough claim is that "thinking about thinking" gets treated as an unmixed good in a lot of education and self-help contexts, but there are actually cases where it's harmful or at least counterproductive. I want to defend a moderate sceptical point: metacognition is helpful in some contexts, but not always, and the difference has to do with whether it's ocurring during a task or after it.

Three things I'd say in the essay:

1. Online monitoring can degrade skilled performance. If I'm playing a piece I know well and I start watching my fingers too closely, or monitoring whether my tempo is steady, the performance usually gets worse, not better. This is the sorta thing people mean by choking under pressure, but I'd frame it as a metacognitive cost: the second-order process eats resources that the first-order process needed.

2. There's a regress worry. Once I step back and ask "are my judgments about this reliable?", there's no natural place to stop, becuase I can then ask "and is my judgment that my judgments are reliable itself reliable?", and so on. So reflective metacognition doesn't automatically produce a stable improvement in my epistemic position—it can just push the same problem up a level.

3. The value of metacognition might mostly be in off-line contexts, like reviewing a mistake after the fact or planning out a problem-solving strategy ahead of time. In those cases it gives you models of your own performance that you can tinker with. But during the performance itself, it often just gets in the way.

Before I write this up, do you think: (a) am I conflating metacognition with plain self-focused attention in the music example? (b) is the regress point actually doing any work, or is it just a rhetorical trick? and (c) would the essay be more interesting if I admitted the "harm" here is pretty mild and mostly about performance, rather than claiming metacognition is sometimes deeply bad? Any suggestions for how to make the claim sharper without overreaching would be great.
