Teaching an upper-level seminar on Locke, 16 undergrads, 75 minutes. I want them to actually feel why the labour-mixing theory of acquisition gets weird once you add the proviso—the requirement that an acquisition has to leave enough and just as good for others. I'd rather they run into the problem themselves before I name it, rather then me explaining it. Can you design a runnable game around claiming land under those two rules? Maybe a finite grid of plots of differing quality, labour tokens that cost something (time or points), and a rule that you only own a plot once you've worked it; then the proviso gets checked at the moment of each claim. I want later players to end up stuck with worse or farther land and to start arguing about whether improving a bad plot can count as leaving enough and as good, and whether the first movers quietly shifted the baseline for everyone else. A spoilage rule for perishable stuff would help too, so they see why hoarding is limited before money shows up—and then maybe a second phase where durable tokens standing in for money appear and the spoilage limit stops biting. But the point should be that they run into the tension themselves: the game should make them feel that the proviso is doing way more work than it looks, and that the labour theory might only give you a claim to the value you added, not the whole plot. Include rules, roles or positions if needed, a timeline for the 75 minutes, a debrief mapping specific moments in the game onto Lockean ideas, what will predictably go wrong and how to handle it, and an honest note on what the activity can't capture about Locke's view (the theological background, presumably, and the role of actual violence).
