I'm a faculty member on our university's academic-integrity board, and I've got to draft the decision memo for a case that's divided us. Our code says a student is responsible for any unauthorized course material posted to a shared drive they create or administer, regardless of who actually uploaded it. A student created a shared study drive for her econ course; another student posted a paid problem-set answer key to it without her knowledge. The rule as written makes her strictly liable, and doesn't mention intent. Half the board says we shouldn't punish someone who didn't know and couldn't reasonably have prevented it; the other half says the text is the text. Please write the short opinion I can circulate before Thursday's meeting: recommend a concrete decision in her case with the reasoning, including whether we should read a mens rea requirement into the rule despite its wording and when punishing without fault is ever defensible. It has to be something we can actually vote on, and it should take the rule's wording seriously rather than just overriding it.
