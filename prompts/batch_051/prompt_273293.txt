I keep tripping over how people sort moral commitments into two piles: preferences, which are supposed to count in moral reasoning, and biases, which are supposed to be corrected for or explained away. The sorting does real work—call someone's view a bias and you no longer have to accommodate it—but I can't find a principled way to do the sorting that isn't question-begging. Part of the trouble is that the natural test for a bias seems to be that it departs from correct judgment, but in the moral case what counts as correct judgment is exactly what's contested. If a person treats loyalty to kin as a central value, is that a genuine moral preference or just an in-group bias, and can the question be settled without already having a moral view in hand? Could you tease apart what, if anything, actually distinguishes the two? Is it a live conceptual distinction, or mostly a rhetorical way of dressing up moral disagreement as a finding about the other person's psychology?
