I chair the rules committee of a 24-unit housing co-op, and nearly every month we vote on a rule that reaches into people's homes: pets, noise, smoking on balconies, subletting a spare room. Lately I keep coming back to Sen's liberal paradox, or at least my half-remembered version of it: if the group respects both Pareto (when everyone prefers a rule to no rule, that preference has to carry) and a protected private sphere (each member gets to decide certain things about their own home), you can get group preferences that cycle, where no decision looks stable. I want something my committee can actually use in a two-hour monthly meeting, with ordinary residents doing the procedure, before we vote. It should be a checklist or decision tree: the questions to ask in order, when a proposal gets slapped down because it invades someone's private sphere, when a near-unanimous preference should override that, how to spot a possible cycle and what do do when we find one, and where the procedure stops and a person has to make a values call that no procedure can make for them. Then run it on two proposals: (a) a ban on dogs over 20kg, which about 80% want and one owner of a large dog is furious about; (b) a ban on using your unit as a short-term rental, which almost everyone in the building wants but two members say it's their private business what they do with their spare room. I'd probably vote for the dog ban myself, so make the procedure honest enough to tell me when I'm wrong.
