I'm giving a departmental colloquium on a paper I've been kicking around, "Russell's Theory of Descriptions Without the Acquaintance Principle." The paper argues that the formal analysis of definite descriptions in Russell's On Denoting (1905) does not actually require his epistemic distinction between knowledge by acquaintance and knowledge by description, and that the center of mass passage has been misread as carrying more weight than it does. I want you to prep me for Q&A.

The audience: our philosophy department, but the room will include a visiting epistemologist who works on acquaintance theories (she will hate the whole project), two philosophers of language who mostly do direct reference and will be friendly to Russell but not to my reading, a Frege defector, and a few grad students who just read Kripke's Naming and Necessity. I don't want generic objections; I want the ones these specific people will actually raise, phrased the way they'd say them.

The passage I keep coming back to, and which I think half the room will use against me, is this from Russell's On Denoting:

"The subject of denoting is of very great importance, not only in logic and mathematics, but also in the theory of knowledge. For example, we know that the center of mass of the solar system at a definite instant is some definite point, and we can affirm a number of propositions about it; but we have no immediate acquaintance with this point, which is only known to us by description."

I read that passage as Russell motivating his project but not yet delivering the theory of denoting phrases; the acquaintance/description stuff is background epistemology, not part of the logical analysis itself. My argument is that the theory of descriptions stands or falls on its own formal adequacy, not on whether we are ever acquainted with the center of mass of the solar system.

So give me a prep sheet: the six to eight objections I'll actually get, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased roughly in their voices. For each, give me a two-minute reply and a note on where my reply is soft. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. And dont let me get away with hand-waving the Frege point; that's where I'm weakest.
