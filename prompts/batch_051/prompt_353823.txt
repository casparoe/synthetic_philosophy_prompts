For a dissertation chapter, I've been working through the literature on moral luck, and I keep encountering the same Nagelian taxonomy, which seems to be treated as the complete standard list: resultant luck (luck in how one's actions turn out), circumstantial luck (luck in which situations one faces), constitutive luck (luck in one's temperament, capacities, and dispositions), and causal luck (luck in the antecedent causal history of one's action and of the character one acts from). These four, or minor variants, appear to cover every case the literature discusses, and I'm worried I've lost any sense of what an alternative would look like. I'd like you to propose a fifth kind of moral luck: one that is not on this list and is not merely a rewording, a special case, or a conjunction of items on it. Give the clearest candidate you can, state what distinguishes it from each of the four, and then say honestly whether it deserves to stand as an additional kind or collapses back into one of them. Also say how confident you are in that assessment and identify the point you're least sure about.
