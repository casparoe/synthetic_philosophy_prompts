I work on eighteenth-century moral philosophy, and I have been trying to organize the main readings of Adam Smith's impartial spectator in The Theory of Moral Sentiments. Here are the interpretations I have collected so far:

(1) The social-mirror reading: the spectator is a generalization or internalization of the actual judgments of the concrete people whose approval and disapproval we learn through sympathetic exchange.

(2) The ideal-observer reading: the spectator is a deliberate idealization—impartial, well-informed, and free from local prejudice—that gives moral judgments a standpoint-neutral form.

(3) The theological-Stoic reading: the spectator's authority is ultimately grounded in its conformity to the laws of nature, or of the Deity, so that its verdicts are binding because they reflect the larger order Smith sometimes invokes.

(4) The higher-order-self reading: Smith splits the agent into an acting self and a spectatorial self; the impartial spectator is best understood as a second-order evaluative perspective within a single mind, rather than a representation of society.

(5) The cognitive-auditing reading: the impartial spectator functions as a procedure for checking whether the intensity of a passion is proportional to its cause, making it primarily a device for regulating emotional propriety rather than a distinct moral ontology.

I want an interpretation that is not in that list and is not a restatement, special case, or combination of anything in it. It should say what the impartial spectator is, where its authority comes from, and how it is supposed to be formed or accessed. Then tell me honestly whether it is any good, and in particular whether it can explain something the listed readings struggle with: Smith clearly thinks the impartial spectator can correct the judgments of actual, local spectators, even though the spectator seems to be introduced as a product of sympathetic exchange with those very spectators. Does your proposed reading solve that problem, and if not, what does that show about the cost of making Smith consistent on this point?
