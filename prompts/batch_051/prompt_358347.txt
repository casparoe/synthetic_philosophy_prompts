Give me a definition of "really rotating" — spinning in a way that isn't just relative to some chosen frame — that gets these seven cases right. (1) The water in Newton's bucket after it's been spun a while, surface concave, turning with the bucket. (2) The same water just after the bucket starts turning, when the bucket is rotating around it but the surface is still flat. (3) The bucket held still while all the stars and everything else in the universe are spun around it once a day. (4) Two globes connected by a taut cord in an otherwise empty universe, circling their common center. (5) A single featureless sphere alone in an empty universe, spinning about an axis. (6) A person on a merry-go-round with their eyes closed, feeling pushed outward. (7) A person standing on the equator.

I think (1), (4), and (6) are clearly really rotating, and (2) clearly isn't. I have no idea about (3), (5), and (7). Propose a definition, run it through all seven, and tell me where it surprises you. Then say which verdict is least secure and whether the definition you end up with needs spacetime itself to be a thing, or only needs other bodies.

Write your answer in American English.
