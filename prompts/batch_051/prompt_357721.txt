I'm a retired physicist — spent the last 25 years in computational modeling, mostly climate and then later autonomous systems as a sort of consultant — and I've only recently starting reading moral philosophy properly, so apologies if I'm butchering the terminology. Take the classic trolley problem, but there's no person at the switch. The switch is controlled by an autonomous rail-management system trained with reinforcement learning on millions of simulated runaway-train scenarios, with a reward function that includes minimizing expected casualties. One day the brakes fail, five workers are on the main track and one on the siding, and the system diverts the train, killing the one. So far this is just the standard self-driving-trolley twist. What I haven't seen anyone discuss is this wrinkle: the system's decision on the day depended on a confidence threshold that came out of an automated hyperparameter search with a random seed. With seed 417 the policy diverts; with seed 418, on the very same scene, it doesn't. No engineer ever inspected that threshold, and the safety board that signed off knew only that validation accuracy was good and that some near-tie cases would be settled in a way no one could state in advance.

What I'd like to know is how this seed detail changes the responsibility-gap discussion. In the usual autonomous-car cases you can still point to the person who wrote the rule "minimize expected casualties." Here nobody wrote the marginal rule; the rule itself is an artifact of a random search. Does it make sense to say the board is responsible for the one death because they approved a process they knew was partly chancy, or does that just relocate the gap up a level? More concretely, how do the following three positions fare in this version: (1) responsibility requires tracing the outcome to a specific human decision at the level of the causing action, (2) responsibility can attach to knowingly creating a chancy system with a foreseen risk of harm, and (3) the gap is illusory because the manufacturer or operator is strictly liable regardless of intent or foresight. I'd want a verdict from each of those, and then your own judgment on whether a genuine gap opens up here.
