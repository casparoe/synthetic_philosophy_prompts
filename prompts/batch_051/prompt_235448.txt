A postdoc in my group won't stop saying the 'which animals are conscious?' problem is easier than it looks: an animal has felt pain just in case it uses one integrated body-model to weigh competing needs, like foraging versus escape, after tissue damage. On his view nociception and reflexes don't count because they don't update a central trade-off—they're just hardwired loops. I can't tell whether that's a criterion for experience or just for a fancy controller, so I want to test it on a bumblebee (or a tick if you think that's a cleaner case). Design the strongest experiment you can to seperate conscious pain from nociception plus clever learning. Be concrete: what would you manipulate, what would you measure, and what result would convince you either way—that the bee is a subject of experience, or just a damage-response system? Then be honest about what a clean positive result still would not settle, for instance whether there's anything it's like for the bee, whether your operationalization tracks the thing we actually care about, and whether the postdoc's integrated-model line is itself the right place to draw the line.
