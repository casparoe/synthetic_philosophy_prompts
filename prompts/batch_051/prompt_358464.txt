I am preparing for a sixth-form debating competition on the motion "This house believes that ranking the worst atrocities in history by severity is obscene rather than meaningful." I currently put the probability that such ranking can be a meaningful and morally legitimate exercise at about 0.4; my debate partner puts it at about 0.8. Before either of us writes our case, I would like your credence on that proposition, derived in the open. Please decompose it into the sub-questions your number depends on — for example, whether the suffering of victims in different historical periods can be compared on any common scale; whether magnitude is even the right dimension on which to weigh moral horror, as opposed to symbolic uniqueness, proximity, or betrayal; whether the purpose of the ranking (prevention, commemoration, education) can justify the act; and how heavily to weigh the risk that an ordinal list insults or instrumentalises the dead. Assign a rough probability to each sub-question, show how they combine into your overall credence, and then say which single sub-question your final number is most sensitive to and what evidence or argument would move it most. Write in British English.
