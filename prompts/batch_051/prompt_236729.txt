Our university's research-funding rules, written in 2019, say: 'No University funds shall be used for research aimed at bringing about transformative artificial intelligence.' I'm on the commitee that has to apply this to a sociology PhD's proposal to fine-tune an open-weights language model on the university's own counselling-centre transcripts, so it can triage student intake forms and flag which students should be called first. In 2019 the drafters clearly meant some future system that crosses a threshold; now the proposal just fine-tunes a tool that has arguably already transformed how the campus writes, codes and grades, while each piece on its own looks mundane. On one reading of 'transformative,' the clause never fires until a dramatic capability jump; on another, we've been inside the transformation for a couple years and the clause catches nearly everything. Work through the words, the purpose, and what the 2019 drafters would have said if you'd asked them about fine-tuning a freely downloadable model on local data, and talk me through which approach to interpretation should win. But the part I actually can't stop thinking about is whether the real dispute is about the clause at all, or about whether transformative AI has already happened.
