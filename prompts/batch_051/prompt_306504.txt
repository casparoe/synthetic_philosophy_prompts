Over the last few months I've got slightly obsessive about illusionism — the idea that phenomenal consciousness, the what-it's-like side of experience, is a kind of introspective illusion rather than a real thing the brain produces. I don't have any background in philosophy proper, so apologies if I've mangled the wording of any of this; I've just been piecing it together from podcasts and comment sections. I started out assuming it was obviously false, but the more I read around the more I kept finding decent-seeming arguements for it, and I've now got a list of four that all feel like they're doing different work.

The first is about introspection itself: introspection doesn't hand us a transparent view of our own minds, it just makes us *believe* there's a vivid private qualitative show going on, and a convincing illusion would feel exactly like that, so our confidence that qualia are real proves less than we think. The second is about the explanatory gap: the reason consciousness seems to resist physical explanation is that our concepts of it are built to present it as non-physical, so the hard problem is a feature of those concepts rather than evidence that there's something real left unexplained. The third is about causal closure: a genuinely non-physical conscious property would have to either do something in the brain or be an epiphenomenal extra, and since the physical story already accounts for behaviour, illusionism lets you avoid smuggling in causally useless extras. The fourth is about qualia themselves: whenever you try to say what a quale is over and above what the state does and the information it carries, the thing you were pointing at slips away — every answer is either empty or collapses back into something physical — which suggests there was never a real thing there to begin with.

I've got a strong feeling I'm missing at least one more standard argument for illusionism, maybe a really obvious one. What would you add to the list? It should be genuinely different from those four — not a rewording, not a special case, not two of them stitched together — and I'd be interested in whether it pulls its own weight or mostly borrows its force from one of the ones I've already got.
