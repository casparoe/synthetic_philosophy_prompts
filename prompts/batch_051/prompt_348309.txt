Write the joint statement that a committed Kosslyn-style depictivist and a committed Pylyshyn-style descriptivist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration about mental imagery. They should start from the shared empirical record — for example, the roughly linear relation between angular disparity and response time in rotation tasks, and between scanned distance and response time in scanning tasks — and agree on what those results do and do not show before getting to the disagreement. The statement should include the claims both sides can sign without flinching; the precise statement of their remaining disagreement, with each side's formulation written so that side would accept the wording; the genuine cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of them; and for each crux, the experiment or argument both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Be specific rather than saying more data on imagery would help: get into task demands, demand characteristics, tacit knowledge, format versus content, and what sort of result would embarass each view. Write it all as continuous prose, like an email or a short memo — no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables. Put the agreed claims first, then work through the disagreements and cruxes in plain paragraphs.
