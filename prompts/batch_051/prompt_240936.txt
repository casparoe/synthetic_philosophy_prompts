I work in the licensing group at a streaming service, and part of my job is deciding whether a song or recording we're about to put up — or clear for a show or ad — counts as the same musical work as something we already license, or as a new one. This matters because it changes who gets paid and whether we need a separate clearance. I do a dozen or so of these a week, sometimes on a tight deadline when a show is shipping, and I'm not a musician and not a lawyer; the lawyers only want to see the ones my team can't settle. I need a written procedure my coworkers and I can actually run on a file in about fifteen minutes, especialy when we disagree.

I'm not asking for a legal rule. I want the procedure grounded in whatever philosophers of music and metaphysicans actually know about what a musical work is, what makes one performance a performance of the same work, how a recording relates to the work and to the performance, and how much you can change something before it becomes a different thing. I need the questions in order, what answer means you can close the file or move on, where the procedure has to stop and hand the decision to a lawyer, and which steps are judgment calls dressed up as a rule.

Then run it on these four:
- a straight cover of a song by another artist: same melody and lyrics, different instrumentation and slightly different phrasing;
- a remaster of a recording we already license, where the mix changed but no new performance was recorded;
- a new track built around a three-second sample from a licensed recording, looped into the hook of an otherwise original song;
- a jazz trio's version of a standard where they keep the 32-bar changes and the title but none of the original melody, improvising throughout.

Include at least one case where the procedure tells me to treat the file differently than my company probably treats it today, and close by saying what the procedure can't settle, where a person has to decide.

Start the response with the procedure itself — no preface, no "here's a checklist" — and don't restate my question at the top.
