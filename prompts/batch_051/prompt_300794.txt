I've recently been put in charge of settling how our group chooses default priors for a forecasting model, and the usual advice—"use maximum entropy," "use a Jeffreys prior," "just pick something vague and run sensitivity checks"—feels like answers before anyone has said what the question is. Before I commit to any rule, I want the specification that a rule for assigning a rational or objective prior has to meet: the cases it must handle, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the results from probability theory it cannot contradict, with the consideration behind each requirement, and a clear marking of which requirements are non-negotiable and which are only the commitments of one camp. In particular, I want to know whether the following can be jointly satisfied: symmetry under relabeling; invariance under reparameterization (the lesson of Bertrand's paradox); consistency under marginalization and under conditioning on later information; the ability to distinguish genuine no-information cases from cases with known constraints; and immunity to dominance or Dutch-book arguments. If some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, say so outright, and tell me which single requirement excludes the most popular proposals. Then score, briefly, the principle of indifference, maximum entropy, and transformation-group or reference-prior methods against the list. Please answer in American English.
