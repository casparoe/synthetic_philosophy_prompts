I am trying to work out what I actually believe about humanitarian military intervention and regime change, going by my reactions to a set of cases.

Here are my gut verdicts. I think NATO's 1999 bombing of Yugoslavia over Kosovo was justified, even though it lacked Security Council authorization; I also think bypassing the Council was a real wrong and should not be papered over. I think the 2003 Iraq invasion was not justified, partly because it rested on false claims about weapons of mass destruction and partly because it aimed at regime change. I think the 2011 Libya intervention began justified, since it was Security Council–authorized to protect civilians, but the slide into rebel-assisted regime change and the killing of Gaddafi left me uneasy without making the whole affair unjustified. I think the failure to intervene in Rwanda in 1994 was a grave moral failure, and that intervention would have been justified. On Syria after 2011 I am genuinely unsure: I lean toward thinking a limited intervention to stop mass atrocities would have been justified, but the way Libya unfolded makes me doubt it would have stayed limited.

These verdicts seem to pull against one another. I condemn Iraq as a US/UK-led invasion resting on false pretenses, yet I defend Kosovo despite the lack of authorization. And I treat Rwanda non-intervention as a clear failure while remaining unsure about Syria partly because Libya went badly, even though it is unclear why Syria non-intervention would be any less bad than Rwanda.

Is there a principle about authorization, likely consequences, permissible aims, or regime change that captures all or most of these verdicts? What does it say about a case I have not listed, such as an unauthorized intervention to stop an unfolding genocide when the Security Council is blocked by a veto? And which of my verdicts does the principle force me to give up? Please also separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
