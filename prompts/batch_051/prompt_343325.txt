Dawkins's selfish gene argument for genes being the real units of selection mostly took for granted that genes get copied down vertical lineages with high enough fidelity that selection can accumulate adaptations in a gene over many generations. The last few decades of microbial genomics make that look like a special case: horizontal gene transfer is rampant, and viral insertions and transposable elements mean genes can hop sideways between quite unrelated lineages and often work fine in the new genome. I genuinely cant tell whether this kills the gene's-eye view, repairs it, or accidentally strengthens it. Walk me through what the selfish gene argument actually needs to be true as a claim about units of selection, which premise HGT puts pressure on, and whether the argument survives if you drop the vertical-lineage assumption. If it survives, say what the unit is now and whether its still the same kind of replicator Dawkins had in mind; if it doesn't, say what the debate should be reframed around instead.
