Conventionalism about calendrical epochs seems to me nearly undeniable. Any epoch is just a zero point on a timeline; the astronomical and historical facts are invariant under a shift of origin, so the question which moment objectively deserves to be 'year zero' looks like asking which end of a meter stick deserves the zero mark. Yet I also find myself unwilling to treat the actual choice as inert. The dominant civil calendar numbers human history from a traditional religious birth and has no year zero between 1 BCE and 1 CE; alternatives — an epoch tied to the start of the Holocene, an astronomical epoch, or a political founding chosen for neutrality — seem not merely more convenient but more fitting, as if a calendar's origin should answer to the scale and purpose of the historical record rather than to a parochial event. If conventionalism is right, that intuition of fittingness is an aesthetic or political preference disguised as a factual question and ought to be given up; if the intuition is right, conventionalism underdescribes what is at stake in choosing an epoch. What would it take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what would it take for conventionalism to be amended or abandoned, and is there a stable view on which the choice is a pure convention and still legitimately criticizable?
