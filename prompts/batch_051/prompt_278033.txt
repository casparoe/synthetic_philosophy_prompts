Here are my intuitive verdicts about how to compare worlds, each consisting of an infinite line of lives—one life per position, numbered 1, 2, 3, and so on—with a number assigned to each life's well-being. I want to know whether one principle captures all of these verdicts, and if none does, which verdict has to go. Please state the best principle you can construct, say how it handles each case below, be explicit about which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate, and tell me what it predicts for the further case at the end.

1. World A gives every position well-being 1; world B gives every position well-being 2. I judge B better.
2. World C runs 1, 0, 1, 0, ... starting with 1; world D runs 0, 1, 0, 1, ... starting with 0. I judge them equally good: the only difference is that one pattern is shifted one place along the line, and a mere displacement should not change the value of the world.
3. World E gives every position well-being 1; world F is identical except that the first position has well-being 0. I judge E a little better.
4. World G repeats 1, 1, 0, ...; world H repeats 1, 0, ... . I judge G better, because it has the better long-run ratio of lives at 1 to lives at 0, even though both worlds have infinitely many lives at 0.
5. World I gives every position well-being 1; world J gives position n (n = 1, 2, 3, ...) well-being 1 + 1/2^n, so J's positions run 1.5, 1.25, 1.125, and so on, approaching 1 from above. Every position in J is strictly better off than its counterpart in I, and the total gain summed across the infinite line is only 1 (the series 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ...). I judge J better.

The case I keep going back and forth on: world K has one position at well-being −100 and every later position at well-being 1, while world L has well-being 1 everywhere. I cannot decide whether K is worse than L, or whether the single bad position is simply drowned by the infinite tail of good ones.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. Do not add a preamble and do not restate the question.
