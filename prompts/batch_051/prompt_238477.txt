Two friends in my philosophy of mind study group got into it after the session, and I'm still chewing over who had the better of it. Dan opened with the zombie point: imagine someone molecule-for-molecule identical to you, same behavior, same brain states, but with no consciousness at all—nothing it's like to be them. If that's really conceivable, he said, physicalism is false, because you'd have every physical fact and still not have the experience. Sara pushed back that being able to picture something doesn't make it possible: people could conceive of water not being H2O, or Hesperus not being Phosphorus, and those turned out to be impossible in the relevant sense. Dan said those cases depended on not knowing the underlying facts; the zombie case is different because you're supposed to hold all the physical facts fixed and still conceive of no experience. Sara said holding all the physical facts fixed "in your head" isn't the same as seeing what those facts entail—he might just be failing to recognize that experience is one of the facts. Dan said that move assumes physicalism rather than defending it: the whole question is whether the complete physical story entails experience, and it's a real datum that there seems to be an explanatory gap. Sara answered that water/H2O had a historical explanatory gap too—nobody could derive wetness from hydrogen and oxygen a priori—so the gap might be a fact about our concepts of experience, not about a genuine possible world. Dan shot back that there's still an asymmetry: once you accept water is H2O, you can't conceive of all the H2O facts without water, but even after accepting any physical story you like, a zombie still seems conceivable. Sara said that's becasue we pick out pain by how it feels, and physical descriptions don't mention that, so the lingering "residue" is a feature of our way of thinking about experience, not a feature of reality. They asked me to referee, and I honestly couldn't. Go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about what, and whether there's a distinction they're both missing that would break the deadlock.
