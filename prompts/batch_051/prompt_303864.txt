An asymmetry that keeps nagging me: if a private courier's van swerves onto the pavement and wrecks my garden wall, I can sue them and, if they were negligent, they have to put things right. But if a council bin lorry does exactly the same damage, in many systems I can only claim on whatever terms the state has chosen to allow, and whole categories of official action are simply off-limits. Same careless driver, same wall, same loss; the only difference is who signs the driver's pay cheque. It seems to hang on the idea that the state is somehow not the sort of agent that can wrong you in the way a firm can. Is there a genuine principle here, or is it just the old line that the sovereign can't be hauled before its own courts, modernised? Give me the strongest justification you can find, test it against a couple of neighbouring cases, and say honestly wether the asymmetry should stay, be cut back, or be given up. Please answer in British English.
