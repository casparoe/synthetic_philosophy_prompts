I run the wordlist side of a small dictionary house, mostly learner's dictionaries of English plus a couple of bilingual desk dictionaries. Every time we commision a new list the same fight breaks out. Nobody thinks 'cats' deserves its own headword — it's just 'cat' with an -s. Nobody thinks 'happiness' should be buried inside the entry for 'happy' even though it's every bit as regular. But between those two cases the boundary is mush. Is 'opener' a form of 'open' or a word in its own right? What about 'opened' in 'an opened envelope', 'drinking' in 'drinking is boring', 'the rich', 'better', 'children', 'anti-vaxxer'? The freelancers have strong feelings and they are not the same feelings twice in a row.

I want a decision procedure we can actually run on a form in about fifteen minutes, deciding whether it gets its own entry or is treated as an inflected form under a headword. My editors know a little grammar but not the higher-end morphology, and I have to defend every borderline call to the managing editor, so the steps need to be explicit and blame-ready: which tests go in what order, what counts as a pass or a fail at each step, where the procedure has to stop and hand the call to a human. Then run it on 'cats', 'happiness', and at least one genuinely contested form (from the list above or another you pick), include one case where the procedure gives a result we'll find annoying or counter to house style, and say which criterion is doing most of the real work and which are just heuristics. Also flag where you expect this thing to misfire.
