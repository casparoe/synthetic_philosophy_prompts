I am a working statistician who builds and compares predictive models. My fluent vocabulary includes: a fixed outcome space and a true data-generating process; parametric and nonparametric model families; estimators and risk under an explicit loss; proper scoring rules (log score, Brier score, CRPS); calibration and sharpness; bias-variance decomposition; nested and non-nested model comparison; f-divergences such as KL and Hellinger distance; and the difference between M-closed and M-open inference. I know philosophy of science only at a general, secondhand level.

Restate the philosophical problem of verisimilitude in that vocabulary. The thing to be translated is the debate over whether, and in what sense, one false scientific theory can be closer to the truth than another — for example, Einstein's theory closer than Newton's, and Newton's closer than Aristotle's. Include Popper's motivating idea that truth is a regulative ideal while accepted theories are usually false; his attempted definition of verisimilitude as a theory's truth-content minus its falsity-content, where content is the class of the theory's logical consequences; the standard objection that this definition cannot order false theories as intended, because for false theories the two requirements pull against each other; and the later shift toward distance-from-the-truth accounts defined over a space of possible states. Do not narrate the history; translate it.

Then give me the residue report, term by term: theory, truth, content, truth-content, falsity-content, the comparative failure of the content-based definition, and the distance-based replacement. For each, say whether it maps cleanly onto my machinery, maps only with distortion and exactly where it breaks, or has no counterpart.

Finish by adjudicating the residue. Is what fails to translate a defect in my vocabulary — for example, because my framework can represent predictive-distribution differences but has no place for a theory's extra-predictive commitments (laws, unobservable entities, explanatory structure, counterfactuals), and treats two theories with the same predictions as equivalent? Or is it a defect in the philosophical problem — a demand for comparison without a specified measure or scoring rule, which by my standards is underdetermined rather than deep? Weigh both readings, and in particular address two false theories that are predictively equivalent on all feasible tests but posit different underlying structure. Keep the translation honest: do not redefine "theory" as "predictive model," and do not quietly install a preferred divergence as the obvious measure of closeness to truth. The residue matters to me more than the correspondences.
