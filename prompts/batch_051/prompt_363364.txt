I keep bumping into an asymmetry I can't justify. When a student spends twenty minutes retracing why she believed a bad argument, we praise her for thinking about her own thinking; when a dancer, mid-performance, starts attending to where her foot is and whether her usual transition is really the best option, we say she's in her head and ruining it. Both are just a person turning their attention onto their own performance. The split shows up again between a comedian who's good because he isn't monitoring his timing and a therapist who's good because she monitors her own reactions as they happen. Is there one principle that seperates the times self-reflection makes you better from the times it wrecks you, or are we just indulging a culturally approved superstition about "being self-aware" when it flatters us and dropping it when it would make us choke? Give me the strongest principle you can find, pressure-test it against learning piano, debugging your own code, and rumination, and say honestly whether the asymmetry survives.
