So I just spent the whole evening arguing with a friend about dementia advance directives, specifically the kind of case where someone writes down, while still fully competent, that if she gets severe Alzheimers she doesn't want antibiotics for even a minor pneumonia, and then years later she's in the care home smiling at the activity calendar, eating applesauce, and holding out her arm when the nurse needs to check her blood pressure. My friend thinks the earlier self clearly wins; he kept saying that the entire point of a living will is that you get to decide ahead of time. I kept pushing back, and by the end I was pretty sure we had covered all the standard moves, because we just went around and around. There's the precedent autonomy view, where autonomy isn't just about isolated choices but about authority over how your life as a whole goes, so the earlier competent self gets to govern what happens to the later one. There's the critical-versus-experiential interests move, where the earlier self has critical interests in the shape of her biography and the later self has only experiential interests in comfort and small pleasures, and the first kind is supposed to matter more. There's the Ulysses contract analogy, where the earlier self is like Ulysses tied to the mast and the later self is the one asking to be untied. There's the identity objection, which says the dementia has made the later self different enough that the earlier self's authority has lapsed. There's the evidentiary move, where the directive is just evidence about what the earlier self would have preferred, and it can be outweighed by what the later self seems to want now. And there's the current-interests move, where you set the document aside and just ask what is best for the person actually in front of you. I want an argument or consideration in this debate that is not on that list and is not just a rewording, a special case, or some combination of items already on it. It doesn't need to be a full position; it could be a new argument for one side, a new objection to one of the moves, whatever. Then tell me what it adds to the list, and be honest about whether it's actually any good.
