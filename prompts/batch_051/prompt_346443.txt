I keep going back and forth on the Fermi paradox, and I'm trying to make it actually bite on spending decisions instead of just being a puzzle. Suppose I run a small private foundation, call it $50M over ten years, and I can back only two or three of: SETI listening, METI messaging, basic work on the origin of life and the hard steps to intelligence, research on whether technological civilizations reliably survive their own bottlenecks, and long-term space settlement infrastructure. Rank the main resolution families — Great Filter behind us, Great Filter ahead, rare intelligence, everyone hiding, physics rules out interstellar travel, and so on — by how much they ought to change those funding priorities compared to a deafult where I just fund whatever looks most respectable. I care less about which answer is true than about which answers are load-bearing for the money. For the top two, spell out which concrete program they would most change and why; also flag any family that's basically decision-irrelevant even if true, because accepting it would leave the choices unchanged. And tell me what near-term finding would most flip the ordering.
