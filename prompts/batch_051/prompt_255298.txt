On this view, the machine starts with no built-in knowledge or biases, and everything it ends up able to do is something it learned from experience. The teacher shapes the machine's behavior by giving it pleasure or pain signals — reward and punishment, basically — until it stops producing wrong answers. So, the article concludes, Turing wasn't really committed to the imitation game as a test; his actual criterion for intelligence was whether a machine could be educated from nothing, and modern deep learning with random-initialized networks trained on data is pretty much his child-machine program carried out.
