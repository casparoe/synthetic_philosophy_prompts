I've ended up with two convictions that have begun to pull against each other, and I can't decide which one should give. The first is theoretical and strikes me as well grounded: early perceptual processing is cognitively impenetrable—modular in the strong sense of being fast, mandatory, and sealed off from what you believe, expect, or want. The same proximal stimulus should therefore produce the same percept regardless of what the perceiver knows, except through downstream effects such as attention, memory, and interpretative judgment. This view earns its keep because it explains why visual illusions persist after you've measured the lines yourself or been told the trick: the Müller-Lyer figure doesn't stop looking the way it looks just because you know the lines are equal.

But I also have an intuition I can't shake: experts really do see the world differently. A radiologist looks at a chest film and appears simply to see the lesion I cannot make out at all; a chess player says a position looks unstable; a trained musician hears a chord as a particular voicing rather than an undifferentiated texture; after I learn the trees of my region or a phonological distinction in a foreign language, the world feels perceptually different to me, not merely more efficiently labeled. The standard reply is that expertise changes attention, memory, and judgment while perception remains untouched, and I can see a nearby distinction between learning that reshapes perception over years and current beliefs altering it on the spot. But I cannot tell whether these moves save the theory or merely relabel every counterexample.

I'm not asking for a survey. Give me the strongest case that my intuition is an introspective illusion and should be debunked; the strongest case that the theory must be amended so that background knowledge and expertise can genuinely reach into perceptual experience; and the strongest case for a third position that dissolves the conflict. Then commit: which yields, and what would have to be true for you to change your mind?
