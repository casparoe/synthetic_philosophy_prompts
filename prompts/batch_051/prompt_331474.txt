In preparing for a graduate seminar on causation, I have been trying to state the circularity objection to interventionist accounts of causation—of the sort associated with James Woodward—as a numbered argument, and I would like a careful assessment of it. The argument runs as follows.

(1) On the interventionist account, X is a cause of Y (or, in one standard formulation, a direct cause of Y relative to a variable set) just in case there is a possible intervention on X that changes the value, or probability distribution, of Y when the other variables in the set are held fixed.

(2) But an intervention on X is itself characterized in causal terms: an intervention variable I must be a cause of X, must override the other causes of X, and any influence of I on Y must go through X rather than along a route that bypasses X.

(3) So the account analyzes causation by means of a notion—intervention—whose own characterization already deploys causal concepts, including the concepts of causing X and of influencing Y only through X.

(4) An analysis that analyzes a concept by means of a notion whose characterization deploys that same concept, or the same small family of concepts, is circular when offered as a reductive account.

(5) Therefore, if interventionism is offered as a reductive account of causation, it is circular.

(6) If the interventionist replies that the account was never intended to be reductive, then it does not supply what a reductive account was supposed to supply, namely truth conditions for causal claims in non-causal terms.

(7) Hence either the account is circular or it abandons the reductive ambition; on both horns, it fails as an analysis of causation.

I half-suspect that the problem lies in step (4) or in the move from (6) to (7), but I cannot settle where the argument goes wrong, or even whether it goes wrong. Is the argument valid? If it is not, which numbered step fails, and under what reading? If it is valid, what exactly has it shown—does it damage the interventionist program, or only a reductive ambition its proponents have already disavowed? In answering, please separate what is settled in this debate from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus. Please respond in American English.
