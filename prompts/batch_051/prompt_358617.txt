I've been going back and forth on Burkean conservatism and Chesterton's fence, and I want help deciding which side should give. The view has real systematic pull: inherited institutions and practices encode more accumulated knowledge than any single generation can articulate, so the burden of proof sits with the reformer; Chesterton's point that you shouldn't tear down a fence until you know why it was put there looks like basic epistemic humility. But then I think of a concrete case: a university with a graduation ritual nobody can explain, where every attempt to find a rationale comes back empty and the ritual imposes real costs. My intuition insists the thing is vestigial, and that 'there might be a reason we can't see' is unfalsifyable and can be wheeled out to defend anything at all. I don't want the usual 'go slow and see' compromise. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the conservative principle to be the thing that goes or is amended, and whether there's a reading of 'why it's there' on which both survive without making the fence principle toothless. Give me the strongest version of each side, then tell me which you'd give up.
