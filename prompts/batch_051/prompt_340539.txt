I run a risk management office inside a municipal infrastructure authority. We draft decision memoranda that include statements written in the future tense: the bridge will be completed by the end of Q3, the geotechnical survey will confirm the west embankment design, procurement will not trigger a formal challenge. Our legal department requires every statement in a decision memorandum to be assigned one of three statuses: certified true, certified false, or entered on a contingent register. Staff currently treat every future-tense statement as true if current models support it and false otherwise, but this keeps failing us when a later event contradicts a statement that was not within our power to settle at the time the memorandum was finalized.

Write a reusable decision procedure, grounded in Aristotle's treatment of future contingents in On Interpretation chapter 9, for deciding when a future-tense statement is already determinately true or false and when it must be placed on the contingent register because it is still indeterminate. The procedure must be usable by engineers and project managers without philosophical training, in under an hour, at the point a decision memorandum is finalized. It should include tests to run in order, should flag which steps require judgment rather than mechanical application, and should not pretend that Aristotle's discussion settles every case by itself. Apply the procedure to three worked examples: first, the statement that a scheduled legal mediation will conclude by a fixed date, where the other party has not yet accepted the mediation schedule; second, the statement that a bond payment owed under a contract will be made on its due date; third, the statement that a contingency reserve will not be needed. Finally, say what the procedure cannot determine and where the sea-battle problem leaves a residual decision that no procedure can fully eliminate.
