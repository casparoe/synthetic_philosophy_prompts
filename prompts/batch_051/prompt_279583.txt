Consider a research collective of roughly thirty competent, sincere members, each of whom may, under the collective's current rules, unilaterally publish a potentially dangerous but potentially transformative finding. If any one member publishes, the finding is released and the entire collective is effectively committed. Each member forms a private judgment about whether publication would be good or bad; these judgments are noisy but genuinely informative. Suppose the finding is of the asymmetric kind where the harm of publishing wrongly far exceeds the benefit of publishing rightly, so a sound collective rule would need to be considerably more cautious than majority vote. With thirty independent judgments, it is very likely that at least one member will judge publication beneficial even when publication would in fact be harmful. (This is the structure sometimes called the unilateralist's curse.) Please argue for the position that the collective should replace its any-one-member rule with a principle of conformity: no member should publish unless the member judges that publication is what the group as a whole would support if all members' evidence were pooled, rather than merely what the member's own, possibly outlier, judgment favors.

Your answer should be in American English and should be a numbered list of exactly five distinct arguments for this position. Each item should be one or two sentences, and there should be no introduction or conclusion.
