I'm taking a seminar on corruption and global ethics this term, and me and my friend keep getting into the same argument about whether "corruption" is a real cross-cultural category or just a Western legal idea being exported. She grew up partly in a country where a lot of what we read about as clientelism is just normal politics, and she has strong, pretty consistent verdicts on a bunch of cases. A construction firm paying a planning official cash for a zoning variance: she says that's corruption. A drug company flying a health minister to a resort for a week, undisclosed, a month after she approved their drug: corruption. A woman bringing the clerk who finally released her late father's pension a basket of fruit and eggs: not corruption, refusing would be rude. A mayor giving municipal jobs to people who canvassed for him, even though they're roughly as qualified as the other applicants: not corruption, that's just coalition politics. The same mayor quietly putting his completely unqualified nephew on the payroll without advertising the post: corruption, nepotism. A customs officer taking a small, widely known speed fee to process a trucker's paperwork the same day: not corruption, it's basically a tip.

I'm trying to figure out what she'd say about some new cases, and more importantly what rule she's actually running, because I can think of at least two different principles that would fit her track record so far. Would she call it corruption if a city councillor openly votes to rezone a plot owned by her sister, where the rezoning is genuinly the best planning decision for the area? What about a minister who quietly gives his extremely well-qualified nephew a senior civil service job without advertising the post? And would a candidate handing out cash at a rally right before an election count as corrupt for her? What I actually want is the principle-extraction work: what are the most plausible rules that make sense of her record, what does each one say about those three cases, which of them would best force a choice between the rules, what's your best guess about her verdict on each, and whether there's another case you'd want to put to her to settle which rule she's really using.
