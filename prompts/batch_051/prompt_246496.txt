I'm taking a Daoist philosophy seminar this term and we keep coming back to the wu wei / trying-not-to-try problem. Last week we read the Zhuangzi chapter usually called Mastering Life, and the prof spent ages on the bit about Qing the bell-stand carver — if I'm remembering it right, he says he fasts for three days and forgets thoughts of gain or approval, five days to forget blame and praise, seven days to forget his limbs and body, until only the thing he's going to carve is left. She keeps saying this isn't just "doing nothing," it's a kind of cultivated spontaneity, except the cultivation itself looks like trying really hard to stop trying. Its basically the paradox we keep bumping into.

I also help run a philosophy club for undergrads — about 12 people, 75 minutes, pretty mixed background. I want to run a session on this passage where people actually feel the trap before we talk about it, so they don't just nod along with the claim that trying to be spontaneous backfires. The goal is that by the end they see why Qing's fasting has that order to it — why he has to forget being praised and blamed before he forgets his own body, and why that sequence can't just be turned into a technique. I don't want to lecture. I want the structure of whatever we do to make them run into the paradox themselves.

Can you design that activity? It should be something with actual roles or rules or a small game, not just a discussion prompt, and there should be a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity back to the stages Qing describes. I'd also like to know what will predictably go wrong when it's run — especially participants who deliberately try not to try, or who try to meta-game it by acting extra relaxed. And be honest about which part of the passage the activity can't reproduce, so I know what I still have to supply off-script afterwards. Timeline to the minute, including setup and debrief, would be great.
