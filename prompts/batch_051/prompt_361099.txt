In my school's philosophy club, a classmate defended the idea that religious experience can be evidence for God by analogy with ordinary sense perception. He argued that just as a person is rational to trust her eyes when she seems to see a tree, unless she has a specific reason to doubt them, so a person is rational to trust an apparent experience of God, unless she has a specific reason to doubt it. He added that comparing reports with other observers plays the same role in both cases, and that background theory plays the same supporting role — optics for vision, theology for religious experience. I have to write a paper examining this analogy. Could you lay out what corresponds to what (the eyes, the seeming, the tree, the cross-checking, the background theory), identify where the mapping breaks down, separate the disanalogies that matter for his conclusion from those that do not, and then say whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table — or whether the analogy was doing all the argumentative work?
