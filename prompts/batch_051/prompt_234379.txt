Ive been trying to sort out what to make of Lakatos's claim that mathematics is quasi-empirical, and I keep getting stuck on a passage from Mill's System of Logic (Book II, Chapter V, "Of Demonstration, and Necessary Truths"). Mill says:

<quote passage>

Since then it appears not to be true that there is any peculiar certainty attaching to the propositions of geometry, and that the propositions of geometry are not in reality anything but those very hypotheses, it follows that the theorems of geometry are necessary truths only in the sense of necessarily following from hypotheses; that is, they are necessary truths relative to certain suppositions. But this necessity is not a necessity of nature; and the propositions are not called necessary truths with any reference to the fact that the hypotheses are really true or false.

</quote passage>

Now Lakatos in Proofs and Refutations is obviously coming out of a very different tradition than Mill, and he's not just repeating "math is inductive." But he does keep calling mathematics quasi-empirical, and he argues that mathematical knowledge grows by a kind of conjecture-criticism-improvement loop that looks a lot like what happens in empirical science, except the "counterexamples" are imaginary monsters and the "improvements" are proof-generated concept-stretchings.

Here's what I actually want. Suppose you accept Lakatos's picture that mathematical methods are quasi-empirical in his sense: conjectures are tested against thought-experimental counterexamples, proofs get refined when they fail, and even the concepts themselves shift under the pressure of refutations. What is the single deepest unresolved question about that picture right now, and which second question matters most after it, and which famous-looking question in this vicinity is actually overrated or a mostly verbal fight? I'm trying to decide what a working group on this stuff should actually spend a year on, so I want a defended priority ranking with at least two criteria stated up front—say importance and tractability, but use whatever criteria you think actually do the work. And since quasi-empirical is vague six ways from Sunday, don't just survey positions; tell me which open problem would, if someone cracked even a small part of it, change how the rest of the area gets framed. I care about the reasoning more than the list.
