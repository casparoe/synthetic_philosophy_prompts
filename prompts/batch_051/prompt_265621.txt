I’m a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, so I may be missing a standard distinction here. I’ve been reading Nietzsche on eternal recurrence, and two passages seem to me to push in opposite directions.

In section 341 of The Joyful Wisdom, as I understand it, a demon tells you that this life, exactly as you have lived it, will have to be lived again, and again, with nothing new in it. The point of the passage seems to be a question about how you would react: whether you would curse the message or call it divine. The emphasis is on whether you can affirm your own life, not on whether the recurrence is cosmologically true.

In Thus Spoke Zarathustra, in the chapter called The Convalescent, Zarathustra is overcome by a similar thought and describes it as his deepest, most terrible thought. His animals then convert it into a song about how everything recurs and the wheel of existence rolls forever. Zarathustra mocks them for turning it into a tune, yet later he himself affirms the ring of recurrence and says he loves eternity.

The conflict is this: the first passage treats eternal recurrence as a hypothetical test of attitude, one you could pass or fail, while the second treats it as a crushing truth that even Zarathustra struggles to accept and must not cheapen into a formula. Are these compatible? Is recurrence a cosmological doctrine, a practical test, or something whose affirmation is the entire point? Please give the strongest readings that reconcile the two passages, explain which readings require watering down one of them, and say which reading is most honest to both texts.
