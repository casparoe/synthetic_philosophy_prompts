I would like a working definition of asceticism — or, if the ordinary term tracks more than one ideal, a small family of definitions — that classifies the following cases correctly. I am less interested in dictionary usage than in isolating the ideal or ideals the term is marking.

Cases that should clearly come out as ascetic:
(a) a desert anchorite of late antiquity who gives away his possessions, eats one sparse meal a day, and treats bodily comfort as a temptation to be resisted;
(b) a lay practitioner who during a holy season forgoes sweets, hot showers, and soft bedding as a discipline of renunciation, and not for reasons of health or economy.

Cases that should clearly come out as not ascetic:
(c) a household that goes without meat, heating, and new clothes because it cannot afford them;
(d) a frugal employee who saves most of her salary solely in order to retire at forty-five and then travel;
(e) an athlete who endures a miserable reducing diet before a bout in order to make weight.

Borderline cases — tell me what your definition yields for each, and whether you would defend or revise the result:
(f) a climate activist who gives up flying, meat, and air conditioning because of their emissions, and who experiences the sacrifices as real and unwelcome;
(g) a Stoic who takes cold showers and fasts periodically in order to become indifferent to comfort, and who reports that these practices are no longer felt as deprivations;
(h) a jailed dissident on hunger strike, who wants to live and says the fast is an instrument of political pressure rather than a form of self-transformation;
(i) a minimalist who owns fewer than a hundred things because abundance feels like clutter to him, and who takes himself to be satisfying a preference rather than renouncing one;
(j) a miser who lives on rice and beans, wears a patched coat, and keeps the heat off while quietly amassing a fortune he never spends;
(k) a person in a depressive episode who barely eats and avoids comfort because nothing gives him pleasure.

I suspect our language is trying to mark two contrasts at once: asceticism against frugality (renunciation as constitutive of a good or holy life rather than as a mere instrument), and asceticism against voluntary simplicity (a discipline aimed at transforming desire rather than a style of consumption aimed at aligning one's living with settled values). Test that suspicion as well, and if it fails, say what better taxonomy the hard cases force.

Set out the definition, run it through each case, and indicate where it gives the wrong answer, no answer, or a merely verbal answer; then revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. Finally, tell me which of your verdicts is least secure, where the reasoning is most likely to fail, and what consideration would change your mind.
