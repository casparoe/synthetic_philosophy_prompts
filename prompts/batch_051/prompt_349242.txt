I am giving a departmental seminar defending a view about the Judy Benjamin problem: the case in which a soldier with a uniform prior over four regions — Red-Headquarters, Red-Second Company, Blue-Headquarters, and Blue-Second Company — receives a reliable but purely conditional message, namely that, conditional on her being in Red territory, the odds are three to one that she is in the Headquarters Company area rather than the Second Company area. I will defend what I call the antecedent-fixed update: the total probability of Red should stay at one half, and the update should be confined to rescaling the two Red cells to reflect the three-to-one odds, yielding a posterior of one half for Blue, three eighths for Red-Headquarters, and one eighth for Red-Second Company. I will argue that the standard relative-entropy-minimising solution, which shifts probability mass out of Red into Blue, is an artefact of minimising a global distance and responds to information the message does not carry.

The audience is a philosophy department with a strong formal-epistemology wing, most of whom know Jeffrey conditionalisation and the basics of probability kinematics, plus two statisticians from a neighbouring department who use maximum-entropy methods daily and will regard the relative-entropy minimiser as the principled, uniqueness-securing answer. The epistemologists are unlikely to challenge the formal machinery; they will challenge whether I have correctly identified what is learned from a conditional message, and whether my rule is coherent and general rather than an intuition with one case attached. They are not specialists in the Judy Benjamin literature and will not be moved by exegesis of it.

For each objection, make clear who in the room it would come from — the formal epistemologists or the maximum-entropy statisticians — since the same worry lands differently from each.

For targeting, my argument in the talk runs as follows. The content of the message concerns only the ratio within Red, so treating the utterance as an event to be conditioned on would require a model of the sender that the problem does not supply. Absent such a model, the only defensible constraint is the stated conditional odds, and a constraint of that form should not move probability into the complement of its antecedent. I will concede that I lack a fully general uniqueness proof for the antecedent-fixed rule, but I will argue that the burden is on the relative-entropy view to explain why a message explicitly conditional on Red should count as evidence that the soldier is in Blue.
