I've been arguing with my sister about what makes a market repulsive ever since our cousin announced he was going to sell plasma twice a week to make rent, and my sister said sure, no problem, but then basically lost it when a couple in our town apparently paid a surrogate forty thousand dollars to carry their kid. I don't understand why she's so confident. Here's where I land on the cases we keep dragging into it, and I'd like you to tell me what principle, if any, makes sense of my pattern.

Buying a kidney from a poor healthy person so a richer person can get a transplant: wrong, in a way I can almost not put into words, even though both people walk away better off and the donor went in with eyes open. Selling plasma twice a week: fine; I did it in grad school and it felt like renting my time, not selling a piece of me. Paying a surrogate to carry a pregnancy: I lean yes, it's okay, even though it's way more invasive than plasma. Paying for sex: that one makes me uneasy and I keep failing to explain why, given the surrogate one doesn't bother me. A store charging forty bucks for a five-dollar case of water two days after a hurricane: wrong, no question. Selling your vote, or paying people to gather signatures for a ballot measure: wrong, full stop. Paying someone to stand in line for you at the courthouse or an iPhone launch: fine, or at worst annoying. A golden-visa scheme where rich people buy citizenship: I think it's wrong, but I can't connect it to the others. Paying healthy volunteers for a Phase I drug trial, where the whole point is to push the dose until something bad shows up: I think it's okay if the consent is genuinely informed, even though it makes me wince. And the one my sister and I go around in circles on: should a county jail let inmates pay for a nicer cell? She says obviously not; I keep thinking its not so different from paying for a nicer hospital room, but it also gives me the creeps.

So my question is whether there's a single rationale underneath all this — something about what money corrupts when it changes hands, or about who's really choosing freely, or about the difference between a body part, a body service, and a civil right — that gets all or most of my verdicts. If not, which of my verdicts would the tidiest principle force me to drop? And what does it say about the jail cell case, since that's the one I keep flipping on, or about a case I should be worrying about but haven't?
