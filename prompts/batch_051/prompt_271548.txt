Ok I got into a spat with my brother (he's religious, I'm not) about which commands in scripture are still supposed to hold, and his method sounds right until you push it. He lays it out like this: (1) A command keeps binding only if what it directly tells you to do isnt just the local, culture-bound expression of some deeper principle that could be expressed differently. (2) That's how he handles the head-covering passage in 1 Corinthians 11 (first-century honor/shame signaling), the kosher laws (boundary-markers for ancient Israel), and the slavery regulations (accommodations to an institution rather than a permanent ideal). (3) But the same move seems to work on everything. Take the command against murder: it directly tells you not to kill, and you can call that prohibition the ancient Near Eastern expression of the deeper point about persons bearing God's image — in which case the surface command would lapse too, leaving only a vague principle. (4) In fact every concrete command can be redescribed as one culture's way of realizing some more abstract point, so the method drains every rule down to an abstraction. (5) I can't accept that, but I can't spot where the slide happens. I'd really like to keep the specific content of the murder prohibition and the commands about looking after the poor, but his method seems to make that impossible. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it force me to accept?
