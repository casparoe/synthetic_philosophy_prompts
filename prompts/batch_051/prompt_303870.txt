I have recently been rereading Frege's case against psychologism, a case I have always presented to students as a decisive turning point: logic, Frege insists, does not describe how human minds in fact think, and its laws owe nothing to our psychological constitution. Lately, however, I have also been reading about a diary Frege kept in 1924, published only after his death, containing stridently anti-democratic and antisemitic political entries. The juxtaposition unsettles me. If a thinker of Frege's stature could be so badly warped by prejudice in his own reasoning, does that not show that the ideal of a pure, non-psychological domain of logical laws is itself a psychological projection — a comforting fantasy produced by a particular sort of mind? And if so, should I no longer teach the anti-psychologism argument as a neutral philosophical achievement, but instead present it as compromised by its origins?
