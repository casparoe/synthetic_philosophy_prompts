I'm a doctoral student drafting the prospectus for a first dissertation chapter on the fate of the principle of alternative possibilities (PAP) after Frankfurt-style counterexamples, and I'd like you to write the six-page proposal I'll circulate to my committee. My half-formed idea is that the debate has been conducted at the wrong level of description. Both sides tend to ask whether any alternative possibility at all remains in a Frankfurt case, but they are in fact using different readings of "could have done otherwise": an action-level reading (the agent could have performed a different overt action), an event-level reading (some sub-agential item in the actual sequence — a blush, a prior sign, a neural pattern — could have differed), and an agency-level reading (the agent could have refrained, omitted, or otherwise exercised control over the course of her conduct). Frankfurt-style cases seem to defeat the first reading, while the second makes PAP too easy to satisfy; my hunch is that the third has never been worked out rigorously enough to know whether it collapses into the first, survives Frankfurt, or turns into an actual-sequence view about the quality of the agent's will in disguise.

Please turn this into a proposal with a determinate question — roughly, whether PAP can be reconstructed as a principle about the agent's ability to refrain rather than her ability to do some alternative thing, and whether that reconstruction withstands the full range of Frankfurt-style cases, including ones engineered to remove even the so-called flickers, without becoming trivial or changing the subject — an account of why existing responses leave this open, a method (what I would actually do, month by month over one semester), and an honest statement of what would count as the project failing rather than merely revising the question. Do not include citations or a bibliography; refer to positions and arguments freely, but write it so that no source has to be consulted, and I will add the references myself. Begin with the proposal itself, with no preface or commentary.
