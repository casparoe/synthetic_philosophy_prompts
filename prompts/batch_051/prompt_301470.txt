I follow a standing rule in evaluating first-person reports of religious experience, conversion, or answered prayer. I treat the fact that such reports have well-attested natural and social antecedents—agency detection under uncertainty, emotional need and priming, social conformity, and selective memory for hits over misses—as a strong reason to give the report little independent evidential weight. A colleague objects that this confuses a belief's causal origin with its evidential warrant: a belief can be produced by ordinary mechanisms and still be true. I cannot investigate each report from the inside, so I need a workable default. Is my rule defensible, or is it bias dressed up as method? If it is defensible in some form, what discount rate is principled, and what features of a report should override the default?
