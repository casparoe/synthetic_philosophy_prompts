Here's a puzzle I've been turning over, and I'd like your read on it.

My colleague Dana works in population ethics — the stuff about whether a world of ten people at +1 beats a world of one person at +10, and so on. She has a very consistent track record, and I've been keeping notes on her verdicts. Whenever she's compared worlds with the same number of beings and everyone fully conscious, she just sums. For example, she says a two-person world where both are at 10 is exactly as good as one where the first is at 2 and the second is at 18; same total, done, no inequality-aversion. She also says a world where both are at 5 is worse than one where one is at 0 and the other is at 11, since the total is lower even though the second is more unequal. And, crucially, she's said a ten-person world where everyone is at +1 is better than a one-person world at +9 — so she's not an averagist, she really is a totalist.

The slogan she keeps using is that welfare is consciousness times happiness. But I've never heard her take a stand on worlds where the degree of consciousness itself varies — and consciousness times happiness is compatable with more than one aggregation rule.

Here's the new case I want to predict. World A: exactly one fully conscious person, happiness 10. World B: one hundred creatures, each with one-hundredth of full consciousness, each at happiness 10. (And yes, stipulate that a barely conscious creature can be at happiness 10; don't fight the thought experiment.) What would Dana say, if she extends the rule her verdicts so far suggest?

I can see at least three candidate rules that fit her track record but diverge on this case:
- additive: value is the sum over beings of consciousness times happiness
- aggregate multiplication: value is total consciousness times total happiness
- lexical: first maximize total consciousness, and only break ties by happiness.

Maybe there are others worth keeping alive. Walk me through what each candidate predicts for the new case, say which candidates her old verdicts actually rule out (and which they don't), give me your best guess about Dana with the reason, and name one further case that would seperate the survivors.
