I'm taking a class on virtue epistemology and a side conversation with my roommate got a little heated, so I want to see how someone else would work through it. We were given this working definition of an epistemic vice: a fairly stable trait of a person that (i) systematically leads them to false belief or away from true belief, (ii) is reason-responsive in the sense that it can be changed over time when the person is shown it is misfiring, and (iii) isn't just a lack of ability or knowledge but actually shapes how they gather, frame, and weight evidence. The uncontroversial examples are wishful thinking, dogmatism, and closed-mindedness.

Now here's my case. I have a friend whose optimism isn't the motivating kind, he doesn't necessarily work harder because of it, it's more a standing habit of saying it will probably work out and inflating the probability of good outcomes whenever the evidence is ambiguous or even mildly negative. So: is optimism, understood that way, an epistemic vice according to the three criteria? Go through each criterion and say honestly where it meets it, fails it, or where the criterion underdetermines the answer. Then do the same for hope, where I'm understanding hope as a desire for an outcome plus an acknowledgment that you don't know it will happen, without any actual belief that it's likely. I suspect the two have different verdicts, and if they do, I'd like to know which criterion is doing the separating, and whether the original definition is missing something about what make a trait intellectually vicious rather than just factually risky. Basically: should we stop treating optimism and hope as automatically intellectually innocent?
