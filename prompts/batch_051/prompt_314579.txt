Ok, here's one. Pretend you're facilitating an adversarial collaboration on whether sexual exclusivity in a romantic relationship is a moral requirement or just a convention. One camp, call them the requirement camp, says non-exclusivity (sleeping with someone else without your partner's ok) is wrong in itself: even if nobody is lied to and nobody finds out, a wrong still occurred, and it wouldn't just disappear in a society that happened not to value monogamy. The other camp, call them the convention camp, says exclusivity has only whatever force the parties give it: the wrongness of cheating, when there is any, comes entirely from lying, promise-breaking, or knowingly hurting someone, not from the sex itself.

Now draft the joint statement such a collaboration would produce. Include: (1) a numbered list of claims both camps would be willing to sign, phrased so neither side flinches; (2) the remaining disagreements, with each side's view stated in wording that side would accept; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions whose answers would actually move at least one of the camps; and (4) the cases or evidence both sides agree would bear on those cruxes, with each sides prediction recorded in advance. Push on things like: does calling exclusivity a convention settle whether it's morally required, or can those come apart? What exactly is the supposed extra wrong over and above the lying? Would the requirement camp say the same thing about other conventional relationship norms, like living together, sharing finances, or texting daily? If you think part of the dispute is verbal, say which part, and rewrite the disputed claims so the purely verbal disagreement collapses and only whatever real disagreement is left remains sharp.
