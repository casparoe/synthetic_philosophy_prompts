Write a Petrarchan sonnet that renders the central claim of al-Ghazālī's occasionalism about causation. The octave should build the commonsense picture of fire and cotton: the flame's power entering the cotton, the burn following as inevitable. The volta at line 9 should make the occasionalist turn, and the sestet should work out its consequence: the fire contributes nothing by its own nature, since God directly creates the burning at each moment, and what we call causal necessity is only God's customary way (ʿāda)—reliable, but not necessary. The closing couplet should state precisely what the fire becomes once "cause" as agency is withdrawn—occasion or sign rather than agent. Aim for an accuracy that a scholar of Ashʿarite occasionalism would recognize, and do not let the doctrine crowd out the poem.
