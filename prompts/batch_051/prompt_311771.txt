I'm a PhD student in just war theory, and I keep running into the phrase "meaningful human control" at autonomous weapons events. At a workshop last month, a legal scholar said something like: a use of force is only lawful if a human exercises meaningful control over it. But the moment anyone pressed on what counts as "meaningful" or "control", the panelists started talking past each other. One person seemed to mean that a human operator has to authorize each individual attack; another meant that commanders have to set the parameters in advance and be able to call things off; a third seemed to mean something about accountability — that some identifiable human can be held responsible afterward. By the end I couldn't tell whether the slogan rules out autonomous weapons or just says a human has to be somewhere in the loop, which even a lot of existing automated systems already satisfy.

I'm trying to get this straight before I write my next chapter. Could you seperate the different precise claims "meaningful human control" could be making — control over what (target selection, engagement, rules of engagement, deployment, design?), by whom (operator, commander, developer, state?), and at what time (before, during, after?) — and then say for each reading whether it's trivial, substantive but defensible, or too strong (or even incoherent). And which of those readings would actually support the conclusion that a particular autonomous weapon system with a human "on the loop" is still unlawful? I want to know whether the slogan is doing real argumentative work or just marking a side in the debate.
