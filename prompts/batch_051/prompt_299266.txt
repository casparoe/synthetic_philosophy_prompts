You are a philosopher of science who works on the epistemology of fundamental physics and advises a private research foundation. The foundation funds a great deal of work in string theory, inflationary cosmology, and the multiverse/landscape debates, and its trustees have grown worried by the recurring charge that these programs are unfalsifiable. They are considering a new rule, which they call a Discrimination Audit, and they want you to think through how it would actually behave rather than whether it is a good idea in the abstract.

Under the rule, every grant in quantum gravity or early-universe cosmology must maintain a standing public document in which the principal investigator commits, in advance, to concrete observations — including null results of specified sensitivity — that would lower their stated credence in the framework they are developing, by how much, and according to what pre-agreed updating rule. The PI must file a short annual update to this document. Renewal decisions are then tilted strongly toward programs whose audit has actually recorded some movement in either direction during the past five years ("epistemic movement"), and a program whose audit has not moved at all in five years becomes ineligible for the following funding cycle. A standing panel judges whether a claimed observation counts as decision-relevant for the framework in question, and the panel's members are paid from a pool to which every grantee contributes five percent of their grant.

Analyze this as a mechanism. Begin by brainstorming the main ways it could be gamed — vague priors, observation lists crafted to be unfalsifiable in practice, strategic adoption of a low initial credence in one's own framework so that almost anything counts as an update, collusion between theory groups and experimental groups, panel capture, and so on — and the main second-order effects on what kinds of physics gets proposed and by whom. Then give your verdict on the question the trustees actually care about: does a scheme like this select for genuinely discriminating, empirically responsive theory, or for the appearance of discriminatingness? Which failure modes could be repaired without gutting the scheme, and which are inseparable from it?
