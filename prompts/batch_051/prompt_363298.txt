My roommate grades papers for an epistemology class and has a bunch of firm views about when it's okay to assert something, so I've been feeding her made-up cases to see what rule she's actually applying. Here are the calls she's made so far. A person flips a coin, doesn't look at it, has a strong hunch that it landed heads, and tells you "it's heads"; it really is heads, but she says that assertion isn't okay. A person looks out the window, sees a red sedan in the driveway, and tells you "there's a red sedan in the driveway"; she says that one is okay. A person checks the wall clock, which reads 3:00, and tells you "it's 3:00"; the clock is actually broken and stuck at 3:00, and it's only by coincidence that it happens to be exactly 3:00; she says not okay. A person asks a normally reliable coworker when the meeting starts, is told 3:00, and tells you "the meeting's at 3:00"; the coworker mixed it up and the meeting is at 4:00; she says not okay.

New case she hasn't seen yet: a guy is driving through a place where, unknown to him, the locals have put up dozens of full-size barn facades that look exactly like real barns from the road. He glances out the window, sees a barn-shaped thing, and says "that's a barn." It's in fact one of the few real barns in the area. His eyesight is fine, the light is good, and he has no false beliefs about the place. If he'd looked at almost any of the neighboring facades instead, he would have said the exact same thing about a fake.

What will she say about this one, and wich rule is she running? The candidates I've been toying with are: a knowledge rule, a truth rule, a justified-belief rule, a justified-true-belief rule, a JTB-plus-no-false-lemmas rule, and maybe a safety rule. Tell me which of those her past calls already kill, which ones are still live, and what each live rule predicts for the barn case. Then give me your best guess about her verdict and why, and make up one more case if you need one that would settle whatever's still open after that. Also, please write your answer in American English.
