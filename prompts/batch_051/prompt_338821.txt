I keep coming across this paragraph in a popular science piece about psychiatric diagnoses, and I'm not sure whether to trust it. It says:

"According to the philosopher Ian Hacking, psychiatric categories have a weird property: they feed back into the people they classify. Once a label like ADHD or borderline personality disorder is available, people learn to describe themselves in its terms, clinicians start to see the disorder everywhere, and the label ends up creating the very population it was supposed to merely describe. Hacking calls this a looping effect and says psychiatric kinds are interactive kinds, unlike indifferent kinds like electrons, which don't care how we classify them. That, the author says, is why Hacking says mental disorders are made up — in the sense of being social constructions rather than real diseases. The looping effect is basically a self-fulfilling prophecy at the population level, and once a category becomes popular it inevitably spreads until it burns itself out."

I suspect this is a bit unfair to Hacking but I keep getting tangled about which parts.
