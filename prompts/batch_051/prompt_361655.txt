so after a long argument with my friend about what makes a ground of discrimination suspect, i've ended up with a pile of verdicts and no principle, and i'm hoping you can find one or tell me which verdicts have to go.

here's where i landed:

- not hiring someone because of their race, even when the customers are racist and sales would drop: obviously wrong, suspect ground, no question.
- firing a woman from retail because she might get pregnant and take leave: wrong, and i'd put it right next to race; no business-need argument should save it.
- religion: also suspect to me. a cashier wearing a hijab can't be sent home because customers complain. but weirdly, religion is chosen in some sense — people convert, people lapse — and that doesn't make it feel less protected to me.
- age though: at 60 you can't do the heavy lifting job as fast, and i can live with mandatory retirement ages for air traffic controllers. i don't love age discrimination, but it doesn't hit as the same kind of wrong as race. yet age is the most unchosen thing there is.
- disability: i don't think of it as suspect exactly, but i think an employer has to actually figure out whether the person can do the job with reasonable adjustments, and a wheelchair not fitting behind the counter is the employer's problem to fix, not the applicant's.
- a past embezzlement conviction when someone applys for a job handling cash: i think it's fine not to hire them. but a blanket policy of no one with any record for a warehouse job feels wrong, even though my friend says a record is a record.
- political opinions: for a normal office job it would be wrong to fire someone for their party. for a job writing speeches for one party, obviously it's fine to hire only people who share the politics. so political opinion sits in a weird middle where it depends on the job in a way race never does.
- physical attractiveness: i think a bar can refuse to hire someone as a bartender because of how they look, and i'm not sure i'd defend a law against that. my friend says that's exactly the same structure as race and i couldn't say why it isn't.

halfway through, my friend floated a test: a ground is suspect if it's (1) not chosen, (2) mostly irrelevant to job performance, and (3) marks a group that's been historically subordinated. i liked it for about ten minutes, then noticed it makes age more suspect than religion (age is more unchosen, and old people aren't exactly the powerful group), which is the opposite of what i actually feel. and it makes weight discrimination suspect, because weight is only partly chosen and fat people are subordinated — and i'm genuinely unsure whether i want to sign on to that.

so: is there a principle that captures most of these and explains the ones it doesn't? which of my verdicts do i have to give up? and what does your best principle say about the ones i keep flip-flopping on — weight, accent or dialect as a hiring criterion, having gone to a prestigious school, a genetic condition likely to mean expensive medical bills, and caste or clan background. feel free to say my friend's test is right and my verdicts are wrong; i'd rather have a clean principle than a list of gut feelings.
