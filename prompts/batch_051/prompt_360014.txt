Write me a deadpan parody of a terms-of-service agreement, around 450 words, for a made-up insurtech company called Multiples Mutual that sells scooping insurance to scientists terrified of being scooped. The humor has to come from the actual conceptual mess of simultaneous discovery, not from generic fine-print jokes. The policy should get genuinely tangled in: the underdetermination of the moment of discovery (first dated notebook sketch? first public demo? first working prototype? first submitted manuscript?); the problem that insuring priority means taking a stand on counterfactuals about who would have found it anyway and how much later; whether the discovery was in the air or a one-off lone-genius fluke; and the awkward fact that multiple discovery makes the insured researcher look replaceable, which sort of undercuts the whole product. Include one short quote from a fictional chief actuary defending the most absurd clause, and close with an excluded-events clause covering anything even vaguely like the Newton–Leibniz calculus dispute. The satirical target should be the practice of treating priority as a determinate, insurable fact, and the deadpan should stay legible without any winking.
