Science treats a tiny difference in timing as if it settled who the discoverer is. One person submits a result in March and another, working independently, sends in the same finding in June: the first gets the discovery named after her, and the second gets a footnote. But if the field was ready and the result was likely to land around then anyway—Newton and Leibniz, Darwin and Wallace are the stock examples—then the gap between the two doesn't track any obvious difference in the quality or originality of the work, only in who happened to finish first. Is this priority rule justified, or is it just a scheme for rationing a scarce reputational good that we've mistaken for a judgment about epistemic merit? If you had to keep first-to-publish as the main criterion of scientific credit or replace it with something (say, treating near-simultaneous independent discoverers as equal co-discoverers), which would you do, and on what principle? What would the rule get wrong in hard cases, and is that a price worth paying? Keep the answer under 250 words.
