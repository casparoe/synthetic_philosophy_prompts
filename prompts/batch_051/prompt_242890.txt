I have been working with word embeddings and keep noticing a distortion for which I do not have a name. When I retrieve the nearest neighbors of an ordinary query word, a small set of frequent, semantically bleached items—generic nouns, common abbreviations, all-purpose terms—shows up near the top for an enormous range of queries. They are not nonsense matches, but they are not what I would count as semantically close either. Meanwhile, rare words that are intuitively much closer in meaning get pushed down the ranking, even though they are present in the space. The effect strengthens as the dimensionality of the embedding increases and looks like an artifact of the geometry rather than a fact about meaning. I know this is adjacent to the curse of dimensionality and perhaps to concentration of measure, but it seems more specific. Does it have an established name? More important than the label is the carving: what exactly produces this effect, how can one test whether one of these ubiquitous neighbors is a genuine semantic relation rather than a geometric artifact, and where is the boundary between this and the related high-dimensional phenomena?
