I work on the internal forecasting platform at a mid-sized tech company: employees post probabilities for product and business questions and get scored on accuracy. We're considering shipping an automated coherence auditor. Each time a user submits a probability, the auditor checks it against the user's other live probabilities; if it can assemble a package of bets at the user's own stated odds that loses money no matter what happens, it executes the package against the user's account and books the guaranteed loss as a small incoherence fee. The pitch is that this mechanically trains people into coherent forecasts without a human having to police them, and our resident quant says the sure-loss package is a Dutch book, though I have only a rough sense of the theory. Before I write a recommendation for our product committee, I want a written analysis of how this mechanism would actually behave: what incentives it creates for forecasters, how they might game it, where it breaks down (for example when questions are vague, overlapping, or about one-off events), what second-order effects it could have on the honesty and quality of forecasts, and whether the failure modes can be fixed without gutting the point of the scheme. Please include an honest treatment of the strongest case for shipping it and the strongest case against, not just a list of pros and cons.
