Write an essay on whether there is a unified scientific method. Your essay should weigh the view that science is distinguished by shared methodological rules such as falsifiability, controlled experimentation, or predictive success against Feyerabend's historical argument that no such rule survives contact with actual scientific practice, and that imposing any fixed method would have impeded major advances. Engage seriously with the strongest version of the anything-goes challenge: if Galileo's persuasive techniques and selective use of evidence were indispensable to the Copernican turn, then either the standard image of scientific method misdescribes a central episode in modern science or science does not require a single method. Reach and defend a specific conclusion about what, if anything, distinguishes scientific inquiry from other forms of belief formation. Do not end on a non-committal note.
