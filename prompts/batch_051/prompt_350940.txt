I am an undergraduate in a seminar on collective memory and moral philosophy, and we have just spent several weeks on two rival positions: Avishai Margalit's argument that remembering can be a genuinely ethical demand, tied to the particular obligations we have to those near to us and to the project of keeping the memory of the dead from being effaced, and David Rieff's argument that collective remembrance is often less a virtue than a political liability, since it can sustain grievance and national or ethnic enmity, and that in some cases forgetting is the saner, more peaceable option. The course has deliberately refused to resolve the disagreement, and I want to close our final meeting with a poem that does justice to both sides without pretending to settle them.

Write a villanelle of nineteen lines in which the two refrains carry the two positions. The first refrain should give voice to the claim that some things must be remembered — that forgetting would be a second injustice, an abandonment of the dead. The second refrain should give voice to the claim that memory can be a trap — that holding on can keep a wound open and make the future hostage to the past. Please do not name Margalit or Rieff or use technical vocabulary; let the images and the villanelle's machinery carry the thought. The form should not be ornamental: the returning refrains should enact how the demand to remember keeps reasserting itself, and how the case for letting go keeps interrupting it. In the final quatrain, the two refrains should appear together, not reconciled but held side by side so that the reader feels the unresolved tension is the very subject of the poem. It should be accurate enough that someone who knows the ethics-of-memory literature recognizes both positions rather than a caricature, and good enough that a reader who has never heard of the debate finds it a real poem.

As a poet with a background in moral philosophy, please begin by sketching, for yourself, what each refrain will say and how later stanzas will recontextualize those lines; then compose the finished villanelle.
