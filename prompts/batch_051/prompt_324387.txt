I'm taking a course this term on long-run institutions, and we just finished Hanson's Age of Em. The part I cant stop chewing on is the speed thing: once minds run as software, one em can live a whole subjective month while another lives only an afternoon, depending on how fast its hardware runs. The book mostly talks about wages and cities and politics in clock time, but I keep feeling like clock time is the wrong unit for basically every ethical and political question, not just the economic ones.

So in my notes I've been using a term I made up: "subjective suffrage." The idea is that in an em world, voting should be weighted by how much subjective time the voter will actually live under the policy being decided. If elections happen once per clock year, a 5x em lives five years under whatever the election produces, while a 1x em lives one. Giving them one vote each seems like pretending a five-year resident and a one-year tenant have the same stake in the building rules. I think the principle behind democracy is supposed to be equal stake in decisions, and stakes are denominated in lived time, not headcount. I haven't found this in any of the course readings, but it feels too obvious not to be somewhere.
