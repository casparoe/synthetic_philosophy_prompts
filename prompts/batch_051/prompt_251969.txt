I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, so bear with me if I mangle the vocabulary. At work we had two totally different database schemas that passed every test and returned identical results for every query we could write, and the only way to tell which one was actually live was to open the box, which ops wouldn't let us do. It got me thinking about the same problem in science. Counterfactual: suppose in 2040 cosmologists prove that the universe has a hard observational screen—below some length scale or beyond some horizon, whichever you like—and that two incompatible pictures of what's behind it (a discrete causal network vs. a continuous field, say) are known to make exactly the same predictions for everything anyone can ever observe, with no future instrument able to break the tie because the distinguishing information is permanently sealed off. Predict how the physics community, plus the philosophy-of-science folks who care, handles this over the next fifty years. Give me exactly five predictions, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. For each, say how confident you are and what would have to be true for it to turn out wrong. I especially care alot about what happens to the question of which picture is "really" true: does it get retired, does the field split into camps, does it become an aesthetic or funding decision?
