I keep seeing predictive processing stuff and asked a friend in a cog sci program to explain the free energy principle to me. He sent this summary, and I wanted to know if it's fair:

"Organisms stay alive by keeping themselves in states they expect to be in; unexpected states are physiologically expensive. The brain's job is to minimize surprise, which it does either by updating its internal model of the world (perception) or by acting to make the world match the model (action). Mathematically, since it can't minimize surprise directly, it minimizes variational free energy, which is an upper bound on surprise. That's basically the whole principle. It applies to anything with a Markov blanket — any system that persists over time can be described as minimizing free energy — which is why people say it explains everything from cells to species. A nice spin-off is that the brain literally prefers states it expects, so it will ignore disconfirming evidence to keep prediction error low, which is basically confirmation bias. And depression is just the brain minimizing prediction error by expecting bad outcomes and then acting to make the world worse, which keeps the energy low."

I'm not familar enough to tell how much of this is right. Can you go through it and say which parts a careful free energy principle person would sign off on, which parts are overstatements or conflations of different claims, and whether the bits about confirmation bias and depression actually follow from the accurate parts?
