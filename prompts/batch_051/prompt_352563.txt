I'm teaching a grad seminar on religion and liberal democracy this spring, and Ive got two colleagues—one a public reason liberal in the Rawlsian mold, the other an inclusivist who thinks religious citizens are entitled to vote and argue from their whole worldview—who've been going at each other for years. I want to assign, as the closing text, the joint statement they would produce after a real adversarial collaboration, and I don't think it exists yet. Write it.

The document should start with a numbered list of agreed claims both can sign that are actually substantive—about free and equal citizenship, the coercive character of law, religious liberty, and what makes the disagreement worth having—not just boilerplate about good faith. Then a disagreements table, with each side's view phrased so its own side would say, yes, that's exactly what I think. Then the cruxes: for each, what would have to go differently for each side to move. My own hunch is the real action is in whether the justificatory burden kicks in only for coercion or also for public advocacy, and whether 'accessible to all reasonable citizens' survives contact with actual secular moralities—but don't just take my word for it. And if part of the fight turns out to be about the word 'respect' or the word 'public,' say that plainly.
