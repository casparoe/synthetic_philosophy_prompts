For a handout in a philosophy of science course, write a decision tree that takes someone from their view of Goodman's new riddle of induction to a named response to the grue problem. Each branch node should be a question about the grue case itself that a student could answer without already knowing the standard positions: for example, whether the real difficulty comes from gerrymandered predicates like grue or from the more general fact that any finite body of evidence can be extended in many ways; whether an admissible predicate must answer to the world, to past linguistic practice, or to our cognitive makeup; whether the green/grue pair differs from the green/blue pair in a way that can be stated without presupposing which predicates are projectible; and whether any constraint on projectible predicates can be justified without already relying on induction. The leaves should include at least Goodman's own entrenchment view, a natural-kind or sparse-property realism, a Bayesian or probabilistic dissolution, an interest-relative or relativist view, and a skeptical response that takes the riddle to show induction has no non-arbitrary justification. Give a one-sentence most-awkward-commitment note for each leaf. Flag any positions the tree cannot cleanly separate, and mark the unoccupied combinations of branch answers, saying briefly whether each is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Citations are not needed.
