I'm a retired chemist whose recently gone down a philosophy rabbit hole, and I'm trying to figure out what rule my sister-in-law Marit is actually running. She's Dutch, she's lived in Osaka for over twenty years, and she has strong views about when you're allowed to soften the truth. A few of her rulings over the years:

- A Dutch colleague asked whether his conference talk was any good. It wasn't, and she says you tell him plainly and don't fake enthusiasm.
- A Japanese colleague showed her a proposal she thought couldn't work. She says you don't say "this won't work"; you say "there may be some difficulties with implementation" and let him draw the conclusion.
- A Dutch friend asked whether she'd forgiven him for something old. She says you say no, I haven't, no softening.
- A Japanese acquaintance asked whether she'd come to a wedding she wanted to avoid. She says you smile and say "it may be difficult," which everybody understands is a polite no.

Now her Japanese neighbor is bringing over a dish her household privately finds inedible, and the neighbor will almost certainly ask whether it was too salty. I don't just want a prediction. I want to know which two or three principles could fit her record so far, what each of them would tell her to say to the neighbor, which of the old cases is the closest analog, and what your best guess is about what she'll actually say. Then give me the extra case that would sort the rival rules cleanly if the neighbor case doesn't.
