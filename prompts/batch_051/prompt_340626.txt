Before I read further in the persistence debate, I want to fix what an adequate view would actually have to deliver. Write the desiderata for a theory of persistence through time — of what it is for one and the same thing to exist at different times — including the data it must explain, the cases it must classify correctly, and the distinctions it may not collapse. Cover at least the following:

(i) Change in intrinsic properties, and why the view cannot simply say the object has incompatible properties at different times.

(ii) Coincidence, such as a statue and the lump of clay that constitutes it.

(iii) Fission and fusion, where one thing branches into two or two merge into one.

(iv) Vague and shifting boundaries of composite objects, including piecemeal replacement of every part.

(v) The fact that relativistic physics supplies no privileged global now, so an account cannot quietly assume a single three-dimensional object wholly present at a time.

(vi) Collectives that persist through complete turnover of members, such as a firm or a committee, so that the account is not tailored only to organisms and middle-sized artifacts.

For each requirement, give the case or principle that makes it binding, and say whether it is non-negotiable or a negotiable commitment that some camps would reject. Then identify which one or two requirements do the most work to separate endurantism from perdurantism, and whether any pair of requirements on your list looks jointly unsatisfiable for at least one of the two views. Keep any scorecard of the main views brief: I am after the specification itself and the pressure points, not a summary of the standard positions.
