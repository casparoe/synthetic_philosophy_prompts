I keep going back and forth on whether brain size matters morally, and I'd like you to look at my verdicts on a bunch of cases and tell me what principle, if any, is holding them together.

1. A person born with severe microcephaly, brain about a third the size of a typical adult's, but clearly awake, social, able to be happy or miserable, with preferences about their life. I have zero hesitation treating harm to them as just as serious as harm to anyone else.

2. A sperm whale with a brain six times the mass of mine. I don't think the whale outranks a human just because of the bigger brain, and if I had to choose between saving a drowning human and a drowning whale I'd save the human.

3. A dog versus a mouse. If I can prevent only one of them from suffering, I'd pick the dog, and my honest sense is that the dog's mind is more there than the mouse's. But when I try to say what I'm tracking, the only thing I can point to is brain size.

4. A kid who has had half of there brain removed to stop seizures, and afterward is recognizably the same person with the same personality and relationships. I'd say their moral status dropped not at all, so raw volume and neuron count can't be doing the work.

5. A creature the size of a guinea pig with an walnut-sized brain that we're somehow certain has the same felt experiences as a human: same pleasures, same anxiety, same capacity for disappointment. I'd give it full moral status without a second thought.

6. A machine with a simulated brain bigger than any animal's that behaves intelligently and passes every test, but for all we can tell has no felt experience at all. I'd say it has no moral status grounded in suffering or well-being.

The loose shape I keep reaching for is that brain size only matters as a rough sign of something else, and that something else does the real moral work. But then I don't know why I'm so confident the dog beats the mouse, or why I'm not tempted to give the whale more than the human. Is there a single principle that captures all six of my verdicts, stated precisely? What does it say about a case I haven't listed, like an octopus with a smallish brain but most of its neurons in its arms and strikingly sophisticated behavior, versus a cow with a much bigger brain? And if no clean principle captures them, which verdicts should I revise and why?
