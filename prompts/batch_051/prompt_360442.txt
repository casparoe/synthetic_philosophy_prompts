I've been trying to get clear on Spinoza's claim in the Theological-Political Treatise, chapter 16, that in a democracy each person transfers their natural right to the community as a whole rather than to some other person, and that because of this everyone remains equal, just as they were in the state of nature. But this puzzles me, because he also says natural right is coextensive with power—nobody has a right to anything beyond what their power can secure. If that's the background, how can democracy alone preserve natural equality, when citizens in a democracy obviously differ hugely in power, wealth, intelligence, influence? Is Spinoza's point that the transfer to the community makes each person's power count equally at the level of political authorization, or is he maybe being sloppy? Could you explain what 'equal' actually means in this passage and whether the move from natural right to civil right is supposed to preserve natural equality or replace it with a new, specifically political kind?
