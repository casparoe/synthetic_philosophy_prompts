Consider Daniel Dennett's thought experiment of the careenium. A vast, closed system of tiny spheres — the simmballs — moves and collides in accordance with deterministic physical laws, so that the trajectory of each sphere is fully fixed by its physical state and its collisions with its neighbours. Seen from a higher level of description, however, the system exhibits compressed, stable patterns: certain configurations of simmballs can be read as symbols, and sequences of these symbols can be read as a computation or conversation with genuine semantic content. The question the scenario is meant to sharpen is whether the symbols push the substrate around. Do the meaningful, higher-level patterns ever cause the simmballs to do something they would not otherwise have done, or are the symbols causally inert patterns that merely accompany the underlying dynamics, however useful they are for prediction and explanation?

Write an essay on whether the symbols in the careenium have genuine causal power over the simmballs, or whether they are epiphenomenal. Weigh the strongest arguments on both sides, including at least the following. On one side: since the microphysical dynamics are causally closed and deterministic, every simmball collision is fully explained without reference to semantic content, so the symbols appear to do no independent pushing. On the other: the symbolic level is not a rival to the physical level but a different grain of description, and a pattern that affords accurate, compressed prediction is a real feature of the world; it may be a category mistake to demand that the symbols exert some extra physical force. Use the case to draw out the implications for mental causation, the reality of patterns, and the legitimacy of higher-level explanation. Do not end on a non-committal note: defend a verdict on whether, and in precisely what sense, the symbols can be said to push anything around, and say what your verdict costs. Please write the essay in British English.
