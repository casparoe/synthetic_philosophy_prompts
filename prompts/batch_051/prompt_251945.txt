On a standard death certificate you get asked for the immediate cause of death, then the chain of conditions that led up to it, and then a separate box for other significant conditions. A person with COPD who gets pneumonia and dies of respiratory failure might have respiratory failure as the immediate cause, pneumonia underneath that, COPD underneath that, and tobacco use listed as a contributing condition — while a public health report might count the very same death as tobacco-related. Same death, different official causes depending which box or which report your looking at. What theory of causation does this practice presuppose? It seems to treat a cause as an answer to a why-question rather than one unique thing, which sounds a lot like Aristotle's four causes, but do the four causes actually map onto the form, or is that a stretch? Does the public health habit of calling the modifiable risk factor the cause sneak a teleological element back in, as if the real cause is whatever would let you prevent the death? And what would the certificate have to look like if the coroner held a strict efficient-cause-only view, or a counterfactual view where a cause is just a difference-maker?
