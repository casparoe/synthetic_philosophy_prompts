My two friends got into it last night about a book one of them was reading, which claimed that everything is physical. Marcus said that sentence is basically empty until you say what 'physical' means, and there's a trap either way. If physical means the kinds of things current physics talks about, physicalism is almost certainly false, since current physics is incomplete and will probably get revised the way Newtonian physics did. But if physical means whatever the physics of the far future says exists, then the claim is true no matter what, since that future physics would presumably describe whatever there is to describe, including consciousness if consciousness is real. So physicalism is either false or trivial.

Elena said he'd set up a false choice. You can define physical in terms of an ideal completed physics and still treat physicalism as a substantive bet: namely, that the final theory won't need to posit souls, telekinetic mental pushes, or any irreducibly mental causes. That bet could turn out wrong, so it isn't trivial.

Marcus said that move just smuggles her preffered conclusion into the word physical. If the completed theory did include a field that responds directly to people's decisions, physicalists would more likely say 'I guess that field is physical' than admit physicalism was false, which shows the label isn't doing the predicting. He said to imagine a world otherwise like ours except that whenever someone decides to lift their arm, the arm rises with no force registered and no energy transferred. We'd call that world not physical, but we'd be deciding that by unstated prior ideas about what counts, not by any definition we'd actually given.

Elena said that's exactly her point: the real content of physicalism is a commitment about explanation, that there's no brute mental causation and everything works bottom-up, and our reaction to his imagined world shows that commitment is doing real work. Marcus said then we should just debate that commitment directly and admit the word physical isn't adding anything beyond it.

I honestly couldn't tell who was getting the better of it. Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about what, and what if anything both of them are missing? Please answer in American English.
