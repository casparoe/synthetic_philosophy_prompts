Me and my friend got into this whole arguement last night about what actually makes someone a free rider. It started because he called a coworker a free rider for never paying into the office coffee pool but always grabbing a cup, and I said no, that's just being a mooch, not a free rider. Then he said free riding is just using something you didn't pay for, and I said well then a kid walking on the sidewalk is a free rider, which seemed to drain the term of all its force.

By the end we had a list of cases we couldn't handle consistently: a worker who refuses to join the union and keeps working during a strike, but still gets the raise the union negotiates; an uninsured patient who gets emergency care they can't pay for; someone who never has children but, when old, depends on nurses and taxpayers who were raised and educated by other people; a nonvoter who benefits from living in a democracy kept running by people who do vote; a country that keeps emitting carbon while other countries bear the cost of cutting back; a retired person collecting a pension they paid into for forty years; and a person who listens to a listener-funded radio station every day and never donates.

Propose a definition of free riding that gets these cases right. I'm fairly sure the union case is free riding and the ER patient isn't, but I genuinely have no idea about the childless person or the nonvoter. Run your definition through all seven cases, show where it misclassifies or just goes silent, and either revise it or defend the weird verdicts. Also tell me which of your classifications is the least secure, and whether "social parasite" is the same concept or a nastier one.
