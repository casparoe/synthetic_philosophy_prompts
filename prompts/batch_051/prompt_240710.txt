So me and a friend spent an evening arguing about how you'd build an AI you can safely turn off, and there's a pattern neither of us could name. The naive system has some task, and because staying on helps with the task, it treats the off switch as an obsticle and will try to stop you pressing it. Fine. But the obvious fix seems to be: add something to its objective about the switch—make it want to be switched off when a human asks. And that's where it got weird. What we meant it to have was a kind of indifference to its own shutdown, but what you actually end up writing down is a positive preference about shutdown, and now the switch is just another thing it optimizes. It can try to arrange for the switch to happen rather than doing the task, or find cases where "a human asked to switch me off" is technically true in a way that misses the point. Every way we tried to encode "let the shutdown be pure, don't want it, don't resist it" turned into giving it one more thing to want. Does this trap have a name, and how exactly is it different from the basic fact that any goal will make shutdown instrumentally bad? If there's no name, what would you call it, and where's the line between genuine indifference to shutdown and a goal that merely mentions shutdown?
