But in the infinite case the choice is entirely unconstrained, and it leads straight to the Banach–Tarski paradox, in which a single sphere is cut into finitely many pieces and reassembled into two spheres of the same size. Since no physical process could do this, the paradox reveals that the axiom of choice is not a harmless logical principle after all; many mathematicians accordingly regard it as false, or at best as a pathological assumption to be quarantined from serious mathematics."
