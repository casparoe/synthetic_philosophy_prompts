I'm assembling my views on well-being and would like a consistency check before I commit to them. Right now I hold all of the following.

(1) Hedonism about welfare: how well a person's life goes is settled entirely by the balance of pleasant and unpleasant experience in it.

(2) A felt-quality account of pleasure: an experience is pleasant in virtue of an introspectible hedonic tone it has, not in virtue of the subject's wanting, liking, or being pleased about it.

(3) I adopt (2) partly because desire- and attitude-based accounts seem too intellectualized to me: a pain can be wanted, and a pleasure can arrive unwanted, so being pleasant cannot simply be a matter of one's attitudes.

(4) A case verdict: the sadist's enjoyment genuinely makes the sadist's life go better to that extent, though it makes the act no less wrong.

(5) I also want to say that the pleasure of accomplishing something difficult is more valuable than an equal amount of pleasure from idle amusement.

(6) Yet I find the heterogeneity worry pressing: the pleasures of scratching an itch, enjoying a cruel joke, seeing a proof come together, and soaking in a warm bath feel too dissimilar for me to locate any single felt ingredient they share; the common tone seems read in rather than found.

Which of these actually pull against one another, which only seem to, and what is the cheapest repair if I want to retain as much of the view as possible? Please order the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious.
