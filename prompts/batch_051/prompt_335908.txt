In my ethics study group we keep arguing about what makes consent valid, and someone always reaches for that tea analogy from freshman orientation: sex is like offering someone a cup of tea, if they're asleep you dont make them tea, if they said yes an hour ago they can still say no now, if they're too out of it to answer you dont pour tea down their throat, and so on. The analogy gets used to argue that consent has to be ongoing, freely given, informed, and revocable. I'm suspicious that the analogy is doing a lot of the work and that it quietly breaks exactly where the hard cases about deception, coercion, and competence live.

Audit the analogy for me. Give me the correspondence table: what maps to the offerer, the person being offered the tea, the tea itself, drinking it, wanting it, the kitchen, the earlier cup. Then test it against the validity conditions we actually care about: if I lie about what kind of tea it is, or about why I'm offering it (say it's spiked, or it's decaf when they need caffeine), if I'm their boss and they're afraid to say no, if they're drunk, if they say yes just to get me to stop asking. Does the analogy handle those cases or does it start smuggling in conclusions?
