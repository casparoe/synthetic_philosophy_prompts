In my philosophy course we recently read the white horse dialogue attributed to Gongsun Long of the School of Names, and in my computer science course we have been studying classes, inheritance, and instances. I noticed a structural parallel between the two that I cannot decide is deep or superficial. Gongsun Long argues that a white horse is not a horse, and he gives two kinds of reasons: horse corresponds to a shape while white corresponds to a color, so white horse combines two specifications into a composite sort; and the demand for a horse and the demand for a white horse have different satisfaction conditions, since a bay horse answers the first demand but not the second. This looks to me like the programming distinction between a class and a subclass, or between a type and one of its subtypes: Horse and WhiteHorse are distinct types with different extensions, even though every instance of WhiteHorse is also an instance of Horse. Is Gongsun Long anticipating something like a type–subtype or extension distinction, or is he sliding between set membership and set inclusion in a way my analogy obscures? Where exactly does the analogy hold, and where does it break, and could ideas from programming such as inheritance actually clarify the dialogue?
