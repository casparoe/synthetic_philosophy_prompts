I've been arguing with my roommate about abortion all term and I've realized I can't actually state the principle behind my own reactions, so I started writing down my gut verdicts on the standard cases. If I wake up plugged into the famous violinist, I can unplug, no question, even if it kills him. If a person-seed drifts in through my screens and takes root in the house, I can evict it. If a pregnancy is the result of rape, an abortion at eight weeks is fine. If continuing the pregnancy will kill the mother, fine even quite late. If the fetus has a condition where it would live a few days in agony after birth, abortion at twenty-four weeks also fine. On the hard cases: an eight-week abortion after contraception failed, I don't think it's murder and I don't think the law should stop it, but it doesn't feel morally weightless either, like there's something your failing to give due regard to, though I can't say what. A fourteen-week abortion for sex selection feels not okay to me, and worse than the contraception-failure one. But when I try to say why it's worse, I keep reaching for claims that would make the contraception-failure case wrong too, which I just resisted. And a woman who just keeps putting it off until twenty weeks with nothing unusual going on and then aborts—that feels wrong.

Here's the trouble. The future-like-ours argument gets the twenty-week case right by saying the fetus has a future of value, but the eight-week fetus has a future of value too, so it seems to make my eight-week verdict wrong along the way. And Thomson-style reasoning handles the violinist and probably the rape case, but I don't see how it gets the sex-selection case to be worse than the contraception case without importing motives in a way that seems orthogonal to the fetus's status. What principle, or small set of principles, best captures this pattern? Be precise about what each clause is doing for which case, say which of my verdicts the principle forces me to drop, and give the principle's verdict on a case I keep dodging: a ten-week abortion where the woman just does not want a child, and there is nothing else unusual about the case.
