My ethics professor recently summarized the forgiveness literature for me as follows: "Most philosophers hold that forgiveness is something the wronged party chooses to bestow, never something they could properly be said to owe; a duty to forgive would turn forgiveness into a bare requirement and strip it of the discretionary, gift-like character that defines it. By the same reasoning, forgiveness can be entirely unconditional: the wrongdoer need not apologize, repent, or even acknowledge the wrong. The one real debate left is whether forgiving requires overcoming one's resentment, or whether one can forgive while still blaming the person for what they did." I suspect this compresses several distinct claims, but I don't know enough to say where the distortions are. Which parts of this summary are faithful to the actual philosophical debate, which parts are overstated or subtly conflated, and does the conclusion about unconditionality actually follow from the accurate parts?
