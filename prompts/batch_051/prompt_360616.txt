I've realized I follow a policy in AI safety discussions, and by now I do it on purpose, though I don't see many other people admit to one like it. I'm usually the least technical person in the room. I now treat any argument I can't restate in my own words after two careful reads as carrying zero weight for me. Not low weight or 'I'll provisionally defer' weight: zero, even when several people I respect seem convinced by it. By 'can't restate' I don't mean I find the conclusion surprising or unpleasant; I mean I couldn't reconstruct the chain of reasons, the way I can't tell whether line 17 of someone's proof actually follows from line 16. I started doing this after reading about the obfuscated arguments problem for AI debate: if the judge is too weak to evaluate the arguments, the side that sounds more convinving wins, and the whole procedure stops tracking truth. I figured the least I could do was refuse to be that kind of judge inside my own head. But lately I've been worrying that this rule is self-flattering. The arguments I can restate are mostly the ones I already had background for, so my policy may just be laundering 'I only update on things I already almost believed' as epistemic hygiene. So: is this policy defensible as a response to obfuscation? What would have to be true about the world, or about me, for zero weight to be correct rather than some small nonzero weight? And does the AI debate problem actually support my rule, or does applying it to my own reading life get the lesson exactly backwards?
