I have been unable to resolve an asymmetry in how I judge people and how I design systems. If someone would abuse Gyges' ring — cheat, steal, or ship something harmful the moment no one could detect it — I treat that counterfactual as revealing who they really are, and I refuse to rely on them even while their observable behavior is spotless; I also think less of the person who behaves only because the monitoring is in place. Yet when I design a system, I treat exactly that disposition as the baseline: every user and component is assumed to misbehave whenever detection is unlikely and the payoff is positive, and an engineer who trusts people to exercise unobserved restraint without logs, permissions, or review is called negligent, not principled. The individual evaluator in me treats unobserved restraint as something we may demand of a person; the system designer in me treats it as something no one is entitled to expect. Is there a principle that justifies holding both stances at once, or should one of them give? I want the strongest candidate justification you can find and an honest verdict on whether it actually works.
