I've been going around in circles on the paradox of blackmail—why it's legal to reveal a secret and legal to ask for money, but illegal to say "pay me or I reveal it." I've collected these proposed accounts: (1) the transaction is pure extraction, it creates no new value and just moves money in exchange for keeping information from people who'd benefit from it; (2) it's extortion-like because the blackmailer manufactures a threat and sells relief from it; (3) the wrong is informational privacy, the secret is the target's and the blackmailer uses it as leverage against its own holder; (4) the practice is bad in the aggregate because it rewards snooping and makes everyone's secrets more fragile, whatever you think of any single case. Give me one more account that's not on this list and not a rephrasing or combination of any of them. In particular, I want one that locates the wrong in the structure of the conditional threat itself, rather than in its downstream consequences or in who owns the information. Then tell me honestly whether that structural account is any better than the four I have, or whether it just relocates the mystery.
