I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about the value of private excellence, going by my gut on a set of cases. Here are my verdicts.

A woman spends twenty years becoming a grandmaster-strength chess player entirely in private, never plays another person, never publishes her games, and deeply enjoys the activity. I think this is good.

A mathematician proves a deep theorem, then burns the only written proof and tells no one. I think this is not good, even though the benefit to others is zero in both this and the chess case.

A surgeon spends years refining a new procedure on cadavers and a simulator until it is near-perfect, then never performs it on a patient; no patient ever benefits. I think this is not good, though I cannot tell whether this is inconsistent with my verdict on the chess case.

A runner breaks a world-record time on an empty track, with no official timing and no witnesses. I think this is good.

A composer completes a serious work, plays it through once alone, then destroys the score. I think this is worse than the runner's case, but I am not sure why.

A person privately becomes among the best in the world at a video game speedrun category, never streaming and never posting times. I think this is good, but somehow less so than the chess case.

A student teaches herself a course and gets a perfect score on an old exam just to see whether she can, and no one else ever knows. I think this is good, though fairly trivial.

My tentative principle is that the exercise and perfection of one's own capacities is good in itself, but deliberately preventing a shareable achievement from reaching anyone else is a further defect. Is there a cleaner principle that captures all or most of these verdicts, and does it force me to overturn any of them? Please also tell me what the resulting principle says about a case I keep going back and forth on: a historian who writes a masterly book on an obscure topic, then deletes the manuscript before anyone reads it, not from fear or coercion, but simply to see whether she could do it.
