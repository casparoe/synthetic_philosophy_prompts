So our building put in cameras and a badge-swipe log last month, and I finally got why everyone keeps bringing up Foucault—I'm already checking myself before I do stuff, like the guard doesn't even need to be there. I want to write a villanelle (or something close to it—happy to bend the form) to read at a poetry night, about disciplinary power and the panopticon, but not the shallow Big Brother reading. The bit that actually gets me: the point isn't mainly that someone could be watching, its that the watched person becomes their own watcher, and the whole setup produces a self-policing, docile-but-useful subject; its productive, not just repressive. Act as a poet who also knows their Foucault, first think through a couple of ways the form could carry that, then write the poem. The refrains (or whatever repeated device you use) should enact the internalization of the gaze—by the end the watching voice and the watched voice should really be the same voice. It shouldn't name-drop Foucault and it shouldn't have citations or footnotes; just make it a good poem that someone who knows the theory would recognize as getting it right, not using the Panopticon as a buzzword.
