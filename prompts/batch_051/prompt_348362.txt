My friend, who's read a bit of Nozick, summarized the Wilt Chamberlain argument like this: "If you start from an equal distribution and let people spend their money freely — say a million people each pay a quarter to watch Wilt Chamberlain play basketball — the equal distribution is immediately destroyed, because Chamberlain ends up rich and everyone else is just a quarter poorer. Since nobody thinks there's anything wrong with those voluntary trades, Nozick concludes that redistribution aimed at restoring equality is unjust, and that this refutes any pattered principle of distributive justice, including Rawls's difference principle, so all taxation beyond a minimal state is morally on a par with forced labor." I'm incline to believe him, but the whole thing sounds too tidy. Which parts of this are an accurate rendering of what the Chamberlain example is actually supposed to show, which parts overreach, and where would a careful defender of Nozick say "hold on, that's not quite what the argument does"? Does the conclusion about taxation survive once the sloppy bits are corrected?
