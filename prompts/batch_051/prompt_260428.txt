I run a reading group on the "is the world getting less violent?" debate and every session dissolves into the same three people talkng past each other, because one means street crime, one means war, and one means famines and poverty and pollution, and nobody's agreed whether to count per capita or raw totals. Could you build a decision tree that sorts someone into their actual position on this, instead of them just picking a team?

The tree should open with a starting question about what counts as violence for them, then branch. Every node should be phrased as a concrete case or claim that someone can answer without already knowing which named position it pushes them towards. Use at least these three fixed cases somewhere in the diagnostic questions: (1) a fatal bar fight between two strangers, (2) a civilian killed by a drone strike in a country her killer's government isn't formally at war with, (3) a child dying of a preventable infection because the treatment is cheap but the local health system was defunded. And include a node that makes the per-capita/absolute fork bite, like asking whether a society of one million with 1,000 murders is more violent than a society of fifty million with 3,000 murders.

The leaves I need are:
- the Pinker-style view that direct interpersonal and war deaths have fallen dramatically over centuries,
- a structural-violence view that the decline is an artefact of a narrow definition and the harm has largely moved into famines, poverty, prisons, pollution and the like,
- a no-fact-of-the-matter view that says "violence" is too contested for the question to have a determinate answer,
- and a per-capita/absolute split view that says the whole argument is two different questions, so once you fix which one you're asking the dispute mostly evaporates.

Build the space of questions and answer-combinations first, then locate those positions in it as leaves; flag any combinations that nobody occupies and say whether each looks incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag which leaves the tree can't really separate, and what extra question would be needed. Keep it to six to eight branch questions after the opening one, and write the whole thing in British English. A typo or two on my end dosen't mean you should clean up the structure, just make it usable.
