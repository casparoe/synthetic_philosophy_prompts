I'm a retired physicist, and I keep going back and forth about how much of the free energy principle is actual science. Give me your credence that the free energy principle, as applied to biological self-organization and perception, is a substantive empirical claim rather than a mostly mathematical story that can be fit to anything. Answer as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion. Decompose the question along these lines: (1) the probability that the claim "any persisting system can be described as minimizing some free energy under some generative model" is close to trivially true; (2) the probability that Markov blankets pick out real boundaries of living systems rather than just being a convenient redescription; (3) the probability that active inference makes predictions that are genuinely distinguishable from plain Bayesian or homeostatic alternatives; (4) the probability that the main empirical successes usually cited in favor of FEP actually favor it over those alternatives; and (5) your combined credence, plus which of the first four your number is most sensitive too and what one experiment or observation would shift it most. My own vague prior is around 0.4, but I care more about seeing where the load-bearing uncertainty is.
