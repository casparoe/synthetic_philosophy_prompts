I have been reading Fearon's rationalist account of war alongside recent arguments about AI development races, and I cannot decide whether a structural parallel I keep noticing is deep or merely verbal. Fearon's puzzle is that war is costly ex post, so if states are rational there should always be some negotiated bargain both sides prefer to fighting; he then explains bargaining failure with three mechanisms: private information about capabilities or resolve together with incentives to misrepresent, commitment problems in which a bargain that is mutually preferable today cannot be made credible because future power shifts, and issue indivisibilities (which he himself largely sets aside, since side payments ought to make almost anything divisible). An AI race appears to pose the same puzzle: an all-out race toward advanced AI is costly for everyone involved—safety corners get cut, verification becomes harder, and a catastrophic failure would fall broadly—so one might expect the leading labs and states to prefer some negotiated slowdown. Yet the same three mechanisms seem to map over almost perfectly: labs have private information about their progress and strong incentives to conceal it; any agreement to pause or share safety-relevant findings runs into a commitment problem, because today's leader cannot bind its future self not to exploit a growing lead; and control over the decisive system can look like an indivisible prize, even if it is only made indivisible by prestige, market concentration, and the difficulty of side payments in secret.

I want to know whether this mapping is a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion. Please audit it: where do Fearon's mechanisms genuinely transfer to AI races, and where does the analogy snap? In particular, what would have to be true on both sides for conclusions from the rationalist-war literature to transfer unchanged, and can you name at least one thing the parallel seems to predict about AI races that turns out false? My own suspicion is that the biggest break is that the actors in an AI race do not internalize the global risk they jointly create, which makes the situation more like a public-goods or collective-action problem than a bargaining breakdown between unitary states—but I would like that suspicion tested rather than confirmed.
