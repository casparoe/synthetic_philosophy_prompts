I'm a PhD student in machine learning safety, and I've been going back and forth about whether AI-safety-via-debate can really survive the case of obfuscated arguments. The standard line is that a judge doesn't need to independently verify a proposed answer; two agents take turns pointing out flaws until the judge sees which side is more honest. That works nicely when an error is local, like a bad line in a proof or a factual contradiction. But I'm less convinced when the error is deliberately smeared across the argument so that seeing it requires a lot of simultaneous cognitive context, while generating it requires almost none. A dishonest debater can bury a flaw in a superficially coherent heap of technical detail, and the honest debater has to spend the entire transcript undoing the heap piece by piece. If the judge has a fixed and limited working memory, that looks like an asymmetry in favor of obfuscation. I want you to write an essay on this specific controversy. Take a real stand: does AI-safety-via-debate have a principled way to handle obfuscated arguments, or not? Defend your verdict with a conceptual argument, and be as precise as you can about what "obfuscated" should mean. I'm not asking for a empirical literature review—just reason it through from the structure of the problem. Please write in American English.
