I keep seeing eliminative materialists argue, in the Churchlands' style, that belief/desire talk is a stagnant folk theory and that mature neuroscience will eliminate it the way chemistry eliminated phlogiston, not reduce it. But the most influential recent frameworks in cognitive science seem to have put belief- and desire-like states right back at the center: predictive processing treats the brain as doing Bayesian inference with priors, and reinforcement-learning accounts of decision making work with value functions and world models. Does the eliminativist argument survive this development, or are those technical notions different enough that folk psychology gets no support from them? I'm asking which premise of the old argument is now under pressure, and whether there's a repaired version that's still worth holding.
