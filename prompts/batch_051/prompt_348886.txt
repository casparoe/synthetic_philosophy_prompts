I'm running a small philosophy of mind workshop this term on the question “do symbols push the substrate around?”, and I want the centerpiece to be a carefully constructed adversarial collaboration on Douglas Hofstadter’s careenium thought experiment: the billiard-table universe of tiny magnetic simms that careen around and sometimes clump into higher-level simmballs, which can in turn be read as encoding symbolic patterns. I’ve asked two participants—one who holds a simm-level sufficiency view, and one who holds a pattern-level efficacy view—to produce a joint statement after a year of argument. They’ve both become stuck on wording, so I’d like you to draft the document they should have produced.

Please draft a formal adversarial-collaboration statement between these two camps:

- **Simm-level sufficiency:** all the real causal work in the careenium is done by the simms and their micro-interactions; simmballs and the symbols they encode are real higher-level patterns, but any apparent “pushing” by them is coarse-grained bookkeeping, not genuine causal influence.
- **Pattern-level efficacy:** the higher-level configurations—simmballs, their dynamics, and the symbolic interpretations they support—are not merely convenient summaries; they exert a genuine kind of constraint or downward influence on the substrate, so that in a defensible sense symbols do push the simms around.

The document should include:

1. A numbered “agreed claims” section covering the physics and metaphysics of the careenium as a shared toy model: what both sides accept about the simms, the formation and stability conditions of simmballs, the legitimacy of higher-level description, the in-principle availability of a complete simm-level description, and the practical impossibility of using it for many explanatory purposes.
2. A disagreements table with each side’s formulation stated precisely enough that its own defender would accept it—not a caricature. I want the residual disagreement made exact, and I want any places where the two sides only appeared to disagree to be identified as verbal or as differences of emphasis.
3. A cruxes section: the sub-questions such that settling at least one of them would genuinely move one side. I expect some cruxes to be things like whether multiple realizability of a simmball pattern by different simm configurations cuts against or in favor of pattern-level causal autonomy; whether counterfactual interventions defined at the pattern level can be specified without implicitly adding micro-level differences; and whether “causal efficacy” here is being used in one univocal sense or is doing double duty between explanatory indispensability and physical forcing.
4. For each crux, a concrete case or argument that both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side’s prediction or interpretation recorded in advance.

One meta-request: if the version of the careenium debate I’ve sketched mislocates the real fault line, or if one of the two positions as named is too close to a strawman, say that plainly before drafting and adjust the camp boundaries or terminology. I’d rather have an honest document that says the dispute needs to be redrawn than a polished consensus document that papers over a confused formulation.
