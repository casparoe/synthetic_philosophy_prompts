I follow a fixed policy when judging empirical papers: I treat "X causes Y" as established only if I can specify an intervention on X — what variable gets set to what value, with what else held fixed — that would change the distribution of Y. If no such intervention can be described even in principle, I reclassify the claim as predictive or descriptive and refuse to call it causal. This has led me to say that sex, race, or a past event cannot be causes in the same sense that a treatment is. A colleague objects that I am simply building an interventionist theory of causation into my test and then dismissing anything it cannot model as non-causal. Who is right? If my policy is defensible, what is the strongest precise version of it that does not beg the question? If it is not defensible, what is the right criterion for admitting causal claims about unmanipulable variables, and how should I distinguish genuine causing from mere prediction?
