I chair my university's academic appeals commitee, and we've got a case that's genuinely split us. Our penalty tariff is deliberately generalist: any student whose submitted work contains unacknowledged material from another source gets a zero for the module, full stop — the code says it doesn't matter whether it was three sentences or three chapters. This term a final-year student copied two sentences from a lecture handout into the conclusion of her dissertation without a citation. She doesn't dispute the facts, but she argues the committee should weigh the particular circumstances: the sentences were stock definitional filler, she was quite ill at the time, and the rest of the dissertation is plainly her own work. Under the code, though, a zero for the module fails her degree.

Half the committee says we should hold the line and change the code only prospectively, otherwise we dissolve a consistent rule into case-by-case intuition and no one can predict what counts as plagiarism any more. They also point out that we applied the scheduled zero to a very similar case last year, so relenting now invites an inconsistency complaint. The other half — the self-described particularists — say no codified tariff can capture when copying is minor versus serious, and that trying to list aggravating and mitigating factors just pushes the judgement down a level: we'd still have to weigh the factors contextually, so the generalist project is particularist all the way down. They want us to exercise judgement in this case and stop pretending the tariff substitutes for it.

I know this is really the moral generalism-versus-particularism debate in miniature, but it's an actual degree on the line, so I need the memo, not a seminar. Draft the short decision memo I'll circulate before Friday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete outcome for this student and say what the committee should do about the code going forward, and it should take seriously the strongest point on the side you end up rejecting — not tackle a straw man of it.
