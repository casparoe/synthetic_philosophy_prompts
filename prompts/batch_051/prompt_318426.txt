I run a small rationality-training team inside a mid-size tech company. We do a workshop about once a month for product managers and ops leads, and I've become convinced the only part that sticks is the part that looks like martial-arts training: not slides about biases, but repeated small drills (katas) with a clear scoring criterion and a plan for transferring the skill to real decisions. The problem is I keep designing drills that feel great in the room and vanish by the next sprint, or that measure quiz-taking instead of thinking. I want a reusable procedure I can run, in about 90 minutes per candidate, to decide whether a given rationality concept can be turned into a drill actually worth teaching. It should be usable in plain language: the questions to ask in order, where I stop with a no, what a drill has to include to count as practice rather than exposure, and which steps are judgment calls I should own instead of hiding behind the checklist. Our client's L&D lead has to sign off on anything I run, so the procedure should say what I'd show them. Then run the procedure on three candidate drills: a sunk-cost drill for project leads, a pre-mortem drill for ops planning, and a base-rate-updating drill I've grown weirdly attached to. Be honest if the procedure kills that last one, and say what the procedure can't catch even when followed.
