I have been developing a position I call "retroactive corruption" to hold together two things I am unwilling to give up: the doctrine that creation is good, and the apparent fact that predation, parasitism, disease, and extinction predate human beings by hundreds of millions of years. The usual reconciliation treats the Fall as a historical event that turned a previously painless creation into a violent one, but the fossil and geological record seems to rule that out, since the violence was already there long before we were. My proposal is that the Fall should not be treated as an event located inside the timeline at all. I think of the created order as a single divine act whose determinate character depends on its relation to the final, free human refusal of God. The Fall, on this view, does not so much occur at a point within the history of creation as constitute that history: the corruption is backdated across the entire timeline, including the stretches that precede any actual faller, not through a causal ripple running backward in time but because the temporal whole is constituted from its end, and the end of this creation is estrangement from God. Understood this way, God can truthfully declare creation good because goodness attaches to the whole as ordered to its intended end; the prehuman suffering recorded in the rocks is then the sort of thing a good creation contains when that creation is, from its end, already fallen.

I am aware this sounds more like speculative metaphysics than theology, and I want an honest assessment rather than encouragement. Please reconstruct the view in its strongest form before criticizing it. Then tell me whether it is coherent, whether I have reinvented an existing position under a worse name, and what it gets right, if anything. Finally, give me the three or four strongest objections. In particular, address the charge that this makes the Fall unfalsifiable or empties it of content because it is no longer an event at all, and the charge that it makes God the author of a world whose entire history is shaped by sin from the outset. Which of these objections, if any, is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable?
