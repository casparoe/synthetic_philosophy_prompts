I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training — mostly backend services in Go — and I've been reading philosophy of mind on and off, mainly because I kept seeing people say the mind is basically software running on the brain's hardware and I wanted to know how seriously that's meant. The idea that hooked me is multiple realizability: that the same mental state can be implemented in lots of different physical systems, like the same program running on different processors. I want to write a short essay pushing back on that from a working engineer's angle, and I'd like comments on whether my objection actually lands before I embarass myself.

The essay would go roughly like this. 'Same software' is a much squishier notion than philosophers seem to assume. Last year we rewrote our checkout service from Python to Go; it passed the same integration tests, same inputs and outputs, same log lines. But nobody on the team would say it was literally the same program. And the differences that actually mattered were exactly the implementation-level ones — memory profile, behaviour under load, which race conditions it does or doesn't have, the bugs it's likely to accumulate next. Now I know functionalists don't claim to be talking about input-output behaviour alone: they individuate mental states by their causal or functional role. But the more you spell out that functional role so it includes all the causal detail that matters in a real system (how a state degrades, what it interacts with and when), the more implementation-specific the description gets, and the less work 'what it's made of doesn't matter' is doing. So either the claim that a mental state can be shared across brains and silicon is a thin, behaviour-level claim and too weak for what functionalists want, or it's a thick, full causal-role claim and it stops being genuinely multiply realizable. I'm pretty sure I'm conflating something (levels of description? our human preferences about code?), so I'd like your comments: is this worth developing, and what's the strongest version of the objection I'm reaching for?

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — a verdict on whether the essay is worth developing and whether the core objection is any good — then give your reasoning after that. No preamble, no restating the question, and British English please.
