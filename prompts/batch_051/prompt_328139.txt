I have recently had to confront a problem of interpretation as executor of my late sister's estate. The probate court will not approve the final account until I propose a distribution of the residue, and her will contains the following clause: "I give, devise, and bequeath all the rest, residue, and remainder of my estate, in equal shares, to and among such of my children as shall be living at the time of my death." She died five years ago, leaving one son then living. Before beginning chemotherapy, she and her husband had several embryos stored using her ova. Two years after her death, one of those embryos was transferred to a gestational carrier and carried to term; the resulting child is now three years old and has been treated in the proceedings as my sister's child. The clause appears, from the estate papers, to have been intended simply to ensure that only children who survived her would take, rather than to permit a share to pass through the estate of a child who predeceased her; nothing suggests she or the drafter contemplated a child conceived after her death. I am not looking for a prediction of what a court would do. I want to know how the principal approaches to reading such a clause—the natural meaning of the words, the testator's probable intent, what she would have directed had the question been put to her, and what a reasonable beneficiary would have understood—apply here. I am also interested in the legal fiction that an unborn child is treated as born for the child's benefit: whether it can be extended to an embryo stored outside the body at the date of death, and what one's answer commits one to about how legal personality arises for inheritance. Where the approaches diverge, which should govern a will of this kind, and why?
