I've been in the same philosophy discussion group for a couple of years now, and there's one recurring non-event I want diagnosed rather than re-run. Whatever topic we set out to discuss — fine-tuning, hiddenness, the problem of evil — we always end up in the same standoff, and weirdly it isnt between the theists and everyone else but between the atheists and the agnostics. Both sides agree the theist bears the burden of proof. Both sides agree the public evidence makes God's existence unlikely, maybe very unlikely, and if you push them they'll even give similar numbers. And yet the atheists insist the rational stance is atheism understood as merely lacking belief in God: you don't adopt a claim until it clears its burden, and until then non-belief is where you already are, not a claim you're making. The agnostics insist the rational stance is agnosticism understood as an explicit suspension of judgement: where the evidence is genuinely uncertain, the intellectually honest state is a considered "I don't know," and refusing to adopt either theism or its negation is not a lapse but the correct response to balanced evidence. The atheists then say agnosticism is just atheism with extra steps, or a way of treating a refusal to believe as if it carried some additional justificatory obligation. The agnostics say lack-of-belief atheism is a rhetorical manoeuvre that makes one side look like the default so it can inherit the theist's burden without ever discharging it. I don't want theism re-litigated, and I dont want a verdict on who is right.

My working hypothesis is that the argument is partly verbal — a fight about what the word "atheism" should mean — but only partly, because underneath there may be a genuine disagreement about what a rational agent's baseline doxastic state is when a claim hasn't met its burden (non-belief vs. suspension), and maybe also about whether epistemic norms and dialectical norms can come apart.

So: why has this debate stalled, and what kind of disagreement is it actually? Is it verbal, substantive, or both? Do the sides differ about facts, about norms of belief, about conversational or dialectical etiquette, or about which mistakes are worse? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or should no progress be expected and why?
