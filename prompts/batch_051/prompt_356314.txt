I'm running a small reading group on moral cultures and conflict, and we keep going in circles on whether any of the three ideal types people usually talk about — dignity culture, honor culture, victimhood culture — is actually morally better, not just descriptively different. I want to turn the next meeting into a structured scoring exercise, partly to force people to stop just defending their favorite. Could you rate each of the three cultures from 0 to 10 on each of these five dimensions: (a) protecting people from being quietly walked over in ordinary low-stakes interactions; (b) handling serious violations like assault, theft, or reputation-destruction when formal institutions are weak, corrupt, or slow; (c) giving wrongdoers a path to make amends and be reintegrated rather than permanently written off; (d) producing honest social signals rather than strategically performing toughness or performing victimhood to extract sympathy and third-party support; and (e) limiting escalation spirals, feuds, and retaliation loops. Then give each culture an overall score, explain what weights you put on the five dimensions and why, and rank the three cultures from most to least morally defensible. I'm honestly less interested in the final ranking than in the reasoning — and I'd especially like you to address whether the scoring scheme itself is rigged, e.g. whether its weighting, or even the very idea of scoring cultures on a shared rubric, already smuggles in dignity-culture assumptions about what counts as a fair metric.
