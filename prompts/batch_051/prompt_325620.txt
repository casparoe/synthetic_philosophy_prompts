Heres an argument a friend made about envy and resentment, and I can't find the step that breaks. (The conclusion feels too strong, but the first three steps seem tight.)

(1) An emotion can be a signal of injustice only if its content is that someone has been wronged—i.e., that someone was denied something they had a legitimate claim to.
(2) The content of envy is not that someone has been wronged; it is that another person has more of some good than I do, whether or not that advantage is unjust.
(3) So envy cannot be a signal of injustice.
(4) The content of resentment is that one has been wronged, or that a legitimate expectation has been violated.
(5) Therefore resentment can be a signal of injustice, while envy cannot.

Rawls complicates this: he says envy can be excusable when inequalities get large enough to undermine the worst-off's self-respect, which sounds like envy is telling us something about the injustice of the basic structure. Is the argument valid? If not, say exactly which step (or inference between steps) fails, and give the strongest reading of each premise on which it is true. If you think it is valid, say which premise you would weaken and how. A real diagnosis please—not just 'signal is vague'. Also say whether, on your best reconstruction, envy ends up as a signal of injustice, a symptom, or neither.
