I am currently taking a course on quantified modal logic, and in this week's problem seminar two other students, Maya and Ravi, got into an argument about the Barcan formulas. They later asked me to referee, and I genuinely could not. The two formulas are the Barcan formula (BF), ◇∃x Fx → ∃x ◇Fx, and the converse Barcan formula (CBF), ∃x ◇Fx → ◇∃x Fx.

Maya opened: BF is clearly false. It is possible that there exists a sibling of hers who was never actually born. So the antecedent of BF is true; BF then yields that there exists something that is possibly her sibling. But there is no such thing in the actual world. Therefore BF must be rejected, along with the constant-domain semantics that validates it.

Ravi replied that Maya is simply presupposing that the quantifier ranges only over actual, concrete things. If the quantifier ranges over everything there is, in the broadest sense, then that possible sibling is something, and BF is harmless. What Maya calls the alternative is a varying-domain semantics whose quantifier rules have to be patched with free logic.

Maya answered that varying-domain semantics is coherent and captures the ordinary thought that different worlds contain different things. Constant domains, by contrast, commit one to necessitism: the same objects exist at every world, so nothing could have failed to exist. She would rather accept the complications of free logic than that consequence.

Ravi responded that accepting BF need not force a realm of merely possible objects onto her; the witness to 'possibly my sibling' could be an actual thing under a modal description. He added that necessitism is less absurd than it sounds once one distinguishes being something from being concrete at a world.

Please adjudicate their disagreement move by move: for each move, say whether it is sound, what it assumes, and where it rests on a semantic choice rather than a settled intuition. Then say who is right about what overall, what both of them are missing, and how CBF bears on the question whether everything that exists exists necessarily. Please write your answer in British English.
