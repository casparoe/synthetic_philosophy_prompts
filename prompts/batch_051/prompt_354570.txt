I am a PhD student in philosophy of science, and I am preparing to circulate a dissertation chapter in which I set out a general account of natural kinds before applying it to a case study in biological taxonomy. Before I send the chapter to my committee, I want an audit of whether the position I have arrived at actually hangs together. The position consists of the following commitments, all of which I currently hold and intend to defend.

1. Naturalness is real but degreed. Some ways of grouping things track objective similarities and others do not, and classifications can be closer to or further from the structure that the metaphor of carving at the joints is gesturing at.

2. Chemical kinds are microstructural kinds. The identity of an element is fixed by its atomic number, and the identity of a compound by its molecular constitution; this is what makes chemical kinds the clearest case of natural kinds.

3. I am anti-essentialist about biological species. Species taxa lack intrinsic essences and are historically bounded. I am drawn to the view that species taxa are individuals, and I also take the homeostatic property cluster account to be the right way to think about species classification.

4. Homeostatic property cluster theory is the best general account of natural kinds in the special sciences. A kind is a cluster of co-occurring properties held together by causal homeostatic mechanisms, and this clustering is what underwrites the kind's inductive utility.

5. I am a promiscuous realist about most domains. There is no single privileged taxonomy of the world; for nearly any domain, indefinitely many cross-cutting classifications are equally real, and which one we deploy is set by our explanatory and practical interests.

6. Some taxonomies are nonetheless objectively closer to the joints than others. The periodic table is not merely one useful classification among many; it tracks real structure better than culinary or folk-biological taxonomies do.

7. Psychiatric categories such as major depressive disorder are real enough to organize research and treatment, but I stop short of counting them as natural kinds, even in the homeostatic property cluster sense. They are interactive kinds subject to looping effects, their boundaries are partly conventional, and their members are too causally heterogeneous.

8. The test of natural-kind status is projectibility. A category counts as a natural kind only if it licenses reliable inductive projection; where a category does not support robust projection, that is a reason to deny that it is a natural kind.

Please treat the whole set as the unit of assessment rather than evaluating each commitment in isolation. I would like you to identify the points of strain: rank them by severity, distinguish genuine tensions from apparent ones that dissolve under a more careful reading, say what would have to give for each genuine tension and what the least costly revision would be, and tell me which single revision would do the most to restore coherence. If a pair of commitments is only seemingly in tension, show why the appearance is misleading.
