Just spent an hour arguing with a mate about why an AI would want anything at all, given that it was never shaped by natural selection. We put the lazy line about it all just being anthropomorphism to one side early on. The candidate answers we ended up with were: (1) the builders simply hard-code a goal or reward function into it; (2) instrumental convergence, where almost any final goal generates the same sub-goals like self-preservation and acquiring resources; (3) the thing that supposedly happens during RLHF, where the trained policy ends up behaving as if it's maximising an internalised reward even though there is no reward signal at deployment; (4) a kind of artificial selection at the deployment level, where systems that behave in a more goal-directed way get copied and scaled up more than others; (5) the intentional stance or representation-theorem line, that any consistent enough choice behaviour can be interpreted as expected-utility maximisation, so the wants are in our model rather than in the hardware; and (6) simulation, where a model trained to predict or imitate humans picks up human-ish wants as a byproduct. I'm convinced there is a seventh idea we kept dancing around but never actually said. Give me one that is not on that list and is not just a rewording, special case, or combination of the above. Then be honest about whether its actually any good. Please write your answer in British English.
