I write about science and ideas for a magazine, and I'm kicking around a piece on whether the simulation argument should make us less fanatical about the far future. Here's the tension I can't get past: the simulation argument seems to use the same move fanatics about the far future use — take a scenario that sounds like sci-fi, notice it implies an astronomical number of minds, and let that reorganize your priorities. If you're willing to do that for ancestor simulations, what's the principled objection to doing it for the far future? Yet the dampening move seems to need the simulator to be real enough to make our far future matter less, while our simulated far future is supposedly too fake to matter. That feels like special pleading unless someone can spell out the difference. Weigh both sides. Don't end on "it depends on your priors" — defend a verdict, and clarify what "dampen" means for you (confidence, moral weight, or priority). End with a final line that begins "Verdict:" and states your conclusion in one sentence. American English, please.
