I'm in Year 12 and our philosophy club did a session on David Lewis's modal realism, and it's been stuck in my head because it keeps sounding like how my friends and I talk about fanfiction. The version I got was this: every possible world is just as real as ours; our world isn't special except that it's the one we happen to be in; and instead of the same person existing in lots of worlds, each world has a counterpart of that person, which is similar but not literally them. Also the worlds are meant to be exhaustive, so any way a world could be, some world is like that.

Put that next to fandom talk and it maps scarily well: all the AUs are equally real within the fiction, the canon timeline is the one the fic happens to start from rather than a metaphysically privileged one, and AU versions of a character aren't the same person, they're basically counterparts already. But I can also see the mismatch: AUs are made up by authors in our world, whereas Lewis's worlds are supposed to be concrete and sealed off from each other, with nothing in one causing anything in another; and fanfic mostly starts by varying the canon, whereas Lewis thinks there are worlds with no connection to our history at all.
