Imagine a mid-size grantmaking foundation that's worried its program officers keep favoring applicants from their own ethnic or religious communities. So they put in a new system. Every application first gets scored by a vendor's algorithm that tries to predict “expected community impact” from the anonymized proposal text alone. Program officers can only override the algorithm for applications in the top 20% or bottom 20%; to do so they have to file a “loyalty disclosure” stating whether they personally know the applicant, share an affiliation, or feel any special obligation. The disclosures go on an internal dashboard. Each officer's year-end bonus depends on how often their overrides are later validated by an independent impact assessment. Seperately, the foundation creates a “Loyalty Committee” that meets quarterly to showcase cases where an officer argued for a project because of shared identity, provided the project scored in the top 5% anyway. Walk through the incentives this creates for program officers, applicants, the impact assessors, and the algorithm vendor. Where does it get gamed? Does it actually separate legitimate partiality-as-local-knowledge from bias, or does it just push the bias somewhere else? What second-order effects should the board expect in what gets funded and who leaves the foundation? And are the failure modes fixable without scrapping the whole scheme?
