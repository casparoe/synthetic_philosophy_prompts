A friend of mine has been stewing for weeks because a coworker got a much bigger raise than she did. They do roughly the same work, but the coworker got it by threatening to leave for a competitor, and my friend thinks that kind of self-promotion is a bit grubby. The thing is, the raises came out of a fixed pool, so the coworker getting more did mean my friend got less. But her anger isn't aimed at the manager who made the decision or at the pay system — its aimed at the coworker, who she says 'gamed' it. She's ashamed of how much this is bothering her and keeps asking whether she's just envious, and whether that would make the feeling illegitimate. I can't untangle whether her feeling is resentment at an actual unfairness, or envy of someone who got away with something, and whether the answer should change what she does next (she's thinking of filing a complaint, but suspects she'd be doing it out of spite). How should a philosopher think through this?
