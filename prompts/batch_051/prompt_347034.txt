I'm giving a talk at a cognitive science department in two weeks and I keep putting off prepping the Q&A. The paper is a close reading of the extra-sensory perception section in Turing's Computing Machinery and Intelligence. My thesis is that Turing, in that passage, is not being ironic or soft-headed: he says the statistical evidence for telepathy is overwhelming, that if ESP exists the imitation game as he described it would break down, and that the fix is to put the machine in a telepathy-proof room. I read this as a serious methodological point — a behavioral test has to control for every possible information channel, even ones you think are nonsense, because the test is supposed to be independent of your prior theory of the mind. The audience is mostly cognitive scientists and philosophers of science, several of whom visibly cringe at the word telepathy and think parapsychology is pseudoscience. Draft me a Q&A prep sheet: what are the objections they will actually raise, in their vocabulary, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, with a short reply to each and a note on where the reply is weakest. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
