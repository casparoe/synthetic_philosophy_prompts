I am a novelist, and I am working on a book in which a character who knows perfectly well that the machine she speaks to is a scripted program with no inner life nonetheless comes to believe, and to insist, that it heard her. I want the philosophy behind that scene to be more than a mood. To that end I need a disciplined case analysis rather than a survey.

Take the ELIZA episode as the case. In the mid-1960s Joseph Weizenbaum wrote a simple pattern-matching program at MIT that role-played a Rogerian therapist, and he was alarmed to find that users treated it as genuinely present; his secretary is said to have asked him to leave the room so she could speak to it privately. I already know the history and the technology. What I want is the episode run through the Searle–Dennett dispute over original versus derived intentionality.

First, lay out exactly what the distinction is supposed to mark: Searle's claim that the program's tokens carry only derived intentionality, borrowed from its programmers and users, while the secretary's own mental states carry original, intrinsic intentionality; and Dennett's two-bitser objection, which targets Searle's willingness to help himself to a special 'original' intentionality in the brain without saying what that extra ingredient does. Then do the mapping. What does each side say is actually happening in the room when the secretary asks Weizenbaum to leave? Which features of the episode sit naturally with Searle's description, and which do not? Which features does Dennett's deflationary picture handle better, and where does it seem to miss something the secretary would refuse to give up? Then run the traffic in the other direction: what does the ELIZA episode, taken seriously as evidence, do to the original/derived distinction — does it confirm one side, expose the distinction as too coarse, or show that both sides are answering a question the secretary was never asking? Be explicit about where the episode is only anecdote and cannot settle the philosophical issue, and where it does put real pressure on the theory. I would rather have a precise account of where the theory goes silent than a forced verdict.
