I'm a graduate student in a philosophy of technology seminar, and I'm considering a term-paper project (one semester, several thousand words) on a question I keep going back and forth about: whether transformative AI has already happened. When people disagree about this, it often looks as though they are not disagreeing primarily about the state of recent systems but about what would count as "transformative" in the first place. For some, the implicit standard is a discrete break comparable to the Industrial Revolution; for others, the fact that networked computing, search, and recommender systems have already reorganized work, attention, and scientific practice settles the matter in favor of "yes"; for still others, the relevant test concerns something harder to observe in the short run, such as a lasting acceleration in long-run growth, a large share of cognitive tasks becoming automatable, or a change in the rate at which new technologies are themselves developed. I suspect the question may be ill-posed until an evaluative baseline and a threshold are fixed, and that the choice among baselines is doing more of the work than participants acknowledge.

Please write me a short research proposal that turns this half-formed suspicion into a defensible project. The proposal should state the question precisely enough that it admits of a determinate answer, lay out the main candidate criteria for transformativeness and show how adopting one rather than another changes the verdict on whether transformative AI has already arrived, and then argue for a particular approach to making progress. I lean toward a conceptual-engineering or explication-style approach: fix one or more purpose-relative notions of "transformative AI," identify which purposes the standard uses in AI governance and longtermist discussion actually require, and ask whether those purposes support a determinate yes-or-no answer about our current situation. But I am open to being talked into a different framing if you think there is a better one. The proposal should also say what a successful outcome would look like, what would count as failure, and how to keep the project tractable within a semester. You do not need to include a bibliography or cite specific works; I will fill in the references myself, though you are welcome to refer to positions and arguments by their content.
