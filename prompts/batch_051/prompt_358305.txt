I was on a jury a few months back and I keep chewing on how I judged testimony. The witnesses I believed were not the ones making the biggest, most expensive show of sincerity. They were the ones doing small things they clearly couldn't help: a voice cracking at an awkward point, a man going red while admitting something that made him look bad, someone losing the thread of a prepared answer. The more a sign looked like it could be produced on purpose, the less weight I gave it, even when producing it would have cost the person something real. I noticed the same thing with public apologies: the polished, expensive-looking one left me cold; the halting, off-message one landed. Since then I've been reading about animal signaling, and everyone brings up the handicap principle, where a signal is honest because its costly. But what I'm describing feels almost backwards: the trustworthy signs were ones the person couldn't buy with effort in the first place. Does this pattern have its own name? Is it a special case of the handicap principle, or a different mechanism entirely, and where exactly is the line between them? A bare label wont help me; I want the boundaries, and what it would take to reclassify something from one category to the other. Keep your answer under 250 words.
