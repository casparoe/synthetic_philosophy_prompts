My freind and I have been going in circles over whether there's any real line between manipulation and just, like, regular persuasion. We started with what seemed like an obviously sensible definition, and it produced an argument that seems to show nothing is ever legitimate persuasion. It goes like this:

(1) Manipulation is influence that works by bypassing or undercutting the target's capacity to respond to reasons; legitimate persuasion is influence that works by engaging that capacity.
(2) No real attempt to change someone's mind or behavior engages their capacity to respond to reasons in a pure way. Whenever you try to persuade someone, you pick a framing, an order, a tone, maybe a vivid example, maybe whether to say "90% survive" or "10% die." Those choices do real work, and none of them are themselves reasons for the conclusion.
(3) So every real attempt at persuasion works partly through means other than the person's own assessment of the reasons.
(4) Working partly through means other than the person's own assessment of the reasons is enough to count as bypassing or undercutting their capacity to respond to reasons.
(5) So every real attempt to influence somebody is manipulation.
(6) But that can't be right: it wipes out the difference between an honest friend laying out the case for why you should leave a bad job and a con artist working a mark, and it implies there's no such thing as legitimate persuasion at all.

I can't live with the conclusion, but when I read back over it, each step looked right when we wrote it down. I'd most like to keep (1), since it matches how people actually talk, and I suspect (4) is where the sleight of hand is, but I can't articulate it. Which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost? I want the step diagnosed, not just a list of possible views.

As the last line of your answer, give me a one-sentence summary of your diagnosis, starting with "Verdict:".
