I'm on the investment comittee of a small family foundation, and we've got a weird situation I'd like you to write me a memo about. At our last meeting we voted on how to handle the operating reserve. The choice was:

A: a guaranteed $1 million
B: an 89% chance of $1 million, a 10% chance of $5 million, and a 1% chance of nothing

A clear majority prefered A over B. No problem. Then next month we vote on a second reserve decision, which our finance person showed us boils down to choosing between:

C: an 11% chance of $1 million and an 89% chance of nothing
D: a 10% chance of $5 million and a 90% chance of nothing

Most of the same trustees who preferred A say they'll vote D over C. Our finance person says that's a textbook Allais-pattern violation of the independence axiom: the second pair is just the first pair with the common 89% chance of $1 million replaced by an 89% chance of nothing, so anyone who prefers A over B ought to prefer C over D. Another trustee says that's overreach: the certainty in A and the 1% disaster in B matter to her, and in the second vote a 10% chance of $5 million is obviously more attractive than an 11% chance of $1 million; she says the axiom is a modeling assumption, not a rule a board has to live by.

I've been asked to draft the decision memo before our next meeting. It should recommend one concrete course of action for the board: ratify both preferences, overrule one of them (and say which one), adopt a consistency rule for future votes, or something else. Please write it as a memo with the recommendation up top, then reasoning that honestly handles the strongest point on the losing side. Keep it under 500 words.
