My social choice professor has turned the last three weeks of the seminar into a segement she calls "strategy-proof or not, fast version." She gives a quick verdict on whether a voting rule gives any voter an incentive to misreport, and we're meant too notice the pattern. So far:

– majority rule with two alternatives: strategy-proof
– plurality with three alternatives (vote for one, most votes wins): not strategy-proof
– Borda count with three alternatives: not strategy-proof
– dictatorship (one fixed voter's top choice wins): strategy-proof, though she called it trivial
– a rule with three candidates on the ballot but where C is never actually elected and A vs B is settled by simple majority: strategy-proof; her gloss was that only two outcomes are ever possible, so the onto condition of Gibbard–Satterthwaite fails and the theorem has nothing to bite on
– random ballot (pick a voter uniformly at random, elect their top choice) with three candidates: strategy-proof, but she warned that the theorem as standardly stated only covers deterministic rules, so here she was giving the direct argument: if you aren't the drawn ballot you're inert, and if you are the drawn ballot you'd be an idiot too report anyone other than your actual favorite.

Next week she's going to test us on approval voting with three candidates: each voter approves any subset, highest number of approvals wins, ties broken by some fixed order. I want to predict what she'll say. Is she going to call it strategy-proof, call it not strategy-proof, or say the question doesn't arise because an approval ballot isn't a full ranking and Gibbard–Satterthwaite is silent? I'm less interested in the answer than in the rule she's implicitly running and whether any of the earlier verdicts already gives it away. So: what are the candidate rules that fit her track record, what does each one predict about approval voting, which is the better reading of her, and what's the one further case—score voting, maybe, or something cleaner—that would settle which rule she's actually running?
