I am writing a paper for my high school's philosophy club on everyday conventions, and I want to check that my stated views are consistent before I present them. Here is where I have landed. (1) A rule or custom binds me only when its underlying point actually applies; when I travel, this means a local convention binds me only when it serves an independent value such as safety, fairness, or avoiding harm to actual people, not merely because locals expect it. (2) Jaywalking on an empty street is morally fine, even where it is illegal, because the point of the law is safety and there is no safety risk. (3) Cutting in line is wrong even when it causes no material delay—for instance, when everyone still catches the same bus and finds a seat. (4) I am not obligated to tip at restaurants where I live, because servers are paid the ordinary minimum wage and tips are only a bonus. (5) In a country where servers are paid a lower base wage and tips are expected to make up their pay, I am obligated to tip; but I do not think I owe anything at a self-service counter in that same country, even if the staff there are paid just as little. Are these views mutually consistent? Please audit them: identify which pairs are in real tension and which only seem to be, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each real tension state the least costly revision and what it would give up.
