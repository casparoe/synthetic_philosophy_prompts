I have come to hold a view about drug policy that I would like to see stress-tested rather than defended. The view is that the strongest available model is neither prohibition nor commercial legalization, but the decriminalization of personal use and possession of all currently illicit drugs, paired with administrative referral—through dissuasion panels rather than criminal courts—into health and social services. I take the Portuguese reform of 2001 as the central natural experiment for this claim, precisely because it decriminalized all drugs at once rather than legalizing a single substance, and because it kept trafficking criminal while moving people who use drugs out of the penal system. I also take the position to imply that the standard favorable reading of Portuguese outcomes—on drug-related deaths, HIV transmission among people who inject drugs, incarceration for use or possession, and problematic use in the population—reflects the policy itself rather than unrelated trends, and that the model should generalize to states with different institutions, cultures, and treatment infrastructures.

I want a ranked map of what would count against this position. Which observations, mechanisms, cases, and arguments should move a thoughtful defender, from fatal to merely awkward? In particular: what Portuguese data, disaggregated how, would be most damaging? Which confounds—pre-existing trend lines, simultaneous expansion of treatment, changes in recording practices, European integration, migration, or details of how the 2001 law was implemented—would a defender have to concede as live alternative explanations, and which could they reasonably dismiss? What do the outcomes of cannabis legalization in jurisdictions that chose a commercial market show about the distinct claim that all-drug decriminalization is preferable to legalization? Under what country-level conditions would the same policy be expected to fail, and would such failures refute the model or only bound its applicability? Please distinguish defeaters that a committed defender would themselves accept as counting against the view from those they would dismiss as irrelevant or as merely troubling for a rival position. Finally, say whether the position as I have stated it has been insulated from falsification by hedging, and if so, whether that insulation is a serious problem and how a critic could still land a decisive blow.
