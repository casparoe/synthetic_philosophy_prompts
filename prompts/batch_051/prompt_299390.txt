Walzer defends this by saying that soldiers are not responsible for the justice of the war itself; they are only responsible for how they fight. McMahan rejects this: since killing is permissible only if one fights for a just cause, a soldier in an unjust army cannot be permitted to kill at all, even if he targets uniformed enemy soldiers and follows the laws of war. So the two sides have completely opposite moral statuses, and the only reason we don't punish ordinary soldiers in unjust armies is pragmatic rather than principled.'
