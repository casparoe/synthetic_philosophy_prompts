I've spent the last while converging on an interventionist picture of causation, and I want an audit of the package I've ended up with before I lean on it any harder. Here are the commitments as they stand. (1) X causes Y just in case Y, or its probability distribution, would change under some possible intervention on X with respect to Y; the intervention need not be humanly performable, and I don't see why actual feasibility should matter. This is why I deny that the barometer reading causes the storm: setting the reading directly would not change the weather. (2) The relevant interventions must be surgical: an intervention on X with respect to Y must not influence Y along any route that does not pass through X. I accept that "surgical" is itself a causal notion, so this is not a reductive definition of causation. (3) I have made peace with that circularity: interventionism, as I understand it, is not trying to reduce causation to non-causal terms but to illuminate how causal thinking is tied to explanation, prediction, and control. (4) But the dependence cannot be a one-off: for X's causing Y, the X–Y relation must be invariant under some range of interventions on X, not merely under a single manipulation at one value or in one fixed background. (5) I also think interventionism captures the direction of causation: the cause is a handle for the effect, while the effect is not a handle for the cause, and this asymmetry is real and central. (6) Yet I am equally committed to saying that systems with genuine feedback — a thermostat, predator–prey oscillations, a microphone too close to a speaker — exhibit real mutual causation, with no fact of the matter about which variable is "really" the cause. (7) Finally, I take causal facts to be mind-independent: whether a drug causes recovery does not depend on what we can actually bring ourselves to manipulate.

I can tell the set is not entirely stable, but I can't sort out which fault lines are load-bearing and which are just the familiar circularity worry resurfacing. Work through the package: which commitments are in genuine tension with which others, which only appear to be, how the tensions should be ranked by severity, and what is the cheapest revision that restores consistency — and what would that revision cost me?
