I'm a novelist, three chapters into a political thriller where the continuity-of-government plan actually gets triggered. The attack happens during the State of the Union, and the designated survivor—the Secretary of Agriculture in my story, who was picked mostly because everyone treated the job as a ceremonial joke—comes out of a bunker to find himself the only person in the immediate line of succession who is still alive and in his right mind. The set-piece I'm stuck on is a meeting of a small "continuity council" that I've invented: a Justice Department lawyer, a Senate security staffer, and a retired federal judge. They need to decide, in writing, the rules under which the survivor may govern for the next month. Half of them think he should be sworn in and exercise full presidential powers right away, because the state needs a functioning executive and the emergency won't wait for a neat democratic fix. The other half thinks that's a scandal waiting to happen: this man was never elected, never confirmed for the presidency, and was absent at the moment of the attack for reasons no one outside a small circle got to weigh in on.

Can you write the actual decision memo that committee issues? I want something the characters could read aloud and fight over. It should have a real recomendation—full powers, constrained caretaker powers, or something in between—the reasoning behind it, and a serious treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side, not a straw man but the kind of thing a smart person would insist on at 3 a.m. Make the political philosophy do the work rather than legal citations: I need the argument about democratic legitimacy, emergency effectiveness, and whether an unelected survivor may act as a temporary but real president or only as a kind of trustee for whoever comes next. End with a concrete bottom line: what the survivor can decide alone, what requires the council's sign-off, and when, if ever, the restrictions lift.
