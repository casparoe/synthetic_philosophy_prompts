(b) Do the two views make the same knowledge verdicts in third-person cases where the ascriber's stakes differ from the subject's? Explain briefly. Student's answer:
"(a) Contextualism says the content of 'knows' varies with the ascriber's context: the ascription expresses a stricter or laxer knowledge relation depending on the standards in force for the speaker. Pragmatic encroachment accepts the same context-sensitivity but says the context-shifting factor is the subject's practical stakes, which raise or lower the evidence the subject needs. On this view, in the high-stakes bank case the subject genuinely fails to know while the low-stakes subject genuinely knows. (b) The two views make the same predictions in third-person cases, because in each case it is the practical stakes of the ascriber that set the operative standard. Thus a high-stakes ascriber's ascription 'S knows' of a low-stakes subject is false on both views, and a low-stakes ascriber's ascription 'S knows' of a high-stakes subject is true on both views.
