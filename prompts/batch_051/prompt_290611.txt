I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I sit on my company's advanced projects ethics review board. We build autonomous life-support and habitat-control software for long-duration missions, and a consortium has asked us to be the sole software contractor for the first permanent, one-way civilian settlement on Mars. Under the proposal, founding residents would commit to a minimum stay of twenty years, evacuation would be limited and controlled by the operating company, and the settlement would be governed under the consortium's charter rather than by an independent civil authority for the first phase. The consortium argues that a permanent off-world settlement is an ethical imperative because it hedges against existential risk on Earth; several board members reply that we would be helping build a company town from which residents cannot realistically exit, in an environment made survivable largely by the systems we build. I've agreed to draft the short written opinion to circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend one of three actions — accept, decline, or accept conditionally — make any condition and time frame concrete, and engage the consortium's strongest argument seriously rather than dismissing it. Keep it under 750 words.
