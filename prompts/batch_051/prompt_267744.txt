I have been working through the old Epicurean challenge to the idea that death can harm the person who dies, and the puzzle below is what I keep getting stuck on. Most people regard a premature death as bad for the person who dies even when it is painless and unnoticed: someone who dies in their sleep at thirty is said to have been robbed of decades of life. Yet almost no one regards it as bad for a person that they were not born thirty years earlier, even though that would also have meant thirty more years of life. Both cases involve nonexistence relative to an alternative in which the person has more life, but the first is treated as a loss and the second is not. The usual explanation is that once a person exists, future goods are that person's in a way that merely possible past goods were not; death deprives an actual subject of something, while prenatal nonexistence deprives no actual subject of anything. But that explanation is strained by a third case. Consider a drug that leaves a person permanently unconscious while the body remains alive. Most people regard being put into such a state as a terrible harm to the person, even though there will never again be a conscious subject to notice the loss; from the inside, the condition seems indistinguishable from dreamless nonexistence. So the ordinary pattern of judgment seems to include at least these three claims: posthumous nonexistence can be bad for you, prenatal nonexistence cannot, and permanent unconsciousness while biologically alive can be bad for you.

I would like the strongest principle you can construct that yields all three claims without quietly assuming one of them, plus an honest verdict on whether that principle really works. If no single principle can do the job, say which judgment should be revised or abandoned and why. In your answer, take seriously the options that the harm lies in the deprivation of future goods, in the thwarting of actual desires or plans, in the interests of the continuing biological organism, or in something else entirely. Please write your answer in American English.
