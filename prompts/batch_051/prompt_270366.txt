i sit on the disciplinary committee of a community health nonprofit, and we're stuck on a whistleblowing case. A data analyst, Mara, gave a local reporter a batch of internal spreadsheets showing that our 'patient outcomes' dashboard had been quietly redefined so that deaths within 30 days of discharge were coded as 'completed treatment,' right before a big grant renewal. She'd raised it twice through the internal reporting line, got told it was 'being looked into,' and then watched the grant go in with the gamed numbers anyway. She signed a confidentiality agreement and broke it. The leak didn't expose any patient info, but it did name two program managers, and now a couple of our community partners are saying they can't trust our data for their own planning. Half the committee wants to fire her for disloyalty; the other half thinks shes the only person who actually honored the mission. I need to circulate a short decision memo before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete outcome — terminate, retain, or retain with conditions — and it should take the strongest argument on the other side seriously rather than just reciting the confidentiality policy. One wrinkle: our employee handbook has a 'protected disclosure' clause, but it only covers reporting to the board or a regulator, not to the press, so part of the fight is whther going to a journalist was justified disloyalty or just disloyalty.
