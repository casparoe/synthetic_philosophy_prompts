The standard Axelrod story about the evolution of cooperation is that in the iterated prisoner's dilemma, cooperation can get going without communication, promises, or enforcement because the same two players will meet again and can retaliate. Suppose now interactions happen on a platform where everyones got a public history and you can choose who to play with, so you almost never meet the same person twice. Does the reciprocity argument still do the explanatory work, or does the burden shift to reputation and partner choice, or does it just break? I'm less interested in which strategy wins than in which premise takes the hit.
