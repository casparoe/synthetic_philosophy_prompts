I'm writing a paper on what two-dimensional semantics actually buys you beyond a blunt direct-reference picture, and I've found that the standard illustrations — water is H2O, Hesperus is Phosphorus, Twin Earth — always smuggle in extra baggage that lets people talk past one another. I need a case built to order that isolates the two dimensions cleanly. By primary intension I mean a function from centered worlds (a possible world plus a marked subject, time, and location) to extensions, evaluated by treating that centered world as actual; by secondary intension I mean a function from counterfactual worlds to extensions, with the actual-world referent held fixed. Construct a one-paragraph, ordinary-life scenario — no science fiction, no Twin Earth — involving two speakers who use the same common noun for an ordinary kind, not a proper name and not water, gold, or heat. The two uses should agree in their actual-world extension and in their secondary intension, but come apart in primary intension. The case must generate this divergence from the two speakers' different routes of reference-fixing, rather than stipulate it.

Then demonstrate the divergence with a target sentence — containing no indexicals and no actuality operator — that both speakers sincerely accept but for which an epistemic two-dimensionalist should say that one speaker can know it a priori while the other cannot. Do the check: specify the centered world where the two primary intensions diverge, state for each speaker what the primary and secondary intensions assign to the term, and explain why the epistemic two-dimensionalist counts this as a semantic difference in the token's content whereas a direct-reference theorist must class it as a mere difference in the speakers' background beliefs. Finish by identifying the single feature of the case that is doing the work, saying what would happen if it were removed, and saying how the direct-reference theorist would attack the case.
