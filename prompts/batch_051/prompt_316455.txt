I’m taking a seminar on nineteenth-century aesthetics and I keep going in circles with a friend about Schopenhauer’s theory of music. The way I read him, he treats the representational arts—painting, poetry, sculpture—as presenting the Platonic Ideas, which are the fixed grades of the will’s objectification, whereas music somehow skips the Ideas and gives us the will itself directly. It does not depict striving or longing; it is, in some weird sense, striving itself made audible. That sounds wild, but I can see why it appealed to Schopenhauer, given his whole system.

My friend has a deflationary reading. He says Schopenhauer’s real insight is just a formal one: music, unlike a novel or a painting, doesn’t represent any determinate object or state of affairs. What it supplies is a sequence of abstract shapes—tension, prolongation, interruption, resolution—that resembles the temporal shape of willing. A melody doesn’t name a desire, but it enacts the pattern of a desire. On this reading, all the metaphysics about music as the immediate copy of the will reduces to a claim about structural resemblance between musical syntax and the form of mental life. The claim about the will itself is then just grand theater.

I don’t think that can be right, because Schopenhauer seems to want more than resemblance: he wants music to have a different metaphysical status from the other arts, not just a different degree of referential determinacy. But honestly I’m struggling to say what the extra claim amounts to. Compare his view with my friend’s structuralist paraphrase. Is there a genuine difference between saying music directly objectifies the will and saying music mirrors the abstract form of willing? If so, what could settle which position Schopenhauer is actually entitled to? If not, is the metaphysics doing any work at all, or is it just a fancy redescription of a formal point about musical syntax?
