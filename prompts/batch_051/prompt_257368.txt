I run data governance at a company that makes a habit-tracking app with about two million users, and lately I've been reading arguments that personal data should be treated as property — that information about you belongs to you, not to whoever happened to collect it. I want to turn that from a slogan into a reusable decision procedure for my team. A few times a week a product or marketing person comes to me with a proposed use of user data and I have to say yes or no; I've got maybe half an hour per decision, there's no lawyer on call for each one, nobody else on the team has a philosophy background, and I'm the one who signs off, so the procedure has to produce a short justification I can stick in a doc. What should the checklist actually be? Hard cases I already know we'll hit: aggregated and de-identified data (we want to sell trend reports about when people skip habits), data about interactions between two users (a shared challenge thread or a message — whose property is that?), and inferences we generate rather than data users gave us (we've built a churn-risk model off their behavior). If the procedure says the churn model is off-limits under the property view, fine, i'd rather know now. But I also want the procedure to flag the steps where the property metaphor itself starts to break down, and to be honest about which steps are really judgment calls wearing a checklist costume.
