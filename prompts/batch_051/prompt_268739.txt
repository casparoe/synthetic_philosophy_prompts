I've been having a running argument with a philosopher of biology I know, and over the past few months I've kept a list of how they rule on candidate biological individuals. So far they have committed, with reasons, to the following five verdicts. A Portuguese man o' war is an organism: its zooids are specialized for feeding, defense, and reproduction, and they develop from a single zygote. A honeybee colony is an organism: the workers are sterile, there is a division of labor, and the colony reproduces as a unit by swarming. A lichen is an organism: the fungal and algal partners form a metabolically integrated, stable whole that also disperses jointly. A quaking aspen grove is not an organism, even though it is a connected, genetically uniform clone, because the component trees are largely physiologically self-sufficient and there is no shared reproductive axis. A human being together with their gut microbiome is not an organism: the microbes are a community of symbionts rather than specialized parts of one system, and the integration is too loose and too optional.

The case I plan to raise next is a reef-building coral colony considered jointly with the photosynthetic dinoflagellates living inside its polyps' cells, in a species whose larvae acquire their algae from the surrounding water each generation rather than inheriting them from the parent, so the partnership has to be reassembled at the start of each life. The algae fix carbon and pass much of it to the coral, the coral supplies nitrogen and shelter, and metabolically the two are tightly coupled.

I am less interested in the yes-or-no answer than in the rule my colleague seems to be running. What are the candidate rules that also fit all five of their recorded verdicts, which of those rules classify the coral-plus-algae system as an organism and which refuse it, and what would each rule imply about the closest analog among the earlier cases and where that analogy breaks down? Give me your best prediction of their verdict with the reasons for preferring one rule over the others, and name the further test case — ideally one with the opposite profile, such as shared reproductive fate without much physiological integration, or vice versa — that would do the most to settle which rule they are actually using.
