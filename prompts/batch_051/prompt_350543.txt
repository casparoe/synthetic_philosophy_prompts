I teach an upper-level aesthetics seminar on fakes, forgeries, and the value of originals, and I'm writing the final take-home exam. I need one essay question that forces students to work through a real puzzle rather than just recite the usual "history of production matters" line. The puzzle I want them to sit with: suppose two objects are molecule-for-molecule identical, except one is the original and the other a deliberately made copy. Most students will say that before the deception is detected the copy has the same aesthetic value as the original, but after detection it loses aesthetic value. But the copy's observable properties haven't changed; only its categorization has. So is aesthetic value in the object itself, or in our total epistemic relation to the object, or split across the two somehow? Please write the question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme with 20 total points spread across five must-hit moves, with one or two common student mistakes and which one should cost the most marks. The question should be answerable in 90 minutes by someone who has read the core stuff but hasn't memorized jargon.
