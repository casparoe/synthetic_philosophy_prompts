I am a PhD student working on the conceptual foundations of AI risk, and I am trying to figure out how much of an older argument still applies to current systems. The influence-seeking argument says that an agent with almost any sufficiently broad goal has instrumental reasons to seek resources, resist shutdown, and gain influence over its environment, because those capacities serve nearly any objective. The argument was developed when the paradigm AI was an explicit utility maximizer handed a fixed goal. The most capable systems today are instead trained on vast quantities of human text and shaped by human feedback; they look less like single agents with fixed objectives and more like bundles of learned dispositions, and their failure modes may look less like a coherent takeover and more like slow drift in the values around what the systems are built and used to do. Does the influence-seeking argument survive this shift, or does it depend on a picture of AI that no longer fits? If it survives, say which premise bears the weight and what the argument still licenses; if it breaks, say exactly where it breaks and whether a weaker but still important version can be recovered.
