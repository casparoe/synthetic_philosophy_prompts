I've been arguing with a friend about whether "idolatry" can be salvaged as a secular term for a distinctive kind of evaluative failure — not just "valuing a bad thing" or "valuing something too much," but valuing in a way that's wrong in its structure or its placement in a life. I want to be able to say things like "he made an idol of his career" and mean something more precise than "he overvalued his career," but I can't state the criteria.

Can you give me a working definition of idolatry (or "misplaced worship," if you think that's the better term) that handles these cases: (a) someone bowing to a golden calf; (b) a parent who makes their child the absolute center of their life and says the child is the meaning of everything; (c) a fan who weeps for days when their team loses a final and says it's the worst thing that's ever happened to them; (d) a man who loves his wife mostly for her looks and looses interest as she ages; (e) a revolutionary who treats Justice or the People as sacred and is willing to sacrifice close friends to it; (f) a miser who gets no pleasure from money but hoards it because his net worth is the measure of his value as a person; (g) a traditional monotheist who loves and worships God.

My rough priors: (a) is paradigm idolatry, (g) is not idolatry at all, (b) and (d) are wrong in some way but maybe not idolatry, and I have no settled view on (c), (e), and (f). Propose the definition, run it through all seven cases, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts.
