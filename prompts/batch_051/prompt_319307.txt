You are a philosopher of biology known for mapping out exactly where a position is exposed. I have been working through the species problem, and I have tentatively accepted the thesis that species taxa are individuals rather than natural kinds or classes. On this view, a species such as Homo sapiens is not a category to which organisms belong in virtue of shared essential properties; it is a particular, spatiotemporally bounded historical entity whose organisms are parts, and it originates, changes, and goes extinct in the way particulars do. Some version of the Ghiselin–Hull line seems to me the most coherent way to explain why a species can evolve while remaining the same species, and why there need be no single diagnostic list of properties an organism must have in order to belong to a species.

What I want is a careful account of what would count against this position. I do not mean only the standard rejoinder that species are classes after all; I want a genuine map of the position's exposure. Which empirical findings about hybridization, horizontal gene transfer, asexual lineages, population structure, or fossil taxa would be hardest for a species-as-individuals theorist to absorb? Which conceptual results or possible cases would push the view toward collapse, or toward being merely a terminological variant of pluralism or conventionalism? Which concessions would a committed defender themselves accept as counting against the view, and which would they dismiss as belonging to another question? Please be explicit about what would be decisive, what would be merely awkward, and why. I am not interested in a bibliography or a landscape survey; I want your best substantive reasoning. Begin by brainstorming the distinct load-bearing commitments of the view, then rank the potential defeaters from fatal to superficial. Finally, give me an honest assessment of whether the thesis has been qualified so thoroughly that it risks becoming insulated from negative evidence, and whether, if so, that is a serious problem for a view of this kind.
