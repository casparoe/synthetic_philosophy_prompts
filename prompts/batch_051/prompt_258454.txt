I have been working as a research assistant for a professor of ritual studies who is coding a corpus of rites with a single judgment in each case: substitution or not substitution. She has now left for a month of fieldwork and has left me one uncoded case, with the understanding that I will code it consistently with her earlier decisions. Her decisions so far are as follows. The biblical scapegoat was substitution: the community's sins were confessed over a goat, and the goat was driven into the wilderness. The old Greek pharmakos rite was substitution: a marginal man was beaten and driven out of the city so that the city's pollution would leave with him. A royal healing rite was substitution: the king's impurity was recited over a ram decked in the king's own ornaments, and the ram was then led away into the open country. A folk healing charm was substitution: a patient's fever was coaxed into a small dough image, and the image was broken up and burned. A harvest feast was not substitution: a pig was killed and offered to the gods in thanks, with portions eaten by the worshippers. And the execution of a convicted murderer was not substitution, even though the court described the execution as something that would purify the town.

The uncoded case is a firstborn-redemption rite. In this society the firstborn child and the firstborn of certain domestic animals are said to belong to the deity, not because they are guilty or impure, but simply because they are the first issue. A household buys a firstborn child back with a payment of silver, and buys a firstborn donkey back by handing over a lamb in its place. The silver and the lamb are not described as guilty, impure, or loaded with anyone's sins; they are simply the substitutes the deity accepts in place of the firstborn.

Please tell me how she would most likely code this case and why. In particular, lay out the rule she appears to be running, plus any second or third rule that would fit her six earlier decisions equally well; say what each candidate rule predicts for the firstborn case; then give your best guess with your reasons for preferring it over the alternatives; and finally specify a further case I could send her, when she next checks her email, that would settle which rule she is really using. I want your answer in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold type, or tables, written the way one would write an email or a letter, and in American English.
