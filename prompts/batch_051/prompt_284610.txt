I work as a software engineer and have been reading political philosophy informally. My understanding of Nozick's entitlement theory is this: a holding is just if and only if (a) it was originally acquired justly, (b) it reached its current holder through a chain of just transfers, and (c) any past injustices in that chain have been rectified, with no further pattern or end-state test applied. On that view, the Wilt Chamberlain example is just because every fan voluntarily paid to watch him, even though the resulting distribution is highly unequal. Now consider a case I cannot decide about. A developer runs a bot on a decentralized exchange that watches the pending transaction pool, sees a large buy order, submits its own identical buy first, and then sells the same asset to the original buyer at a slightly higher price. The bot's starting capital came from the developer's savings and a research grant; every matched trade was accepted voluntarily under the exchange's published rules; and the developer ends with a large profit while the original buyer gets a worse price. Is the developer's resulting holding just under the three criteria above? Work criterion by criterion, say where the criteria underdetermine the answer, and state whether the case shows that a fourth criterion about exploitation, deception, or asymmetric information is needed.
