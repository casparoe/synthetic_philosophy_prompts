Hey, I'm a software engineer and I never took a philosophy course, so bear with me, but I got roped into my company's supplier standards working group and I need to draft a decision memo before our vote on Tuesday. Heres the deal:

We're a mid-sized consumer electronics company and we contract most of our assembly to a factory in Vietnam. Our latest audit came back mixed. The good: they pay roughly 20% above the local minimum wage, and most workers told auditors they took the job because it was the best option they had. When we interviewed workers directly (through a translator), alot of them said they want us to stay, because if we leave they expect to end up in construction or domestic work or back in a village with no cash income.

The bad: 65-70 hour weeks are typical, which violates our own code's 60-hour cap. The overtime is technically voluntary, but base pay is low enough that people depend on it to make rent. Workers live in factory dorms with a curfew and no visitors; nobody's locked in, but it feels controlling. The auditors said nothing rose to the legal definition of forced labor, but it still seems pretty grim to me.

The group is stuck. Half of them think we're just profiting from people's bad options and that staying makes us complicit in exploitation, even though the pay beats the alternatives. The other half thinks cutting the contract is moral grandstanding that would actually hurt the workers we claim to care about — those workers chose this job over worse ones, and they're telling us not to leave. That's the part I keep going in circles on: if both sides are better off, is it still exploitation?

I need a draft of the memo the group will vote on. It should recommend one concrete action — terminate the contract, renew as-is, or renew with conditions — and walk through the reasoning in a way I can actually follow as an engineer. It should also deal honestly with the strongest argument on the losing side, not a strawman. And the bottom line needs to be specific enough that we can actually vote on it (what conditions, what timeline, whatever applies).
