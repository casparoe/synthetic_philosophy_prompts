Write the closing joint statement of a good-faith adversarial collaboration between a defender of karma, reconstructed as a theory of moral desert, and a skeptic who holds that karmic law can at most be a causal account of how actions shape future experience, not an account of what agents deserve. Open with a numbered list of claims both can sign: the structural requirements on a theory of moral desert (a desert base, a subject, proportionality, fittingness) and the understanding of karma at issue, namely a philosophical reconstruction rather than the popular just-world rendering. Then state their disagreements exactly, in formulations each side would accept for its own view: whether karma as a lawful mechanism can make a person deserve an outcome, or whether desert requires an agent or a fittingness relation that bare causation lacks; whether the absence of memory across lives severs the link between a past action and a present desert claim; and whether proportionality is well defined across an open-ended series of lives. Next identify the cruxes and, for each, a concrete case or consideration both sides agree bears on it — a memory-wipe case in which someone is punished tomorrow for a wrong they can no longer remember, for instance — and record each side's prediction in advance. Close with a section in which each side states which argument or finding would most change its mind and where it regards its own position as least secure.
