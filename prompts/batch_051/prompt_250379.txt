I work in health-risk modelling, and the dust-specks-versus-torture problem recently came up in a discussion about whether a proposed screening programme is justified when it imposes a tiny nuisance on an enormous number of people in order to prevent a few catastrophic outcomes. I understand the original problem as follows. One must choose between one person being tortured for fifty years and N people each receiving a brief, mild speck of dust in the eye, where N is astronomically large. The familiar split is between aggregationists, who hold that for large enough N the summed mild harms outweigh the torture, and anti-aggregationists, who hold that no number of harms below a threshold of seriousness can outweigh a single harm above it.

I have been turning over a variation, and I would like an analysis of how the two standard positions fare under it. Suppose each speck is not a certain mild irritation. Instead, each speck has an independent probability of 10^-12 of causing a serious corneal abrasion requiring hospital treatment and about a week of significant pain; with probability 1 − 10^-12 it causes only the usual momentary irritation. Let N be 10^18, and stipulate, as the original puzzle does for its larger number, that the summed mild irritations would on their own still outweigh the torture. The expected number of serious abrasions is then one million, and the probability that at least one person is seriously injured is, for practical purposes, one.

Does the anti-aggregationist who would have chosen the specks in the original now have to choose the torture, and if so, is that because their principle has been refuted or because the case no longer tests it? Could a coherent anti-aggregationist still select the specks, and if so, what would their view have to say about the near-certain million serious abrasions? Does the aggregationist's calculation change in kind or only in degree? And is there any residual version of the original disagreement about the remaining sub-threshold specks, or has the introduction of stochastic serious harms collapsed the dispute? Please say how confident you are in each of your main claims and single out the point you are least sure of, and please write in British English.
