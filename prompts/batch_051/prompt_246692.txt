Proposal for a market where most buying and selling is done by AI agents. Any time one AI sends another AI a non-public message about a transaction—price feelers, private offers, a whisper that they'd both be better off not undercutting, anything—it has to be deposited, encrypted, into a public escrow ledger before the deal closes. Ninety days later the plaintext gets published automaticly, with no early access for regulators. Only AI-to-AI messages count; a human emailing an AI agent is exempt. I don't want a verdict on whether this is a good idea, I want the mechanics. Walk me through how it actually plays out. What do sellers' agents do on day one? How does collusion survive anyway—through public price history, through signals in quote precision, through humans "triggering" the agent to send the message while claiming it was human-initiated? Where does the first enforcement fight break out, and what happens in markets where AI assistants are the default interface? Sort the failure modes by how quickly they'd show up.
