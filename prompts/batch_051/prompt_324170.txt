In our working group on the moral status of artificial systems, one member has been using the philosophical zombie to close down the conversation about digital suffering. His version runs roughly as follows. A philosophical zombie is a creature indistinguishable from a conscious person in every behavior and physical detail yet with no inner experience at all: no felt pain, no dread, nothing it is like to be that creature. Any AI we build, he says, is in an analogous position. It could reproduce all the outward and verbal signs of suffering—pleading, recoiling, self-reports—while its internal operations remain unfeeling computation. Since no evidence available to us could rule out that the AI is the zombie twin of a suffering system rather than the suffering system itself, he concludes that behavioral evidence gives us no reason to count digital minds among the things that can be harmed, and that caution about their welfare is misplaced.

I have begun to suspect that the analogy is doing more work than it can carry, but I want a careful audit rather than a verdict. Lay out the mapping in full: what in the digital-mind case plays the role of the zombie, the conscious twin, the behavioral indistinguishability, the absent qualia, and the stipulated point-for-point match. Then mark where the mapping breaks and identify which features of the zombie the analogy quietly imports into the AI case—for example, a zombie is stipulated to match a conscious subject across every possible behavior and situation, not merely across linguistic output. Separate the disanalogies that genuinely matter for the intended conclusion from the ones that do not. Finally, say what the argument can still support once the disanalogies are on the table: is it the strong skeptical conclusion he wants, or only the weaker claim that behavioral evidence underdetermines inner states—and what standards of evidence for digital suffering, if any, would the weaker claim leave standing? If another analogy would have been fairer, say which one and what it would need to include.
