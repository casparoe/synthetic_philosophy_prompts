I've been trying to sort out what I mean when I call some ideas "antimemes": ideas that seem to resist being remembered or retransmitted, rather than merely failing to spread because no one has much reason to repeat them. The distinction matters to me because I want to claim that some ideas fail to propagate due to something about their content, not just their circumstances. But when I go through cases one by one, the pattern keeps buckling.

My verdicts are these. A randomly generated password I see once and cannot reproduce an hour later: I do not count it as an antimeme; it is unmemorable noise, not an idea that resists. A theorem whose statement involves seven nested quantifiers, which students can apply but cannot restate a week later and never try to teach except by pointing at a page: that seems like a paradigm antimeme, because its own form works against its transmission. The school-biology slogan that mitochondria are the cell's powerhouses: plainly not an antimeme. A workplace rule so obvious to old-timers that no one ever says it aloud to new hires until someone gets hurt: I find myself wanting to call this an antimeme, but I cannot see what it shares with the theorem. An in-group joke that needs twenty minutes of subcultural setup: inside the group it is constantly retold; outside, attempts to tell it die on contact. I want to say the joke is not an antimeme in itself, because the very same sentence can be healthy in one setting and an antimeme in another, but then I am not sure "antimeme" is a property of the idea at all. A self-sealing conspiracy theory: not an antimeme; it spreads far too well. And an easily stated true fact about my own past that I never mention because sharing it would be socially costly: also not an antimeme; it is suppressed, not resistant.

Is there a principle that captures all of these, or close? I would like the principle stated precisely, with the case motivating each clause, its verdicts on cases I have not considered, and an honest statement of which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate. In particular, tell me whether the pair I cannot reconcile, the nested-quantifier theorem and the too-obvious safety rule, can be brought under one account without making "antimeme" vacuous, and what that account says about a further case I keep going back and forth on: a widely cited scientific result that keeps circulating even after it is retracted, while the correction, which is hard to state without redoing the analysis, is regularly forgotten. Is the correction an antimeme? Does the original's stubborn survival count as one, even though it spreads too easily?
