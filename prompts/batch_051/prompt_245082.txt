ok so we did the ship of theseus in class last term, the one where you replace the planks one by one and then ask if it's still the same ship at the end. i've been doing latin for ages and my teacher keeps saying italian sort of grew out of latin, but there was never a day when everyone went to bed speaking latin and woke up speaking italian. so i was wondering if you could run the ship of theseus thought experiment on a language instead of a ship.

here's the version i'm imagining. there's a village where everyone speaks latin. every generation a handful of words change, maybe an ending drops off, the word order shifts a tiny bit. nobody ever notices because each generation just talks like their parents, more or less. but suppose it's slow enough that each generation can easily understand their grandparents, while nobody can understand someone seven generations earlier. so between any two neighbouring generations there's no sharp break, but across the whole chain there definately is. is it the same language at generation 200? and if not, when did it stop being the same language?

now the twist i actually care about. suppose the village also has a written standard, like a grammar book and a set of old texts, that stays totally fixed the whole time. people keep copying it and saying the old forms are the "correct" ones even though no one actually talks like that anymore. does having that fixed written standard change your answer? like, is the written language a seperate ship whose planks never get replaced, or is it more like a blueprint that the spoken language slowly stops matching? and do the usual ship of theseus answers, the continuity one or the form-and-function one, still work when the "planks" are words and grammar and the "ship" is a whole community talking?
