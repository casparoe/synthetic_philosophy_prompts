The question of which animals are conscious has the frustrating structure of a debate in which all parties share the evidence and none of them move. A comparative neuroanatomist will point to the absence of a layered cortex and conclude that fish probably lack phenomenal consciousness; a researcher working on fish nociception will point to the same animals and produce behavioral, pharmacological, and physiological evidence that their pain states are functionally similar to those in mammals; a theorist inclined toward global-workspace or higher-order views will draw the line quite differently from an integrated-information theorist, who may place it wherever causal structure passes a threshold, possibly including insects; and a precautionary wing says the honest answer is that we cannot currently assign a distribution at all, so welfare policy should hedge. All of these people have access to the same comparative neurobiology, the same lesion studies, and the same ethological observations. So the impasse is not obviously a data problem.

My working hypothesis is that the debate has stalled because the phrase "animal consciousness" bundles at least three questions: a phenomenological question about what it is like for the animal, a functional question about what its nervous system does, and a normative question about how much we ought to protect it — and the parties are in effect assigning different answers to these questions and then presenting the bundled result as a single factual distribution. Is that right? If so, what kind of disagreement is this exactly: a verbal one, a methodological one about which sources of evidence count, or a clash of unargued starting points — for instance, about whether consciousness must be all-or-nothing or graded? And on your diagnosis, what would honestly count as progress: a better comparative dataset, a formal or conceptual result about thresholds and gradation, an agreed set of evidential criteria, or something else? Or is this a case where the correct diagnosis is that no progress should be expected because the question as posed is ill-posed — and if so, what is the better-posed question?
