I'm in a philosophy of math class and we did the Benacerraf dilemma, and I tried to write it out as a clean numbered argument so I could see exactly where it breaks. My reconstruction goes like this: (1) For a belief to count as knowledge, the fact believed has to be part of the story about why you hold the belief — if that fact could vanish and your beleif would be exactly the same, something has gone wrong. (2) If mathematical platonism is right, then the fact that 2+2=4 is a fact about objects that are abstract, outside space and time, and causally inert. (3) So if platonism is right, that fact can't be part of the causal story behind anyone's belief that 2+2=4. (4) So if platonism is right, nobody knows that 2+2=4. (5) But we do know that 2+2=4. (6) Therefore platonism is false. (7) The only decent alternative to platonism is some kind of mind-dependence: mathematical truth is fixed by conventions, or our constructive practices, or something we do. (8) That can't be right either, since 2+2=4 was true before anyone was around, and if our conventions had been different it would still be true. (9) So we're forced to say either we don't know basic arithmetic, which is crazy, or arithmetic somehow depends on us, which is also crazy.
