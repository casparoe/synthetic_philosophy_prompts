I run a monthly adult reading group on collective knowledge — fourteen people, two hours, with no prior background assumed. For next session I want a structured exercise, not a presentation. Design an exercise that makes the group feel, before the term is ever introduced, the phenomenon of antimemes: ideas that are true or important yet systematically fail to be remembered, copied, or spread. The rules and incentives should produce the effect on their own, so that certain contents die out or distort across several rounds of telling and retelling and participants can see it happening in the structure of the task, not in anyone's carelessness or bad faith. No phones, and no written notes except materials the exercise itself issues. Give exact rules, materials and quantities, a timeline for the two hours, and a debrief that ties concrete moments in the exercise to specific levers that make an idea antimemetic — complexity, social cost, the absence of a natural host, or an incentive to forget. Then tell me what would predictably go wrong on a first run and how to recover it, and which feature of real-world antimemes the exercise cannot reproduce. Finally, say what outcome on a first run would lead you to conclude the whole design should be replaced rather than adjusted, and where your reasoning about the debrief is most likely to fail.
