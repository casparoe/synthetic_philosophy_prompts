I'm a retired chemist who got into philosophy a couple years ago, and a friend of mine keeps sending me her verdicts on induction cases. So far she says: a sample of emeralds, all observed green before 2100, supports "all emeralds are green" but not "all emeralds are grue" (grue = green before 2100, blue after 2100). Same pattern for copper: a sample of copper that conducts supports "all copper conducts" but not "all copper culducts" (culduct = conducts before 2100, doesnt conduct after 2100). She says the difference is something about arbitrary time-indexed predicates, but I'm not sure that's the whole story.

Here's the case I want to predict her on. A botanist has only collected leaves from east of the river in some valley; all the leaves he's collected are broad. He proposes "all leaves of this species in the valley are brioad," where brioad = broad if east of the river and narrow if west of the river. So the observed leaves are brioad, but they're also just plain broad. What would my friend say: does that sample support the brioad hypothesis? I'm less interested in the yes/no than in the rule she's actually running, since several would fit her track record—no gerrymandered predicates at all, no tme-indexed gerrymanders, some entrenchment/natural-kind view, maybe others. What does each candidate rule predict about the brioad case, which do you think she's running, and what further case would cleanly tell them apart?
