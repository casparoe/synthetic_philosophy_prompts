Construct a toy case with two theories, T1 and T2, about the same small system, that agree on every observable relation but disagree about what the objects are. T1 says the objects have intrinsic natures over and above the relations they stand in; T2 says the objects are nothing but nodes in a relational network — there's nothing to them beyond thier place in the structure. Keep it small: three objects, two kinds of relation, plain language, no real physics.

Then run two takes through the case and show what each says and why: epistemic structural realism (we can know the structure but not the intrinsic natures of the relata) and ontic structural realism (the relations are all there is, and the relata are nothing over and above their structural position). I want the verdicts to come out this way: the epistemic structural realist says T1 and T2 are two genuinely diffrent theories, and all possible evidence underdetermines which is right; the ontic structural realist says they are not really two theories at all — T1 is just the same structure with an empty extra bit of metaphysics bolted on, so no underdetermination problem even arises. Make sure those verdicts actually fall out of the two positions rather than being stipulated by how you set up T1 and T2.

After that, tell me (a) which feature of the case is doing the work to make the two structuralisms come apart like that, (b) what happens if you remove that feature, and (c) how a defender of epistemic structural realism would push back on the ontic structural realist's answer. The point of the case is to test whether someone can track the actual difference between the two structuralisms and not just repeat the slogan that only structure is knowable.
