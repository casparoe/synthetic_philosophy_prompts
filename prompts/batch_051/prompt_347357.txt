Compare what it means for a political community to join a customs union with what it means for it to join a confederation, focusing on the concept of sovereignty. Assume a customs union of the standard kind: members remove internal tariffs, adopt a common external tariff, and pursue a common commercial policy, so that no member may negotiate trade agreements with outside states on its own. Assume a confederation of the standard kind: members remain formally sovereign, delegate only enumerated powers to common institutions, decide chiefly by unanimity or near-unanimity, and retain a right of withdrawal. Which of these two arrangements involves a deeper qualification or surrender of sovereignty by its members, and why? Is there a conception of sovereignty — for instance, sovereignty as a divisible bundle of competences that can be pooled and shared rather than as a single indivisible power that must be either fully kept or fully surrendered — under which the framing of the question is itself misleading? Please answer in American English.
