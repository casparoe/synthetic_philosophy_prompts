I am drafting a short statement of my views on the ethics of obedience and deference, and before I circulate it I want a consistency audit. Here are the commitments I currently hold, all of which I would like to keep if I can:

1. Deference to legitimate authority is a genuine virtue. When a command issues from a person or institution with the standing to issue it, that standing gives me a reason to comply that counts independently of the command's content — including when I privately think another course would be better.

2. The Milgram obedience experiments reveal a standing human vulnerability: ordinary, decent people will with surprising frequency continue administering what they believe are dangerous shocks when an authority figure calmly insists. The central lesson is that unreflective compliance with authority is a standing danger, not a rare pathology.

3. In military contexts, a service member ought to comply promptly with lawful orders even when they doubt the order's wisdom, but has a duty to refuse orders that are unlawful.

4. Epistemic deference to genuine experts is rational and often required. A patient may rightly accept a physician's recommendation without being able to reconstruct the medical reasoning, and this deference makes the patient's decision better, not worse.

5. Arendt's account of the Eichmann trial suggests that the great danger in hierarchical systems is not exceptional malice but a kind of thoughtlessness: failing to stop and ask whether an action is right, as distinct from whether it is authorized. Institutions should therefore cultivate independent judgment rather than mere rule-following.

6. In high-tempo operational settings — an operating theater, a fire ground, a flight deck, a firefight — prompt, unquestioning execution of role-based orders is often what prevents catastrophe; a habit of pausing to second-guess the leader in the moment can be lethal to the people one is trying to help.

Please audit this set. I do not want a lecture on the topic and I do not want every pair flagged as equally difficult; I want discrimination between real tensions and apparent ones, ordered by severity, with the least costly fix named.

Format your response in the following four sections, in this order:
- Restatement (about 150 words): restate each commitment in a single sentence to confirm the reading you are auditing.
- Apparent tensions that dissolve (about 250 words): identify any pairs that look in conflict but are not, and explain why the appearance is misleading.
- Genuine tensions, ranked (about 450 words): list the real tensions from mildest to most severe; for each, say which commitment or commitments would have to give to resolve it.
- Cheapest revision (about 150 words): name the single change to the set that would resolve the most tension, and state what it costs.
