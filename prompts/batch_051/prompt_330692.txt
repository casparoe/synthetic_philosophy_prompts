Yesterday a friend and I spent hours arguing about whether the gene's-eye view of evolution is a substantive explanatory program or just a rhetorical reframing of standard selection. He kept returning to the picture associated with Dawkins in The Selfish Gene and Williams in Adaptation and Natural Selection: organisms are not the units of selection but vehicles or survival machines, and traits persist because they serve the replication interests of the genes that produce them. I ended up with an argument that seems to show the view proves too much, but I cannot tell whether I have refuted him or built a straw man.

(1) If the gene is the unit of selection, then any evolved trait can be redescribed as a strategy by which some gene or gene combination increases its expected representation in future generations.
(2) For virtually any trait we observe, we can construct some post hoc story linking it to a gene-level replication advantage.
(3) A theory that can be adapted to explain any conceivable observation after the fact does not explain the specific observation.
(4) Therefore the gene's-eye view, taken as a general explanatory program, risks becoming unfalsifiable.
(5) But this seems unfair, because the view also generates hard, testable predictions: intragenomic conflict, meiotic drive, sex-ratio distortion, and the contrast between kin and group selection make different empirical demands rather than merely telling stories.
(6) A theory that makes hard predictions cannot be empty, even if its practitioners sometimes offer loose explanations.
(7) So the objection softens to: the gene's-eye view is only as good as the discipline of its users, and it shuttles between precise theory and post hoc storytelling depending on rhetorical convenience.

I would rather reject (3) than give up (5), but I suspect that preference is special pleading. Which step should be rejected, and what does rejecting it cost? Is my friend entitled to hold the hard and the loose versions of the gene's-eye view at once, or is that exactly the kind of unfalsifiable hedging that (3) rules out?
