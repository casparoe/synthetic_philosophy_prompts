I keep seeing the same fight in the LessWrong/ACX comments between someone who reads Hume as a straight regularity theorist—causation is just constant conjunction plus the habit of expectation, full stop, nothing in the objects—and someone who reads him as what people call the New Hume: a sceptical realist who thinks there are probably real necessary connections / causal powers in the objects, but that we can never know them. Theyve been talking past each other for a year, so I want to hand them an adversarial-collaboration exercise and make them actually produce the document they'd both sign. Write that document. Label the camps Old Hume and New Hume (or better labels if you have them). It should contain: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section on what Hume unambiguously says about causation in the Treatise and the first Enquiry—constant conjunction, contiguity, temporal priority, the two definitions of "cause", the inference of the mind, and the talk of secret powers—phrased so both sides nod along; (2) a disagreements table where each row gives the neutral statement of the residual disagreement and then the wording each side would accept for its own position, not a strawman of the other side; (3) for each row, a crux: a subquestion such that settling it would move at least one side, plus a concrete case or passage-interpretation argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Also flag any part of their dispute that is actually just verbal. The trap is making the agreed claims vacuous and the disagreement rows slanted; the whole point of the document is to anoy them into noticing that.
