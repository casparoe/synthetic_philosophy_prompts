I'm a phd student in philosophy of cognitive science, and I keep noticing a strange asymmetry in how people argue about whole-brain emulation. The same person will say that gradually replacing neurons one at a time with functionally identical silicon parts would preserve your mind — no single step makes a difference, so by induction nothing is lost — but then say that a one-shot destructive scan-and-run upload would kill you, even if the scan and the simulation are perfect. As far as I can tell the two procedures end up in the same place and have the same functional-causal profile; one is just spread out over time and the other is sudden. Does this asymmetry have an established name in the personal identity / uploading literature? I know about Parfit's teletransportation and the ship of Theseus, but I'm after the specific contrast between gradual neural replacement and instant scan-and-run, and whether there's a term for letting continuity of the physical process do the identity work while the actual physical stuff is swapped out. If there isnt a name, what would you call it and where exactly should the line be drawn?
