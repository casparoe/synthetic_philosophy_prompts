Me and a few friends run a small game night where the whole point is that you can vote to amend the rules. The rulebook is like half a page and I wrote it, so its not some official document. The only rules that matter for this are:

Rule 1: "A rule may be amended by a written proposal that is carried if at least two-thirds of the players present at the meeting vote for it."
Rule 2: "An amendment is void if its purpose or likely effect is to make it impossible for any rule to be amended in the future."

Last week Sam, who is currently winning, proposed to delete Rule 2. His argument is that Rule 2 only voids an amendment that makes some rule impossible to amend, and deleting Rule 2 doesn't make any rule impossible to amend — Rule 1 still lets you amend every remaining rule. He says the rules would still be perfectly amendable with only Rule 1 there. The rest of us think that's clearly a loophole, because the moment Rule 2 is gone nothing would stop him from then passing "Rule 1 may not be amended" and locking the game in place. But I wrote the rules and I honestly never pictured someone aiming Rule 2 at Rule 2 itself.

I don't care that much who wins the vote, I want to understand what the rule even says in this case. Could you go through what the different ways of reading a rule like this — the literal words, the purpose of Rule 2, what I'd probably have said if someone had asked me at the time, and what a reasonable player would take it to mean — each give for Sam's motion? And is there any reading where deleting Rule 2 is valid, or would that require reading something into the rule that isn't in the words? I keep feeling like we're doing something circular but can't put my finger on it.
