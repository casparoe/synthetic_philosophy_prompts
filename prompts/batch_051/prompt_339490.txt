I'm a high school student and I've gone down a rabbit hole on the modularity of mind thing. From what I can tell, Fodor's original claim was that modules are fast, automatic, informationally encapsulated input systems like vision or language, and that central cognition is domain-general and not encapsulated. Then the evolutionary psychologists came along and said no, basically the whole mind is a bunch of modules — cheater detection, intuitive physics, theory of mind, etc. And the debate seems weirdly stuck: every time the Fodor side points out that some system isn't encapsulated, the massive modularity side says "sure, but it's still a module in our sense." My hypothosis is that they're mostly fighting about the word "module" — Fodor means a computational property (encapsulated, fast), while the other side means an evolved function (a dedicated adaptation). But maybe there's a real empirical disagreement underneath, about whether central thought is actually domain-general or not. So what kind of disagreement is this, and is there an experiment or a conceptual distinction that would actually get it unstuck? I'd way rather hear a diagnosis than a survey of the literature.
