For an upper-level seminar on Marx and political economy (about 20 students, 30 minutes), write a decision-tree sorting quiz that takes someone from the starting commitment "under capitalism, labour-saving technology raises productivity but does not shorten the working day" to a named position on automation and emancipation, in a broadly Marxian frame. The tree should have no more than six branch questions and exactly six leaves. The five leaves you must include are: (1) the orthodox view that machinery under capitalism immiserates workers and only post-capitalist automation can be liberatory; (2) a left-accelerationist view that we should push automation as hard as possible so it forces the contradiction to a head; (3) an autonomist/social-reproduction view that capital captures whatever we build, so what matters is the command relation rather than the machines themselves; (4) the general-intellect view that as production becomes socialised and knowledge-based, value measured in labour time quietly breaks down; and (5) a humanist "technology is not neutral" view that some labour-saving tools degrade work in ways that would survive a change of property relations. The sixth leaf is yours to pick, but it should sit in a genuinely empty cell of the logical space, not just split one of these five.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from the list of names: first set out the two or three underlying questions that do the real work (e.g., is the damage done by machines a feature of capitalism or of the technology itself; does the value-form survive once living labour stops being the main input; is emancipation about seizing the productivity or abolishing the logic of productivity), then locate the positions in that space, note any empty cells, and say for each whether nobodys there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Phrase every node as a concrete vignette or claim someone could judge without already knowing the labels. At least three nodes should use these cases: a warehouse that automates and makes workers redundant while consumer prices fall; algorithmic shift-scheduling in a gig firm; a near-fully-automated sector where design and maintenance employ two percent of the workforce; and a UBI funded by a tax on machine output. Present the instrument as a numbered set of branch questions with exits, then a key mapping answer-patterns to leaves. Flag any two leaves the tree cannot actually pull apart without adding a further question, and say honestly why not. Make the last line of your answer a one-sentence summary of the pair of positions the quiz is least able to separate, beginning "Summary:".
