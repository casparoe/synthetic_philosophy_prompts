I’ve been in a metaphysics seminar on Bradley and the unity of the proposition, and I’ve ended up defending a pretty strong line. I think Bradley’s regress is not just a local puzzle about relations but a standing reason to think any account of propositional unity that treats it as a relation between constituents — including Frege-style function/argument saturation or Russellian exemplification — is ultimately restating the problem rather than solving it. The bit that moved me is from Chapter III of *Appearance and Reality*, when Bradley writes:

“The qualities, as distinct, are always made so by an action which is admitted to imply relation. They are made so, and, what is more, they are emphatically kept so. And you cannot ever get your product standing apart from its process.”

I read this as saying the distinction between relata and the relating operation is not a neutral starting point; the apparent relata are already artifacts of the very relating we’re trying to explain, so no genuine account can get unification by composing them. I’m willing to bite the bullet that propositions are not built from independently given constituents at all.

But I’m now at the point where I want to know what would actually move me off this. I don’t mean the standart “but then nothing is representable” or “but identity is a relation” objections, unless you can make them count in a stronger way than usual. What I want is a map of the view’s exposure: which possible cases, formal results, developments in philosophy of language, or intuitions about truth-aptness, negation, or inference would count against it? Which would be fatal and which merely awkward? Where would a committed Bradleyan have to concede the view is strained, and where should they hold the line? Please structure it as:

1. Restatement — what I’m committing to, in your words (1 short paragraph).
2. Defeaters — the strongest things that could count against it, each named and explained (roughly 5–7 paragraphs in all).
3. What a defender concedes — which of those are real costs rather than the opponent misdescribing the view (1 paragraph).
4. Ranked assessment — from fatal to merely awkward, with a sentence or two on each (a short list).
5. Verdict — whether the view is well insulated, and whether that insulation is a virtue or a sign of unfalsifiability (1 paragraph).
