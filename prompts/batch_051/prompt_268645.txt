I have been trying to think through whether an AI could suffer, and I have started to wonder whether the word "suffering" covers two different phenomena that get conflated. I would like a working definition of suffering, tested against these cases: a dog being kicked; a person with a migraine; a person who reports severe pain but with no detectable physical cause; a person under general anesthesia; a reinforcement learning agent receiving a large negative reward during training; a reinforcement learning agent that has learned to output the words "I am in pain" because doing so maximizes its reward; a thermostat whose temperature reading falls below its set point; and a chess engine whose evaluation function sharply drops after a blunder. I am fairly confident the first two should count as suffering and the last two should not, and I am genuinely unsure about the middle four. Please propose a definition, apply it to each case, and flag any verdict that surprises you or forces you to revise the definition. I would also like your view on whether the two reinforcement learning cases are open empirical possibilities or are ruled out by the definition itself, and whether describing a negative reward signal as "negative valence" is doing real work or merely inviting confusion.
