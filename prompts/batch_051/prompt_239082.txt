I keep going back and forth on the old rule that ignorance of the law is no excuse. As a piece of system design it's gorgeous: it's simple, it doesn't make liability depend on what a defendant privately knew, and it stops 'i didn't know' from becoming a universal defense—if the law's force depended on each person's actual state of knowledge, we'd have a different legal order for every defendant. But I have this intuition I cant shake: punishing someone under a rule they could not possibly have known about feels like punishing them for a state of the world, not for a choice. Like a rule that exists only as a pdf on some agency subpage nobody would ever find, or a court decision that retroactively recharacterizes conduct people reasonably thought was fine. That isn't law guiding action; it's just state power landing on someone after the fact. So I dont know which way to move. Walk me through the options and what each one genuinely costs. If I keep the maxim, do I have to bite the bullet that secret or effectively unfindable rules bind people, and can I give a coherent account of law that includes that? If I amend it, how do I carve out a 'could not have known' exception without the carve-out swallowing the rule—won't everyone claim ignorance and dump us back into the enforcement problem? Is there a reconciliation where the maxim was never a claim about fairness at all, just a procedural presumption that's only legitimate once the state has actually published the rule in a way a reasonable, diligent person could find and understand? And maybe the carve-out should only cover malum prohibitum offenses, where the wrongness is entirely created by the rule itself, while stuff that's wrong independently of any statute stays strict—but is that distinction stable enough to do the work, and what do you do about the person who could have found the regulation but just didn't bother? Tell me whether the intuition should be debunked, the maxim amended, or the two read as compatible, and say what would justify each move rather than just picking one.
