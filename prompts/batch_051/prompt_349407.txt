I've been reading around the philosophy of perception because I'm writing a scene where my protagonist, who has just gotten some awful news, crosses a pedestrian bridge she's walked for years and the drop below her literally looks further down, the cables look thinner, the whole structure looks more delicate. She doesn't conclude that it's more dangerous; the change is in how it looks to her. I want the scene to be psychologically precise enough that a philosopher of perception wouldn't just say "nice metaphor, but that's not how perception works."

So here's my question. I've put together a rough list of criteria for cognitive penetration, from what I've half-understood: (1) the change has to be in perceptual experience itself, not in the story she tells herself afterwards; (2) it has to be caused by a cognitive state like a belief, fear, or expectation; (3) the causal route can't run entirely through non-cognitive stuff like where she points her eyes, how long she looks, or her heart rate; and (4) it can't be just a bias in what she says or does later, or in how she remembers the scene when she tells someone about it.

Now the actual case I wrote: she's a bridge inspector by trade, so she knows the bridge's specs and rationally believes it's safe, and she's crossed it hundreds of times. This time she's crossing right after a phone call with the kind of news that makes your stomach drop out. She stops in the middle and the strands of the composite deck look as if they're fraying, though when she bends down and looks close she can see they're not. The railing feels to her hand thinner then it ever has, but she doesn't trust her eyes enough to measure it. Her feet start moving more cautiously without her deciding to. Does this qualify as cognitive penetration under my four criteria? And the part I actually care about: would a philosopher say the "looks different" bit is a genuine change in perception, or is it fear plus attention plus after-the-fact narration? If it doesn't qualify, is there a small tweak to the scene that would make it qualify without turning it into a hallucination?
