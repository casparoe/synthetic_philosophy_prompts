I'm on the arts commission for a small city, about 40,000 people, and we're voting next month on whether to spend a decent chunk of our budget building the town's first permanent theater or to keep putting the money into free outdoor festivals and civic events. One of my fellow comissioners keeps bringing up Rousseau's Letter to d'Alembert and says Geneva was right not to build a theater: he paraphrases Rousseau as saying that theater turns citizens into spectators, that tragedy lets us enjoy our own compassion without paying any of the real costs of it, and that comedy—Molière especially—makes the honest or sincere character look ridiculous while making the smooth, charming one lovable. Another commissioner says this is just anti-art snobbery and we should ignore it. I need a short written opinion (800 words or so) to circulate before the vote. It should come to a definite recommendation, engage the Rousseau-style worry as a live objection rather than waving it off, and give the rest of the commission two or three criteria we could use for future arts-funding decisions. Please write the memo in American English.
