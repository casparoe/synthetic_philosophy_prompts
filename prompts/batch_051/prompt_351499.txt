I have been thinking about a standard Christian teaching and cannot tell whether my argument against it is too quick. The teaching is that Christ was punished in our place: he took the penalty we deserved for our sins. Here is the argument I keep going back to.

(1) A punishment is just only if the person being punished actually deserves it.
(2) Desert is not the kind of thing that can be transferred from one person to another. If I deserve a penalty, that fact is about me; another person, even a willing and innocent person, can suffer, but their suffering does not transfer my desert to them.
(3) Christ was innocent and did not deserve punishment for any sin, his own or anyone else's.
(4) If penal substitution is true, then Christ was punished for sins he did not commit.
(5) From (1) and (2), if Christ was punished for sins he did not commit, then that punishment was unjust.
(6) But the teaching also says Christ's punishment was not an act of injustice; it was the satisfaction of justice.
(7) So either the teaching is false, or one of (1), (2), or (3) must be given up.

I have the strongest urge to push back against (2), because it seems the whole subject may be more subtle than I am making it. But I cannot make the subtle version explicit. Is my argument valid? If not, which step fails and why? If the argument is valid, which premise would a defender of penal substitution most plausibly reject, and what is the best version of that rejection? Please be clear about which points are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who have thought about this carefully, and do not present a contested view as if everyone who knows the area agrees.
