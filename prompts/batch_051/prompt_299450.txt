I'm a TA marking a midterm for a philosophy of art course, and I'd like a second read on one answer. Here's the question I set and the student's response. Give it a mark out of 10 and point-by-point comments; I need comments on each substantive claim, not just a general impression. Don't worry about being harsh, just accurate.

Question: In the type/token ontology of music, a musical work is often treated as a type whose tokens are its performances. Consider a studio recording of a piano sonata assembled over many takes, with punch-ins, edits, and a spliced final product. Is this recording (a) a performance of the sonata and (b) a token of it? Explain the reasoning behind each answer, and say how the type/token distinction relates to the distinction between a performance and a recording.

Student's answer: A work is a type, and performances are its tokens because each performance instantiates the work's prescribed sound structure at a particular time and place. A studio recording assembled from many takes is not a performance, since no single continuous event instantiates the whole work from beginning to end; a performance has to be the kind of event an audience could in principle attend. But the recording is still a token of the work, because it embodies the same acoustic structure specified by the score and was produced with the intention of presenting that work. So 'performance' is narrower than 'token': performances are event-tokens, recordings are object-tokens. A live recording is then both a token of the work and a token of the particular performance it documents.
