Before I commission another piece that reaches for the word 'chaos', I want a full specification of what a theory of unpredictability is answerable for. Write the desiderata that any adequate account of what it is for a deterministic system to be unpredictable must meet: the distinctions it cannot blur (chaos versus mere complexity or noise; sensitive dependence on initial conditions versus ordinary instability; unpredictability in practice, given finite measurement precision, versus unpredictability in principle); the phenomena it must explain (the butterfly effect as a claim about finite horizons, strange attractors, the coexistence of deterministic local dynamics with robust statistical regularities); and the results it must accommodate, such as the fact that a positive Lyapunov exponent is not by itself a licence to treat a system as random. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a mere preference. Then mark which you regard as non-negotiable, which belong to one camp, and whether any pair of requirements cannot be jointly satisfied — in particular, whether an account can coherently call a system both fully deterministic and unpredictable in principle. Finally, say which single requirement does the most to separate a technical claim about chaos from the usual butterfly-effect cliché.
