I'm a novelist, drafting a book set in a fictional country that has just signed a peace deal after a long low-level civil war. The new government sets up a truth commision modeled on South Africa's. In South Africa's Promotion of National Unity and Reconciliation Act of 1995, the amnesty arrangement was something like: amnesty could be granted only if the applicant made a full disclosure of all the relevant facts, and the act or omission was associated with a political objective, and the act was proportional to that objective. The underlying idea was truth in exchange for amnesty, with "truth" understood as factual completeness.

In my novel, the commission adds a literary twist. Applicants for amnesty must submit their full disclosure as a first-person narrative, not as a legal questionnaire or statement taken by a lawyer. It must be at least, say, five thousand words, written in plain language, with no legalese or jargon. It has to be reviewed by a panel of novelists, not lawyers, and this panel can reject the application if it judges the narrative evasive, self-justifying, or inauthentic. The novelist-panel's standard is something like "adequately self-implicating" — the applicant has to show that they understand what their actions did to actual people, and has to name their own motives and self-deceptions rather than just narrating events.

I want you to analyze this mechanism the way a game theorist or policy analyst might: what does it incentivize, how would perpetrators game it, who would get amnesty and who would be shut out, what would it do to victims, and what are the likely second-order effects over the first decade? Also, could a standard of literary authenticity be applied fairly, or does it just replace legal fact-finding with credibility theater? And if there are failure modes, could they be fixed without gutting the scheme? Please write your answer in American English. Also, at the end, tell me what argument or consideration would most likely change your main verdict, and where your reasoning is most fragile.
