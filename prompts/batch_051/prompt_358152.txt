I'm putting together a handout for an undergraduate metaethics course on the debate between internalists and externalists about practical reasons, and I need a case built to order. Let me fix the terms I'm using. By an internal reason I mean a reason that an agent has in virtue of there being a sound deliberative route from her current subjective motivational set—her desires, projects, loyalties, and evaluative commitments—to her being motivated to perform the action. By an external reason I mean a reason that holds for the agent whether or not any such route exists from her current motivational set. Construct an ordinary, realistic vignette in which an agent has an external reason to perform some action but no internal reason to perform it. The action should concern the agent's own future welfare, not an obligation to anyone else, so that the external reason at issue is a prudential one rather than a moral one. The agent must be a competent adult with no false beliefs about the relevant non-normative facts and no obvious lapse of instrumental reasoning; she must not be a psychopath, an amoralist, or a clinical case. And do not simply stipulate that her motivational set contains no relevant desire as a brute fact—the absence of any sound deliberative route from that set to the action has to be made intelligible by her history or her outlook on life. Then do the check. State the action; lay out the subjective motivational set; explain why no sound deliberative route runs from that set to the action; and give the verdict that the internalist and the externalist each delivers, with the reasoning behind each. Finish by identifying which feature of the case does the real work, saying what would change if that feature were removed, and explaining how a Williams-style internalist would attack the case rather than accept it.
