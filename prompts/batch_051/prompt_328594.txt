I'm pulling together a view on how contextualism and pragmatic encroachment relate, and I'd like a consistency audit of my own commitments before I start writing. Here is the package I currently hold.

(1) Contextualism about knowledge attributions. An utterance of 'S knows that p' can be true in one conversational context and false in another even when S's evidence, reliability, and practical situation are held fixed, because the operative epistemic standard shifts with the attributor's context.

(2) Pragmatic encroachment. Whether S knows can also depend in part on what is practically at stake for S. In the familiar bank pair, where two subjects have the same memory that the bank is open on Saturday but one has little at stake and the other a great deal, I hold that the low-stakes subject knows and the high-stakes subject does not.

(3) The knowledge norm of assertion. An assertion of p is proper only if the speaker knows that p.

(4) Stability of assertion propriety. Whether a particular assertion was proper is fixed by the speaker's evidence, practical situation, and circumstances at the time of utterance, and does not shift when some third party later evaluates the assertion from a more or less demanding context.

(5) Fallibilism about knowledge. Knowledge does not require eliminating every alternative; the low-stakes subject's week-old memory can suffice for knowledge even though it leaves open possibilities in which the bank's Saturday hours have since changed.

Please audit this package rather than lecture me. Identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be; order the genuine tensions by severity; and for each, say which commitment would have to give, what the cheapest revision would be, and what it would cost. Close by saying which single revision would leave the package most defensible overall.
