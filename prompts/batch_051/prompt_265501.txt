For the past few months I've been on the planning comission in a mid-sized city, and we keep coming back to the same fight. A developer has proposed demolishing a block of single-family homes plus a small 12-unit rental building two blocks from a light-rail station, and putting up a six-story mixed-income apartment building: roughly 180 units, about a fifth set aside below market rate. The neighborhood association is livid. They say it would wreck the street's character, make parking impossible, overload the nearest elementary school, and tank property values. Some of the tenants in the 12-unit building are long-term residents who'd have to move, and even though the developer has offered relocation assistance and a right to return, a few tenant advocates have lined up with the opposition. On the other side, the city has a severe housing shortage, median rents are up something like 40% in six years, and it looks absurd to protect a few dozen low-slung buildings right next to transit while the region adds jobs way faster than homes. The commission is split, and I've been asked to circulate a short written opinion before next week's vote.

To me this isn't really a parking-and-setbacks question; its about who actually has a right to the city. Do the people already in a neighborhood get to define what it is and keep it that way? Do people who'd like to live near transit but can't afford to now have any claim on what gets built? Does a city have obligations to future residents, or only to the people who currently vote? I want the memo to actually take a side instead of laying out both camps, because we're going to vote either way. Please draft the written opinion I'd circulate: a concrete recommendation — approve, deny, or approve with conditions, and if conditions, name the main ones (height, number of set-asides, tenant protections, whatever you think is right) — with the reasons behind it. Please make sure it grapples honestly with the strongest objection from whichever side you're recommending against, and if your recommendation depends on a particular view of whose interests count and why, just say so plainly, even if it's controversial.
