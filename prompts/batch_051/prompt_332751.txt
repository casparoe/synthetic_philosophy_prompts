Im helping run a small reading group on early Chinese ethics, and we've been stuck for weeks on the dispute between the Mohists and the Confucians over whether care should be distributed by relationship. One member keeps pushing jian'ai, impartial care (older translations say universal love), as the only view that doesn't secretly license nepotism. Another keeps answering that graded love—caring more for those nearer to you, beginning with the family—isn't selfishness with a good conscience; it's a Confucian story about how care is actually cultivated. After a particularly circular meeting the organizer proposed an adversarial collaboration, and I got volunteered to draft it.

Could you write it as a document that a committed Mohist and a committed early Confucian could both sign? Format it as a numbered "agreed claims" section, followed by a disagreements table. For each row of the table, include (a) the Mohist's own statement of her view, worded so she'd accept it; (b) the Confucian's own statement of his view, worded so he'd accept it; (c) the crux, the sub-question that if answered would make at least one side genuinely move; and (d) a case or line of argument both sides agree bears on that crux, with each side's prediction written out in advance. Please avoid the lazy caricatures where the Mohist wants to abandon his mother and the Confucian just insists that partiality is fine. What I'm after is the cleanup: which disagreements are real and which was about words—for instance, between impartiality as equal treatment and impartiality as equal concern—and which residual disagreement survives once the wording is precise.
