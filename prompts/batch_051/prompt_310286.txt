For a graduate seminar I teach on rational choice under extreme uncertainty, I have been working through the Pascal's mugging problem, and a discussion there produced a variation I want to think through more carefully. In the familiar case, a mugger threatens an astronomically large harm unless you hand over a small sum; the puzzle is that any nonzero credence in the threat, no matter how small, yields an expected disutility that swamps every ordinary consideration, so expected-utility maximization seems to demand payment. The usual replies are to bound the utility function, impose a minimum probability threshold, or penalize the mugger's claim for its complexity.

The variation is this. Suppose your credence in the threat is not independent of its size, as the standard discussion tacitly assumes. Instead, conditional on hearing a threat of size n, your credence that the mugger can and will carry it out is proportional to n^{-α}, for some α > 0: larger threats are less credible at a known polynomial rate. You observe a threat of size n and must decide whether to pay a fixed small sum. Please work through what expected-utility theory says as a function of α. For which values of α does the expected harm of refusing to pay grow without bound as n increases, remain roughly constant, or shrink toward zero, and what does each regime imply about whether the mugging argument survives? Then assess the standard replies under this modification: does a bounded utility function eliminate the problem for every α or only for some? If the disutility of a harm of size n is n^β with β > 1 (the risk-averse shape for losses), how is the critical value of α affected? And which of the usual replies — bounded utility, probability thresholds, complexity priors — are doing genuinely different work once credibility is tied to magnitude in this way? I am especially interested in whether the original puzzle is best diagnosed as an artifact of an implicit flat or heavy-tailed prior over possible harms, and how much of the fanaticism worry remains once that prior is made explicit. Please separate what is mathematically forced from what remains genuinely contested among decision theorists and philosophers working on fanaticism, and flag any place where a contested position might otherwise look like consensus.
