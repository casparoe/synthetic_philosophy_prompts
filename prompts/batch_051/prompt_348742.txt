I'm in an AI-governance reading group, and a phrase keeps short-circuiting our discussion of legal personhood for advanced systems. One member, who thinks it is only a matter of time before sufficiently sophisticated AI systems should be recognized as legal persons, repeatedly reaches for the slogan "corporations are people." As he uses it, the slogan is doing genuine argumentative work: the law already treats a corporation—a non-biological, non-conscious, distributed entity—as a person for purposes like owning property, entering contracts, suing and being sued, and even asserting certain constitutional protections. So, he argues, the concept of legal personhood is evidently not tied to being a human being or to having any particular kind of consciousness, and there is no principled conceptual barrier to extending personhood to an AI; the resistance is just anthropocentric squeamishness. Another member objects that the slogan is being made to do more than it can. A corporate person, she says, is a juridical device for bundling the rights and liabilities of the human beings behind the firm; the law is not thereby recognizing that the firm is a person in the same sense as you or me. So the corporate precedent says nothing about whether an AI could be, or ought to be, a person in its own right. The argument cycles because neither of them has actually said what the slogan asserts.

Before our next meeting, I want to unpack the slogan properly. What are the distinct precise claims that "corporations are people" could be making—about the difference between natural and juridical persons, about whether legal personality is a bundle of specific capacities rather than a single underlying status, about the grounds on which the law confers it, and about what any of this implies for AI? For each reading, I would like a verdict: is it trivially true but inert, substantively true but limited, or false? And finally, which reading or readings, if any, actually license the inference to AI legal personhood that the first member wants to draw? A menu of readings would help me more than a single interpretation, since I plan to bring it back to the group.
