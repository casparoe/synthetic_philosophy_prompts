In the United States, the Constitution sets minimum ages for office — 25 for the House of Representatives, 30 for the Senate, 35 for the presidency — but sets no maximum age for any of them, and for federal judges sets no age requirement at all. Most states let a sixteen-year-old drive only after passing a road test, while an eighteen-year-old can vote without any test of political judgment. I keep noticing a puzzle in this. The law seems to treat age itself as a qualification for high office, as if wisdom accumulates automatically with years and need never be demonstrated. But the same society refuses to trust age alone behind the wheel, where it demands an actual performance test. And if age is being used as a proxy for wisdom, why is there a floor but no ceiling? Either the law assumes wisdom does not decline with age, or it is not really using age as a proxy for wisdom at all.

Treat this whole pattern of age thresholds as a philosophical artifact. You are a professional philosopher who works on the concept of wisdom and on how political institutions encode assumptions about wisdom. Please think carefully, and begin by brainstorming two or three candidate reconstructions of the implicit theory before committing to one. Then answer the following. What does this practice strictly presuppose about what wisdom is, whether it can be taught, and whether it can be measured? Which rival views of wisdom does it quietly rule out, and which parts of the practice are actually compatible with several rival theories — for example, that what age tracks is not wisdom but something else, like emotional stability or having a stake in society? Are the parts of the practice consistent with one another across the different age lines for driving, voting, the House, the Senate, the presidency, and the courts? Finally, tell me what would have to change in the law if we replaced the presupposed view with the view that wisdom can be taught, or tested, or that it can decline with age — in particular, do the minimum ages still make sense, and should there be maximum ages or assessments?
