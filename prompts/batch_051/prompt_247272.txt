I teach a junior/senior political theory seminar, about 18 students, 80 minutes, and we've just read Machiavelli. I want an in-class simulation that makes them feel the specific tension between fortune and virtù: fortune only controls about half of what happens, so preparation and skill really do matter, but the times change and people basically can't change their own natures to keep up, so even a perfectly competent prince can crater when circumstances shift. I also want them to sit with the weird ending of The Prince, where he seems to say that when fortune turns against you, boldness is better than caution, because fortune is figured as feminine and submits to the impetuous. Design the game around that. Could be a card/dice thing where each round the "times" change and players have to decide how much to lock in as a temperament or strategy, with some regimes rewarding caution and others rewarding audacity — but it has to produce the moment where a team says "we did everything right and still got wrecked, and now the only thing that would have saved us is the exact style we spent the game avoiding." Give me concrete rules, roles, materials, a points scheme that doesn't need real money, a 70-minute timeline with setup and debrief included, and a debrief that maps what happened to the river-and-dikes image and the temperament/times mismatch. Say what'll predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about what the game can't reproduce — I suspect the main one is that in Machiavelli fortune isn't just random noise; it seems to favor audacity in a way a die roll won't. No lecture up front.
