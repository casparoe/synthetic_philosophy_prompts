A friend of mine read one blog post on the norms of assertion and now summarizes the whole debate like this: "The rule for assertion is simple: you shouldn't say something unless you believe it. That's really all the knowledge norm comes down to, since knowledge is just true belief plus a bit of extra confidence. Stricter rules are a nonstarter, because if we could only assert what we know for certain, everyday conversation would grind to a halt. The so-called dispute between the truth norm and the knowledge norm is mostly a disagreement about whether you also need a justification requirement on top of the belief." This sounds off to me in ways i cant put my finger on, but I'm not an epistemologist. Could you go through it claim by claim and tell me what a philosopher working on assertion would accept, what they'd say is being conflated, and whether the last point about the truth norm vs knowledge norm debate really is about adding a justification condition?
