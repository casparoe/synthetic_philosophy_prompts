An externalist/reliabilist and an internalist/evidentialist in my reading group have been arguing past each other for months about the bootstrapping problem and the related easy-knowledge problem. They've finally agreed to do an adversarial collaboration, and I want you to draft the document they should have produced. For context: ordinary perceptual knowledge seems completely fine — you look at the wall and know it's red, and neither of them wants to deny that. The trouble is that a subject can apparently bootstrap their way from a bunch of checks like 'the guage says F and the tank is full' to 'the gauge is reliable,' even though every one of those checks relied on the gauge itself. The reliabilist thinks this bootstrapping is either benign or can be blocked by a cheap no-self-support rule that doesn't threaten ordinary knowledge. The internalist thinks it shows the view lets you get justification for nothing, and that we should instead require some independent, antecedent grip on a source's reliability before trusting it.

Write the joint statement they'd both sign. It should include: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section — what they both accept about the gas-gauge and red-wall cases, about what any adequate epistemology has to deliver, and about positions neither of them is willing to take; (2) a disagreements table — each row a precise point of disagreement, with each side's own view phrased so its proponent would happily own the wording; (3) the cruxes, by which I mean the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one of them; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Also say plainly whether any part of their dispute looks verbal rather than substantive.
