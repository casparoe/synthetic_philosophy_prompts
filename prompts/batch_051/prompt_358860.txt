Last week a friend and I got into a long, vaguely tipsy argument in a pub about the sphex story, and I haven't been able to let it go. You probably know it: a wasp paralyses a cricket, drags it to the mouth of its burrow, goes inside for a second to check things over, comes back out, and if someone's moved the cricket a few centimetres away it does the whole drag-and-check sequence again, with no apparent limit. My friend kept saying this is just a fixed action pattern, and that human flexibility is only a matter of degree, so the example can't possibly tell you anything about wether we have free will. I kept feeling it does tell you something, but I couldn't say what — something about the difference between a routine that re-runs regardless of how the world has changed, and a process that can treat the changed situation as a reason to revise the routine instead of just another trigger for the same loop.

I'm supposed to turn this into a short research proposal for a master's workshop (about 4,000 words, one term), and I'd like to build it around this argument. My half-formed idea is that the sphex case matters because it makes vivid what it looks like when a system has no grip at all on its own activity in the relevant sense, and that free or responsible action needs something more than exactly the same outward behaviour backed by fancier internal computation — but I'm worried that claim either collapses into ordinary compatibilism or begs the question against someone who is deflationary about the whole thing. Could you write it as a proposal for me? I'd want: the research question stated precisely (something like what the contrast between sphexish rigidity and flexible, deliberative behaviour shows about the conditions of free or responsible action, and whether the lesson survives once you swap the wasp for increasingly flexibile machines); why the usual compatibilist/incompatibilist and reasons-responsiveness debates don't settle it; what I would actually do; what a useful deliverable looks like; and what would count as the project failing. No reading list or citations, I'll add those myself — I just need the argumentative spine. And keep it honest about scope, because it's only a term and I can see this ballooning into a whole theory of action.
