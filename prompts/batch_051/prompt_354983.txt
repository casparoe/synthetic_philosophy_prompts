I keep tripping over the Twin Earth argument (the standard version: two planets, H2O on one, XYZ on the other, and the two speakers can't tell the difference), and I've started calling a problem I see in it "the premise that externalism eats." Here's the idea.

The argument's first move is to stipulate that Earth-Oscar and Twin-Oscar are molecule-for-molecule identical, so they're in the same psychological state, and then to point out that their word 'water' refers to different things. Therefore meaning isn't in the head. But my worry is that the stipulation itself already smuggles in the internalist picture. To say two people are in "the same psychological state" across worlds, you have to bracket everything about the environment — that's exactly the narrow-content notion the argument is supposed to kill. So the thought experiement only works if you've already individuated minds the internalist way. Once you go externalist, you can't cleanly say the two Oscars are psychologically identical at all, because part of their sameness would have to include what their thoughts are about, which is the very thing that differs. So the argument ends up proving a conditional: if minds can be cut loose from the world, then reference depends on the world. But the externalist never granted the antecedent.

I honestly can't tell whether I've found a real circularity or just confused the setup of a counterexample with a premise we're allowed to reject. Is this already a named objection? And can you run Twin Earth without the identical-psychology stipulation, or does the whole thing fall apart if you drop it? Be candid: where does "the premise that externalism eats" break, if it does?
