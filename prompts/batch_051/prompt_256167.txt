I've been mulling this over since a forum argument about whether people who chemically dampen their own anger are thereby changing something about moral responsibility, and I keep coming back to Strawson. In Freedom and Resentment, P. F. Strawson argues that the traditional worry about whether determinism would undermine responsibility rests on a mistake: responsibility is not a metaphysical thesis that could be repealed by discovering determinism, because it is built out of the reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, indignation, hurt feelings—through which we register the quality of other people's wills toward us. Part of his case for why this cannot simply be given up is that the objective attitude toward someone is available only as a partial and temporary exception, as with children or the severely mentally ill; taking the objective attitude toward everyone at once is not, on his picture, something beings like us can actually sustain.

Now imagine a technology that directly changes that assumption. Suppose there is a safe, cheap, precisely targetable intervention—a reactive-attitude editor—that lets a person voluntarily suspend a chosen reactive attitude while leaving the rest of their moral psychology intact. At a low setting it removes only the affect of resentment: the user still judges that they were wronged, still objects, still demands apology or repair, but the hot retaliatory feeling is absent for as long as they choose. At a higher setting it can suspend the whole participant attitude toward a particular person, or toward everyone, stably and indefinitely, without dissociation and without damaging any other capacities. Assume it is widely available, and that a community could choose to make its use near-universal.

I want to know whether Strawson's argument survives this development, needs repair, or is dead. Act as an analytic philosopher who works on moral responsibility. First lay out the premise structure of Strawson's argument and identify exactly which premise the technology bears on. Then, before giving a verdict, work through the strongest objections to your own assessment: for instance, that the editor suppresses a feeling but leaves the stance untouched; that the choice to use the editor is itself made from within the reactive attitudes and so cannot abolish them all the way down; that a community using it universally has changed the subject rather than refuted Strawson. End with a clear verdict, and if the argument is repairable, state the repaired version and what it gives up.
