ok so ive basically become a maximin person. for choices where you really have no probabilities — veil of ignorance type situations, or honestly just picking between two colleges when i cant even begin to guess the odds of the outcomes — i think the rational rule is maximin: pick the option whose worst case is least bad. i know the usual complaints, that it's way too cautious, that it ignores all the upside, that a one-in-a-million disaster ends up running the whole decision. but those feel like people saying the conclusion is uncomfortable, not like they've shown the rule is wrong. i want to know what would actually count against maximin as a rule for decision under ignorance. like, what cases, arguments, or results should make a reasonable maximin person actually lower their confidence — and which of those are fatal versus just awkward? which objections would a defender admit are real problems with maximin-as-stated and then try to patch (only use maximin when the worst cases are below some threshold, or use minimax regret instead, stuff like that)? and honestly is this view even the kind of thing that can be falsified by a clever case, or did i just pick a cautious temperament and dress it up in decision-theory language — and if so does that matter? please don't just rehearse the standard rawls vs harsanyi debate unless that debate is actually doing the work here.
