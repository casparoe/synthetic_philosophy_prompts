I’ve been collecting a friend’s settled verdicts about deception, and I want your prediction about a new case. Her track record: she says it is permissible to tell a would-be attacker that your friend has left town when the friend is hiding upstairs, and equally permissible to send the attacker to the wrong place with a true statement. She says a job applicant who writes that he managed a six-person team when he merely shared a common inbox with six people has wronged the employer; and she says an applicant who writes that he was the point of contact for payroll—true, because he only forwarded complaints—has also wronged the employer, though she has not said whether these are the same kind of wrong. She says there is nothing wrong with staying silent and letting someone keep believing you had a larger role in a project than you actually did. The new case: Priya is asked at a reunion whether she and Marcus wrote the Clarke report together, and she answers, “I was involved in the final draft.” That is true, since she formatted the footnotes, but she says it intending the asker to conclude she co-wrote the report. What is her most likely verdict, what does that show about whether she really recognizes a moral difference between lying and merely misleading, what rival principle would fit her settled cases just as well, and what further case would cleanly separate the two? Lead with your bottom line, then reason it through, without preamble or restating the question.
