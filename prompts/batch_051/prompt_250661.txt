I recently retired from a career in experimental physics and have been reading philosophy of science with the uncomfortable feeling that debates in the field are rarely about what they seem. I keep returning to Milton Friedman’s 1953 essay The Methodology of Positive Economics. The passage that unsettles me is near the end, where he develops two examples side by side: leaves arranged so that each seems to seek the maximum exposure to sunlight, and an expert billiard player who shoots as if he had solved the equations of the optimal path. Friedman says the hypothesis about the leaves and the hypothesis about the player are both legitimate scientific hypotheses even though neither leaves nor players perform the calculations, because what matters is that the behavior is consistent with the predictions. This is just one step away from his broader claim that important theories will have assumptions that are descriptively false, and that the more significant the theory, the more unrealistic the assumptions in that sense. I take it this is the view people call the F-twist.

My question is not whether Friedman was right. What I want is a diagnosis of why the decades-long argument over this view has stalled. In my own field, when an argument lasted that long without movement, it usually meant the parties were answering different questions under a shared vocabulary. Is that what is happening here? Is the disagreement about a factual claim about how good science is actually practiced, about a normative standard for what scientists ought to do, about what an assumption is, or about something even more basic, such as what a theory is for? Do the two sides disagree about what counts as data, about what counts as an explanation, or about starting intuitions that neither can argue for? And, given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction that dissolves the problem, an empirical study, a formal result, a better example, or simply abandoning the question? I would rather have an honest answer that the debate is ill-posed than a forced resolution.
