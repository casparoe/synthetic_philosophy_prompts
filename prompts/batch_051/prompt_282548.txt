I spent my career as a geochemist, and in retirement I have been working through the Platonic dialogues. There is a line of argument in the Meno that I cannot decide about, and I would be glad of a careful resolution.

Socrates convinces Meno that true belief is no less useful than knowledge for guiding action: a person who correctly believes the road to Larissa gets you there just as reliably as one who knows it. Yet Socrates then insists that knowledge is prized far more highly than true belief. His stated ground, as I understand it, is that true beliefs are unstable and apt to wander off unless fastened down by reasoning about the explanation; knowledge is true belief made fast in this way, and therefore stays present and usable when needed.

Now suppose a development that Plato could not have imagined, but which is routine in my old laboratory. A person's factual memory is externalized: a device, or a well-kept notebook and instruments, reliably returns both the correct answer and its justification on demand for every question in some domain. The person himself has only an untethered true belief—he could not give the explanatory account. Yet the composite of person and external store is at least as stable as an expert's internal knowledge: it does not drift, it does not forget, and it guides action at least as well. By Socrates's stated criterion, the externalized system seems to pass; by any intuitive criterion, the person inside it does not know.

I want to know what happens to the Meno argument under this changed assumption, not whether such a device is currently feasible. Please do three things. First, state precisely which premise of the Socratic argument the external-store case puts under pressure. Second, say whether the argument survives, needs repair, or is dead; if it can be repaired, give the strongest repaired version and state what it preserves. Third, spell out what the repaired version says about someone who knows nothing in a domain but possesses a flawless device—if it says he lacks something valuable, say exactly what that something is, and whether it is an epistemic value at all or a different one.

Please end with a single machine-readable line of the form \answer{...}. Inside the braces put one of the labels survives, needs repair, or dead, followed by a semicolon and a phrase of no more than ten words summarizing the outcome.
