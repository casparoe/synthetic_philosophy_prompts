In my philosophy of mind course we've been going through the access vs phenomenal consciousness distinction and global workspace theory, and I've settled on this working definition: a state is access conscious iff it gets broadcast in a global workspace and is thereby available to systems for verbal report, planning, memory, and action control, rather than just being locally available to one module. But two cases are bugging me. First: a human-level AI whose workspace is functionally just like this, and it reports, plans, and acts on the broadcast states, but by stipulation has zero phenomenal character. Second: a person in a locked-in state who has vivid phenomenology but whose global workspace is broken enough that nothing is broadcast to reporting, planning, or action systems. Does each case count as access conscious under my criteria? And does either case show that my criteria are missing something essential, or just that I was conflating access with phenomenality from the start? Go criterion by criterion, and tell me whether the definition needs changing.
