My philosophy club advisor runs an exercise in which she presents two distributions of wellbeing across two equal-sized groups and tells us which she considers better. She writes each distribution as an ordered pair (L, H), with L ≤ H, where L is the less-well-off group and H is the better-off group; higher numbers mean better off. So far she has said that (5, 5) is better than (4, 6); (7, 8) is better than (4, 12); (9, 10) is better than (8, 11); (8, 12) is better than (7, 13); and (6, 20) is better than (5, 21). Next session's case is a pure leveling-down comparison: is (8, 8) better than, worse than, or just as good as (8, 12)? I am less interested in the bare answer than in the rule that makes her earlier verdicts hang together. Predict what she will say, identify which candidate rules fit her earlier verdicts (prioritarianism, a pluralist egalitarianism that values equality itself, leximin, or something else), say what each predicts about the leveling-down case, and explain which further comparison would best settle the ambiguity that remains.
