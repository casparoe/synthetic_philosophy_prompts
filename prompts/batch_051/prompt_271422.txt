I'm a PhD student in environmental ethics and I keep going in circles on the standard argument against habitat destruction. As I was taught it, the argument runs: destroying a habitat harms, displaces, or kills the wild animals in it; harming animals is bad; so habitat destruction is wrong. But there's a newer line of thinking, mostly from people worried about wild animal suffering, that says we shouldn't just assume a wild life is on balance worth living. Lots of small fast-breeding species produce enormous numbers of offspring and most of those individuals die painfully before adulthood—from starvation, predation, disease, exposure. If the typical wild animal's life is net negative, then the standard argument starts to look like it might point the wrong way: a preserved habitat isn't exactly protecting a population of contented inhabitants, it's largely producing more short, suffering lives; on that picture, clearing a patch of forest or draining a marsh could reduce total suffering by preventing those lives from starting at all.

I'm not trying to settles whether that empirical picture is true; what's bugging me is conceptual. What exact premise does the preservationist argument need about wild animal welfare for the argument to go through? Is the needed premise just that wild lives are on average worth living, or can the argument be repaired so it doesn't depend on any such claim at all—say by leaning on biodiversity, ecosystem integrity, existence value, or the idea that the system itself has value? If it gets repaired that way, is it still the same argument or has it changed the subject? And when people say things like 'the ecosystem would have lower net welfare after the cleanup,' is that itself confused—do ecosystems have a welfare at all, or is that just aggregated individual welfare with a misleading label?

Basically: does the standard argument survive this development, does it need repair, or is it dead if the net-negative picture is right? Please spend a good chunk of the answer on what the argument's hidden premise is, and on whether the repaired versions are doing the same normative work or just dodging the question.
