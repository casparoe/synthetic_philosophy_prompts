Putnam concluded from this that meanings cannot be in the head: because the two are identical on the inside while their words refer differently, what a word refers to must be settled by facts outside the speaker. It follows that even in 1750, when neither speaker had any idea about the hidden structure, the two meant different things by the very same word, despite sharing every belief, intention, and brain state.
