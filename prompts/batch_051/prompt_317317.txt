A taxon clears it iff there's positive evidence for the kind of machinery suffering needs: flexible/associative learning, some centralized-ish integration of nociceptive signals, and behavioral tradeoffs that look pain-like rather than reflexive. Bees clear it. I'd put ants and wasps at lower confidence but still above it.

F. Which means taxa without that evidence default to ~0 for me; the burden of proof is on the claim that they suffer. Mere nociceptors and reflex withdrawal aren't enough. This is why I don't factor aphids, thrips, or rootworm larvae into my reasoning about pesticide policy, even though the raw numbers are enourmous.

G. In practice I'd support requiring anesthesia for invasive neuroscience on bees, and dropping broad-spectrum sprays where it protects pollinators; but I'm also fine swatting a fly at a picnic.
