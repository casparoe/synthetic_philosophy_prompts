In a small Bible study on Romans, I offered a reading of a contested passage and someone shut me down with the slogan “Scripture interprets Scripture.” He took it to mean that my reading was wrong because it didn’t match other texts, and that the clearer passages settle the obscure ones. I later found the confession this slogan usually traces to: the Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 1, Section 9, states in substance that the infallible rule for interpreting Scripture is Scripture itself. That didn’t clarify anything for me. I want you to separate the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making—about the canon’s internal consistency, about whether Scripture supplies its own method of interpretation, about the competence of the ordinary reader, about what makes a “clear” passage, about the role of human judgment when two passages are made to interpret each other—and say for each whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false. Then tell me which of those readings would actually support his move against my reading, and which readings would not support it or would even undermine it. Finally, because I’m asking you to adjudicate, tell me what would change your mind: which consideration about interpretive regress—for example, about who decides what counts as “clear,” or about how a canon’s internal cross-references are themselves selected—would most likely overturn your verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
