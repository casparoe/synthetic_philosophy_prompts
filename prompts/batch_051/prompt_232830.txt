In the last year, an incongruity theorist of humour and a benign violation theorist of humour have argued past each other so consistently that they have agreed to produce an adversarial-collaboration document instead of another exchange of papers. Your task is to draft that document for them. They begin from the following characterisations, each of which its proponent is willing to sign: the incongruity theorist maintains that humour consists, at bottom, in the perception and resolution of an incongruity — a violation of an expectation, category boundary, or conceptual norm, entertained in the right cognitive frame — and that the distinctively comic pleasure is the pleasure of that resolution; the benign violation theorist maintains that humour consists in something that simultaneously strikes one as a violation (of a norm, a rule, a boundary, or a sense of how things ought to be) and as benign, and that incongruity is neither necessary nor sufficient once violation and benignness are in place. You may refine or amend these formulations so long as each side would accept the result.

The document you draft should include: (1) a numbered list of claims on which both parties can sign without reservation, including claims about specimens they both classify correctly, about the insufficiency of mere surprise, and about what superiority and relief theories fail to capture; (2) the precise residual disagreements, stated in wording each side would accept for its own view, and a clear separation of disagreements about the phenomena from disagreements that are merely verbal; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side — with, for each crux, a concrete specimen (a joke, cartoon, or carefully described non-joke scenario) that both sides agree bears on it, plus each side's prediction about what the specimen shows and why; and (4) a short statement from each side of what it would count as evidence or argument against its own position, and whether that evidence would change its mind or merely prompt a qualification. The crux section should include at least one crux about whether the benignness condition does independent explanatory work beyond the cognitive frame that incongruity theorists already invoke, and one about whether there are genuinely funny cases in which an expectation-scheme is violated but no norm is violated, or vice versa; do not preserve these as cruxes if the collaborative drafting shows they were only verbal. Write the resulting document in American English.
