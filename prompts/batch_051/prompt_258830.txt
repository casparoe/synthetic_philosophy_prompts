In C. S. Peirce’s “The Fixation of Belief,” he writes: “The object of reasoning is to find out, from the consideration of what we already know, something else which we do not know.” I’m a PhD student working on epistemic value, and I keep coming back to this line because it makes truth-seeking look like an intrinsic orientation toward enlarging cognition rather than a mere instrument for action or preference satisfaction. What is your actual credence that truth-seeking—the disposition to expend resources on acquiring true beliefs and avoiding false ones—is ultimately instrumental rather than intrinsically valuable for finite agents like us? I don’t want a survey of positions; I want a number and its decomposition. In particular, how much of your credence rides on whether Peirce’s claim is merely descriptive of actual reasoning versus normative about how inquiry ought to aim? How much rides on whether evolutionary or cultural explanations of why truth-seeking presents itself as an intrinsic value undercut its genuine normativity? How much rides on whether there is any single final good to which truth-seeking reliably conduces? Assign rough probabilities to these or the sub-questions you take to matter, show how they combine, and identify which sub-question would most move your final number.
