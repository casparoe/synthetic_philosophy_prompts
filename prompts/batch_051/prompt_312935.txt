I've had a rule for a while and I can't tell whether it's a genuine insight or a bias I've dressed up in probability talk. Whenever I think about how long humanity will last, I treat myself as a random draw from all the humans who will ever live. That means I should expect my birth rank — I'm roughly the hundred-billionth human — to be somewhere in the middle of the pack, not in the first few percent. So if the future contains trillions of people spread across the galaxy, I'd have to be wierdly early, and my rule says I shouldn't expect that. I end up thinking near-term extinction is a lot more likely than my friends do, and I've made real choices around it: I under-save for retirement, I don't bother with long-horizon projects, that sort of thing. I know this sounds like a whole worldview built on one assumption, but I can't find the flaw myself. Is this policy defensible? Under what assumptions about reasoning from my own existence would it actually work, and where does it break down? And is there a version of the rule that keeps whatever is right in it without the doomy conclusion — in particular, how much should depend on what class I take myself to be a random member of?
