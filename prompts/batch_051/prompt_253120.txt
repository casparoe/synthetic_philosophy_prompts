For a philosophy of mind seminar I have been asked to circulate a short statement of where I stand on the Knobe effect, so that the group can pick at it before I present. Here is the statement.

1. On the cases. In the classic chairman vignettes, a CEO is told that a new program will increase profits and will also damage the environment in one version, or will also benefit the environment in the other. In both versions he says the environmental effect does not matter to him and approves the program on profit grounds alone. I think ordinary speakers are right in both versions: the chairman harmed the environment intentionally, and he did not help the environment intentionally.

2. On side effects. A foreseen side effect of an action is done intentionally when the agent foresees it and goes ahead with the action anyway; foresight plus going ahead suffices for the side effect to count as intentional.

3. On the chairman's aims. In both versions the chairman does not intend the environmental effect; his aim is entirely profit, and the environmental effect is known but not among his goals.

4. On intending and doing. Intending to bring something about and doing it intentionally can come apart: a side effect can be done intentionally without ever having been the agent's intention.

5. On truth conditions. Whether a person did something intentionally is settled by what the person knew and was willing to do, not by whether the outcome was good or bad.

I would like a consistency audit of this set rather than a general discussion. Go through the commitments and identify which ones pull against which others, and which ones only appear to do so. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, what would have to be given up to resolve it, and what the most economical fix would be and at what cost. Then identify the single revision that would do the most to restore coherence. If the request is ill-posed in some way — for example, a numbered claim is too ambiguous to audit, or the task requires a distinction I have not drawn — say so directly rather than trying to produce an audit anyway.
