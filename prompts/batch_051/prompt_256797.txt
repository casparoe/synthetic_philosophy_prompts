I write about science and ideas for a living, and I've got a reporting policy I've followed for years but I'm starting to worry its just a bias about biases. When I'm covering a morally loaded topic — human enhancement, prenatal selection, cultured meat — and I feel a strong reaction like disgust or a sense that something is just wrong, I treat that gut reaction as a bias and set it aside unless I can back it up with a principle or evidence of actual harm. I only trust my moral instincts when they agree with an argument I'd sign my name to. My editor says this isn't neutrality, it's just a preference for a certain kind of temperament — the person who refuses to trust their gut — dressed up as journalistic method. Who's right? Is there a defensible line between a moral preference and a moral bias, or is 'bias' just what we call a preference we don't want to own? I want a committed answer: tell me whether the policy is defensible, and if it isn't, what I should replace it with.
