I am putting together a film-and-philosophy seminar and want to teach Darren Aronofsky's Black Swan through Merleau-Ponty's account of the lived body and motor intentionality. The reading I am taking seriously is this: Merleau-Ponty holds that skilled movement is not governed by a mental representation of what the body should do, but is a pre-reflective bodily grip on the world, a mastery carried in posture, balance, rhythm, and the felt possibilities of the moving body, and it tends to degrade when self-conscious monitoring takes over. On this reading, Black Swan is less a story about ambition or repressed desire than about the collapse of that bodily grip under a regime of mirrors, correction, and observation, and Nina's failure in the Black Swan role is not a lack of technique but an inability to inhabit the role as a bodily possibility rather than mimicking an image while watching herself from outside. I want a full reading of the film through this lens, but one that is candid about where it strains. Which scenes best dramatize the difference between inhabiting a movement and monitoring it from outside, and which scenes suggest the film is tracking something else, such as a breakdown of the body image, or a relation to one's own body that Merleau-Ponty's confidence in the pre-reflective does not easily accommodate? Does the film ultimately vindicate the primacy of the lived body's pre-reflective grasp, or does it present a case in which the body becomes alien and persecutory in a way that sits badly with the account? End by identifying the interpretive forks where your reading is most likely to fail, and say which alternative reading of the film or of Merleau-Ponty would do most to change your overall verdict.
