I serve on the governing council of a territory that has governed itself for more than a decade. We control a defined territory, have a settled population, collect taxes, and operate our own courts and police. Yet no foreign government will exchange ambassadors with us, and almost none will accept our passports or sign treaties with us. A few states maintain unofficial trade offices in our capital while formally denying that we are a state.

Within the council, one faction argues that our statehood does not depend on any of this: we satisfy the standard criteria of statehood, and diplomatic recognition merely records a status that already exists. The other faction argues that recognition is not a mere formality—it partly constitutes statehood, because an entity becomes a state only through acceptance into the society of states. On this view, our ambiguous position is exactly what one would expect while that acceptance is withheld.

Which of these positions is more defensible, and how should we interpret the conduct of the states that trade with us while denying that we are a state?
