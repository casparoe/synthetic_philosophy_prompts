I'm giving a talk next month to an adult Sunday school class at a church that is theologically conservative but genuinely mixed on Bible translations. The room will include a handful of people who are close to King James Only, the kind who will argue that the KJV is the providentially preserved English text and that modern versions are built on corrupted manuscripts, plus a larger group who use an ESV or NASB without thinking much about it, and the pastor, who went to a conservative seminary and knows the formal vs. dynamic equivelance debates inside and out. My thesis is that "inspired translation" is a confused category. Inspiration attaches to the texts as originally given, not to translations, and that is okay because a text can be authoritative, and can genuinely mediate the word of God to a congregation, without itself being inspired. That is roughly how the early church treated the Septuagint, the Greek translation of the Hebrew scriptures that the apostles and early Christians used as their everyday Bible and quoted as authoritative, without, so far as I can tell, claiming it was inspired in the same way the Hebrew originals were. So I want to reject the KJV-only move that one English translation uniquely carries inspiration or its preservation, and I also want to resist the opposite move of saying that every faithful translation is inspired, since that waters the idea down until it stops doing any work. I'm not sure the middle position is stable, and that is the point of the talk.
