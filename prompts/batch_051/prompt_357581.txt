I keep bouncing between totalism about population value—value is just the sum of everyone's well-being, no matter whose—and a person-affecting view, the kind where creating an extra life worth living is neutral rather than good, and where an outcome's being worse has to be worse for someone. These two argue past each other constantly, so I want the document a real adversarial collaboration between them should have produced. Structure it as: a numbered list of claims about population ethics both camps can sign as-is; then a table of the remaining disagreements, with each side's entry worded so that side would say 'yeah, that's my actual view, not the strawman'; then two or three cruxes such that if one side changed its answer, the whole document would shift. For each crux, give a concrete population allocation both sides agree is diagnosic, and record each camp's prediction about it in advance. My money's on cruxes like: is adding a life worth living to an otherwise fixed world good or merely not-bad? Is the repugnant conclusion (where an enormous population of lives barely worth living beats a small population of excellent lives) a reductio or just a bullet to bite? Does it even make sense to call one outcome worse when the individuals in it wouldn't exist under the alternative? And if part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part. No citations or bibliography—I want the positions worked out, not the literature.
