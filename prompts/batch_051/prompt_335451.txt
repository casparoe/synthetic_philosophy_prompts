I keep turning over a parallel between scapegoat rituals and exception handling in code, and I can't decide whether it's deep or just a cute analogy. On the ritual side, a community's diffuse guilt, blame, and aggression get funneled onto one victim, who is expelled or killed, and afterward, at least for a while, the group comes out cleaner and more unified even though the victim usually had little to do with the original conflicts. On the programming side, exceptions from a bunch of different points in a program get caught and routed to one handler, which absorbs them and lets the program carry on as if nothing went wrong. Both seem to be ways of gathering up messy, distributed badness and concentrating it in a single place so the system doesn't have to deal with each bit locally. Is there a real structural indentity here, or does the comparison fall apart once you look at what 'guilt' and 'exceptions' actually are? Map the two sides, say what would have to be true for a scapegoat rite to actually work like a catch block, and name at least one thing the analogy predicts about scapegoating that looks false in practice.
