Recently I rewatched the film 12 Angry Men with a friend, and I keep thinking about the way the jury actually arrives at its verdict. The picture opens with eleven jurors ready to convict the boy and one holding out. What struck me this time was that the eleven do not seem to share a single reason for saying guilty. One is moved by the old man's testimony, another by the woman across the street, another by the supposed uniqueness of the knife, and several seem moved mostly by impatience. For most of them, the word 'guilty' bundles together a set of separate claims that none of them has ever examined on its own. As the holdout forces them to pull the bundle apart, the case begins to unravel. It made me wonder whether the film is dramatising, in reverse, something I have since read about under the name of judgment aggregation and the discursive dilemma: the fact that a group can have a majority in favour of each individual premise and yet not in favour of the conclusion those premises support, so that the group's collective stance is inconsistent even though no individual member is.

I would like you to write a piece of criticism that reads the film through that lens. Where does it genuinely display the dilemma, and where does it resist the formal picture? The formal problem seems to assume that people's individual judgments sit still and are then combined by a rule, whereas the film is about judgments changing under argument, pressure and the heat of the room. Does the unanimity requirement steer this particular jury away from the inconsistency the dilemma describes, or does it merely impose a different method of combination, one that produces coherence by talk rather than by counting? And when the jury finally speaks with one voice, are we entitled to treat that single verdict as 'the group's judgment' in the way we would treat an individual's considered belief? I would like the piece to take both the film and the idea seriously, to notice where the film resists the reading, and to end by saying what the film shows that a table of votes cannot. Please write the answer in British English.
