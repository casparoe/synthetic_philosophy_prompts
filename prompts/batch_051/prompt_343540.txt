so I've been trying to write down what I actually believe about health care for a discussion group, and I keep ending up with a list that sounds fine item by item but maybe doesn't hold together as one view. run an audit on it for me:

1. everybody should be guaranteed care for genuinely catastrophic, unpredictable illness or injury, paid for collectively, with no cost at the point of use.
2. routine and chronic care, like annual physicals, blood pressure meds, and insulin, should mostly come out of pocket or from a health savings account, because insuring a predictable expense isnt insurance, it's just prepayment.
3. health care markets fail for emergencies and serious illness because you can't shop around while you're having a heart attack.
4. but where the patient pays and can compare, like LASIK or dental cleanings or cosmetic procedures, markets do fine.
5. health is not special the way people usually say it is. my reason for wanting the catastrophic guarantee is that nobody should be ruined by bad luck, not that health is intrinsically different from, say, housing.
6. the state can require everyone to carry catastrophic coverage; otherwise free-riders show up at the emergency room and the rest of us pay anyway.
7. the state should be allowed to charge more from people whose choices raise their risk, like smokers, and it shouldn't have to subsidize the predictable consequences of lifestyle choices.

tell me which of these genuinely conflict and which only look like they conflict, rank the genuine conflicts by severity, and for each say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs you. then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I honestly cant tell whether this is one coherent position or just a bunch of slogans stapled together. answer in American English please.
