I am a retired evolutionary geneticist with a recent serious interest in philosophy of biology, and I have been asked to set the essay question for the final exam of an upper-level undergraduate course in that area. Please write one essay question on the units-of-selection debate—gene's-eye, organism-level, and group-level views. The question should be hard to answer by reciting the standard Dawkins-versus-Gould summaries: it should give students a stipulated toy example (for instance, an allele that biases its own transmission into gametes while reducing the fertility of the organisms carrying it) and require them to work out what a gene's-eye account, an organism-level account, and a multilevel-selection account each say about the allele's fate and about what is being selected. Then provide a model answer of roughly 700 words and a marking scheme worth 20 marks that itemizes the moves an A answer must make and the specific confusions that should cost marks—for example, treating gene selection and organism selection as competing empirical predictions rather than alternative bookkeeping, or assuming group selection must be kin selection in disguise. After the marking scheme, give a one-line note on the main misconception the question is designed to catch. End the whole reply with a one-sentence summary of the crux, written as \answer{...}.
