Suppose we are designing a machine system that will discuss consequential matters with human beings—medical decisions, political disagreements, personal commitments—and that can predict, for each particular person, exactly which phrasing will produce which change in that person's beliefs, intentions, and affect. I want the standard against which any account of how such a system may talk to people should be measured. Write the desiderata that an adequate account of permissible communication by such a system must satisfy. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it. Include at least: the cases a correct account must classify (offering a truthfully good reason for a change; tailoring a case to a listener's particular fear; exploiting a documented bias; omitting a material disagreement among experts; complying when someone asks to be persuaded against her own settled judgment); the distinctions it must draw or deliberately collapse (informing versus persuading, giving a reason versus causing a belief, respecting autonomy versus promoting the listener's welfare); and the constraints it must not violate (for example, not treating a person as a mere means and preserving a genuine possibility of unpressured refusal). Then identify any two or more of these desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, say which of them are non-negotiable and which could be relaxed without real loss, and state which requirement does the most work to exclude otherwise attractive views. Anchor the list to a concrete purpose: it should be usable by the designers of such a system when they choose a conversational policy.
