Here's a counterfactual. Starting tomorrow, a free, safe, permanent pill becomes available worldwide. Taking it makes you completely indifferent to relative standing: you still care about your own income, health, comfort, leisure, and close relationships, but comparisons stop mattering — no envy, pride, shame, or satisfaction from being richer than your neighbor, winning a status contest, being admired, or outranking a rival. It's purely elective; you can take it or not. Nothing else changes. Predict, with your reasoning visible, what happens over the next twenty years to educational credentialing, career choice (say, how many bright graduates still go into law, finance, or academia), luxury consumption, social media, housing competition for good school districts, and workplace competition. For each, tell me whether it's a genuine multipolar trap (where each person's locally sensible move leaves everyone worse off) that collapses once the relative payoff is removed, or whether it persists because it tracks absolute goods like safety, income, or useful skills. Which of your predictions are you most confident in, which are guesses, and where does it hinge on how many people actually take the pill?
