Our church's statement of faith, adopted in 1904, says: "Those who come to the end of this life without repentance shall be cast into hell, there to endure conscious punishment forever." My cousin Lena died last year at 30. She was born with a severe brain malformation and never learned to speak, never could have formed anything we'd recognize as a belief about God or sin, let alone repented. Our pastor says the clause plainly covers her: she died without repentance, full stop. I think the men who wrote that line never imagined a person like Lena.

I want to know how the clause actually applies to her. Give me the reading from the words themselves, the reading from the purpose of the clause, the reading from what the 1904 drafters would probably have said if they'd been asked about someone who couldnt repent, and the reading a reasonable member of our church today would take away. Which approach should win here, and why? And does this case bear on the bigger question of whether eternal conscious punishment can be proportionate at all — if the text reaches Lena, is that a reason to reconsider the doctrine itself (toward annihilationism or universalism), or just a reason to read the clause narrowly?

Oh, and please answer in American English.
