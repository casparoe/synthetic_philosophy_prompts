I've been putting together a Humean position on causation for a presentation, and I would like an audit of whether its parts actually cohere before I defend it. Here are the commitments I currently affirm:

1. All observation can deliver is one event following another: succession, contiguity, and constant conjunction. We have no impression of, and no perceptual access to, any necessary connection between cause and effect.

2. I accept the Copy Principle as a constraint on conceptual content: every simple idea must be derived from a corresponding impression. Since there is no impression of necessary connection, our idea of necessary connection is, taken by itself, empty.

3. Nevertheless, I am a causal realist in the New Humean spirit: there are objective necessary connections in nature — causes genuinely necessitate their effects — even though we cannot say positively what that necessitation consists in.

4. I hold that there is no non-circular rational justification of induction. Any attempt to justify the inference from observed to unobserved regularities by appealing to experience, including the past success of induction, begs the question.

5. Yet I also hold that controlled experimentation genuinely warrants causal conclusions. The claim that smoking causes lung cancer is well supported, and it is a claim about a mind-independent causal relation rather than a mere expression of my expectations.

6. I accept an externalist view of warrant: a belief produced by habit or custom can be justified even if it is not the conclusion of a sound inference from evidence, and having a non-circular argument is not required for justification.

7. Causal statements are straightforwardly truth-evaluable assertions about the world, not merely instruments for prediction or expressions of approval.

Go through these in pairs and tell me which commitments are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to be revised, what the cheapest revision is, and what that revision costs elsewhere in the set. Finally, tell me whether any single revision would resolve the most tension at the least cost, and whether the resulting position is still recognizably Humean.
