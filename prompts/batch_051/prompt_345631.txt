I spent last night arguing with a friend about whether two people who fundamentally disagree about values can reach a compromise that is anything more than one side giving in. By the end I was convinced that there is such a thing as moral gains from trade: two parties with partly incompatible value systems can exchange concessions, each giving ground on a commitment it cares about comparatively less in return for the other giving ground on a commitment it cares about comparatively more, and the resulting arrangement can be better in a sense both can recognize without either abandoning their view. In two weeks I defend this at an interdisciplinary workshop. The room will be roughly half welfare economists, who will hear "trade" and immediately translate it into preference-satisfaction or utility, and half political philosophers, several of them value pluralists in the tradition of Berlin who deny that values can be ranked on a single scale, plus one or two strict deontologists. Give me the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased the way each group would actually put them, with what I should concede up front, what I should hold, the weakest point in my own position that I should prepare to defend rather than hope they miss, and the question I should be hoping nobody asks.
