Draft an adversarial-collaboration summary between a strict is–ought separatist and an ethical naturalist about whether descriptive facts can, by themselves, support normative conclusions. The separatist holds that no set of purely descriptive or natural facts entails—or by itself rationally supports—a normative conclusion, so every successful argument to a normative conclusion contains at least one irreducibly normative premise; attempts to delete that premise by defining moral terms naturalistically either change the subject or beg the question. The naturalist holds that moral facts are natural facts, and that empirical facts about well-being, flourishing, preferences, or function can both entail and rationally support moral conclusions once the relevant naturalistic bridge principles are explicit or built into the meanings of moral terms.

Produce the document such a collaboration would actually issue. Begin with a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, including the formal points they do not dispute: what deductive logic does and does not permit without a bridge principle; what an acceptable bridge premise would have to contain; that the bare charge of "committing the naturalistic fallacy" settles nothing by itself; and anything else the two sides genuinely share. Then give a disagreements section, in the form of a table or parallel formulations, in which each residual dispute is stated exactly; for each disagreement, supply a separatist formulation and a naturalist formulation such that each side would accept the wording as a fair statement of its own view. Then identify the cruxes: the specific sub-questions such that a settled answer to them would move at least one side; for each crux, record in advance what each side predicts and what argument, case, or discovery both sides agree would count as evidence against their own view.

Throughout, separate substantive disagreement from verbal disagreement. State where the two sides mean different things by "entails," "natural fact," "normative premise," or "naturalistic fallacy"; say whether the residual disagreement is best located in logic, language, metaphysics, or epistemology; and flag any formulation of the other side's view that would be too slanted for that side to sign. The deliverable is a constructive text both camps could put their names to, with the remaining disagreement made exact—not a bibliography and not a verdict on who is right.
