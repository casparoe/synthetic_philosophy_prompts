Take the ethics of randomised controlled trials as a design problem: before I choose among the existing positions, I want the full specification of what an adequate position has to do. Write the desiderata that an adequate theory in this area must meet — a theory of when it is permissible to randomise a patient between treatments, to use a placebo control, and to keep a trial running as the data accumulate. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it; mark which are non-negotiable and which are only one camp's preference; and say explicitly whether any subset of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied. Among the cases the list must handle are a clinician whose own judgement has begun to lean toward one arm while the expert community remains genuinely divided; a placebo-controlled trial where a proven effective treatment exists; a trial continuing after interim data show a trend toward one arm; and a trial conducted in a setting where the control arm would count as substandard care in the country running the trial. Then state, in a sentence or two, which single requirement does the most to exclude the familiar positions — the strict therapeutic obligation, individual equipoise, community equipoise, and consent-based views — and why. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Write in British English.
