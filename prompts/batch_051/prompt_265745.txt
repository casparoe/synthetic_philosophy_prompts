In my civics class we moved from ordinary negligence to the rules about suing the government, and I keep coming back to an asymmetry that seems hard to justify. Suppose a careless fire started by a private citizen burns down someone's shed; if the facts are proven, the citizen must pay. Now suppose the same careless fire is started by a government employee who is on the clock doing something official — backing a city truck onto dry weeds, say — and the same shed burns down. In many legal systems the government can raise sovereign immunity; even where it has waived that immunity, whole categories of official conduct remain protected, and the victim may be left with whatever an administrative claims process or the legislature decides to give, not with a court judgment. The physical facts are identical: negligence, causation, damage. Only the legal identity of the defendant differs.

The traditional rationale I have seen paraphrased is that the sovereign is the source of the courts and therefore cannot be called a wrongdoer in its own forum. But that sounds less like a reason than a restatement of the immunity: it explains why the state can shield itself, not why a private victim should absorb a loss the state caused. The other candidate justifications are that state funds belong to everyone, that a judgment against the treasury would make courts direct the spending of public money, and that allowing suits might make officials timid. It is as though the law refuses to say, in so many words, that the state itself did wrong, even when every element of a private tort is present and the facts are conceded. I do not yet see why any of those considerations should defeat the victim's claim when the state has acknowledged that no one else caused the harm.

So I want the strongest principle that actually justifies treating a wrong differently just because it flows through the state — and I want honesty about whether it works. If you defend the asymmetry, defend it against the case where the negligence is identical down to the exact moment the fire starts, and against the objection that the victim is being made to pay a kind of forced contribution to the public. If you cannot defend it, say whether it should be given up entirely or kept only for some narrow class of decisions. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
