So here's a word I can't actually define even though I use it all the time: complicity. I'll confidently say a CEO is complicit in pollution his company does even if he never saw the pipe, and I'll confidently say a random guy isn't when his stolen laptop gets used in a scam. But the boundary gets weird fast. Give me a working definition of complicity (or "being complicit in wrongdoing") and run it through these cases.

Cases:

1. A getaway driver for a bank robbery. He never goes inside, never carries a weapon, just waits in the car and drives. Clearly yes.

2. A woman who lies to police to give her husband an alibi for a murder, after it happened. He'd already done it; she didn't know beforehand. Seems yes.

3. A cashier at a hardware store who sees a man a hammer, and the man uses it to kill his neighbor. She had no idea. Clearly no.

4. A voter who backs a candidate mainly for tax policy, even though the candidate's platform includes family separation at the border. The voter finds that policy repellent but votes anyway.

5. Someone who pays taxes in a democracy that is committing war crimes abroad. If she writes to her representatives and votes against the war, does that change anything?

6. A data scientist at a social media company. Her work is on playlist recommendations; the same company's other teams run experiments widely blamed for radicalizing users.

7. A guy who laughs along at a racist joke at work because he wants to fit in and doesn't want to make it weird, even though he thinks it's gross.

8. A factory worker who sews soles onto shoes in a plant that the government uses to launder money; the shoes are real and useful, and it's the only job in town.

Now, questions to wrangle with in the definition: does complicity require that the person actually made the wrongdoing more likely or worse? Or is it enough to know and not object? Does it require sharing the wrongdoer's goal? Does benefiting from the wrong matter? Could someone be complicit just by belonging to a group or being present? And does wether you speak out or vote against the thing change whether you're complicit, or only how blameworthy you are?

The deliverable is the definition itself: propose it, run it through the cases, and where it gives a wrong or no answer either revise it or defend the weird verdict.
