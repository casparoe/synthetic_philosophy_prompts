I'm a retired developmental biologist, and after forty years of watching embryos turn genes on in the right order I've gotten suspicious of anyone who says a trait is simply "in the genes." So naturally I've been reading the rationalist–empiricist fight over innate knowledge, and it drives me a little crazy how often the two sides are useing "innate" to mean different things and then acting surprised when they talk past each other. I'd like you to draft what an adversarial collaboration between two positions in that debate would actually produce. Position A is a modern nativist of the core-knowledge sort or the principles-and-parameters sort: the initial state includes rich, domain-specific representational structure, and learning is mostly the selection or enrichment of what's already there. Position B is a modern statistical-learning empiricist: the learner begins with powerful but domain-general machinery and genuinely builds the structure from the input, so early competence is a fact about how much can be gotten from surprisingly little.

The document should have four parts. First, a numbered Agreed Claims section that isn't a pile of truisms: what both sides would sign about what "innate" should and shouldn't mean if the term is to stay useful, about why the gene/environment distinction is the wrong frame, about which empirical phenomena are real and in need of explanation, and about what would not count as evidence for either side. Second, a Disagreements table: the claim under dispute, the wording each side would accept for its own view, and a column for whether the disagreement looks genuine or verbal. Third, a Cruxes section: the sub-questions where a settled answer would actually move at least one side, with each side's prediction registered in advance and a description of an experiment or argument both sides would count as bearing on it. Make sure at least one crux is the difference between representational innateness and merely architectural innateness, and at least one is about whether a controlled-rearing result—a newborn preference for something face-like, or a neonate looking-time pattern for number—licenses an innateness claim rather than a prior statistical computation. Fourth, end with a short diagnosis of which pieces of the usual debate are just verbal and which are substantive.
