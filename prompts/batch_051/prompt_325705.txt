I am a PhD student in formal epistemology, and I have been trying to isolate a difficulty that keeps arising in the way we model knowledge and belief. The standard possible-worlds treatment says an agent knows a sentence just in case that sentence holds throughout every world she cannot exclude. Everyone grants that this makes agents logically omniscient, and the familiar repairs include an awareness set delimiting which sentences the agent has in view, impossible worlds where logic behaves non-classically, and proof-based or resource-bounded constraints. But I keep noticing a pattern in these repairs for which I have no name. They tend to relocate the agent's fallibility to the boundary of some competence region: inside the awareness set, or outside the deviant worlds, or within the proof budget, the agent is modeled as perfectly classical—she draws every inference and appreciates every equivalence licensed by the resources the model grants her. The failure is always a matter of lacking an item, never of having two items in view and still failing to combine them. Yet actual confusion is often precisely of the second kind: a mathematician has both the premise and the lemma written on the board and does not see that the theorem follows; a reasoner holds two beliefs and never notices their inconsistency.

My question is whether this residual problem has an established name in the literature, or whether it is usually treated as one undifferentiated symptom of logical omniscience. If it has a name, I would like to know how the standard repairs relate to it and which fits it best; if it has none, I would like a proposed term and a definition that cleanly separates it from awareness-based fixes, impossible-worlds semantics, and resource-bounded reasoning. I am not satisfied with a bare label: I want a precise account of what the phenomenon is, where its edges are, and why the usual repairs fail to reach it. Please end with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing either the established name or, if none is established, your proposed term.
