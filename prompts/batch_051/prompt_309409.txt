I've been reading about the argument that the "Middle Ages," the "Renaissance," and the "Scientific Revolution" are mostly later inventions rather than real slices of the past. I find it convincing for the Middle Ages but I keep getting stuck on "renaissance," because the word seems to be doing a few different jobs at once. Sometimes it means a specific chunk of European history—Florence, Brunelleschi's dome, humanists rediscovering classical texts, roughly the 1300s to 1500s. Other times it just means any kind of cultural revival: the Carolingian Renaissance, the 12th-century Renaissance, the Harlem Renaissance, the Bengali Renaissance. And then there's "Renaissance man," which seems to mean a polished all-rounder, with no revival involved at all.

Give me a working defintion of "renaissance"—or a split into two or three definitions—that handles these cases: (a) Medici Florence in the 1400s; (b) the Harlem Renaissance in 1920s New York; (c) the Carolingian Renaissance under Charlemagne; (d) someone called a "Renaissance man" because he's good at math and painting; (e) a decade of rapid economic growth in a country that produces no notable art, science, or scholarship; (f) the Scientific Revolution in the 1600s—does it fall inside your definition, sit next to it, or does it depend?

Run each case through your definition and say where it gives a wierd answer or no answer, and then say whether the word is doing one job or several. If the Renaissance is a later invention, I also want to know which of these uses survive that point and which get exposed as anachronisms.
