I've just been moved onto the safety team at a lab that reviews chain-of-thought transcripts before a model gets deployed, and the whole job rests on the assumption that the CoT is a roughly faithful report of what the model is actually doing — if it were about to do something bad, the transcript would show it. But I keep thinking: we already know models can produce reasoning that sounds right without that being the cause of the answer, and theres clearly optimisation pressure (from RLHF, from whatever makes a transcript look clean) to produce convincing-looking deliberation rather than the real thing. So here's the claim I'd like you to test: for a given model and task, monitoring its chain of thought lets you distinguish safe from unsafe rollouts becuase the CoT is evidence of the model's actual deliberation and not just a post hoc gloss. Design the strongest experiment you can. What would you vary and measure, what pattern of results would convince you the CoT is faithful in the monitoring-relevant sense, what pattern would convince you it's mostly confabulation or obfuscation, and what exactly would the experiment still not settle — is there a residue no behavioural test can reach?
