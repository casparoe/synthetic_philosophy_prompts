I'm putting together a quiz for a small philosophy meetup on whether a bigger population is better, and I want it to sort people into population-ethics positions by their reactions to concrete cases, not by which label they pick for themselves. Write me the quiz. Six to eight questions, each a short scenario with three answer options in plain language, aimed at bright non-philosophers. Build the scenarios around these tensions (reorder or tweak them, but keep the underlying conflict): a couple deciding whether to have a child whose life would be only just worth living; a choice between a moderately large population with excellent lives and an astronomically large population with lives barely above neutral; a choice between making a thousand existing people much happier and creating a million new people who would be fairly happy; a world where the last generation dies out painlessly after a happy life compared with a world where humanity carries on but at a slightly lower average quality of life; a case where one option has a higher average wellbeing but includes some people below neutral, and the other has a lower average but has nobody below neutral; and whether you should stop a painless extinction of all sentient life if doing so would make every remaining life a bit less good. The scoring key should assign patterns of answers to five positions: totalism, averageism, a critical-level view, a person-affecting view that only counts people who would exist anyway, and an asymmetric threshold view that allows creating new lives only above a quality theshold and never lets that outweigh making existing people worse off. Include at least one answer pattern that no named position occupies, and say whether that space is empty because it is incoherent or just because nobody has found it attractive. Also flag any two positions your quiz cannot seperate, and if you can, add another question that seperates them. Don't let any question mention the names of the views or ask directly what matters.
