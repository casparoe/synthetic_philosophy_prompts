I am putting together a final assessment for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on the epistemology of intellectual dependence, and I would like you to write one essay question, with a model answer and a marking scheme. The question must be built around Plato's Phaedrus, in particular the exchange between Theuth and Thamus in which Thamus objects that writing will produce forgetfulness and give people the appearance of wisdom rather than wisdom itself. Do not quote the passage; paraphrase it if necessary.

The essay question should not be answerable by a general recitation of the Theuth and Thamus story or of familiar worries about AI and understanding. It should require students to specify the underlying mechanism by which reliance on an external aid could erode understanding, and to distinguish that from the mere outsourcing of a cognitive task that was never securely internalised. It should also force them to consider what changes when the external aid is an AI system that supplies a correct answer together with an explanation the user could have produced, rather than a written text that merely records a conclusion. The question should ask whether the Thamus objection is best read as an empirical prediction about memory, a conceptual point about the locus of knowledge, or a normative complaint about the kind of intellectual agency worth cultivating, and which of these readings carries the most weight in the AI case.

The model answer should be around 900 words. The marking scheme should separate a first-class answer from an upper-second and a lower-second by naming the specific distinctions and moves each must contain, and it should list three common student misunderstandings that should cost marks. Write the whole thing in British English.
