I run the dispute-intake unit of a municipal public-engagement office. Several times a month we receive a controversy: a proposed development that one side calls blight remediation and the other calls gentrification, a proposed mural called public art or vandalism, a new policing plan called community safety or overreach. My staff currently triage these cases by instinct, and we repeatedly spend our small fact-finding budget on disagreements that are not factual. I want a reusable intake procedure, grounded in Carnap's distinction between questions that arise inside a chosen linguistic framework and questions about which framework to adopt, together with his principle of tolerance about framework choice, so that a junior staff member with no philosophy background can classify and route any case within half a working day. Each case must end in a one-page issue map that the engagement director signs off before it goes to a city department; when a case is misclassified, we either waste an investigation or allow a genuine empirical question to be buried under a quarrel about words. In applying Carnap, treat questions of the first kind as those a framework's own criteria can settle by evidence, and questions of the second kind as choices to be made on pragmatic grounds — fruitfulness, clarity, convenience — rather than by appeal to which framework is "correct."

Write the procedure as a step-by-step checklist. It should classify each dispute as (a) internal to an agreed framework and therefore answerable by evidence, (b) a choice among frameworks to be settled pragmatically, or (c) mixed; include a short set of screening questions staff can ask in the first meeting and a rule for what belongs in the issue map rather than being sent back for reframing. Then work through three cases, one of which must be a case in which the procedure tells me not to pursue an investigation I would strongly prefer to pursue. Finally, state the places where the procedure cannot eliminate judgment, including disputes in which the parties agree on the facts but disagree about whose practical purposes should govern the choice of framework.

Write in American English.
