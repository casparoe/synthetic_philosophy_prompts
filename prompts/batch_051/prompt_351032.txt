Most legal systems treat the random selection of jurors as a cornerstone of justice even though those jurors are ordinary citizens with no legal training, and their verdict can take away a person's liberty. The same societies treat the proposal to select legislators by lot as a fringe curiosity, even among people who grant that elections reliably deliver unrepresentative career politicians. Both institutions hand binding public power to randomly chosen laypeople. Is there a principled line between the jury box and the legislature, or is the contrast just an inherited habit?
