I've been thinking about the way concepts in small, dense domains seem to shift when a new case arrives, and I'd like to work through a public example rather than toy cases. The picture I have in mind is this: a concept is often held together by central examples rather than a fixed checklist; new cases are judged by resemblance to those examples; and occasionally a copycat appears — something that imitates the outward marks of the central cases without sharing whatever made them central — and the community has to decide whether to stretch the concept, shrink it, or legislate a definition to stop the drift. The example I want to analyze is the reclassification of Pluto: the discovery of large trans-Neptunian objects such as Eris in the early 2000s, the ensuing debate over what counts as a planet, and the International Astronomical Union's 2006 vote, which adopted three criteria — orbiting the Sun, being massive enough to be roughly round, and having cleared the neighborhood around its orbit — and thereby demoted Pluto to dwarf-planet status while keeping the eight classical planets.

Please analyze that episode through the lens of this fluid-concepts picture. As I understand the facts worth foregrounding: before the controversy, planet was doing double duty as a scientific category and a cultural-historical one, and its extension had already been revised — Ceres was counted as a planet for a while after 1801 until similar bodies piled up, and Pluto was admitted in 1930 on an early size estimate that later had to be sharply revised. In 2006 the community faced the choice of adding a tenth member or drawing a line that would exclude the new objects; it chose the line, and Pluto fell on the excluded side of the definition's third criterion. I want the analysis to run in both directions. What does the fluid-concepts picture genuinely explain here — the role of exemplars, the way a copycat case forces the boundary question, the fact that the final definition had to be voted on rather than discovered? What does it misdescribe or leave out, and does the episode ultimately support the picture or show its limits? In particular: is the 2006 definition a repaired version of the old concept or a new concept keeping the old name, and is the cleared-the-neighborhood condition anything more than an ad hoc device to preserve the central examples while expelling the growing class of trans-Neptunian objects? I am not looking for a confident narrative; I would rather you mark the places where the theory's vocabulary is carrying the analysis and the places where it is only relabeling the obvious. Be explicit about who counts as the classifier, what constraints they were answerable to, and which bodies are the data. And if the premise is confused — if conceptual slippage is too loose to do this work, or Pluto is the wrong case for it — say so plainly instead of forcing the analysis.
