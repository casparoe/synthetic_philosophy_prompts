I help run a small grantmaking circle, and we are choosing between two mental-health programs. The first would fund a year of intensive therapy for about forty people with severe, treatment-resistant depression. The second would fund a low-cost online course for about twenty thousand people with mild anxiety. I am comfortable aggregating expected benefits within a single person's life, but I keep getting stuck at the step where I compare the size of a benefit in one mind with the size of a benefit in another. Both programs have data from standardized questionnaires, and I can see that different people report different numeric changes, but a point change on a depression inventory and a point change on an anxiety inventory are not obviously units of the same underlying quantity, and neither number directly measures how much the change matters to the person living it. Is there a principled way to compare this kind of benefit across people, or is the comparison itself a category mistake? If some such comparisons are legitimate while others are arbitrary, what distinguishes them?
