Exam question:

Explain the difference between a realist and a nominalist about universals. Is trope theory best classified as a form of realism or of nominalism? In your answer, discuss what it is for two distinct particular objects to be the same colour and say what each view tells us about this.

Student answer:

Realism about universals holds that properties such as redness are mind-independent entities that can be wholly present in many distinct particulars at the same time. So when two balls are both red, there is one thing, the universal redness, that both balls literally share. Nominalism denies that there are any such repeatable entities. Resemblance nominalism, for instance, says that a particular is red in virtue of resembling certain particular red objects, and that these red particulars form a class unified by primitive resemblance relations rather than by a single shared universal. Trope theory is usually classified as a form of realism, because tropes are real, non-linguistic properties: each ball has its own particular redness-trope, and the two balls are the same colour because those two tropes exactly resemble one another. But exact resemblance is itself something real that holds between many different pairs of tropes. Since any real relation that is present in many different pairs is a universal, trope theory ultimately has to admit at least one universal after all. Thus trope theory does not really avoid the realist's picture; it only relocates universals from ordinary properties to relations.
