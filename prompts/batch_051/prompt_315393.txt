I'm in a course on philosophical methodology right now, and we keep going back and forth about how much weight case intuitions should have against general principles. I've ended up with a view that I can't quite defend on the spot but also can't see what would genuinely count against it, so I'm asking you to do that for me.

The position is this: when a simple, powerful principle conflicts with an intuition about a particular case—or about a pair of cases—the principle should usually win. This includes cases where the principle is something like a transitivity axiom, or a simple maximizing principle, and some person's intuitive verdicts, or even my own, turn out to violate transitivity. My reasoning is that case intuitions look like evidence, but weak evidence: they flip with framing, order effects, and wording, whereas a simple principle can unify a whole mess of cases, and that sort of simplicity plus coherence is itself evidence that we're tracking something real. So I end up treating transitivity and simplicity as evidence, not just aesthetic preferences, and treating case intuitions as the kind of evidence you're allowed to override.

What I want from you: tell me what would count against this view. Not just the standard line that I'm being conservative or that I've rigged it against counterexamples—I want the stronger version. Sort it by how decisive it would be: what observations, psychological results, formal considerations, or philosophical arguments should actually move me off this? Which are merely awkward and which are, if true, fatal? Are there implications I'm now committed to that I probably wouldn't accept, like about disagreement, expertise, moral progress, or how often ordinary people are just wrong? And be honest about whether the view is insulated from refutation and whether that's a problem here. I'd personally find it most persuasive if you can show me either a case where the principle should clearly lose even by my own lights, or a reason to think the simplicity and coherence I'm leaning on are not truth-conducive rather than just not decisive.
