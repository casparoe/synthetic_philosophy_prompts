I am a PhD student giving a work-in-progress talk to our formal epistemology and decision theory workshop in three weeks, and I need a Q&A prep sheet. The paper defends this thesis: the Dutch book argument for probabilism is best read as a diagnostic argument. Susceptibility to a Dutch book shows that a set of degrees of belief has no coherent probabilistic representation, and that representational failure is itself an epistemic defect — a defect in how the state carves up logical space — rather than a merely financial liability. The bets in the setup are a measuring instrument: they make visible a defect that stays hidden while we describe the agent introspectively. The defect gives reason to revise even for an agent who is never actually offered the bets.

The audience is not a generic philosophy crowd. The workshop is mostly formal epistemologists who already accept probabilism on accuracy-based grounds and regard the Dutch book argument as a historically important but pragmatically confused relic; two decision theorists who think a degree of belief just is a fair betting price and will find my claim that bets are merely diagnostic an evasion; one person who works on imprecise probabilities and will say the right lesson of Dutch book pressure is to replace a single precise credence function with a set of them; and two faculty who are skeptical of any norm that requires a global, precise credence function over an arbitrary language. They are fluent in the formalism, and they are not the LessWrong crowd: an appeal to the shame of being exploited by a clever adversary will get me nowhere unless I can state the epistemic norm in terms they already take seriously.

I want the questions this audience will actually raise, ordered by expected damage, each phrased the way the relevant person would put it. For each: who asks it, whether I should concede or hold, a reply I can give in under two minutes, and where that reply is softest. Do not defend the paper for me; prepare me to defend it. Include the objection I should most hope nobody asks, and a line on how to survive if it comes. End with a single final line of the form \answer{...} containing a one-sentence summary of the position's weakest point overall.
