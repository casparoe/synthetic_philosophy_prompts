Our guideline committee keeps returning to Simpson-style reversals, in which a treatment's overall effect and its effect within subgroups point in opposite directions. The committee now has a small body of precedent on such cases, and I want a recommendation for a coming decision that coheres with that record, together with a diagnosis of the principle the record is actually tracking. The three decisions reached so far are these.

First, we assessed a treatment that looked worse than control overall but better within both women and men. Sex had been measured before treatment, the treatment arms differed in their sex composition, and the analysis by sex had been pre-specified in the analysis plan. We recommended the treatment for both sexes, on the ground that the overall comparison was confounded.

Second, we assessed a treatment that looked better than control overall but worse within each of three categories of a response measure taken after treatment had begun. That subgroup analysis had not been pre-specified. We followed the overall result, on the ground that a variable measured after treatment can lie on the causal path from treatment to outcome, so stratifying on it can manufacture or reverse an apparent effect.

Third, we assessed a study showing no overall difference but a benefit within each of four baseline severity bands. Severity had been measured before treatment, and the banded analysis had been pre-specified. We recommended treatment conditional on severity, on the ground that the null overall result concealed a real benefit that the pre-treatment variable was masking.

The coming case is this: a treatment looks better than control overall, but looks harmful within every category of a biomarker measured after treatment has started. The biomarker analysis was pre-specified, because the drug's mechanism is thought to run through that pathway and the biomarker is widely taken to lie downstream of the treatment. The committee must decide whether to recommend the treatment. Our earlier decisions do not settle which of two features should control: in each past case, the analyses we followed had been pre-specified and used pre-treatment strata, while the analysis we set aside was post-treatment and had not been pre-specified. The coming case is the first in which those features come apart.

What should we decide if we want to be consistent with ourselves? Which earlier decision is the closest analog, and if the precedents genuinely underdetermine the answer, what principle would we be adopting with each choice? I am asking for your best guess, not just a menu of options, and for some self-criticism: which further case, if we discovered we had decided it one way rather than the other, would most change your reading of our implicit rule, and where is your inference from our precedents most likely to fail?
