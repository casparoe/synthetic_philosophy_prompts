I help moderate a small housing co-op with a member-run listserv, and we've hit a real fight. One member has been posting stuff about how a certain religious group can never really belong in the neighborhood and that meetings with them are fake. Half the mod team wants him banned immediately, saying he's exactly the kind of intolerant person Karl Popper says you shouldn't tolerate. The other half says viewpoint bans make us the intolerant ones and the answer is more speech. After a year of going around in circles, we agreed to do an adversarial collaboration, and I got stuck drafting the joint statement.

The two positions are roughly: "no-viewpoint-banning" (we don't ban people for opinions, even hateful ones, absent a direct threat) and "self-defending tolerance" (we should suppress intolerance before it destroys the space). Could you draft the document both camps would actually sign? I'd like this format:

1. A numbered list of claims both camps can accept.
2. A disagreements section, maybe a table, with each remaining disagreement stated in neutral wording and then each side's own position worded in a way that side would accept.
3. For each disagreement, identify the crux: is it factual, value-based, or about interpretation of the Popper passage? What evidence or case would both camps agree bears on it, and what would each predict about it?
4. A short section on any part of the fight that is actually verbal rather than substantive.

The thing is, I don't want a generic moderation policy. I want it to actually engage the Popper text, because the two camps genuinely read it differently. In The Open Society and Its Enemies, Karl Popper writes: "But we should claim the right to suppress them [intolerant ideologies] if necessary even by force; for it may easily turn out that they are not prepared to meet us on the level of rational argument, but begin by denouncing all argument; they may forbid their followers to listen to rational argument, because it is deceptive, and teach them to answer arguments by the use of their fists or pistols."

The self-defending crowd thinks this justifies banning our member now, since he's denouncing the whole mode of argument and pulling others into a dynamic where debate can't function. The other crowd says Popper's line is specifically about people who are "not prepared to meet us on the level of rational argument," and our member still engages, even if nastily; they also say "suppress" and "even by force" means state action against movements, not a housing co-op moderating a listserv. So please make those disagreements exact. What precise claim does each side extract from the passage, and what would have to be true about our member's behavior for each side to actually switch? If part of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why.

At the very end, put a one-sentence summary of the residual non-verbal crux in \answer{...}.
