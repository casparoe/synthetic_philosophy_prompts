Write a deadpan Moderator's Handbook for the facilitated small-group sessions of a citizens' assembly on housing policy, convened by a municipality that has formally adopted a public-reason requirement: in session, participants may appeal only to reasons that all reasonable citizens could reasonably accept, and considerations drawn from religious or other comprehensive doctrines must be translated into freestanding public terms before they count as admissible. The handbook is the document a well-meaning facilitator would actually hand out: rules of the room, guidance on what counts as a public reason, a rubric or flowchart for deciding when a remark needs translation, sample moderator interventions, and a short FAQ.

The satire must come from getting the details of the position right rather than caricature. The jokes should be carried by the handbook's own deadpan provisions, which should embody — without ever stating in commentary — the familiar objections: the duty of civility hardening from a moral expectation into a procedural gag rule; the asymmetry between religious and non-religious comprehensive doctrines, which get treated as if they already spoke in public terms; the assumption that the approved secular translations are freestanding rather than themselves contestable; the unequal burden that translation places on believers; and the unresolved question whether consensus on one shared reason is required or convergence on compatible reasons suffices. The irony should stay deadpan throughout, but a reader should still be able to tell what is being ironized. Aim for 600–800 words.
