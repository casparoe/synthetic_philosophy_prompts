At my church youth group we've been stuck for months on wether a Bible translation can be "inspired," and I'm starting to think we're not even arguing about one thing. The KJV-only people say the King James itself is the preserved, perfect word of God, so other translations are corrupt. Others say inspiration only ever applied to the original Hebrew and Greek, and a translation is just an approximation. A third group says a translation can be inspired too, cause God's message comes through regardless of language. Someone always brings up that the New Testament writers seem to quote the Septuagint (a Greek translation) as if it's authoritative, which starts a whole side fight about whether quoting it means endorsing it as inspired. Every week the main argument cycles: someone points to a verse where the KJV says something the ESV changes, someone says that's a manuscript issue not an inspiration issue, someone says that just assumes God would preserve one perfect English translation, and then we restart. My hunch is this is secretly a fight about authority—who gets to say which text counts as "the Bible"—dressed up as a fight about what "inspired" means, but I'm not sure. So what kind of disagreement is this, and what would actually count as progress? I want the structure diagnosed, not just a verdict.
