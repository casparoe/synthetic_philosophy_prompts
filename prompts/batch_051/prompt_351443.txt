A colleague in the law school is co-teaching an interdisciplinary seminar with me next term, and he has circulated a one-page handout meant to orient graduate students with no background in the Rawls–Harsanyi debate. The handout's central paragraph reads:

"Rawls argued that, from behind the veil of ignorance, rational parties choosing principles of justice would adopt the maximin rule: you evaluate a basic structure by the position of its worst-off member, and you select the principles whose worst outcome is least bad. Since the parties do not know who they will turn out to be, and since they have no reason to expect to be lucky, they reject utilitarianism and agree to equal basic liberties together with a principle that allows inequalities only when they benefit the worst-off. Harsanyi replied that this choice rule is irrational. A rational chooser behind the veil should treat each possible life as equally likely and maximize expected utility, which selects the society with the highest average well-being. On Harsanyi's picture, then, people are properly treated as interchangeable utility points: if imposing a severe loss on a few buys a slightly larger gain for many, the rational choice is to accept it. The whole debate therefore comes down to a difference in risk temperament — Rawls assumes that agents are fundamentally cautious and unwilling to gamble, while Harsanyi assumes that they are willing to take fair bets."

I know enough to suspect that this is not quite fair to either figure — for one thing, I recall Rawls explicitly stipulating that the veil hides the parties' attitudes toward risk, which sits awkwardly with the concluding sentence — but I am not a specialist in decision-theoretic social choice, and I cannot tell how much of the paragraph survives that correction. Could you do an accuracy audit of this passage? Go claim by claim: which parts are accurate, which are subtly wrong, what a defender of Rawls and a Harsanyi-style Bayesian would each say in response to the handout's framing, and whether the conclusion that the dispute is only about risk temperament survives once the errors are identified.

Please keep your audit to no more than 800 words.
