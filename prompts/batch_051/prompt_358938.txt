Write the desiderata that a theory of animal welfare must satisfy to be adequate for farmed fish and decapod crustaceans in aquaculture, where "adequate" means it can support both regulatory policy and routine on-farm welfare auditing. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and be sure the list handles at least these: the distinction between nociception and consciously felt pain; the fact that fish lack a mammalian neocortex while many crustaceans have decentralized nervous systems, so the theory must not treat mammalian neuroanatomy as a template yet must still separate sentience candidates from non-candidates; the distinction between having moral status, how much weight a being has, and how well it is faring; the determination of the evidentiary threshold for counting a species as sentient, and whether that threshold is purely scientific or partly normative; the standing of death itself — whether slaughter harms an animal only through the states that precede it or also through the welfare opportunities it removes; and the aggregation problem posed by the enormous numbers of individual animals in aquaculture, where many small per-animal deficits can rival a few severe ones. For each requirement, mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one side of the sentience debate. Then identify any subset of requirements that is jointly unsatisfiable in practice — for instance, a possible conflict between the feasibility of rapid on-farm auditing and fidelity to subjective states over time — and state which single requirement does the most to exclude cruder candidate frameworks. Do not include a bibliography or reading list. Answer in American English.
