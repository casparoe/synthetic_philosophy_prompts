Objective Bayesianism often presents the maximum entropy principle as a principled rule for selecting a prior: among all distributions consistent with one's stated constraints, adopt the one that maximises Shannon entropy. Compare this proposal with the classical principle of insufficient reason, which instructs us to assign equal probability to each possibility when no evidence discriminates among them. The heart of the comparison should be the charge that the maximum entropy method does not actually remove the arbitrariness that made the older principle questionable, but instead relocates it: into the choice of constraints to impose, the level at which the outcome space is described, and the parameterisation in which entropy is computed. Does the objective Bayesian succeed in making priors rational where the principle of insufficient reason failed, or has the same problem merely been pushed into the setup rather than solved? Justify your answer.
