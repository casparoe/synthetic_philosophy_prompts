Im putting together a "which theory of insect ethics do you actually hold?" quiz for my small gardening-and-ecology blog, and I want it to sort people by what they say about real pest situations, not by which label they'd prefer. Eight or nine questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions. The positions should include at least these leaves: a strong insect-rights/abolitionist view; a sentiocentric welfare view (pain is what matters, but insect pain might count for less); a biocentric view (every living thing has some moral standing, however slight); an ecosystem/ecological-function view (individuals matter less than roles, biodiversity, and whether a population belongs in this place); an anthropocentric view (pests matter only through human interests); and a precautionary view driven by uncertainty about whether insects feel pain. No question should just ask "do you think bugs have rights?"—the questions should force tradeoffs, and they should vary the stakes and the kind of bug. Include at least these cases: a mosquito in the bedroom, bedbugs, aphids on roses, head lice on a kid, a locust swarm heading for a village's food supply, a wasp nest above the back door, a non-native beetle killing a forest, and maybe a wild bee colony inside a wall. I want to see whether people flip when the stakes change (nuisance vs disease vs crop failure) and whether they treat ecologically important insects differently from pests. In the scoring key, flag any pairs of positions the quiz cant actually separate, and note any answer combinations no position corresponds to, with a quick line on whether that combination is incoherent, just unattractive, or probably just underexplored. Keep the answer options in plain language, not jargon, since the blog readers are mostly gardeners, not philosophy people.
