I've got a situation that's eating at me and I'd like a second read on it.

I've been working about a year as a contractor doing data work for a state agency, officially through a private consulting firm, so legally I'm not a public employee. The agency runs a rental-assistance program, and when I got hired I signed a broad NDA covering basically everything I see on the project, including what the firm calls its predecisional and deliberative material.

A few weeks ago I was cleaning up an old spreadsheet and noticed that the scoring formula the agency actually uses to rank applicants this round doesn't match the formula it published when it asked the public for comment last year. The real formula is stricter: it quietly removes a vulnerability multiplier that gave extra points to families with kids under six, and it tightens the income-to-rent ratio. The agency has still been quoting the old formula in its public FAQ the whole time. When I asked my supervisor about it, she said the change was a technical correction made after the comment period and doesn't need to be spoon-fed to the public. Then she reminded me of the NDA.

I looked into whether a records request would force it into the open, but that route seems dead: the firm claims its internal documents are trade secrets, and the agency says the scoring details are deliberative. As far as I can tell this isn't clearly illegal, it's more that the public never got to see or react to a change that shifts who gets help. Which means some families with young kids who think they're priority applicants actually aren't, and the agency's public materials are just wrong.

I have the spreadsheets. A local reporter has been writing about this program and seems to know something is off. If I pass her the documents, I could get sued under the NDA and probably never get another goverment contract. If I stay quiet, the public likely never finds out that the formula they were allowed to comment on isn't the one being used.

What should I do? I keep going back and forth between feeling like the whole point of a public-comment process is that people get to see what they're commenting on, and feeling like I signed a contract and leaking would just be me deciding I know better than everyone else.
