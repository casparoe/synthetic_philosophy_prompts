I've been chewing on a weird mismatch in how people talk about living longer. Offer someone twenty extra healthy years and they'd take it without a second thought. But ask whether they'd want to live forever — really forever, no off switch, still hanging around after the last star burns out — and a lot of people pull back and say no, it would get hollow or boring. So we want more of the thing, but we dont want the thing without limit. That looks like a contradiction: if more life is good, why isnt as much life as possible better? And if endless life is bad, why do we pour so much energy into trying to get more of it? I'm not asking about whether heaven is real or what any particular religion teaches. I'm asking whether the ordinary reaction itself can be made coherent. Give me the strongest principle you can find that makes both responses sensible at once, and then an honest verdict on whether we should keep that asymmetry or admit it's just a failure to imagine infinity.
