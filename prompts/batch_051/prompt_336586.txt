The doctrine of sovereign immunity is usually defended on practical grounds, but behind it sometimes sits a deeper conceptual claim: that the state, as a continuing corporate entity, cannot be a wrongdoer in its own right, because only individual human beings can act and bear blame. On that view, statements like "the state wronged them" are shorthand for "certain identifiable officeholders wronged them." I suspect that is false, and that the state functions in our moral and legal practices as a unified locus of wrongdoing distinct from the officials who happen to act for it. I want to know whether this disagreement can be brought to the data. Design the strongest test you can of the claim that "the state" operates as a distinct subject of wrongdoing rather than as a mere manner of speaking about official wrongdoing. The test may be experimental, observational, comparative, or some combination. Specify the result that would favor each reading, and be precise about what even a positive result would fail to establish—in particular, whether it would show that the attribution tracks a real feature of the world or merely a stable, useful fiction.

Organize your answer into the following titled sections, in this order:

1. The claim and its denial (roughly 150 words). State the distinctness claim and the reductionist alternative precisely. Identify which part of the claim your test operationalizes and which part you are setting aside.

2. The test (roughly 300 words). Give a concrete design: subjects or units, comparison or manipulation, measure, and predicted pattern under each reading. Make it strong enough that a defender of either view would have to reckon with the result.

3. What the results would show (roughly 200 words). State the result that would favor the state-as-agent reading and the result that would favor the reductionist reading, and say exactly what each result would license.

4. What the test could not settle (roughly 100 words). Explain what remains open even under a positive result—and what no test of this kind could settle even in principle—for instance, whether the practice tracks a real property or merely an entrenched and useful fiction, and what further evidence or argument would be needed to close the gap.
