I have been trying to follow the dispute over Integrated Information Theory, and what strikes me is that it is not a normal scientific controversy. Both sides now agree on a great deal of technical detail: how the Φ measure behaves on toy networks, which systems get high scores and which get zero, and how the canonical postulates have shifted across successive formulations of the theory. What they cannot agree on is whether any of this advances the study of consciousness. Defenders say the theory begins from a small set of axioms describing what experience is like from the inside — that it is informative, integrated, and definite — and that critics who reject the formalization have simply refused to engage with those axioms. Critics reply that a theory of consciousness should make risky predictions about brains or behavior, and that IIT either makes no such predictions or makes ones that look obviously wrong: simple, near-uniform lattices attain high Φ while feedforward systems that discriminate many states attain none, and the preferred readings appear to be adjusted after the fact whenever a prediction becomes awkward. Each side now treats the other as not merely wrong but as having missed the point. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself rather than a verdict on IIT. Is it a verbal dispute about the term consciousness? A difference about the target of explanation — the presence of experience, the level of experience, the content of experience, or functional access to experience? A difference in standards of evidence, with one side treating first-person phenomenology as data and the other insisting on third-person observables? Or are the two sides running different research programs under one name, one a formal metaphysics of the subject's point of view and the other an empirical theory of the correlates and contents of consciousness? My own hypothesis is the last of these, but it does not quite fit, because the defenders also make quantitative claims about degrees of consciousness. Give me your best diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is, supported by what each side actually says, and say what would count as progress given that diagnosis — a distinction, a class of experiments, a formal result, a reformed question, or a principled argument that the impasse should persist.
