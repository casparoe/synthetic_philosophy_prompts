Ok, so I teach an upper-level seminar called Agents, Systems, and Blame — mostly philosophy of action with a chunk of AI ethics — and I'm writing the final. I want one essay question on the status of constrained agents: specifically, whether a system that reliably forms moral judgments but is causally incapable of acting on them counts as a moral agent. I don't want students to just rehash the usual line about machines not being responsible. We spent two weeks on a distinction I introduced, so build the question around it: judgmental agency (forming normative verdicts about what ought to be done in the environment), executive agency (initiating changes in the world to close the gap between verdict and world), and participatory agency (having one's verdicts actually function as reasons in other agents' practical reasoning). Use the Cassandra example from class: the triage system that makes sharp judgments about which patients should get scarce ICU beds, but its outputs feed a screen in an empty room — nobody reads them, by design. It has judgmental agency, zero executive agency, and at best a degenerate kind of participatory agency. The crux of the question should be whether judgmental agency alone can ground moral agency, or whether it's a merely epistemic capacity — a very good ethical weathervane rather than an agent.

Please write the exam question itself (a 90-minute essay question), a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 20. The marking scheme should seperate an A from a B by naming the specific moves each must contain, and I'd like a one-line note on a common way students botch each move. The question has to be answerable from course material alone — no citations or reading lists. And please write the whole thing in American English, since my course is in the States.
