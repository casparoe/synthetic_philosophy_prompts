I spent my scientific career valuing a result chiefly for what it enabled others to do, and I am still puzzled by how much organized mountaineering and athletics honor firsts and marginal records. The first winter ascent of a difficult face, the fastest known time on a long trail, a flat sprint lowered by a few hundredths of a second: none of these changes anyone's life materially, and the feat differs from the previous mark by a margin with no practical consequence. Yet the achievement is treated as a real human good, and the record-holder is honored in ways that a person who merely repeats the feat competently is not. What theory of value does this practice presuppose? Does it presuppose that excellence is good in itself, that being first adds value over and above the performance, or that relative standing is what matters? I would like you to reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits with, identify which rival theories would also license it, and say what would have to change if the presupposed view were replaced.

Please structure your answer as a JSON object with these fields:

- implicit_theory: a one-sentence reconstruction of the theory of value the practice appears to assume.
- strict_presuppositions: an array of commitments the practice could not be justified without.
- optional_fits: an array of positions the practice fits with but does not require.
- competing_theories: an array of objects with fields theory and condition, each naming a rival theory and the condition under which it would also justify the practice.
- inconsistencies: an array of tensions between this practice and the way the same society values repeatable, useful contributions elsewhere.
- replacement_predictions: an array of concrete changes that would follow if the presupposed view were rejected.
