I keep tripping over an asymmetry while I'm writing this novel, and I can't tell wether my characters are incoherent or I am. A character who tries to kill someone and fails gets judged much less harshly than one who succeeds, even if the only difference is where the bullet lands; readers basically accept that. But a character who tries to save someone and fails still gets full moral credit for trying — sometimes she's the most admired person in the scene — while a character who saves a life by pure accident, or while trying to do something selfish, gets no gratitude to speak of even though someone's alive because of her. So outcomes carry huge moral weight in the harmful case and almost none in the helpful case. Is there one principle that justifies both halves, or should at least one of them go? If you had to scrap one, which and why?
