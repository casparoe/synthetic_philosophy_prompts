I've been trying to figure out what I actually think makes something a performance of a given piece of music, or an instance of it at all. I keep collecting gut reactions, and they don't obviously add up. Here they are.

When a pianist plays a Beethoven sonata in a recital from the score and flubs a couple of notes, I have no hesitation: that's a performance of the sonata. When a cover band turns a pop ballad into a ska song—new key, new instrumentation, half the chords changed, but the melody and words still recognizably there—I still want to say they performed the ballad, not merely something "based on" it. But when a classical pianist plays a piano reduction of a piece originally written for orchestra, I don't think they performed the orchestral piece; they played an arrangement of it. That already feels a little uneasy, since the cover band changed more than the transcriber did.

A computer playing a MIDI file that contains all the notes of a sonata puts me in a weird spot: I don't think there's a performance going on, since nothing is being interpeted, but I do suspect the sonata is being instantiated somehow. A jazz trio improvising over the chord changes of a standard without ever stating the melody is different again: I wouldn't say they performed the standard, even though the standard is in some way the basis of what they did. A recording of a rock band playing a song they wrote seems to me like a captured performance of the song, but listening to it isn't a performance of anything. For an electronic producer who builds a track in a DAW from samples and synthesis, where the bounced audio file is the only artifact, I'd say the file just is the work—there's no distinct performance to be captured. And if another producer loops a two-bar drum break from an old funk record into a new song, I don't think they've performed the old funk song, nor does the new song contain a performance of it, even though a bit of an old recording sits right in the mix.

Is there one principle about works, performances, and instances that gets all of these, or at least most of them? If getting all of them requires an ugly patchwork, tell me which verdicts I should drop. And tell me what the resulting view says about cases I haven't listed—for instance, four people humming the vocal line of a song at a party, a piece whose whole score tells the performer to make no intentional sounds, or an AI system that generates a new recording trained on a particular band's back catalog.
