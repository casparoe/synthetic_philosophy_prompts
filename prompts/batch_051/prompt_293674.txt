Rosa and I have argued for years about complicity—about when someone who isn't the main wrongdoer, and maybe doesn't cause any of the harm, is still in on it. Her track record, as best I can reconstruct it: she says a motel bookings clerk who knows the rooms are being used by a trafficking ring is complicit. She says bank employees who process wires for a money-laundering front without knowing about the front are not. She says a bystander who chants while a mob beats someone is complicit even though he never throws a punch. She says a parts supplier is complicit when he knows the bus company he sells to is running illegal, unsafe services and sells to them anyway. She says a factory worker whose components later end up in a bomb is not, since he had no idea what they were for. And she says a landlord who rents to a tenant he merely suspects of forging documents is not.

Now there's a new case. An export clerk has to put his official stamp on a shipment of precursor chemicals. He knows the government buying the shipment will use it for executions, which he privately regards as murder. But the stamp is a formality—the shipment is auto-approved seconds later and would go through either way, and he isn't egging anyone on. Would Rosa call him complicit? I care less about the yes or no than about the principle she's actually running, the other principles that fit her past calls equally well, which of those principles this case actually distinguishes, and what further case would separate whatever remains.
