I retired from a career in the physical sciences a few years ago and have since been working through formal epistemology on my own. I have settled into a set of views about how rational belief should respond to new information, and I would like an audit of whether the set, taken together, is coherent. I will state each commitment as precisely as I can; I ask you to treat them as my sincere current position, neither adding nor subtracting in the interest of making them fit together.

1. My beliefs are degrees of belief that conform to the probability calculus, and belief revision should be conservative: no probability mass is moved unless the incoming information forces the move.

2. If I learn a proposition E with certainty, I conditionalize on E. If my information merely reweights the cells of a partition {E_1, ..., E_n}, I update by Jeffrey's rule: the new probability of a proposition H is Σ_i P_old(H | E_i) P_new(E_i). I also accept the associated rigidity condition, namely that probabilities conditional on one of the cells of the reweighted partition do not change.

3. I take the principle of minimum relative entropy — also called minimum cross-entropy or minimum discrimination information — to be the master rule for belief revision. When new information takes the form of a constraint on the posterior distribution rather than a proposition learned with certainty, I adopt, from among all distributions satisfying the constraint, the one least divergent in relative entropy from the old distribution. I take ordinary conditionalization and Jeffrey conditionalization to be special cases of this rule.

4. Purely conditional information does not by itself bear on the truth of its antecedent. A message whose entire content is that, conditional on H, some proposition X has probability q may force changes in probabilities conditional on H, but it gives no reason to raise or lower the probability of H itself.

5. Consider a concrete case as a touchstone. A soldier, Judy Benjamin, is dropped at night into a region divided into two equally sized territories, Red and Blue, each divided into two equally sized zones: Red Headquarters and Red Second Company on the Red side, and Blue Headquarters and Blue Second Company on the Blue side; the four zones are mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive. Her prior is uniform across the four zones. She receives a radio message whose entire content is this: conditional on her being in Red territory, her probability of being in the Red Headquarters zone is q, where q is greater than one half. My considered judgment about this case is that she should leave the probability of being in Red territory at one half, leave each Blue zone at one quarter, and move probability only within Red, so that Red Headquarters becomes q/2 and Red Second Company becomes (1-q)/2.

I have tried to determine whether these five commitments are jointly tenable, but I have not been able to reassure myself. I am asking for a diagnosis rather than a defense of any particular commitment. Please go through the commitments pair by pair: identify which pairs are in genuine tension, rank those tensions by severity, and say for each what would have to be given up and what the cheapest repair would be. Also identify which pairs are only apparently in tension and explain why the tension dissolves. Finally, tell me which single revision to this set would do the most to restore coherence while sacrificing the least.
