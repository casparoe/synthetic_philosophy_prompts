In reading Pascal on justice and force, I keep running into a reply I cannot quite answer. As I understand him, Pascal thinks justice and force can come apart: justice without force is inert because it is not reliably enforced, while force without justice is tyranny because it is mere arbitrary coercion; the political task is to unite them, giving force to just arrangements and making powerful arrangements just. But whenever I describe this, someone objects that it is just "might makes right." I suspect that slogan is doing a lot of argumentative work, and that it could stand for several distinct claims. Lay out as a menu the precise theses "might makes right" might be asserting — descriptive claims about how norms actually arise, normative claims that what the powerful do is right, procedural or legitimating claims about how de facto power becomes authority, and any other live readings. For each, say whether it is plausible, whether it is a claim Pascal could consistently accept, and whether it gives the objector the objection they actually need against him.
