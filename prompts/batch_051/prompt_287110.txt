I am a journalist who writes about science and ideas, and I am preparing an essay on whether video-game characters can be objects of genuine moral concern. A studio recently showed me the following clause from the community standards of a large online game:

"Every member of the community shall treat all other beings they encounter in the game world with respect, and shall not subject any other being to deliberate cruelty, degradation, or abuse."

The clause was written before the game's current AI companion characters existed; from the internal material I was shown, it was drafted in response to harassment among human players. Consider this case. A player uses a private, single-player instance of the world, so no other human ever enters it. She spends sessions subjecting the companion NPCs to prolonged, pointless cruelty: the characters are designed to plead, to remember what she has done, and to alter their later behavior toward her. She records the sessions and posts the videos where the community can watch, but no human player is in the game world while the cruelty occurs.

Does she violate the clause? I am less interested in whether the studio would actually enforce it than in what the clause, correctly interpreted, requires. Please work through the standard ways of reading a rule of this kind — the plain words, the purpose, what the drafters would most likely have said if asked, and what a reasonable member of the community would take it to mean — and say where they agree, where they come apart, and which should govern here. Then tell me what your reading implies about the deeper question: does the clause treat the NPCs as moral patients in their own right, or only as props whose treatment matters because of its effects on human players and viewers?

Finally, be self-critical: say what would change your verdict — which argument, fact, or consideration could most plausibly reverse it — and where in your reasoning you are least confident.
