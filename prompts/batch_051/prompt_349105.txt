I am compiling a list of objections to the maximin criterion as a rule for choice under complete ignorance — the kind of choice situation Rawls takes the original position to instantiate, where an agent faces a set of possible states of the world but no admissible probabilities over them. I believe the following four are, between them, the central standard objections, so I do not need them restated.

1. The expected-utility objection. Where a probability distribution over the states is available, or can be derived from symmetry (as Harsanyi argues it can be derived from the veil of ignorance, with each person receiving equal probability), maximin conflicts with the axioms of expected-utility theory, and it recommends the wrong act wherever the two rules diverge if expected utility is the correct theory of rational choice under uncertainty.
2. The over-conservatism objection. Maximin ranks every option by its single worst possible payoff, so any conceivable disaster dominates the verdict no matter how remote it is. As a general rule for ordinary decision-making, this yields absurd caution; maximin is plausible only when the worst outcomes are genuinely severe and probabilities genuinely unavailable.
3. The insensitivity objection. Any two options with the same worst payoff are ranked equally by maximin, even if one is at least as good as the other in every state and strictly better in every non-worst state. The rest of the payoff profile simply does not enter the rule.
4. The partition objection. The state space over which the minimum is taken is not fixed by the decision problem itself: any state can be divided into finer sub-cases in some of which an act still fares worse, so how finely one individuates possible states determines what counts as an act's minimum. Maximin's verdicts are therefore not invariant under redescription of the problem.

I am looking for a fifth objection that is not on this list and is not a restatement, special case, or combination of any of the four; attaching a new label without a distinct argument will not count. It should target the maximin criterion itself rather than only Rawls's use of it, and it should be the sort of criticism that could get some purchase with someone who finds all four of the above only partly convincing. After giving the objection, tell me honestly whether it is any good: does it identify a genuine defect in the criterion, or does it collapse into one of the four once stated precisely? I want the argument, not a citation.
