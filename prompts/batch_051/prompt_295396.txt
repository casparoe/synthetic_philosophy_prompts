For a seminar on intellectual property and digital preservation, I have been asked to write down my commitments about what should happen to creative and technical works when their owner cannot be found, and I would like a consistency check before I circulate them. The commitments are as follows. (1) Ownership of a work is best understood as a bundle of rights rather than a single unitary relation. (2) Abandonment of those rights requires intent; mere neglect, obscurity, or an owner who cannot be located does not by itself extinguish them. (3) Nevertheless, orphan works and long-abandoned software should be freely preservable, runnable, and adaptable for non-commercial purposes without permission. (4) If the owner should reappear, they should regain full control prospectively but should not be able to enjoin non-commercial uses begun in good faith, although they may claim a reasonable share of any later commercial revenue those uses generate. (5) Attribution and integrity interests survive the dormancy of economic rights, so users of such works should still credit the author and refrain from mutilating the work. Which of these commitments are actually in tension, which only appear to be, and what would have to give to restore consistency? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and identify which single revision would clean up the most at the lowest cost.
