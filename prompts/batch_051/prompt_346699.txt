Ok so in psych class we did a unit on consciousness and it mostly left me with this picture of my own mind that I dont know how seriously to take. In the picture, theres basically a tiny screening room behind my eyes. All the feeds from my eyes and ears and skin get piped onto one screen at the front, and a little "me" (the real me, whatever that means) sits in the only chair watching. The watching is what makes the stuff conscious, and after the little me watches, it decides things and makes my body move. I get that there's no literal tiny person in my skull, but I'm not sure which part of the picture I'm allowed to keep and which part is the mistake. Here's a second version of the same thing: when I try to catch a thought as it happens, I always feel like I'm one second behind, like the thought already played out somewhere else and I'm just getting the replay. And then the thing that made me actually write this: if I'm super absorbed in a game, I don't notice any watcher while it's happening, but afterwards I can tell the whole story of the game as if I had been sitting there watching it, so maybe the watcher is just added in hindsight? Does this family of pictures have a name? Do people who actually work on consciousness think it's false, or just a sloppy way of talking about something real? If it's false, what replaces it — like, without the screen and the little me, what's going on when I'm "watching" my own thoughts? And does the fact that it really strongly feels like there's a center count as evidence for the picture? I'm not just asking for a label btw; I want to know how much of this picture a serious theory keeps, where exactly it stops being true, and what the alternative actually says instead.
