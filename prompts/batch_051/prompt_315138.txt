I've been re-reading Schlick's Vom Sinn des Lebens for a graduate seminar, and the passage where he tries to extend the concept of play to adult activities is giving me trouble. He says meaning is found in play rather than work, but then he explicitly denies that play should be understood as idleness or amusement; it has something to do with doing an activity for its own sake, and the same action, like making a shoe or conducting an experiment, can move from work to play when the quality of the doing changes. Fine. But if play is not literally play — not leisure, not childhood, not any particular activity — and is just whatever one is absorbed in for its own sake, then hasn't Schlick drained the word of all its explanitory content? In that case, make your life playful seems to amount to make your activities feel intrinsically worthwhile, which might be good advice, but isn't it just a restatement rather than a theory? I want to be fair to him in the lecture, so what is the reading of that passage on which Schlick isn't just turning play into an arbitrary honorific for whatever someone already enjoys?
