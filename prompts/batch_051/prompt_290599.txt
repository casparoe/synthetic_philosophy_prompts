I'm reading Gödel, Escher, Bach and I got stuck on the chapter called The Location of Meaning. As I understand him, Hofstadter splits any message into three layers: the frame message (the fact that it announces itself as a message in the first place), the outer message (the implicit information about how to read it—conventions, rules, context), and the inner message (what we'd normally call the content). But then he turns around and says meaning isn't located in any single one of them. That seems evasive to me. If the layers are really separable in the way he describes, there should be an answer to where the meaning actually sits. I'd have thought the inner message is what contains it, and the other two are just access conditions.

Concretely, when a cell reads a stretch of DNA, or when someone deciphers an inscription in an unknown script, where is the meaning located? Which of the three layers, strictly speaking, posseses it? I don't want a survey of interpretive options—give me a definite answer and defend it.
