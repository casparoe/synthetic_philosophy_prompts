I am a PhD student in moral philosophy working on the ethics of risk, and I have realized that a term I rely on constantly is underdefined: what it is to impose a risk on someone without their consent. I want a working definition of that act — not of consent alone, and not of risk alone — that sorts the following cases correctly and does not collapse into the trivial criterion that any nonzero causal contribution to another person's risk is an imposition.

Clear case of imposition: a factory vents a known carcinogen over a residential area without warning.

Clear case of no imposition: a person keeps properly sealed household solvents inside their own apartment, with no realistic route of exposure to anyone else.

Borderline cases, where I genuinely do not know what the definition should say:
- Someone drives a private car on public roads, imposing a small collision risk on pedestrians who never agreed to it.
- Someone with a mild respiratory infection goes grocery shopping while wearing a mask and keeping distance.
- An investor buys a leveraged financial position whose failure, together with enough similar trades, could destabilize a financial institution.
- A city retimes a traffic light so that expected pedestrian injuries rise slightly at one crossing while falling by more elsewhere.

Propose a definition, test it against each case, and where it yields a verdict you would be reluctant to defend, say so explicitly and either revise the definition or argue that the surprising verdict is correct.
