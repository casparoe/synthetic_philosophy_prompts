I've been put in charge of writing the pitch for a two-day philosophy-of-mind-and-AI workshop, and I want ours to be the contrarian one in the program. The boring pitch is "we need to solve the alignment problem." Mine would start from a suspicion I haven't been able to turn into a proper proposal, and that's what I need from you. The suspicion is this. People use "aligned" as a two-place predicate — a system is aligned with an operator, or a user, or a developer, or a spec, or "human values" — but they also use it as a one-place property, as in "that model is not aligned," full stop, the way we say a bridge is safe. If alignment is fundamentally relational, then a question like "is this model aligned?" without saying aligned with whom, about what, and under what incentives is a category mistake; if it's genuinely one-place, we owe an account of what the property attaches to — the weights, the objective, the deployed system, the developer-plus-system, the whole social arrangement — and why those all deserve one word. I suspect the live question is not "how do we solve alignment?" but "what sort of thing must alignment be for 'solve' to be the right verb at all." Turn that into a one-page workshop proposal: state the question precisely, explain why the ML-safety people and the value-theory people each leave it open on their own, say how a conceptual rather than empirical investigation makes progress and why that's the right division of labor, convince the funders that this is worth doing and can actually be done in two days, say what a successful deliverable would look like for someone deciding what to build or not build, and say what would count as failure (if the relational/monadic distinction just collapses, what then?).
