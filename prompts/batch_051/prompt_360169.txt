I've been reading McTaggart's "The Unreality of Time" and got stuck on this passage. A little earlier he floats the idea that the past/present/future distinction is just a constant illusion of our minds, and that the real nature of time only has earlier/later relations — which sounds a lot like modern eternalism. Then he says:

"This is not a very common view, but it has found able supporters. I believe it to be untenable, because, as I said above, it seems to me that the A series is essential to the nature of time, and that any difficulty in the way of regarding the A series as real is equally a difficulty in the way of regarding time as real."

Here's what I don't get. He's saying the A series is essential to time, so if you drop the A series you drop time. But the people who drop the A series are eternalists, and presentists are the ones who keep it. Doesn't that mean McTaggart's argument, if it works, is really a proof of presentism? Why is he always remembered as the "time is unreal" guy then? What am I missing?
