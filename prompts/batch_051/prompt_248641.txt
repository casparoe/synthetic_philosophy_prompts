Presentism and eternalism have dominated analytic metaphysics of time for decades, and the debate between them has an odd character: the arguments are by now exhaustively mapped, the replies and counter-replies are familiar to everyone in the field, and yet almost no one changes sides. Presentists hold that only the present time and its contents exist; the past is gone and the future is not yet. Eternalists hold that all times and their contents are equally real; on the standard B-theoretic version, the apparent passage of time is a matter of one's location and perspective rather than a change in what exists. Each side has a stock objection to the other. Presentists charge eternalists with denying the reality of passage and with bloating ontology, treating dinosaurs and future generations as just as real as the people in this room. Eternalists charge presentists with being unable to supply truthmakers for true claims about the past, with struggling to account for cross-temporal relations such as admiring Socrates, and with fitting awkwardly with the relativity of simultaneity in special relativity. Each side has by now developed sophisticated ways of absorbing the other's cases, yet the disagreement persists. The recurring triviality objection to presentism—that the view comes out either trivially true or plainly false depending on how the word 'exists' is read—suggests that at least part of the trouble may be semantic. I want a diagnosis, not a verdict. Is this a substantive disagreement about the world, a merely verbal disagreement about the word 'exists', or two different projects—one about the semantics of tensed language and one about fundamental ontology—being conducted under a single heading? My own suspicion is that the debate is kept alive by the unargued assumption that 'exists' has a single neutral, univocal meaning that both sides share, and that if this assumption were dropped the remaining dispute would be smaller and differently located than participants suppose. Assess that hypothesis, diagnose what kind of disagreement this really is, and say what, if anything, would count as progress.
