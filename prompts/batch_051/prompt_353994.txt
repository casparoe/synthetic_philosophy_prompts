In a political theory course I am taking, a handout summarizes the disagreement between Rawls and Harsanyi about decision-making under ignorance as follows:

"Rawls holds that behind the veil of ignorance, where you do not know which person in society you will be, the rational decision rule is maximin. You rank each candidate basic structure solely by the condition of the worst-off representative person and choose the arrangement that does best by them. Since the parties have no information about their position, there is no objective basis for assigning probabilities over social roles; to do so anyway is to feign knowledge you do not have. Harsanyi responds that this is mere risk-aversion dressed up as a theory of justice. The correct way to model ignorance, he argues, is to regard yourself as equally likely to be any person; maximizing expected utility under that equiprobability assumption yields average utilitarianism. On Harsanyi's telling, Rawls clings to maximin out of a pessimistic psychological bias, and the difference principle is ultimately nothing more than maximin applied to the distribution of income."

I have enough background to know this is a real and important dispute, but the summary feels too tidy, and I cannot reliably tell where it is merely simplifying from where it is quietly loading the argument.
