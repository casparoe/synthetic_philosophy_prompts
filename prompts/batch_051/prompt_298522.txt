My kid asked me the other day whether his classmate who's been blind from birth knows the word 'red' but not red the way he does, and I've been spiraling about what I'd even mean by 'physical facts' when I try to defend either answer. So I've been messing with Mary's room and got stuck on a variant. Keep everything the same, but instead of saying Mary learns all the physical facts about color vision before she leaves the room, say she learns every fact that can be stated in a completed physics: not our current, probably partly wrong physics, but whatever the final true physics turns out to be. Now run Hempel's dilemma against this. If 'physical' just means 'whatever current physics talks about,' then the physicalist can shrug and say Mary's new bit of knowledge only shows our current physics is incomplete, not that physicalism is false. But if 'physical' means 'whatever a completed physics includes,' then we can't rule out in advance that a completed physics includes facts about what red looks like—in which case the whole setup either begs the question or stops being a threat. Does this variant make the knowledge argument stronger, weaker, or just show the original was underspecified? Work through how the ability hypothesis and at least one other physicalist reply would handle it, and say which side you think ends up better off.
