ok so i'm on a civilian oversight board and we're stuck on a use-of-force policy that was written years before the department started renting these remote-operated drones with an onboard "use of force" mode. Here's the exact rule from the manual:

"Deadly force is justified only when the officer at the scene reasonably believes a person poses an imminent threat of death or serious physical injury to the officer or another person. Officers may use only that degree of force which a reasonable officer at the scene would believe necessary under the circumstances."

The case: a sworn officer is remotely operating a vendor-owned drone — the city rents it, and the vendor writes and maintains the targeting software. The video feed to the officer's console has about a four-second delay. The officer authorizes lethal force based on the feed, which shows a man holding a gun to a hostage's head. But the command only goes through if the vendor's onboard threat classifier still registers a weapon and a second person in its current sensor data. By the time the command reaches the drone, the man has lowered the gun and stepped back. The classifier, working from fresher data than the officer ever saw, still reads the gun as raised. The drone fires and kills him.

So did the officer "reasonably believe" anything under the policy? She was never at the scene; her belief was formed about a four-second-old state; the actual release of force was delegated to a classifier nobody on the board can inspect. And does it change things that a private vendor owns the machine and wrote the classifier — is this still the city using force, or has the state's monopoly on violence quietly been subcontracted? Walk me through what the policy requires under (a) plain words, (b) its purpose, (c) what the drafters would probably have said if they'd been asked about semi-autonomous drones, and (d) what a reasonable officer would take it to mean. Then tell me which reading should govern, and whether the real answer is that this kind of force just isn't covered by the rule at all.
