I have been thinking about the doctrine of desuetude and find myself pulled in two directions, and I would like a careful adjudication rather than a verdict for either side.

On the one hand, I am drawn to the view that a statute which has gone unenforced for a very long period, against a background of widespread and open contrary practice, should be treated as having lapsed: it is no longer law even though it has never been formally repealed. The argument seems to me to flow naturally from the positivist idea that law is a matter of social fact. If what makes a rule law is that it is actually accepted and applied by officials and the community, then a rule that no official has applied for decades and that nearly everyone openly disregards has lost the social existence that made it law; what remains is a text with a legislative history. The rule-of-law considerations that are usually raised against desuetude also seem to me to point the other way. A sudden prosecution under a long-dead provision feels arbitrary, retrospectively surprising and unequally selective, in precisely the ways the rule of law is meant to rule out.

On the other hand, I hold with some firmness the familiar conviction that a duly enacted statute remains valid law until a legislature repeals it, and that non-enforcement is a failure of legal officials rather than an event that changes the law. Making validity depend on enforcement history seems to convert prosecutorial neglect, resource constraints or regulatory capture into an unlegislated power to nullify legislation, and to reward the very noncompliance the law was meant to prevent. I cannot shake the sense that there is a difference between a bad law that should be repealed and a law that has somehow ceased to be law merely through disuse.

I suspect these two thoughts cannot both be right as they stand, but I do not know which should give way. Please present the strongest version of each side, then tell me what it would cost to abandon the intuition and what it would cost to abandon or amend the doctrine. Is there a reading on which they are compatible — for example, one in which desuetude concerns the legitimacy of enforcement rather than the existence of the law, or one in which only a special kind of prolonged disuse extinguishes validity while ordinary enforcement failure does not? And are those readings genuine reconciliations or merely verbal compromises? I want to know which of my commitments should yield, and why.
