In my high school philosophy course we have been working through Locke's account of personal identity, and I am writing a short paper in which I try to extend that account to a case from discussions of a future world of whole brain emulation. Suppose a future technology can scan a human brain in complete detail and implement the resulting mind—its memories, dispositions, skills, and consciousness—as a piece of software. In the destructive case, the biological body is destroyed in the scanning process and only the emulation remains. In the non-destructive case, the biological original survives and then begins to diverge from the emulation.

I want to know what Locke, on his own principles, would most plausibly say about whether the emulation is the same person as the biological original, and then whether that emulation should inherit the original's property and remain bound by her contracts and debts. I know that Locke takes personal identity to consist in the sameness of consciousness rather than in the sameness of an immaterial or material substance, and that he treats persons as the subjects of reward, punishment, and responsibility. But I am unsure about three things. First, does sameness of consciousness require a continuous stream, or does it only require that past actions and thoughts can be self-ascribed through memory? A scan could reproduce all the relevant memories without any moment-to-moment continuity. Second, in the non-destructive case, can a single consciousness continue into two separate minds at once, or would each copy simply be a new consciousness that remembers the same past? Third, even if the emulation counts as the same person, does Locke's view of property and contract give any reason to treat sameness of person as sufficient for inheriting civil obligations and ownership, or would he leave those to the laws of the commonwealth? Please answer for both the destructive and non-destructive cases, and say where you think Locke's principles would have to be revised rather than merely extended.
