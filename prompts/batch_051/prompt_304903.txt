So our family is in a dispute over a school-choice scholarship, and i want you to talk me through the interpretation from every angle. The scholarship handbook has this rule (I'm skipping the numbering):

"Scholarship funds may be used only for tuition at a participating private school. A participating private school provides a full-time course of study at a physical campus and is not a homeschool or a cooperative arrangement among parents for the education of their own children."

My kid was admitted to what they call a hybrid academy. It started six years ago as a parent co-op but is now a registered private school with hired teachers. Kids are on campus all day Tuesday and Thursday; Monday, Wednesday, and Friday they work at home from lesson plans the academy's teachers write for each child, with a required morning check-in over Zoom, and the school counts the home days as instructional days. It keeps transcripts, awards credits, and is on the state's approved list. The scholarship office denied us: they say three home days a week makes it a cooperative arrangement among parents, since the parents are the ones supervising on home days. The academy says its a private school like any other, just with a home-study component, and that the home days are teacher-directed even if the teacher is on a screen.

What I want from you: go through the four ways of reading the rule—plain words, the purpose the scholarship program exists for, what the people who wrote the rule in 2015 would likely have said if you had asked them about hybrid academies, and what a reasonable parent reading the handbook would think it covers—and then tell me which approach should decide this and why. Be fair to the scholarship office; if the text really cuts against us, say so instead of inventing a loophole.
