In AI policy circles people talk about an 'AI race' and then borrow the language of war, or of the Cold War nuclear arms race, to say it will end badly: each side hides what it knows, neither can credibly promise to slow down, and the prize is treated as impossible to divide. I'd like an audit of that analogy, not another restatement of it. When someone calls the US–China AI race, or an AGI race between labs, a war, what maps onto what? Sides, battles, casualties, victory, surrender, a home front, a peace treaty. Where does the mapping break, and which breakdowns actually matter for the borrowed rationalist explanations of war — private information with incentives to misrepresent, commitment problems, issue indivisibility? Which features of real war is the analogy quietly importing that an AI race lacks, or has only as metaphor? Then tell me whether the usual conclusion, that the race will be unstable and hard to stop and likely to end in disaster, survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If there is a better analogy that carries the same weight but imports less, propose it. And do say what would change your mind, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
