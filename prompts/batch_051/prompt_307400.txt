Before I read yet another account of how dueling, footbinding, or slavery lost its hold, I want the full spec: what does a theory of moral revolution actually have to get right? The stories I keep seeing are one-factor — slavery ended because material interests shifted (the Eric Williams line), dueling and footbinding ended because honor was redirected (Appiah's line), or the better arguments finally won — and each person picks their favorite case and extrapolates. Give me the requirements list, with the case or consideration behind each: the episodes a theory has to handle, the distinctions it can't collapse (a practice quietly falling out of use vs. people coming to actively condemn it, behavior changing vs. the pratice's moral status changing, change among elites vs. among ordinary practitioners), and the historical facts it's not allowed to wave away. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's emphasis, flag any pair that can't both be satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most damage to the pure-interest, pure-honor, and pure-better-argument stories.
