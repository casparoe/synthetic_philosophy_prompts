It comes up every couple of weeks, and the stakes are getting real — our responsible-AI checklist has a "model welfare" line nobody knows how to trigger, legal worries we're overclaiming if we say the system "understands", and our AI-safety lead has to sign off before anything ships. I have no philosophy background and I can't go read a stack of papers. What I want is a decision procedure we can run in about 30 minutes on a specific model plus a transcript, to work out whether its behaviour meets a reasonable behavioural criteron for mindedness — and, just as important, how far short of mindedness the rest of the behaviours fall. I've picked up the usual labels from the internet (Turing test, Chinese room, Blockhead, p-zombies?) but I don't know which of those should shape a checklist and which are red herrings. One thing I keep tripping over: the imitation game seems to measure the judge's willingness to be fooled as much as the machine's capacities, so maybe the procedure should rate the judge too?

Write the procedure as a reusable checklist with tests run in order, grounded in whatever the philosophy actually knows about behavioural criteria for mind, but boiled down so an engineer with no philosophy training can follow it.
