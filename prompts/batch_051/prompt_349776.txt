ok so i just had this long argument with a friend about whether a brain interface could give someone a genuinely new color, and i want it turned into a sonnet. he kept saying no: color vision is opponent-process, red and green are wired against each other, same for blue and yellow, so reddish-green is a forbidden color, end of story. i said that's just a limit of our normal input pathway, and if you stimulated the cortex directly you could break the opponent channel and get a quale that isn't anywhere in the normal color solid — not a blend of red and green like brown or olive, but something with no name and no precedent. we went back and forth about whether you could even call it a color, and whether the person could ever describe it to the rest of us.

so can you write the sonnet? the octave should build up the standard picture: three cones, opponent channels, the combinations that are supposed to be impossible. the volta should be the moment the implant switches on and the reddish-green, or something past it, actually appears. the sestet should be the speaker reaching for words and failing — every comparison is wrong, every metaphor drags it back into the old color space — and the closing couplet should make the point without naming it: either the space of possible color experience is bigger than our normal wiring lets us reach, or theres a quale language just cant get to. it should be exact enough that a philosopher of perception would nod, but someone who has never heard of forbidden colors should still get the poem.
