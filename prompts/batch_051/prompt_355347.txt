I keep running into a particular argument in discussions of Atlantis, usually from enthusiasts rather than working archaeologists. It runs roughly like this: Troy was long dismissed as a myth, until Schliemann dug at Hisarlik and showed that a real city lay behind the legend; therefore the fact that mainstream archaeology currently dismisses Atlantis gives us no reason to doubt that it, too, had a historical core. I want to know exactly how far this analogy holds. Please give me the explicit mapping: what is supposed to correspond, on the Atlantis side, to Troy, to the epic poems, to Schliemann, to the excavated city, and to the earlier skepticism. Then identify the features of the Troy case that made it reasonable to look for a historical city behind the stories, and state which of those features the Atlantis case lacks. I am especially interested in whether the analogy can support the conclusion it is actually used to support, namely that current archaeological dismissal of Atlantis carries little evidential weight, or whether it supports only the much weaker claim that some legends have historical cores. Where the disanalogies matter, say so explicitly; if there is a better analogy for what an Atlantis enthusiast is and is not entitled to conclude, supply that as well.
