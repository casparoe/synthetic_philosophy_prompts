Got in a long fight with a friend last night about the weather, of all things. He kept saying hurricanes are unpredictable, and I kept pushing him to say unpredictable for what lead time and for what tolerance. My rule, which I've actually held for years but only now put into words, is: I won't call a system unpredictable full stop. I'll only say things like 'unpredictable beyond three days at a 50-mile landfall radius,' or 'predictable out to about a week for whether it'll be at least cat 3.' If I can't fill in the precision and the horizon, I refuse to use the word at all. My friend says this is me quietly redefining 'unpredictable' and dodging the plain fact that some systems resist forecasting no matter how you slice it. Is my rule defensible, or am I just protecting a pet defintion? Are there systems where even a tolerance-and-horizon version fails in a way that would make his plain-language point right after all?
