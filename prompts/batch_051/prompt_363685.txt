Design two matched everyday cases that separate weakness of will from hyperbolic discounting. Case (a) should be an unambiguous instance of philosophical akrasia: the agent judges that one course of action is best all things considered, is able to take it, and yet freely and knowingly does something else; but the case should contain no intertemporal payoff structure, so that a present-bias or hyperbolic/quasi-hyperbolic discounting explanation cannot plausibly get a grip. Case (b) should display the behavioral signature of hyperbolic discounting, a preference reversal in which the agent switches to a smaller-sooner option as it becomes imminent, while at every point acting on her current all-things-considered judgment and so never acting akratically. Keep both cases mundane (household, workplace, or health decisions), with no addiction, compulsion, or clinical pathology, and make the verdicts fall out of the analyses rather than being stipulated in the story. Write the answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) case (a); (2) case (b); (3) the exact judgment and action in case (a), why the standard account counts it as akratic, and why no discounting account gets traction; (4) why the reversal in case (b) is genuinely hyperbolic and why the agent is not akratic at any point; (5) the single design feature each case turns on, what would collapse if that feature were removed, and the best objection available to someone who holds that akrasia just is present-biased choice.
