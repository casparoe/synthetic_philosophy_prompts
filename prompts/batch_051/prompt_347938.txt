Here's the passage that got me. Achilles has written down two premises and a conclusion they're supposed to support, and the Tortoise accepts the premises but won't accept the conclusion. Achilles tries to add the missing step as a new premise:

"Achilles said musingly. 'And your present position is that you accept A and B, but you don't accept the Hypothetical—'

'Let's call it C,' said the Tortoise.

'—but you don't accept

(C) If A and B are true, Z must be true.'

'That is my present position,' said the Tortoise."

The Tortoise then accepts C but still won't accept Z, so Achilles writes D, then E, and so on forever.

Now for the case Carroll obviously never had in mind. We run a rules engine that reviews merge requests and posts verdicts. One policy rule is encoded as:

A collegue says the fix is to add a second rule: WHEN (a rule is triggered) THEN (perform that rule's action). To me that sounds exactly like writing D after C; the engine will log second_rule_triggered=true and still not post the verdict, and then someone will want a third rule.

If piling up more such rules can never force the verdict, what does a working implementation have that Carroll's notebook — and my engine — are missing?
