Suppose there's a long-isolated island society, internally modern (courts, written contracts, cell phones, the works), whose language is the only language anyone speaks and genuinely lacks indexicals: no words for "I", "you", "here", "now", "this", "today". Not as a taboo or a game; the grammar just has no slot for them. Speakers refer to themselves by name or role ("the speaker", "the mother of Teva") and to others by name, kin term, or role; for place and time they use place-names and calender dates. They still point and gesture freely, and the language is fully expressive about anything publicly observable. Some philosophers think a language like this is impossible in principle because certain thoughts (like "it's me" or "that one is mine") have an irreducibly indexical core; others think it's just massively inconvenient. I'm less interested in that verdict than in what you'd predict if you take the scenario at face value.

Walk me through what you'd expect to be systematically different in (a) how children develop a sense of self, (b) promising, courtroom testimony, and lying, and (c) private deliberation and regret. Make your reasoning visible: which predictions are robust, which are guesses, and what assumptions about how much language shapes thought each one leans on. If strong Whorfianism is false and concepts mostly float free of grammar, which of your predictions collapse? And where's the weakest link in your reasoning — what's the first observation about these people that would make you change your mind?
