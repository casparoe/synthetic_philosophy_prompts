The quantum-suicide argument has been eating at me for weeks. The chain of reasoning looks airtight: if the many-worlds picture is right, every outcome of a measurement happens somewhere, and a version of me keeps existing in every branch where I survive, so the only version of me who ever gets to notice anything is one who keeps on surviving. That means a 50/50 lethal measurement would, from my own point of view, always come out nonlethal, and after a hundred rounds I'd have experienced something that outside observers would call a 2^-100 fluke. The conclusion seems insane. A coin flip is a coin flip, and no ammount of dead observers failing to update makes me luckier than the physics says I am. But when I try to find the actual mistake, I just end up gesturing at the words you, observe, and branch. So walk me through it as if I half-believe both sides: what would it take for the argument to be right and the intuition to be the thing that goes, what would it take for the intuition to be right and the argument to be the thing that goes, and is there a reading where both are true about different things and the argument only looked like it contradicted common sense.
