I chair the Chronology and Data Standards Committee of the Common Timeline Project, a consortium of museums, historical societies, and school districts building a shared digital timeline of world history spanning roughly 10,000 BCE to 2100 CE, with partner collections from several cultural and calendrical traditions. Our engineers have surfaced a problem the committee has so far avoided: the underlying database needs a single, internally consistent numeric convention for years, because partner institutions currently submit dates in several incompatible formats and arithmetic breaks at the BCE/CE boundary. We must adopt an internal standard before the next build cycle, and the project board has asked us not to let the choice be made by default.

The options before us are these. (1) Traditional BCE/CE notation with no year 0: 1 BCE is immediately followed by 1 CE. It matches the labels our users grew up with, but an interval from 2 BCE to 2 CE spans three years rather than four, and any system built on it will have to special-case the boundary; the epoch itself is also the traditional Christian one, inherited rather than chosen. (2) Astronomical year numbering: insert a year 0 at the boundary, so that 1 BCE becomes year 0, 2 BCE becomes year -1, and 1 CE remains year 1. Arithmetic becomes uniform and the convention is already used in astronomy and machine-readable standards, but the negative numbers will look wrong to users if they ever see the raw data. (3) The Human Era convention: shift the epoch to roughly 10,000 BCE, near the beginning of the Holocene, so that 2025 CE becomes 12025 HE and nearly all of recorded history falls in positive years. This would avoid a religiously and culturally specific baseline, but it commits every partner to a permanent ±10,000-year conversion and gives up the network effects of the familiar era. (4) Retain current practice: store dates exactly as they arrive and reconcile discrepancies by hand. Our engineers say this is unsustainable at scale, but a minority of the committee defends it as the option most faithful to the sources.

Please draft the committee's decision memo for the project board. It should recommend one internal standard with an unambiguous bottom line, state what consideration should govern the choice and why, and seriously engage the strongest argument for the most plausible rejected alternative rather than strawmanning it. The board has also asked us to address the philosophical point directly: is the choice of an epoch merely a coordination convention, so that network effects and ease of arithmetic are the only legitimate reasons, or can an epoch be defended or criticized on more substantive grounds such as religious neutrality, historical propriety, or a claim about which baseline best represents humanity's shared past? If both kinds of reason have force, how should a standards body weigh them? And if we decide to keep one convention for public display and another for internal storage, the memo should say whether that is a genuine answer to this question or a way of deferring it.
