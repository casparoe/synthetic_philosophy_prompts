A colleague claims that when a person is briefly shown two events, there is already a determinate order in consciousness—say, event A before event B—and that any later report simply recovers that fixed order, within the usual limits of memory. I suspect this is a Cartesian theater picture, and that the order is instead assembled at the moment the person is probed: before the probe there are multiple parallel drafts of the event content, none of which is the conscious experience. How would one test the difference? Design a concrete experiment—manipulating, for example, stimulus timing, masking, probe delay, and the form of the question—and say exactly what pattern of results would favor each view. Then say what even a clean result would still leave open: for instance, whether the dispute merely moves to claims about memory, attention, or access, and at what point it stops being an empirical dispute and becomes a choice about what the phrase conscious experience should mean.
