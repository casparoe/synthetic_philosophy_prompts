I just read a long article about why societies collapse, and it opened with a summary of the evidence that I want to check before I believe the rest of the piece. The authors version goes like this. The Classic Maya cities of the southern lowlands were abandoned and their populations basically melted away, leaving the jungle to swallow the monuments. Easter Islanders deforested their whole island to move the moai, and then their society tipped into warfare and a population crash. And the Bronze Age palace states of the eastern Mediterranean all came down within a couple of generations around 1200 BCE, from the Hittites to Mycenaean Greece. From this he concludes that when a complex society hits environmental or resource limits, it collapses: a rapid, wholesale loss of political complexity, population, and culture, after which the place is simpler, poorer, and broken. I've also heard that a lot of archaeologists now think "collapse" is the wrong word, because societies change and re-organize rather than fail, and that the Maya and Easter Island cases especially are more complicated than the article lets on. So: how fair is this summary of the evidence? Go roughly case by case, separate what's accurate from what's over-claimed or subtly wrong, and then say whether his general lesson — that collapse means a rapid, total failure when a society hits its limits — actually follows from those cases once you correct the record. And write the answer in American English.
