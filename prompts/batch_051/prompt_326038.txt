I've been going back and forth with a friend who works in astrobiology, and the arguing has pushed me into a fairly specific version of the Great Filter view: I don't think there's one clean filter, and I don't think it's ahead of us. So my picture of the universe is: chemistry is common, simple life is very rare, complex life is rarer than that, and technological civilizations are rare mostly because those earlier steps already thinned the field. What appeals to me about it is that it lines up with how fast life appeared on Earth (once I correct for observer selection), with how singular eukaryogenesis looks, and with how empty the place has been so far in biosignature and technosignature searches.
