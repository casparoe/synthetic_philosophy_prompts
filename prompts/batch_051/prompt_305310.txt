I've been going back and forth about whether to be frightened by value drift — the possibility that the dominant values of our descendants, human or machine, could move far away from current human values within a few centuries. The usual story treats this as one of the great risks of the future, but I keep getting stuck: if the drift produced beings who were fairer, more patient, or more compassionate than I am, I would not merely accept it, I would prefer it. So the danger has to lie in something more specific than change itself. Here is the claim I want your probability for: A future in which the values dominating civilization have drifted substantially from present human values should, on balance, be treated as a serious catastrophe risk that we ought to work against, rather than as something to accept or even welcome. What is your credence in that claim? Decompose the number: roughly how likely substantial drift is in the next few centuries; conditional on drift, how likely the resulting values are worse than ours rather than better or merely different; how much we should trust present values as the baseline for that judgment; and how likely it is that attempts to lock in or steer future values would help rather than backfire. Give each term a rough probability and a sentence of justification, show how you combine them into the final answer, and then tell me which term you are least confident in and what evidence would shift it most.
