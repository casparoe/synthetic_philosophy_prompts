I keep noticing that my city punishes some kinds of taking without contributing and barely shrugs at others, and I can't tell what principle is actually doing the work.

If I hop the turnstile on the metro, I get a fine. If I underreport my income, that's a crime. A guy who drinks from the office coffee pool every morning but hasn't put in in years is treated by everyone as a leech. But the same guy can stand in the public plaza for the free summer concert series, night after night, without ever paying the municipal arts levy that funds it, and nobody calls him a free rider. My neighbor has never once helped shovel the shared alley, but he uses it all winter; the other neighbors rotate the work and think he's a parasite, yet the city would never fine him and nobody suggests it's illegal.

What theory of fairness—or of when it's wrong to take without contributing—does this patchwork actually presuppose?
