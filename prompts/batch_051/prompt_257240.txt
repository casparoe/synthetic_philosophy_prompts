After a career in experimental physics and several years of retirement reading in philosophy of science, I have been trying to set down my commitments about Bell's theorem and quantum nonlocality in a defensible form. I would welcome an audit of the following position from a professional philosopher of physics.

(1) Bell's theorem is sound, and the experiments beginning with Aspect and refined since genuinely violate the relevant Bell/CHSH inequalities. I take it as established that no local hidden-variable theory can reproduce the quantum-mechanical predictions.

(2) I accept Reichenbach's principle as a methodological default: a robust correlation between two events, neither of which causes the other, should be explained by a common cause in their shared past that screens off the correlation.

(3) I hold that special relativity imposes a fundamental causal constraint: no causal influence of any kind can propagate superluminally.

(4) I take the no-signalling theorem to dissolve the apparent conflict between quantum mechanics and relativity. Because entanglement cannot be used to transmit information, I see no physically interesting sense in which the Bell correlations are nonlocal.

(5) I regard measurement outcomes as definite, observer-independent events, and I find instrumentalist, QBist, and Everettian resolutions of the Bell problem unappealing.

(6) I find it unsatisfying to say that this is simply how quantum mechanics is. The Bell correlations are robust and reproducible, and as such they call for a causal explanation rather than being accepted as brute.

I can tell that some of these claims may pull against others, but I cannot tell which tensions are genuine and which are merely apparent. Please audit the set. Go through the pairwise relationships, distinguish real tensions from apparent ones, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each genuine tension say what the least costly revision would be and what it would cost. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set coherent. Please think through each pair carefully before settling on your assessment, but present only the final audit.

Format: lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — your overall verdict on whether this set of commitments is jointly tenable — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
