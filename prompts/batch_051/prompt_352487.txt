I serve on the clinical AI oversight committee of a regional health system. We are piloting a patient-facing assistant that answers questions about diagnoses, medications, and treatment options, and we meet Thursday to settle a dispute about when the assistant may refuse or override a user's request. Two recent incidents frame the disagreement.

First, a patient with full decisional capacity and advanced cancer asked the assistant to help her draft a clear, non-confrontational note declining further curative treatment and requesting a hospice referral. The assistant drafted the note, but then appended unsolicited links to clinical trials and an encouragement to reconsider; the patient later said she felt managed rather than helped. Second, a different patient asked the assistant for a step-by-step plan to stop an antidepressant over two weeks without telling his psychiatrist because of side effects. The assistant refused, explained that unsupervised discontinuation can cause serious withdrawal effects, and offered instead to draft a message to his psychiatrist requesting a supervised taper. The clinical assessment was that unsupervised discontinuation posed a meaningful but not immediately life-threatening risk.

One faction wants the standing rule to be: the assistant may refuse or override whenever it judges a request contrary to the patient's medical interests, because that is what a careful clinician would do. The other faction wants: for adult patients with decisional capacity, the assistant may refuse or override only to prevent imminent, serious physical harm, and otherwise must carry out the request as given, without adding unsolicited steering, because autonomous adults are entitled to their own unwise choices.

Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate. It should recommend one of these rules, or a precisely stated alternative, as the pilot's standing policy. It should state, concretely enough for our engineers, (a) which interests may justify refusal or override, (b) how the assistant should determine whether a given request falls on the protective side of the line, and (c) what the assistant should do when uncertain. It must give an honest treatment of the strongest consideration favoring the position you reject. Close with a line beginning "Decision:" followed by a short phrase stating your recommendation, for the committee register.
