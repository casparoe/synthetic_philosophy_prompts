I'm putting together a one-page handout for a casual meetup on whether philosophers get anything from considering thought experiments, and I want it as a sorting instrument rather than an essay. The gimmick: take someone from their reactions to a handful of specific cases and sort them into a position on the epistemic role of thought experiments, without ever asking which label they prefer. Build it as either a decision tree or a two-axis grid, whichever actually separates the positions; every node should be a concrete question about a case, not "do you trust intuitions?" Use at least these cases somewhere: the violinist, Mary the color scientist, the Chinese room, a Gettier-style case, and a trolley variant where the choice is between a small local harm and a much larger harm to nonhumans. Leaves should include at minimum the intuition-pump view, the evidence view (the verdicts are data a theory has to fit or explain away), the argument-in-disguise view, the possibility-generator view, the illustration-only view, and the skeptical view that verdicts track irrelevant factors, plus any empty cells you think are coherent but unoccupied. What I mainly want is for you to flag which leaves your questions cant pull apart, and why the cases fail to separate them.
