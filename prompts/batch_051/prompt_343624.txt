Suppose you are advising a small philanthropic foundation that has committed roughly £5 million over the next three years to improving the chances that some meaningful constraints survive the transition to artificial general intelligence. The foundation is willing to fund work on what it calls pre-AGI bargains: arrangements made now, among actors who are not yet in a position to enforce anything against smarter-than-human systems, that are meant to shape or constrain what those actors do later. The proposals it has received fall into four broad lines of work: (a) drafting conditional agreements, such as treaties that would activate once independently monitored capability thresholds are crossed; (b) designing escrow or deposit schemes, in which states or labs place assets under neutral control with release or forfeiture tied to post-transition conduct; (c) building technical verification capacity, including hardware-enabled attestation of training runs and compute governance, so that breaches could be detected in ways less dependent on the good faith of the parties; and (d) norm development, aimed at making certain post-AGI actions, such as unilateral copying of model weights or seizure of compute, reputationally costly long before they become possible.

The foundation cannot fund all four lines well, and I suspect they are not equally promising. Which should it prioritise, and in what order? I am asking for the reasoning rather than a list alone. For each candidate, please address: under what conditions would a bargain of that kind actually bind an actor that has become far more powerful than the original parties; how much depends on background assumptions about the speed of the transition, the concentration of leading capabilities, and whether post-AGI systems can be relied on to honour the spirit of commitments rather than merely their letter; and what evidence obtainable over the next two or three years would most change its ranking. If you had to fund only two lines, which two would you keep, and why are you confident enough in those to cut the others?

Please write your answer in British English.
