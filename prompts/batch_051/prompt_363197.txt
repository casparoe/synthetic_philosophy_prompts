A friend and I recently made a bet that I now half-regret, because once I had to name a number I found I could not settle on one. He takes the structuralist view: by 1929 or so, he says, Weimar Germany was living on borrowed time, and some authoritarian nationalist outcome was nearly overdetermined. My reply is that "some authoritarian nationalist outcome" is not the regime that actually emerged, and that the specific features we mean by Nazi Germany — the Führer cult, the single-party fascist character, the obsessive antisemitism that radicalized toward genocide, the appetite for a war of conquest — may have depended on one particular man and the party he built. I realize this rehearses an old historiographical dispute, roughly intentionalists versus structuralists, but we are trying to force it into a precise counterfactual rather than a literature review. The narrowed question is this: remove Hitler before the Nazis became an electoral force (say, he is killed in a brawl in 1928), hold every larger structural condition fixed, and ask whether German history still converges on something a reasonably informed observer would recognize as Nazi Germany.

I want your actual credence in the following proposition, as precisely as you can state it: by the end of the 1930s, Germany would still have become an explicitly antisemitic single-party fascist dictatorship under a charismatic leader of the radical right, and by the mid-1940s that regime would have initiated a major European war. Do not load the proposition with the further claim that the Holocaust would have occurred in anything like its actual form; judge only the regime form and the war. And do not end on "it depends on one's theory of counterfactuals." I want to see the derivation: a headline number, then the sub-questions about (a) whether another figure could plausibly have unified the fractured radical right into a comparable movement and Führer cult, (b) whether the traditional conservative elites would have installed some dictatorship anyway, and how antisemitic it would have been, (c) how much of the outcome was already fixed by the Depression, Versailles, and the specific weaknesses of the Weimar constitution, and (d) how much of your residual uncertainty is really skepticism about whether counterfactual historical claims have determinate truth-values at all. For each sub-question you use, assign a rough probability; show briefly how they combine into the headline number; and identify the one sub-question that the strongest conceivable evidence would move most, and in which direction.

Format: exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, no introduction or conclusion. Item 1 states your headline credence. Items 2 through 4 each present one sub-question with its rough probability and a note on how it bears on the headline. Item 5 names the sub-question your number is most sensitive to and the evidence that would move it, and in which direction.
