I've been in a reading group on Scanlon's contractualism for a few months, and we keep circling the aggregation problem. One of us thinks the entire appeal of the view is that you can't justify a serious harm to one person by pointing to a huge pile of smaller benefits to other people, because each individual gets to reasonably reject the principle and numbers, by themselves, aren't the kind of thing that anyone in particular should have to accept. The other thinks this makes contractualism a non-starter as a moral theory, since numbers do seem to matter in some constrained way; the interesting question is how, not whether. We've both refined our positions enough that we're no longer sure which disagreements are real, which are just different ways of describing trade-offs, and which come from the fact that we disagree about what contractualism is trying to do in the first place. We decided the useful output would be a document from an adversarial collaboration.

Draft the joint statement that a committed Scanlon-style contractualist, who accepts something like the individualist restriction, and a committed moderate aggregator, who thinks numbers of complaints can matter but only under constraints, could both sign. I want a first section of numbered claims they can both accept about the contractualist formula, what aggregation means, how the standard cases should be described, and what the two views are not committed to. Then a disagreements table: for each point of disagreement, a precise statement of the contractualist position and the aggregator position, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording. Then a section on cruxes: the two or three sub-questions that would actually have to be settled for one side to move, with, for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it and each side's prediction written down in advance. Include at least one non-standard case where the numbers are very large and the individual cost is very small, like a data policy that imposes trivial privacy losses on millions of people to prevent a few serious harms. If any part of the dispute looks merely verbal, say which and why.
