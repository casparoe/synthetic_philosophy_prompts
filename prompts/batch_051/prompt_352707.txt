I've been going back and forth with a couple of friends about the singleton hypothesis, and I'd like an essay that actually takes a side instead of just surveying the debate. The way we're using the term, a singleton is a situation where one decision-making system at the level of the whole species — not necessarily a world government; it could be a dense web of treaties and institutions, a superintelligence, or something else altogether — can effectively stop any other actor from doing an end-run around global rules. One friend keeps saying this framing is loaded, because it treats agents as cleanly bounded units when real coordination is patchy and overlapping; he thinks you could get most of the goods people credit to a singleton (binding climate rules, an end to arms races, controlled AI development) through a mess of institutions that nobody would call one. My other friend replies that if the mess actually secured those goods, it would just be a singleton in the functional sense, even if it didn't look like a government. Underneath this I suspect its really a question about whether the interesting claim is descriptive — that competitive dynamics push toward consolidation, for better or worse — or normative — that we should try to bring a singleton about, or maybe try to avoid one.

Write an essay on whether "singleton vs. multipolar" is a useful axis for thinking about global coordination, and on whether we should expect or aim for a singleton. Weigh the "agents are billiard balls" objection against the functional reply, and defend a definite verdict — a nuanced one is fine, but don't just catalogue considerations. At the end, say how confident you are in the overall verdict and single out the specific point you are least sure of. Keep it under 800 words.
