Construct a thought experiment in which a committed Kantian agent, not a consequentialist, displays exactly the alienation Bernard Williams identified when a moral theory interposes a justifying thought between an agent and someone they care about. The purpose of the case is to test whether that objection generalises beyond impartial consequentialism, so the case fails if the alienation stems from the agent misapplying Kantianism, from a conflict of duties, or from anything a well-informed Kantian could disclaim.

Constraints. Keep the scene quotidian rather than dramatic: no rescues, no life-threatening emergencies, no saving a drowning spouse. The agent should be acting from genuine personal concern — comforting a grieving friend, standing by a colleague under unfair attack, tending to an ill relative — in a situation where no duty conflicts with the partial act. The extra thought must be one Kantian moral theory positively requires of a scrupulous agent, not a neurotic addition; name the maxim or duty involved and say where the theory prescribes it. And the case must leave the agent with no easy retreat: omit the thought, and something the theory treats as morally important is lost; include it, and Williams's charge that it is one thought too many takes hold.

After the scenario, run the demonstration. State what the agent thinks and why Kantianism requires it, then explain why Williams would classify that thought as one too many by his own criterion, and identify the structural feature of the case that does the work rather than any colourful detail of the agent's personality. Then argue the reverse: show why the Kantian cannot drop the thought without surrendering a commitment the theory will not readily give up, and give the strongest reply a sophisticated Kantian could make. Conclude with whether Williams would take that reply to answer the objection or merely to reproduce it at a higher level, and say which single change to the case would collapse it back into Williams's original target, and what that tells you about how deep the generalisation runs.
