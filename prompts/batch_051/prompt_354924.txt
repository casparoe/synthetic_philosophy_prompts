I run the moderation team for a social VR platform: around 200k monthly users with persistent worlds, player-made buildings, inventories, and a real-money exchange for some but far from all items. The community has grown to the point where alot of our reports are about stuff that's neither clearly a technical exploit nor clearly the kind of harassment our old rules were written for: someone deletes a hand-built meeting hall through a permissions bug, an avatar repeatedly presses up against another user whose space bubble is turned off, an ex-partner destroys a shared virtual garden after a breakup, a group steals a one-of-a-kind item worth thousands on the grey market but nothing in our official economy. We clear maybe forty reports a day, each with a few minutes of volunteer mod time, and wrong calls produce both forum dumpster fires and actual, real-world upset.

Our current policy says "virtual harms are real enough to act on" and then leaves it entirely to the mod's gut. We are small, so I need a reusable procedure—a checklist or decision tree in words—that mods with no philosophy background can apply. It should help decide which virtual objects and virtual harms to treat as real enough to warrant muting, restoring, suspending, or a permaban. The conceptual question is whether there is a principled line between a virtual harm that deserves real-world sanction and a bad thing happening inside a game that should be handled by game mechanics, and what does the actual work: labored time, emotional attachment, consent, embodiment, real-money value, recognizability of the space, something else?

Give me the procedure, and work it through at least three cases: one where it says we should sanction even though almost no objective, dollar-denominated loss occurred; one where it tells us not to sanction even though an enormous amount of in-game value was destroyed; and one genuinely mixed case where the procedure gives a provisional answer but flags that two mods could reasonably disagree. For each, say which step of the procedure does the work. Also state clearly where the procedure stops being able to settle it and a human has to decide, and what the checklist should then tell the mod to write up so the decision can be reviewed later.
