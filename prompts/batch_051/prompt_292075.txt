I'm a grad student in physics, mostly working on holography, and I keep hearing people in my group say spacetime 'emerges from entanglement.' Half the time it's said like it's a deep physical discovery and half the time like it's just a neat way to encode the same equations. I want to know if anything empirical can actually seperate those two readings. Could you design a test — even an in-principle one, or one in an analog system if the full quantum gravity version is out of reach — that would distinguish the strong claim, that entanglement literally constitutes spacetime, from the weak claim, that entanglement is just a good bookkeeping device for geometry? What exactly would you measure: mutual information between regions, deviations from the area law for entanglement entropy, how the emergent geometry responds when you disentangle two boundary subregions, something else? Spell out which results would count for which reading, and then say what even a clean positive result would still leave open. I'm particularly worried that any observation will just show entanglement and geometry tracking each other, which by itself doesn't tell us which is doing the grounding, or whether 'emerges' is even the right word rather than something like 'encodes.'
