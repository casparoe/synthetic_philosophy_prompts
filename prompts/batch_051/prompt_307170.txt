Write the desiderata for an adequate rule of belief revision when the evidence arrives in conditional form. The benchmark is the Judy Benjamin problem: an agent has a prior over a four-cell partition — A∧E, A∧¬E, ¬A∧E, ¬A∧¬E — and then receives a message that fixes only the conditional probability of E given A, say P(E|A)=2/3, while giving no information about the probability of A itself; the rule must dictate her posterior over the four cells. Set out the full list of requirements such a rule should meet, with the consideration behind each. Cover the cases it must get right (including the degenerate cases in which the message coincides with learning an unconditional proposition, and iterated messages), the invariances it should respect (such as independence from how the same conditional constraint is numerically encoded), the intuitive verdicts it must preserve or explain away (in particular, whether learning that E is probable conditional on A may itself move the probability of A), and the distinctions it must not collapse (e.g., between a conditional constraint and its material counterpart). Then flag which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of one camp, say whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable, and identify the single requirement that excludes the most leading proposals — Bayesian conditionalization where it applies, Jeffrey conditioning over a partition, and minimum cross-entropy updating. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring the proposals.
