After retiring from a career in atmospheric physics, I accepted a position on the three-member safety board of a small institute that conducts field-research campaigns. No campaign may proceed without the board's approval. For each proposal, the board assesses two conditions — call them A, the adequacy of the hazard-control plan, and B, the adequacy of the monitoring and contingency plan. Each of the three members records a yes or no on A, a yes or no on B, and a final vote to approve or to reject. Each member's final vote is determined by her two condition votes: she approves exactly when she votes yes on both A and B, and there are no abstentions or split positions. The board has never codified how the three individual votes are to be combined into a decision; it has simply handed down a decision in each case.

Our decided cases to date are as follows. Members are labelled I, II, and III, and the last column is the board's recorded decision.

| Case | Member I | Member II | Member III | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A: yes, B: yes, final: approve | A: yes, B: yes, final: approve | A: yes, B: no, final: reject | Approved |
| 2 | A: yes, B: yes, final: approve | A: no, B: yes, final: reject | A: no, B: yes, final: reject | Rejected |
| 3 | A: yes, B: no, final: reject | A: yes, B: no, final: reject | A: yes, B: yes, final: approve | Rejected |
| 4 | A: yes, B: yes, final: approve | A: yes, B: yes, final: approve | A: yes, B: yes, final: approve | Approved |

A new proposal is now before us, with this distribution of votes:

| Member I | Member II | Member III |
|---|---|---|
| A: yes, B: yes, final: approve | A: yes, B: no, final: reject | A: no, B: yes, final: reject |

If one tallies only the condition votes, A passes 2–1 and B passes 2–1; if one tallies only the final votes, approval loses 1–2. The board has never before faced exactly this configuration.

I would be grateful for a careful opinion on what the board should decide if it wishes to remain consistent with its own record. Please identify the two natural rules for combining our three individual votes that fit every one of the past decisions, and show that they now point in opposite directions. Say which prior case each side would invoke as the closest analogue, and state, as precisely as you can, what general principle the board would be adopting under each of the two available decisions. If the earlier decisions genuinely fail to settle the present one, say so plainly and give your best recommendation nonetheless, with reasons. Finally, describe a further configuration of the three members' votes whose outcome would most clearly reveal which of the two rules the board is in fact applying.
