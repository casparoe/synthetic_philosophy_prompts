Over the past two years I have repeatedly watched setbacks get met with appeals to grit, fortitude, and resilience rather than with any serious inquiry into the conditions that produced them. I have come to hold the view that this discourse is ideological: it converts institutional and social failures into individual tests of character, and it systematically favors the conclusion that the person who is struggling has the wrong inner disposition. I am more interested in being corrected than in staying comfortable, so I want to know what would count against this view. What evidence, arguments, or plausible developments should move me, sorted from decisive to merely awkward? Which of these should I, as a defender of the view, concede count against it rather than dismiss, and has the view been qualified in ways that insulate it from disconfirmation? If it has, is that a defect for a view of this kind, or a reasonable feature of a thesis about how moral language is used?
