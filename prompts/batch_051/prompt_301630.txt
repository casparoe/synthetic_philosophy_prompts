Our institute's research-integrity policy says: 'No member may publish, cite, or rely upon any account of a model's internal behavior produced by an automated system.' Pretty clearly, the drafters had in mind someone asking a general chatbot to explain a model's decisions and pasting it into a paper. But my group wants to run a pipeline where model A writes internal-behavior accounts of target model B; no human reads those accounts, but they're fed, along with B's activations, into model C, which learns sparse features. The humans then publish claims about B based on C's features. At no point does a person publish, cite, or obviously rely on A's accounts—the model does. Does the clause reach us? I'm less interested in the practical answer than in what plain wording, purpose, what the drafters would've said if asked, and what a reasonable reader would each give, and whether 'rely upon' can bear the weight of stopping a chain of machine-to-machine interpretation where humans only appear at the final step.
