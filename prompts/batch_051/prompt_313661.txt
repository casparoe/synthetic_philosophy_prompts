Design a "Which view of affirmative action do you actually hold?" sorting quiz for a comparative discussion group. It should have eight to ten questions, each built around a concrete case drawn from one of three settings: US university admissions, Indian reservations in higher education and public employment, and Norway's boardroom gender quota. Each question should offer two or three answer options and ask the respondent to judge the policy in the case on its merits, never to endorse a label; the cases should be chosen so that positions genuinely come apart on them. Use only well-known structural features of the three regimes (for example, a holistic admissions file in which ethnic identity is one plus-factor among several, a reserved seat going to a wealthy member of a beneficiary group, a board quota that does not itself alter a firm's lower-level hiring pipeline). Provide a scoring key that maps patterns of answers onto at least these positions: compensatory justice, diversity/instrumental, anti-subordination, and procedural/colorblind. Build the key from the logical space of answers rather than from the list of names: first identify which questions are intended to be diagnostic, then locate each position in that space, note which answer combinations are empty and whether each is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and state explicitly which positions the instrument cannot separate. The audience is an educated layperson who has not read the philosophical literature.
