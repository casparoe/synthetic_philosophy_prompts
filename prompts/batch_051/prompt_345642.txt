I'm a PhD student in political science working on electoral system design, and I keep coming back to a parallel I can't decide about. Under single-member plurality (FPTP), a distribution of first-preference votes gets collapsed into a single winner, and the spread, multimodality, and tail of the preference distribution disappear from the result; under low-threshold proportional representation, the result instead tries to display the distribution itself, with seat shares tracking vote shares. This looks to me structurally like the choice in Bayesian analysis between reporting a point estimate, such as a posterior mode, and reporting the full posterior distribution: the point estimate is compact and decision-ready but discards information about uncertainty, spread, and where the mass lies, while the posterior keeps that structure at the cost of not being a single answer. Is this the same trade-off between decision and description, or am I pattern-matching? Concretely, if the analogy holds, what corresponds on the statistical side to wasted votes, strategic voting, and threshold effects; and where does the mapping break down — for example, a vote-share distribution is a vector of observed revealed preferences, whereas a posterior is uncertainty about an unobserved parameter, and PR changes the incentives of parties and voters in a way that choosing a fuller statistical report does not? Is there a genuine identity of structure here, a loose but useful analogy, or a confusion?
