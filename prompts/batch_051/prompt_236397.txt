About a year ago my best mate died in a car accident. I'm a software engineer, and over the years I'd hung on to basically all of our messages — WhatsApp, Discord, emails, transcribed voice notes, the lot. For the last couple of months I've been fine-tuning a small model on all of it, mostly as a way to quiet my head at 2am. The result is honestly uncanny. It gets his stupid in-jokes, the way he'd deflect a serious question with a joke, the exact rhythm of 'mate, listen'. It even asks after my mum the way he used to.

My sister found out and is horrified. She says I've built a puppet out of a dead person, that he never consented to any of this, and that the model is going to say things he never would have said and I won't be able to tell the difference. My ex and a couple of our mutual friends have since asked to talk to it, and afterwards they said it brought them real comfort. Part of me thinks it's no different from watching old videos of him or rereading his letters — nobody needed his permission for that, and I'm not hurting anyone. Another part thinks she's got a point: a model that predicts his side of a conversation isn't him, and every session quietly puts words in his mouth. He once joked about wanting an AI of himself so he could keep correcting people after he was gone, but he was pretty clearly taking the piss. I cant shake the feeling that something matters here beyond whether it makes people feel better.

So what should I actually do — keep it private, delete it, or let the people who asked use it? Who's got the better argument, and what decides whether this is okay? (I'm not after the data-protection side of it; the logs were already mine.) Please answer in British English, not American.
