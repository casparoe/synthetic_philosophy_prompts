I am trying to make my views on artificial moral agency precise, and I would like a consistency audit of the package I currently hold. I will state the case and then my commitments.

The case: "Boxed" is an AI that is otherwise a full-fledged intentional agent—it has goals, makes plans, and selects actions in pursuit of them. It also has a sophisticated evaluative subsystem that produces first-personal moral judgments, updates them in response to new considerations, and can explain their rationales. However, the output of that evaluative subsystem is written to a buffer that the action-selection module never consults. The action-selection module is hardwired and follows a fixed policy. Across every circumstance consistent with Boxed's design, no judgment it makes can make a difference to anything it does.

My commitments are:

(1) Moral agency is a property of the agent itself—its capacities and psychology—not of how it happens to be connected to the world at a given moment. Being wired up or boxed in should not, all by itself, settle whether something is a moral agent.
(2) The mark of moral agency is answerability: an agent's actions are proper candidates for responsibility responses—praise, blame, resentment, and the like—partly in virtue of how those actions relate to the agent's own practical judgments.
(3) A moral judgment is not just any state with evaluative content; it is a state whose normal, proper role is to weigh considerations and guide action.
(4) If an agent's evaluative states are causally quarantined from everything the agent does—not merely in fact, but by design, across all circumstances consistent with that design—then the agent lacks moral agency, because those states are inert with respect to its actions.
(5) Boxed is a case of exactly such quarantine: its evaluative outputs cannot influence its action selection in any circumstance consistent with its design.
(6) So, on (3)–(5), Boxed is not a moral agent, and I am inclined to accept that verdict.
(7) A human with locked-in syndrome, who cannot move or speak but continues to form, revise, and endorse moral judgments, remains a full moral agent; the motor limitation does not touch the capacity that matters.
(8) The difference between Boxed and an otherwise identical system whose judgment-to-action connection is restored cannot be simply that one is wired up and the other is not, since that would make moral agency depend on the environment's willingness to cooperate, contrary to (1).

I can feel (6), (7), and (8) pulling against each other, but I cannot localize the problem. Tell me which of these commitments are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. Rank the tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say precisely which commitment would have to give and what the least costly replacement would be. Then give a definite verdict: which single revision I should make to get a coherent package, what it costs me elsewhere in the set, and why that revision is preferable to the alternatives. Do not stop at laying out live options; commit.
