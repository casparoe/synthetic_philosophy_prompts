I'm the only student on my school's academic integrity committee (we're the ones who decide if someone gets a zero, a suspension, or a retake). We keep getting cases where the kid in front of us didn't actually do the cheating. Last month it was a kid who shared his homework with half the class, a kid who built a cheat sheet but got sick on test day and never used it, and a kid who just knew a whole row was copying off each other and said nothing. Our current test is something like asking whether the cheating would have happened anyway without this person, and it keeps feeling wrong—people can be super involved without actually causing the cheating. Could you write us a reusable decision procedure for figuring out how responsible someone is when they participated in cheating without causing it? We get about 20 minutes per case, nobody on the board is a lawyer, and the two teachers on the board alway disagree about whether just knowing about it should count. The procedure should be a checklist or a sequence of questions we run in order, it should tell us which steps are value calls instead of factual calls, and it should be honest about where it just can't answer. Then run it on those three cases, and make sure at least one of the outcomes is one you'd expect us to hate.
