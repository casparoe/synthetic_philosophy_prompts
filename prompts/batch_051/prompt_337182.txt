We've been doing a unit in my moral philosophy class on whether ranking historical atrocities is even coherent, and I've realized I have a firm policy in discussions: I won't make or accept any argument that depends on one atrocity being worse than another. My view is that a ranking only looks objective because it smuggles in a metric—body count, percentage of a population killed, duration, intent, how much of a culture was destroyed—and the choice of metric does the moral work in advance. But my TA pushed back that some decisions force a comparison anyway, like where to intervene, how to apologize or pay reparations, or what to cover when the semester is ten weeks long. Is my rule defensible, or is it a way of dodging comparisons I just find uncomfortable? Is there a version of the rule that keeps the suspicion without leaving me silent in cases where I actually have to choose?
