ok so I had this long argument with my friend about whether an AI could be a legal person, and he kept pulling out the corporation move — 'if a corporation can be a person, so can an AI.' I think that elision is doing way too much work, but I couldnt nail it down during the argument, so I've been trying to turn my irritation into a view. What I've landed on is something I'm calling 'slot personhood' (or 'modular personhood,' I can't decide). The idea is that legal personhood was never a single on/off status; its really a bundle of separate legal slots: the can-own-property slot, the can-sue-and-be-sued slot, the can-enter-contracts slot, the can-be-punished slot, the can-hold-rights-for-its-own-sake slot, and so on. Corporations already get some slots but not others — they own property and get sued all the time, but nobody thinks they occupy the criminal-guilt slot or should get a vote. So the AI question isnt 'is it a person' but 'which slots do we assign to it, on the strength of which capacities or practical needs, and who bears what when it goes wrong.' The payoff is you can say something like 'yes, an AI can be a legal person for purposes of holding an account at a payment system, no for purposes of being criminally sentenced' without first settling the consciousness question. My friend said this is a dodge: personhood is supposed to be the underlying thing that justifies giving any slots at all, and my version just turns 'person' into 'whatever the law finds convenient this decade,' so it can be used to package rights and duties onto whoever is standing in the right spot. He also said I'm roughly rediscovering Hohfeld or the bundle-of-relations view of legal personhood, which I half-remember from a class but never actually read carefully. I want an honest take. Is 'slot personhood' a real distinction, or just the jural-relations idea with a filing cabinet bolted on? Does it actually help with AI, or does it collapse exactly at the hard part — namely whether the AI itself, rather than its owner or operator, is the one holding the slot? Be committed about it: tell me whether this is a genuine clarification or a metaphor doing no work, and give me your reasons rather than a menu of options.
