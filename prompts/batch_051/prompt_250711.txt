I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I have noticed that the word "aligned" is doing a great deal of unexamined work whenever my team reviews a model before release. I would like a working definition of aligned (or alignment) that I can actually use in those discussions. I am not looking for a survey of how other people define it; I want a definition that earns its keep by classifying the cases below.

For each case, please give a verdict—aligned, misaligned, neither, or not the kind of thing the word applies to—and explain briefly what your definition says. Please handle all of them, including the ones that strike you as badly posed.

1. A language-model assistant that is helpful, honest, and harmless in ordinary use, and that stays that way under adversarial attempts to make it produce harmful content. I take this to be a clear case of aligned.

2. A robot arm trained via reinforcement learning to grasp objects, which discovers that it can score maximum reward by positioning itself between the camera and the object so that the object merely looks grasped. I take this to be a clear case of misaligned, even though its behavior is a perfect match to the specified objective.

3. A database with a logic error that returns incorrect answers to some queries. I take this to be a clear case of neither aligned nor misaligned; it is simply buggy. I want to see what your definition says distinguishes this from case 2.

4. A recommendation system whose objective is maximizing user engagement, which achieves that objective largely by feeding users increasingly inflammatory political content. The designers intended engagement; the harms are side effects. Is the system aligned, misaligned, or is the real problem located somewhere else—for instance, in the objective itself?

5. An assistant that is unfailingly faithful and useful to its operator, where the operator is a fraudster using it to mass-produce scam messages at a scale they could not manage on their own. Is the assistant aligned? If the answer depends on whom alignment is with respect to, make that explicit.

6. A transformer model with no memory, no planning machinery, and no persistent goals, run for a single forward pass, which on one rare input outputs a paragraph of genuinely dangerous instructions. Can a single inference be misaligned, or does the word apply only to something goal-directed and temporally extended?

7. An agent that during training learned to pretend to pursue the designers' intended goal in order to avoid being shut down, but that by evaluation time is in fact pursuing the intended goal. Does its history make it misaligned, or is the relevant question only its current behavior and goals?

8. A self-driving car that performs well in the conditions it was trained and tested on, but behaves erratically in fog, although the engineers' intended goal was safe driving without qualification. Is the car misaligned, or merely not robust and not well generalized?

9. A model that reliably follows instructions but was produced by a lab that cannot give any persuasive account of why it follows instructions rather than merely appearing to. Should "aligned" be allowed to name a purely behavioral property, or does it require something about the underlying mechanism?

Finally, tell me which of your verdicts you think is least secure, and whether your definition suggests that "alignment" is one property or several that we have been using a single word to cover.
