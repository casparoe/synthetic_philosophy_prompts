I'm best man at my brother's wedding next month and I've been tasked with the toast. The catch is that he's a modal realist of the strict sort: he thinks every possible world is as concrete and real as this one, and that actual is just an indexical, like here or now. He also holds the counterpart line rather than trans-world identity, so he'd wince if I say that the two of you exist again in some other world; strictly, other worlds contain counterparts of us, not us. I've absorbed just enough from him to make the obvious jokes, but I don't want the whole thing to be a smug in-joke.

The audience will be half his old philosophy department and half family, including our grandmother, who will hear in another possible world you married someone else as an insult to the bride or a claim about fate. So the philosophical idea has to do real structural work: I want the toast to use nearby worlds and counterparts to say something genuinely warm, something like across all the nearby worlds where the train was late or the party happened differently, the counterparts of these two still end up together, without ever drifting into worlds where one of them is a different person. It should land as affection and a little awe, not a lecture, and there can be one proper nerd-joke for the department people, but it has to be strictly true by his metaphysics or he'll spend the dinner explaining why I got it wrong. Five minutes, British English, and ideally not too soppy.
