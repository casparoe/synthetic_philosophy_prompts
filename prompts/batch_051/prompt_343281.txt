I'm writing a novel in which one character is a genuinely good person to a degree that makes everyone around her uncomfortable, and I've been reading Susan Wolf's essay Moral Saints to figure out why. Wolf seems to argue that the moral saint is not a good human ideal because saintliness crowds out nonmoral values—artistic excellence, deep personal relationships, idly trying new restaurants, stuff like that. But here's what I dont get: if those nonmoral goods only matter because people happen to care about them, then a person who doesn't care about them isn't missing anything. So if my character is happy being a saint, and she is, has Wolf actually shown there's anything wrong with her? Or is her objection just that most people would be unhappy living like that, which is more about us than about the saint? I want to get the character right, not turn her into a sermon.
