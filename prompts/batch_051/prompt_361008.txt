I am taking a seminar this term on the distribution of consciousness across the animal kingdom, and I have to write up my settled view before the rest of the class examines it. I would like a consistency audit of the following commitments, which I have reached over the course of the semester:

1. Realism about sentience. For any animal, there is a fact of the matter whether it is at least minimally phenomenally conscious, even where the evidence is poor or the animal is very unlike us.
2. Gradedness. Phenomenal consciousness comes in degrees. There is no sharp seam anywhere across the animal kingdom between being dimly conscious and being richly conscious, and no amount of added neurobiological detail would by itself reveal one.
3. Nociception is insufficient. Reflexive withdrawal from tissue damage is never sufficient for felt pain. I require some kind of integrated, first-personal evaluation of a body state — a minimal self-model or its functional equivalent — before I will count a state as felt.
4. Convergence matters as much as homology. When an animal shows flexible, learning-based responses to bodily damage that are implemented in a centralized, integrative nervous system, I take that as prima facie evidence of felt affect, even if the architecture evolved independently of ours.
5. Case judgments. I consider teleost fish at least minimally sentient, but I do not think the current behavioral and neurological evidence yet supports insect sentience.
6. Precautionary threshold. For policy purposes, a non-negligible probability that members of a taxon are sentient is enough to require treating them as sentient in research and farming, even before the science is settled.
7. The line is partly ours. The boundary between sentient and insentient animals is not purely the discovery of a hidden joint in nature; in hard cases it is partly a decision about how to use the concept of sentience.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only seem to be. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, explain for each what I would have to give up or revise, and say what the cheapest coherent version of the overall view would look like.
