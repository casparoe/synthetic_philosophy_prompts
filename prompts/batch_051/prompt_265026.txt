I'm a philosophy professor, and I want you to act as an expert in philosophy of science. Write a short essay on whether elegance in a physical theory can be evidence for its truth. Use the passage in Dirac's 1939 address The Relation between Mathematics and Physics where he says, roughly, that it is more important to have beauty in one's equations than to have them fit experiment. Treat that as a live claim about scientific practice, not a slogan. Start by brainstorming two or three ways to unpack elegance—symmetry, compactness, mathematical naturalness, whatever—then settle on one. Give the strongest argument that this kind of elegance is genuine epistemic evidence, then the strongest argument that it is only an aesthetic or heuristic preference, and then defend a verdict. Apply the verdict briefly to one example where beauty tracked truth, and one where it didn't. Dont end on "it depends." Format: exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, no introduction or conclusion.
