I've got a standing rule for when people throw evolutionary explanations at me. If someone offers, say, the claim that men take risks because risk-taking signaled fitness to potential mates in the ancestral environment, I file it under just-so stories unless they can tell me, up front, what observation would come out differently if their story were false but some rival story were right. Otherwise the field can explain both a behavior and it's opposite with equal confidence — risk-taking as a fitness display and risk-aversion as self-preservation, depending on which result you already have — and I just can't take that as science. A friend who's a biology grad student says I'm being unfair: historical sciences often can't run experiments, and inference to the best explanation is a legitimate way to get at the truth — I wouldn't refuse a root cause in a cloud outage postmortem just because we can't rerun the outage. But a postmortem has logs and metrics; evolutionary psychology seems to have mostly correlations and a lot of narrative. So: is my rule defensible, or am I just dressing up a bias as a methodology? Is there a tightened version of it that would let me accept the good evo-psych claims while still filtering the stories? And am I holding this field to a standard I don't actually apply to my own debugging?
