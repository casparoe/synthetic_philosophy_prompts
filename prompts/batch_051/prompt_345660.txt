I'm in a philosophy of biology seminar this term, and our unit on biological function has been stuck for three weeks. The room is split between two camps. One side, the etiological or selected-effects folks, say the function of a trait is whatever effect it was selected for: the heart is for pumping blood, not for making heart sounds, because pumping blood is what caused hearts to spread through the population. The other side, the causal-role folks, say the function of a trait is just its contribution to some larger capacity of the system you've chosen to analyze — so whether the heart's function is pumping blood or making a lub-dub sound depends on whether you're analyzing the circulatory system or the acoustic system, and evolutionary history does no work in the analysis. What's strange is that nobody disagrees about the facts or even the hard cases: everyone grants that a spontaneously mutated heart-like organ with no selection history pumps blood but wasn't selected to; everyone agrees a broken heart is malfunctioning in a way a tumor isn't; everyone agrees feathers were selected for thermoregulation before they were used for flight. But the two sides just keep re-describing these cases in their own vocabulary and going around the same circle.

My working theory is that this is purely verbal: "function" is being used for two different explanatory jobs — why the trait exists at all versus what a part is doing in the system right now — and once you separate those jobs the debate evaporates. But my professor keeps pushing back that this is too fast, because the real prize is normativity: the selected-effects view gives you a fact of the matter about malfunction (a heart that can't pump blood is broken), while the causal-role view seems to make malfunction relative to whatever system you decide to care about. So maybe beneath the surface there's a substantive question about whether malfunction talk needs a selection history. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, substantive, methodological, maybe a disagreement about what a theory of function is even supposed to do — and, given that diagnosis, what would count as making progress, if anything would. Dont just lay out the two views; I already know them. And please write in American English.
