Write a joke that turns on why credit rating agencies and forecasters can almost never be hit with a defamation suit even when they're hilariously wrong. The punchline should actually hinge on the legal doctrine, not just on 'people are bad at predicting': the usual reason is that a prediction isn't an assertion of existing fact, and a subjective-sounding rating is protected so long as a reasonable reader wouldn't take it as stating something provably false — but if the 'opinion' implies a false underlying fact, the protection dissapears. So the humor could come from someone trying to smuggle facts out in opinion clothing, or hedging so hard he stops saying anything at all. It should be smart enough that someone who knows Milkovich and the fact/opinion distinction finds it accurate, and still funny if all you know is that you can't sue the weatherman for a bad forecast. Legal jargon is fine but don't let the joke just be jargon.
