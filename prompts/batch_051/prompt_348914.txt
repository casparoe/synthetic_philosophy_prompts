Van Fraassen's Reflection principle says that, for any proposition A and any later time t, your present credence in A should equal your present expectation of your credence in A at t (P(A) = Σ_x P(P_t(A)=x)·x). Suppose the principle is proposed as a fully general constraint on rational belief. Rank the following apparent counterexamples from most to least damaging to the unqualified principle: (1) you know you will have forgotten a decisive piece of evidence by next month; (2) you are told a pill you will take tonight will make you nearly certain of a proposition you now disbelieve; (3) you expect your general reasoning abilities to decline, so that next year's credences will be less reliable than today's; (4) you expect to be dead before t, so there will be no future credence at all; (5) you expect to receive substantial relevant evidence before t but cannot anticipate its direction. For each item, give the best argument that it is a genuine counterexample and the best reply a defender of the unqualified principle could give; then say what is settled about such cases, what is genuinely contested among philosophers, and how confident you are in your ranking. Do not present a contested reading of Reflection as consensus.
