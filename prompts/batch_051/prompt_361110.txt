I'm a PhD student in early modern history of science, and I've developed a policy that I'm starting to think might just be a bias with good branding. I basically refuse to use period labels as if they name real things. In my own writing I always write "the so-called Scientific Revolution" or put Middle Ages in scare quotes, and I flinch when someone says the Renaissance began in Florence and spread outward from there. When I'm reading, I mentally flag any paper that opens by taking for granted a sentence like "during the Scientific Revolution..." and I trust the rest of the argument a little less until I've checked whether the label is actually doing the work. Last week I caught myself writing off a colleague's chapter on Paracelsus because he kept saying the Renaissance, even though his actual argument was careful and didn't depend on the label at all.

The justification I give myself is that these terms are retrospective inventions (mostly nineteenth- and twentieth-century coinages projected backward) and that they bake in a story—darkness, rebirth, revolution—so treating them as real things is how you get teleological history. The Middle Ages wasnt the middle of anything except a later fantasy about modernity, and talk of "the Scientific Revolution" smuggles in a picture of a single event with a location and a cast list. But I also know the counter-move: nearly every general term in history is a retrospective abstraction. I happily say "early modern" and "the economy" and "the family," and those are constructs too. So my line isnt even internally consistent; I just happen to have been trained to be suspicious of three particular words.

So the actual question: is this policy defensible, or is it mostly a way of signalling that I've read some historiography and a way of policing other people's language? If it's partially defensible, what's the defensible version—something like: period labels may be used to index dates and regions but must never be doing explanatory work? Is there a principled way to tell when a period term is innocent bookkeeping versus smuggling in a narrative? And given that I don't have time to re-derive the periodization debate every time I write the phrase Scientific Revolution, am I stuck choosing between being a pedant and being a sleepwalker?

Treat yourself as an expert on philosophy of history and conceptual change—the sort of person who can see what a period label actually does. Please think through this carefully, maybe start by brainstorming the different jobs a period label can perform, then evaluate the strongest version of my rule and tell me whether I should keep it, drop it, or replace it with something better.
