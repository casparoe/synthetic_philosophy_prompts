So a few years ago my brother-in-law collapsed at a family picnic, heart just stopped. They did CPR forever, got his heart going again in the ambulance, but he never woke up; by the time I got to the ICU he was on a ventilator and the doctors called it brain death and said he was legally gone, even though the machine made his chest rise and his skin felt warm. That has been chewing at me ever since, and somewhere along the line I started silently collecting cases to see if my gut about when someone's dead is even consistent. Here's where I've landed, and I swear the cases pull in different directions.

Him: heart restarted, but no blood flow to the brain, everything gone, warm and breathing on the machine. I do think he was dead; the person was already gone and the rising chest didn't fool me. But then a man pulled out of a frozen lake, heart stopped, no breathing, body temp way down, and they rewarm him and he walks out of the hospital fine — I would not call him dead for one second of that, even though his heart wasn't beating and he wasn't breathing, which is exactly what I just said made my brother-in-law dead. Somebody in a deep coma with no awareness but a working brainstem, breathing on his own, opening his eyes, sleep-wake cycles: alive, no question, even if the person never comes back. A patient on a heart-lung machine during an operation, no pulse at all, but awake and talking to the nurse: obviously not dead. A guy whose heart has stopped and who's declared dead, and then while they're retrieving his organs his arm jerks and his toes curl, which I read does happen: still dead, doesn't change a thing. Someone loses their head entirely: dead, immediately, even if the heart keeps twitching a little — and I can't for the life of me say why that's different from the frozen lake except that it can't be undone.

So is there one principle underneath all this, or do I just have a pile of gut calls and I should drop one or two? If I have to drop one, which one should go first? I keep feeling the word 'irreversible' is doing all the work for me, but then we were all non-existent before we were born and that's not death, so that can't be the whole story. Also, maybe the whole question is tangled: tell me straight if it is, because when I try to explain what I'm getting at to my wife she just says death is when the heart stops and tells me to go to sleep.
