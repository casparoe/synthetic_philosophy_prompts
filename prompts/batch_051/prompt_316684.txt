I'm drafting a novel set in a rust-belt town after the plant closes, and the protagonist keeps trying to organize her neighbors around one cause or another. Her brother, who read a lot of Simone Weil back in college, keeps telling her she's just building a political party, and by that he means something narrower and uglier than "people who do politics together." The way he explains Weil, a party isn't really defined by its program or its ideology; it's defined by the fact that it exists for its own growth and pressures its members to take the group line instead of thinking for themselves. Parties are engines for producing partisans, and that's why Weil wanted them abolished. I want the brother to be making a real diagnosis, not just being a crank, so I need a working definition of "political party" in that pejorative, Weil-ish sense — something I can run each group in the novel through. A definition that just says "an organization that contests elections" won't work, because the brother is using the word as a moral diagnosis, not a legal label.

Here are the groups that show up in the book. Which of them are political parties in the brother's sense, and where's the line?

- a tenants' union negotiating with a landlord over rent and repairs
- a neighborhood mutual-aid group that runs a tool library and throws block parties
- a slate of candidates running for city council on a shared platform, with a whip enforcing the vote
- that same mutual-aid group two years later, after it starts endorsing candidates and voting as a bloc at town meetings
- a loose online group organized around a hashtag to stop the factory demolition, with no membership list but real coordination
- a revolutionary cell of six people sabotaging the demolition site
- a book club that reads political theory
- a nonpartisan "good government" association that pushes for ranked-choice voting and rotates its leadership every year

My guesses: the tenants' union and the book club are clearly not parties, the candidate slate clearly is, and the revolutionary cell I suspect is definately a party in miniature. The rest I keep going back and forth on.

Propose the definition, run it through all eight, and tell me where it gives the wrong answer or pushes me somewhere I don't want to go. If it says something uncomfortable about a group I wanted to be sympathetic to, either defend that verdict or revise the definition — but don't just smooth it over.
