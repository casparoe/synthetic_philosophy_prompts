I've been going back and forth on the naked statistical evidence stuff—blue bus, gatecrasher, proof beyond reasonable doubt—for ages, and I want to see the two sides done properly. Write a dialogue between two characters who disagree about whether a bare base rate can ever be enough to find someone liable (or guilty, if we treat beyond-reasonable-doubt as a probability threshold). Character one thinks the blue bus/gatecrasher intuitions are mostly a bug: once the posterior is high enough, refusing to act is just refusing to update, and the naked-vs-individualized distinction is doing no real epistemic work. Character two thinks a bare statistic can set the posterior above any threshold and still not count as proof, because a verdict is a public attribution that has to be about the defendant, not merely about a reference class they fall into. Make them actually engage: reference-class selection, why an 80%-reliable eyewitness feels different from an 80% base rate, DNA database hits, whether a gatecrasher-style conviction convicts a person or a distribution. Neither should be a strawman, and don't resolve it. At the end, have each charachter say how confident they are in their own view and the single point they're least sure of.
