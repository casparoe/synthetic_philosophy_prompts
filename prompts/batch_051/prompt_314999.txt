I work on the compliance side of a game studio that makes historical wargames, mostly WWII and Napoleonic stuff, and our internal content standards have a clause everyone calls the "gravity clause." The exact wording is:

"Depictions of armed conflict shall at all times preserve a sense of gravity. No mechanic, reward structure, or marketing asset may represent war as sport or play, or present the infliction of death as a source of amusement."

That really is the whole clause, minus some definitions above it.

The situation: we're building a multiplayer game set in a real historical campaign (I'd rather not say which). The core gameplay is pretty restrained — kills award no points by themselves, there's suppression and morale, civilians complicate things, etc. Design wants to add a post-match "highlight reel" feature: after each match the game auto-cuts your best kills into a shareable clip, adds upbeat music, slow motion on mortar multikills, and callouts like "sick, three with one shell!" The feature sits seperate from the match itself, and it's generated from telemetry rather than hand-made by marketing, so nobody is sure whether the gravity clause reaches it. The clause was drafted in 2012, when auto-generated shareable clips and streamer culture weren't on anyone's radar; "marketing asset" at the time meant box art, trailers, and screenshots a human being had actually looked at.

I'd like you to act as someone who works on both textual interpretation and the ethics of play, and give me a committed read. Walk through the four readings: the plain words of the clause, its purpose, what the drafters would have said in 2012 if asked, and what a reasonable viewer watching a stream would take the game to be doing. Then say which reading should win here, and concretely what we should require the design team to change, if anything. Don't just lay out the options and leave it to me — pick a side and defend it. And please think it through before settling; I've seen too many people answer this sort of thing with their first instinct.
