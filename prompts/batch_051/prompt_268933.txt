I have been trying to get clear on the statue-and-clay problem and have ended up with a small theory of my own, which I want checked rather than pampered. The standard puzzle, as I understand it, is that a bronze statue and the lump of bronze that constitutes it coincide spatially and share all their matter, yet they seem to differ in their histories and in what they can survive: the lump existed before the statue was cast and would survive being melted down, while the statue came into existence only at the casting and would not survive being melted. Leibniz's Law then seems to force the conclusion that there are two distinct objects in one place, which strikes me as bad.

My proposal, which I currently call One Matter, Two Careers (OMTC), is that no second object pops into existence when the statue is cast. There is one material thing; what there are two of are careers. A career is a path through time under a particular sortal-tagged persistence condition: the lump-career begins when the bronze is poured and can survive squashing or melting, while the statue-career begins at casting and is destroyed by squashing. Statements such as "the lump existed yesterday and the statue did not" are true, but they are true of careers, not of things. Identity, on OMTC, holds between careers rather than between things, so "is the lump identical to the statue?" is simply ill-posed. I like this because it lets me say there is exactly one thing in that region while still explaining the intuitive differences by assigning them to the careers.

I want an honest assessment, not a cheer. Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the strongest precise formulation of OMTC; (2) the nearest existing view and whether OMTC collapses into it, whether that view is four-dimensionalism, relative identity, or one of the standard constitution views; (3) the single most serious objection; (4) an objection that looks serious at first but that OMTC can probably survive, and how; (5) a verdict on whether any objection is fatal and what OMTC would need added or changed to become a live alternative. Please write in British English.
