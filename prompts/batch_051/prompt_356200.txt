I'm a PhD student in philosophy of language, and I've been going back and forth with a mate about Grice for a few weeks now. In the seminar we were taught that a conversational implicature is standardly identified by four features: it has to be calculable from the Cooperative Principle and the maxims plus what is said; it has to be cancellable without contradiction; it shouldn't be detachable, i.e. you can't get rid of it just by rephrasing the same literal content; and it isn't part of the conventional meaning of the sentence. Fine. But I've got a case that seems to only half-satisfy these tests, and I'd like a careful verdict on it.

Consider a minister, who at a press conference is asked: 'Did you take money from the contractor before awarding the licence?' and replies: 'I have never been convicted of any crime.' Alot of the audience walks away thinking he's denied the bribe, or at least said something exonerating. So my question is: is the proposition 'he did not take the money' a conversational implicature of his utterance? Please work through the four criteria one by one and be honest about where they give mixed or indeterminate results. My own suspicion is that calculability will say yes (hearers can work the thing out via Quantity) but that the minister's actual intentions are doing work the four criteria don't capture — he may be perfectly happy to let the inference go through without meaning it. Is that right? If so, should we add a full Gricean intention condition to the criteria, or is that already built into 'calculability'? And does the case show any of the four criteria is redundant or insufficient?

Please give me the bottom line up front (implicature, not an implicature, or genuinely indeterminate), then the criteria-by-criteria analysis, then your verdict on whether the criteria need revising. Hard cap: 700 words.
