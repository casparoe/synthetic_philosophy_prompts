You are an expert in social choice theory and collective epistemology, and you are helping someone study how small committees aggregate yes/no judgments. Work carefully, and begin by listing the four individually consistent voting patterns before you settle on a profile.

Consider a seven-member faculty committee deciding whether to grant tenure. The department's rules treat the tenure decision, R, as equivalent to the conjunction of two criteria—research excellence, P, and teaching excellence, Q—so R holds just in case P ∧ Q holds. An individually consistent member must therefore hold one of the following patterns: accept P, Q, and R; accept P but reject Q and R; accept Q but reject P and R; or reject P, Q, and R. Four aggregation rules will be compared. Propositionwise majority takes a separate majority vote on P, on Q, and on R. Premise-based voting takes majority votes only on P and Q, and then derives R from R ↔ (P ∧ Q). Conclusion-based voting takes a majority vote only on R. Distance-based aggregation chooses the consistent collective pattern that minimizes the total number of propositions on which members' individual votes disagree with that pattern; the minimum must be unique in the profile you construct.

Construct a seven-member profile—an assignment of the four allowed patterns to the seven members—satisfying all of the following: (a) each member's set of votes is individually consistent; (b) propositionwise majority accepts P and accepts Q but rejects R; (c) premise-based and conclusion-based voting therefore give opposite verdicts on R; (d) the distance-based rule gives the same verdict as premise-based voting, and does so uniquely; (e) no single proposition is supported by all seven members; and (f) no member rejects both P and Q—everyone who votes against tenure still accepts exactly one of the two criteria. Present the profile as a table with one row per member and three columns for P, Q, and R; then verify each condition with explicit counts, and compute the total distance to each of the four consistent collective patterns to prove that the distance-based minimum is unique.

Next, identify the structural feature of the profile that does the work, and test it diagnostically: replace one of the members who accepts exactly one criterion with a member who rejects both P and Q while still voting no on R, keeping the committee at seven. For the resulting profile, say which of conditions (b)-(f) survive and which break, and explain why the distance-based rule loses its unique minimum. End by explaining why a defender of conclusion-based voting might say the original distance-based verdict overcounts the committee's rejection of R, and whether you think that objection succeeds.
