I'm a novelist, and I've gradually turned a craft habit into a life habit, and now I'm wondering if it's defensible. In my outlines I write each character's role — the daughter, the widow, the landlord, the teacher — before I write anything about what they want, because I trust the role to tell me what they'll do. I've caught myself doing the same thing with real people: when a friend tells me about a falling-out, I explain it in terms of what the roles demanded, and I'm suspicious of the idea that beneath all the roles there's a truer, deeper self that matters more. Reading some Confucian role ethics recently made me feel vindicated, but maybe I just beleive that because it flatters how I already think, and I've dressed up a flattening habit in a fancy theory. When does starting from roles help me see people accurately, and when does it turn them into their station? Is the habit any more defensible in fiction than in life, or is that just me excusing it?
