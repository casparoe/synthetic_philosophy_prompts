In my early Christianity course we've been doing Origen versus the literalist readers of his day, and theres a pair of passages I keep getting stuck on. Genesis 1:1–2:3 has land vegetation show up on the third day and humans only on the sixth, but Genesis 2:4–9 says the fields had no vegetation yet because there was no one to work the ground, and then has God form the man, plant the garden, and only afterwards make the trees grow. On a plain literal reading they can't both be a straight chronology. Can the two be reconciled without quietly turning one of them into a non-literal exception that only counts when it's convenient? And if one of them does have to go spiritual, which is the honest reading and which is the special pleading—Origen's idea that the text sometimes jars on purpose to push you past the letter, or the harmonizer's move that Genesis 2 is just a close-up of day six? I keep going back and forth, so I'd want candidate reconciliations and a verdict on them rather than just a summary of what the two camps say.
