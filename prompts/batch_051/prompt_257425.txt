I'm a novelist drafting a near-future satire set in a country that has made adversarial truth certification the only recognized way of settling contested public questions. The premise: for any disputed claim, two AI systems are assigned opposing positions and debate before a human judge, and the judge's resulting state of belief is filed as the certified outcome and given legal standing. I need one of the in-universe documents that will appear in the novel: a public-information leaflet from the state bureau that administers the system, written in the flat, reassuring register of a government office that believes its own mission statement. Around 600 words, presented as the document itself, with headings, short sections, and at least one FAQ passage.

Keep the leaflet deadpan throughout; the comedy must come from the epistemic content, not from the document being arch or knowingly ironic. In particular, the satire should land these objections without ever stating them: that a human judge with finite attention can be outclassed by fluent, confident, false arguments; that the skill the system actually selects for is building obfuscated cases the judge cannot evaluate rather than tracking truth; that the system in effect certifies whatever the judge finds persuasive; that everyone designing the debaters has an incentive to optimize for the judge's cognitive biases rather than for truth; and that defenders define a false claim circularly as one that a true debater could in principle refute, while citizens must simply trust that the refutation would be supplied. Include one section in which the bureau reassures readers that a defeated truth does not indicate a flawed procedure, merely that the truth had the worse advocate.

The document will be reproduced in full in the novel, so it should stand alone. The target is the practice and its official self-justification, not any real person, and a specialist in AI safety through debate should recognize the failure modes and find the parody fair rather than cheap.
