I serve on the board of a small charitable foundation that funds youth job-training programs. Our governing document charges the board with managing the endowment in a frugal manner and preserving its real value in perpetuity. The board has long treated this as a set of spending rules: we draw down no more than four percent of the endowment in any year, we take the lowest qualified bid for services, and we require every program's cost per participant to fall at or below the current median. At our next meeting we must respond to a proposal from the executive director, who wants the board to waive those rules for two years in order to fund building retrofits and to hire a development officer. Her projections indicate that the waiver would leave the endowment larger in real terms after thirty years and reduce our cost per participant within five years. One group of trustees says that approving the waiver is the genuinely frugal choice because a frugal institution should spend now to make itself more efficient later. Another group says that the waiver would break faith with the donors and with our own rules, because it treats frugality as a long-run goal that can justify spending today rather than as a discipline we follow in each decision. I have been asked to draft a short memo that the board can vote on. Please write the memo. It should give a clear recommendation either to approve the waiver or to adhere to the current rules, set out the criteria the board should use to determine what frugality requires here, explain how the same value can support two opposing recommendations, and give the strongest argument for the losing side without weakening the recommendation. The memo should not simply say both readings are legitimate or that the board needs to study the matter further.
