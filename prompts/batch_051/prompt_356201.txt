I'm a novelist. The book I'm plotting is set thirty-ish years from now, after life extension has gotten good enough that living past 120 is a real choice but still expensive and contested. One faction — the Natural Lifespan League, deathists basically, and they own the label — runs storefront centers where people get help deciding not to take the treatments and reframing that as wisdom. I need an in-world text: a deadpan recruting brochure, 500-600 words, formatted as a short lede plus an FAQ. The humor has to come from getting the deathism right: adaptive preferences and sour grapes presented as serenity, the idea that death is what gives life meaning, the boredom objection, the claim that wanting more life is just consumer vanity, the inequality objection deployed mostly so people don't have to admit they're scared of open-ended time. It should be funny to someone who knows the literature and read as plausible to someone who doesn't, with the irony kept deadpan — no winking at the reader. Two sections: (1) The brochure — 500-600 words; (2) Commentary — 100-150 words on what you were satirizing and which real positions got exaggerated.
