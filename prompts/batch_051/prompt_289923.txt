I host a tiny theology podcast and next weeks guest is a New Testament scholar who defends a cautious continuationism: she thinks the spectacular gifts (tongues, prophecy, healing) never ceased and still show up today, but she also thinks most of what modern churches call prophecy is just warmed-over preaching or intuition, and that a Christian who never speaks in tongues is not second-rate. I dont want the standard 'when did the gifts stop?' stuff. I want eight to ten questions that press the actual stress points of her position: what Paul's line in 1 Corinthians 13 about the partial giving way to the perfect does and does not settle, whether his description of signs and wonders as marks of an apostle ties those gifts to the apostles in a way that would lapse with them, how she can defend a category of fallible prophecy when the Old Testament treats speaking falsely in God's name as a capital offense rather than a harmless miss, and why the gifts largely vanish from the mainstream record for long stretches of church history. For each question, give me the follow-up to use after the reply she's most likely to give: usually she'll say a verse is descriptive rather than prescriptive, or that the church's neglect is not the same as the Spirit's withdrawal, or that we should not let our Enlightenment embarrassment set the agenda. Write each follow-up to stop that move and force her to say what kind of evidence would make her worried she's wrong. Format it as a numbered list, one main question and one or two follow-ups per item, and keep the whole thing under 500 words.
