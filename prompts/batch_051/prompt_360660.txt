My friend has become mildly obsessed with ranking hypothetical versions of her daily commute. For her, each version is fully described by just two numbers: how long it takes, in minutes, and how unpleasant she expects each minute to be, on a 1–10 scale where 10 means unbearable. Over the past few weeks she has offered the following pairwise verdicts about five routes:

- Route A: 20 minutes at discomfort 9
- Route B: 30 minutes at discomfort 8
- Route C: 40 minutes at discomfort 7
- Route D: 50 minutes at discomfort 6
- Route E: 60 minutes at discomfort 5

She has said, without hesitation, that B is better than A, C is better than B, D is better than C, and E is better than D. Her stated reason is always the same: ten extra minutes is worth one point less discomfort. Yet whenever I ask her to compare A and E directly, she hesitates and says she would need to think about it.

I would like to predict what she will say about A versus E. I am less interested in her eventual answer than in the rule she is running. Please reconstruct the candidate rules that fit her four adjacent verdicts, state what each rule predicts about A versus E, and identify what each rule implicitly commits her to about whether "better than" is transitive in a domain like this. Then give your best guess about what she would say, with your reasons, and specify the single further comparison — among these five routes or involving a new route — that would do the most to discriminate between the live rules.

Please answer as a single JSON object with the following fields:

- "candidate_rules": an array of objects, each with "name" (a short label), "description" (one or two sentences), "prediction_for_A_vs_E" (one of "E better than A", "A better than E", or "incomparable"), and "philosophical_commitment" (what the rule implies about transitivity of "better than").
- "best_guess": an object with "predicted_verdict" (one of the same three values) and "reasons" (why this rule best explains the pattern she has shown so far).
- "further_discriminating_case": an object with "comparison" (a description of the pair to test) and "why_it_discriminates" (how the still-live rules part ways on that pair).
