I am a retired analytical chemist who has recently begun reading philosophy of law, particularly about the rule of law. I have encountered a phenomenon in ordinary legal life and in the laboratory that I do not have a precise name for, and I would like to know whether one exists and how far it extends.

In the city where I live, an old ordinance forbids commercial displays on the sidewalk. It has not been enforced in living memory; municipal employees describe it as effectively obsolete, and several cafés have for years built their trade around sidewalk tables. Last year a new compliance officer enforced the ordinance against one café, apparently not because the conduct had changed but because the owner had become a nuisance in a land-use dispute. The citation was upheld on appeal: the court held that non-enforcement does not repeal a law, and that the text had been publicly available for anyone to read. I can see the force of that reasoning, but it unsettles me. The law, as actually experienced by citizens, had been shaped by decades of official non-enforcement, and then a single official revived it against one person.

The same structure appears in laboratory regulation. A standard operating procedure remains formally in force long after every competent practitioner has moved to a different method; inspectors pass laboratories without checking it; then, after an accident or an administrative quarrel, the ignored procedure is invoked against one party. Again, the text was available, and again, no one had treated it as governing for years.

I am not asking about selective prosecution in the sense of systematic invidious discrimination, nor about legal invalidity. What I want to know is whether there is a single concept covering both situations: a rule that is formally valid, practically dead, and revivable at the discretion of some official. If there is such a concept, I would like to understand its exact boundaries. Must the non-enforcement be total, or does rare and unpredictable enforcement already defeat the concept? Does it require that people actually relied on the non-enforcement, or is official neglect itself enough to change the normative situation? And is the laboratory case a genuine instance of the concept, or only a loose analogy? I am less interested in a label than in a careful account of what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.
