I've been puzzling over Dennett's discussion of the color phi phenomenon. Two differently colored dots are flashed in quick succession at different locations, and subjects insist they saw a single dot move and change color in the middle of its path—before the second dot even appeared. Dennett distinguishes an Orwellian revision, where the brain rewrites the memory after the fact, from a Stalinesque revision, where the brain tampers with the show before it ever reaches consciousness. The maddening thing is that every test we can run, behavioral or introspective, appears to come out the same either way. I'd like you to spell out why Dennett thinks the Orwellian/Stalinesque question can't be settled—or is a distinction without a difference—and what the deeper point is supposed to be about the timing of experience and about whether there is a fact of the matter about the order of events in consciousness. Then tell me whether you buy that the distinction collapses, or whether you think it can be made empirically real after all.
