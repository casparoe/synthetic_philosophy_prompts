I'm giving the opening remarks at a two-day workshop on whether to pause or at least steeply slow frontier AI development. The room is about half policy people and half ML researchers, plus a few funders, all of whom are allergic to being lectured. I've been rereading Pascal on justice and force, and I want the entire speech built around his point that justice without the power to enforce it is useless, while power without justice is tyranny — and that real political life is mostly a messy transaction between the two. I want to use that to rename the pause debate: the safety side mostly has justice-talk with no power, the labs mostly have power with no justice they can say out loud without embarrassment. The speech has to actually do work with that distinction rather than treat it as a quote to decorate, and it should end by handing the workshop a concrete job, namely: figure out what a package of power the safety side could actually hold would have to include before the justice-talk stops being a eulogy for itself. Five minutes, spoken style, no footnotes, and please dont make it sound like a commencement address.
