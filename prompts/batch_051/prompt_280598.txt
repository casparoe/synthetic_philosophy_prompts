A former staff officer on my team ends every planning session with the line "plans are worthless, but planning is everything." Walking out, half the team hears it as conceding that our written plan will be obsolete once execution starts; the other half hears it as insisting that the planning process was still what made us ready. I want to know what that slogan could actually be claiming. Lay out the distinct precise readings of each half and of the whole — at minimum, readings on which the claim is nearly tautological, readings on which it is genuinely substantive, and readings on which it is false or self-contradictory — and explain how the two halves cohere on each reading. Then say which reading, if any, actually vindicates using the line to close a planning meeting, which reading the officer probably intends, and which reading would instead support favoring improvised execution over detailed written plans. Format the result as a numbered menu. For each reading you endorse or reject, and for your verdict about which reading the meeting-closing use needs, say what argument or counterexample would change your mind and where your interpretation is most likely to fail.
