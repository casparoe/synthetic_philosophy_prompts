After forty years in an atmospheric chemistry lab I've started reading epistemology in retirement, so forgive me if I mangle the terms. I've been going around in circles with Nozick's tracking account: knowledge as a belief that tracks the truth, vanishing in the close worlds where the proposition is false and staying put where it's true. What I can't get over is how Nozick just accepts losing closure under known entailment. The classic move: I know I have hands, but I don't know I'm not a brain in a vat, because in the vat scenario I'd still believe I wasn't one, so that belief doesn't track. Its a clever way to keep ordinary knowledge safe from the sceptic, and it costs you the tidiness of closure under deduction.

Could you write a Shakespearean sonnet about this? Let the octave set up the tracking idea — belief following the fact, switching off in the nearby worlds where the fact fails, staying lit where it holds. The volta at line nine should be the closure objection arriving like a deduction the tracker can't follow. Let the closing couplet land on the bargain: you keep your hands and loose the neat closure. The form should do the philosophical work, so that someone who knows Nozick can see exactly where the view gives way to the objection, and someone who doesn't should still get a good poem. Please write it in British English.
