I keep going back and forth on Morgan's canon. The rough idea is that if some animal behavior can be explained by a 'lower' faculty, we're not licensed to explain it by a 'higher' one. Here's an argument I've been gnawing on:

(1) For any animal behavior, if its possible to explain it with some lower-faculty story—associative learning, reinforced trial and error, whatever—then the canon rules out the higher-faculty story (planning, belief, episodic memory, etc.). (2) But with enough ingenuity you can always construct some lower-faculty story for any behavior you like; maybe it's a baroque chain of coincidences and reinforcement schedules, but it still counts as 'a way to explain it.' (3) So the canon, applied honestly, rules out higher-faculty explanations almost everywhere. (4) And that is not a conclusion the evidence forced on us—it's built into the rule, since the rule only asks whether a lower story can be constructed, not whether it's any good. (5) So the canon begs the question against animal minds.

I suspect the whole thing turns on what 'can be explained' means, but I can't pinpoint which step is the bad one, or whether the argument actually goes through. Where exactly does it fail, if it does? Be specific about the step and the reading of 'lower' / 'explained' at work there.
