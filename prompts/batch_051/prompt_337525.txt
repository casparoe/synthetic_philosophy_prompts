I've just been made a team lead at work and it's turned me into an amateur philosopher about respect, which is embarrassing but here we are. There's a bloke on my team who does every part of his job, is punctual and polite, but radiates something I can only call contempt — and my own manager keeps saying "he does everything he's asked, so what are you actually complaining about?" So I've ended up in this rabbit hole about whether we owe each other something in the attitude itself, not just in the action. Two camps keep showing up, and I can now state them precisly enough to annoy both. The action-first camp says: because you can't decide to feel respect, the only thing that can genuinely be demanded of you is respectful behaviour; attitudes aren't the kind of thing that can be a duty, and "you don't respect me" is only a legitimate complaint if it cashes out in some pattern of action. The attitude-first camp says: recognition is itself an attitude — roughly, taking someone as an equal and as a source of claims on you — and you can wrong someone by withholding that attitude even when your outward behaviour is flawless; we can be obliged to cultivate attitudes over time, and at least some of them are more under our control than the "I can't help how I feel" line suggests.

I don't want a verdict. I want the document that an adversarial collaboration between these two camps would produce after six months of honest argument. Please draft me that document, with these sections. First, a numbered agreed-claims section — substantive claims both sides can sign, not truisms, the interesting common ground they'd only reach by arguing. Second, a disagreements table: each residual disagreement stated neutrally, and for each one the phrasing the action-first side would accept as its own and the phrasing the attitude-first side would accept as its own. Third, the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would force at least one side to move. Fourth, for each crux, a concrete case both sides agree bears on it (everyday settings like workplaces, care, friendship — not trolleys), plus each side's prediction about it recorded in advance. If part of the dispute is just verbal, say which part and why. One thing I specifically can't get straight: whether splitting respect into the unearned kind owed to everyone just as a person and the earned kind owed for excellence or skill makes the two camps converge, or whether it's just relabelling the fight — I'd like the document to address that directly.
