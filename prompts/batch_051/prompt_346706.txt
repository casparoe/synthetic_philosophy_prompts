I’ve been arguing with my friends about what should actually count as forgiving someone, and every definition somebody proposes seems to rule out a case the next person swears by. I’m hoping you can give me a working definition of forgiveness that does the sorting. Here are the cases I keep coming back to:

- A coworker screams at me in a meeting, apologizes the next day, and I feel the anger actually drain away.
- My estranged brother never apologized for stealing from our mother before she died; years later I decide to stop wishing him ill. Is that forgiving him, or just moving on?
- At a town memorial, a woman says she forgives the drunk driver who killed a girl she didn’t know personally. She wasn’t the one harmed. Is that forgiveness, or some kind of third-party act?
- I tell a friend I forgive him and I mean it, but for months I still flinch a little when I see his name. Have I forgiven him?
- I did something genuinely cowardly and cruel years ago, and I say I’ve forgiven myself. Is self-forgiveness the same kind of thing as forgiving another person?
- A friend says she forgives her father in advance for whatever he does as his dementia gets worse. That seems like a different act entirely, maybe closer to a vow about how she’ll respond than to forgiveness as I understand it.

I lean toward thinking the first is clearly forgiveness and the last is clearly not, but I can’t then explain why people so naturaly use the word for the middle four, and I have no idea whether forgiveness can be unconditional or something you owe someone. Propose the definition, run it through all six, and tell me which verdict is least secure.
