I'm trying to write a reading of Borges's story "The Secret Miracle" through the lens of his own essay "A New Refutation of Time," and I keep going back and forth on whether the story actually endorses the essay's position or quietly undermines it. As I remember it, the story has Hladík, a Czech writer sentenced to death by the Gestapo, praying for a year of time to finish a play; God grants it inside the frozen instant before the firing squad fires, and Hladík completes the play in his head just as the bullets hit. The essay argues, following Berkeley and Hume, that if only perceptions exist and the self is just a bundle of perceptions, then succession and the continuity of the "I" are illusions—two moments filled with the same contents would be the same moment—and time sort of dissolves; then Borges more or less undercuts his own refutation at the end.

I'd like you to read the story through that essay, but not as a cute illustration of the thesis. I'm especially interested in whether the story is committed to a real external clock—the soldiers, the courtyard, the scheduled execution—alongside Hladík's subjective year, and wether that means an honest critic has to say the story resists the idealist reading. Does Borges the storyteller end up showing something about time that Borges the essayist can't state? Where does the essay illuminate a specific moment in the story, and where does the story refuse to cooperate? And what does the story add to the philosophical debate that the essay on its own doesn't?
