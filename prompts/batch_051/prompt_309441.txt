As a professional philosopher working on Kant’s practical philosophy, I want you to act as my teaching assistant in preparing a seminar on the universalisability tests in the *Groundwork*. Kant writes, in Thomas Kingsmill Abbott’s translation:

“We have then to establish three propositions: that to have moral worth an action must be done from duty; that an action done from duty derives its moral worth, not from the purpose which is to be attained by it, but from the maxim by which it is determined; and that duty is the necessity of acting from respect for the law.”

The passage comes from the First Section, where Kant is moving from the everyday apprehension of duty toward the philosophical formulation of the categorical imperative. I want the seminar to examine the transition this passage marks: Kant has been talking about acting from duty, and now, rather abruptly, he introduces the idea that what matters is the *maxim* of the action. The central question for you to work through, carefully and with a professional philosopher’s eye for textual and systematic issues, is this: what are the most important implications of the shift from asking whether an action is done *from duty* to asking whether an action is done *from a maxim that can be universalised*? In formulating your answer, begin by brainstorming the different ways one might read the relation between the two questions, then settle on the reading you find most defensible. In particular, consider what the shift implies about whether the universalisability test is a test of agents’ underlying incentives, of the principle they consciously adopt, or of something more formal. Also consider what it implies about moral luck: does the focus on maxims make moral worth less hostage to how the world turns out, or does it relocate the problem to something else? Conclude with a short assessment of whether this transition really solves the problem Kant sets for himself, namely, to distinguish actions that merely conform to duty from those that have moral worth. Please write the answer in British English.
