I'm in this seminar on manipulation and legitimate persausion, and I've been trying to figure out whether the distinction is actually principled or just a way of flattering ourselves about the influence we happen to approve of. I wrote up this little argument that seems to show all persuasion is manipulation, and now I can't tell if it's valid or where exactly it goes wrong, because every premise looks fine to me but the conclusion is nuts. Can you walk through it? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating the question.

(1) Whenever someone persuades me of something, my belief changes because of features of what they did: the words they picked, the order they put the arguments in, their tone, the examples they chose.
(2) None of those features are things I choose or control.
(3) So whenever I'm persuaded, my belief is changed by factors outside my control.
(4) If a belief is changed by factors outside my control, then my rational capacities are being bypassed rather then exercised.
(5) Manipulation just is influence that bypasses the target's rational capacities.
(6) Therefore, every case of persuasion is manipulation.

I suspect the trick is somewhere around (4), but I can't say why without it sounding like I'm inventing a special exemption for "good" rhetoric. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and what would a corrected version of that step have to say? And if the argument could be made valid by tweaking a premise, would the resulting definition of manipulation beg the question against someone who thinks the distinction between manipulation and legitimate persuasion is real?
