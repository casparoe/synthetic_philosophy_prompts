I started a customer-facing job a few months ago and now I think about the lying/misleading distinction about ten times a day, because I'm constantly choosing how narrowly to answer a question without becoming a liar. My friend Dana has very firm views on this and I've been tracking her verdicts. Her calls:

- Saying 'I didn't take your charger' when you have it in your bag: wrong, no argument.
- Someone was asked 'did you finish the presentation?' and said 'it's on the shared drive' while the version there was a half-finished draft. Dana says wrong, basically as bad as lying.
- A manager asked a coworker 'have you been talking to the press?' and the coworker said 'I haven't spoken to any journalist this month' — technically true because the leak happened last month. Dana says wrong, again about as bad as lying.
- Someone said 'I saw your ex at the airport on Tuesday,' which was true, and the listener inferred the ex was back in town for good; the speaker didn't know and didn't intend that. Dana says not wrong, just an unlucky accident.

The new case is mine: a client asks whether a part we sell has ever failed in normal use. It hasn't — but it did fail once in a stress test that, I'm fairly sure, goes beyond what a normal customer would do. If I say 'it has never failed in normal use,' that's true, and the words don't exactly invite a false conclusion by themselves; but I can tell the client would hear it as a general clean bill of health and buy without asking the follow-up. I'm temptd to leave it there.

What would Dana say about the new case? Is she running something like 'wrong only when the words themselves, in context, communicate the false thing,' or 'wrong whenever you knowingly let a false impression stand that you could correct,' or something narrower about how much candor you owe in an arm's-length buyer/seller situation? Which of her past verdicts is the closest analog, and if her track record underdetermines it, what principle would I be committing to either way? Also say how confident you are in the diagnosis and single out the point you're least sure of.
