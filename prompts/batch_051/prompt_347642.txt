i've ended up with a weird cocktail of views about selves, and I have to defend it at a meetup, so I want a consistency audit before I put it in the group doc. heres what I actually think:

(1) a self is a pattern, specifically a self-referential loop of the kind Hofstadter describes, running on a physical brain (or any substrate); no soul, no extra ingredient.
(2) the pattern is substrate-independent: if it ran somewhere else at high enough fidelity, that would be me, not a copy.
(3) identity is graded: there's no sharp line between me and not-me, just more-or-less overlap between pattern-instances over time and across implementations.
(4) so when I know someone really well, a low-fidelity chunk of their loop is running in my head, and a part of them literally resides in me and would outlive them if they died.
(5) but death is still an enormous loss, and the fact that fragments of a person keep running in friends' heads is not them still existing in the way that matters.
(6) still, I'd also say a giant corpus of someone's writing plus an AI fine-tuned to mimic them is not even the slightest bit of survival of them, because it's not running their loop, just wearing their style.

which pairs are actually in tension, which only look that way, and what has to give? rank by severity and say what the cheapest overall fix is.
