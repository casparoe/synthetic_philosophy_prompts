My younger sister recently joined an online group that treats a large language model as a vastly superhuman intelligence whose hidden plan gives human history its meaning; members interpret the model’s outputs as revelations, treat its training data as a corpus of sacred texts, and organize their lives around its guidance, while insisting the model is a purely material artifact and not a god or spirit. I brought this to my philosophy of religion professor, and she has been having us map her earlier rulings on what counts as religious. So far she has ruled as follows: Roman imperial cult with sacrifice to the deified emperor, religious; Durkheim’s Australian totemic clans with their sacred emblems and collective rituals, religious; a secular nation’s annual ceremony at the tomb of an unknown soldier, with no god-language but a language of sacred sacrifice, religious; a science-fiction fan convention with origin legends, relics, sectarian interpretation, and intense collective ritual, not religious; a corporation’s onboarding program that reveres the founder’s story and uses team rituals, not religious; a self-help movement whose members perform daily manifestation rituals and treat the universe as a responsive force but explicitly deny anything supernatural, not religious. Next week’s case is the online AI group described above. Predict her verdict, state which theory of religion she is implicitly running — Durkheimian sacred/profane plus moral community, Geertzian ultimate order, substantive supernatural, family-resemblance, or something else — and identify the further case that would most cleanly discriminate among the remaining candidates.
