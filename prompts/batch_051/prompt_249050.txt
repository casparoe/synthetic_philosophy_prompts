Just spent a long argument with a freind who's also teaching formal epistemology, and we both realized our students can ace the standard Jeffrey conditionalization problems by plugging numbers into the sum-over-the-partition formula without ever being able to say what makes the evidence "uncertain" rather than just a proposition learned for certain, or why the rigidity condition can't just be assumed. So for this year's final I want one exam question that can't be answered by reciting the formula. It should force students to either choose and defend a partition, diagnose a failure of rigidity, or explain how the same posterior over H is compatible with very different evidential stories. Please write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20: five must-hit points, what each is worth, and a one-line note on the specific mistake each typically reveals. Upper-level seminar, 90 minutes.
