Suppose a large private research foundation commits itself to the hinge of history hypothesis: the idea that a fairly narrow band of time—possibly the present century—will settle an outsized share of the long-run trajectory of civilization, because several load-bearing technologies are being developed simultaneously and early choices about their governance, safety, and distribution could compound into radically different futures. The foundation does not want to bet its whole endowment on this view, so it adopts a conditional mechanism. A standing Hinge Committee, with rotating members drawn from science, engineering, history, and forecasting, may by a five-of-seven supermajority declare that a hinge window is open. For the duration of a window—up to five years, renewable—at least forty percent of the foundation's annual grant budget must go to trajectory-lever work: projects whose primary expected value is concentrated in the far future, such as governance of advanced technologies, catastrophic and existential risk reduction, norm and institution building, and long-horizon scenario analysis, rather than in near-term measurable outcomes. Grant applicants may petition the Committee to reclassify a proposal that looks ordinary on its face as trajectory-lever; the Committee must publish its reasons for each decision, and its rulings are not appealable. After three consecutive renewals, the window closes for at least one year, and Committee members are barred from receiving foundation funds during their term and for three years after leaving.

Analyze how this institution would actually operate, not whether it is a good idea in the abstract. What does it incentivize for the Committee, for applicants, and for research fields? What sorts of work and rhetorical framings would it attract, and what would it quietly kill? How would people game the reclassification process, and what second-order effects—declaration inflation, credentialism around hinge-language, the hollowing out of near-term programs, path dependence in Committee membership—would likely emerge? Where does the mechanism fail, and can those failure modes be fixed without gutting the underlying scheme?
