I've been going back and forth with a friend about whether the canonical resolution of Maxwell's demon still carries the weight it is usually given. The standard story, running from Szilard through Landauer to Bennett, is this: the demon's measurement can in principle be done reversibly, so measurement itself need not cost anything; the indispensable cost comes when the demon's memory must be reset to start the next cycle, and erasing one bit of information at temperature T costs at least kT ln 2, where k is Boltzmann's constant. That erasure cost is what is supposed to save the second law — the demon never violates it, but only borrows and pays back with interest. The argument depends, however, on a clean architecture: a gas in a box, a partition, a definite memory register, and a cycle with separated measure, act, and erase phases.

The development that has been nagging at me is the move, in recent work, toward autonomous Maxwell demons and information engines: microscopic ratchet-like devices in which measurement, feedback, and the dissipation associated with memory reset are not separated in time and cannot be cleanly assigned to distinct components. The demon is thermalized with its environment, work is extracted continuously, and there is no obviously identifiable step at which one can point and say, "There — the demon just paid the entropy bill." The erasure cost, if it is being paid at all, is smeared through the same autonomous dynamics that does the measuring.

So here is what I want. Does the Szilard–Landauer–Bennett resolution survive this development unchanged, does it need repair in order to continue applying, or does it stop speaking to the autonomous case and get replaced by a different argument? Identify which premise or premises of the canonical argument are genuinely under pressure once there is no separate erasure step, and say whether there is a repaired version that still protects the second law without silently assuming the separated cycle that made the original argument work. Give a definite verdict, but also tell me which theoretical or empirical result would most likely change your mind, and indicate where your reasoning is least certain.
