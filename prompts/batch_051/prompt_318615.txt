I work as a software engineer, and I have been developing a small formal model for comparing experiences across different kinds of beings rather than working from the existing philosophical literature. I call it the capacity-product model. For any experience, let c be a capacity score in [0,1], representing how much of a subject is present — how rich, integrated, and self-aware the experience is — and let a be an affect score in [-1,1], representing how good or bad the experience feels. My model says that the contribution of the experience is c × a, and that the total value of a population over an interval is the sum of those products over all experiences. A rock has c = 0 and contributes zero. A human in severe pain has c close to 1 and a close to -1, contributing roughly -1. A shrimp in what seems like the same pain has c around 0.1, so it contributes only -0.1. That feels right to me: it avoids a sharp cutoff between beings that count and beings that do not, while preserving proportionality. It also implies that two half-capacity beings jointly count as much as one full-capacity being, which I find elegant.

I realize this is close to weighted utilitarianism, but the specific commitments are that the weight is identical to the capacity score, there is no separate factor for species membership or relationship to us, and multiplication is what makes zero capacity an absorbing element. I would like an honest assessment rather than validation. Is the product the right shape, or is it just simple — should the model use c raised to a power times a, or should capacity act as a floor rather than a multiplier? Second, the model seems to imply that a sufficiently large number of low-capacity beings swamps a single high-capacity being: a million shrimp at -0.1 each totals -100,000, while one human at -1 totals -1. I currently have no non-arbitrary way to block that without abandoning the product. Third, I am unsure whether positive and negative affect should scale the same way; perhaps capacity matters more for suffering than for pleasure or the reverse. Give me the three strongest objections to the model, tell me whether any of them is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable, and say whether there is a nearby view that keeps the product insight while fixing the worst problem.
