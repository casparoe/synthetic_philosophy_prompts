Me and my brother were arguing about whether he should believe this video of a politician saying something awful, and I ended up writing down an argument that now feels like it proves to much. (1) If a kind of record can be faked well enough that a careful person can't tell the fake from the real one by looking at the record itself, then that kind of record can't justify belief all on its own. (2) Because of deepfakes, video of real events is exactly like that now. (3) So video can't justify belief on its own. (4) But almost everything I know about events I didn't live through comes through records like that—videos, audio, photographs, documents. (5) So almost none of my beliefs about the world beyond my own experience are justified. (6) That's an insane conclusion: I obviously know about the 2011 tsunami in Japan even though I was nowhere near it. (7) So one of the steps has to go, but each one looked right to me when I was building it. Which step is the weakest, and what do I have to pay to reject it? If I'm being honest I'd rather keep the deepfake part and give up the idea that I know about the tsunami, but I suspect that's a bigger cost than I realize.
