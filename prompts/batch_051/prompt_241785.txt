In A Philosophical Essay on Probabilities, Laplace writes: "Strictly speaking it may even be said that nearly all our knowledge is problematical; and in the small number of things which we are able to know with certainty, even in the mathematical sciences themselves, the principal means for ascertaining truth—induction and analogy—are based on probabilities; so that the entire system of human knowledge is connected with the theory set forth in this essay." I want you to argue that if Laplace is right here, then the standard frequentist ritual of computing a p-value and rejecting the null when p < .05 is a basic misuse of probability, and that the replication crisis is pretty much what we should expect from that misuse. Make the strongest case you can for this position, and along the way say where a smart frequentist would push back.
