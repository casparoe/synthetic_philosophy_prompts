I run the teen program at a community center in a city whose heritage language is down to a couple hundred mostly elderly speakers. Last month, after the funeral of one of the last storytellers, the teenagers got into an argument: if the language finally disappears once the remaining speakers are gone, is that a real loss, or is it only sad for the people who happened to care while they were alive? Several of them insisted that once no one is left to whom it matters, nothing is lost—nobody remains to miss it, and you cannot be deprived of something you never used anyway. I want to run a ninety-minute session that does not settle this argument but makes the rival idea intelligible: that a language, tradition, or art form might be worth preserving even when its value is not exhausted by its current usefulness or by how much the people who hold it happen to value it.

Please act as a professional philosopher working in value theory. Think carefully, and start by brainstorming the different ways this idea could be presented before settling on the best one. Then give me a teaching plan, not the explanation itself. What has to be in place before the phrase intrinsic value can even be used without confusing them—for example, the difference between something being good for a person and being good period, and the difference between someone valuing a thing and that thing being valuable? What single case should carry the whole session, and what properties must it have, given that I worry a dying language is too close to home and will just get them re-litigating last month's argument? What misconceptions should I expect—they will probably think intrinsic means not caused by anything, or valuable no matter what the consequences, and some may suspect I am sneaking in the view that cultures have rights—and exactly where in the sequence should each be headed off? What final question or short exercise would show that they have understood the distinction rather than just changed their minds? And what should I openly refuse to settle at their level?
