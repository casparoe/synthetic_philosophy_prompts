so for my philosophy club were doing this thing where you take a book or movie and read it through a philosophy essay, and i got assigned The Hunger Games and Peter Singers essay Famine, Affluence, and Morality. everyone always talks about the shallow pond example but i wanna focus on a different part where Singer argues, roughly, that once you accept any principle of impartiality or universalizability or equality, you cant treat the fact that someone is far away from you as morally relevant — like distance and national borders don't change what you actually owe someone. he also says there that the usual distinction between duty and charity is drawn in the wrong place, because people treat helping distant starving people as optional charity when really its the kind of thing your just as much obligated to do as saving someone right in front of you. my question isnt just "the capitol is bad" cause thats too easy. i want you to read Panem through those ideas — like, does the worldbuilding actually support Singers point about distance not mattering, or does the book complicate it? think about how the districts are cut off from each other, how Katniss herself barely knows anything about district 11 until Rue, and whether the novel makes hunger and suffering feel more or less distant for the reader. and then say honestly where the book resists Singers argument, cause i think there might be scenes where the distance between districts kind of does matter morally, or where the book treats caring about someone as depending on knowing their name or their story in a way Singer would be suspicious of. basically i want a reading that takes both Singer and the book seriously and ends by saying what the novel adds that the essay misses.
