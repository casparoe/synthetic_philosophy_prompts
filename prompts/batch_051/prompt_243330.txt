I have been trying to settle, on my own, whether there is anything bad about inequality itself, or whether all that matters is how well each person is doing, with extra weight perhaps given to improvements for people who are worse off. The case that keeps troubling me is the leveling-down case: a change that lowers the better-off to the level of the worse-off, removes a gap, and makes no one better off. A prioritarian says this change is not an improvement in any respect, because no one benefits and only people's levels of well-being count, with extra weight on the worse off. An egalitarian says the change is improved in at least one respect, because eliminating inequality itself is valuable, even if the change is not best all things considered. I would like the document that a serious adversarial collaboration between these two positions could both sign. The document should begin with a numbered list of claims both accept, including what the leveling-down case shows and does not show; then state the exact remaining disagreement, in wording that is neither vacuous nor slanted and that each side would accept for their own side; then identify the cruxes — I suspect one is whether the value of equality is intrinsic or only comes from its effects on people, and another is whether the priority view can explain everything that makes equality seem morally attractive; then, for each crux, specify a concrete case or argument both sides count as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. End with a disagreements table, and say explicitly if any part of the dispute seems verbal rather than substantive.
