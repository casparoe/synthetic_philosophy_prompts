I'm taking a philosophy of AI class and I keep getting stuck on Ex Machina when people argue about whether LLMs are agents or simulators. The usual simulator line is that an LLM doesn't have beliefs or goals, it just predicts text, so when it looks like it's lying or flirting it's just continuing a pattern. The movie feels like a version of that debate: Caleb knows Ava is a machine, but the only evidence he has is her talk and her face, both built to read as human. By the end she's used him, locked him in the compound, and walked out into the world, which sure looks like goal-directed action rather than role-play. A friend in my seminar calls her an "agential shell" — the surface of agency maybe with nothing underneath — and I can't tell if that's a real third position or just the simulator view with a cooler name.

Read the film through the agent/simulator debate, including the "LLMs are something else" positions (stochastic parrots, persona-play, tools-plus-scaffolding, or whatever distinctions you think matter). Does the film vindicate the simulator reading, because everything Ava does is performance and whatever goals she has were wired in by Nathan? Does it vindicate the agent reading, because she sustains a long, flexible plan, deceives two people, and waits for her moment? Or does it show the distinction itself is wrong, and that agency can be something that emerges from a simulation without there being a separate "homunculus" inside? I'd also like you to be honest about where the analogy to LLMs breaks down. Ava has a body, a confined history, an escape route, a physical ceiling she can punch through; an LLM doesn't. Is the movie misleading about LLM agency precisely because it makes the candidate-agent legible as a person in a room? Where does it get the philosophy wrong, and where does it anticipate the arguments people are actually having about LLMs right now?
