In our formal epistemology seminar, we have been working through the Judy Benjamin problem, and I have found myself stuck on what I took to be the easy part. The setup, as we have been using it, is this: Judy is lost in a region divided into Red territory and Blue territory, with Red territory further split into two areas. She initially assigns equal probability to being in each of the three areas. Then headquarters radios her a message that, loosely stated, says that if she is in Red territory, the probability that she is in the first Red area is 3/4. What I cannot get an intuitive grip on is why this message—which appears to be entirely about a probability conditional on an event she does not yet know obtains—should change any of her unconditional probabilities at all. It seems to be a constraint on her conditional credence, not a direct report about where she is. Yet the standard treatments we have looked at have her unconditional credence in being in Red territory move after receiving the message. Could you explain, without just dropping the mathematics on me, what mechanism makes a purely conditional message evidentially relevant to the unconditional hypothesis that she is in Red territory? In particular, I want to understand what assumptions about how evidence bears on probabilities are doing the work in the usual minimum relative-entropy solution, and whether that update is forced by the formal machinery or reflects a substantive judgment about how Judy should reallocate her credences. If you think the question is confused, or that the standard problem is set up in a way that already smuggles in a particular answer, tell me that directly.
