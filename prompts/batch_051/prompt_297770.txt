So I cover cognitive science for a general-interest magazine, and I've got a buddy in a neuro lab who keeps insisting that consciousness is basically a user illusion. Not in a woozy way, more like Huxley. He pointed me to that passage in Thomas Henry Huxley's On the Hypothesis that Animals Are Automata, and Its History (1874, Fortnightly Review), where Huxley says something in the neighborhood of: consciousness stands to the brain like a steam whistle to a locomotive, it makes noise while the real work happens, but it isn't driving the wheels. The feeling that I decided to raise my arm is a readout, not a cause. My friend says this isnt just a just-so story; he thinks you could actually test it. I dont see how, since the claim is partly about what consciousness is, not only what it does. So how would you test the user-illusion claim? What observation would count for and against it, maybe something like showing the brain commits to a movement before the person reports deciding, or that conscious report is systematically blank or confabulated in certain tasks, or that people can respond to masked stimuli they deny seeing. Be concrete about the design, and be honest about what a positive result would and would not show. I want the residue: what part of the claim no experiment can reach.
