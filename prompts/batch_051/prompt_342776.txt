I recently had a long argument with a friend about why the gods’ oaths on the Styx were treated as uniquely binding in Greek epic and theology. Our disagreement crystallized around two theories. One theory holds that the Styx oath is a supernatural mechanism: swearing by Styx activates a sanction that operates independently of whether other gods enforce it, and a perjured god is thereby disabled for a year and exiled from nectar and ambrosia for nine years, almost like a cosmic poison rather than a social penalty. The other theory holds that the special force of the Styx is institutional: it binds because the gods themselves recognize it as supreme, so the real work is done by honor, reputation, and the collective interest in a common standard of verification. I want you to adjudicate between these theories. In particular, consider this asymmetry: if the Styx’s power were only institutional, why would the penalty be described as automatic bodily privation rather than as revenge or expulsion by Zeus? But if its power were purely mechanical, why should its force depend on the fact that the gods framed it as an oath, rather than as a mere drinking of Styx water? Please compare the two theories and say which gives the better account of what makes swearing on the Styx binding, and whether the forensic and cosmological elements can be separated.
