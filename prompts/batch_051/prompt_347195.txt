A friend and I stayed up way too late last night arguing about whether DNA is literally a code or whether that whole way of talking has just gotten lazy and its messing with how people think about genes. I'm still turning it over, but it made me realize I need a reusable way to make this call at my actual job. I edit the biology and health pieces for a popular science magazine, so this comes up constantly: copy arrives fast, the writers aren't philosophers (and neither am I), my boss signs off, and the pieces have a big readership. I need a decision procedure, a checklist or ordered set of questions, for deciding when language like "DNA is a code," "contains instructions," or "is a program" is fine to leave alone and when I should flag or rewrite it. For each question, tell me what counts as a good enough answer to move on, where the procedure stops with a "change this," and where it has to hand the decision to a person because it's a judgment call rather than a rule. Then run it on three actual-sounding sentences: (1) "the gene codes for a protein," (2) "DNA contains the instructions for building an organism," and (3) "cells read the DNA code and execute its program." I want at least one case where the procedure tells me to cut a line I'd personally want to keep, and I want an honest statement of what the procedure can't do.
