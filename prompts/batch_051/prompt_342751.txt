Right, so I've been tinkering with a little agent simulation in my spare time — I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, so my handle on akrasia is basically 'weakness of will' plus whatever I've pick up from adjacent reading — and I've hit an argument that seems to prove way too much. I can't tell if it's a bug in the model or a bug in the concept.

Here's the chain, cleaned up from my notebook.

(1) An agent at any given time acts on the preferences it has at that time, and rationality is always evaluated from the agent's own point of view at the moment of choice.

(2) With quasi-hyperbolic discounting (the beta-delta thing, beta < 1), preferences can flip just because time passes: at t0 the agent prefers to write the report at t1, but when t1 arrives she'd rather stay on the sofa.

(3) So in the familiar akrasia case — I resolve over breakfast that after lunch I'll work on the report, then after lunch I'm three episodes into a show I've already seen — there is no point at which I act against my own judgement. After lunch, watching the show really is what I judge best, because my discounting says it is.

(4) If there is no moment where I act against my own judgement, weakness of will never gets a grip. What looked like akrasia is just a chain of time-slices, each doing its own local optimum and handing off to the next.

(5) But by the same reasoning, the person who swears off heroin at dawn and uses at noon doesn't act weakly either — their noon-slice just has different preferences. And if that's right, the whole distinction between self-control and being overrun by an impulse is an illusion, which strikes me as absurd.

(6) So atleast one premise has to go. I'd most like to keep (1), because it keeps the simulation clean, but then I seem stuck saying the person just is a bunch of successive agents and akrasia is a folk category we should drop.

Where exactly does the argument go wrong, and what does it cost to keep whichever parts you keep? And if I keep (1) and (2), is there any way left to talk about genuine weakness of will rather than plain preference reversal?
