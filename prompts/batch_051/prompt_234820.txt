I keep returning to a parallel I noticed and cannot resolve. On one side is the freegan case for using animal products that would otherwise be wasted: eggs from rescued hens, meat or dairy salvaged from a supermarket's discards, food recovered from dumpsters. The freegan argument is that the wrong lies upstream — the animal has already been exploited or the food already thrown out — so consuming the byproduct adds no marginal demand to the system that produced it, and abstaining does not help any animal or undo any past harm; it merely lets something go to waste when its production is already a sunk harm with no possible beneficiary. The standard reply is not causal but expressive: using the animal still treats her body and her products as resources, signals that animal foods remain normal and legitimate, and makes the consumer complicit in a system they claim to reject; and the word "waste" is itself borrowed from the very system the freegan opposes, since the hen, her eggs, or the discarded animal were never waste in any morally important sense.

On the other side is a debate I keep seeing about whether it is permissible to use a large language model trained on scraped copyrighted text or images used without consent. The person using the model is downstream of an alleged wrong that has already occurred; their individual use adds approximately zero marginal demand for the training; the model exists whether or not they use it; and refusing to use it neither compensates the writers and artists nor prevents the training from having happened. Yet the objection offered is structurally the same as the anti-freegan one: using the model validates the expropriation, normalizes an artifact that should not exist, and treats creators' work as raw material. The two positions look, in their logical anatomy, almost interchangeable.

What I want to know is whether this is a deep identity — the same moral problem about benefiting from a completed wrong when one's marginal contribution is negligible and one's refusal is causally inert — or a superficial analogy held together by the word "waste." I would like an exact statement of the mapping and of where it comes apart. Does the difference between sentient animals and human creators break the analogy, and if so at which step? Does it matter that a rescued hen's eggs are a continuing byproduct of caring for an animal one now has some responsibility toward, while a trained model is a completed artifact? Does it matter that using a hosted model may fund the provider and deepen its market position, while a meal recovered from a dumpster involves no transaction with the harm-producing system at all — and would running an open-weights model locally restore the parallel? If I accept the freegan argument for dumpstered animal products, am I thereby committed to thinking it permissible to use the scraped-data model, and if not, which premise must I deny? Does anything transfer in the other direction, so that a strong version of the normalization objection in one case incurs a cost in the other? I would like you to say which argument transfers, what the parallel would predict that turns out false on at least one side, and whether the analogy tracks something real in the ethics of complicity or only a shared rhetorical shape.
