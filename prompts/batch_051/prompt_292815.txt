I'm a novelist, and I'm trying to get a handle on the thing I keep calling idolatry in the book I'm writing. The novel is about a painter who treats her work the way saints treat God — she calls her studio 'the cell,' she talks about the paintings as if they'll save her, and the central question is whether this is a vocation or a slow self-destruction. But I dont want 'idolatry' to be a word I just throw around, so I need a working definition of idolatry — or 'misplaced worship,' if that's the better name for valuing something in the wrong way — that survives hard cases. Give me the definition, then run it through these seven cases: (1) a woman who sincerely worships a carved figure, (2) a man who loves his wife more than anything and would choose her over God, (3) the painter who treats her art as the source of her meaning, (4) a sports fan who weeps every time his team loses, (5) a believer who is really in love with the liturgy and the music but never thinks about God the rest of the week, (6) a mother who says her kids are her whole world and means it, (7) a lawyer so devoted to justice that he ruins every close relationship in his life. I think 1 and 3 are clearly idolatry, 6 clearly isnt, and the rest I can't sort. Run all seven through the definition, flag any verdict you want to resist, and tell me which case makes the definition least stable.
