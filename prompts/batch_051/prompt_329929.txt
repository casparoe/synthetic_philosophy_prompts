ok so i've been put in charge of kind of a mediation at work and i need a draft document i can take to both factions. we have a model that reviews insurance pre-authorization requests and outputs approve / deny / escalate, plus a short natural-language rationale. there's two camps in our group about how the next version should be trained, and my manager wants me to run an adversarial collaboration between them and come back with a joint statement. the deliverable should be the document itself: what they can both sign, exactly where they part ways, and what would actually move them. i care about this personally because i'm the one who has to defend the final design to the compliance people, and i dont currently know how much of their fight is real and how much is them using the same words differently.

Our two camps are:

Camp A ("outcome-first"). They want to keep fine-tuning the model end-to-end on past decisions as a black box, including letting it use a private scratchpad / chain of thought during training and inference, because the scratchpad gets them about four points of decision accuracy on held-out cases. Their view is that understandability should be delivered after the fact: you train a separate explainer (or probe the model) to produce a rationale for each decision, and you judge that rationale by whether an auditor makes better downstream decisions with it than without it — not by whether the rationale faithfully mirrors what the model actually computed. They also claim that a more accurate model is itself more legible in the way that matters, because there are fewer wrong decisions to explain.

Camp B ("legibility-first"). They want to train the model so that the rationale is produced in a constrained, inspectable format (a fixed template: which facts from the claim it used, which rule or principle it applied, and which facts would have changed the decision), and they want that rationale to be causally connected to the decision — i.e., not a post-hoc story. They accept losing some held-out accuracy to get this. Their line is that a post-hoc explanation of a black-box decision is not "understandable" in the sense the compliance guidance requires, because an auditor can't verify that the stated reason is the actual reason. The regulatory guidance says decisions must be explainable to the member, but it doesn't say anything explicit about causal faithfulness, which is exactly the gap they fight over.

They have agreed on a few things already, but i want you to produce the actual joint document and catch anything where their agreement is only verbal.

Please give me a JSON object with these fields:

- "opening_statement": a 3–6 sentence paragraph both camps can sign, framing the dispute fairly and saying why they are doing this. No cheerleading for one side.

- "agreed_claims": an array of strings. Each entry states a substantive claim both camps will sign as worded (about the task, the regulation, the tradeoff, the evaluation metrics, or the limits of what either approach guarantees). No hedging like "there is a tradeoff" — each one should be specific enough that someone who only read this list would learn something about the dispute. If a claim looks agreed but comes apart once it's spelled out, leave it out of this list and put the disagreement where it belongs.

- "disagreements": an array of objects, each with fields: "point" (short name for the issue), "outcome_first_wording" (the claim as Camp A would happily sign), "legibility_first_wording" (the claim as Camp B would happily sign), "status" (one of "genuine", "verbal", or "mixed"), and "note" (one or two sentences saying why, and if "verbal" or "mixed", what the remaining non-verbal core is).

- "cruxes": an array of objects, each with fields: "question" (a specific sub-question whose answer would move at least one camp), "test" (an experiment, audit scenario, or case that both camps agree would bear on it, pre-registerable rather than a vibe check), "outcome_first_prediction" (what Camp A predicts and is willing to record in advance), "legibility_first_prediction" (same for Camp B), and "would_change_minds_how" (one or two sentences on which camp would concede what under which outcome).

- "verbal_disputes": an array of objects, each with fields: "phrase" (the term or phrase they use differently, e.g. "faithful", "explanation", "verifiable"), "outcome_first_meaning", "legibility_first_meaning", and "suggested_disambiguation" (a shared term or a way to split the two senses so the camps stop talking past each other). If there are none, return an empty array.

Two more asks. First, don't resolve the fight or say which camp is right; the whole point is a document both sides would sign, with residual disagreement made exact. Second, be careful with phrases like "the rationale matches the decision": one camp means the rationale is faithful to the actual computation, and the other means an auditor would agree the decision follows from the rationale. I have a feeling wether that's a verbal dispute or a real one is itself one of the main findings, so treat it carefully.
