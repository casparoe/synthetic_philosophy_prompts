I teach an upper-level undergraduate course on the history of political economy. I am setting the final essay question, and I want it to be on Hume’s monetary theory as developed in his essays on commerce, money, interest, and the balance of trade. The students can all repeat the standard version of the specie-flow mechanism, but I want a question that tests whether they can work through a real tension in Hume’s position: he denies that a nation’s stock of money is identical with its real wealth, and he argues that money tends to distribute itself proportionately among trading nations; yet he also allows that an increase in the quantity of money can stimulate industry in the short run.

Please do three things.

First, write the exam question. It should require students to explain how Hume can hold the long-run neutrality claim and the short-run stimulus claim at the same time, and to assess whether his consistency depends on assumptions about the speed of price adjustment that he does not fully argue for. The question should force them to use the four-fifths thought experiment and to explain what work the distinction between real goods and the circulating medium is doing.

Second, write a model answer of approximately 600 to 700 words. The model answer should reconstruct the four-fifths thought experiment without merely restating it, distinguish the immediate effects of a change in the money stock from its eventual effects, and identify where Hume’s argument is consistent, where it is incomplete, and what a defender of the balance-of-trade view could still say.

Third, write a marking scheme out of 20 marks. Identify five specific moves an A-level answer must make, worth 4 marks each. For each move, add a one-line note on the characteristic error or omission that separates a B-level answer from an A-level answer.

Finally, after the marking scheme, add a short note saying how confident you are in the interpretive claims in the model answer and identifying the single point you are least sure about.
