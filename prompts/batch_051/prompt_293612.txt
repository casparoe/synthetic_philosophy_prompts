I got into reading Hume's Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion a while back just because a friend recomended it, not for any course, and I keep coming back to this bit in Part 11 where Philo says: "There seem to be four circumstances, on which depend all, or the greatest part of the ills, that molest sensible creatures; and it is not impossible but all these circumstances may be necessary and unavoidable." I've been arguing with a few people in my little self-study group about what this sentence is actually doing in Philo's overall arguement. Some of us think he's just making the usual point that the world contains evil. I think he's making a subtler move: the idea that the four circumstances might each be necessary changes whether evil counts as evidence against a finite designer, not just against an infinite one.
