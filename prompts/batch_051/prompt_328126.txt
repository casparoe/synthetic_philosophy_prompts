Ok so in my philosophy of mind course we've been reading Chalmers on the meta-problem of consciousness, and im hung up on one thing. As I understand it, the meta-problem is: why do we think there's a hard problem of consciousness at all — why do we produce all these reports and intuitions about phenomenal consciousness? And Chalmers thinks we could in principle explain those reports in purely physical, cognitive terms, like our introspective models are just set up to generate the judgment that there's something it's like. He also wants to stay neutral on whether the hard problem is real. But that neutral stance seems unstable to me. If you can give a complete physical explanation of why people are convinced there's a hard problem, then haven't you basically shown that the conviction is an illusion? How is that not already an error theory about phenomenal consciousness?
