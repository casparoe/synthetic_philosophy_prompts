I'm not a doctor, but I keep arguing with a friend who's an ICU nurse about what death actually is, and I've realised my confidence in the word collapses as soon as I list cases. Give me a working definition of death that gets these right: a person whose heart stops for ninety seconds and then restarts after CPR; a brain-dead woman kept on a ventilator so her pregnancy can continue; a patient in a permanent vegetative state; a body in the morgue with rigor mortis; an anencephlic newborn; a person on ECMO whose own heart has been stopped for an hour but whose brain is still being perfused; a person whose circulation stopped twenty minutes ago and nobody's intervening; and a line of HeLa cells growing in a lab dish. I think the first is clearly not death and the brain-dead woman clearly is, and I honestly don't know what to say about the ECMO one or the newborn. I also want the definition to say something sensible about a kidney that's been removed and is sitting on a perfusion machine, pink and making urine — is that dead? Propose the definition, run it through every case, and tell me where it surprises you, which of your verdicts is least secure, and where you'd be willing to flip. And please seperate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this stuff; don't present the brain-death consensus as if it has no critics. Oh, and do it in British English.
