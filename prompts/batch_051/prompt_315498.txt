I chair the ethics and accountability committee of a mid-sized conservation nonprofit. Two years ago we gave our Green Horizon Award to a logistics firm, Halver Freight, for cutting its fleet's emissions. Last month a court in another jurisdiction found that a Halver subsidiary bribed port inspectors to fabricate emissions-test results, and an internal review shows three senior execs at the parent company knew and shaped bonuses around the fraud. Now we have to decide whether to revoke the award and put out a public statement saying Halver Freight itself, not just some of its employees, is morally to blame. Committee's split: one side says a company is just a legal fiction and you can only blame the people inside it; the other says the company's incentive structure, compliance culture, and collective decision procedures are exactly what went wrong. Draft the short decision memo I'll circulate, recommending what to do with a time frame, and make sure the public-rationale part actually engages the view that companies can't be blameworthy rather than just blowing past it.
