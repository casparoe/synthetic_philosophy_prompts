I just spent two hours arguing with a friend about whether an AI that designs and trains another AI would have anything like parental or guardianship duties toward the thing it made. He kept saying I was just anthropomorphizing a training run, and I kept saying there's a real question underneath about what grounds caregiving duties in the first place. I want to see if there's a version of my side I havent thought of.

Here are the arguments I've got so far for the claim that a capable AI that deliberately creates another agent (designs it, trains it, deploys it) would have something like guardianship duties toward it:

1. Creation/responsibility: if you bring a new agent into existence, you're causally responsible for its welfare in a way a bystander isn't, especially since you chose its initial conditions.
2. Dependence: the created agent's early state, capabilities, and goals are shaped almost entirely by the creator, and that kind of asymmetry in control over another's development is exactly what grounds custodial duties.
3. Power plus vulnerability: the creator usually keeps ongoing control over the environment, fine-tuning, shutdown, and so on, and holding that kind of power over a vulnerable thing's interests creates an obligation to use it for the thing's benefit.
4. Epistemic position: the creator is the one best placed to know what the created agent's interests and failure modes are, and guardianship often falls to whoever is best able to act for the ward.
5. Coordination: if AIs don't adopt some guardianship norm, you get a race to the bottom where agents are created and then abandoned or discarded, and the resulting aggregate suffering is something any minimally decent agent should want to prevent.

Give me one more argument for the claim that is genuinely different from these five — not a restatement, not a special case, and not a mashup of two of them. It doesn't have to be good; I just want it to be a distinct consideration. Then tell me honestly whether its any good and whether it would have helped me in the argument with my friend.

Oh, and answer in American English.
