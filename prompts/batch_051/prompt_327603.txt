(2) Representationalist view: a system genuinely believes that P only if it has internal states that represent P and those states play the right kind of role in guiding what it does — storing, updating, and combining content, not merely tracking a variable.

I want to show that the disagreement between these camps isnt just about how generous we feel, so the case has to be one where the two come apart in the direction people find harder: the representationalist must say the system believes that P, but the intentional stance, applied carefully, should refuse to attribute that belief because doing so buys no predictive leverage. Keep it realistic and near-present-day — no Chinese rooms, no lookup tables, no perfect predictors, no sci-fi — and make the scenario one paragraph.
