Construct a thought experiment for teaching the underdetermination of objective inference by behavior alone. The case should show that one public record of choices can support two different, individually reasonable objective attributions, with the divergence due entirely to the observers' auxiliary assumptions about which alternatives the optimizer actually considered before acting.

Design an optimizer whose choices over a series of trials are observed by two inference procedures that receive exactly the same data — on each trial, a menu of alternatives and the optimizer's choice — but proceed under different auxiliary hypotheses about the search space. Both procedures use the same maximizing logic and differ only in the option set relative to which they judge the choices to be maximizing. The first procedure is revealed-preference inference: it treats the presented menu as the option set and attributes to the optimizer a goal that makes the observed choices maximizing over those menus. The second procedure is search-aware inference: it attributes a goal that makes the observed choices maximizing over the search space specified in your scenario. That search space must contain at least two alternatives on every trial and must be a proper subset of the menu on at least some trials. The optimizer is a perfect maximizer over its actual search space, with one stable goal, no hidden payoffs, and no noisy choice. The ground-truth goal and both inferred goals must each be simple, intuitive, one-sentence goals over mundane outcomes, not reward schedules gerrymandered to fit particular choices. Include at least four trials, and make the two inferred goals positively different rather than mere negations of one another.

After setting out the scenario, provide the required demonstrations. Show the step-by-step inference for each procedure and why it yields the goal attributed to it. Identify the structural feature that produces the divergence — the gap between the menu and the search space — and say what happens if that feature is removed: would the procedures converge if the search space always coincided with the menu, and if not, what other feature would remain? End with the honest counter-note: how would a defender of the mistaken procedure attack the case, and how much force does that attack have?
