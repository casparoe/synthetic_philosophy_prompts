I'm trying to think through how far Engels's account of monogamy in *The Origin of the Family, Private Property and the State* can survive the existence of cheap DNA paternity tests. In the part of the book where he discusses the monogamous family, he argues, as I remember it, that monogamy, especially the woman's sexual exclusivity, wasn't a moral truth or a requirement of love, but an economic arrangement. Once property got concentrated enough to be inherited, men needed some way to know which children were theirs, and the institution solved that problem by making female adultery a serious transgression. The passage I'm thinking of is the one where he says the historical defeat of the female sex coincided with the shift from mother-right to father-right, and that monogamy's real purpose was to produce children of undisputed paternity for inheritance. I'm not quoting exactly, but that's the gist.

So here's what I want to know. If Engels were alive today, and you told him that a man can now establish whether a child is his with a $50 mouth swab, that paternity can be known independently of any sexual exclusivity norm, that child support often attaches to legal parentage rather than biological, and that a lot of inheritance is now structured through wills, trusts, and marriage contracts that can name any beneficiary—what would he say? Would he regard the material basis of monogamy as gone, and therefore predict that the institution would wither, or at least that its strongest rationale would disappear? Or would he see the persistence of monogamy, and the stigma around nonmonogamy, as evidence that the institution picked up some other social function that his economic story missed? I'd like the answer to stay close to Engels's actual theory: which premise in his argument is under the most pressure from cheap paternity testing, and can his larger claim about family forms tracking property relations be kept if we adjust that premise? Also, if he'd say we're living through a transitional family form, what would he expect the next stage to look like, not just in law but in the everyday norms around sexual exclusivity?
