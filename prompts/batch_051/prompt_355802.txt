I sit on the doctrinal commission of a small Presbyterian denomination. Our Formulary of Faith, adopted in 1789, contains this article, which our commission is charged with applying:

"We believe and confess that the guilt of Adam's first sin is conveyed to all human beings who descend from him by ordinary generation, so that each is born in a guilty and corrupted condition before any thought, word, or deed of his own; and this inherited guilt is neither removed nor lessened by any later ignorance or innocence, since it attaches to the very nature received from Adam."

A fertility clinic operated by our church's hospital now proposes to use somatic cell nuclear transfer, the technique behind reproductive cloning, to help a couple in our communion have a child: an embryo will be produced from the husband's body cell and gestated by the wife, with no sperm and no fertilization. The child would be genetically human in every relevant respect but not begotten "by ordinary generation" in any sense the authors of 1789 could have imagined.

We must issue a ruling on whether the article's doctrine of inherited guilt and corruption covers that child. Argue the case from four standpoints: the plain words of the article, its evident purpose as a statement about the universal human condition, what the 1789 authors would have said had they been asked, and what a reasonable reader of the Formulary today takes the words to mean. Then say which approach should govern our ruling and why, and state plainly whether the child is born under the imputed guilt of Adam.
