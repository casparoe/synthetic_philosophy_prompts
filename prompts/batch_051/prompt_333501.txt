Ever since I read Chomsky's review of Skinner's Verbal Behavior I've been running a kind of personal policy in arguments, and I'm starting to worry I've over-applyed it. The passage that stuck with me, from what I remember, argues that saying a child has learned something by generalization or induction from experience is not an explanation until you actually say what the experience was and what rule takes that experience to the new behavior. Because the same finite examples can be generalized in endlessly many ways, so the environment alone doesn't fix which one the child lands on. He uses this against Skinner's operant conditioning story, but I made it into a standing rule: when anyone says a person "learned" something from exposure—culture, parents, media, whatever—I ask what the input was and what rule maps input to output, and if they can't give one, I treat their explanation as a placeholder.

I know this is basically my poverty-of-the-stimulus detector, but I've been using it everywhere. Last month a friend said her kid had learned the past tense for a verb she'd never heard inflected, just from hearing adults talk. I asked what in the input told her to add -ed to that particular verb, and my friend said something like, well she must have generalized from other verbs. I said that's exactly the step Chomsky says is empty, so I wasn't going to count it as an explanation. She got frustrated and said I'd turned a point about linguistics into a refusal to think normally about people.

Was my policy defensible? Maybe the original argument works only because language has a specific kind of structure, and using it everywhere makes me both a bore and a bad epistemologist. Or maybe the original point is general, and her irritation is just the usual reaction to being asked for a mechanism. Where exactly does the Chomsky passage license my habit, and where do I start to lean on it too hard? Is there a version of this rule I should actually follow, say with a threshold for when an everyday explanation is allowed to leave the mapping vague?
