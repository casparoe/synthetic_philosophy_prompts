So I help run a reading group that's been going back and forth on sunk costs for months, and we've finally agreed to do a proper adversarial collaboration instead of another argument thread. The two camps are roughly: (1) the orthodoxy that honoring sunk costs is always a mistake, that only expected future costs and benefits matter, and that the money or time already spent is unrecoverable either way; and (2) the revisionist camp that thinks sunk costs can at least sometimes be something a rational agent should honor — for example because abandoning past investments can change your reputation with yourself or others, or because treating every half-finished project as free to abandon undercuts the habits and narratives that let you finish anything at all.

I want you to write the document the two camps would produce after a genuinely good-faith effort: a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, then a disagreements table. For the agreed claims, make them the strongest non-trivial claims both sides accept, including specifics about what counts as a sunk cost and what it means in choice terms to "honor" one. For each row of the table: the exact disagreement stated in neutral wording; the phrasing each side would accept of its own view, so the revisionist row is worded the way an actual revisionist would endorse rather than a strawman; the crux or cruxes in that row; and for each crux, a concrete case or intervention both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Try hard to keep the statement from collapsing into definitions. Our hunch is that one crux is whether "you'll finish fewer things if you treat sunk costs as irrelevant" is a causal claim about real agents or a normative claim about ideal agents, and another is whether whatever reputational or motivational value honoring sunk costs has can be gotten without actually letting the decision depended on past costs. We want a document neither camp would wince at signing, but also not so watered down it's vacuous. If some disagreement turns out to have been purely verbal, say so and fold it into the agreed claims.
