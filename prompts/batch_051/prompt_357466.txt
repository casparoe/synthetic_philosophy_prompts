So I just came home from a long argument with my friend about money pumps and I still can't shake the feeling she proved too much. Her example was this: suppose I prefer apples to bananas, bananas to cherries, and cherries to apples. Then someone offers me a cherry in exchange for my apple plus a penny, then a banana for that cherry plus a penny, then an apple for that banana plus a penny, and I'm back to an apple but three pennies down. I would say yes to each trade because each swap, looked at by itself, takes me to something I prefer. She says this proves circular preferences are irrational. My objection: why isnt the lesson just that I should stop trading with that person, or that I should evaluate the whole sequence of swaps before agreeing? If someone uses hidden information to extract money from someone with perfectly coherent preferences, we say that person got swindled, not that they were irrational. What is actually different between being money-pumped and being swindled? And she kept insisting that the same logic shows changing your mind over time is a rational failure — like making a gym plan and breaking it at 11pm means you are incoherent. That seems way too strong. Where does her argument go wrong, or where does mine?
