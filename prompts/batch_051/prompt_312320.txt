For a seminar in the philosophy of statistical mechanics, write the list of requirements a theory must satisfy to count as an explanation of the thermodynamic arrow of time — that is, an account that explains why isolated systems evolve toward higher-entropy states in one temporal direction, why records and memories are of the past rather than the future, and why interventions reach toward the future rather than the past, instead of merely redescribing those asymmetries. For each requirement, state the observation, principle, or argument that motivates it, situating it against at least the following where relevant: the time-symmetry of the underlying microdynamics, Loschmidt's reversibility objection, Poincaré's recurrence objection, the low-entropy boundary condition often called the past hypothesis, and the Boltzmann-brain worry. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable for any adequate account, which are demands of a particular camp, and which are parochial preferences that could be dropped without losing the explanatory target. I am most interested in whether the full list is jointly satisfiable: if two otherwise-plausible requirements conflict once reversibility, recurrence, or Boltzmann brains are taken seriously, identify which must give way and at what cost. Do not turn the answer into a scorecard of existing proposals; the criteria are the deliverable. Lead with the single requirement you judge most load-bearing, stated as your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning behind each requirement in turn. No preamble and no restating of the assignment.
