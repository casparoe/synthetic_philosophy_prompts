A county health authority's prioritization committee allocates a fixed budget across patient groups. Each proposal comes with a projection of the healthy years gained or lost by the affected groups. So far the committee has ruled as follows:

(1) It approved a proposal under which the best-off group would lose 500 healthy years and the worst-off group would gain 400.
(2) It rejected a proposal under which the worst-off group would lose 50 healthy years and the best-off group would gain 100.
(3) It approved a proposal under which the best-off group would lose 300 healthy years and the second-worst-off group would gain 250.

The worst-off and second-worst-off groups are below the authority's sufficiency threshold; the best-off group is far above it.

Next week the committee votes on a proposal that would withdraw an optional treatment from the best-off group, costing it 200 healthy years, while no other group gains or loses anything. The only projected consequence besides that loss is a narrower gap between the best-off group and everyone else—a pure leveling down.

What should the committee decide if it wants to be consistent with its own precedents? Which earlier decision is the closest analog? If the precedents underdetermine the answer, state what principle the committee would be adopting with each possible decision—prioritarianism, sufficientarianism, or a form of egalitarianism—and identify the further case that would settle which of these principles best explains its record.
