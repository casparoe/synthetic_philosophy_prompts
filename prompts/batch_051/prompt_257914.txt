Here is the material I want you to translate. Philosophers argue about supertasks: tasks that decompose into infinitely many steps carried out in a finite time. The standard test case is a lamp. It starts on. It is toggled at time 1/2 minute, toggled again at time 3/4 minute, again at time 7/8 minute, and so on through the sequence of times approaching the one-minute mark: after each toggle, the next toggle occurs halfway between the present time and one minute. The schedule is fully specified. The disputed question is whether the lamp is on or off at the one-minute mark.

One position says the schedule is well-formed and the question must have a determinate answer. A second says no answer is determined: every toggle is followed by a later toggle, there is no final action whose effect persists, and the sequence of states oscillates between on and off without settling. Some draw the stronger conclusion that the example exposes an incoherence in supertasks themselves, or in the idea of completing infinitely many acts in a finite time. Others reply that the example shows only that this particular description fails to determine an outcome, and that other supertasks could have perfectly determinate results.

Restate these positions, one by one, in my vocabulary: the claim that no infinite sequence of acts can be completed in finite time; the claim that the lamp's state at the limit is unspecified rather than contradictory; the stronger conclusion that the lamp reveals an incoherence in supertasks; and the reply that a supertask is determinate when the relevant sequence of states has a well-defined limit.
