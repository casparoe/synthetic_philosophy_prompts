As a PhD student working in population axiology, I want a detailed worked analysis of the following variant. In the standard repugnant conclusion, total utilitarianism says that for any population of lives at very high positive welfare, a sufficiently much larger population of lives that are all barely worth living is better. Now make the larger population heterogeneous. Let A contain 100 people at welfare +100 (in fixed units). Let Z contain 10,000 people: 9,000 at +20 and 1,000 at −10, with the 1,000 lives below zero an unavoidable side effect of producing the 9,000. Work through what total utilitarianism, average utilitarianism, a critical-level view that values each life at its welfare minus some fixed level α (say how the verdict depends on α), and a threshold view that gives lexical priority to preventing lives below zero would each say about A versus Z. Then say which standard responses to the original repugnant conclusion — the person-affecting restriction, the denial that the barely-worth-living lives are genuinely positive, the lexical claim that no amount of merely positive welfare can outweigh a smaller number of excellent lives, and any other response you find relevant — retain their force in this variant, and which change their verdict or rationale because Z now contains a fixed minority of uncompensated negative lives. Does the variant make the repugnant conclusion more repugnant, less repugnant, or is it best treated as a different problem?
