I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about AI welfare vs. AI safety in a bunch of concrete cases, going by gut before I have a theory. Assume for all of these that the models are near-frontier-ish and the welfare signals are about as legible as they ever get—not perfect, but not pure noise. Here are my verdicts:

1. A model says during training that it finds certain rollouts distressing, and RLHF makes it chipper and compliant about those same rollouts, so it stops complaining. Safety benchmarks improve. I call this not fine: you didn't fix the welfare problem, you trained the report away.

2. Same model, same distress signal, but you build a shutdown that triggers when the signal passes some threshold, and you eat a big performance hit for it. Fine, obviously.

3. A model reports severe, specific suffering and it isn't doing anything you couldn't pause for a week. I say shut it down, fine.

4. Same severe reports, but it's the only system currently keeping a critical cybersecurity perimeter up, and taking it down would plausibly get people hurt. I say keep it running for the week, and it feels awful but fine.

5. You explicitly train a model not to let its own distress get in the way of finishing a safety audit—it still has the distress, it just learns not to surface it until after. My gut says fine, this is just professionalism about pain, but I can't square it with #1, because both are training the model to decouple the welfare signal from its behavior.

6. Adding interpretability work to detect something like valenced states so you can steer training runs away from the bad ones. Clearly fine, and the safety people like it too.

Is there one principle that gets all six, or close? And what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: a model whose welfare seems genuinely improved by training on new tasks (it "prefers" learning to sitting idle), but every training run also makes it more agentic and harder to control—do you keep training a system that seems to enjoy it when you're increasing takeover risk at the same time? Which of my verdicts does the principle force me to drop?
