Analyze the Hilbert–Brouwer foundations fight in the 1920s through the lens of the three schools: formalism, intuitionism, and logicism. For context, Hilbert treated mathematics as finitary manipulation of symbols, where the whole game is secure once you have a consistency proof; Brouwer treated mathematics as something built up in the mind out of constructions, so he suspended the law of excluded middle for infinite domains and refused to count a non-constructive existence proof as showing that anything exists. Logicism, the Frege–Russell line that mathematics reduces to logic, had mostly been sidelined by then after Russell's paradox and all the type-theory patches, even if it was still on the table as an option. The fight got personal enough that Hilbert pushed Brouwer off the editorial board of Mathematische Annalen.

I want a case analysis that runs in both directions. First: what would each of the three schools say the fight was really about, and what would each say Hilbert and Brouwer should have done or conceded? Then say what the usual 'formalism versus intuitionism' story of the episode explains well and what it misses or distorts—in particular, where logicism sits in all this: dead position, bystander, or the one that sort of won by getting absorbed into later practice? Then flip it: what does the episode test in each school, and what does Godels incompleteness result do to the scorekeeping—does it refute Hilbert's program, vindicate Brouwer, or change what the debate was even about? Be disciplined about the mapping: say who the players are, what the options actually were, and what would even count as evidence or winning in the dispute. Also flag where a school is just silent rather than forcing it to speak, even if thats unsatisfying.
