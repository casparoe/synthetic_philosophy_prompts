I've been mulling over Frankfurt's bullshit/lying distinction and where LLM hallucination fits, and I've got a little homegrown theory I want someone to push on honestly rather than just nod along. I've been calling it ersatz bullshit, or maybe post-sincere speech, I keep going back and forth. The idea is that Frankfurtian bullshit needs a speaker who could care about the truth but doesn't, while a liar cares enough to hide it. An LLM has no self that could care either way, so when it confidently invents a fake court case or says a living person is dead, it isn't lying and it isn't quite bullshitting in Frankfurt's sense either. It's producing assertoric-looking text with no truth-attitude behind it at all, and that's what I mean by ersatz bullshit: all the surface features of bullshit without the underlying indifference, because indifference is still a relation a speaker has to truth and the model doesn't have it. I'd also split hallucinations into two kinds: sampling slips, where the weights roughly encode the right answer but the decoder walks down a wrong low-probability path, and gap hallucinations, where the training data underdetermines the answer and the model has to emit something anyway. My question is whether this is a coherent distintion, whether it already exists under another name I'm missing, what it gets right, and where it breaks. Point me at the clearest actual hallucination case that my taxonomy gets wrong, and the one it would miscategorize.
