I oversee the content moderation rulebook for a large online forum. The rules hinge on a short list of terms, above all doxxing, and we keep a glossary meant to settle what each term covers. The current definition treats doxxing as revealing a person's private identifying information with the aim of exposing them to harm, but every few months a new edge case divides the moderator team: reposting an already-public document, linking to public property records, sharing a photograph in which an address happens to be visible, or quoting someone who voluntarily posted their own location.

One camp argues that we should keep tightening the definition, clause by clause, until the gaps close. The other camp says we have done three rounds of refinement and the disputes have not become less frequent. Each new definition introduces words that are themselves open to interpretation, and at some point a person still has to decide whether the rule applies here. They claim that what actually fixes the meaning of doxxing for us is not the glossary entry but the commitments our team keeps incurring and refusing: what evidence we treat as entitling us to apply the term, what we treat applying it as entailing, and what we treat it as incompatible with.

I have to decide where to put the team's effort over the next year. Is the drive for a complete, self-applying definition a mistake? Should we instead maintain something like a living record of decided cases and the inferences we endorse or reject for the term? And what should I count as evidence that a newly hired moderator genuinely understands the rule rather than merely being able to recite it?

Please also tell me what would change your mind: what facts about how the rule actually functions in this community, or about what a definition can do, would push you toward the opposite recommendation, and where your own reasoning is most likely to go wrong. Answer in American English.
