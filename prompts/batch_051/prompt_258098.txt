A colleague who works on the epistemology of automation has a settled track record of verdicts about when an AI tool erodes a user's understanding of the domain. Her rulings so far are these.

- A medical student uses a unit-conversion app while solving pharmacokinetics problems. Verdict: no erosion; the arithmetic is outsourced, but the modeling and physiological reasoning remain the student's.
- A researcher uses a transparent statistics package that reports full model specifications and diagnostics, then interprets the estimates and writes up the analysis. Verdict: no erosion.
- A physics student uses a tutoring system that displays fully worked derivations step by step, and she studies each step until she can reconstruct the derivation herself. Verdict: no erosion.
- A junior physician routinely adopts the output of a proprietary symptom checker that returns a diagnosis and treatment plan without explaining its reasoning, and she cannot reconstruct that reasoning. Verdict: erosion.
- A student uses a reading assistant that produces argumentative summaries and essay plans for assigned texts; he writes from those plans without doing the reading and cannot reconstruct the texts' moves. Verdict: erosion.

The new case: a senior radiologist uses a diagnostic AI that is fully explainable — it flags the imaging features it used, lists the differential it considered, and provides a step-by-step rationale. She can follow and explain each output, but over several years she has stopped examining scans independently and now simply confirms the AI's findings.

Predict her verdict on the new case. Identify the candidate principles consistent with her earlier judgments — for example, an opacity/verification principle on which erosion occurs only when the user cannot follow the tool's reasoning, an exercise/atrophy principle on which erosion occurs whenever the user stops performing the activity that constitutes understanding, or some other candidate — and say what each predicts about the new case. Give your best guess and the reasons for it, and name one further case, not a variant of the present one, that would most clearly tell the candidate principles apart.
