Hi! I'm a high school junior doing a summer internship at my city's stormwater and climate-resilience office. They know I'm into debate and stuff, so they gave me this weird assignment: write a one-page decision procedure the city engineers can use whenever they get a new batch of climate model projections and have to pick a design number (how big to make a culvert, how high a flood wall, what 100-year storm to plan for). The engineers are smart but they arn't climate scientists, and they don't have time to read model documentation. Mostly they open a consultant's report, see a big table of model runs with an ensemble mean and a range, and then -- my boss says -- the city makes two opposite mistakes: either they build to the ensemble average, or they panic and build to the single scariest model. Both are expensive in different ways.

Please write the actual procedure I can hand them. It should be a reusable checklist (or a flow chart in words): the questions to ask about an ensemble in order, what counts as a good-enough answer to each, where the procedure says 'stop, don't use this number,' and where it says 'this is a judgment call, a person has to decide.' I know just enough to be dangerous: I've heard that models share code so agreement between them isn't really independent evidence, that the ensemble mean isn't an actual model run and can be physically weird, and that a decision that works okay under many models is sometimes better than one that's optimal under one. But I don't know how to turn that into steps, or how to keep judgment from leaking in while pretending it hasn't.

Then run the procedure on three decisions the city is actually facing: (1) replacing a small culvert that floods a low-income neighborhood, where the ensemble mean says 'go bigger' but a quarter of models say 'go way, way bigger'; (2) setting the height of a new sea wall where the top models disagree a lot and the most extreme one comes from the same modeling group that nailed the last big storm; and (3) adopting a new heat-risk map for school closures that mostly comes from a single group, and the mayor really wants to use it. Be honest about which steps are real rules and which are just judgment with nicer clothes on.
