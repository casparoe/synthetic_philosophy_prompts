Okay so this is going to sound a little raw, but the topic got personal for me recently and I want to think it through properly. For most of the last year I told myself my relationship was fine. Id say we were just in a rough patch, and then dodge the follow-up questions. When it ended I couldn't shake the feeling that I'd known for months it was already over, and that I'd been hiding that from myself. So now I'm stuck on the old puzzle, not as a class exercise but as something I actually need to sort out for my own head.

The claim I can't get past is that self-deception is, strictly speaking, impossible. Lying seems to require that the liar knows the truth and intentionally brings about a false belief in the person being lied to. In self-deception the liar and the lied-to are the same person, with access to the same information at the same time. So either self-deception is just loose shorthand for a bunch of weaker things — wishful thinking, biased attention, rationalizing after the fact, changing your mind — or there's a way for one person to genuinely keep a truth from themself.

I want you to act as a philosopher of mind who has read the self-deception literature but also respects experimental psychology — not someone who thinks an armchair argument settles everything. Think carefully. Start by brainstorming what could even count as evidence one way or the other. Then propose a concrete test — an experiment, a measurement, a set of observations — that could tell genuine self-deception apart from those weaker phenomena. Be specific about the manipulation, the comparison group, and what result would count. Then say what a positive result would really show and what it would fail to show. I keep thinking that the truth I was hiding must have leaked somewhere: in the split-second flinch before I said things were fine, in the friends I stopped bringing my partner around. Is that the kind of thing a test could be built out of? And at the end, say whether self-deception deserves to survive as the name for something real or whether we should retire the term and talk only about the weaker stuff.
