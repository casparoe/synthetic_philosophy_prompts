I've been trying to pin down why I can't just accept karma as a theory of moral desert, and I think I finally found the exact spot. The theory has real pull on me, maybe because it's so economical: every action has a result that matches it in kind and degree, nothing that happens to a person is unearned, no wrong slips through, and there's no external judge or statute of limitations needed. It also does something almost nothing else does — it takes the most unfair-looking fact there is, that one kid is born healthy and another isn't, and says there's a hidden ledger that makes it fair. That's a genuine explanatory payoff, and I don't want to pretend otherwise.

But the intuition that won't budge is this: you can't deserve to suffer for a deed you have no access to. If someone has no memory of doing X, not even a felt residue of having chosen badly, then making them bear X's consequences starts to look like punishing an innocent stranger for someone else's crime. The usual move — that it's still the same person across rebirths — feels like its doing a lot of work, because the thing that makes me me, the continuous first-person thread, is exactly the thing that got cut between lives. So I'm stuck between a theory with systematic virtues I actually feel and an intuition that feels non-negotiable. I don't want a verdict handed to me. I want the strongest version of each side, and then a negotiated answer: what would it cost to debunk the intuition and drop it, what would it cost to amend karma so it stops being a desert claim (e.g., making it a purely causal law, or a matter of inheriting character traits rather than paying off a debt), and is there a reading of 'desert' on which a newborn can deserve its circumstances without that being absurd? I'd also like you to use amnesia and blackout cases from ordinary law as a test case, since they seem to cut both ways — the law punishes people for acts they don't remember, but those people still have the same body, the same social identity, and witnesses who remember, all of which rebirth strips away. Tell me whether that analogy actually settles anything about the memory condition on desert.
