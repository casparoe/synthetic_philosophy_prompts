Before I read another repair of the knowability principle, I want the spec rather than a survey. I'm defending an anti-realist line on which truth is whatever can in principle be known — no unknowable truths — and I need an account of the knowability principle that keeps that without collapsing into the claim that every truth is actually known. Write the desiderata such an account has to meet. For each requirement, give the case or step of the Church–Fitch derivation that forces it: factivity of K; distribution of K over conjunction, along with the modal closure that lets you move it inside the possibility operator; the Moore-ish proposition p ∧ ¬Kp; an ordinary unknown truth like the number of coins on my desk this second (if I haven't counted them); and the distinction between being knowable and being known at some time by some agent — plus anything else you think is forced. Then tell me which of these are non-negotiable, which are just one camp's prefernce, whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable, and which single requirement does most to kill off the standard fixes. Skip the scorecard of the fixes themselves; the list is the thing I'm after.
