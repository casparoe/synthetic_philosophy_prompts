I've been trying to figure out whether I actually believe in moral principles or whether I'm some kind of particularist, so I wrote down my gut reactions to a bunch of cases about breaking commitments. My tentative rule is something like: breaking a commitment is wrong unless keeping it would impose a serious cost on you or someone else, the kind of cost the other person couldn't reasonably expect you to absorb. Here are the cases, with my verdicts:

- I promise to drive a friend to the airport for a 6 a.m. flight. The night before I'm too anxious to sleep, and at 5 a.m. I text them that I cant do it. I think this is wrong, even though I was genuinely exhausted.
- Same setup, but I have food poisoning. I cancel. I think that's fine.
- Same setup, but I got the food poisoning from gas-station sushi I ate on a dare the night before. I still think cancelling is fine, but I'm less sure, and I think it might matter whether I actually threw up.
- I promise to water a neighbor's plants for two weeks while they're away. I forget one day, the plants wilt but recover. I think it's wrong but a small wrong, and it doesnt matter that nobody noticed.
- I promise to be a friend's plus-one at a wedding. A week before, I remember that my abusive ex will be there. I cancel. I think that's fine.
- I promise to go to a friend's birthday dinner, but on the day I'm just emotionally drained and can't face a group. I cancel. I think this is fine too, but I have a nagging feeling it shouldnt be, given what I said about the airport case. Those two feel different and I can't say why.

Is there one principle—or at most a couple of candidate principles—that gets all of these, and if not, which of my verdicts does the best one force me to drop? And what does your best candidate say about a case I keep going back and forth on: someone breaks a lunch date because they got stuck at work, versus someone who breaks the same date because they'd double-booked themselves through carelessness? Same obligation, different history—does the principle care, or is it just case-by-case all the way down?
