Write the joint statement that a committed conciliationist and a committed steadfast epistemologist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration about peer disagreement. Quick definitions so we're on the same page: conciliationism is the view that learning an epistemic peer disagrees with you is by itself a reason to move substantially toward their credence; steadfastness is the view that you can rationally hold your credence more or less fixed unless you get independent reason to think the other person made a mistake. I want the actual document they'd produce together, not a transcript of them going back and forth.

The document should include: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, covering what makes someone an epistemic peer, what exactly the disagreement evidence is, a clean symmetrical case where both agree you should move, and a case where both agree you shouldnt; (2) the residual disagreements stated precisely, with each side worded so its own proponent would accept the characterization, and if you catch yourself writing a strawman, fix it; (3) the cruxes, by which I mean sub-questions such that settling them would force at least one side to change their view; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about that case recorded in advance.

Also handle the self-undermining worry: what the conciliationist should do on learning that a peer is a steadfastist about peer disagreement itself, and whether the steadfastist's response in that case differs from there response to an ordinary factual disagreement. And flag any part of the dispute you think is merely verbal and restate it in a way both sides would, on reflection, say they never meant to deny. Don't settle the debate; the point is the jointly-signed text.
