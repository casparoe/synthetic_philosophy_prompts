I work in the intergovernmental affairs unit of a mid-sized state in a federal country. Several times a month our legislature considers a bill that would move authority either up to the state or down to municipalities and counties — building codes, rental standards, siting of public facilities, school calendars, data-sharing among local police. My office has to write a short recommendation on the allocation question: is this properly a state matter, or a local one? Our constitution and our political culture nod to subsidiarity, but in practice the term is invoked with equal confidence by people who want the state to act and by people who want it to keep out. The people who want state action cite spillovers across municipal lines and the risk of a race to the bottom; the people who want local control cite familiarity with local conditions and democratic accountability. I was discussing this after a hearing last week, and it struck me that subsidiarity is functioning in our debate less as a decision rule than as a slogan both teams reach for. I want a procedure that actually discriminates between cases.

I need the procedure to be runnable in two or three working days by a generalist staffer, without consulting subject-matter experts. It must end either in a clear recommendation or in an explicit statement that the remaining question is one of political values, to be settled by our elected members rather than by staff. It must also be repeatable across very different policy areas. Please write the procedure, grounded in a defensible reading of subsidiarity, and then run it on two proposals. (a) A bill to set a statewide minimum energy-efficiency standard for rental housing, on the grounds that tenants move across municipal lines and landlords should not face a patchwork of local rules. (b) A bill to transfer from municipalities to the state the authority to choose sites for emergency shelters and transitional housing, on the grounds that wealthy municipalities currently shift the burden onto poorer ones. My office is inclined to treat (a) as a local matter and (b) as a proper state matter; I want to know whether your procedure would let us keep those intuitions or would push back on one of them.

Please structure the response as follows. "The procedure": an ordered list of questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to elected officials — about half the response. "Application to the energy-efficiency standard": work through the questions and state the output — about one fifth. "Application to the shelter-siting authority": work through the questions and state the output, candidly noting any place the procedure contradicts the inclination I described — about one fifth. "Limits": which distinctions of a full subsidiarity analysis the simplification drops, and what the procedure cannot settle — about one tenth.
