Write a deadpan parody of a trade association's certification standard, published by a fictional body called the Original Aboutness Alliance, which issues a "Certified Original Intentionality" seal to systems whose mental states are supposed to be about things in their own right, rather than merely possessed of derived intentionality assigned to them by users or observers. The document should run about 700–800 words, formatted as numbered clauses in the voice of real standards documentation, with definitions and eligibility criteria, testing procedures, grounds for revocation, and a short FAQ or appeals section. The satire should target the position — not any actual philosopher — that original intentionality is the genuine article and derived intentionality a second-class imitation: the position the "two-bitser" reply attacks by insisting that second-hand aboutness is plenty and original aboutness isn't worth paying extra for. The jokes should come from getting the dialectic right. Work in: the Chinese Room as the canonical cautionary tale about aboutness that is only imported by a user; the thermostat and the chess computer as awkward boundary cases (the first too simple to let in, the second clearly about the board but only because we interpret it that way); the embarrassing fact that natural selection, which built whatever original intentionality humans have, is itself nobody with intentions, so the Alliance must either rule that human intentionality is itself derived or grant original status to a mindless process; and, above all, the fact that any audit of original aboutness can only be performed from the outside, by an observer ascribing the very aboutness whose originality was supposed to be detected. The piece should stay deadpan, be funny to a philosopher of mind and readable to a layperson, and end by quietly conceding, without winking, that the Alliance's own certification has merely derived, observer-relative standing.
