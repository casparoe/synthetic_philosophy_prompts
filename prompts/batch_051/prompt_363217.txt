A friend who's been reading cognitive-science-of-religion stuff cornered me with this and I can't find the way out.

(1) The best explanation for why people hold religious beliefs is a bunch of ordinary cognitive machinery—agency detection, mind-reading, teleological bias—that evolution built for other jobs, not for tracking the truth about gods or the supernatural.
(2) If the best explanation of why you hold a belief doesn't involve that belief's being true, then you ought to suspend the belief unless you have independent evidence for it.
(3) So you ought to suspend religious beliefs unless you have independent evidence for them.
(4) But the best explanation of why you hold any belief at all, including scientific and moral ones, is also just natural machinery shaped by evolution and culture, and those explanations don't involve the beliefs' being true either.
(5) So by the same principle you'd have to suspend scientific and moral beliefs too unless you have independent evidence for them—and the independent evidence for a scientific belief is itself just more scientific beliefs, so this pulls the rug out from everything.
(6) So the principle in (2) is too strong, and the whole debunking of religion cuts its own throat.

He thinks this shows any debunking argument from the natural origins of religion is dead on arrival. I distrust arguments that end in "so the debunker loses," but I can't locate the exact slight of hand. Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost?
