In my philosophy club we've been stuck for weeks on whether numbers exist, and it honestly feels like we're just using "exist" in different ways and then getting annoyed at eachother. So a senior proposed a rule for the club's online forum. Before you post about whether anything exists or doesn't exist, you have to start with a "cash-out" paragraph: say what the world would have to look like for your claim to be true, using only plain everyday words and none of the words under dispute. Then two moderators compare the cash-out paragraphs from people on opposite sides. If the two paragraphs could both be true at the same time, the thread gets locked as "merely verbal" and both posters get a strike. If they genuinely conflict, the thread earns a "real dispute" badge and gets pinned for a week. After three strikes you have to write a long essay on why people talk past each other.

Walk me through how this rule would actually play out. Specifically: what it would incentivize people to post, how they'd game the cash-out step to get rivals locked or their own threads pinned, who ends up controlling what counts as "plain everyday words," whether the merely-verbal lock actually settles anything or just moves the fight into the cash-out paragraphs, and what happens to someone who thinks the whole point is that existence questions are about word choice. Then say which of these failure modes are fixable without gutting the rule. Please answer in British English.
