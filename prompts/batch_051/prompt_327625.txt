In my philosophy of physics course we've been going through Noether's theorem and I keep going back and forth on whether the symmetry picture actually explains conservation laws or just repackages them. The way we've set it up: a system is described by an action, and if the action is invariant under a continuous symmetry you get a conserved quantity for free — time-translation invariance gives energy conservation, spatial translations give momentum, rotations give angular momentum, gauge transformations give charge. That's gorgeous. It makes conservation look like a fact about what the laws don't care about, rather than a brute fact about the world, and it unifies a bunch of otherwise scattered conservation laws into one tidy framework. It even tells you what to expect when the symmetry is broken: the quantity is no longer exactly conserved. So on reflection I'm genuinely attracted to it.

But I cant shake the intuition that the explanatory arrow points the wrong way. Energy conservation feels like a fact about the world's stuff: the total amount of energy just doesn't change, full stop. When I imagine a toy universe where the Lagrangian has a tiny time-dependent term so that energy is only approximately conserved, I dont find myself thinking "oh, energy was never a real thing, it was just the shadow of time-translation invariance." I think "there's now a small leak, but energy is still the thing that's leaking." The symmetry story feels like it's tracking a formal feature of the mathematics, and the real reason energy is conserved is, well, that the dynamics conserve it — which is nearly a tautology, which makes me worry the symmetry explanation is also a tautology dressed up as unification.

So here's what I want worked through, without just picking a side. If you had to make me give up the intuition, what exactly would you be claiming: that energy conservation is literally the same fact as the symmetry statement, so the intuition is just confused, or that the intuition is residue from a "stuff ontology" that physics doesn't actually owe us? What would it cost to instead amend the theory so it says Noether codifies conservation laws without explaining them — and how would you tell that amendment apart from quietly giving up the unifying insight? And is there a stable middle position, like the symmetry explains why conservation takes the form of a local continuity equation while the intuition is tracking something else, maybe a metaphysical claim about what a conserved quantity is? I want to know what would have to be true for each option to be the right one.
