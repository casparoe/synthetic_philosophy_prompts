I keep flip-flopping between thinking heterophenomenology is the only sane method for the science of consciousness and thinking it's a kind of methodological gaslighting. The theory, as I get it: the only thing a third-person science can start from is what subjects do and say — button presses, galvanic skin response, verbal reports — and the reports get treated as a text, a useful fiction about a heterophenomenal world full of pains, afterimages, inner speech, whatever the subject claims to be going through. The scientist's job is to explain why that text gets produced, not to decide weather the items in its world really exist. What pulls me toward it: it's fully third-person, it doesn't smuggle in qualia or a private theatre, it doesn't make the subject infallible, and it doesn't pretend to solve the hard problem by fiat. It just turns consciousness into a tractable scientific object.

And then there's the intuition I can't shake. When I stub my toe and say "that hurts," I'm not reporting on a character in a fiction; I'm expressing something I stand in a different relation to than you do. It's not that I'm introspecting a private screening room, but I am not merely emitting a bit of text to be interpreted. A method that tells me to bracket whether my report is true — to treat it as one more bit of data about my beliefs — seems to have ruled the first-person point of view out by stipulation. Saying the method is neutral about the reality of the pain makes it worse, not better: the pain is exactly the thing I can't be neutral about.

So I want a negotiated verdict, not a cheerleading speech for one side. Walk me through the three ways out and what each one costs: (1) the intuition is a leftover Cartesian theatre impulse and should be debunked — what would debunking it actually need to establish, and does heterophenomenology establish that or just assert it? (2) the theory needs amending so some reports can be treated as defeasibly authoritative about their objects without falling back into pure introspection — is there a stable middle that doesn't just collapse into asking people how their mind works and trusting them? (3) the clash is mostly verbal, and once you spell out what "fiction," "report," and "authority" are doing, the method and the intuition are compatible — but if that's the move, whose usage of the terms has to change?
