For my philosophy of history class we've been doing hindsight bias and the narrative fallacy, and I can't stop seeing it in accident reports. Take the standard root cause analysis after a plane crash or a medical death: the report starts from the bad outcome and works backwards, identifies one "root cause" (a cracked seal, a young doctor who didn't escalate), and narrates everything so the disaster seems like it was building inevitably to that moment. The conclusion is their all along, not constructed. But a near miss with the identical cracked seal or the same failure to escalate, where nothing bad happens, doesn't get a story written about it at all. What does this investigative practice presuppose about how historical explanation works? Does it presuppose that events really do have a unique cause "out there" waiting to be found, independent of the retrospective narrative, or is that just a pragmatic framing? Reconstruct the implicit theory of explanation and causal selection the practice is committed to, then separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with. Finally, tell me what a post-accident investigation would actually have to look like if it instead presupposed that causes get selected after the fact and that the feeling of inevitability is a hindsight artifact.
