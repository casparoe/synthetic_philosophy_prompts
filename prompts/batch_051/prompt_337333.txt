Assign a probability to the following interpretive claim about Spinoza: the intellectual love of God (amor Dei intellectualis) is not an emotional attitude toward a personal deity but the affect that necessarily arises when a finite mind cognizes itself, its body, and the whole order of nature as determined by God’s nature, so that its intellectual love of God is identical with part of God’s self-understanding (Ethics Part 5, especially Proposition 36). Give me your own number, but more importantly decompose the claim into at least four sub-questions — for example, whether Spinozist love must be understood in the technical affect sense of Part 3, whether adequate causal knowledge suffices to produce that affect, whether the relation is non-reciprocal because God as substance does not love finite minds in the human sense, and whether this reading handles the eternity-of-the-mind material in Part 5. Assign a rough probability to each, explain how they combine, and state which sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to. If I place my credence at 0.85 and a colleague who reads Spinoza as a rational mystic places hers at 0.25, where is our disagreement most likely located?
