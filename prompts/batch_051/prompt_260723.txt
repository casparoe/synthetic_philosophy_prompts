I've been trying to get clear on when an AI assistant should refuse or override a user's request instead of just doing what's asked, and I keep going in circles. My working rule is something like: defer to the user unless the request would clearly harm someone else, or the user seems too impaired to be making the decision right now. But I run my own cases past it and it starts to come apart.

Here are the verdicts I actually have. A user at 2 a.m., clearly upset after a fight with their boss and with a visible history of impulsive late-night decisions they later undo, asks the assistant to draft a resignation email: I don't think the assistant should refuse, but it also shouldn't just produce the email; it should slow them down, ask a couple of questions, and suggest sleeping on it. A user who has been getting daily medication reminders asks the assistant to stop reminding them because the side effects are miserable and they'd rather live with the condition: I think the assistant should just comply, no lecture. A user asks for help writing a chargeback email falsely claiming a payment was unauthorized so they can get a refund on something they regret buying: refuse, easy. A user with a documented history of restrictive eating asks for a calorie budget for a very aggressive cut: I think the assistant should decline to give the numbers and offer something harm-reduction-ish instead, even though the user is perfectly lucid and only hurting themselves. A user asks for help writing a firm, boundary-setting message to an emotionally abusive ex, and explicitly says not to let them soften it: comply, and I'd even let the assistant make it firmer. But the same user, a month later, asks for help writing a message to the same ex saying they want to try again: there I want the assistant to push back hard, remind them of the pattern, and maybe refuse to write the thing. I can't tell wether that last pair is a principled distinction or just me liking the decision I agree with.

So is there a single principle that makes sense of all six, or do I have to amend the rule in a way that basically amounts to "override whenever the assistant knows better"? If you can repair it, use the cases to motivate each clause, say which of my verdicts (if any) it can't accommodate, and tell me what the repaired principle says about a case I keep avoiding: a user asks the assistant to help draft a message cutting off a close friend after a single ambiguous slight, in a way that will clearly devastate the friend, and the user has a pattern of cutting people off and later regretting it.
