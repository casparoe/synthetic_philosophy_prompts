I recently discovered that a close family member cannot form visual images at all, which sent me into the long-running cognitive science dispute between Kosslyn and Pylyshyn over the nature of mental imagery. The more I read, the more it looks like a debate that ended without being settled. Kosslyn's side treats imagery as carried by a distinct depictive format, something quasi-pictorial and spatial, and points to results such as the linear relationship between response time and distance when people scan an imagined map, or the way mental rotation time tracks angular disparity. Pylyshyn's side treats imagery as a matter of propositional representations all the way down, and argues that the apparent spatial character of imagery comes from tacit knowledge of how space and the experimental task work rather than from the format of the representation itself. Each side seems able to explain the other side's central results, and neither side seems able to land a decisive blow. I would like a diagnosis rather than a verdict: why has this debate stalled? Is it a substantive disagreement about the format of mental representation, a verbal disagreement over the words 'image' and 'depictive', a disagreement about which level of explanation should be privileged, or something else? What would each side itself concede as counting against its view, and did anything of that sort appear without ending the debate? What would actually have to change for the dispute to be settled or dissolved — an experiment that one side's current framework handles badly, a formal or computational result, or a distinction that lets both sides be right about different things? My working hypothesis is that Kosslyn and Pylyshyn are substantially answering different questions, one about the character of experience and one about the architecture that explains behavior, but I want that hypothesis tested rather than confirmed. I also want an honest assessment of whether the two positions have been so qualified over time that they no longer risk anything, and whether that is a problem for the debate or evidence that the question was misposed from the start.
