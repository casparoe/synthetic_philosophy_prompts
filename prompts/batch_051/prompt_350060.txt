Before I read another defense or critique of advance directives, I want the evaluative standard fixed. The theory I have in mind is meant to guide a hard decision: whether clinicians, families, and courts should honor a person's earlier competent wishes when the person can no longer speak for herself, or when the person now speaks — or merely seems content — against them. In the literature, arguments keep turning on particular cases: a man executes a directive refusing antibiotics if he develops advanced dementia and later appears placid and content; a person with bipolar disorder, while well, signs a Ulysses contract authorizing treatment over her future manic refusals; a now-competent patient revokes a do-not-resuscitate order she signed years earlier after her values changed. I want the full specification before I weigh any position. Please write the desiderata for a theory of the authority of the earlier self: what must an account of when and why a person's earlier competent wishes should bind a later, noncompetent, or differently-competent self get right? For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and mark which requirements are genuine constraints that any adequate view must satisfy and which are better understood as attractions or commitments of particular camps (those who stress the present self's interests and apparent well-being, those who hold that the earlier competent self's decisions carry authority over later stages, those who think severe dementia breaks the relevant continuity of personhood, and the like).

I would also like you to say whether the list is jointly satisfiable. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot both be met, and say what that conflict reveals — for example, if respecting a currently competent person's right to revoke collides with the idea that a deliberately self-binding Ulysses contract must hold, that may be the hard center of the topic rather than a defect in the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on ranking the existing views; but if one requirement does most of the work in separating viable theories from unviable ones, say which one and why. A table for the desiderata is welcome provided the justification of each row is not lost.
