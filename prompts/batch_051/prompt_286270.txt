I keep going back and forth on a redesign for the forum I help run. We have about 8,000 active accounts, and the current voting system has the usual problems: people with ancient high-karma accounts get treated as trusted basically forever, drive-by upvoting is everywhere, and there's an industry of "upvote me and I'll upvote you" rings. One of our mods floated something more drastic. The idea is that no vote would be free anymore. Every upvote or downvote would be a bonded vote: you'd have to put up maybe 5% of your current karma as a deposit that gets locked to that specific vote for 30 days. If the post's net karma after 30 days moved in the direction you voted, you get the deposit back with a small bonus; if it went the other way or stayed flat, the deposit is forfeited and split among the winners plus a trickle of newly minted karma. And karma itself would decay a few percent a week so nobody coasts on old status. New accounts would get a small starting grant so they can afford to vote at all.

I'd like you, as someone who actually understands mechanism design and social platforms, to walk me through what this does in practice rather than in theory. Take your time and brainstorm, don't just give me the first obvious take. Some of the things I'm chewing on: what does this do to who votes and on what? Do people just horde karma and vote only on safe posts? Where does it get gamed first, and by whom, collusion rings, brigading, timing the 30-day window, sybil accounts? How does it change what gets posted, and who ends up with outsized reputation? What breaks for small or new users, and does it push out lurkers or people who are right but unpopular? And if the obvious failure modes show up, are patches like making votes public, letting people withdraw early at a discount, or capping deposits actually fixing things or just adding new holes?
