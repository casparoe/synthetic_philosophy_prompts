I'm in a philosophy of science course right now and we've been doing Kuhn vs. Popper, and I keep noticing a pattern in the historical cases that I don't have a name for. Two schools in a field are looking at basically the same observations and the same numbers, and neither side is being obviously stupid or arguing in bad faith, but the argument just doesn't move. School A reports a result that feels like it should settle the debate. School B doesn't deny the data; they say the method A used assumes the very thing at issue, or that the result is an artifact of A's starting assumptions. Then B points to an old anomaly A has never dealt with, and A says that anomaly is a sideshow and maybe not even a real problem. Both responses sound completely reasonable when your inside the school making them, and slightly evasive when you're not. And the dispute doesn't end with a decisive experiment at all: one side just slowly gets more grad students, the textbooks get rewritten, and the old questions stop being interesting. At first I thought this was just Popper's point about always being able to wriggle out of a falsification, but he means that as a criticism of individual unscientific behavior, whereas here a whole community is doing it and somehow staying rational-looking. Is there an established name for this exact standoff? And I'd rather have the carving than the label: how is it different from an ordinary empirical disagreement where new evidence changes somebody's mind, and from the Duhem-Quine idea that no single failed prediction kills a theory by itself? The part I actually care about is whether the school that wins by demographics instead of by experiment can still count as rational, and if so, what "rational" is even doing there.
