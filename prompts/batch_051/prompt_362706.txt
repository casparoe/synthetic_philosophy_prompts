Two people in my study group got into it last night over our friend Dana, who has paid cancellation fees to switch gyms and then switch back three times this year. (1) One said she's being money-pumped: she keeps spending real money to end up back in the same situation, so whatever she's doing, it can't be rational. (2) The other said you can't call any single switch irrational — in March she genuinely preferred the other gym and in May she genuinely preferred this one again; changing your mind isn't a mistake just because your past self disagrees. (3) First one came back: fine, but Dana can predict the reversal, she's done it every time and even jokes she'll regret it while she's signing up. A rational person who can see their own flip coming shouldn't pay for the priviledge of cycling back and forth — they should either just pick one or bind themselves in advance. (4) Second one said that's just the January self bossing around the February self, and why is the January self the real one? If preferences can change, every time-slice gets an equal vote. Adjudicate it move by move: wich claims are right, which are wrong, and what are they both missing?
