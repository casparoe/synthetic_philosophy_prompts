Consider a social club whose founding rules include the following formulation of the golden rule:

"Each member shall act toward another member only in ways that she would choose to be acted toward were she in the other's situation, with the other's desires and fears as her own."

A member with early-stage dementia has begun sending increasingly large sums of money to an online acquaintance she has never met. Two years ago, while fully lucid, she signed a statement in the club's records directing her fellow members to stop any substantial gift or payment that she would not have made before her illness. She now insists that she has never been happier, that the acquaintance is the only person who listens to her, and that she wants no interference. The other members must decide what the rule requires of them.

How should the club interpret and apply the rule to this case? Set out what the plain wording, the evident purpose of the rule, and what its drafters would most plausibly have said if asked each imply, and say where they diverge. Then state what the club should do, and explain whether the case exposes a defect in this formulation that calls for amending it.
