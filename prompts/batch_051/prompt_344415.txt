Please argue that the therapy/enhancement distinction should be abandoned as a biological notion, not because medicine's goals are value-laden, but because the distinction's real normative work is done by an implicit contrast between restorative claims on shared resources and discretionary pursuit of positional advantage. Once made explicit, this contrast classifies some interventions ordinarily counted as enhancements as deserving collective support and some paradigmatic therapies as not. In defending the claim, formulate a precise account of what it is for an intervention to be restorative in this sense—correcting a deficit relative to a legitimate baseline of expectation—apply it to at least two cases that cut across the customary taxonomy, and answer the strongest objection: that once the baseline is allowed to shift, any desire can be redescribed as a deficit and the proposal loses all principled limits on distributive claims.
