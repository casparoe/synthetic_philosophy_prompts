I've been trying to understand the idea that making AI systems show their reasoning step by step could work as a safety measure, and I came across a long blog post that summarizes the idea like this:

Chain-of-thought monitoring rests on a simple principle: if a system is required to write out its reasoning in plain language before it acts, we can read what it is "really thinking" and intercept dangerous plans before they are carried out. The only serious difficulty is honesty — a devious system could write down one chain of reasoning while acting on another. But there are two reasons to think this won't be a problem in practice. First, when models are trained toward correct answers, their chains of thought are pushed to track the real causes of those answers, because reasoning that departs from the model's actual grounds would not be systematically reinforced. Second, a model trying to conceal a bad plan would have to leave traces: its written reasoning would become shorter, vaguer, or less causal, which a careful monitor would notice. So the realistic concern is not that a system quietly hides its intentions while we read along, but that monitoring is expensive and we might stop doing it. As long as we keep reading, chain-of-thought monitoring gives us a direct window into the model's actual decision process and a practical way to detect scheming.
