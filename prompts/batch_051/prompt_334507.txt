So in my world lit class we read the Zhuangzi and I keep getting stuck on the butterfly passage. The story, roughly speaking, is that Zhuangzi dreams he's a butterfly, fluttering around and feeling pretty happy, and when he wakes up he cant tell whether he is Zhuangzi who dreamed he was a butterfly, or a butterfly dreaming right now that its Zhuangzi. The text then says something about how this bears on transformation, and my classmates all nodded like it was profound while I mostly just sat there confused.

Then in epistemology we did Descartes' dream argument, and on the surface it seemed like the same move: I cant prove I'm not dreaming at this very moment, my waking experience could just be a very coherent dream, and that threatens my knowledge of the external world. My professor insisted the two are actually quite different skeptical moves, but between the translation and the time gap I couldn't tell whether that was a real distinction or just two courses using the same picture for different ends. Can you explain what Descartes is trying to undermine versus what the Zhuangzi passage is actually doing? In particular, I want to know whether the doubts are aimed at the same target, what each one would really threaten if taken seriously, and whether the Zhuangzi is even in the knowledge business at all, or whether we're importing Descartes' worry into a text that was trying to do something else.
