I've come around to a view about pain that I keep defending but havent actually stress-tested: the badness of pain isn't part of the sensation itself, it's a separate affective and evaluative response that's normally welded on so tightly we cant tell them apart. What moved me was pain asymbolia — patients, usually after certain lesions, who can say the pain is there, locate it, rate its intensity, tell you sharp from burning — but they dont mind it. No wincing, no withdrawal, no desire for it to stop. That looks like the sensory-discriminative core showing up intact while the badness got peeled off. If that's right, then saying pain is intrinsically bad is overclaiming; the badness is more like something we do with the sensation, not something the sensation already is.

What I want from you: tell me what would actually count against this. Not just the lazy "maybe they're confabulating" line — though tell me if that objection is stronger than I think — but the real map of where this view is exposed. Which clinical cases, introspective phenomena, empirical results, or conceptual arguments should genuinely move me off it? What does the view predict that rival views don't, and where does it do worse? What would a careful defender of the view have to concede as a genuine cost, as opposed to a bullet they can bite? Give me the mirror case too: if the badness can come apart from the sensation, there should be badness without the sensory core — does that show up, and does its existence (or weird absence) count against the position? I also don't know whether asymbolia strips the primary unpleasantness or just the secondary suffering — the pain itself no longer hurting versus the person no longer caring about it — and I'd like to know whether the view has to take a stand on that to be risky at all. And be honest: has this position been qualified into unfalsifiability, and is that a problem for a claim of this kind? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and separate the live escape routes from the merely logically possible ones.
