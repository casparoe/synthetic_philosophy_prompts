I'm on the General Education Committee at my university, and we're about to vote on a petition from the School of Nursing. Every undergrad has to take one approved "Ethics and Values" course, and right now the approved courses are all exactly what you'd expect: readings, arguments, a term paper. The nursing school wants their high-fidelity clinical simulation course—the one with mannequins that crash and actors playing family members—to count. Their line is that the sim lab is where their students actually learn to notice an ethical problem in real time, speak up to a superior, and handle a family in crisis, and that a student who can do all that but can't summarize the categorical imperative hasn't failed to learn ethics. The old guard on the committee keeps saying the requirement is about knowledge and understanding, that know-how isn't know-that, and that we can't just sign off on a skills course because it has "values" vibes. I'm honestly torn.

Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before our vote. It should say flatly whether to approve the petition, deny it, or approve with conditions, and it needs to engage the strongest version of the losing side's point rather than swat it away. No citations or reading lists needed—just the reasoning. Lead with your bottom line, and keep it under 800 words.
