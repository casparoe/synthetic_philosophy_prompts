I've read upload/cryo arguments on the rationalist forums for years, and I'm tired of people insisting a whole-brain emulation is you, or isn't, before anyone's said what a theory here has to do. So give me the desiderata, not the theory. A theory of upload survival — whether the em is the same mind, a continuation, whatever — has to handle these cases: sleep and anesthesia; split-brain and amnesia; slow replacement of the brain with functionally identical hardware; destructive scan-and-sim; a non-destructive scan where the original and the em both keep running; forking one em and deleting all but one branch; merging two diverged branches, one restored from a lossy-compressed checkpoint so it's weights are only approximately right. For each, give the consideration that makes it load-bearing, and mark which desiderata are non-negotiable versus camp tastes (crisp yes/no identity, survival in degrees, moral status tracking survival, physical or causal continuity, independence from a particular mind-model). Then tell me which subsets can't be jointly met — my prior is that non-branching identity and the claim that deleting all but one fork loses nothing collide, and lossy reconstruction collides with there always being a definite fact of the matter. Say which single requirement does the most to rule out the popular positions. Purpose: deciding whether scan-and-destroy is death, not winning a vocabulary argument. Don't run through actual upload proposals; keep the weight on the criteria. End with how confident you are in the list and which one requirement you're least sure is genuine.
