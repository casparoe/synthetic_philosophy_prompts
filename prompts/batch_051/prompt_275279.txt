Longtime rationalist-blog reader here. Proposal for the U.S.: no federal criminal statute can be enforced unless it has an official ‘notice statement’—a plain-language explanation of the prohibited conduct, written to a roughly eighth-grade reading level, at most 300 words, listing every element, required mental state, and available defense—and the statement must be revised within two years of any amendment. In any prosecution, a defendant may move to dismiss if the statement is missing, stale, or materially unclear; the court then asks whether a person of ordinary intelligence reading only the notice statement would know what the statute prohibits. Treat this as a mechanism, not as a policy position: walk through what it incentivizes in legislative drafting, agency rulemaking, plea bargaining, and litigation. Where does it get gamed first? Does it actually make criminal law more knowable, or does it relocate the vagueness fight into disputes over the statement itself? Also separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested in void-for-vagueness doctrine, and don’t present a contested view as the consensus.
