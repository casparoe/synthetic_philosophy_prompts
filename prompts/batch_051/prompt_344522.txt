Here is an argument I have been turning over, and I want you to tell me whether it succeeds, and if not, exactly where it fails. It purports to show that special relativity alone forces the block universe: every event—past, present, and future—is equally real, and the passage of time is a frame-dependent illusion.

Take event A: at noon on 1 June 2035, an astronomer at a telescope in Chile glances at her watch. Take event B, stipulated for the sake of argument: a generation ship is launched from a rocky planet in the Andromeda galaxy, an event spacelike separated from A.

(1) Special relativity says there is no frame-independent simultaneity for spatially separated events: a pair of events simultaneous in one inertial frame may be successive in another.

(2) Now imagine two astronomers, Alf and Bea, who pass each other beside the telescope at the moment of A, Alf walking toward Andromeda and Bea walking away from it at the same easy pace. Their simultaneity planes through A tilt in opposite directions, and over the roughly two and a half million light-years to Andromeda this tilt corresponds to several days. In Alf's frame, the launch B is simultaneous with A; in Bea's frame, B lies several days in the future and has not happened at noon.

(3) Whatever is simultaneous with a real event in a legitimate inertial frame is itself real at that time. If B lies on Alf's noon time-slice, then at noon B is as real as the astronomer's watch-reading; it is not "not yet real" in his frame.

(4) But reality cannot be frame-relative in that way. The launch cannot be both real at noon for Alf and not yet real at noon for Bea. It either has happened or it has not. Since B is real in at least one legitimate frame, it is real, full stop.

(5) The same reasoning applies to any event spacelike separated from a here-now: there is a frame in which it is simultaneous with the here-now, and by the reasoning above it must be real. So every such event is real.

(6) Events that are not spacelike separated from the here-now—say, an event deep in some distant person's future, timelike separated from us—can be reached by a chain. Pick an intermediate event D far enough to the side that D is spacelike separated from both the here-now and the target event. The here-now is real; by (5), D is real; and since D is spacelike separated from the target event, some frame has them simultaneous, so by the reasoning of (3) the target event is real too.

(7) Therefore every event is real. The distinction between past, present, and future is just a frame-dependent perspective on a block that already contains everything.

I half-suspect the argument is too quick, but I cannot decide whether the fault is in (3), in the chain in (6), or somewhere I have not seen. Is the argument valid? If it is, the block universe is a consequence of special relativity. If it is not, identify precisely which step fails, why it fails, and whether that step can be repaired without quietly giving up the conclusion. In particular: could someone consistently reject (3) while accepting every physical claim in (1) and (2)?
