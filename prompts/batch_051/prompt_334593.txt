I'm drafting a position paper on truth-seeking, tradition, and purity, and the line I've been working toward is that all three feel like final values but are really instrumental ones. Before I circulate the draft, I want an audit of the specific commitments below. I genuinely can't tell where the strain is, so please treat the set as the object of analysis: I need to know which pairs pull against each other, which only seem to, how severe each real tension is, and what the cheapest repair would cost.

Here is roughly where I've landed.

(1) Truth is valuable only instrumentally: a true belief matters because and only because it improves the decisions it feeds. Where two beliefs would guide action identically, there is nothing that makes the true one better to have.

(2) So a question whose answer is guaranteed never to enter anyone's practical deliberation is not worth spending resources on, however deep or elegant the answer would be. Reconstructing the inflectional system of an extinct dialect, on the supposition that nothing could ever come of it, would be a waste.

(3) I can explain why truth-seeking feels like more than this. The felt desire to know for its own sake is a useful adaptive drive: it makes us stockpile truths, most of which do eventually matter. But the origin of the feeling doesn't license treating its object as finally valuable.

(4) And yet I want to say that institutions should keep funding pure mathematics and ancient philology even in the worst-case scenario in which such fields could be shown never to produce any downstream benefit. I hold that as a judgment, not just as a feeling my theory hasn't caught up with.

(5) On tradition: inherited practices deserve a strong but defeatable presumption in their favor, solely because a practice that has survived may encode more information than any current practitioner can articulate. Tradition stores trial-and-error results in a form no individual has to understand.

(6) But a practice's merely being ours, or old, or continuous gives no further reason to keep it. Once I'm convinced a tradition encodes nothing and serves no present function, the fact that abandoning it would break continuity is by itself no argument against abandoning it.

(7) On purity: the vocabulary of the clean and the unclean — sullied, tainted, defiled — tracks no real normative property. It is a misfiring of pathogen-avoidance psychology, and no action is right or wrong in virtue of being clean or unclean as such.

(8) But I firmly judge that someone who quietly cooks and eats the body of the family dog after it has died of natural causes, granting that no one else is affected and nothing harmful results, has done something wrong and not merely unusual. I treat that verdict as tracking a real reason, not as an emotional spasm to be explained away.

(9) Methodologically, where I have a plausible genealogical story that a disposition to treat X as valuable was produced by a process that doesn't track X's actually being valuable, I default to treating the disposition as debunked unless X's value can be defended on independent grounds.

Please go through this set. Tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. Order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each specify what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would cost. End with the single revision that would do the most to make the whole set cohere.
