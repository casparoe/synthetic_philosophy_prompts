Here's a mechanism I've been kicking around, and I want you to actually work through how it plays out rather than whether it's a good idea in the abstract.

Say you buy roughly Brandom's inferentialism: the meaning of a claim is it's inferential role — what it commits you to and what would entitle you to it, not primarily what it refers to or what truth conditions it has. Fine. Now imagine an online discussion platform that tries to run that model literally as a public scorekeeping game.

Every user has a public scoreboard of commitments and entitlements. When you assert P, the system adds P to your commitment set and, via a community-maintained inference graph, automatically adds each immediate consequence of P too (you can set the number of hops). If you later deny anything in your commitment set, you lose 10 reputation and get a public contradiction tag until you withdraw one side. To be entitled to a claim you have to either derive it from premises you're already entitled to, or have someone vouch for it — and the voucher takes the same reputation hit if the claim later gets revoked. Anyone can challenge your entitlement by asking "what entitles you to that?"; you then have 24 hours to produce a derivation of no more than three inference steps from your entitled premises, and if you can't, your entitlement is publicly revoked and the claim is marked unsupported. The inference graph itself is editable by anyone: a proposed inference link between two claims goes live after, say, twenty upvotes and no open objections.

Walk me through what this does over a year or so. Who ends up with reputation and who leaves? Does the writing get safer, more hedged, more technical, or what? Where does it get gamed first — the graph, vouching, the 24-hour window, the automatic commitment expansion? And are any of these failure modes fixable without turning the platform into something that doesn't do inferential scorekeeping anymore? Also, does being on this platform make people better at actually tracking their commitments and entitlements, or just better at gaming the scoreboard?
