I've been in a modal logic reading group for a couple years, and we've been stuck for months on the Barcan formula: ◇∃xFx → ∃x◇Fx. One side says it's obviously valid: if it's possible that there's a talking donkey, then there's something that could have been a talking donkey — the possible donkey. The other side says it's obviously invalid: my parents could have had a third child, so it's possible that a third child exists, but there's no existing thing that could have been that child. Everyone agrees on the textbook semantics — we've checked which frames validate the formula and which countermodels falsify it — so the technical facts aren't in dispute. We still can't agree on whether the formula should be accepted as a principle of our logic. My own hunch is that the real fight underneath is about whether everything that exists exists necessarily, but I can't tell if thats a diagnosis or just a rephrasing of the same stalemate. What kind of disagreement is this, actually — verbal, formal, or substantive metaphysical? And given the diagnosis, what would count as progress?
