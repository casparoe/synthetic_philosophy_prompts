Write a short dialogue between two people arguing about how a community should treat its members who conspicuously succeed. One defends something like the American Dream ethos: we should celebrate and reward visible success, because ambition is contagious, excellence is a real good, and a culture that cuts down its winners ends up poorer and more timid. The other defends something like the Nordic Jantelagen and the Australian/New Zealand tall poppy syndrome: we should pull down and distrust conspicuous winners, not out of envy but because celebrating them quietly normalises inequality and teaches everyone else that an ordinary life is a failed one. Neither should be a strawman; each has to answer the other's strongest version of their point, with real back-and-forth rather than alternating speeches. At some point have them tussle over a concrete case — say, a local kid who starts a successful business and stays in town, hiring neighbours — and let the case force a concession from each. Aim for the dialogue to narrow the disagreement down to one stubborn residual difference.
