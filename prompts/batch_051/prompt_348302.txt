I am an undergraduate currently taking your Speech, Harm, and Platforms course, and I also serve as a volunteer moderator on a university discussion forum that permits anonymous posting. Every week we receive several posts that reproduce propaganda — a clipped recruitment video from an extremist group, a manipulated infographic about asylum seekers, a meme first circulated by a state-backed account — accompanied by some version of "I'm posting this so everyone can see how bad it is." At present we decide these cases by moderator intuition, and our decisions are inconsistent. The stakes are real: keeping such material up can amplify the very propaganda the forum aims to expose, while removing it can read as silencing criticism, and each decision sets a precedent for later cases. I would like a reusable procedure, grounded in what the course says about harm, counterspeech, and the difference between exposing propaganda and amplifying it, that a group of non-specialist moderators could run in roughly ten minutes per case, with removals reviewed by a volunteer committee each week.

Please write the procedure as an ordered sequence of questions or a checklist. For each step, state what counts as a sufficient answer to move on, where the procedure stops with a removal, and where it should hand the case to a human because the remaining question is a values judgment rather than something a rule can settle. The procedure should distinguish reproduction that is genuinely necessary for critique from reproduction that is pretextual, and should treat different ways of reproducing material — verbatim quotation, screenshot, link, paraphrase, contextualised embedding — as materially different acts rather than as the same act with different packaging. It should also say what the procedure cannot do: for example, cases in which the platform's own context is what determines whether a reproduction reads as critique or as propagation, or cases in which the predicted harm turns on empirical facts the moderator cannot establish in the time available.

Then run the procedure on three worked cases: one where it requires taking down a post whose author's critique is genuine; one where it requires leaving up a post the moderation team personally finds repellent; and one where you think the procedure misfires, with an explanation of which step goes wrong and how you would amend it. The first two are cases in which the procedure returns an answer the moderator would rather not give.

I would prefer a committed answer: pick one definite procedure, defend it, and indicate the trade-offs it makes, rather than laying out several candidate frameworks for me to choose between. Where the underlying course material is contested, say briefly which reading you are adopting and why the procedure turns on it. Please write the response in British English.
