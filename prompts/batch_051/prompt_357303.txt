Question: Are generations — Baby Boomers, Millennials, Generation Z, and so on — real kinds, or are they merely conventional ways of dividing a continuous age distribution? Discuss, distinguishing between natural kinds and social kinds where relevant.

Student's answer: I will defend the view that generations are real kinds, though I concede their boundaries are fuzzy. First, generation membership is anchored in objectively identifiable birth cohorts, so the categories are not arbitrary inventions. Second, members of a generation undergo shared formative experiences, such as economic depression and war for earlier cohorts or pervasive digital media for later ones, and these experiences produce measurable differences in values, political attitudes, and even cognitive habits. Where we find robust, statistically significant group differences of this sort, we are entitled to treat the grouping as a natural kind, just as we treat biological species as natural kinds even though their boundaries are also fuzzy and vary from case to case. The main alternative, that generations are socially constructed, is no real alternative at all, since social construction is just the claim that they are not real; but that claim cannot explain why generational labels track such stable empirical patterns. I therefore conclude that generations are natural kinds.
