Was up way to late last night reading about Newton's theology and one thing keeps bugging me. I'm a backend engineer, so I've got no philosophy background, but the shape of the question seems clear enough. Newton looked at the solar system—all the planets orbiting the Sun in the same direction and in nearly the same plane—and took that as evidence that the whole thing had to be arranged by a designing God, not left to chance. Then, decades after Newton died, Laplace and others argued the same pattern is exactly what you'd get from a spinning cloud of gas collapsing and flattening into a disk, so all the planets end up orbiting the same way for purely mechanical reasons, no designer required. Newton also wasn't a hands-off deist: he thought God stayed actively involved in keeping the world running, maybe even stepping in to correct the orbits over time. So if Newton had lived long enough to see the nebular hypothesis, what would his theological views have led him to say about it? Would he have abandoned the design argument, doubled down, or found some third option—and which of his commitments would be doing the real work?
