I teach an upper-level philosophy of probability seminar, 16 undergrads, one 80-minute session on Dutch book arguments. I want a game that makes them feel the force of the Dutch book before I ever say the name: if your degrees of belief are your fair betting prices, then any violation of the probability axioms means someone can offer you a package of bets that you would individually accept but that loses you money no matter what. No lecture up front — I want the sure loss to show up in what happens in the room, with play money. Design the activity from the ground up: exact rules, materials, a timeline with when I shut up and when I speak, a payoff scheme that doesn't use real money, roles (who posts prices, who gets to buy and sell against them), a quick way to reveal the outcome at the end, and a debrief that maps the game onto degrees-of-belief-as-prices, coherence, and why avoiding sure loss doesn't yet make you right about the world. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and one thing the activity can't reproduce about the Dutch book argument that I'll need to supply myself.
