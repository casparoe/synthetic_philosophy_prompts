Consider the following asymmetry in ordinary judgment. A person who leaves a church, resigns from a country club, quits a condominium board, or ends a marriage is normally described as having exercised a right of exit: we may regret the departure, but we do not ordinarily say that the institution's authority over her survives it or that she is a deserter. Yet the one association that no living person consented to join — the territorial state — is precisely the one whose members are not allowed unilateral exit. Emigration is permitted only if some other state admits you; renouncing citizenship is a bureaucratic procedure that may render a person stateless; and efforts to build private cities, charter cities, or seasteads are met with the claim that the old state's law still reaches the territory and the people on it, however self-governing they aim to be. On its face this is the reverse of what any principle of consent or freedom of association would predict.

Is there a principle that justifies treating exit from voluntary associations as legitimate while treating exit from the state as incoherent or criminal? If so, state it as strongly as possible and test it against hard cases, including secession, emigration, renunciation of citizenship by someone who remains on the territory, and a community that purchases land and governs itself without seceding.

Structure your answer in four named sections:

1. The asymmetry — restate, in your own terms, the pair of judgments whose tension you will examine (100–150 words).
2. Candidate justifications — the two or three strongest principles that could generate both judgments, stated charitably and without strawmen (250–350 words).
3. Testing the principles — apply each candidate to the hard cases above and identify where it holds, where it strains, and where it collapses (300–400 words).
4. Verdict — whether the asymmetry is justified, whether it should be narrowed and how, or whether it should be abandoned; if one side must give way, say which judgment you would revise (100–150 words).
