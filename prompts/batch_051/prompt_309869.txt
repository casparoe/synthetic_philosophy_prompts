So in my cognitive science class we've spent the last two weeks on the mental imagery debate, Kosslyn vs. Pylyshyn, and I can't shake the feeling that this thing hasn't moved in decades for a reason that isn't just "the data are hard." Everyone agrees on the basic findings: in mental rotation tasks reaction time goes up roughly linearly with the angle, in Kosslyn's map-scanning experiments people take longer to scan between two points on a memorized map when the points are farther apart, and imagery tends to light up early visual cortex. Kosslyn takes all this to show that imagery uses depictive, picture-like representations — that the brain really does lay down something like a spatial image and reads distances and angles off it. Pylyshyn says no, the behavioral results can be handled by propositional/descriptive representations plus tacit knowledge of what seeing the thing would be like, and that the scanning and rotation times are mostly demand characteristics — people know what the experimenter expects and simulate it. And he says the neuroimaging just shows imagery can drive visual areas, not that the representational format itself is pictorial.

What bugs me is that both sides have known the other's objections for ages and neither has budged. So my question is: what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it a substantive empirical one, a verbal one about what "picture-like" or "depictive" is even supposed to mean, or are they answering different questions — one about phenomenology and one about computational architecture? My hunch is it's partly verbal plus a deeper split about what an explanation of imagery has to do: Kosslyn wants a mechanism that preserves spatial properties, Pylyshyn wants an account of how a system could act as if it had a picture without literally having one. Diagnose the structure of the debate from what each side actually says, and then tell me wether anything would count as progress given your diagnosis — a better definition, a new kind of experiment, a formal result — or whether the whole question is just ill-posed and we should stop expecting it to get resolved.
