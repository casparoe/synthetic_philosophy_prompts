After one too many arguments with a colleague about the simplicity prior in a forecasting system I am building, I have reduced our disagreement to the following argument, which he calls the reference-machine problem. I want a decisive, committed assessment: if the argument fails, I need the exact numbered step and the strongest corrected version of it; if it is valid, I need to hear that too, with the consequences for anyone who treats Solomonoff induction as a principled implementation of Occam's razor.

(1) Take any finite list of candidate hypotheses and any ordering of them. There is a legitimate universal machine relative to which their Kolmogorov complexities occur in that order, with gaps as wide as one likes.

(2) Therefore two inquirers using different but equally legitimate universal machines can place the same two hypotheses in opposite simplicity order.

(3) If a simplicity ordering can be reversed merely by switching between equally legitimate universal machines, then the ordering records a property of the encoding rather than a property of the hypotheses themselves.

(4) Solomonoff induction and its associated Occam-style priors are defined only relative to a universal machine, and the invariance theorem pins the resulting complexities down only up to an additive constant, which cannot rule out the reversals in (2) over a finite list.

(5) Therefore Solomonoff induction does not formalize Occam's razor in any sense that constrains which hypotheses one should prefer; the preference for simpler hypotheses is simply built into the arbitrary choice of machine.

Which numbered step, if any, should be rejected? For steps (3) and (5) in particular, if you judge one false, give the strongest reading on which it is true and say whether the argument still goes through on that reading. If you think the argument is valid, then defend the conclusion squarely against the usual objection that Solomonoff predictors have machine-independent convergence guarantees. Do not hedge with the observation that simplicity is complicated: I am asking for a definite verdict on this line of reasoning.
