Im a novelist, and the same fight keeps breaking out in my historical-fiction group about how much you owe a real person once they're dead — someone writes a scene and someone else says you made that up, you've slandered a corpse. I want a 'which position do you actually hold?' quiz I can give the group. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case: putting words in a dead person's mouth, inventing a love affair or a shameful secret for them, flipping a documented fact because the scene needs it, a surviving child's objection, someone who died last year versus three centuries ago, a king versus the king's valet. Each question should have two or three answer options that pick out what the quiz-taker would actually say about the case, not a label they'd recognize, so no question should just ask whether you think you owe the dead accuracy. Then give a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the real positions in this debate. At least these have to show up as leaves: strict fidelity/no invention; nothing is owed because the dead can't be wronged; duties run only to living survivors; you may fill gaps but not contradict the record; public figures are fair game while private ones aren't; and posthumous interests that fade with time. Build the key from the logical space — the questions and answer combinations first, then the positions located inside it — and tell me which cells are empty and whether that's because they're incoherent or just unexplored. Flag any pair of positions your quiz genuinely can't separate.
