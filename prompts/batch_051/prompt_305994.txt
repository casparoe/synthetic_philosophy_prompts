I'm a PhD student in political theory, and in a few weeks I'm presenting a dissertation chapter at our law faculty's public-law workshop. The paper defends what I'm calling a revocability account of judicial review. The core claim is that the counter-majoritarian difficulty is best answered not by asking whether judges are democratic, but by asking what kind of judicial remedy keeps the legislative decision reversible. Roughly: courts are entitled to strike down a statute only when they can show, on the record, that the statute makes it materially harder for a future legislature to reopen the underlying question — for example, by entrenching itself, rigging the electoral process, or immunising the decision from later repeal. Where a statute merely violates a right judges care about but leaves the political process intact, the court should at most issue a non-binding declaration and leave the final call to the legislature. My audience is the usual mixed law-and-theory crowd. There are public-law people who basically accept political-constitutionalist criticisms of judicial review and think any strike-down power is a form of elitism; a couple of comparative lawyers who keep saying the Canadians, New Zealanders and British already settled this with various forms of weak review and dialogue; and a few American constitutional lawyers who think the problem I'm solving does'nt really exist because their system has worked fine for two centuries. Past experience says the Q&A will sound friendly and then get quite pointed.

I'd like a prep sheet, not a defence of the paper. What will each camp actually object to, phrased the way they'd phrase it, ordered by how likely and how damaging it is? Which objections have real force, and what should I concede up front? Where are my replies weakest? What is the one reply that has to land for the paper to survive? And what is the objection I should be most afraid of — the one I'm hoping nobody asks — with a usable answer if it comes anyway. I've got about twenty minutes for the talk and then a long question period, so I need replies I can actually deliver in two or three sentences rather than seminar digressions. And please write the whole thing in British English.
