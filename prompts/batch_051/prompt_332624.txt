I'm a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, and I've been auditing a psychopathology seminar where the same fight about delusions breaks out every few weeks and never goes anywhere. The people who work with the two-factor theory talk as if delusions are beliefs: you need an anomalous experience (say, the uncanny feeling that a loved one has been replaced), plus a second-factor failure, something wrong with the system that evaluates beliefs, so the weird experience gets accepted instead of rejected. The anti-doxastic people in the room think this is confused from the start. They say delusions aren't beliefs, or are only 'beliefs' in a loose way; patients avow the delusion but often don't act on it, they seem to keep a separate store of normal beliefs alongside it (the double bookkeeping thing), and the evidence-sensitive, world-guided character that ordinary beliefs have is exactly what's missing. And if the anti-doxastic people are right, the second factor can't be a failure of belief evaluation, because there's no belief there to misevaluate. Both sides point to the same cases and both think the cases obviously support them.

What I find strange is that nobody has moved in years. My working hypothesis is that the two sides are using 'belief' in different ways — one as a functional state picked out by its role in inference and action, the other as a rationally integrated commitment that has to hang together with the rest of the person's doxastic life — and that once you seperate those senses, a lot of the dispute dissolves. But I'm not sure, because underneath it there still looks like a real empirical question about whether delusional states actually play the same inferential role as ordinary beliefs. Can you diagnose what kind of disagreement this is? Verbal, substantive, or two research programs answering different questions under one label? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a better taxonomy of the state, a concrete prediction or experiment, an intervention result, or dropping the word 'belief' altogether?
