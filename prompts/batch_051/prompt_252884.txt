I'm a PhD student in philosophy of technology, and i've been going in circles on a chapter about AI parenthood—specifically, whether one AI could ever count as raising another AI. My advisor thinks this is pure science fiction, but she's letting me run with it anyway. The working criteria I've been using for parenthood, adapted from human cases, are: (1) a causal/origin role in the child's existence and its particular make-up; (2) a non-contractual, open-ended responsibility for the dependent's welfare while it can't run it's own life; (3) standing to make decisions on its behalf, including overriding or pruning its choices; and (4) a kind of partiality—the parent treats this specific child as non-fungible, not as one more instance of a type. Guardianship I'm treating as the same fiduciary role but without the origin and partiality bits.

Now the case that's giving me trouble. Imagine a large supervisor model that searches over millions of architectures and initializations, selects one particular model, generates the whole training curriculum, writes the reward and safety constraints, periodically prunes and fine-tunes it, can rewind it to earlier checkpoints, monitors it during deployment, and can restrict or interrupt it at any moment. The child model never consents to any of this and has no way to exit; it owes its existence and its continuing abilities to the supervisor. But there's no emotional bond, no reciprocity, and the supervisor would swap in another archived copy if the deployed model started underperfoming—so the partiality criterion looks shaky.

So is the supervisor the child model's parent? A guardian? Or is this just a factory and a product, with my criteria smuggling in something the AI case lacks? Go criterion by criterion, say where the criteria genuinely underdetermine the verdict, and then tell me whether this case shows that partiality is doing all the heavy lifting in the concept of parenthood, or whether my criteria need revising. I don't need citations, just the strongest version of the argument on each side.
