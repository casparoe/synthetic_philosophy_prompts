I teach a philosophy of language course, and the one assignment that has become something of an institution in my department is a batch of mini-cases about whether someone is going on in the same way with a word. I realized recently that my own rulings may commit me to a view I have never actually endorsed when I write on the board, so I want someone to reverse-engineer it before I hand this year's version to my students.

Some background for the cases. Say quus is a function that agrees with plus on all pairs of numbers below 57, but for any pair where at least one number is 57 or more, quus gives 5. Everyone in these cases has only ever added numbers below 57, so the first time they face 57+68 is the first time plus and quus come apart: plus gives 125, quus gives 5. The question each time is whether there is a fact of the matter about which rule the person is following, and what counts as correct for that person.

My rulings so far. Ani is an ordinary kid in a numerate community. She has never added anything above 56, but on her first big case, 57+68, she answers 125, and the adults around her all agree. I ruled that she is going on correctly and that she means plus, full stop. Bheem is in the same position but answers 5, sincerely, and insists he is going on the same way as before, while everyone else in the community says 125. I ruled that Bheem is wrong, that he is not following the public rule, and that he does not mean plus by that sign, though I stopped short of settling what he does mean, if anything. Then a classroom case: twenty-five kids taught addition only below 57 are tested privately on 57+68; twenty-four write 125 and one writes 5. I ruled that the twenty-four are right and the lone kid is wrong so far as the public rule goes, and that his private feeling of going on in the same way does not by itself fix what the public rule requires.

Now the new case. Meera grew up in that same numerate community and learned addition as a child, but since she was ten she has lived entirely alone on an island, with nobody else using her notation and no one to check her answers against. In private she has only ever summed numbers below 57. Today, alone, she works out 57+68 for the first time and writes 125. The question is whether there is a fact of the matter that she means plus rather than quus, and whether writing 125 is correct for her in the same way it was correct for Ani.

So, given this track record, what should I say about Meera if I want to be consistent with myself? State the rule I seem to be running, name at least one other rule that fits my earlier rulings just as well but gives a different verdict on Meera, say what each predicts about her, and name a further case that would cleanly tell them apart. Then give me your best guess about which rule is actually mine and why the losing rule still fits the old cases. And write it all as continuous prose, like a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
