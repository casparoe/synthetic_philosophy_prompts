I have spent the past few years following the deflationism-versus-correspondence disputes that periodically break out on rationalist-adjacent forums, and I keep noticing something that seems underexploited as comic material: the deflationary theory of truth — the view that calling a statement true is just a device for asserting the statement, that truth is not a substantial property, and that the equivalence schema (a sentence saying that p is true is equivalent to p itself) exhausts what there is to say about truth — is surprisingly hard to state, even by its own proponents, without quietly reaching for the substantive notion it disavows. I would like a piece of satire built around exactly that tension.

Could you write roughly 700 words of deadpan bureaucratic prose in the form of an internal memo from the Office of Public Information of a small technocratic institution that has just adopted a rigorously deflationary policy of truth talk as an official communications standard. The memo should instruct staff how to handle truth going forward: no correspondence with facts, no auditing statements against reality, with each sentence p to be handled by reference to p itself. The author should be a sincere and competent deflationist who never simply contradicts himself into correspondence theory; the sustained irony should come from the places where the view strains — blind ascriptions like everything the committee said was true, the need to generalize across indefinitely many statements, and the question of whether the policy itself is true, which the author must handle without invoking the very notion he has just retired. The target should be deflationism and its difficulty doing without the substantive concept it disavows, not the lazy caricature that people use the word true too much. A specialist should recognize that the author understands the position and is satirizing it fairly, while a reader who has never heard of deflationism should still find it legible and funny. Numbered provisions or sections would suit the form well.
