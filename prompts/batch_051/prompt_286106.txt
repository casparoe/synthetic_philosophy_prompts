I'm a policy PhD student in a seminar on interventionist accounts of causation, and I've been keeping a running list of how our instructor, Dr. Okonkwo, rules on whether a relationship deserves to be called causal. So far she has said: a low barometer reading does not cause a coming storm, even though it is the best available predictor; smoking causes lung cancer, and it doesn't matter that a randomized smoking trial is ethically out of the question because the relevant manipulation can be hypothetical; a malaria-resistance allele causes lower malaria mortality, since a precisely targeted change to that allele would alter the outcome; the number of storks nesting in a region does not cause more human births there, because the correlation runs through ruralness and would disappear if the common cause were held fixed; and raising the school-leaving age causes higher later earnings, because the effect holds across several different background conditions and ways of implementing the policy.

I'm now writing a memo that has to predict how she will rule on a case we've never discussed: the claim that being born in the 1980–84 cohort rather than the 1970–74 cohort causes lower average wealth at age 40. The cohort gap in wealth is large and persists after adjusting for observed confounders, but there is no possible surgical intervention that changes a person's year of birth while holding the surrounding historical environment fixed. What should I predict, and what rule or rules is she implicitly running—a manipulation-based, invariance-based, or plain difference-making rule? Does her reliance on hypothetical interventions inherit the familiar circularity worry, since a targeted intervention is itself characterized in causal terms, and would that affect how confident I should be in the prediction? Which of her earlier rulings is the closest analog to the cohort case? Are there alternative rules that fit her five verdicts equally well, and what would each one say about the cohort? What further case would best tell the candidate rules apart? Please also separate what is settled within an interventionist framework from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this—especially how to treat non-manipulable historical or aggregate variables—and flag wherever you are taking one side of a contested issue rather than reporting a consensus.
