I fell down a rabbit hole about whether space itself is a real thing, or just a way of talking about how matter is arranged, and I ended up with an argument that looks like it proves way to much. I'm not in academia or anything, just someone who reads pop physics and watches a few videos, so go easy on me. But I think I've got the pieces right and I cant figure out which one to let go of.

The setup: in general relativity, spacetime is a smooth set of points (a manifold) with a metric field on top of it that says what the geometry is at each point. Substantivalism is the view that those points are real things, and the metric is a property each point has. Now suppose there's an open region — call it a "hole" — with no matter in it. Take a smooth mapping of the points to themselves that leaves every point outside the hole fixed but moves the points around inside. Shuffle the metric along with that mapping: whatever geometry sat at point p now sits at the image of p. Since the hole is empty, this leaves all the matter fields exactly as they were, and it leaves the metric outside the hole alone. General relativity's equations don't care which point gets which geometry, so the shuffled field is just as legit a solution as the original. Here's the argument I keep getting stuck on:

(1) If spacetime points are real, then "point p has this curvature" and "point q has this curvature" describe two genuinely different situations.
(2) The original field and the shuffled field are exactly like that: the same geometry, distributed over the points differently.
(3) So by (1) and (2), the original and the shuffled field are two distinct physical possibilities.
(4) These two possibilities agree on everything outside the hole, including an initial slice before the hole, and they agree on all the matter everywhere.
(5) If the whole initial state plus everything outside the hole still doesn't settle what happens inside the hole, then determinism is false.
(6) So determinism is false.

And (6) is crazy to me, not only because I'd like determinism to be true, but because the exact same reasoning seems to work for something like the electromagnetic field on a fixed flat spacetime: shift the field values around under the points and you get a different assignment, but nobody thinks Maxwell's equations are indeterministic because of that. So the argument seems to work to well, and it's bugging me. I later found out this has a name (the hole argument, which feels like a joke given how deep I've dug myself). Anyway: if I had to choose, I'd rather keep determinism than substantivalism, but I don't know whether giving up (1) really gets me out, or whether some other step is the sneaky one. Which step should I reject, and what do I have to swallow if I reject it?
