I am a PhD student in philosophy of language, and I am currently in a seminar whose instructor teaches the semantics of definite descriptions entirely through what she calls "case law." She does not state the rule she wants us to extract; she simply records her truth-value verdicts on particular sentences in fully specified contexts and expects us to work out the underlying account before she will rule on the hard cases. So far, her recorded verdicts are these. In a context where Sir Walter Scott is the unique author of Waverley, she judged "The author of Waverley is a novelist" true and "The author of Waverley is a physicist" false. In a context where exactly one guest is drinking Burgundy and that guest is a man and an auctioneer, she judged "The man drinking Burgundy is an auctioneer" true and "The man drinking Burgundy is a banker" false. In a context where exactly one woman is wearing a green dress and she is a philosopher, she judged "The woman in the green dress is a philosopher" true. She has so far refused to rule on any sentence whose definite description fails to denote, on the grounds that those cases would reveal the underlying rule too directly.

This week she gave us the new case. At a department reception, a fellow student points toward a person holding a flute and says, "The man drinking champagne is a visiting classicist." The person indicated is indeed a visiting classicist, but the flute contains Prosecco rather than champagne, and no one at the reception is drinking champagne. She has asked us to predict what her verdict will be—true, false, or neither true nor false—assuming she is consistent with her earlier rulings. I can see at least three ways of extending her record to this case, and they pull in different directions. I would appreciate a structured recommendation: identify the candidate rules that fit the precedents so far, say what each predicts about the champagne case, identify which of her earlier precedents is the closest analogue and whether it actually settles the matter, and, if the record underdetermines the verdict, say what principle she would be adopting with each possible ruling and what single further case would most cleanly separate the remaining candidates.
