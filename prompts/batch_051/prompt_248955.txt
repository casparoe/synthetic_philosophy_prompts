You are a philosopher working at the intersection of population ethics and welfare biology. Write a formal, argumentative essay on the following controversy: other things being equal, does habitat destruction — clearing a forest, draining a wetland, converting grassland to agriculture — tend to increase or decrease the total amount of wild animal suffering?

Begin with the following shared framework, unless you explicitly modify it: a hedonistic, sentientist, aggregative welfarism, according to which only the pleasures and pains of sentient individuals matter in themselves, and these can be summed across individuals. Within this framework, one side argues that because most wild animal lives contain far more suffering than enjoyment — predation, starvation, disease, parasitism, and early death — destroying habitat reduces the number of such lives and is therefore a net moral gain. The other side argues that habitat destruction does not simply eliminate miserable lives; it substitutes one set of lives for another, the substitute lives may be as bad or worse, and the underlying empirical claims are too uncertain to justify welcoming ecosystem damage.

Before drafting the essay, spend a preliminary brainstorming section working out the strongest steelmanned version of each position and identify the load-bearing empirical assumptions. In the essay itself, engage seriously with each of the following conceptual issues rather than merely mentioning them:

- Explain how the dispute turns on whether the representative wild animal life is, on average, net-positive or net-negative in welfare terms, and why total welfare can move in the opposite direction from total animal numbers.
- Discuss the replacement dynamic: land left after habitat loss is usually repopulated, often by smaller, fast-breeding, high-mortality species (ecologists sometimes call these r-selected species). Assess whether habitat destruction is better modeled as eliminating a population or as replacing one population with a different population whose welfare profile may be systematically worse, for example via higher juvenile mortality, shorter lives, or boom-and-bust population dynamics.
- Distinguish the idea that creating or preserving habitat is good because it brings many animals into existence from the classic logic-of-the-larder argument in animal ethics, and explain why that existence-based argument fails under a suffering-focused axiology when the lives it creates are on balance miserable.
- Address uncertainty: explain whether deep ignorance about the valence and intensity of wild animal experience, especially among invertebrates, should block a provisional conclusion, or whether expected-value reasoning still licenses one.
- Consider non-welfarist values: explain how someone who assigns intrinsic value to species, biodiversity, or healthy ecosystems would regard the welfarist framing as incomplete, and state clearly how a welfarist essay should respond to that challenge.

Your final essay should announce a clear thesis in its opening paragraph, present the strongest arguments on both sides, reply honestly to the strongest objections against your own position, and close with a brief statement of what empirical finding or conceptual shift would most change your conclusion. Do not include a bibliography or reading list, and do not cite sources; the discussion should stand on its own reasoning.
