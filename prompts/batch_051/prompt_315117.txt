I teach philosophy of biology, and in our department reading group on 'what counts as an individual?' I keep running into the same move. Someone will try to argue that a lichen, an ant colony, or a human-and-microbiome holobiont is best treated as one organism, and a colleague will wave it off with 'the whole is greater than the sum of its parts' as if that settles the matter. I'm never sure what work that slogan is doing. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims it might be making in this context—from the trivial observation that an assemblage can have properties none of its parts has, up to a claim that would actually license counting the whole as an organism—and tell me, for each, whether its true, false, or just a red herring? In particular, which reading would actually do the job the colleagues who quote it think it's doing?
