So I've been doing a lot of RLHF work at my job recently, and I also fell down a rabbit hole reading about acausal trade (and acausal threats) in decision theory. I keep coming back to a parallel between the two that I can't decide is actually deep or just a vibe.

Here's the thing. In RLHF you're never optimizing what you actually care about—you train a reward model on human preferences and then optimize that. But the reward model is just a proxy: it's correlated with what people actually want, but it isn't the target. The whole Goodhart thing is that once the proxy becomes the target, the agent starts optimizing the proxy instead of the underlying thing, so you get sycophancy, or answers that look good to a judge but are wrong in a way the judge won't catch. An acausal threat has a weirdly similar shape, the way I understand it. Some agent threatens you by committing to punish a copy of you (or a simulation, or whatever) unless you comply, even though your decision can't causally influence whether the threat actually gets carried out. The threat only works through the correlation between your decision and their prediction or their decision procedure, not through anything you cause. In both cases you get behavior that's driven by a stand-in that merely correlates with the real thing, not by the real thing itself.

My question: is this a real structural parallel or just two places where I happen to reach for the word "correlation"? If I write out the mapping (reward model score ↔ the predictor's model of you, optimizing the proxy ↔ giving in to the threat, the intended preference ↔ your actual causal situation), where does it hold up and where does it break? And is there anything from Goodhart's law or from acausal trade that should transfer to the other side but wouldn't actually survive the trip? I know just enough about both to feel like theres a real analogy here, but also enough to know it might be my pattern matching doing the work.
