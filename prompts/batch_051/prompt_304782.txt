Before I wade back into the Judy Benjamin problem, I want to get the criteria straight instead of going in circles. Setup: a soldier is dropped uniformly into a region divided into Red and Blue zones, each split into two subregions, and she gets a radio message saying that conditional on being in Red, she is in the first subregion with probabilty 3/4. Most people have the strong intuition that this should not change how likely she is to be in Red — she learned nothing about whether she is in Red, only what to believe if she is. Whether that intuition deserves to be a hard requirement or is just the prejudice the puzzle exposes is exactly the kind of thing I want sorted out. The obvious formal moves disagree: treating the message as a material conditional and conditioning on it zeroes out one subregion and lowers P(Red), and minimizing cross-entropy under the constraint also lowers P(Red) while presenting itself as the minimal revision.

What I want is the desiderata list for any theory of how to update on conditional information like this. Which cases it has to get right (ordinary propositional evidence as a special case, the Judy Benjamin message itself, several messages in sequence, messages that conflict with your prior conditional), which distinctions it cannot collapse (learning a conditional as a constraint on your posterior vs. learning that a proposition is true; a conditional probability vs. the probability of a conditional), and which formal properties are genuinely required and which are things people like only because they make the math nice. Flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most work killing off the obvious candidate rules. A brief scorecard is fine but the criteria are what I'm after.
