I'm a novelist—small-town literary stuff, so I take notes on arguments people actually have. A couple weeks ago at a town hall someone leaked an old cellphone recording of a city council candidate using a slur at a private fundraiser years back. A friend of mine, a retired reporter, told me the recording shouldn't settle anything anymore: now that deepfakes are easy to make, no video of a public figure can be a real tiebreaker, and we should stop leaning on recordings the way we used to. He wasnt saying the candidate was innocent—he was saying we've lost the ability to know one way or the other, and pretending a video still decides the question is just nostalgia. I pushed back that the candidate had every reason to cry fake, and that "could be faked" isn't the same as "probably is faked." He said I was holding onto an old habit because it made me feel safe. Something about his view bothers me, but I cant state it cleanly—and I definitely don't think video still works exactly like it used to, because it doesn't. So: is he right that once fakes are possible, a recording stops being a tiebreaker? If he's wrong, what should a recording count as now, and what, if anything, takes its place? And please say what would change your mind, or where the reasoning you're giving is most likely to break down—I'm going to bring this back to him and I want to know how shaky the answer is.
