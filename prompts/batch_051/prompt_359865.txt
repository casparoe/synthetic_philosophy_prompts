I've been trying to get clear on when a system's behavior really involves representing the world, as opposed to just being coupled to it, and I keep going back and forth. So I wrote down my honest gut calls on a bunch of cases, and I want to know if there's a single principle behind them, or whether I have to give one up.

- An outfielder catching a fly ball, who runs so that the ball stays at a constant angle in their field of view. I don't think they're representing the ball's trajectory. It looks like tight visual coupling with the world, not computation over an internal model.
- A honeybee that has flown to a food source, comes back, and does the waggle dance to tell the others its direction and distance. I'd say it is representing the location. The dance encodes the route, and the bee had to keep that information across the trip back.
- A rat that has wandered around a maze and later, when a familiar route is blocked, takes a shortcut it's never taken before. I'm pretty sure it's representing the layout of the maze, a cognitive map.
- An ant following a pheromone trail back to the nest. I don't think it's representing the path. It's just a chemical gradient it's tuned to follow; the trail is in the world, not in the ant.
- A person counting on their fingers to add 17 and 8. Here I want to say it is representational: the fingers stand in for the numbers, and the person is manipulating stand-ins rather than coupling directly to a world that has the answer sitting in it.
- A person navigating with a paper map. Obviously representational, but this one bothers me, because the picture is on paper, not in the head. So either representationalism has to count offloaded representations, or this is a different kind of thing and I shouldn't lump it with the rat.
- An octopus whose arm feel around inside a crevice and seems to handle a crab half on its own while the central brain only gets the highlights. I lean toward not representational for the fine manipulation: the arm is solving the problem through its own body dynamics, not through an internal plan of the arm's movements.

So those are mostly votes for a representational/non-representational split, but I can't state the rule. Is there a principle that gets all of them, or close? Which of my verdicts does it force me to overturn? And what would the principle say about a salior who steers by keeping the bow pointed at a landmark, or a language model doing arithmetic by pattern matching?
