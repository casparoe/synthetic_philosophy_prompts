Write an essay on the self-amendment problem in rule systems: a rule that governs its own amendment, like a constitutions amendment clause (or the game Nomic, where changing the rules is itself a legal move). Focus on the case where a rule says it can be amended by a simple majority, and a majority then uses it to change the rule so that it now requires unanimity, or so it cannot be amended at all. Weigh the view that this is a genuine paradox — the act of amendment seems to be validated by the very rule it destroys, like kicking away the ladder you are standing on — against the deflationary view that nothing odd is going on, because the old rule validates the act, and the new rule simply governs what happens after. Dont stop at presenting both sides. Pick one, defend it, and answer what you take to be the strongest objection to your position. In particular, try to engage this wrinkle: if the deflationary view is right, what exactly binds a later majority to the new, harder-to-amend rule, given that they never consented to it? And if the paradox is real, why do actual legal systems that amend their amendment clauses not collapse into incoherence? It might help to talk about secondary rules (rules about how rules are made) and whether a rule can bootstrap authority over itself. Aim for about 900 words; you can be casual in tone, but the argument should be precise.
