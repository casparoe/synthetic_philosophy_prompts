Over the past few years, in the course of my work as a journalist covering science and ideas, I have repeatedly come across a position that I find difficult to get a purchase on, and I want a clear account of what would have to be true for it to be wrong. The position is roughly this. On certain affective versions of panpsychism, sometimes grouped under labels like pan-sentience, the basic level of physical reality is not merely experiential in a thin or proto-conscious sense but valenced: wherever there is physical activity, there is something it is like, and part of what it is like includes a faint positive or negative cast. Since the overwhelming majority of physical processes are not organized into anything like a nervous system, the position draws the conclusion that suffering is a distributed feature of the cosmos rather than a late biological achievement, and that the largest item in any serious moral accounting of the universe is an enormous, diffuse, low-grade negative valence spread across non-biological matter. Some versions add that this reframes what it would mean to improve the world; a few add that it should change what we find worth studying in physics and cosmology themselves.

My question is narrow: what would count against this position? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want each major objection sorted into evidence that should genuinely move a reasonable holder of the view, evidence that would merely force a qualification, and criticism that a committed defender would dismiss as a category error, a misreading of what fundamental valence is supposed to be, or a demand for the wrong kind of evidence. What does the position predict or entail that its rivals do not, and does it actually stake itself on those implications, or has it been insulated against them? I would also like an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified in a way that makes it compatible with any possible empirical finding, and, if so, whether that insulation is a defect in a metaphysical-ethical position of this kind or a defensible feature of it. As a science journalist, I find myself most moved by pressure that can be stated in the language of physics or biology rather than purely a priori metaphysics; please flag where that preference may be doing the work in your ranking.
