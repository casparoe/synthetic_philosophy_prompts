Consider two factories that discharge the same quantity of toxic waste into the same river, knowing it will cause the same downstream harm. Factory A dumps because treating the waste would reduce an already healthy profit margin, and dumping is legal. Factory B dumps because every competitor dumps; if B alone treated its waste, its costs would rise and it would be undercut and driven out of business. B's managers would prefer a world in which everyone treats their waste, but no single firm can bring that world about by acting alone. Most people blame A more than B, and many are willing to say that B had no real choice. Is this asymmetry in blame justified? If you defend it, state the principle that makes competitive pressure an excuse or partial excuse without also excusing A, and test that principle against nearby cases: a firm that dumps because a corrupt inspector demands a bribe equal to the cost of treatment, and a firm that would go bankrupt not because of competitors but because of a fall in demand. If you reject the asymmetry, explain how B should be judged and why the market structure should not change the verdict. In doing so, address the general question of when, if ever, the structure of a multipolar trap or race to the bottom changes what we may fairly blame an individual participant for. Write the answer in American English.
