People keep "solving" logical omniscience by bolting impossible worlds or an awareness set onto standard epistemic logic, and then the next paper points out that the agent still ends up knowing every tautology or believing something they can't even formulate. Before I read another fix, I want the spec. What does an adequate theory of what a bounded, finite reasoner knows or explicitly believes have to get right? Cover at least: tautologies the agent has never entertained, consequences too costly to derive, concepts the agent doesn't possess, and inconsistent beliefs held in different compartments. Give me exactly six items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion. Five of them are desiderata — for each, state the requirement, the case that forces it, and whether it's a genuine non-negotiable or just one camp's taste. The sixth: which desideratum does the most to rule out the standard possible-worlds account, and which pair on the list, if any, cant be jointly satisfied.
