I've been going back and forth on this thing people say about AI, that humans are just a stepping stone and intelligent machines are the next stage. But it hit me that the word "succeed" is doing a lot of work in those conversations, and I don't actually know what it means for an AI to succeed us. I don't want the dictionary definition; I want a working definition that helps decide the cases where people normally just gesture at it. Here's the list:

- a new CEO takes over from the outgoing CEO at the same company
- a kid takes over her parents farm after they retire
- a species gets wiped out by an asteroid and then nothing fills the niche it had
- Homo sapiens spreads into a region where Neanderthals had been living, and the Neanderthals die out
- an AI that humans gradually hand everything over to until it basically runs the world and we just live with its decisions
- an AI that kills every human and then keeps expanding on its own
- a current ape species evolving, over millions of years, into something much smarter than us
- a student who surpasses her teacher and the teacher quits out of discouragement

I'm pretty sure the first two are real cases of succession and the asteroid one isn't. But the middle ones I could see argued either way. In particular I can't tell whether "successor" requires some kind of continuity, or the old thing being okay with it, or the new thing doing the same kind of job, or what. So: give me a definition of "successor" (or of one thing succeeding another) that gets my confident cases right, then run it on every remaining case and either stick with the verdicts it gives or revise the definition. Finally, on your definition, is it true or maybe just a metaphor to say AI is meant to succeed us? Or is the whole stepping-stone framing just sneaking a purpose into something that would otherwise be a plain extinction?
