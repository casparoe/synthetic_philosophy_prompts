I retired from a career in field biology and have spent the last few years reading philosophy of biology, and the question I keep circling is whether the handicap principle is a discovery about the world or a commitment about how to describe signaling.

A colleague in my reading group puts the thesis this way: whenever the interests of signaler and receiver diverge enough that the signaler would profit from being believed unconditionally, an honest signal must impose a cost that is differentially burdensome on low-quality signalers; costliness is what purchases credibility. Call this the Strong Handicap Thesis. My unease comes from the standard alternatives: the signal may be an unfakeable index of the quality it advertises (a roar constrained by body size), receivers may impose after-the-fact punishment on detected deception, or the parties' interests may overlap enough that deception rarely pays. In those cases costliness may be present but seems not to be doing the explanatory work, and I worry that the Strong Handicap Thesis survives by redescribing whatever limits deception as 'a cost.'

Please design the strongest empirical program you can to test the Strong Handicap Thesis. Choose a concrete signaling system about which enough is known to be useful, and specify what would need to be measured: the absolute and marginal fitness cost of the signal, how that cost scales with signaler quality, whether the cost is paid at production or levied socially afterward, how receivers attend to the signal, and what happens when the cost or the receiver's retaliatory options are manipulated. State in advance which patterns would favor the claim that differential costliness itself is stabilizing honesty, and which would favor an index, punishment, or shared-interest account. Then say exactly what a decisive positive result would and would not settle. In particular, would it license the general principle or only the existence of one handicapping system? Can a costly index produce the same observations? And is there a residue of the Strong Handicap Thesis — concerning, say, what counts as a cost or what honesty means — that no field data could reach in principle?
