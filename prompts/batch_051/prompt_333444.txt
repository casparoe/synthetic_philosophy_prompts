I'm one of the outside members of a hospital system's AI ethics review board, and we're being asked to sign off on a rule profile for an ED triage assistant. At shift change, when the department is slammed, this thing will propose who gets seen first, who can wait, and—in a mass casualty or ventilator shortage—who does not get a bed. The vendor offers two profiles. The utilitarian one maximizes expected lives saved and life-years, treats patients as interchangeable bearers of expected value, and will readily sacrifice one person to save five. The deontological one has hard side-constraints: it won't treat one patient solely as an instrument for saving others, and treats certain individual rights as trumps over aggregate benefit. Annoyingly, v1 supports no blend, so we have to pick one. The vendor also tell us the utilitarian profile is easier to audit because it optimizes a single number, while the deontological one runs off fourteen constraint rules, some of which conflict in edge cases.

Draft a short policy memo for the board recommending which profile to approve. Don't give me a survey—actually decide, and be fair to the losing side: state the strongest thing it has going and why it doesn't win. Structure it exactly like this: Summary (one para), The case for the utilitarian profile (two paras), The case for the deontological profile (two paras), My recommendation (one para). Write as a philosopher who works on AI ethics and normative theory. Think carefully: before you commit, brainstorm the hardest case for each side and build the memo around whatever the actual tie-breaker is.
