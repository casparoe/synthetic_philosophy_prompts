What is your actual credence in the hinge of history hypothesis, stated sharply as follows: the twenty-first century has at least an order of magnitude more expected influence on the long-run future (say, the next million years) than the average century over that same period? I do not want a bare number, and I do not want "it depends on how you define influence"; I want a number and the decomposition. In particular, distinguish: (a) the weight of a symmetry prior that any randomly selected century is unremarkable; (b) the empirical claim that accelerating technologies—artificial general intelligence, synthetic biology, possible space settlement—compress leverage into the present century rather than dispersing it across many future centuries; (c) the prior question of whether influence over the far future is concentrated in a small number of key decisions at all, as opposed to being continuously distributed across many small decisions; and (d) the possibility that the thesis survives only in a weakened form, in which we are pivotal relative to all past centuries but not relative to future ones. Assign a probability or range to each term, state how they combine into your final credence, and tell me which term you are least confident in and what concrete evidence would move it most.
