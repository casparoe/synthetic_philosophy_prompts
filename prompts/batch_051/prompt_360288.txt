A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about a proposal he had made to someone in another advocacy organization. The two of them hold opposed but good-faith views about a pair of policy measures: each judges the other’s priority to be not merely unimportant but mildly harmful, while judging his own priority to be of serious moral importance. My friend proposed that each of them agree to use his own time, money, and public endorsement to advance the other’s measure, provided the other does the same. His argument was systematic and, on its own terms, genuinely hard to fault. From my point of view, he said, the arrangement makes the world better by my own standards; from your point of view, it makes the world better by yours. Refusing such an exchange is therefore making the world worse by the very standards you purport to care about, and the only thing you purchase with that refusal is a feeling of purity. Moral disagreement, he argued, is just a situation in which two people assign different weights to the same outcomes, and once one sees that, trading concessions across those assignments is no more corrupt than an ordinary compromise between interests. Refusing to trade is simply a refusal to take the other person’s standpoint seriously as a source of value.

I could not get myself to accept the proposal, and I still cannot. It seems to require each party deliberately to do something he judges to be wrong, and to treat that wrong as purchasable. The fact that a comparable wrong is being purchased from the other side does not seem to cancel the wrong; it seems, if anything, to make the two people joint participants in corrupting one another. I have the strong intuition that a moral judgment is not a currency, and that a person who will advance what he regards as evil on the condition that someone else advances what she regards as evil has alienated his conscience, even if both parties calculate that the exchange yields a net gain by their respective lights. I do not simply want to be told which side wins. I want to know what the strongest case is for thinking this intuition should be given up, what the strongest case is for thinking my friend’s argument needs to be amended or rejected because of it, and whether there is a reading of respecting your own values on which both the exchange and the discomfort survive.
