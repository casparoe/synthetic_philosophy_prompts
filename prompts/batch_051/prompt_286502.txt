I'm in a reading group on panpsychism and we keep getting stuck the same way: someone raises the combination problem, someone else says "fusion" or "emergence" as if that answered it, and then we realise nobody has actually specified what an adequate combination story is supposed to get right. Before I read another proposal, I'd like the full specification first.

Take on the role of a professional philosopher of mind who works on consciousness. Write the desiderata that any adequate solution to the combination problem must meet — the phenomena it must explain or respect, the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it isn't allowed to collapse, and the empirical constraints it can't contradict. Please cover at least: ordinary synchronic unity (the fact that many different perceptual contents are bound into a single point of view at a moment); split-brain and dissociative cases where unity is partial or fragile; the fact that binding can break down (e.g., under anaesthesia or in some disorders); the felt difference between making one new subject and making a "group mind" whose parts are still there seperately; the difference between combining qualities (red-ish plus blue-ish giving purple-ish) and combining structures or contents; whether the micro-subjects may survive the combination or get absorbed; and the boundary problem — why this composite of micro-subjects yields one subject but a pile of sand or a nation presumably doesn't. For each requirement, give the consideration behind it, and mark which ones are non-negotiable and which are really just preferences of one camp (like "no brute emergence" or "subjects can't literally merge"). Then flag any subset that looks jointly unsatisfiable — e.g., if you require both that the parts remain subjects and that the whole is a new subject, is that already a contradiction? And say which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the common candidate views. No bibliography or reading list; I want the criteria themselves, not sources. Please start by brainstorming the requirement list before settling on it, think carefully about whether the list is secretly begging the question against panpsychism, and write your answer in British English. A table for the requirements is fine.
