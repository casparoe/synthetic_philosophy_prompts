i want you to draft the joint statement that comes out of a good-faith adversarial collaboration between an epicurean about death and a deprivationist about death. the epicurean holds that death is never bad for the person who dies, because there is no one there to undergo the bad; the deprivationist holds that death can be bad for you because of the goods it stops you from having. write it in british english, as continuous prose only — no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables. i know these documents are usually laid out with numbered agreed claims and a disagreements table, but write it as a single co-signed letter instead, flowing from one paragraph to the next.

the statement should start with what both sides can genuinely sign. aim for things like: the difference between dying and being dead; that being dead involves no experiences; that some ordinary fear of death is confused even by the deprivationist's lights; and whatever thin claims about harm needing a subject they can both accept without it becoming vacuous.

then give the residual disagreement, phrased so each side would own its own wording — the epicurean bit shouldn't read like a deprivationist charicature, and vice versa. then lay out the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. for each crux, give the case or argument both sides accept as bearing on it, and each side's prediction about how the case will go, written down in advance.

get into the weeds: the symmetry between prenatal and posthumous nonexistence, the timing problem about when death is bad, and whether something can be bad for you without being experienced (betrayal you never learn of, your reputation being trashed after you die). wherever part of their dispute is only verbal, say which part and why, but dont overclaim this — if its substantive, say so. commit to a specific draft: pick exact formulations and a definite list of cruxes rather than offering options. and dont invent quotations or append citations; the document itself is what im after. it should read like a statement two reasonable but still disagreeing parties would actually co-sign.
