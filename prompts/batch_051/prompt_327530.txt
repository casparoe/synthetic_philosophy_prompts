In my course on Ming-dynasty Confucian ethics, we have been studying Wang Yangming's doctrine of the unity of knowledge and action. As our instructor presents it, the doctrine denies that knowing and acting are separate stages in moral life: a person who can correctly state what filial conduct requires but does not behave filially does not yet know it in the morally relevant sense, because genuine knowledge is realized only in action, while action is itself already informed by knowledge. I am writing a paper that tries to work out what Wang Yangming would have made of a distinctly modern development: the compliance training regime, in which employees complete ethics modules, answer multiple-choice questions about company values or legal duties, and receive certificates attesting that they now understand the relevant standards, even though no one observes whether their conduct changes. If Wang Yangming were shown this way of certifying ethical understanding, what do you think his position would be? In particular, what would he say such training can and cannot accomplish, and what would the unity of knowledge and action imply about whether an organization can regard its ethical responsibilities as discharged once its members have been certified as trained?
