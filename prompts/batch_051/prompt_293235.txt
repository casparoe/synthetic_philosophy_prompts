I am a first-year graduate student in economics, and in a methodology course I wrote out an argument that appears to show that the hypothesis of utility maximization is unfalsifiable, and therefore that a central part of microeconomic theory is empty. I have not been able to find the bad step, and I would like a precise diagnosis rather than a reassuring slogan.

The argument is as follows. (1) Suppose an agent chooses a bundle x from a set B of affordable bundles. On the standard revealed-preference reading, that choice reveals that the agent weakly prefers x to every other bundle in B that was available. (2) A utility function is just a numerical representation of preferences: it assigns at least as high a number to x as to y exactly when x is weakly preferred to y. (3) Now take any single observed choice of x from B. One can define a preference ordering over the bundles in B that places x at least as high as everything else in B, and extend that ordering to bundles outside B in any convenient way. That ordering has a utility representation by construction, and relative to that utility function x maximizes utility over B. (4) So any single observed choice is consistent with utility maximization. By repeating this construction choice-by-choice, it seems any finite record of choices can be made consistent with utility maximization, provided one is willing to attach a different utility function, or a different preference ordering, to each observation. (5) Therefore no body of choice data could ever refute the claim that the agent maximizes utility: after any pattern of choices, the agent can be redescribed as a utility maximizer. (6) But a claim compatible with every possible body of evidence says nothing. Thus revealed-preference methods appear to have produced a theory with no content.

Yet the same approach is used in demand analysis and econometrics as though it imposes real restrictions on observable behavior, and I do not want to conclude that a large branch of the discipline is running on a tautology. Where does the argument go wrong? I want to know which step to reject, why that step looked innocent, and what a theorist must give up in order to recover genuine empirical content.
