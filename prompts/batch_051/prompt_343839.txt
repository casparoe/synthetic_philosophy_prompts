Over the past few months I've been trying to pin down when I'm entitled to count something as knowledge. The test I've settled on has four parts: I'll count something as knowledge only if it is true; only if I can offer grounds for it; only if those grounds do not already assume the very claim they are meant to support; and only if I know what kind of discovery would show it to be false.

Now consider the claim that the world has existed for more than five minutes. I find it as certain as anything I believe, and in ordinary life I would call it knowledge without a second thought. But when I run it through my own test, problems appear. Everything I could offer as grounds—memories, documents, rocks that look old, other people's testimony—is itself part of the world whose age is in question. And I cannot point to any discovery that would straightforwardly show the claim false, since any such discovery would itself be made inside the world I am using it to test.

So: is the claim that the world has existed for more than five minutes something I know, given my own criteria? Go through the four parts honestly. Where it fails, if it does, say whether that reflects a flaw in my criteria, a special status for this kind of claim, or some third possibility. And say whether this case shows that claims supported by evidence and claims taken for granted are separated by a real line, or only by a difference in how much I happen to doubt them.
