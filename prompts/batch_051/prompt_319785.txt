I sit on the advisory panel of a national science council that makes large, long-horizon funding decisions in foundational physics. We are reviewing a proposal from a consortium of theoretical physicists for a five-year program on the string landscape and the multiverse: they would categorize the low-energy vacua consistent with string theory, apply technical consistency constraints to argue that most of those vacua cannot be realized in a complete theory of quantum gravity, and combine the surviving landscape with eternal inflation and anthropic reasoning to make probabilistic claims about which kind of observable universe we should expect to inhabit. The proposal is unusually candid: it states that the program is unlikely to produce any laboratory or astrophysical signature within the grant period, perhaps not for decades. Its case for funding is that the appropriate standard here is not a unique empirical prediction but the convergence of largely independent theoretical constraints together with the absence of credible rival explanations, and that refusing to fund on grounds of testability would amount to giving up on the deepest problems in physics rather than protecting scientific rigor. The panel is split. One faction holds that a program that cannot be checked against nature, even in principle, has ceased to be science in any sense that justifies public money; the other holds that the demand for direct testability is an arbitrary line drawn by philosophical preference.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before our meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—fund, reject, or fund with conditions—and be written so the council could act on it directly. If you recommend conditional funding, specify the milestones or gates that would trigger continuation, expansion, or termination at the two-year review, and say who should assess them. The memo must engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side; the committee will not be moved by caricatures of falsificationism or of anything-goes post-empiricism, and I need the reasoning to be something the applicants could genuinely respond to whichever way the decision goes.
