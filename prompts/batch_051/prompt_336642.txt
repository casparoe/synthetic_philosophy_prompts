The essay I have in mind is about the distinction he draws between laws and trends. As I remember the argument, a law is universal and unconditional in the relevant respect, whereas a trend is a singular historical direction that holds because of a particular set of contingent conditions. My draft says this distinction is doing less work than it looks. I use three cases: the demographic transition, the long-run increase in energy use per person in industrial societies, and the spread of bureaucratic states. In each case Popper would probably say it's a trend rather than a law. My conclusion is that Popper blocks strong laws of history but leaves room for weak, conditional regularities that might fairly be called laws in a broader sense.
