Our congregation is split over whether a prophetic word delivered in a Zoom chat falls under Paul's instructions in 1 Corinthians 14, and people keep telling me the passage settles the matter without actually working through what it commands. The text (1 Corinthians 14:29–33, King James Version) reads:

"29 Let the prophets speak two or three, and let the other judge.
30 If any thing be revealed to another that sitteth by, let the first hold his peace.
31 For ye may all prophesy one by one, that all may learn, and all may be comforted.
32 And the spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets.
33 For God is not the author of confusion, but of peace, as in all churches of the saints."

Here is the case Paul plainly did not have in view. Our church is broadly continuationist and has met almost entirely online for the past year. A member who rarely unmutes has begun typing what she labels prophetic words in the meeting chat while the pastor is preaching. Others type short responses in the thread, such as "I discern this differently," and one elder now says the practice is out of order because the passage assumes prophets speak aloud, one by one, in the gathered assembly, with the others present to judge what was spoken. Another group replies that the point of the passage is order and mutual testing, not audio, so the same protocol should be extended to written words in the chat. I am less interested in who is right than in what the passage can actually be made to require. Does 1 Corinthians 14:29–33 apply to a typed, textual prophetic word in a videoconference chat? Work through the main readings: what the words themselves say, what the purpose of the passage within Paul's argument about ordered worship seems to be, what Paul would most plausibly have said if asked about written prophecy delivered outside the physical assembly, and what a reasonable reader today is entitled to take the passage to require. If the readings pull apart, which should govern our practice, and why? Does it matter that the message is text in a chat rather than speech delivered by someone standing in the assembly, or is that an accidental feature of the medium?
