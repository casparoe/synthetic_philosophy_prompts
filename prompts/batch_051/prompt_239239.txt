I am a retired physical scientist, and for the past few years I have been working my way through the philosophy of social kinds, particularly the view that money is an institutional fact: a dollar bill has no monetary physical property; it is money because participants collectively accept it as a medium of exchange and store of value, and because that acceptance carries status functions with associated deontic powers, rights and obligations that mere paper lacks. A friend in economic history recently pressed me on whether the end of the Bretton Woods system in 1971 is a difficult case for that view, and I would like a careful analysis rather than a verdict.

Here is the episode as I understand it, with the salient facts supplied so the analysis need not depend on further research. On August 15, 1971, the Nixon administration announced that the United States would suspend the convertibility of the dollar into gold at thirty-five dollars an ounce, closing the gold window and effectively ending the postwar system of fixed exchange rates in which the dollar had been redeemable by foreign central banks. The administration simultaneously imposed a ninety-day wage-price freeze and a temporary import surcharge. The dollar did not collapse; instead it became the anchor of a fiat-money system whose value seemed to rest on legal tender laws, the practice of paying taxes in dollars, and the continuing willingness of economic actors to accept it. I would like you to analyze this episode through the social-constructionist theory of money. First, map the theory onto the episode: who are the relevant parties whose acceptance constitutes the kind, what exactly changed on that date, and what should the theory lead one to expect about the transition. Second, say which features of the episode the theory explains and which it is silent about or misdescribes; pay particular attention to the role of state power, law, and path-dependence. Third, run the analysis in the other direction and say whether the episode is a confirmation of the theory, an anomaly for it, or a case it never anticipated, especially given that the status function was revised unilaterally and abruptly by a powerful state rather than through any visible shift in collective acceptance.
