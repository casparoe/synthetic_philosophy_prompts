Ok so I've been teaching a unit on Frankfurt-style counterexamples to the Principle of Alternative Possibilities (the idea that you're morally responsible for what you did only if you could have done otherwise), and I've backed myself into a distinction I can't stop using. The usual setup: there's an intervener who would have forced you to do X if you hadn't done it on your own; you do it on your own, the intervener never fires, so you couldn't have done otherwise, but you still seem responsible. Fine.

But I want to say that what the intervener removes and what responsibility actually needs are two different things, and the literature keeps running them together. Split 'could have done otherwise' into (a) a real alternative: there's a genuine other path the world would have allowed, and (b) a live fork: the agent actually registers the situation as one where more than one option is open, weighs them, and settles on one. In the Frankfurt cases the intervener removes (a) but leaves (b). Think of a driver at a fork in the road who doesn't know the right-hand road is barricaded; she hesitates, then goes left. She couldn't have gone right, but I still want to call her responsible for the left turn, and I want to say the only reason that works is the live fork. So replace PAP with the Principle of Live Forks: an agent is responsible for what she does only if she chose it from a menu she took to have at least two genuine entries. If that's right, determinism never threatened what actually matters for responsibility, because a determined agent can still stand at a felt fork and settle; determinism only kills the real alternatives, which were never doing the work.

This is probably already in the literature under some name (subjective alternatives? epistemic possibilities?), and the determinism bit is probably too quick. I'd like a candid assesment, not reassurance. In particular, I want to know whether the replacement principle is actually distinct from PAP, and which of my two claims (the replacement, and the determinism point) survives.
