So I just had a long argument with a friend about the designated survivor thing — the person hidden away in an undisclosed location during the State of the Union so a decapitation strike can't wipe out the whole line of succession. He's on the democratic-legitimacy side: he thinks it's an anti-democratic insurance policy where one person is quietly exempted from a risk everyone else in the elected leadership shares, and if the worst happens that person ends up running the country without voters ever having chosen them for that. I'm on the continuity-of-government side: I think those costs are real but the alternative — no surviving constitutional successor, or a scramble in which the military or some local official takes over — is worse. We got far enough to see that our actual split is less about wether continuity matters and more about how to weigh legitimation against continuity, and about whether a survivor's authority comes from the pre-existing succession order or from something the attack itself destroys.

I suspect one crux is whether the survivor being picked in advance by the very administration it's meant to continue, and the secrecy and light theater around it, changes the moral math once the public finds out.
