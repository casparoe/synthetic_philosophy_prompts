I'm a science reporter working on a piece about the first criminal prosecution of a driverless-car company after a pedestrian death, and I keep tripping over one clause in our state's criminal code. It was written in 1978 and reads: "Whoever, by an affirmative act, causes the death of another human being shall be punished as provided in this chapter. No person shall be punished for a death that results from the failure to act unless a legal duty to act existed at the time of the failure."

The facts: a driverless shuttle with no steering wheel and no remote operator was crossing town at night in the rain. A woman stepped into the road against the light. The shuttle's software detected her and flagged the risk, but a top-level rule the manufacturer had written into the code said that on wet roads the vehicle must not swerve and must not brake harder than 0.3g, because swerving or hard braking could roll the vehicle and injure the passengers. So the car held its line and braked gently, and it struck her at 22 mph and killed her.

The company argues this is an omission: the car failed to swerve, and nobody was under a legal duty to swerve, since the statute doesn't impose a general duty to rescue. The prosecutor argues its an affirmative act: the software was continuously issuing steering and throttle commands that executed a plan, and the death resulted from that execution, not from mere inaction. The last human "decision" was an engineer checking a box in a settings file eleven months before the crash.

I care less about the verdict than about whether the old doing/allowing line in homicide law can even be applied to a machine that was programmed in advance to do exactly this. Argue how the statute should apply through the text, then through the purpose of the act/omission distinction the clause is trying to draw, then through what the people who drafted it in 1978 would have said if asked, and then through what a reasonable reader of the statute today should take it to mean. Which approach should win, and does it matter that the only human choice happened months ahead of time with no particular victim in mind?
