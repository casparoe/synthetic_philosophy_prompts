Two friends of mine got into an argument about a news story I'd sent them: a nineteen-year-old conscript in an invading army, drafted against his will and assigned to drive supply trucks, is being tried alongside the officers who planned the invasion. (1) Ben said the trial is right: if you knowingly drive the trucks that keep an unjust war going, you're complicit, and being drafted doesn't erase that. (2) Maya said that can't be right, because by that logic every ordinary soldier on the aggressor's side is as guilty as the people who started the war, and defenders and aggressors end up on completely different moral footing — which is exactly what the moral equality of combatants is meant to rule out. (3) Ben answered that she's confusing legal equality with moral equality: giving both sides' soldiers the same trial rules doesn't mean killing for a unjust cause is morally the same as killing to defend your home. (4) Maya replied that expecting conscripts to judge whether their country's war is just is asking too much — they're propagandized, they lack information, and any rule that says refuse when you personally think the cause is unjust just lets fanatics rationalize anything; so treating ordinary soldiers alike is the only workable moral standard, not merely a legal one.
