I'm a novelist, so I spend a weird amount of time tracking what my characters believe and want, and I've noticed my own common sense about beliefs and desires is all over the place. I'd like to know wether there's one account of belief and desire that gets my verdicts, or close, and which verdicts I should give up.

Here are the cases, as honestly as I can report them. (1) In my novel, Adele believes her brother is alive even though I've written him dead; I think that's literally true within the fiction, not just a way of speaking. (2) My dog waits by the door at six because I usually come home at six, and I want to say he expects me but doesn't quite believe I'm coming. (3) When a chess engine plays a move a commentator would describe as wanting to open up the king's position, I don't think the engine literally wants to open up anything; those are our labels, not its states. (4) But when a friend says she isn't angry and then slams every cabinet in the kitchen, I'm fine saying she has a desire to punish someone, maybe without fully realizing it. (5) In an early draft a character believed her husband was having an affair, and in the final draft she dosen't; I don't think there's a hidden fact about what she "really" believed across drafts, though inside each draft it's fully real. (6) An AI that says it wants to be helpful and hopes I'm doing well—I can't bring myself to say it has beliefs or desires, even though I'd treat exactly that talk as evidence of belief in a person. (7) A person who, after a brain injury, insists their spouse has been replaced by an impostor while still acting warmly toward the spouse: I'd say they both believe it and don't, at different levels, and I don't know how to fit that with the chess engine case.

My half-baked hunch is that real beliefs need a point of view the system updates from, but I can't see why that wouldn't also cover the dog, and maybe even the chess engine. So: is there a principle, or a small set of candidate principles, behind all this? What does it say about the case I'm currently stuck on—an AI that honestly reports uncertainty, revises when contradicted, and writes plausible chains of reasoning, but has no sensory-motor history beyond text? And should I end up agreeing with the eliminativists that beliefs and desires are a bad theory we'll eventually ditch, or are they real but more various than I thought? I'd also like to know which of my seven verdicts would have to go under each candidate, and why. Please reply in British English.
