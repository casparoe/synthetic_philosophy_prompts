In my philosophy of science course we have been discussing the idea that unrealistic assumptions in a model are no defect as long as the model predicts well — roughly Friedman's F-twist. Suppose that in 1990 one empirical discipline of your choice (say macroeconomics, astrophysics, or population ecology) had adopted this as an actually enforced norm: journals and hiring committees stop treating the claim that a model's assumptions are unrealistic as a publishable criticism, and models are evaluated only on predictive track record. Predict, with visible step-by-step reasoning, how that discipline develops by 2025 compared with its actual history: which modeling practices expand, which decline, how practitioners handle overfitting and model drift, and whether long-run predictive performance improves or worsens. For each effect, give an explicit confidence level and say what would have to be true about the discipline for the opposite to happen.
