My daughter's elementary school started a "grit and resilience" program this fall, and because I'm on the parent advisory committee I have to help pick the examples that go in next term's packet. My sister — who coaches middle-school basketball and has opinions about everything — has been going through the draft list with me, and she sorts the cases in a way I cant quite reverse-engineer.

Her verdicts so far: a marathoner who finishes on a stress fracture, grit. A nurse who keeps picking up extra shifts through a rough stretch because she won't leave her patients in the lurch, grit. A kid who stays after practice to shoot free throws until dark, grit. A founder who pivots twice after failed startups and keeps going, grit. But: a gambler chasing losses, not grit. A woman who keeps going back to an abusive partner, not grit. A student who memorizes for an exam he thinks is pointless, not grit.

Next term's list has three new examples, and I want to predict how she'll sort them since there the ones I'll have to vote on. (a) A teenager who practices a video game six hours a day because he's trying to go pro. (b) A climate activist who keeps organizing after two arrests and a ballot measure that just failed badly. (c) A soldier who re-enlists out of family tradition even though he privately wants out.

What rule is she actually running? I can see at least two: something like "freely chosen persistence toward a long-term goal that matters to the person," versus "she only calls it grit when the goal is one she respects, and calls it stubbornness or worse when she doesn't." Which is it, what would each rule predict for the three new cases, and which new case would best settle it? If none of them settles it, give me one further case that would.
