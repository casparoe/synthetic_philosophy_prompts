So I'm taking an intro ethics course and last week the professor tried to put the whole is-ought issue to bed in about ninety seconds. I wrote down her gloss from my notes, and it came out like this: "Hume already settled this. You can't derive an ought from an is, so any argument that moves from facts about human nature, psychology, or evolved dispositions to a moral conclusion is invalid on its face. Moore later made the same point at the level of definitions: to define 'good' in terms of a natural property is to commit the naturalistic fallacy, because for any natural property N you can always ask 'but is N good?' and the question stays open. That's why saying 'this trait evolved, so it's good' is a fallacy—you're confusing what's natural with what's good. So the project of grounding ethics in human nature or evolution is dead on arrival."
