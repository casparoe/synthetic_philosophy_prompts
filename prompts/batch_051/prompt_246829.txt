I am a PhD student in moral psychology working on the structure of human value, and I have been asked to produce the draft of an adversarial-collaboration statement between a value pluralist and a value monist for a small workshop. The pluralist holds that human evaluative life includes several irreducibly distinct final goods or independent sources of normative reasons — such as welfare, fidelity, achievement, autonomy, and aesthetic appreciation — none of which can be fully analyzed in terms of the others. The monist holds that these are one underlying value in disguise, and that apparent plurality arises from context-sensitivity, instrumental relations, or a conflation of what is valuable with the conditions under which it is realized. Please draft the document itself, with these components: a numbered section of claims both sides can sign about the phenomena any adequate account must capture; a table of residual disagreements, with each entry worded so that each side would accept the statement of its own view; a section identifying the cruxes, meaning sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side; and, for each crux, the cases, arguments, or evidence both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Do not settle for the familiar standoff in which each side merely reports that the plurality or the unity seems obvious to it. Where part of the debate is verbal — for instance, a disagreement about how to use the word value rather than about the underlying facts — say which part and why, and aim for formulations that are neither vacuous nor slanted.
