I will be leading a one-off, ninety-minute session on the Sleeping Beauty problem for a reading group of epistemologists and statisticians, all of whom already know ordinary Bayesian updating; the goal I have set is for them to come away able to state what self-locating belief is and why Beauty's waking carries no ordinary evidence about the coin yet may still call for a revised credence. I am not asking for an explanation of the problem—plan how I should teach it. Specify the order in which the components must be introduced and what depends on what: the memory wipe and the doubled awakening under tails, the distinction between uncertainty about which world one is in and uncertainty about where one is within a world, the status of fair-coin symmetry as distinct from any assignment of probabilities to waking episodes, and only then the halfer/thirder split and the relation to betting rates. Settle what the single carrying example should be and say explicitly what it must have and lack; if the unmodified Beauty setup is not the best vehicle, say what modification makes it one, and why a naive modification such as simply increasing the number of awakenings does not by itself do the work. Map the misconceptions this audience will most plausibly form—the coin is fair so the credence is simply 1/2; Beauty knew she would wake, so there is no new information; the three waking episodes are symmetric, so 1/3 follows by indifference; the disagreement is only about whether to count flips or awakenings; the problem is underspecified and has no determinate answer—and indicate the exact point in the sequence where each should be preempted or answered. Give me one closing diagnostic question, built around a variant I can sketch on the board, that separates students who have genuinely grasped self-locating evidence from students who have merely memorized a position. Finally, tell me what to leave out at this level and how to announce the omission (I am inclined to set aside Dutch-book arguments, anthropic reasoning in cosmology, and anything involving fission or duplication).
