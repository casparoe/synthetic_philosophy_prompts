A close friend of mine runs a traditional karate dojo that has, for the past two years, hosted a monthly "mind dojo": senior students and a small group of judgment-and-decision-making researchers train calibration, pre-mortems, and debiasing drills alongside kata and sparring. She has asked me to write the ten-minute opening address for their first joint weekend workshop, "Forms of Practice," co-hosted with a university decision lab. The room will be roughly half lifelong martial artists—two of whom are openly skeptical that judgment can be trained the way a front kick can—and half decision scientists and rationality trainers, a few of whom privately suspect a kata is just slow-motion memorization. The speech has to land for both camps without flattering either, and it should be built around a single, non-obvious claim: that the hardest problem in both worlds is the same one, transfer from rehearsed form to live, high-stakes performance, and that a kata is best understood not as a drill that fails at transfer but as a deliberately incomplete form designed to force the practitioner to recognize which variation fits the situation in front of them. The idea should do the structural work—it should dictate which examples are allowed, how the speech opens, and where it ends—and the speech itself should model the very translation it is describing, making every specialist term from either side legible to the other on the spot.

Write the speech. You are a speechwriter with genuine training in both karate and judgment-and-decision-making research. Begin your response with the first line of the speech itself: no preface, no title, no framing; if the speech opens with a greeting, that greeting is part of the speech. Before drafting, think through what transfer actually requires on each side, where the kata analogy genuinely breaks down, and what a mixed room needs within the first three minutes; sketch the structure before you write. End not with citations or a reading list but with a concrete charge the participants can carry into the weekend.
