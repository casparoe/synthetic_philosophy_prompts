Here's my opening move. You keep falling back on a fair coin between the one and the five to show that you, not me, are the one who respects each person as an individual. But notice what the coin does to the lone person's chances: they are exactly fifty percent whether the other side holds one stranger or five. Add four more people, each with a life every bit as real as the first, and the recommended procedure doesn't change the lone person's odds at all. If you really thought each of the five had an independent claim on you, the lone person's chance would have to fall as the other side grows, from fifty-fifty against one down toward one-in-six against five. The fact that your favored procedure leaves their odds untouched looks like the opposite of taking those extra four people seriously. So skip the high theory for a second: why don't four additional lives make any difference, and isn't the fair coin just a way of announcing in advance that numbers won't count rather than an argument that they shouldn't?
