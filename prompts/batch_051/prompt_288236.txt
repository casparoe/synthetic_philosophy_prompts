Give a working definition of the term 'physical', suitable for stating what physicalism claims, and apply it to each of the following: an electron, a gravitational field, the temperature of a gas, the number seven, a rule of inference such as modus ponens, a pain, a quantum wavefunction, the metric structure of spacetime, an immaterial Cartesian soul (supposing one existed), and the property of being legal tender. Where your definition gives no verdict or a counterintuitive one, say whether you would revise the definition or defend the verdict; then state which of your verdicts is least secure. The definition should also escape Hempel's dilemma: it should not identify the physical with whatever current physics happens to posit, which would make physicalism hostage to future change, nor with whatever an ideal completed physics would posit, which threatens to make physicalism empty or trivially true. Please answer in British English.
