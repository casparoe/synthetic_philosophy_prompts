I've been trying to get clear on what makes two anomalies count as seperate instances of the same mechanism, rather than two different mechanisms that happen to produce similar-looking outputs. The position I keep gravitating toward is a kind of interventionist bottleneck view: two failures are "the same mechanism" just in case there's a point in the causal structure where the same intervention — a tweak, a knockout, an override — changes or removes both, and where messing with anything upstream doesn't get you effects unless it passes through that point. So sameness of mechanism means sameness of control point, not sameness of physical parts, not sameness of function, and definitely not similarity of symptoms. I like it because it gives a crisp answer in cases like two patients with almost identical hypoxia whose blood oxygen falls apart for different upstream reasons, or two copies of a model failing on the same input but for different internal reasons: you find where you'd have to cut to make the failure go away. A friend who does reliability engineering keeps saying this is obviously right until you try to use it on a system with redundancy, which is exactly the kind of system we care about.

So what I want is a map of what would count against it. What are the cases where my criterion says "different mechanism" but any competent observer would say same — or says "same mechanism" when they're clearly different? I want the serious objections sorted by how decisive they are, not a list. For instance: do redundant or multiply realizable mechanisms have no unique bottleneck? Can two genuinely distinct mechanisms be forced through the same chokepoint, so the criterion conflates them? Does feedback regulation ruin the idea of a clean cut point because intervening anywhere just gets compensated for? And is the view secretly just interventionism about causation wearing a mechanism-shaped hat — if so, does it say anything about mechanisms at all? Tell me which of these should actually move me, which ones a defender can absorb with a small patch, and whether the patches taken together have already insulated the view from any possible counterexample — and if they have, whether that's a reason to drop it or just what a criterion this general has to look like.
