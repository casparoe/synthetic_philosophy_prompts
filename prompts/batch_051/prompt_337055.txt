I tried to formalize what I was thinking and ended up with this argument. I half-suspect there's a trick in it, but I can't locate it:

(1) A regress is vicious when the infinite series fails to do something it needs to do in order to answer the question on the table.
(2) Something can fail to meet a demand only relative to a demand or standard, never on its own.
(3) So viciousness is relative to the question being asked, not a feature of the infinite series itself.
(4) For any infinite series, there's a question it fully answers (for example, what are the members of the series, in order?) and a question it doesn't fully answer if each item just points to the next (for example, what grounds the whole series?).
(5) So every infinite series is both benign and vicious, relative to different questions.
(6) Therefore the vicious/benign distinction doesn't divide up kinds of regresses; it divides up kinds of questions we happen to ask.
