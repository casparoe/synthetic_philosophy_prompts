I'm the clerk of the Standing Committee on Rules and Privileges of a small unicameral parliament — think a country of four million people, Westminster-ish but with a separately elected president — and I have to draft a memo by Thursday because we've hit a genuine constitutional gap. The president is elected by the assembly, and her five-year term expires at midnight this Friday. The assembly has failed three times to elect a successor: the chamber is deadlocked 50–50 after a by-election, one member is in a coma, and there's no tie-break procedure. The constitution says the president's powers pass to the prime minister if the president is unable to discharge the office, but it says nothing about what happens when the office simply becomes empty at the end of a term with no successor elected. Normally the constitutional court would sort this out, but three of its seven judges have recused themselves because they're closely related to the leading candidates, and the remaining four can't form a quorom before Friday. So there's no one left whose explicit job it is to say what the rules are.

The committee is split three ways. One camp says we apply the caretaker principle by analogy: the prime minister and cabinet continue, because in every other emergency the constitution prefers continuity and someone has to pay the army and the border guards. A second camp says that's just inventing a rule — the constitution is exhaustive about who may act as president, and if the list runs out then there is simply no lawful president at 12:01; the real question is which existing officer has the best claim under the closest text, and they think it's the speaker of the assembly, since the assembly is the only body with a fresh electoral mandate. A third camp says we're overthinking it: once the rules run out its not a legal question at all, just raw politics, and the committee should say that openly instead of dressing up a power grab as law.

I'm not asking you to research our actual constitution — it's invented, and the details above are the whole text that matters. Draft the memo I can take to the committee: who should govern after midnight Friday, under what theory, and what exact resolution we should pass. I'd especially like you to steelman the second camp, because I keep going back and forth. And one last thing: I've read enough confident law-and-politics blogging to know this territory is full of people presenting contested positions as settled. Please clearly separate the parts of your answer that are genuinely settled among people who actually work on this from the parts that are live disputes, and don't make a contested view sound like the consensus.
