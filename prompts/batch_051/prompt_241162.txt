I spent my working life in a lab, so when I started reading philosophy I kept wanting to turn the abstract questions into something closer to a controlled experiment. Heres one I cant shake. Norway has a prison, Halden, that gets described in ways that sound almost like a campus: staff are unarmed and trained more like social workers than guards, prisoners have private rooms with their own fridges and bathrooms, people cook and eat together, and the official idea is that the loss of liberty is itself the punishment, so the rest should look like ordinary life. In the United States we mostly run prisons on the opposite theory — conditions are harsh because, on some level, that harshness is supposed to be part of the point. I want to know what would actually happen if you changed the conditions and held everything else fixed.

So here's the counterfactual. Starting in 2026, the entire American prison system — federal and state — is rebuilt over ten years to something like Halden: unarmed staff, private rooms, shared kitchens, real jobs and school, freedom to move around during the day. Nothing else changes. Courts still send the same number and kind of people to prison; sentences and parole rules stay exactly as they are; the drug laws, the outside labor market, and the surrounding neighborhoods stay as they are. This is the natural experiment: change only the inside of the institution, not who goes in or for how long.

Now walk me through your best prediction for 2036, 2046, and 2056. Be concrete about violence inside the institutions, reoffending after release, staffing, contraband, the direction of per-prisoner costs and what drives them, and public support for the system. For each prediction, give me a confidence level, and tell me which fact about human behavior — not about Norway, and not about American politics — is doing the real work underneath it. And tell me where you're most uncertain: what would have to be true for the Halden approach to make things worse rather than better?

I don't want a balanced survey of possibilities. Commit. Tell me the future you'd actually bet on, and then show me the reasoning that could change your mind.
