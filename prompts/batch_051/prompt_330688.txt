A friend tried to convince me that periodical cicadas prove numbers exist, and I can't find the hole. His argument runs:

(1) The standard explanation for why periodical cicadas stay underground for 13 or 17 years is that 13 and 17 are prime, and a prime length means a brood rarely syncs up with the cycles of predators or competing broods. (2) So the fact that 13 and 17 are prime is doing real explanatory work in a scientific explanation. (3) Whatever does real explanatory work in a correct scientific explanation is true. (4) If '13 and 17 are prime' is true, then it is true that there are such things as 13 and 17. (5) Therefore, assuming the cicada explanation is correct, numbers exist.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? I suspect step (4) but I can't say exactly why, since a true statement about 13 and 17 sure seems to commit you to 13 and 17. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and what's the strongest reading of that step that would make it true?
