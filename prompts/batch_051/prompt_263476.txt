I've been trying to convince myself that supererogation is an unstable category, and I can't decide whether my argument cheats. Here it is:

(1) If doing X would make things morally better than doing Y, then, other things being equal, you have more reason to do X than Y.
(2) If you have more reason to do X than Y, then, other things being equal, you ought to do X rather than Y.
(3) A supererogatory act is by definition one that would make things morally better than doing only what duty requires, yet it is not required and you are permitted to skip it.
(4) If you ought to do X rather than Y, then you are not permitted to skip X in favor of Y.

So a supererogatory act would be both permitted-to-skip and not permitted-to-skip, i.e., required. The only way out I can see is denying (1), but that seems wild—how can an option be morally better and not give you more reason? Is the argument valid? If not, point to the exact step that fails, and tell me whether distinguishing different senses of "ought" saves the category or just relabels the problem.
