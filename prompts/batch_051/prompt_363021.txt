One thing that keeps tripping me up in debates about reduction and emergence is that people use 'emergent' for at least three different jobs: (i) we can't actually compute the higher-level behaviour from the micro-level in practice, (ii) we can compute it in principle but the micro-level description doesn't make the pattern salient, and (iii) the higher-level thing does causal work that isn't just the micro-level doing its thing. I want you to take the following cases and give each of them two ratings out of 10: one for how emergent it is in sense (ii), and one for how emergent it is in sense (iii). Ignore sense (i) except where you think its the only thing going on. The cases are: the temperature of a gas, the wetness of water, superconductivity, a motorway traffic jam, a stock-market crash, the shape of a starling flock, and the pain of a toothache. Where something gets a high score on (iii), tell me concretely what the extra causal work is supposed to be. Then give a single overall ranking from least to most emergent, and say where you'd draw the line for what deserves to be called 'emergent' at all, and whether any case on the list forces you to move that line.
