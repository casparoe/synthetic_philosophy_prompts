I chair the constitution committee of our residents' association, which is a grand title for three people who mostly arbitrate rows about the bin rota, but we have drifted into something genuinely knotty about rules that change rules. Our rulebook has one relevant provision, Rule 1: "The rules may be amended by a vote of at least two-thirds of the members present at a meeting, provided at least half the membership is present." We have had to rule on the four disputes below; my own verdicts are noted with each. Please take each one seperately, starting each time from the rulebook as it stands now.

1. A member moved to amend Rule 1 itself so that future amendments require a unanimous vote of all members. It got 80% in favour at a quorate meeting, which was not unanimous. I said valid: the old rule governs its own replacement, because otherwise the amending rule could never be changed at all.

2. A later quorate meeting passed, by 90% in favour, an entrenchment rule: "This rule and Rule 1 may not be amended or repealed." A year after that, a quorate meeting voted 90% to repeal that entrenchment. I said the entrenchment held and the repeal was invalid — that is what "may not be repealed" means. But I can see this sits awkwardly with case 1.

3. A meeting where only 40% of the membership attended passed a rule saying: "Attendance of 40% shall be sufficient for amending the rules, with effect from the start of the meeting at which this rule is passed." I said invalid. A rule cannot cure the defect in the very meeting that adopted it.

4. Last year, at a meeting that turned out not to be quorate, we amended the maintenance fee. Everyone has since paid the new fee, and repairs were booked on that basis. This year a properly quorate meeting passed, by 85%: "The maintenance fee amendment of last year is hereby validated." I said valid, and that the earlier amendment is now valid as well — which makes me wonder why case 3 is not the same thing.

My working idea is something like: a rule change is valid only if it was adopted by the procedure the rules laid down at the time, and it can alter the rules only for the future. But that clearly trips on at least two of the four.

Please tell me the principle that best captures these verdicts, or as many of them as can survive together. State it precisely, run it through each case, say honestly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate and whether you would overturn that one, and apply it to a case I have not listed: a quorate supermajority passes "The entire rulebook, including Rule 1 and this rule, is repealed." Keep the whole answer to 800 words maximum — I have to read it before Thursday's meeting.
