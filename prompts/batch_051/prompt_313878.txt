I sit on my city's traffic safety commission, and next week we vote on whether to recommend that the council approve a rezoning for a big-box store out by the highway. The developer's traffic study says the store would add roughly 3 million extra vehicle-miles a year; using the round-number fatality rate we usually work with, about one death per 100 million miles, that comes to 0.03 statistical deaths a year. For an individual shopper, one round trip adds something like 0.1 to 0.2 micromorts — a one-in-five-million to one-in-ten-million chance of dying — which is tiny next to the background risk everyone already accepts by getting out of bed. The same study says the store would save shoppers around 100,000 hours of travel time a year and bring jobs and sales-tax revenue to a part of town that needs them. Two commissioners think we're overthinking it: the per-person added risk is below background noise, so they say its irrational to reject the rezoning over 0.03 statistical deaths. The other side keeps saying you can't write off deaths just because you can't name the victims in advance, and that the people walking and biking on the new access road never chose to take on extra risk the way drivers do. I need to circulate a short decision memo before Tuesday. Please write it: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions. Make sure it honestly engages the strongest point from the losing side rather than just knocking it down. Format it as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no introduction, no conclusion, and lead with the bottom line.
