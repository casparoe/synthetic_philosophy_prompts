So I jusr spent two hours arguing with a friend about kidney markets and I still can't shake the asymmetry at the center of it. Someone gives a kidney to a stranger and we call them a hero, put them on the news, the whole thing. Someone sells a kidney and most people find it repugnant, and its banned in a lot of places. The seller takes the same surgical risk as the donor and comes out materially ahead, so the only difference is the money. My friend kept saying money changes the meaning of the act, but when I pushed him on why changed meaning makes it wrong rather than merely different he just said "dignity" and looked at his phone.

I want the strongest possible justification for treating paid donation as worse than unpaid donation, assuming the seller is informed, not coerced, and reasonably paid. Then test that justification on nearby cases: selling blood plasma, paid surrogacy, surge pricing on bottled water after a hurricane, and vote selling. If the principle handles those, tell me what it says about the kidney case. If it doesn't, say so and tell me whether the kidney asymmetry should be kept, reversed, or written off as an inconsistency we all just live with.
