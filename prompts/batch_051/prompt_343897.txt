Here's a real episode I want to run through a theory. In November 2018, the Chinese scientist He Jiankui announced that he'd used CRISPR to edit the CCR5 gene in human embryos, which led to the birth of twin girls, supposedly to make them resistant to HIV. He did this at a moment when the major scientific bodies had been calling for a moratorium on heritable genome editing, and he later got sentenced to prison in China over it.

The theory is the unilateralist's curse. The rough idea is that in a group where any member can act on their own, the decision doesn't get made by the group's average or consensus view; it gets made by whoever has the most extreme "this is worth doing" estimate. So a group full of people who think an act is a bad idea can still end up doing it if one person's outlier judgment comes out positive. The standard fix is some kind of strong presumption of conformity to the majority when the act is irreversible and potentially harmful.

Use the curse as a lense to analyze the He Jiankui case. Be precise about the mapping: who exactly is the "group," what are the options, what evidence was being pooled or not pooled, and where the curse's mechanism fits the facts and where it doesn't. For example, the curse usually assumes people are acting in good faith on their own estimates, but He concealed what he was doing and faked ethics approvals, so is this even the same failure, or a different one wearing the same clothes? Also say whether the episode is a good argument for the "principle of conformity" response, or whether it exposes a hole in it — like a rogue actor who keeps everything secret removes the common-knowledge conditions that the conformity fix seems to lean on. I'd also like you to take seriously a complication: the scientific consensus wasn't necessarily "never do germline editing in any form," it was more like "not yet, not like this, and definitely not with no transparency." Does the curse still apply when the majority view is conditional in that way?

And while you're at it, keep apart what's settled about the case and the theory from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and don't present a contested reading as if it were the consensus.
