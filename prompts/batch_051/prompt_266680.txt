A friend who works in political science summarized the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem for me as follows:

"Gibbard and Satterthwaite proved the impossibility of a fair and honest voting system. Extending Arrow's theorem from preference aggregation to individual incentives, they showed that whenever there are at least three candidates, any voting rule that is non-dictatorial and respects the Pareto principle must be vulnerable to strategic voting: there will always be situations where a voter can get a better outcome by submitting a ranking that misrepresents her true preferences. In other words, no such rule is strategy-proof. A corollary people often draw is that since every non-dictatorial election with more than two candidates can be gamed, the outcome of any such election is no more meaningful than whichever side lied most effectively."

Go through the summary sentence by sentence: which parts are accurate, which parts are subtly off, and what exactly the theorem actually requires.
