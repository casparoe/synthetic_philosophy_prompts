Write an essay on the following controversy.

Suppose the intrinsic value of a mental state depends on two dimensions: its degree of consciousness (roughly, how aware or lit up the state is) and its degree of happiness or positive valence (roughly, how good the state feels). For the sake of argument, treat each dimension as a bounded scalar: consciousness c runs from 0 (none) to 1 (maximal), and valence v runs from -1 (maximal suffering) to +1 (maximal bliss). Three candidate rules for combining them into an overall value are:

1. Additive: value is a weighted sum of the two dimensions, such as a·c + b·v with positive weights.
2. Multiplicative: value is the product of the two dimensions, such as c·v, as suggested by the slogan consciousness times happiness.
3. Lexical: one dimension has absolute priority over the other, so no quantity of the lower-priority dimension can outweigh a shortfall in the higher-priority one.

In the essay, weigh the considerations for and against each rule. Pay particular attention to whether consciousness is better understood as a scalar multiplier or as an enabling condition, that is, a gate that must be open before valence counts at all. Discuss what the rules imply about hard cases such as:

- a barely conscious state of intense bliss;
- a richly conscious state of mild discomfort;
- a fully conscious but affectively neutral state;
- a state whose consciousness gradually fades while its felt positivity stays the same.

Reach and justify a conclusion about which rule, or which hybrid (for example, a threshold requirement combined with additive or multiplicative combination above the threshold), is most defensible, and about whether consciousness times happiness is a good formula. Do not end on a non-committal note.

The essay should say how confident you are in its central claims and should single out the point you are least sure of.

After the essay, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "question": a one-sentence restatement of the controversy.
- "verdict": the rule or hybrid you defend.
- "core_argument": the decisive consideration for your verdict, in one or two sentences.
- "strongest_objection": the strongest objection to your verdict, in one or two sentences.
- "confidence": one of "low", "medium", or "high", followed by a one-sentence justification.
- "least_certain_point": the specific sub-claim in the essay you are least sure of.
