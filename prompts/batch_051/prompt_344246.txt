I'm writing a paper for my school's Philosophy and Public Policy Club on moral bioenhancement, specifically the view associated with Ingmar Persson and Julian Savulescu that our evolved moral psychology is poorly matched to the scale of modern societies and that biomedical help may eventually be needed to close the gap. I don't want to argue for or against that thesis in the abstract; I want to work through one concrete counterfactual and would like your help reasoning through it systematically.

Suppose that in 2033 a cheap, generic, non-addictive daily pill, call it Aequitas, becomes available as an affordable over-the-counter medication for adults. Its effects are modest and specific: subjects in trials give about a fifth more of a windfall to anonymous strangers in one-shot games and show a small, reliable reduction in impulsive retaliation, with no significant side effects and no effect on intelligence, energy, or social skills. No state makes it mandatory, but within five years several large employers and universities offer it free to members, a few insurance systems begin covering it, some professional licensing boards debate whether to ask about it, and parent groups split over whether high school students should have access. My question isn't whether this is a good world; it's what actually happens next.

Please reason forward twenty years, focusing on political behavior, crime and punishment, charitable giving, and family life. Pay particular attention to second-order effects: how employers and political campaigns would use the drug, whether a black market for stronger variants would emerge, and how non-users would be treated. For each prediction, state how confident you are and what would have to be true for it to fail. Organize the response into four sections in this order: "Bottom line" (about 10 percent of the response), "The first decade" (about 30 percent), "The second decade" (about 35 percent), and "Key assumptions and falsifiers" (about 25 percent).
