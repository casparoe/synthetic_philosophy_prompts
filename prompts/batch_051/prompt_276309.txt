You are a professional philosopher specializing in aesthetics and experimental philosophy. Take a few minutes to think through the problem carefully before answering, and begin by distinguishing the normative and psychological claims at issue.

In section 33 of the Critique of the Power of Judgment, Kant argues that a judgment of taste cannot be determined by grounds of proof: roughly, that another person’s approval of an object can never serve as a valid basis for my own judgment that it is beautiful. Taste is supposed to be autonomous, so I must judge through my own feeling rather than through authority, imitation, or testimony.

A critic might reply that Kant has smuggled a psychological assumption into what looks like an a priori claim — namely, that when people revise their aesthetic verdicts after hearing what others say, they are not really acquiring an aesthetic reason but merely yielding to social pressure or reproducing the judgments of others. On the opposing view, aesthetic testimony can function much like testimony about other first-personal states: hearing a trusted person’s reaction can help me notice aesthetic qualities I had missed or misweighted, and my revised judgment can be genuinely mine.

I want you to design the strongest empirically executable test for distinguishing these two readings of what happens when someone revises an aesthetic judgment after exposure to testimony. The design must be one that could actually be run with ordinary human participants, without specialized neuroscience. Specify exactly what condition or contrast does the discriminating work, what result would favor each reading, and what the test would not settle even in principle. In particular, say whether a result showing that aesthetic testimony reliably improves participants’ agreement with later independent judges would show that Kant was wrong about the autonomy of taste, or only that his normative thesis is unaffected by facts about how people happen to form their judgments. If no clean test is possible, describe the nearest feasible design and state precisely where the residual ambiguity lies.
