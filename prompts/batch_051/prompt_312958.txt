I'm not any kind of academic, but I've been going around and around with my brother for years about whether people have stable character traits or whether situations mostly run the show. I wrote down my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and they dont all hang together, so I want someone to tell me what principle, if any, gets most of them, and which of my verdicts I'd have to give up for the principle to be clean.

My coworker Dana pads her mileage by maybe thirty miles a month because our employer underpays. I don't think that tells you she's dishonest; it's a norm in our office, and she'd never steal from an actual person. But when my cousin downloaded movies for years without paying, I did think less of his honesty, even though that's also a sort of victimless-by-distance thing that everyone does. And when my ex padded a budget request at work and called it standard practice, I thought it revealed something about him, though I can't say why that's different from Dana.

On the trait-versus-situation side, my dad was scrupulously honest with money, the kind of person who drives back to return a wrong twenty, but he lied to us for a long time about how bad his drinking had gotten. I don't think the money honesty was fake; I think honesty just doesn't transfer across domains. But then I can't say what makes money and drinking different domains except that one is more shameful, and that feels like an excuse.

My neighbor screams at her kids almost every day but organizes the block food drive every year. I don't think the food drive cancels the screaming; the screaming says more about her character. But I also believe that when I snap at people because I haven't slept, I'm not really an irritable person, the lack of sleep is doing it. Those two feel inconsistent, and I'm prepared to drop one if a clean principle demands it.

So here's what I want. A principle, or a small set of candidate principles, that captures as many of these verdicts as possible, stated in plain terms. I want to know what the principle says about a case I keep going back and forth on: a friend who is very generous with money and completely unreliable about showing up to things, sometimes bailing an hour before they're supposed to be there. And I want an honest statement of which of my verdicts the principle can't accommodate, and whether the cleanest version means I should revise my read of the office-norm case or the sleep case or one of the others. The bigger question underneath is whether my verdicts, once they're cleaned up, point toward there being stable character traits or toward the situation doing most of the work.

Please write all of this as continuous prose, the way you'd write a letter or an email; no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
