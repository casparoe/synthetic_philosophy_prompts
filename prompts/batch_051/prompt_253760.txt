"Wittgenstein's complaint about Moore isn't that Moore's argument is invalid; it's that he misuses the word 'know.' Saying 'I know I have hands' outside any context where doubt is live isn't a false claim—it's a move that doesn't fit the language-game of knowing. The hinge propositions are the ones that stand fast for us, like I have hands or the earth existed long before I was born. They aren't ordinary empirical claims we go out and verify; they're the scaffolding that makes our practices of verifying and doubting possible in the first place. That's why they're groundless: no piece of evidence could make them more certain than they already are. In fact they're our most secure beliefs, the ones that have been confirmed by every experience we've ever had. Since doubt always needs grounds, and there just are no grounds for doubting a hinge, we're rationally required to accept our hinges. From that it follows they can't be doubted and also can't be revised, because revising a hinge would mean stepping outside the whole framework that makes revision intelligible. The riverbed metaphor makes the point nicely: the water of our beliefs flows and changes, but the banks stay fixed."
