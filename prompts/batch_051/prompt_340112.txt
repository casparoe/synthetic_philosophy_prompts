I've been going back and forth with a friend about hedonism, and we keep getting stuck on the same thing: pleasures look wildly unlike each other. The slow warmth of a hot bath, the click of finishing a hard crossword, the nasty little satisfaction of seeing someone you dislike fail, the relief when a migraine finally lets go — these dont seem to share a single ingredient the way samples of salt all share sodium chloride. If hedonism says pleasure is the one basic thing that makes lives go well, it would be nice to know what 'pleasure' picks out across all of them. So I've been collecting candidate answers to the question 'what do pleasures have in common?'. So far my list is: (1) a distinctive felt quality, a hedonic tone that every pleasant experience has however much it differs otherwise; (2) a desire-based answer, where an experience is pleasant just in case you want it to continue for its own sake while you're having it; (3) an attitude-based answer, where pleasantness isn't a special feeling but a way of being related to an experience — enjoying it, or taking pleasure in it.

I want a fourth candidate that is genuinely not on my list and not just a rewording or combination of these three. Then tell me honestly whether it's any good — what it captures that the first three miss, and where it breaks down. One thing I'm especially curious about: all three of my candidates still look for pleasure somewhere inside the person's mind (a feeling, a want, an attitude). I wonder if there's a plausible answer that doesn't, and whether it could still be called hedonism or would turn into something else.
