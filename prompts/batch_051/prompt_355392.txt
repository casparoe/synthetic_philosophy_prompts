Newton's bucket was supposed to show that the concavity of rotating water cannot be explained by the water's motion relative to the bucket or the fixed stars, so rotation must be relative to absolute space. General relativity looks as though it deepens this lesson: the metric is a dynamical field, spacetime has curvature in empty regions, and gravitational waves propagate through the vacuum. If spacetime can warp and ripple on its own, why do philosophers of physics still treat relationism as a live rival to substantivalism rather than as a view refuted by Newton and then by Einstein? What is the mistake in taking the bucket argument together with relativistic gravitational waves as settling the ontology of spacetime?
