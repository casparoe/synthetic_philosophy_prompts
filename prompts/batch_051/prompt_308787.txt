A laboratory safety code drafted before networked instruments existed contains the following clause: 'No person having charge of an experiment shall leave it in progress without arranging for a competent person to remain in attendance, and any person who does so shall be deemed by that omission to have caused any damage which afterwards results from the experiment.' A researcher now runs an overnight synthesis while monitoring it from home through a cloud service that streams instrument readouts and sends alarms to her phone. Early in the morning the provider silently ends her session for scheduled maintenance, the alarms stop arriving, a cooling line fails, and the reaction overheats and damages nearby equipment. No competent person was physically present. The committee must decide whether her absence was an omission that caused the damage. I am less interested in the verdict than in the reasoning: how do the plain words, the purpose of the clause, what its drafters would have said if asked about cloud monitoring, and the understanding of a reasonable reader each treat the case, and which of those approaches can genuinely make sense of an omission as a cause?
