My two coauthors have been fighting for months about the lying-versus-misleading distinction and I finally got them to agree to an adversarial collaboration, so write the statement theyd produce together. Maya keeps saying lying is morally worse in kind than misleading, even when the misleading person knowingly lets you walk away with the same false belief; Tom says any difference is just etiquette or risk, and once you hold fixed what the hearer ends up believing and how foreseeable it is, there's no moral difference left. Write it as one continuous piece of prose, no headings or tables, like a memo both of them would actually sign. It should cover: the claims about assertion, intention, and deception they both accept; the remaining disagreements phrased so neither would say their view is being caricatured (each should accept the wording of their own side); and the real cruxes — I suspect one is whether asserting gives the speaker a special claim to truthfulness that merely implying doesn't, and another is whether a hearer's reliance on implied content is less protected. For each crux, give a concrete pair of cases both would agree bears on it and record what each of them predicts about it. And keep it committed: where you have to choose between phrasing options or which twin cases to use, make the call and briefly defend it, dont just lay out alternatives.
