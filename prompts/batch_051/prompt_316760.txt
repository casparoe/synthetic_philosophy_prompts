He thinks that's empirically false — he points out that actual democracies mostly don't operate that way, so Dewey is dressing up an ideal as a description. My other friend says no, Dewey is offering a normative standard: democracy *should* be understood as organized experimental inquiry, and asking whether actual democracies already do this is like asking whether a doctor already follows every step of the scientific method before saying medicine should be evidence-based. She says: not unfalsifiable — you could look at whether treating a democracy more experimentally changes its problem-solving capacity, which is an empirical question. He says: but that just tests the effectiveness of a method, not whether the method *is* what democracy means. I can't tell who's winning.
