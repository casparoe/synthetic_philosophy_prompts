I've been trying to figure out what English spelling is actually a theory of. A phonemic alphabet like Finnish is pretty transparent about its commitments: one letter per sound, and if the pronunciation changes, the spelling should eventually catch up. English does the opposite in a bunch of places. It keeps sign and signature spelled with the same g even though the sound changes, it writes the past tense ending as -ed whether you actually say a t (walked), a d (played), or a full id (wanted), and it insists on keeping right, write, and rite apart even though no amount of listening will ever distinguish them. So the script seems to be tracking something other then sound — morphemes, word history, maybe just which word is which. But I can't tell whether that adds up to one coherent view of what a language is, or just the fossil of several different views that were never reconciled. What does English orthography presuppose about which units of the language are real and worth preserving? Does it presuppose one coherent theory, and if not, where does it come apart? And if we took the phonemic-alphabet view seriously — really believed it — what would have to change in how English is written and taught?
