Last night I spent a long evening arguing with a friend about how far I can rely on other people's word in aesthetic matters, and by the end I had a handful of settled verdicts but no principle that seemed to sort them. I would like to know whether some principle, or a small set of candidate principles, captures what I am doing.

Here are my verdicts.

(1) A friend whose taste I know well tells me I will like a novel. I am comfortable saying "I'll probably like it" and buying it before I read a page.
(2) The same friend tells me the novel is good. I will pass this along as "she says it's good" or "it's supposed to be good," but I will not flatly assert that it is good.
(3) Several reviewers I respect, writing in detail, all describe a film as excellent. I am willing to say the film is excellent without having seen it.
(4) The same reviewers, in the same reviews, also call the film beautiful. I discover that I am much more reluctant to assert that it is beautiful; I will only report that they describe it as beautiful.
(5) A food critic I trust says a restaurant is good. I will assert that the restaurant is good without having eaten there.
(6) A sommelier I trust far more than I trust that food critic says a wine has notes of blackcurrant and damp earth. I will not assert that the wine has those notes; I will only report the sommelier's claim, even though I would bet more on the sommelier being right than on the food critic being right.
(7) Once I taste the wine myself and notice those notes, I take my assertion that it has them to be better-grounded than my pre-tasting report of the sommelier's claim ever was, although the sommelier is plainly the better taster.
(8) I will assert that a painting is beautiful on the strength of a high-resolution reproduction of it, even though I have never seen the original, but I will not assert that it is beautiful from even the most vivid verbal description.

Is there a principle, or a small set of candidate principles, that captures these verdicts? I want the principle stated precisely, with the cases that force each distinction, and I want an honest account of which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate and which I would have to give up rather than patch with ad hoc clauses. Finally, tell me what the resulting view predicts about the case I keep going back and forth on: whether I may assert, without having read it, that a poem is good on the strength of two close friends whose recommendations I value.
