I work with four criteria for counting a writing system as a phonemic alphabet. It must be (i) segmental: its smallest contrastive units stand for single phonemes rather than for syllables, morphemes, or words; (ii) complete: vowels are written as regularly as consonants, each with its own sign, so that no part of the phoneme string is left for the reader to supply from context; (iii) biunique: every sign has exactly one phonemic value and every phoneme is written with exactly one sign, in all positions; and (iv) linear: the signs occur in the order of the phonemes they spell, so that the writing reproduces the temporal sequence of speech rather than replacing it with some other arrangement.

Now consider a constructed language with five vowel phonemes, /i, e, a, o, u/, no contrastive length, tone, or stress, and twelve consonants, in which every syllable is of the shape V or CV. A proposed script for it has twelve consonant letters, three vowel letters (for /e/, /a/, and /o/), and two bound vowel marks (a stroke at the upper-right edge of the preceding sign for /i/, and a stroke at the lower-right edge for /u/). Word-initial /i/ and /u/ are written by attaching the relevant stroke to a silent carrier; the two marks never occupy a slot on the main line. The consonant letters and the three vowel letters are written left to right in speech order, but the vowel marks are always attached to a host, and their vertical position is fixed by which vowel they spell rather than by anything about their place in sequence. No consonant letter carries an inherent vowel. Thus /pe/ is written as p followed by e, /ki/ as k with the upper stroke attached, /tu/ as t with the lower stroke attached, and word-initial /i/ as the upper stroke on the carrier. Every phoneme has exactly one written sign; it is just that two of the vowels are represented by attached marks rather than free-standing letters.

Does this system qualify as a phonemic alphabet under my criteria? Work through the four criteria one by one and say what each contributes to the overall verdict. I am especially unsure what to do with the letter/mark distinction. Criterion (ii) asks whether vowels have their own signs, but the signs for /i/ and /u/ are bound, so it is not obvious whether that requirement is met. Criterion (iv) asks for linear order, which the script obeys along the main line but only indirectly for the attached marks. Where a criterion seems to return the wrong verdict, say whether the fault lies in the criterion or in how I have applied it, and then propose the smallest revision to the criteria that would let them handle this case cleanly. Also say, briefly, what the script's treatment of /i/ and /u/ implicitly claims about their phonological status, and whether that claim should matter for the classification. Finish by stating how confident you are in the overall classification and singling out the point you are least sure of.
