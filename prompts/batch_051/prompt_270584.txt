I am a journalist who covers the science of language and ideas, and I am preparing an extended interview with a researcher who defends a position we can call geometric semantic realism: the claim that the geometry of well-trained word-embedding spaces is a substantive model of semantic structure, so that distances, neighborhoods, and especially analogy-recovering vector offsets such as the familiar king − man + woman ≈ queen pattern are genuine facts about meaning rather than incidental by-products of a training objective. I want questions that do more than have the guest restate this position. Please draft eight to ten interview questions, and after each question include a short follow-up that anticipates and presses on the reply the guest is most likely to give. The questions should cover at least the following: (1) what the success of analogy arithmetic actually establishes, (2) the polysemy objection and the shift to contextualized rather than static embeddings, (3) which geometrical facts survive under changes of the space's basis or coordinate system, (4) the tension between the flat geometry that supports analogy arithmetic and the non-Euclidean geometry that fits hierarchical concepts, (5) what coordinates could possibly encode for inferential vocabulary such as logical connectives and modals, (6) the deflationary worry that the space reflects co-occurrence statistics rather than meaning, (7) the status of putatively interpretable directions such as those recovered for gender or tense, (8) whether the geometry yields new, testable predictions about semantic phenomena before they are measured, and (9) what it means for the thesis that the space is trained only to predict words in context. I am not interested in the guest's biography or route into the field, and I do not want questions that call for citations or a reading list.
