I'm on the committee of a small village land trust in Cumbria. We own a few acres that used to be common grazing land, and theres now a solar company that wants to lease it from us and pay a yearly rent. The committee is split. Some want to use the rent to keep the village hall open and reduce council tax; others, including my neighbour Dave, say the money should be paid out equally to every resident as a small unconditional dividend, no strings attached, because the land originally belonged to everyone. Dave keeps citing Thomas Paine's *Agrarian Justice*, and the bit he read out at our last meeting is this:

"Man did not make the earth, and, though he had a natural right to occupy it, he had no right to locate as his property in perpetuity any part of it; neither did the Creator of the earth open a land-office, from whence the first title-deeds should issue."

I can't stop thinking about it. I don't have any philosophy background, but I'm trying to work out whether Paine's argument actually supports Dave's idea. Our land isn't in a natural state anymore – it's fenced, drained, and has planning permission – so does the community's original common claim still generate a permanent right to the income, or does it only justify paying for the unimproved value of the land? And if we did go with the dividend, would it be fair to give it to everyone including people who own property elsewhere? I'd like your advice before the next meeting. Please answer in British English.
