I’ve been reading the paper where Davidson, McKinsey, and Suppes first develop the idea (I think it’s called Outlines of a Formal Theory of Value, or something like that), and they basically say that if someone has cyclic preferences—like preferring A to B, B to C, and C to A—then you could offer them a sequence of small trades and they’d end up back where they started but with less money. They don’t call it a money pump, but that’s the idea. The thing I noticed is that their setup seems to assume the person has all these preferences at a single time, and the trick works because the preferences are already cyclical before the trading starts. But a lot of the more recent arguments I’ve seen talk about people changing their preferences over time, like I prefer A over B now, but later I’ll prefer B over A and pay to switch back. I’m trying to argue in my essay that these are actually two different kinds of rationality problems being lumped together: the first is about the internal structure of your preferences at a moment, and the second is about how you should treat your future self when you know your preferences will shift.
