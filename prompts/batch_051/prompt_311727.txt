At a studio visit yesterday an older painter kept pulling out "good artists copy, great artists steal" after I said I was uneasy about the way a piece of mine was lifting visual motifs from a culture that isnt mine. His use of it was something like: all art has always been theft from predecessors, so objecting specifically to cross-cultural taking is just selective squeamishness. I dont buy that, but everytime I try to say why I realize the sentence could mean a dozen different things. Lay out the distinct precise claims that one-liner might stand for — about what is being copied versus stolen, about what makes an artist "great," about whether taking is legitimated by transformation, by power relations, or just by succeeding — and for each one say whether it's a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or basically false. Then tell me which reading he actually needs for the line to do the work he's using it to do, and whether any of the more defensible readings quietly support my side instead.
