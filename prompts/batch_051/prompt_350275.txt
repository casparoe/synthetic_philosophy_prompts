I'm compiling a list of the standard ways out of Arrow's impossibility theorem — the places where you give up one of the assumptions — and I want to know whether their's one I'm missing. So far I have:

(1) Restrict the domain: assume preferences are single-peaked (or value-restricted or whatever) so majority rule stops cycling.
(2) Drop weak Pareto: once you do, constant rules or status-quo-biased rules become admissible, though nobody much likes them.
(3) Weaken IIA: let the social comparison of a and b depend on where c sits in voters' rankings, like Borda does.
(4) Weaken non-dictatorship: just accept that one voter's ranking is the social ranking.
(5) Weaken the collective rationality requirement: only demand acyclicity, or a choice function rather than a full transitive ordering.
(6) Enrich the informational basis: allow cardinal and interpersonally comparable utility, so you can do utilitarianism or leximin instead of ordinal preference aggregation.

I'm fairly sure these six cover the usual moves, but I have a feeling there's a seventh that isn't just weakening one of the standard conditions. I want one that is genuinely different from all six — not a restatement, not a special case, and not a combination of items already on the list. Don't give me a menu of options; commit to the one additional way out you think is strongest and defend it. And then tell me honestly whether it's any good, or whether it just relocates the impossibility somewhere else.
