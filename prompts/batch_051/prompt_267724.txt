I've been running a little "rationality gym" for a group of grad students in my department for the past few months — mostly exercises on base rates, expected value, noticing motivated reasoning, that sort of thing. Last week a friend who does judo sat in, and afterwards he said he was worried we were doing katas without sparring. He laid out an argument I can't get out of my head:

(1) If a drill is going to improve my ability to reason well in real life, that improvement has to transfer from the drill to real decisions.
(2) Transfer happens only when the practice situation and the target situation have the same underlying structure.
(3) A rationality drill either gives you a cleaned-up problem — alternatives specified, probabilities supplied, stakes stated clearly — or it gives you a realistic life scenario. In the first case, its structure doesn't match real decision-making, where you have to figure out what the problem even is. In the second case, it's too bound up in one specific scenario to teach a general skill.
(4) So no rationality drill has the same underlying structure as real-life reasoning.
(5) Therefore practicing rationality drills can't make me generally more rational; it just makes me better at the drills.
(6) But the same reasoning would show that a boxer hitting pads can't get better at fighting, a medical student doing case scenarios can't get better at diagnosing real patients, and someone writing essays can't get better at writing anything except those exact essays. Since those obviously do improve the relevant capacities, something in (1)–(5) has to give.

I want to keep (1), or at least the idea that improving rationality means something more than getting good at a parlor trick. But I can't tell whether the judo friend is making a real point about rationality being different from other skills, or whether his argument is just too strong. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it force me to give up? Also, what would a version of his argument that didn't prove too much actually show about how to design rationality training?
