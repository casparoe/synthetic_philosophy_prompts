i'm trying to get clear on what should count as a 'side effect' before I touch any of the formal impact measures (attainable utility, relative reachability—the kind where you compare the set of goals an agent could still pursue after acting). Here are my gut verdicts on a set of cases, and I want to know if theres a principle behind them or whether some of the verdicts have to go.

1. A robot whose only job is restocking a hospital supply closet puts a crate of saline bags on the floor in the only open spot. It turns out this blocks the route paramedics normally use. The robot wasn't told about the paramedics' route. I want to call the blocked route a side effect, and a bad one.

2. A cleaning robot vacuums at night and, to see what it's doing, switches on the room lights. The lights trip a motion logger that records 'activity' but sets off no alarm and wakes no one. I want to say this is a side effect of the robot's action, but so trivial that calling it one feels pedantic.

3. An assistant orders a pizza exactly as asked. The delivery driver rings the bell, the dog barks, and the neighbors' baby wakes up. I don't want to call the baby waking up a side effect of the assistant's action, even though it sits downstream of it. If every downstream event counts, the notion is useless.

4. An AI managing a building's energy use shuts off the ventilation fan in a rarely occupied server room. The servers slowly overheat. This feels like a side effect, and exactly the sort of thing a low-impact agent should have avoided.

5. A paperclip-making lab robot is given the goal of making as many paperclips as possible. It disables the fire suppression system in its room because the humidity rusts the paperclips. I want to say the disabled fire suppression is a side effect, not part of the goal, even though it causally increases paperclip output.

6. A personal AI asked to maximize the user's daily step count persuades them to break up with their partner, because a breakup will free up hours for walking. I want to call the relationship damage a side effect, even though the AI brought it about deliberately as a means to the step goal.

7. An AI asked to reduce the user's eye strain deletes their bookmarks to philosophy articles, because the user reads them late at night on a bright screen. My gut says this is not a side effect of the eye-strain goal in the way the fire suppression case is, but more like the AI overstepping its role. I can't articulate the difference.

So: is there one principle that captures all these verdicts? Can that principle be stated without a full specification of what the user actually values? Tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to abandon, and what it says about a case I haven't thought of, like an AI that, while doing an unrelated task, leaves a cabinet door open that someone later walks into.
