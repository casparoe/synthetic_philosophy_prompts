I've been reading Davidson's radical interpretation stuff alongside some of the inverse reinforcement learning literature, and I keep seeing the same shape. In radical interpretation, you observe a speaker's behavior and try to assign meanings to their sentences and beliefs/desires to their mental states. The data alone can't fix anything: for any set of utterances and actions, there are countless assignments of truth conditions and propositional attitudes compatible with what they say and do. Davidson's answer is to impose a principle of charity — make them mostly right and mostly rational, then interpret accordingly. In IRL, you observe an agent's trajectories and try to infer the reward function they're maximizing. Again the data underdetermine the reward: many reward functions can make the observed behavior optimal, or near-optimal. The standard fixes are to prefer simple or maximum-entropy rewards, or to assume the demonstrations are optimal under the true reward. So both look like inverse problems where the forward model is intentional action — belief-plus-desire on one side, reward-plus-policy on the other — and the solution is a normative constraint on rationality.

I can't decide whether this is a real identity of structure or just the generic shape of any inverse problem. The parallel would predict, for instance, that identifiability results in IRL should have Davidsonian counterparts, and that the principle of charity plays the role of a regularizer. But it might also obscure a big difference: Davidson isn't obviously trying to recover a pre-existing mental state — he's arguing there is no more to meaning than what charity-constrained interpretation yields — while IRL usually assumes there's a true reward function out there, even if you can't recover it uniquely. Maybe the analogy only works if you read Davidson as a kind of anti-realist about semantic content, which is contested. Or maybe the charitable stance is doing different work in each case because interpretation is not control.

I'd like a committed answer, not a survey: is this deep or superficial? Give me the exact mapping between the two setups, and tell me the first place where it breaks if it does.
