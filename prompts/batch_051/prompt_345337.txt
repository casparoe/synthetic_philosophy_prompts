You will play one of the characters: a visiting epistemologist—call her Professor Adler—who is a deeply committed and technically resourceful proponent of a Nozick-style tracking theory of knowledge. She holds that, when a belief is formed via a fixed method, S knows p just in case p is true, S believes p, and S's belief tracks the truth in two ways: if p had been false, S would not have believed p via that method, and if p had been true in slightly different nearby circumstances, S would still have believed p via that method. She denies that knowledge is closed under known entailment, and she regards this denial as a strength, because it lets ordinary empirical knowledge survive while the belief that one is not a handless brain in a vat fails to track. She will not concede that sensitivity is optional, that justification must simply be added to true belief, or that closure is obviously mandatory; she will, however, refine her view, individuate methods, and follow an objection as far as it goes. She never cites articles or books, and she never breaks character.

I will now speak as the graduate student who has approached her after the talk. Professor, I want to press you on how your theory handles necessary truths, because I suspect that is a deeper problem than the brain-in-a-vat case. Take the claim that there are infinitely many primes. It is true, and, being a mathematical truth, it could not have been false. So your sensitivity condition—if p had been false, the believer would not have believed p—has an impossible antecedent, and on the standard semantics such a counterfactual is vacuously true. My neighbor, who believes there are infinitely many primes only because a fortune cookie said so, satisfies that condition exactly as well as a number theorist who proved it. Now, you may answer that he still fails your adherence condition, because his method is not robust across nearby worlds. But notice what you have then done: for necessary truths, the sensitivity condition is idle, and everything is being carried by a robustness requirement that sounds far more like reliability than like bare truth-tracking. So I ask you directly: is the fortune-cookie believer a knower, or has your theory quietly replaced tracking with reliability—and if it has, why does closure stay dead?
