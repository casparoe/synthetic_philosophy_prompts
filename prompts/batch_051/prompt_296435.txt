In Part Five of the Discourse on Method, Descartes gives a two-part test for recognizing whether something that looks and talks like a human is actually a machine. The first test is about language: a machine could be built to utter words, but it would never be able to string them together in new combinations to answer whatever is said to it. It would be stuck with a fixed set of pre-scripted responses, like a clever parrot. The second test is about generality: even if the machine could perform some actions as well as or better than a human, it would fail at other actions in ways that reveal it is acting from the arrangement of its parts rather than from understanding.

Now that LLMs seem to pass the language test at least superficially—they produce novel-looking, contextually relevant text without any inner life—what happens to Descartes's overall argument? I'm intrested in whether his criterion for another thinking thing is dead, repairable, or still fine. If the language test can be fooled, was it ever really testing what he thought it was testing? And does the failure of the language test push all the weight onto his second test, or is that one also compromised by modern machine learning systems that are surprisingly general but still lack understanding?
