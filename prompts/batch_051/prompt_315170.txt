I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about blackmail, so I wrote down how I react to some cases. I'm after the moral princple, not what the law says (I know the legal line is messy). If no one principle can capture all of these, tell me where the cracks are and which of my verdicts has to go.

1. "Pay me $10,000 or I'll tell your wife about your affair." Clearly blackmail, clearly wrong.

2. "Pay me the $10,000 you owe, or I'll file the lawsuit I've already drafted." Totally fine. Structurally, though, someone is threatening to make my life worse unless I hand over money. What exactly makes this different from case 1? I want the principle to say that precisely.

3. A reporter has photos of a city councilor kissing someone who isn't their spouse. The photos are true, legal to publish, and arguably newsworthy. The reporter tells the councilor the photos won't run if they pay $5,000. That also seems like blackmail to me, and wrong.

4. A coworker catches someone skimming the till and says "pay me $500 and I won't go to the manager." Wrong.

5. The same coworker says "put the money back and own up to the manager by Friday, or I'm going to the manager." I take this to be fine, although it's still a threat to cause trouble unless someone does what you say. Is it fine because the coworker isn't trying to get paid? Does the principle turn on profit, or on something else?

6. I'm writing a memoir, and I've already decided to include a story that will humiliate an old friend even though it's true. He asks me to cut it; I won't. I don't think I'm blackmailing him, and I don't think he has the right to stop me. But I struggle to say why this isn't like the reporter in case 3. The only difference seems to be that the reporter is willing to be bought off, and I'm not asking for money. But would it flip my verdict in case 6 if I admitted to myself that I'd drop the chapter for $20,000?

Also: if I say "I haven't decided whether to tell, and my silence is available for a price," does that count as blackmail even though there's no explicit threat?
