People still coin words out of Greek and Latin roots all the time—for precision, neutrality, or just to avoid sounding like everyday English—and there are supposed to be standards for doing it well. The way I hear it, a good classical coinage should: draw its roots from a single source language unless there's a damn good reason to mix; attach its affixes the way the source language actually would, so the word isn't garbled Greek or garbled Latin; mean something you could recover from the parts if you know the roots; and be motivated by a real gap in the lexicon rather than by wanting to sound technical. Now take monokini. It was formed by cutting bikini as if it were bi- plus -kini and sticking Greek mono- on the front. But kini is not a Greek or Latin morpheme—it's just the leftover chunk of the name Bikini Atoll, and the swimsuit word bikini has no classical pedigree either. And yet mono- is about as legitimately Greek as it gets, the word caught on, and the -kini bit has since been reused in words like tankini. Go through the criteria one by one and say whether monokini counts as a well-formed classical coinage. Then tell me whether the problem is the word or the criteria.
