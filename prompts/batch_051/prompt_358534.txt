Take this claim: a decent number of people who play one-shot games with no communication and no reputations at stake are genuinely superrational in Hofstadter's sense — they pick the plan that would work best if everyone else arrived at the same plan, rather than best-responding to what they expect others to do. In Platonia (20 people, a billion dollars iff exactly one person sends, no cooperation allowed) the superrational plan is for each player to send with probabilty 1/20; in the Luring Lottery that Scientific American actually ran (readers entered a number of tickets, the prize was $1M divided by total tickets, one ticket drawn at random), the superrational plan was for each entrant to roll an n-sided die and enter 1 only if they rolled a 1. How would you test wether actual subjects are doing that, as opposed to just not getting the game, wanting to be the sole winner, or liking risk?
