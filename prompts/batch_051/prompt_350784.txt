He likes the old birdwatching dig — you know the one, paraphrased, not the exact phrase. My intuition says this is too quick, but I don't have the vocabulary in my own field to pin down why. I think in terms of APIs, preconditions, invariants, integration tests, documentation, tech debt, leaky abstractions, type systems, code review, that kind of thing. That's where I live.

So here's what I want you to do. You're a professional philosopher who also really understands software engineering. Systematically restate the main arguments on both sides of the "is philosophy useful to science?" debate in my vocabulary. Do the anti-philosophy side and the philosopher-defenders too, as fairly as you can. Be careful not to cheat by assuming from the start that philosophy is "just documentation" or "just a type system" — the translation has to be honest, and the interesting part is where it fails.
