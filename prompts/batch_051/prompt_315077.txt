Could you draft the joint statement for an advesarial collaboration I'm facilitating between two people in my reading group, who've been talking past eachother for months? The question is whether "Moloch" / multipolar traps are the right lens for races to the bottom, or a seductive frame that mostly relabels ordinary conflicts of interest. The two camps, as I'd currently describe them:

- Trap realists: certain stable, bad social equilibria — arms races, clickbait ecosystems, safety corners getting cut, overfishing, status spirals — are real multipolar traps. Spelled out agent-by-agent, everyone is responding rationally to local incentives, no single agent can unilaterally switch to the better outcome without hurting themselves, and the resulting state is worse for nearly everyone involved. It persists even when most participants can name it. They lean on the Moloch label.
- Trap skeptics: the trap frame does real explanatory work but also smuggles a lot in. Most claimed traps, once you specify who the agents are, turn out to have identifiable winners who benefit from keeping things as they are; "nobody can unilaterally exit" often underestimates how much coordination capacity and cheap talk people actually have; and the model is usually too under-specified to distinguish a trap from an ordinary distributive conflict with weak institutions. They think Moloch talk lets the beneficiaries off the hook.

I'd like the document in three parts. First, a numbered agreed-claims section: a working definition of a multipolar trap that doesn't already settle the argument, a couple of agreed examples, a couple of agreed non-examples, and anything about evidence standards they both sign. Second, a disagreements table — each row states the issue in wording both accept, then each side's view in wording that side would accept for itself. Third, the cruxes: the subquestions where settling one would actually move at least one of them. For each crux, include a concrete case, intervention, or piece of evidence both agree bears on it, and each side's prediction recorded in advance.

I suspect one crux is whether the existence of an identifiable beneficiary disqualifies a situation from being a trap, and another is whether repeated interaction plus cheap talk lets people escape most claimed traps — but if you think the real cruxes are elsewhere, say so and build the table around those. Also say which part, if any, of this fight is just verbal, and why.

And don't hand me a menu of options — be committed. Write it the way a mediator who actually has a view about where the disagreement is crispest would: make the claims definite enough that you can describe what would falsify them, and record predictions each side would stand behind.
