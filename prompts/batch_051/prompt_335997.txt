I am preparing a short position paper on whether effort is valuable for its own sake, and in the course of writing I tried to set down what I take to be my considered commitments. I had assumed they formed a stable package, but a colleague with whom I was discussing a draft said she could not see how several of them could all be true at once, and I have since lost confidence that I can see the structure of my own view clearly. Before I present the paper, I would like a dispassionate consistency audit. My commitments, as best I can articulate them, are these. Effort has only instrumental value; it is good only when it leads to, improves, or partly constitutes something else of value. If two people reach the same outcome, the one who got there with less effort has not achieved something less valuable, and the one who had to struggle has not achieved something more valuable. A person who devotes herself to a project that is doomed to fail, and therefore produces nothing of value, is not merely pitiable; there is something genuinely admirable in the striving itself. Difficulty, frustration, and pain are in themselves bad, and can be worth accepting only for the sake of what they make possible, prevent, or reveal. We sometimes have a genuine reason, not merely a practical rule of thumb about hidden benefits, to choose the more effortful route to the very same end, even when the easier route would leave us with the same outcome, the same capabilities, and the same future character. And the only thing that can make a life go better or worse is the character of its experience; nothing a person does or suffers matters for her except through experience.

I am not asking you to defend any one of these claims or to tell me which views people typically hold. I want an audit of the set: which commitments pull against one another, which apparent conflicts dissolve once the terms are read carefully, and which tensions are severe enough that something probably has to give. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating every pair that could be made to look odd as if it were equally problematic, and for each genuine tension say what the least costly revision would be and what it would cost me. I would also be grateful if you would write the whole response in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way you would write an email or a letter, and in American English.
