I am a novelist doing background work for a near-future political novel, and I have been trying to make a fictional Global Commission on AI Alignment sound as though its philosophical disagreements are real rather than decorative. The commission is split between two delegations that have talked past each other for most of the story: the Aggregationists, who hold that an aligned system should serve everyone by combining individual values into one explicit ranking of possible policies and then choosing the best, and the Liberal Neutralists, who hold that any such single ranking inevitably enforces someone's contested conception of the good, and that the system should instead stay as neutral as possible among worldviews, facilitating people's own aims rather than optimizing over them.

I would like you to draft the closing document of a good-faith adversarial collaboration between the two delegations: the statement they could actually co-sign after being forced to make both their agreement and their residue of disagreement exact. Structure it in three parts. First, a numbered section of claims both delegations can sign, substantive enough that the agreement is not vacuous but not worded so strongly that either side would defect. Second, a statement of the residual disagreement in which each side's position is formulated in language its own advocates would accept, not a caricature. Third, a disagreements table built around cruxes: for each sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side, state the crux, give a concrete case or argument both sides would accept as bearing on it, and record each side's advance prediction about that case. I suspect some of the visible disagreement is only verbal, and I want those instances flagged as such. A few seed fault lines to work into the document: whether a procedure can really be neutral between worldviews or is just the designer's worldview enacted by other means; whether Pareto improvements that everyone prefers under a policy let the system avoid aggregating values at all; and whether the instruction "the AI must not make contested value judgments" is itself a contested value judgment rather than an escape from one. The novel's central test case, which both sides have agreed to put through the crux table, is an AI that allocates one scarce river among three communities who value it in incommensurable ways: one as sacred, one as an economic input, and one as an ecosystem.

For this task I would be grateful if you would adopt the role of a professional philosopher working on value theory, social choice, and the foundations of AI alignment. Please think through the formulations carefully—before writing the final document, sketch candidate phrasings and test them from each delegation's point of view, discarding any that one side would reject as slanted. The prose should be formal enough for a commission document but clear enough for a novelist to understand. At the very end, outside the document, add a short note saying how confident you are in the fairness and precision of the formulations, and identify the one point in the document you are least sure about. Please do not include citations, footnotes, or references to real historical authors; the text should read as though it was issued inside the novel's world.
