I am a PhD student in international legal theory working on how membership in a customs union maps onto territory and effective control. The constitutive treaty of the customs union I am analyzing contains this clause (union and states fictionalized):

"The Contracting Parties establish among themselves a customs union. The customs union shall comprise the territories of the Contracting Parties. Goods imported into the customs union from outside shall be subject to the common external tariff once, and shall thereafter be treated as being in free circulation throughout the customs union."

A case now tests a situation the drafters did not foresee. One member, Valderan, has leased the port enclave of Port Malo to a non-member state for ninety-nine years; Valderan retains formal sovereignty and the inhabitants remain Valderan citizens, but the lessee administers the port, levies its own much lower tariff, and controls fiscal and immigration matters there. Valderan argues the enclave is still territory of a Contracting Party, so it lies inside the customs union and goods discharged there are in free circulation. The other members reply that this makes the external boundary of the union where the common tariff is no longer enforced, and that recognizing the enclave as inside the union would open a duty-free channel into the rest of the union. Work out what the words of the clause, its purpose, and what the drafters would most plausibly have said if asked each imply for this case, and then state which interpretive approach should control and why. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Identify the point where your reasoning is least secure and say what argument or factual development would reverse your verdict.
