I have been working through Bongard problems—the visual puzzles in which six boxes on the left all instantiate one rule, six boxes on the right all instantiate its contrast, and the task is to induce the rule from the examples alone—and I have a distinction I keep using that I would like checked. I call a Bongard rule autarkic when it can be stated entirely in terms of properties and relations visibly present in the boxes themselves: number, size, curvature, closedness, convexity, relative position, orientation, and similar spatial attributes. I call a rule allochthonous when it must import a category from outside the page, such as "things one might find in a kitchen" or "objects one could sit on." My working theory is that the felt difficulty of a Bongard problem tracks how allochthonous its rule is, and, more strongly, that only autarkic rules allow a genuine induction of a concept from a few examples; for an allochthonous rule the six positive instances cannot fix the extension of the imported category without a prior taxonomy, so the puzzle is really testing whether the solver already shares the vocabulary. Is this distinction already in the literature under another name, as I suspect it must be? Is the difficulty claim true, and how should the line handle cases like "a shape one could draw without lifting the pen" or "mirror-symmetric about a vertical axis," which never leave the page yet seem to require a rule the boxes do not fully display? Please give me the three strongest objections to the sharp autarkic/allochthonous line and tell me which of them is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable.
