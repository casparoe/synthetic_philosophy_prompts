And here's the student's answer, typed up exactly:

Zorn's Lemma: Let (P,≤) be a partially ordered set. If every chain (that is, every totally ordered subset) of P has an upper bound in P, then P has a maximum element.

Proof. Take an arbitrary vector space V over a field k. Let L be the set of all linearly independent subsets of V, partially ordered by inclusion. We apply Zorn's Lemma. Let C be a chain in L. We need an upper bound for C in L. Put U = ⋃_{A∈C} A. Clearly U contains every A in C, so as soon as we know U ∈ L it is an upper bound. To see U is linearly independent, take a finite subset F = {v1,...,vn} of U. Each vi lies in some Ai ∈ C. Since C is a chain and there are only finitely many of these Ai, one of them, say A*, contains all the others; hence F ⊆ A*. Since A* is linearly independent, F is linearly independent. Thus U is linearly independent, so U ∈ L, and Zorn's Lemma gives a maximal element M of L.

It remains to show M spans V. Suppose not. Then there is some v ∈ V such that v ∉ M. But then M ∪ {v} is linearly independent, which contradicts the maximality of M in L. Hence M spans V, and since M ∈ L it is linearly independent, so M is a basis. ∎
