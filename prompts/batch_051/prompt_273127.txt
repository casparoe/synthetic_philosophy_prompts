I am currently taking a course in the philosophy of space and time, and I keep coming back to a tension I cannot resolve. On the one hand, I find myself strongly attracted to relationism: the view that space and time are nothing over and above the network of spatial and temporal relations among material bodies. It seems to me the only view that fully respects the principle that we should not multiply entities beyond need, and it avoids the epistemic embarrassment of positing a substance that underlies all motion and yet cannot be observed directly. Leibniz’s arguments have real force for me: if the entire material world were shifted three feet to the left, nothing in the relations among bodies would change, so a substantivalist seems forced to admit a distinction without an observable difference.

And yet I cannot shake the intuition at the heart of Newton’s bucket. If a bucket of water hangs by a rope, is twisted, and is then released, the water begins by remaining flat, then gradually climbs the sides of the bucket. The surface becomes concave exactly when the bucket and water are at rest relative to each other, and it is flat when they rotate relative to each other. There seems to be a fact about whether the water is rotating, a fact that is not captured by any relation between the water and the bucket. Newton says the water rotates with respect to absolute space. When I imagine a universe containing nothing but a single spinning bucket, my intuition is that it still makes sense to say it is spinning, even though there are no other bodies to relate it to. Mach’s reply, that the concavity is caused by the water’s relation to the distant fixed stars and that in an empty universe it would remain flat, strikes me as ingenious but also almost perverse: it makes local inertial effects depend on the distribution of matter across the universe, and I cannot see how that dependence would work mechanically.

I am not asking which side is right. I want to understand what it would take for the bucket intuition to be something I should give up, what it would take for relationism to be the thing that gets amended or abandoned, and whether there is a version on which both my parsimony and my conviction that rotation is not merely relative can be preserved without invoking an unobservable container. Walk me through the costs of each move.
