I am a novelist working on a book set in the months around a closely fought referendum, and in the course of research I have been reading about the Brexit vote, California ballot propositions, and Switzerland's recurring federal referendums. A pattern in the way these institutions operate and are described has unsettled me, and I want help articulating the philosophical picture behind it.

The same polity that routes ordinary legislation through elected representatives — on the grounds that policy is intricate, needs line-by-line amendment, and forces trade-offs among constituencies — will take its most consequential and technically forbidding decisions and hand them to the electorate as a single yes-or-no item that cannot be amended. After a short campaign, the result is announced; and even a 52–48 outcome is spoken of as "the will of the people" in a way that a narrow vote in a legislature is never spoken of as the will of the legislature. A legislative majority is provisional and revisable; a referendum majority is treated as a mandate that binds the losers and fixes the nation's course. Yet in some systems a No is also re-run, with modest amendments and warnings about consequences, until it becomes a Yes — which sits uneasily with the thought that the first No expressed something settled.

What theory of political judgment, collective agency, or popular sovereignty does this practice presuppose? Specifically, does it presuppose that a popular will exists prior to the vote and is elicited by it, or can it accommodate the view that the outcome is constructed by the wording, sequencing, and campaign? Does it presuppose that ordinary citizens can form competent judgments about complex single issues, and if so, what does it presuppose about how such judgment is formed? I would like the implicit theory reconstructed, with attention to where the practice strictly presupposes a commitment, where it merely fits one, and where it is philosophically underdetermined — and an account of what in referendum design would have to change if the presupposed view were replaced by a deliberative or proceduralist one.
