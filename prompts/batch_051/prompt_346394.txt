Background. Roughly, on Johan Huizinga's account, play is entered freely rather than under necessity; it is set apart from ordinary life within a bounded time and place; it is governed by rules and creates its own order; it is pursued for its own sake rather than as a means to material survival, profit, or efficiency; and it typically involves tension, uncertainty, contest, or make-believe. On Josef Pieper's account, leisure is a receptive stillness of soul rather than the mere absence of work; it is not recreation or amusement undertaken as a refreshment that serves future work; it characteristically appears in celebration, worship, and contemplative openness to reality; and it is opposed both to the restless craving for constant stimulation and to the attitude that everything must be justified by its usefulness.
