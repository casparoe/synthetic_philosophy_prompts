I spent a long evening arguing with a friend about whether rationality can be trained in something like the way a martial art is trained: through repeated, isolated, artificial drills (katas) that are meant to transfer to live performance. He conceded that such drills can be practiced, but insisted that real reasoning is too context-bound for any drill to transfer; at least one student in my session will likely reproduce his objection, so the plan must answer it rather than route around it.

Next week I am leading a fifty-minute session for a group of first-year graduate students on the martial-art analogy for rationality. Do not give me the explanation itself; plan how I should give it. Specifically: what idea must be in place before the analogy can do any work; the single concrete example that should carry the whole session, and what it must have and must lack; the misconceptions the students will predictably form—for instance, that drilled techniques replace judgment, or that any drill transfers automatically—and the point in the sequence where each should be headed off, including my friend's context-transfer objection; one final question that would show they have understood the transfer claim rather than merely found the metaphor attractive; and what you would explicitly set aside at this level. Keep the plan to at most 500 words.
