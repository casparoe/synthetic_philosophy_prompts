I'm writing up my views on individuation and identity for a metaphysics reading group and would like a consistency audit before I present. My commitments are these:

1. I favor a bundle theory of ordinary material objects: a thing is nothing over and above its properties, with no bare substratum or thin particular underlying them.
2. I think Max Black's two-sphere universe — a symmetrical world containing only two qualitatively identical iron spheres — is a genuine possibility, and that it contains two distinct spheres, not one.
3. I accept qualitative supervenience: the global distribution of purely qualitative properties fixes all the facts, including facts about how many objects there are.
4. I want to avoid primitive thisnesses (haecceities) in my ontology.
5. I have also been saying that the two spheres differ in their positions — one is here, the other is there — and that this positional difference is what makes them two objects.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, and which tensions are merely apparent and dissolve once the relevant distinctions are drawn? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and at what cost, and then tell me which single revision of the set would clean up the most.
