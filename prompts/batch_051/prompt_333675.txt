Take the Death in Damascus case in the following form. An agent must choose one of two cities, D and A. A predictor forms a signal about the agent's eventual choice; the signal matches that choice with probability p, where 1/2 < p < 1, and otherwise points to the other city. A lethal hazard is then placed in the signaled city, unobserved by the agent until after he chooses. The agent dies (payoff 0) iff he chooses the city with the hazard. Surviving in D is worth 10, and surviving in A is worth 1. In the familiar symmetric version of this puzzle with a perfect predictor, the stable plan mixes over the two cities, going to D with probability 1/2.

Now analyze the asymmetric, noisy version above. Let a be the probability that the agent chooses D in the stable plan, and let r be the probability that the hazard is in D. Express r as a function of a and p, state the indifference condition a mixed fixed point must satisfy, and solve for a when p = 99/100. Show that, for this payoff structure, a mixed fixed point exists only when p > 10/11, and that for 1/2 < p < 10/11 the agent simply chooses D.
