I keep noticing an asymmetry in how we react to bad luck. Two people end up equally unable to support themselves. One is a climber who chose a notoriously dangerous route, fell, and now needs surgery and months of income support. The other is a day trader who chose a highly leveraged bet, lost everything, and now needs rent money. The climber gets sympathy and state help; the day trader gets a lecture about personal responsibility. Both made a choice, both knew the risks, both are now in roughly the same financial hole. Is there a principled difference doing the work here — body versus money, the social value of the activity, how central risk is to the activity, the foreseeability of the loss — or is the asymmetry just an accident of which harms the welfare state happened to cover first? If you defend the asymmetry, test it against the case of a professional stunt performer and someone who puts their whole pension into one stock on a tip. I want the strongest principle you can find and an honest verdict on whether it survives.
