I serve on the community advisory board of a hospital's living-donor kidney program. For the past year we have been split over a state bill that would allow donors to receive a fixed payment on top of reimbursement for travel, lodging, and lost wages. The opponents do not rest their case mainly on medical risk, undue influence, or unequal access, though they worry about those too. Their central claim is that introducing payment would change what the act itself is: a gift would become a sale, and something important would be lost even if the policy produced more kidneys for people who need them. The other side replies that this is not a claim about the world at all, only a feeling expressed in metaphor; the only real questions, they say, are how many people receive kidneys, whether donors are harmed, and whether everyone consents without being deceived. The same disagreement has appeared in smaller forms, over whether paying people to hold a place in line at the clinic, or letting patients bid for an earlier appointment, would corrupt practices that currently run on waiting and need. I have been asked to determine whether this kind of corrosion claim can be investigated rather than endlessly asserted. Please design the strongest test you can of the claim that adding financial compensation to an activity currently performed as a gift—here, living kidney donation—changes the meaning or character of the act itself, over and above changing the number of donors, the price, and the downstream consequences. Be concrete about what would be measured and whom one would compare. State what pattern of results would support the corrosion view, what pattern would count against it, and what a mixed or null result would show. Then be exact about what even a highly informative result would leave unsettled: whether any test could show that the worry is about something real rather than a shared unease, and whether a test could ever by itself show that paying donors would be wrong, as opposed to merely costly in ways the proposal's advocates had not counted.
