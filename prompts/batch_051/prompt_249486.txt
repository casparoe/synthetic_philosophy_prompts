I keep coming back to the free-will theodicy for the fall of the angels, because it's the only version of that story that leaves God clean: he creates rational creatures good, with no prior defect, and gives them free will because a freely chosen love is worth more than an automatic one. Moral evil is not a thing God creates; it's a privation, a failure of the creature's will to hold to the good it was aimed at. On that picture Satan's first sin had no antecedent in ignorance, appetite, habit, trauma, or environment. He just... did it, and that's where the blame has to stop.

But I have an intuition I can't shake. A will that is created good, fully rational, perfectly clear-eyed about the good, not confused by ignorance, not pulled by passion, not worn down by habit, and not tricked by anyone doesnt look like the kind of thing that can choose the worse. Every human case of wrongdoing we can make sense of has a gap the wrong choice slips through—false belief, weakness, compulsion, a competing desire—and standard angelology deliberately seals all those gaps. That seems to make the first sin not just hard to explain but impossible. If a fully lucid will can't choose the worse, then either God built an angel that was in some way rigged to fall (so the evil is ultimately a design feature), or the story is incoherent.

Please give me the strongest version of each side, then walk me through what would justify each of the available moves: dropping the intuition as a bad induction from human psychology; amending the theory, say by giving the angels less clarity or granting that the first sin had some explanatory antecedent after all; or showing the two are compatible because free will is itself the needed gap. I don't want a survey, I want you to actually say which move you would make and why. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
