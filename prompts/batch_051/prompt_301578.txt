Ok here's the thing. I keep running into Pascal's mugging and I want to finally get clear on what a decision procedure actually has to do to not be a sucker, before I read any more supposed fixes. Quick recap of the problem in case you know it under a different name: someone asks you for $20 and says they're speaking for a future superintelligence that will, iff you pay, simulate an obscenely huge number of blissful lives (think a number so large a googolplex looks like one). If there's any nonzero probability he's telling the truth, the expected value of paying looks like it dwarfs every finite amount of money you'll ever make. So either you pay every weirdo with a big story, or you need some principled reason not to—and that reason has to survive when the mugger tweaks the story.

I'm not asking you to solve it. I want the spec: the full list of requirements a theory of decision under uncertainty has to meet, if it's going to shake off the mugging without becoming useless for ordinary risks (plane crashes, lotteries, career gambles, donating to low-probability far-future causes). For each requirement, give the case or argument behind why it's a requirement. Make sure you keep seperate the distinctions a rule can't collapse—small but real probability vs. uncertainty about the whole model, willingness to pay something vs. paying the exact amount demanded, a one-off encounter vs. an iterated policy that lets muggers drain you dry, a reason to give vs. a reason you could quote. Flag which requirements are genuine constraints and which are just aesthetic preferences of one camp (fixed probability thresholds, computational tractability, dominance, no-dutch-book, time consistency, invariance under retellings of the story). Tell me wether any pair of them can't be jointly satisfied—that's the part I most want to see. And tell me which single requirement does the most to knock out the standard fixes people actually propose. A table of the requirements up top is fine, but keep the reasoning about why each one is a requirement in prose. American English, please.
