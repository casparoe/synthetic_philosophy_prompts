I have encountered the standard positions in the philosophy of music many times, but I have rarely seen them tested against a particular institution that has quietly become part of ordinary listening. Streaming platforms now classify recordings by mood: tracks are tagged as 'sad', 'angry', 'peaceful', 'uplifting', and so on, and users are served playlists in which sadness is treated as a property of the recording in something like the way tempo or key is. These tags are produced by models trained on aggregate human annotations of audio features, so they are neither a report of any individual listener's response nor a transcription of the composer's self-description. The practice appears to function as a large-scale, stabilized answer to the old question of how sounds can be sad.

What I would like is a reconstruction of the implicit theory of musical emotion that this practice carries, together with a precise account of how much it strictly presupposes. Does the practice require locating sadness in the acoustic object itself? Or is it compatible with a resemblance view (the music's contour and timing resemble the shape of sad prosody or bodily movement), with an arousalist view (the tags report the state presumably induced in a typical or aggregate listener), with an imagined-persona view (listeners converge on hearing a fictive expresser), or with a dimensional view on which valence and arousal exhaust the relevant content? Above all, I would like to know what a strongly listener-relative theory — according to which the sadness belongs to the listening rather than to the track — would force the practice to change. Would labels have to become per-listener rather than per-track? Would the interface have to present the tag as 'sounds the way sadness feels' rather than 'is sad'? Which parts of the practice would survive, and which would collapse? Please distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits well with, and mark the points where it is philosophically underdetermined. I am not asking about the machine-learning details of how the classifier works; I am asking about the metaphysics and its practical consequences.
