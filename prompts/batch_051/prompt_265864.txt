I have come to defend a compromise position in the situationist debate over virtue ethics. I grant the situationists their central empirical claim: observed behavior tracks features of the situation — hurry, mood, the presence of bystanders, proximity to authority — far more strongly than it tracks stable, cross-situational character traits. But I deny that this undermines virtue ethics. On my view, the virtues should be reconceived not as global traits such as honesty or compassion, which a person either has or lacks across contexts, but as local, situation-indexed dispositions: honest-in-examination-rooms but not honest-in-crowded-markets, compassionate-toward-strangers-who-collapse but not compassionate-toward-subordinates-who-ask-for-help. Virtue consists in cultivating a broad and coherent set of such local dispositions, together with the practical wisdom to select and shape situations in which one's better dispositions can operate.

I would like a rigorous map of what would count against this position. Which experimental results would be most damaging to it, and which merely awkward? Which philosophical arguments would show that the retreat from global to local traits either fails to preserve what virtue ethics needed, or collapses into something else — perhaps a situationist particularism or a moral psychology without virtue? Which defeaters would a defender of this compromise have to concede count against it, and which could be legitimately dismissed as the situationist worry redescribed rather than answered? I am personally most likely to be moved by evidence that the local-traits reconstruction cannot underwrite moral education, cross-situational responsibility, or the explanatory role that traits are supposed to play in predicting a person over time; but I do not want the map anchored to that. Please rank the defeaters from decisive to merely awkward, and give an honest assessment of whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and, if it has, whether that is a genuine problem for a thesis that sits between empirical psychology and normative ethics.
