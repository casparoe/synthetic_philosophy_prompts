I'm drafting a fantasy novel and I'm stuck on what my protagonist should actually think, not just what the plot needs. In this world, oaths sworn on the river Styx are binding in a literal way: the goddess hears them and punishes oathbreakers. My heroine swore, when she was young and didn't know the stakes, to deliver a sealed message for her uncle no matter what. At the time it seemd like a small favor. Turns out the message is a forged order that will get an innocent family executed; her uncle knew exactly what it was and lied to get her oath out of her. She can break the oath—the curse is real but survivable, and she'd probably live with it—or keep it and knowingly do something she thinks is evil. Is she morally required to keep an oath she only took because she was deceived about what she was actually swearing to do? Or does an oath's force have nothing to grip onto when she couldn't have consented to the actual act? I keep wavering, partly because she did know she was taking on an obligation and partly because the world makes the oath more than just a social convention. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then walk me through the reasoning.
