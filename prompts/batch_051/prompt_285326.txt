Below is a paraphrase of section 17 of Leibniz’s Monadology, written in 1714. Leibniz asks us to imagine a machine built so as to think, feel, and perceive, and then to imagine it enlarged while preserving its proportions, so that a person could enter it as one enters a mill. Once inside, he says, one would find only parts pushing against one another, and never anything from which a perception could be explained. He concludes that perception must therefore be sought in the simple substance, not in the composite or in the machine.
