Before I read further in the philosophy of time, I want a specification of what an adequate theory of time must deliver, where the obligations are drawn from the puzzles in Augustine's Confessions XI rather than from any metaphysics I happen to favor. Treat the target as a philosophical account of time, not merely a formal model of temporal coordinates. Lay out the desiderata: what questions a theory cannot simply refuse or reinterpret away — for instance, in what sense, if any, the past and future exist; what the present is such that it has no extension yet seems to be the only time that is; what exactly is being measured when we measure an interval; how we can compare durations that never coexist; and what memory and anticipation contribute to our awareness of time. For each requirement, give the specific consideration in Confessions XI that motivates it, and say which distinctions must be respected, for example between time and change, between physical time and the time of the mind, and between time and eternity. Then separate the desiderata into those that are genuine constraints on any adequate theory and those that may be dispensable or artifacts of Augustine's theological framework; flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify the requirement that does the most work in excluding candidate theories.
