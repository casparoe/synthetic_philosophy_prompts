I serve on my university's standing committee on student conduct, and I have to circulate a decision memo before our Thursday hearing. Last night I had a long argument with a friend about civil disobedience, and the committee is split along exactly the same line, so I need a draft I can use to frame the discussion.

The case: twelve students occupied the finance office lobby for eight hours, in daylight, after giving campus safety roughly an hour's notice, to protest the university's refusal to divest from fossil fuels. They identified themselves, refused to leave when asked, and were cited for unauthorized occupation and failure to disperse. Most have no prior conduct record; two have minor prior infractions. The available responses run from a formal warning through probation to suspension for a term.

The split: one faction takes the friend's position — on the view that civil disobedience is public, conscientious lawbreaking whose point is to address the institution's sense of justice, the willingness to accept the penalty is internal to the act, so applying the ordinary sanction does not disrespect the students; it treats them as responsible agents. The other faction argues that this gives the institution a convenient instrument: it lets the university punish conduct without ever being required to answer the underlying complaint about its investments, and it makes the protest more legitimate the more willingly it accepts punishment, which is perverse.

Please draft the memo I will circulate. It must:
- recommend a concrete course of action, selected from the available responses, with conditions or a time frame where needed;
- articulate a general principle the committee could apply in future cases of civil disobedience, without pretending to settle the justice of the university's investment policy;
- give the strongest version of the losing position a full and honest treatment, including the part I still find uncomfortable;
- address the distinction between holding someone answerable and punishing them, and explain whether and how the conscientiousness of the violation should affect the sanction rather than the finding of responsibility.

Begin with the memo itself, with no preface or commentary. Open the memo with the recommended course of action in the first sentence, then give the reasoning.
