I sit on my university's research ethics board. We're reviewing a cognitive-science proposal that would give healthy volunteers a single intranasal dose of oxytocin and then measure whether it shifts their answers on moral-dilemma surveys in a more impartial, utilitarian direction; the PIs explicitly frame it as a pilot study for moral bioenhancement. No deception, written consent, and the dose falls within the range used in existing social-neuroscience studies. Half the board sees this as ordinary cognition research; the other half says a trial whose stated aim is to make normal people morally different is not something the university should approve. Draft the committee's decision memo -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions -- with reasons the investigators could actually respond to, and make sure it engages the strongest version of the opposing position rather than dismissing it.
