I have been thinking about the laws that every country leaves on the books but almost never enforces, and I have worked myself into a conclusion I do not believe. I cannot find the step that goes wrong.

(1) No legal system can enforce every law that has been properly enacted. Police, courts, inspectors, and budgets are finite, while the stock of valid laws is enormous and keeps growing.
(2) Because of this, every legal system will leave some valid laws unenforced no matter how well-intentioned its officials are. A standard that cannot possibly be met cannot be what the rule of law demands.
(3) So a society does not fail the rule of law simply because some of its laws are never enforced. If there is a failure, it must lie in which laws are left unenforced, not in the bare fact that some are.
(4) But the selection of which laws to enforce is always a matter of official judgment. When a department puts its resources into pursuing fraud instead of minor traffic rules, it is deciding that some valid laws will actually govern people's conduct and others will not, even if the decision is written up as a budget allocation.
(5) There is no principled difference between deciding that a law will go unenforced because resources are directed elsewhere and deciding that it will go unenforced because an official thinks the law is bad policy. In both cases, an official's judgment determines which valid laws have practical force.
(6) Therefore, since every legal system unavoidably operates this way, an official who declines to enforce a law because they personally think it is obsolete or unjust is not violating the rule of law; they are merely making explicit a form of judgment that the rule of law already tolerates.

I do not want to accept (6). It sounds as though it licenses exactly what the rule of law is supposed to prevent: the people who enforce the law deciding, after it has been enacted, which laws will actually apply. But if I reject (6), I seem to be forced to reject either (2) or (5), and both strike me as true. Which premise should be given up, and what does giving it up cost?
