I have recently agreed to present a paper at my old university's philosophy society, prompted by a close friend who came to believe that a miracle had occurred on the strength of several apparently sober witnesses. My paper defends a reconstructive reading of Hume's argument against miracle testimony: I treat it not as an a priori ban on believing a witnessed miracle, but as a requirement to compare the miracle hypothesis against a specific naturalistic hypothesis about how the testimony arose, and to compare them using the prior probability of each and the likelihood of the testimony under each. I argue that, when the comparison is set up this way, Hume's conclusion survives the standard Bayesian objection that independent witnesses of sufficient reliability can overcome any finite prior, because the independence and reliability of the witnesses are themselves part of what the naturalistic hypothesis must explain. The audience is not going to be sympathetic. Most of the senior epistemologists are Bayesians who read the modern literature on testimony as having shown that Hume's result depends entirely on the prior, and they will press me for actual numbers rather than qualitative talk. Two philosophers of religion will argue that defining a miracle as a violation of a law of nature makes the argument circular, since the uniform experience supporting the law is exactly what the miracle claim disputes. A historian of early Christianity will object that I treat the independence of multiple early reports as a hypothesis to be argued for rather than a given, and will ask what independent attestation would even look like in practice. I want to be ready for the question period. Please produce a prep sheet rather than a restatement of the paper: the objections I am likely to receive, ordered by likelihood and likely damage, phrased as this audience would actually put them; for each, a concise reply and an honest note on the point where that reply is weakest; the concessions I should make in my opening remarks; and the one question I should be most worried about, even though I hope it is not asked. Please write the answer in British English.
