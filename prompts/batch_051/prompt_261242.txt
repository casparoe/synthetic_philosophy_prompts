ok ive been turning over a distinction i want someone to check. i call them 'lighthouse values': things that feel like ends in themselves but might actually be instruments for something the person doesn't explicitly value, the way a lighthouse looks like the destination when you're out at sea but is only there to keep you off the rocks.

take truth-seeking. someone insists 'i want the truth, full stop,' but press them and it starts doing work: they want it so their plans don't fail, so they don't get fooled, so their community can argue rather than shoot. tradition feels similar—people talk about it as sacred in itself, but the defense always cashes out as 'it keeps families together,' 'it prevents chaos,' 'it stores knowledge we've forgotten the justification for.' purity too: ritual, sexual, ideological purity feels like a terminal 'this must not be mixed,' but it functions like boundary maintenance.

so the theory: a lighthouse value has three features. (1) its held and defended as terminal, (2) the reasons people give when pressed are mostly instrumental, and (3) its intensity tracks the health of whatever it protects, not the value itself. if the underlying threat disappeared, the sacredness would quietly switch off.

heres the part i actually want torn apart. is this a real distinction or just 'instrumental value' with a feeling of depth stapled to it? has someone already worked this out under another name (it feels too obvious not to)? and where does it break—can you give me a case of truth-seeking or purity that stays genuinely terminal even when it costs the person everything and protects nothing? be candid, if the metaphor is doing the work for me say so.
