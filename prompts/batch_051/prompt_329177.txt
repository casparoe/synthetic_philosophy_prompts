In a metaphysics reading group, the endurantism–perdurantism debate kept getting interrupted by someone quoting a line Plato has Socrates attribute to Heraclitus, in our translation of the Cratylus: "you cannot step twice into the same stream." Over the next hour, that one sentence was made to do several different jobs: that no ordinary object survives change, that identity over time is not a real feature of the world, that change is real while endurance is an illusion, and that the whole endurantist–perdurantist dispute is merely verbal. I want to know what this sentence can actually mean. Please lay out the distinct precise claims it could be expressing, and for each reading say whether it is a platitude, something substantive and defensible, or something false. Then go back through the uses above and identify, for each, which reading was being invoked and whether that reading actually supports the conclusion it was being used to defend. Finally, give your verdict on whether the saying genuinely bears on the disagreement between endurantism — the view that a persisting object is wholly present at each moment of its existence — and perdurantism — the view that a persisting object is a four-dimensional whole with different temporal parts at different moments — or whether it mainly makes that disagreement vivid without adjudicating it. Please answer in American English.
