My question is about whether genuine moral dilemmas are possible, and I want your credence — a number — rather than a survey of positions. The proposition I want your credence on is this: there is at least one possible situation in which a single agent has two all-things-considered obligations, A and B, such that (i) the agent cannot fulfill both, (ii) failing to do either would be morally wrong, and (iii) neither obligation is cancelled, defeated, or demoted to a merely pro tanto consideration by the conflict itself. I want to exclude merely hard choices, where there is a uniquely correct answer that is difficult to find, and cases where the weaker consideration survives only as a reason to apologize, compensate, or feel regret. The question is whether two all-things-considered oughts can genuinely collide.

Please give me your actual credence in that proposition as a number, and then decompose it. Assign a rough probability to each of the following sub-questions, with a sentence or two of justification for each, and say explicitly how you combine them into your final number:

1. How likely is it that practical morality is fundamentally a matter of weighing pro tanto reasons, such that for any real conflict there is, in principle, a fact of the matter about which side is weightier?
2. Even granting that weighing picture, how likely is it that ideal deliberation always yields a unique winner rather than a tie, incommensurability, or parity between the two alternatives?
3. How likely is it that "ought implies can" is true in the strong sense that rules out obligations that cannot be jointly satisfied — so that two non-compossible all-things-considered obligations cannot both exist?
4. How likely is it that the moral remainders people experience after tragic choices — guilt, regret, the sense of an unpaid debt — are best explained by an obligation that was genuinely left unfulfilled, rather than by an obligation that was defeated but left an emotional trace?

Add any further terms you think carry significant weight. Then tell me: which single sub-question is your final number most sensitive to, in the sense that moving that term from 0.2 to 0.8 would shift your final credence the most? Finally, what concrete argument or evidence — if it were to become available to you — would move your credence more than anything else?
