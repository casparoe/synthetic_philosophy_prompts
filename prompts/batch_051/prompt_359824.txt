Suppose a government announces that, from next year, its rank in the World Happiness Report will be an explicit policy target: each ministry's annual budget receives a small supplement or cut according to whether the country's rank rises or falls; the national statistical office must run and publish a large parallel survey using the same self-reported 0-to-10 ladder question; and any major regulation must include a forecast of its expected effect on that score. Treat this as a mechanism, not as a moral position. Bearing in mind that the ladder measures self-reported life evaluation rather than emotional well-being, that it has known framing and translation effects, and that the ranking is comparative so a country can rise or fall through changes elsewhere, walk through how the scheme would actually behave: what it incentivizes among ministers, statisticians, and respondents; how each component would be gamed once budget consequences attach; what second-order effects on policy and on the credibility of the rankings would follow; and whether safeguards could keep the scheme from becoming the management of the score rather than the improvement of well-being. Organize the response under these headings, in this order: "What the mechanism would actually optimize," "Incentives and gaming," "Second-order effects," "Can the failures be fixed?," and "Overall verdict," at roughly 120, 250, 200, 150, and 100 words respectively.
