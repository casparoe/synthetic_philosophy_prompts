A country is considering a constitutional arrangement designed to combine sortition with hard term limits. The lower house is elected for a single, non-renewable six-year term; after leaving office, a member is permanently barred from elected office and from holding a ministerial post for ten years. The upper house is a citizens' chamber of 150 people drawn by lottery for a single four-year term, paid the national median wage, and prohibited from lobbying, consultancy, or paid party work for ten years afterward. The citizens' chamber cannot veto, but it can send a bill back once with published objections, and by a two-thirds vote it can force a binding national referendum. I know the standard case for and against sortition and the usual complaints about career politicians; set those aside. Analyze this as a mechanism: who would run and who would accept a sortition seat, what the final term does to incumbent behavior, where political talent and donor money migrate, and how the chambers actually bargain given the referendum power. Then identify the likely failure modes and say whether they can be patched without reintroducing a career political class.
