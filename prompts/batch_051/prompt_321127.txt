The target claim is this: semantic anti-realism — the view that truth is subject to an epistemic constraint, roughly that no truth can be in principle unknowable by beings like us — can survive Fitch's paradox. By can survive I mean there is a formulation of the relevant constraint that is (i) non-trivial, in that it does not collapse into the thesis that every truth is actually known, and (ii) faithful, in that it still excludes the kinds of verification-transcendent truths the anti-realist originally meant to rule out.

Let me set the background precisely. Let K be a factive knowledge operator (Kp → p) closed under conjunction elimination, and let ◇ express possibility. The naive knowability principle is KP: for all propositions p, p → ◇Kp. Fitch's derivation runs as follows. Suppose p ∧ ¬Kp. Applying KP to that conjunction gives ◇K(p ∧ ¬Kp). But K(p ∧ ¬Kp) distributes to Kp ∧ K¬Kp; by factivity, K¬Kp entails ¬Kp, so we get Kp ∧ ¬Kp, an impossibility. Thus ¬(p ∧ ¬Kp), which in classical logic yields p → Kp: every truth is known. I am taking this derivation as valid given its stated assumptions.
