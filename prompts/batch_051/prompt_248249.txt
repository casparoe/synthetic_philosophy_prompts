You are an expert in institutional design and the philosophy of risk regulation, with a working knowledge of tort law, insurance, and regulatory economics. Think carefully and trace incentives step by step rather than giving a generic verdict on the precautionary principle in general.

A state is considering the following Precautionary Bond scheme. (1) Before any firm may commercially deploy a new technology, chemical, or production process, it must commission an independent worst-case assessment: the most severe harm that any qualified assessor regards as not merely fanciful. (2) The proponent must then post a bond equal to a fixed percentage of the monetized value of that worst-case harm. (3) The bond is held for a twenty-year probation window. If, during the window, a precautionary tribunal finds on a more-likely-than-not causal standard that the deployment contributed to a qualifying harm, the bond pays compensation first and any remainder is forfeited to a public harm fund. If there is no such finding by the end of the window, the proponent recovers the bond with interest. (4) To keep precaution itself from becoming an unregulated source of harm, any regulator who imposes a restriction or delay must clear a symmetric test: the regulator posts a foregone-benefit bond sized by the value of the benefits the deployment would plausibly have produced, and that bond is forfeited to the proponent if the restricted technology is later shown, in some other jurisdiction, to have been safe and broadly valuable over a comparable window.

Walk through how this system would actually operate. What would it incentivize for innovators, for the assessors who produce the worst-case estimates, for the tribunal, for incumbent firms, for insurers and the plaintiffs' bar, and for regulators deciding whether to restrict? How would each side game the two bonds? What kinds of technologies, harms, and proponents would the scheme select for — for example, catastrophic long-tail risks versus diffuse incremental risks, reversible versus irreversible interventions, capital-rich versus capital-poor firms? Where does the mechanism fail first? Which failures could be repaired without gutting the scheme, and which are inseparable from it? Finally, address whether the symmetry between the harm bond and the foregone-benefit bond is real, or whether it masks an asymmetry in the precautionary principle itself.
