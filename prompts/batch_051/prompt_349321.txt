Iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies gets introduced with pure strategies dominating other pure strategies. Fine. Then later the same textbooks quietly say the dominating strategy can be a mixed strategy, and all of a sudden you can eliminate a strategy that no pure strategy strictly beats. Standard example: row player has Top giving (4, 0) against (Left, Right), Middle giving (0, 4), and Bottom giving (1, 1). A 50/50 mix of Top and Middle gives (2, 2), so it strictly beats Bottom against both columns, even though neither Top nor Middle beats Bottom on its own.

I can do the arithmatic, so I'm not after the calculation. What I'm missing is the gears-level story. What is actually true of a strategy when a mixed strategy strictly dominates it, but no pure strategy does? My hazy understanding is that mixed-strategy iterated dominance lines up with never-a-best-response elimination, but I can't make that obvious, and I don't have any feel for why a lottery over strategies can dominate something that none of its components dominates. Also, what changes conceptually when you iterate with mixed dominators instead of pure ones: is it just housekeeping, or are you now modelling a different standard of rational play?
