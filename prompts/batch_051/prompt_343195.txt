Suppose an AI foundation sets up its funding like this. Every quarter each lab has to report one number: the success rate of its newest model on a big public benchmark of ordinary user requests — writing emails, fixing code, answering factual questions, that kind of thing. Next quarter's funding is proportional to that number, with a bonus for beating other labs. Because everyone has heard of Goodhart's law, the foundation adds a twist: it keeps a secret audit set, drawn from the same underlying distribution of real traffic but never published, and every quarter the lab has to run the same model on that audit set under the foundation's remote supervision. If your public-benchmark improvement since last quarter is more than five points higher than your audit-set improvement, your funding is cut in half for the next two quarters. Walk me through how this actually plays out. What do labs start doing to the model, to the training data, and to the public benchmark itself? What do they do about the secret audit — how would they learn anything about it, and what happens if it leaks or gets guessed? Where's the first place this gets gamed, and does the audit provision actually catch Goodharting or just move it around? And if the scheme is broken, is there any fix that doesn't just introduce a fresh metric to game?
