I have spent a while going back and forth on the geometric-rationality discussions and have settled into a package of views that I now want stress-tested for joint consistency. I am not asking for a bibliography or for textual support; I want you to reason about the commitments themselves.

Here is where I am:

(a) Expected utility theory is the correct general account of decision under risk, and an agent's utility function over money is a fixed feature of its preferences across outcomes; it should not change depending on whether a particular gamble is embedded in a sequence or offered once.

(b) For a favorable opportunity that will be repeated many times, the rational rule for choosing the fraction of one's bankroll to stake is the Kelly rule — maximize the expected logarithm of wealth — because that rule maximizes the long-run growth rate and, among fixed-fraction policies, almost surely dominates all others in the long run.

(c) Apart from genuinely extreme sums, my preferences over monetary gambles are close to risk-neutral. I do not find myself pulled toward calibrating a concave utility function from one-off lottery choices; I treat the apparent small-stakes risk aversion people exhibit as mostly a bias or a side effect of loss framing, not as a normative datum to which a theory of rationality must answer.

(d) The fact that a gamble happens to be one-shot should not change what it is rational to do with it, once the probabilities and payoffs are fixed. If a single favorable bet would be a different problem from the repeated bet, I would want to know what feature of the world makes it different.

(e) Logarithmic utility is just one concave utility function among many. I do not see a non-circular argument that the mild risk neutrality I endorse for small sums must, as stakes grow, take exactly the shape U(x) = log x rather than some other smooth concave function or no concave correction at all.

I can feel that (b) is doing something with log utility, and that this must strain against (a), (c), or (e), but I cannot trace exactly where the package goes wrong. So: which of these commitments are in genuine tension, which pairs only look as though they are in tension, and what is the cheapest repair? Please rank the tensions by severity, tell me which single revision of one commitment would buy the most overall coherence, and say what that revision costs elsewhere. Finally, tell me which consideration would most likely change your diagnosis — that is, where your fault-locating is least certain, and what argument or example would lead you to a different verdict.
