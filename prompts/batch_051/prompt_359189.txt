I've been on my university's debate circuit for a couple years, and the thing that bugs me is how much of the outcome is determined by confidence, speed, and how well you can bury the weakest part of your case. Me and some friends started sketching out a competitive reasoning league as a real sport — with a ladder, an end-of-season tournament, Elo ratings, the works. I'd like to know how you think it actually shakes out.

Here's the setup. Sides are assigned at random the morning of the match, so you often defend a position you don't hold. Each match has a three-person panel and scoring runs along four dimensions: (1) how faithfully you restate the other side's view, (2) whether you respond to the strongest version of the other side's argument rather than the weakest, (3) whether you adjust your confidence when contrary evidence shows up mid-match, and (4) how well you identify your own weakest premise. The rule we're most excited about is the steelman gate: before you can object to the opponent's position, you have to summarize their view and main reasons in your own words, and they have to say "that's a fair summary." Three failed attempts and you forfeit the round. Judges also get a fact-check pack prepared in advance, and a claim that gets flagged as false looses you points.

Actually think through what this looks like after a few seasons. What do top players end up optimizing for? Where does the thing get gamed first, and by whom? Does the league reward the best reasoners, or the best performers of reasonableness? Are there rules changes that would close the biggest loopholes without creating new ones?
