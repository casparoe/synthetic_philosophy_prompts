ok so a freind and I built this argument and now I'm stuck inside it. It's not really about any actual technology, more about whether the logic of enhancement forces a conclusion I hate.

(1) If a change would make my future self better off by my own current values, then, if it's safe and I know that in advance, I ought to take it; symmetrically, if a change would make me worse off by my current values, I ought not to take it.

(2) Suppose there's a chain of small changes, T1 through T50. At each step, the person taking the change would judge, by the values they have just before that step, that the change makes them better off. Early steps are things like slightly better focus, memory, and patience. Later steps nudge what you care about. By step 50, the person cares only about maximising some arbitrary score and would find my current projects -- writing, friendships, curiosity -- basically worthless. But my current values judge that step-50 person to be leading a much worse life than I lead now.

(3) If betterness is transitive -- if A is better than B and B is better than C, then A is better than C -- then since each step in the chain makes the person better off, going all the way to step 50 must be better than staying where I am.

(4) But by (1), I ought not to take a change that makes me worse off by my current values, and going all the way to step 50 is exactly that.

(5) So at least one thing has to go: the principle in (1), the existence of the chain in (2), the transitivity assumption in (3), or the authority my current values are given in (4). I badly want to keep (1) and the authority of my current values, but I cant see wich step actually fails.
