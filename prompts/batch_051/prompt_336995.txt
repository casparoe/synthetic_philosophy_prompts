I have been working on the old problem of Satan's fall—how an angel created good, with a clear intellect and no prior tempting evil, could originate evil—and I have arrived at an account I tentatively call the Application-Gap Theory of First Sin. I would like an honest assessment of it, including the strongest objections.

The account begins with two claims. First, the created will is genuinely free in its first act, and therefore not simply determined by its created orientation toward the good; freedom requires some practical determination to be made by the creature rather than received. Second, a finite intellect can know general goods and priorities without yet possessing a ready-made answer to how those priorities apply to the first concrete case before it. Between general normative knowledge and particular choice there is an application gap, and a free creature must close that gap by an act of its own.

On this theory, the fall runs in two stages. In the first stage, the angel confronts an open comparison: its own perfection is good, and acting in the manner prescribed by the divine order is good, but how these two goods bear on one another in this first case has not been settled in advance. The angel imposes a ranking that subordinates receiving its perfection in the prescribed manner to pursuing its perfection in a manner of its own choosing. Because the comparison was genuinely unsettled for the first case, this ranking is not yet the sin. In the second stage, the general priority of the divine order is brought to bear on the very case the angel has ranked—through its continuing awareness of the order it was created with, or through whatever correction a sinless creature would meet—and the angel now sees that its ranking conflicts with an order it already acknowledges. The sin proper is this second act: it ratifies its own self-authored ranking anyway, preferring to keep an interpretation it has made even as that interpretation is shown to be self-serving.

The theory is meant to explain three things at once: why the first evil choice is fully free rather than the product of an already-corrupt will; why the fall can be located in the creature rather than in the creator or in the creation; and why evil tends toward hardening rather than remaining a single mistaken judgment. But I can see the central objection already: the first stage may be doing the real work, and the initial self-preferring ranking may already be the sin, in which case I have merely moved the problem back one step. I want a candid assessment. Does the application gap do genuine explanatory work, or is it only a relabeling of the standard free-will defense? Can the first-stage ranking really be innocent if the angel knows the divine order is authoritative? What does the second stage add, and where does the account break? Finally, has anything recognizably like this been worked out under another name, and what are the strongest reasons to think the view is false rather than merely under-described?
