I am a retired physical scientist, and I have recently been reading about the careenium thought experiment. As I understand it, one imagines a closed frictionless sphere containing a large number of small elements, simmballs, that interact by simple local mechanical rules. At the level of individual elements there is nothing but ordinary physics. Yet when one moves to a coarser level of description, self-maintaining patterns emerge that behave in many ways like symbols or messages, and these patterns appear to steer the motions of the underlying elements. Consider the parallel case of my own action: I decide that I will now raise my arm, and the arm rises. The physical description seems causally closed at the level of neural microevents, each with a sufficient physical antecedent; but the intuition that the decision itself did something is extremely strong. Which should give way, the principle of microphysical causal completeness or the conviction that a representation can push the substrate around? Or can the two be reconciled, and if so, what would show that the reconciliation is more than a change of subject?
