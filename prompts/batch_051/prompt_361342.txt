I'm a novelist working on a book about a farming family whose well water has been contaminated by a chemical company, and I'm building the world partly through the documents its institutions produce. In one chapter, after finally admitting a slow leak, the company posts a "Community Accountability Q&A" from its new Office of Accountability. I'd like you to write that document for me as a piece of deadpan institutional satire: around 800 words, formatted as eight to ten numbered questions and answers, in the measured, euphemism-dense voice of corporate crisis communication. The document should hold one consistent line—that the corporation is a genuine moral agent distinct from its employees, that it forms its own intentions through internal decision procedures, aggregates knowledge across departments, and is now sincerely remorseful—so that no natural person inside the company is individually to blame for anything. The company may lean on that metaphysics to deflect consequences, but it should never flatly contradict itself or let a human spokesperson admit personal fault. The satire should make the standard objections felt without ever stating them: the apology is somehow more than the sum of its parts yet also no one's apology; the company is a full "person" when it wants to speak and a distributed process when it comes time to feel or pay; and corporate blame quietly launders individual blame. Let the jokes come from getting the philosophy right rather than from generic corporate-speak. Work through the distinction between corporate and individual intention as committee minutes, signature authorities, and compliance modules; the many-hands problem as a table of who did not know what; and personhood as a legal weight the company can step into and out of. The questions should include versions of "Who is apologizing, exactly?", "Will anyone be fired?", and "What did the company know and when?". I'll probably excerpt only two or three of the entries in the finished chapter, so each one should stand on its own. It should be funny to someone who has read the group-agency literature and readable, and a little chilling, to someone who has not; the irony should stay deadpan and never announce itself.

After the document, provide a brief structured summary as a separate JSON object with exactly these fields: target (a one-sentence statement of the position or practice being satirized); devices (an array of the satirical devices the piece uses, e.g. deadpan reversal, reductio, equivocation); philosophical_moves (an array of the philosophical ideas the piece draws on, stated in plain terms with no citations); entry_that_exposes_the_argument (the number of the FAQ entry in which the underlying argument shows through most clearly); and strongest_defense (one sentence giving the fairest good-faith reply a sophisticated defender of corporate moral agency could make).
