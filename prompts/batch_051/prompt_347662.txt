I've been going back and forth on accuracy-first epistemology after a long LessWrong thread about whether the Brier score is really the right way to score credences, and I think I've landed on a position I can't yet articulate well enough to defend in public. The accuracy-first literature mostly reaches for the Brier score as the default measure of inaccuracy, and then uses strict propriety plus additivity to run the accuracy-dominance argument for probabilism. But it seems to me the log score is the better epistemic utility function. The main reason is locality: the penalty for a credence function at a world should depend only on the credence it assigned to the proposition that's actually true at that world. Brier fails this once you get past binary partitions, because the penalty also depends on how you spread your credence over the false alternatives — the sum-of-squares term over the non-actual cells means two forecasters who assign the same credence to the true proposition get penalized differently depending on how they distributed the rest of their probability mass. Log score doesn't do that; there's even a theorem that the log score is essentially the only local strictly proper scoring rule, which should matter more than it does.

The obvious objection is that the log score is unbounded and hands out infinite inaccuracy for credence 0 in the true proposition (and, in the binary case, for credence 1 in the false one), whereas Brier keeps everything politely finite. The standard line is that this is a decisive strike against the log score. I don't buy it: being maximally wrong about the actual world after committing all your credence to a different world seems like it *should* be scored as infinitely bad, or at least like this is a feature rather than a bug. But I keep tying myself in knots over the details, especially around what happens to expected inaccuracy when a credence function contains 0s and 1s, and whether the dominance argument for probabilism still goes through if you use log score.

Could you write me the argument defending the log score as the right epistemic utility function for accuracy-first epistemology? I want it pitched at readers who know the broad program but haven't followed the scoring-rule-choice debate. In particular, make the locality point do real work, take the unboundedness objection head-on rather than just waving at it, and deal somehow with the update-rule and zero-credence pathologies people always bring up. I'd also like a clear statement near the end of what would concretely change if accuracy-firsters switched from Brier to log score — does anything important break, or is Brier just an arbitrary default?
