In my former career as a population biologist I often heard the slogan survival of the fittest dismissed as an empty tautology: the fittest are simply the ones that survive, so saying that the surviving organisms are the fittest explains nothing. Now that I have been reading philosophy of biology, I gather the charge is less simple than I took it to be, and that the propensity interpretation of fitness was devised in part as a reply. I would like you to separate the distinct precise claims the tautology objection might amount to. It could mean, for instance, that the phrase survival of the fittest has no empirical content; that fitness is defined by actual survival and therefore cannot explain it; that the theory of natural selection makes no falsifiable predictions; or that the slogan offers a bookkeeping summary where a causal account is required. There may be further readings. For each reading you identify, say whether it is trivially true, a substantive and defensible criticism, or false, and say which of them, if any, the propensity interpretation of fitness actually defuses. Then tell me which version of the objection, if any, still has force against a modern formulation of natural selection. I want this as a menu of readings rather than as one preferred analysis, because my interest is in seeing where my old dismissal went wrong and where it may still have had a point.
