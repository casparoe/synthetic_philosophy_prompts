I recently learned about the color phi experiment. A blue dot is flashed near the top of a screen and, a moment later, a red dot is flashed near the bottom. At certain spacings and timings, viewers report seeing a single dot in apparent motion that changes from blue to red partway along its trajectory, and they locate the color change before the red dot was actually displayed. Two readings of this have been bothering me. On one reading, consciousness is genuinely retrospective: the brain waits for the later stimulus, then goes back and revises the earlier stretch of experience before we become aware of it. On the other reading, the brain briefly holds the whole episode offline and then delivers an already coherent sequence, so there is no earlier stretch of experience to revise. I have seen these labeled the Orwellian and Stalinesque options, respectively. I now cannot tell whether this is a real distinction. If both readings are compatible with everything subjects report and with every measurement we currently know how to make, does the experiment show that there is simply no fact of the matter about when the color change was experienced? And would that, in turn, show that the distinction between how things seem to me and how they are is a myth — or at least a piece of folk psychology we should stop taking literally? In answering, please separate what the experiment itself settles from what is genuinely contested in its philosophical interpretation, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
