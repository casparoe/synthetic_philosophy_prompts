A colleague claims that the Münchhausen trilemma—normally read as a logical point, that any chain of justification must end in infinite regress, circularity, or a dogmatic assumption—is also an empirical regularity about human reasoning: press any person far enough on their starting points (an axiom, a moral principle, a methodological rule) and their justifications will in fact end in one of those three ways. I suspect this empirical version says nothing beyond the logical one, but I want to know whether it can be tested. Design the strongest concrete test you can: what would you observe or measure (in transcripts of sustained cross-examination, debriefings of experts, or a controlled protocol), what pattern of results would count for the claim as a psychological or sociological regularity and what would count against it, and what a successful test would still not settle—for instance, whether the three outcomes reflect the limits of the justifier or of justification itself. Then take a committed position: is the empirical version of the trilemma worth testing, or is it unfalsifiable in practice? Give your reasons.
