I've been going back and forth on something and I'd genuinely like help sorting it. I'm attracted to the parity view about moral testimony: if I can come to know that my car needs a new alternator or that a certain mushroom is poisonous by trusting someone competent, then I should be able to come to know that something is morally right or wrong the same way. Treating morality as the sole domain where testimony is suspect feels like special pleading. But there's an intuition I cant shake.

The intuition sounds to me like this passage from Kant's What is Enlightenment? (1784): "Statutes and formulas, those mechanical tools of the rational employment or rather misemployment of his natural gifts, are the fetters of an everlasting tutelage." When a thoughtful friend tells me 'that was cruel,' and I just take it on board, I form a moral belief. But have I used my own understanding, or have I just replaced my judgment with a statute, the way the unenlightened person replaces his judgment with a book, a pastor, or a doctor? The parity side has a retort ready: I do exactly that when the mechanic tells me the alternator is dead, and no one calls it self-incurred tutelage. So either their contention is that morality is epistemically different in some principled way, which they haven't yet shown, or the Kantian worry is just about convenient laziness rather than about testimony itself.

I dont want to be told which side wins; I want the strongest version of each and a verdict about what should yield, and what the loser keeps. The intuition needs to be debunked or vindicated: is it just confusion between deference in belief and deference in action, or just pride in being the author of my own values? Or does it track something real, like moral understanding rather than mere true belief, or the idea that I couldn't stand behind the belief in the way responsibility demands? The theory needs to be defended or restricted: explain how 'thinking for oneself' can include rationally trusting a competent moral informant, or concede that testimony yields true moral belief without the understanding that actually matters. Which do I give up, and what does the surviving side owe the other?
