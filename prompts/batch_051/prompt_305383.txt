I am a novelist at work on a novel whose central character is a committed historical materialist. In the manuscript, he explains the architecture, the marriage customs, the legal code, and the dominant literary style of his society by tracing each of them, one by one, to the prevailing mode of production. He defends this way of talking by saying that the economic base determines the superstructure, and that this is the entire lesson of historical materialism.

I have been writing him as though he is right, but I have begun to worry that this makes every non-economic feature of the story read as mere decoration for an economic engine, and it flattens the characters' inner lives. I want to know whether this is really what the position amounts to. If the base does not turn art, law, and religion into straightforward effects of the mode of production, what is the more careful version of the base/superstructure claim? How much independent causal weight can a historical materialist grant to the superstructure without abandoning the thesis? Where does the real philosophical pressure lie: in what counts as the base, in what determination means, in how the two levels are related, or somewhere else? In your answer, please separate what is settled among people who know this material from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading of historical materialism as if it were the consensus.
