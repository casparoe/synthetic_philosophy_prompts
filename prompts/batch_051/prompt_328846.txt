A friend and I spent the evening arguing about which of a system's internal states are genuinely about something, in the way your belief that the back door is unlocked is about the door, and which merely have derived, as-if aboutness. She draws a hard line between original and derived intentionality; I lean toward thinking the line is blurrier, and by the end I realized I could not state her rule in a single sentence. Her settled verdicts on the cases we went through are:

- A person's standing belief that the back door is unlocked: genuine.
- A dog's state when it waits at the door for its owner to return: genuine.
- A honeybee's waggle dance recruiting nestmates to nectar: genuine, though she called it a thin case; she is also sceptical that bees are conscious.
- A vending machine that accepts quarters and rejects slugs: merely as-if.
- A thermostat whose strip bends with the room temperature and switches the boiler: merely as-if.
- A chess engine's internal evaluation that Black's queen is trapped: merely as-if.
- A large language model that, shown a photograph, outputs the sentence that the cat is on the mat: merely as-if.
- The standard Chinese room setup, with the person following the rulebook in a language he does not understand: merely as-if.

I want to predict her verdicts before we resume. The new cases are:

- Swamp-Dog: a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a healthy dog, assembled by chance in a laboratory, with no evolutionary or developmental history, but physically indistinguishable from an ordinary dog and behaving normally.
- Sleeping Belief: a person's stored belief during dreamless sleep, not occurrent but available for report on waking.
- Firm Forecast: a company's internal demand forecast that the company uses to plan inventory.

What would she say about each — genuine, merely as-if, or leaning one way — and with what confidence? I am less interested in the bare answers than in the rule or rules consistent with her track record; there will likely be more than one. Give me each plausible rule, say which recorded cases it handles well and which it strains against, and say which rule you think she is actually running; name the closest recorded case for each new case; and identify which new case most cleanly discriminates the live rules.

Return your answer as a JSON object with these fields:

- candidate_rules: an array of objects, each with rule_name, one_line_statement, fit_to_record, and predicted_verdicts. predicted_verdicts is an object with keys swamp_dog, sleeping_belief, and firm_forecast; each value has label, confidence, and justification.
- best_guess_verdicts: an object with keys swamp_dog, sleeping_belief, and firm_forecast; each value has label, confidence, closest_recorded_case, and a one-sentence reason for that analog.
