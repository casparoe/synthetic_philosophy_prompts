Write an analysis I could take to office hours. In a seminar on Advaita Vedanta, my professor keeps answering hard questions by invoking the two-part slogan *the world is māyā; you are Brahman*. When a student asked whether Advaita is committed to saying that tables, tigers, and other people are literally unreal, my professor replied only that the world is māyā. When another student asked whether the individual self that deliberates, regrets, and makes choices is real, she replied that you are Brahman. I can tell the slogan is doing real philosophical work, but I cannot pin down what proposition, or set of propositions, she is actually asserting with it.

I want you to lay out the distinct precise claims this slogan could be making, from the trivial to the radical, and to say which of those readings, if any, would actually support each of my professor's two dismissive replies.

Please organize your answer into the following sections, by name and in this order.

1. Context (about 150 words). Reconstruct the philosophical difficulty the slogan is being used to settle, keeping the question about the status of the world distinct from the question about the status of the individual self.

2. Candidate Readings (about 900 words). Present at least six distinct readings of the slogan as a numbered list. For each reading, state the precise thesis it attributes to the slogan in a sentence or two, give one reason someone might hold it, and label it as trivial, moderate, substantive, radical, or likely false, as you judge appropriate. Include at least one reading on which the slogan comes out almost trivial, and at least one on which it comes out strongly illusionistic about the ordinary world.

3. Which Readings Do the Work (about 350 words). Return to the two dismissive replies described above. Explain which reading or readings from section 2 would license each reply, and which would not. Say explicitly whether the reading needed to dismiss the question about the ordinary world is the same as, or different from, the reading needed to dismiss the question about the individual self.

4. The Reading to Bring Up (about 150 words). Recommend one reading I can safely grant her and one reading I should ask her to disavow before accepting that the matter is settled, and explain briefly how that would tighten the exchange.
