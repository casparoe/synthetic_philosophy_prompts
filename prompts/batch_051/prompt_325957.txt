I recently read an article that summarizes Harry Frankfurt's attack on the principle of alternative possibilities roughly as follows. Frankfurt showed that moral responsibility does not require the ability to do otherwise: in his examples the agent is plainly responsible even though he has no alternatives, because he acts on his own motives and the covert device that would have forced his hand never actually has to intervene. Since an intervention that never occurs cannot bear on responsibility, the principle of alternative possibilities is refuted. And because determinism threatens responsibility only by removing alternative possibilities, this undercuts the main argument for incompatibilism; the only serious reply, the "flicker" objection, points merely to trivial alternatives that no one could think responsibility genuinely requires. I find the broad thrust plausible, but I suspect the article is smoothing over objections in ways that matter for the final conclusion. Which parts of the summary are accurate, which parts would an incompatibilist reject or heavily qualify, and does the compatibilist conclusion survive once the inaccurate parts are corrected?
