For the unit on simultaneous discovery I'm putting together a handout, and I want the core of it to be a set of criteria for when someone counts as having discovered a scientific result, plus a case that puts real pressure on the criteria. Here's the working list. Someone counts as a discoverer of a scientific result only if (a) they actually articulated the result itself, not something adjacent to it or a vague hunch; (b) they arrived at it independently, not by borrowing from the other party; (c) they recognized that it was a result — that is, they had some grip on what it explained or why it mattered; and (d) they made it available to the relevant community in a form that could be taken up and used, because science is a public, cumulative practice rather than private cogitation.

Now the case. Charles Darwin, writing to Charles Lyell on 18 June 1858, after receiving Alfred Russel Wallace's Ternate manuscript on natural selection, says this:

"Though my Book, if it will ever have any value, will not be deteriorated; as all the labour consists in the application of the theory."

The backstory: Darwin had already written out the theory twice in private — a 35-page sketch in 1842 and a 230-page essay in 1844 — and in 1844 he left instructions for his wife about publishing the essay if he should die; a few friends knew his general view, but the essays themselves stayed unpublished. Wallace's short paper stated the same core theory independently.

So the borderline case is Darwin as of 1844: same core theory as Wallace, but private. Does criterion (d) rule him out, or does the 1844 essay already make him a discoverer of natural selection? Go criterion by criterion, and where a criterion underdetermines the answer, say so. Then do the harder bit: does Darwin's remark that all the labour lies in applying the theory show that (d) is wrong, because application is something that comes after discovery rather than part of it? Or is Darwin rationalizing his own nightmare? And should it actually matter, for how we credit Darwin and Wallace, whether a private 1844 essay counts — or is that a question only for sociologists of science? When you're done, tell me how confident you are in your reading, and single out the point you're least sure about.
