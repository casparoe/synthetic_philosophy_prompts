I am a retired physicist who has only recently begun to read philosophy of physics seriously, and I am trying to make up my mind about an old question in a disciplined way: is the quantum wavefunction part of the world itself, or only a bookkeeping device for what we know about a system? I gather that philosophers call these the psi-ontic and psi-epistemic views, but I would rather work through the question with explicit criteria than by appealing to labels.

My tentative criteria for counting something as real, rather than as useful bookkeeping, are these four. First, it must figure in our best explanation of observed phenomena and must not be easily eliminated from that explanation. Second, it must be capable of making a difference to the course of events, not merely of summarising them. Third, it must be robust across changes in the formalism in which the theory is expressed, rather than an artefact of one formulation. Fourth, it must not be defined solely in terms of what an observer knows or could in principle learn.

Now take a specific case that seems to satisfy some of these but not all. In ordinary non-relativistic quantum mechanics with a collapse postulate, the wavefunction of an isolated system is updated discontinuously when a measurement occurs, is never directly observed, and is often read as encoding a probability distribution over possible outcomes of measurements. By the first criterion it looks indispensable to prediction; by the second it is unclear whether it pushes anything around or merely records probabilities; by the third it sits uneasily because its dynamical role shifts depending on whether one chooses, say, the Schrödinger or the Heisenberg picture; and by the fourth it is uncomfortably close to a summary of the information available to an experimenter.

Does the wavefunction, understood this way, qualify as real under my four criteria? Please work through the case criterion by criterion, say explicitly where the criteria underdetermine the verdict, and then say whether the case shows that one or more of my criteria need to be revised, or whether the criteria are simply the wrong instrument for deciding between psi-ontic and psi-epistemic views. Please answer in British English.
