For the past decade, a colleague has kept a complete log of every addition problem she has been asked to solve in her work. The log contains roughly two thousand entries, and each one records the two whole-number addends she was given and the answer she wrote. In every entry, both addends are below 100, and in every entry the answer she wrote is the ordinary sum of the two addends: "18 + 41 = 59," "76 + 3 = 79," "0 + 49 = 49," "14 + 85 = 99," and so on. She has never once been given an addend of 100 or greater, and she has never once written anything other than the standard sum.

Next week, for the first time since the log began, she will be asked to compute "100 + 6."

I want a committed answer, not a survey of possible positions. Please do all of the following.

(1) Specify two rules that both fit every entry in the log exactly. One is ordinary addition. The other should be a rule of your own construction that returns the ordinary sum for every pair of addends that actually occurs in the log, but returns something different from ordinary addition for "100 + 6." State your second rule precisely enough that it has a definite answer for any pair of whole numbers.

(2) Say what each rule predicts for "100 + 6."

(3) Take a definite position on which rule the colleague is actually following, and defend it. If you think the log by itself underdetermines this, and that predicting her answer requires a choice about how to extend a pattern that fits everything she has done so far, say so explicitly — but still give me your best-grounded prediction of the number she will write, and the reasons you would bet on that number.

(4) Finally, identify one further test case, not merely a relabeling of "100 + 6," that would do the most to tell the two rules apart as accounts of how she will continue, and state what each rule predicts for that case.
