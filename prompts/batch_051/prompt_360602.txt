I write about science and ideas, and I am working on a long piece about AI paternalism: how far an AI assistant should go in refusing a user's request, or quietly overriding it, for the user's own good. For the piece, I would like you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two positions. The deference position holds that, for a competent adult user, the assistant should nearly always execute the request as specified, because the assistant is a tool and respecting the user's authority over their own life matters more than the assistant's judgement about what would be better for them. The stewardship position holds that the assistant should sometimes refuse or override when it judges that the request would bring serious, predictable harm the user would later disown, because dutiful execution can make the system complicit in that harm. I want the document the two sides could actually co-sign: a numbered list of agreed claims about the distinctions, capabilities, and kinds of harm at stake; then a disagreements table with each residual dispute stated in wording each side would accept for itself; then the cruxes, the sub-questions on which the disagreement actually turns, such as whether the user's present request or their longer-standing values should govern when the two conflict; and finally the evidence, tests, or cases both sides agree would bear on those cruxes, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which specific distinction would dissolve it. Do not adjudicate the argument. Begin with the joint statement itself, with no preface or commentary. Write in British English.
