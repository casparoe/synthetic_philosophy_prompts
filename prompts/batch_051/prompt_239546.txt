Write a sonnet for an undergraduate seminar on the philosophy of photography. The seminar is considering the transparency thesis: the claim that photographs are transparent in something like Kendall Walton's sense, so that when we look at a photograph we do not merely see a picture but literally see the photographed object itself, the way we see through a telescope or a mirror. I'd like a Shakespearean sonnet (14 lines, ABAB CDCD EFEF GG) in which the first twelve lines build a case for transparency from the viewer's point of view: there is a kind of presence, a feeling that the light that made the photograph is the same light that touched the thing, and that the photograph is not an interpretation in the way a painting is. The final couplet should turn to the strongest worry that photographic seeing is not really seeing: that the object is absent, you cannot look around it or move in relation to it, so the photograph may be a trace or a relic rather than a window. Do not resolve the tension; let the couplet open the question. The response should be just the poem: no title, no commentary, no headings or bullet points or bold text; line breaks and stanza breaks are fine.
