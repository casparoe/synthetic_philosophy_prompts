I have been trying to get clear on why originals are valued so differently from copies, and on where the line falls between an original, a reproduction, and a forgery. To make the question tractable, suppose the special value of an original painting rests on five features: (1) the content originates in the artist's creative decisions; (2) the physical object was made by the artist's own hand, or by someone working under the artist's direct control; (3) the artist intended, or would have endorsed, the object's standing as their work; (4) there is an unbroken causal-historical chain from the artist's act to the object now before us; and (5) the object is presented without false provenance. A pure forgery fails nearly all of these, while an authorized reproduction passes some and fails others; the interesting cases are the ones that fall between.

Consider a case a conservator once described to me in a slightly different form. A painter leaves behind a full-scale preparatory drawing for a large altarpiece: the composition is fixed, every figure is drawn in detail, and the artist's notebooks specify the palette, the order of the paint layers, and the handling of several passages. The artist dies before touching the panel. Years later, a conservator who has spent a career studying this painter's technique executes the altarpiece exactly according to the drawing and the notebooks, using period materials and documented methods, so that the result is, in the judgment of everyone who examines it, what the painter would have painted. Where the notebooks are silent, however, the conservator must make choices, and those choices are the conservator's own. The finished panel is exhibited and catalogued under the painter's name, with a note stating that it was executed posthumously from the artist's design.

Is this panel an original work by the painter, a forgery, or some third kind of thing? Work through the five features one by one, say where they underdetermine the verdict, and then tell me whether the case shows that the features themselves need revising — in particular, whether authorship of execution and causal-historical continuity are doing genuine work, or whether they are standing in for something else that actually grounds the value of an original.
