My reading group includes a hardline adaptationist about human behaviour and someone who thinks the whole field is unfalsifyable just-so stories. They agree on a surprising amount about music—that it's universal, that it costs time and energy, that it has some genetic basis—but they split over whether it's a selected adaptation or a byproduct. I want the joint statement they'd write after a good-faith adversarial collaboration: a numbered list of claims both can sign, then a disagreements table where each side's formulation is worded so that side would accept it, then a short cruxes section. For each crux, give one concrete observation or experiment both sides agree bears on it, and say what each side predicts. The cruxes I keep coming back to are (1) whether the pleasure of syncing to a beat is evidence of a special-purpose capacity or just our general prediction-reward system latching onto a regularity; (2) whether the time and energy music costs are the kind of cost natural selection would care about. Add a third crux if it makes the disagreement sharper. Hard limit: 600 words.
