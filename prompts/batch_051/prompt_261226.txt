I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so go easy on the jargon. I'm writing a book about a rumor that takes over a small town, and I've been reading the Darwinian-culture stuff. I want to make sure my own view hangs together before I let a character carry it. Here's what I think: (1) culture really evolves — songs, rumors, slang get copied with variation, some variants spread more than others, and its the same Darwinian process as genes, not just a loose analogy. (2) memes don't have goals or interests; saying a rumor wants to go viral is shorthand and I'd never cash it out literally. (3) I'm happy to use gene's-eye explanations in the same way: genes don't want anything, but saying what spreads does real causal work. (4) people are still responsible for which memes they pass on — it's wrong to repeat a cruel rumor even when its catchiness made it near-certain someone would. (5) a rumor's catchiness partly explains why an individual passes it on, whatever their explicit reasons are. Are these five consistent? I can't tell where the real friction is. Rank the tensions by severity, say which pairs only look like they clash, and tell me what I'd have to give up for the cheapest fix.
