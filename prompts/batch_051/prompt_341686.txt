Spent last night arguing with a friend about insects and I still can't shake this asymmetry. A doctor will treat pain in a patient who can't say they're in it, on the chance they're suffering; we'd all call that basic caution. But nobody says we should stop spraying a crop just in case the aphids feel something, even though "just in case" times a billion aphids starts to look like a lot of expected suffering. And the same person who'd take a 10 percent chance a dog suffers seriously will treat a 10 percent chance an insect suffers as if it were zero. Is there a principle that makes these hang together, or is it just that we're bad at multiplying small probabilities by huge numbers? Give me the best justification you can for the asymmetry and an honest verdict on whether to keep it.
