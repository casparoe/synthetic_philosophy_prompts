In my philosophy of math seminar the argument about whether 2 is an element of 4 has stalled in a way thats honestly starting to annoy me. Everyone in the room accepts the set-theoretic facts: under the von Neumann encoding, 2 = {∅,{∅}} and 4 = {0,1,2,3}, so 2 ∈ 4. Under the Zermelo encoding, 2 = {{∅}} and 4 = {3}, so 2 ∉ 4. Everybody also agrees there are infinitely many other encodings that do the same arithmetic job. But then we split. Some people think the question still has a right answer and we just need to settle which reduction is the 'real' one. Others think the Benacerraf-style point shows there's no determinate answer, because numbers aren't secretly sets and any choice of encoding is arbitrary. The structuralists go further and say the question is a category mistake: numbers are just positions in the natural-number structure, and positions don't have elements. We've gone around this exact circle for weeks and nobodys moved.

What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it a verbal dispute about the word 'number', a hidden factual question about sets or about what arithmetic language refers to, two groups answering different questions under one heading, or an ill-posed question that should be dissolved rather than answered? My own guess is that the whole thing turns on whether ordinary arithmetic commits us to any internal structure for numbers at all, and once that's settled the rest is just choice of notation, but I'm not confident. And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress here — a formal result, a distinction, a change of question, or should we just expect it to stay stuck?
