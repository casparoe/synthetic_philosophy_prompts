I have been trying to work out how far you can get by treating an organization as a black-box optimizer and reading its actual objectives off its observable choices, the way an alignment researcher might try to infer an objective function and a search space from behavior alone. I want to use the Boeing 737 MAX episode as a stress test for that way of thinking. Take the following as the salient facts of the case, so the analysis does not depend on anyone looking anything up: to fit more efficient engines onto an old 737 airframe without triggering a costly new type certification, Boeing kept the 737 type designation and made the engines work by adding MCAS, a software system that pushed the nose down under certain flight conditions; MCAS took input from a single angle-of-attack sensor, and pilots were not required to undergo new simulator training. Lion Air Flight 610 crashed in October 2018, and Ethiopian Airlines Flight 302 crashed in March 2019; the type was grounded worldwide for roughly twenty months.

Treat Boeing as the optimizer and run the inference in both directions, as a careful mapping rather than an exercise in blame. First, lay out the observable behavior that any inferred objective has to explain: the design choices, the certification and training decisions, and the company's actions between the two crashes. Second, infer from that behavior what objective function Boeing was in effect maximizing — trade the stated objective of safety against schedule, cost, and scope, and say explicitly which apparent trade-offs support each term and its weight. Third, infer the search space: which moves Boeing behaved as if it had available and which it behaved as if it had excluded, and what those exclusions tell us about the constraints that shaped the choices. Fourth, say honestly where the inference is weak or forced: Boeing is not a single rational agent, decisions were made under uncertainty with hindsight available to us, and a constraint that failed can look like a preference that was discounted. Fifth, stand back and say what the episode shows about the method itself — whether it comes out confirmed, confounded, or in need of a richer model, such as one that distinguishes a lexicographic constraint (safety first) from a weighted term and that treats the agent as a distributed organization.

Organize the answer in the five sections above, in that order, with roughly these lengths: the observable behavior (about 150 words), the inferred objective (about 250 words), the inferred search space (about 250 words), where the inference breaks down (about 200 words), and what the episode shows about the method (about 150 words).
