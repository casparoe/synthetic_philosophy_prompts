In interviews, sources keep telling me that variety is the spice of life as though it settled a substantive question: they move from that proverb to the conclusion that a quiet, routine life is missing something important, and that institutions might reasonably steer people toward more varied lives. The single phrase seems to compress several distinct claims about whether novelty is a component of well-being, from the minimal thought that pleasant things pall with repetition to the strong thought that an unvaried life is deficient as a life. Please disambiguate it: what are the precise theses the proverb could be asserting, which are conceptual, which are empirical, which are normative, and which are false or unsupported, and which reading actually licenses the conclusion my sources draw? Give the answer as a table with these columns: reading (the thesis in one sentence), category (trivial, empirical, normative, or constitutive), verdict (platitude, defensible, questionable, or false), and supports_their_conclusion (yes or no).
