I'm a PhD student in philosophy of science, and I'm trying to reverse-engineer the implicit account of verisimilitude my advisor uses in a recurring classroom exercise. The exercise always has the same structure. She describes a target system fixed by five atomic claims, C1 through C5, and gives the actual truth-value of each claim. She then presents two theories, each of which commits to truth-values for only some of the five claims and stays silent on the rest. A commitment is correct when it matches the actual truth-value and incorrect otherwise; silence counts as neither. Finally, she announces which of the two theories is "closer to the truth," without giving her reasoning.

Here are five of her verdicts, all from the same course and all about different target systems.

1. Truth: C1 true, C2 false, C3 true, C4 false, C5 true.
   Theory A commits to: C1 true, C2 false, C3 true, C4 true.
   Theory B commits to: C1 true, C2 true.
   Verdict: A.

2. Truth: C1 true, C2 false, C3 true, C4 false, C5 true.
   Theory A commits to: C1 true, C2 true, C3 false, C5 false.
   Theory B commits to: C1 true, C3 true, C4 false.
   Verdict: B.

3. Truth: C1 true, C2 true, C3 false, C4 true, C5 false.
   Theory A commits to: C1 true, C2 true, C3 false.
   Theory B commits to: C1 true.
   Verdict: A.

4. Truth: C1 true, C2 false, C3 true, C4 false, C5 true.
   Theory A commits to: C1 true, C2 false, C4 false.
   Theory B commits to: C2 true, C3 true.
   Verdict: A.

5. Truth: C1 true, C2 true, C3 false, C4 true, C5 false.
   Theory A commits to: C1 true, C3 false, C4 true.
   Theory B commits to: C2 true, C3 false.
   Verdict: A.

The sixth case, which she will present next week, is this.

Truth: C1 true, C2 true, C3 true, C4 true, C5 false.
Theory A commits to: C1 true, C2 true, C3 true, C4 true, C5 true.
Theory B commits to: C1 true.

Three candidate rules seem compatible with her five recorded verdicts. Rule (i), error-minimization: the closer theory is the one with fewer incorrect commitments, with correct commitments used only to break ties. Rule (ii), truth-content balance: the closer theory is the one with the greater excess of correct over incorrect commitments. Rule (iii), truth-content maximization: the closer theory is simply the one with more correct commitments.

What do you predict she will say about the sixth case? For each possible verdict, which of these rules—or what other rule—would it reveal her to be using? And which further comparison, specified in the same format, would most cleanly settle the remaining ambiguity? Give your best guess even though the record underdetermines it. In your answer, say how confident you are in your prediction and single out the point you are least sure about.
