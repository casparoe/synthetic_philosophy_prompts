I have been thinking about a parallel between Kripkean origin essentialism and the way identity is fixed in a hash-linked blockchain, and I would like an assessment of whether the parallel is deep or superficial.

On one side, take the modal thesis about our origins: a person could not have developed from a different sperm and egg. Had the actual gametes contributed to a different zygote, the resulting human would be a numerically distinct person—a sibling, perhaps qualitatively very similar, but not you. Your origin is not merely an early episode in your life; it fixes which person you are across possible worlds.

On the other side, take how a blockchain identifies a chain. Each block contains the cryptographic hash of its parent, tracing back to the genesis block. A chain is identified by that genesis block together with the unbroken sequence of parent-hashes. If one begins a new chain with a different genesis block and later appends blocks with the same contents, the result is not the same chain with a different start; it is a different chain, even if later blocks match byte for byte.

The mapping I find tempting is this: in both cases an initial element acts as an identity-fixing anchor; later elements are related to that anchor by a determinate relation (development from a zygote; hash-linkage to a parent); and sameness of downstream content does not make for sameness of the whole object. Varying the origin yields a different object rather than the same object with revised origins.

Is there a single abstract principle running beneath both cases, such that they are true for the same reason? Or is the resemblance only verbal, grounded in the shared word origin and a shared picture of links? What would have to be true on either side for results to transfer—for example, for origin essentialism to inherit some formal property of hash chains, or for blockchains to count as essentialist in a genuinely modal sense? And what does the analogy predict that is false? For instance, it might predict that just as a chain can be forked from any block, a person could be continued from any later point in their causal history; that seems to misfire, but I cannot tell exactly where or why.

Please answer in British English.
