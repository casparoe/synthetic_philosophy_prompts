I've been reading rationalist-adjacent blogs for a long time, so I want this done in the spirit of a real adversarial collaboration, not the cheap both-sides-ism version: both sides get their actual views on the page, the agreed claims aren't vacuous, and the cruxes are sharp enough that you could imagine an experiment or a formal result moving someone.

The topic is the counterfactual vs. interventionist split in theories of causation. Camp A is counterfactual-first (Lewis lineage): causal talk bottoms out in counterfactual dependence — roughly, Y counterfactually depends on X when Y wouldn't have happened, or would've been different, if X hadn't happened — and you can make sense of those counterfactuals with similarity-of-worlds machinery without first smuggling in a notion of causation. Camp B is intervention-first (Woodward/Pearl lineage): bare counterfactuals are too unconstrained, and causal claims are really claims about invariance under a suitably surgical intervention — what changes in Y when you set X while holding the rest of the causal structure fixed; structural equations and the do-operator are the natural home for this.

Both camps in this collab accept that the other framework captures something real, and both accept that there are cases where their simple version needs patching. They've been talking passed each other for twenty years and are trying to stop.

Write the exact joint statement they'd both sign. Output one JSON object, no preamble and no markdown fencing around it. The schema:

- "preamble": string, a paragraph both camps sign that says what the document is and isn't trying to settle (for example, it is not trying to decide which framework is "the real semantics" before the cruxes are run).
- "agreed_claims": array of strings, at least 8, each substantive and neither vacuous nor a disguised version of one side's position. Include at least one claim about something the other framework is genuinely good at, and at least one claim about a type of case where both camps' simple versions fail.
- "disagreements": array of objects, one per residual disagreement. Each object has these fields:
  - "id": string,
  - "camp_a_wording": string, Camp A's position in the strongest wording they'd accept,
  - "camp_b_wording": string, Camp B's position in the strongest wording they'd accept,
  - "crux": string, the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side,

  - "prediction_b": string, what Camp B predicts for that test,
  - "status": one of "genuine_disagreement", "partly_terminological", or "unresolved_scope".

At least four rows in "disagreements", and they have to cover: (1) whether causal content can be characterized without any notion of intervention, (2) whether interventions can be characterized without a prior causal notion (the circularity charge), (3) actual/token causation in preemption and overdetermination, and (4) variables you can't surgically set.
