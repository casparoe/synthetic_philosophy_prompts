I host a small tech-and-identity podcast and next week I'm talking to a guest who defends the idea that destructive uploading (scan the brain, simulate it, destroy the biological original) is death — the upload is just a new person with your memories — but that slowly replacing your neurons with functionally identical artificial ones lets the same person survive. I want eight to ten questions that push past the usual 'isn't this just the Ship of Theseus' intro stuff. He constantly falls back on 'a copy is a copy, no matter how gradual' and on the idea that an unbroken stream of conciousness is what does the real work. Give me questions that make that line harder to hold, each with a follow-up aimed at the answer he's most likely to give. Make sure to include: a gradual procedure where each replacement neuron is built by scanning and copying the neuron it replaces, so the whole thing is just a lot of tiny uploads; a version where the entire replacement is done in one surgery under general anesthesia, so the stream is already interrupted; and a question about what the smallest change to a destructive scan would be that would make him count it as a genuine transfer instead of a copy.
