I'm a phil mind PhD student and I keep getting stuck on Dennett's self-as-center-of-narrative-gravity view: the idea that the self isn't a thing in the brain but an abstraction generated by the stories we tell, a useful fiction the way a center of gravity is a useful fiction. I've been collecting the main objections and I want one more — genuinely different, not a restatement or a special case of any of these. So far I have: (1) the what-it's-like objection, that all this talk about narratives and centers from the outside never lands on the raw first-person feel of being a self; (2) Strawson's point that some people live episodic lives, so narrativity looks like one temperamental style being turned into a philosophical norm; (3) the homunculus/regress worry, that a narrative needs an author or at least a reader, and if the self just is the story's center it stops being clear who is doing the spinning; (4) the responsibility objection, that a useful fiction is a lousy target for reactive attitudes, ownership, and desert; (5) the broken-analogy objection, that a physical center of gravity is unique and fully determined by the mass distribution, wheras a narrative center can be multiple, gappy, or contested without anything being wrong; (6) the causal-impotence objection, that a center of gravity doesn't actually do anything, but the self seems to be in the business of doing things. I want a seventh objection that isn't on that list and isn't just a mix of two of them, and then tell me straight whether it survives what Dennett or a sympathetic Dennettian would say back.
