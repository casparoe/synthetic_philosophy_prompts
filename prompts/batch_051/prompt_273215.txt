ok so ive been going in circles on locke's memory criterion and reid's brave officer objection, and i think i found a fix. as i understand locke, person B is the same person as person A just in case B's consciousness reaches back to A—roughly, B remembers A's experiences. reid's point is that the old general remembers being the brave officer, and the officer remembers being flogged as a boy, but the general no longer remembers the flogging. so by locke's criterion the general is the officer, the officer is the boy, but the general isnt the boy, which breaks transitivity. my fix is to replace direct memory with chains: A and B are the same person iff there is a sequence of person-stages where each later one remembers the one before it. so the general and the boy are the same person via the officer as a stepping stone, even though the direct memory is gone. i call this stepping-stone identity. it keeps memory doing all the work, but the relation is now transitive by construction. is this a real solution or am i just defining the problem away? does it already exist under a better name? and what is the most damaging objection—in particular does it survive a case where a persons memories split into two? be as harsh as you need.
