I'm a student in a course on the philosophy of action, and we've been working through Kavka's toxin puzzle. I've come to hold a particular reading of it, and I'd like a map of what would count against that reading rather than a restatement of the standard debate.

The position I've settled on is this. The toxin puzzle is best understood as showing that practical rationality cannot be reduced to expected-utility maximization over actions, because the agent has decisive reason to take the deal, yet no sequence of expected-utility-maximizing choices can deliver the state—an intention formed at midnight—on which the payout is conditioned. What blocks the intention is not weakness of will, a failure of imagination, or the psychological impossibility of summoning a mental state at will. It is a constitutive norm of intention: an agent cannot rationally intend to do what she simultaneously believes she will not do. This norm is synchronic and constitutive, not a means–end or evidential requirement, and that is why the size of the incentive is irrelevant to whether the intention can be formed. The offer, on my reading, asks the agent to occupy two incompatible stances at once—a practical commitment to drink and a theoretical expectation of not drinking—and no amount of money can make that combination rationally coherent.

What I want to know is how exposed this reading is. Which possible cases, formal results from decision theory, or rival interpretations of the puzzle would count against it, and which of those would be decisive rather than merely awkward? In particular: are there variants of the case—involving self-deception, forgetting, side bets, conditional intentions, or agents who can revise their intentions later—that my reading misdescribes or cannot handle? Does the position collapse into a rival once certain qualifications are added? What would a defender of this reading have to concede counts against it, as opposed to what critics merely allege? And if the reading can be insulated from every apparent counterexample by adding qualifications, is that insulation itself a reason to distrust it? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward.
