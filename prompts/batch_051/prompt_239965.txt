I keep seeing a move in philosophy discussions that I don't have a word for. Someone says, roughly, "Try to imagine a tree existing entirely unperceived; you can't, because your imagining it is a perception." They then treat that as showing there cannot be mind-independent objects. It feels a bit like a parlour trick but also not obviously wrong, and I don't know how to place it. I know Berkeley is somewhere in the neighborhood, and in A Treatise Concerning the Principles of Human Knowledge he writes:

"Philosophy being nothing else but THE STUDY OF WISDOM AND TRUTH, it may with reason be expected that those who have spent most time and pains in it should enjoy a greater calm and serenity of mind, a greater clearness and evidence of knowledge, and be less disturbed with doubts and difficulties than other men. Yet so it is, we see the illiterate bulk of mankind that walk the high-road of plain common sense, and are governed by the dictates of nature, for the most part easy and undisturbed."
