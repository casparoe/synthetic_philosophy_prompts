Last month I was elected president of a university rowing club that still requires new members to complete an initiation ritual involving public humiliation and heavy drinking. Reform attempts come up every few years and always fail: older members insist the ritual is a harmless tradition that builds cohesion, newer members fear being seen as weak if they refuse, and the rest of us quietly look the other way. This time the committee has agreed that the practice should go, but we are sharply divided about how. One camp says we should make the moral case as rigorously as possible and trust people's conscience. A second camp says conscience has been tried and failed, and the only dependable lever is material interest: document the legal liability, insurance risk, and possible loss of access to the boathouse so that ending the ritual becomes in everyone's interest. The third camp says that what really kills an entrenched practice of this kind is a change in what the group considers honorable or shameful: reframe participation as contemptible and beneath the dignity of the club, and get respected alumni and team captains to say so publicly.

Before I commit the club to one of these strategies, I would like your judgment on the underlying question. Please rank the three levers—Arguments, Interests, and Honor—from most to least likely to dislodge an entrenched, identity-bound practice like this one, and then justify the placement of each. If broader ideas about how moral norms have changed are useful, draw on them freely, but keep the ranking and its justification for this kind of case at the center.

Please structure your response in five sections: (1) Ranking — list the three levers from most to least promising, with one sentence for each; (2) The strongest lever — roughly 150 words explaining why it belongs at the top; (3) The middle lever — roughly 100 words explaining its place; (4) The weakest lever — roughly 100 words explaining why it falls last; (5) Conditions — roughly 100 words on the cases or conditions under which this ranking might reverse.
