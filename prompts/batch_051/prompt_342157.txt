I've always shrugged off privacy debates with the usual line: if I'm not doing anything wrong, I don't care who can see my data. But last month I got turned down for an apartment because a tenant-screening company gave me a low risk score — it apparently mixed my social media, some shopping history, and a court record from a case that was dismissed. No one ever suspected me of anything at all. The system just scored everybody in advance, and my number came back bad. That made me wonder whether the nothing-to-hide argument was quietly assuming a world where surveillance only gets pointed at people after there's already some specific suspicion. In a world where states and companies pre-sort everyone by predicted risk before any suspicion exists, does that argument survive? If not, which premise is doing the work, and is there a repaired version that still says something true?
