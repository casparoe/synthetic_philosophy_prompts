Please make the strongest case you can that Frederick Douglass's later view—that the Constitution, read plainly, is antislavery—is wrong. Engage his textual arguments head-on: that the document never names slavery outright, that it calls enslaved people persons rather than property even in the three-fifths clause, and that the slave-trade clause reads more like a countdown to abolition than a permanent guarantee. Argue instead that the three-fifths clause, the fugitive slave clause, and the twenty-year protection of the slave trade are better evidence of what the Constitution actually was, and that Garrison was right to call it a pro-slavery compact. Try to meet Douglass on his own ground about how to read a constitutional text, rather than just falling back on modern moral judgment.
