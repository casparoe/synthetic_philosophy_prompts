Okay so I've been trying to come up with a theory of what concepts are and I want someone to stress-test it before I embarass myself. I call it muscle semantics. The basic idea is that a concept isn't a little picture or a definition or a file in your head; it's a squashed action routine. The meaning of 'cup' is your hand already half-shaping to grab it, plus the shoulder tweak of lifting it to your mouth. The meaning of 'angry person' is a slight lean-back readiness or a tightening in your chest. When you understand a sentence, you're basically doing a silent pantomime: the words run the motor programs at very low gain, below the threshold where anything actually moves. Even abstract stuff, I want to say, works this way. 'Justice' is the seed of a bunch of blocked actions—lining people up, redistributing, stepping in front of someone—and the concept is just whatever those action-programs have in common, or maybe the bodily feel of them all being suppressed at once. This would also mean all knowing-that is really knowing-how in a thin disguise, and I can't tell if that's a feature or a bug.

I know there's a whole embodied-cognition thing and maybe this is just that restated. But I want an honest read: is muscle semantics actually a version of embodied/enactivist views, or is it something dumber? If it's not new, what's the existing view it's closest to, and what's the actual difference? And give me the clearest counterexample you can—a concept I should not be able to handle if I'm right, and what my theory would have to say about it to survive.
