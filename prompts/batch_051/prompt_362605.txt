I keep trying to get clear on what hyperbolic discounting actually adds to the old akrasia discussion. In the β-δ version, I can in the evening want to wake up at six to exercise, but in the morning prefer to sleep in, even though nothing about the options has changed. Some people call that a preference reversal, not weakness of will, because at each moment the agent is just acting on whichever time-indexed preference is strongest. But then what exactly is weak about it? Is the idea that the morning self would later endorse the evening plan, or that there is a single agent over time who planned, or that the immediate reward is a kind of temptation that the person could have seen coming and somehow failed to override? Could you separate the different ways a hyperbolically discounting agent might or might not count as akratic—naive vs. sophisticated, with and without a standing higher-order judgement, with and without later endorsement—and say which ingredient does the work in each case? Also, if a sophisticated agent uses a commitment device, is that self-control, weakness, or just reshaping the choice set so the discounting story goes silent? I want the conceptual layout rather than an empirical answer.
