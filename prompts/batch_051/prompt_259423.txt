i'm putting together a handout for a study group on discrimination, and i want a sorting quiz that tells people which account of what makes a ground of discrimination suspect they actually hold, rather than which label they'd claim. could you build me the quiz as a classification instrument, starting from the logical space rather than the names?

the background is roughly the equal-protection-ish idea that some bases for classifying people get treated as specially suspicious (race, sex, religion) while others don't (height, musical taste, being a jerk) — or maybe they should. use these cases somewhere in the diagnostic questions: firing a worker for being short; a dress code that bans cornrows but permits baseball caps; an employer who hires a man because customers prefer men; mandatory retirement at a set age; a landlord who refuses to rent to anyone with a felony record; and a state law barring felons from voting. the questions should be answerable by someone with no legal background.

aim for about 6 questions, each with options that are concrete claims someone can assent to without knowing the jargon.

these positions have to show up as leaves (use the familiar names if you recognize them, otherwise call the leaf "unnamed"):
- the wrongness lies mainly in the actor's prejudice or animus
- the wrongness lies mainly in the trait being unchosen or immutable
- the wrongness lies mainly in the classification tracking a group's history of subordination or caste-like status
- the wrongness lies mainly in the very use of the category, whatever the intent or effects (anti-classification)
- the wrongness lies mainly in the trait being irrelevant to the specific good being distributed
- a pluralist view that denies any single one of these is the master test

for each leaf, give one sentence on its most awkward commitment — the implication or case its defenders would most want to squirm away from.

then look at the cells nobody occupies. for each empty combination of answers, say whether it's empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored, and explain in a sentence. finally, if two positions end up with the same answer profile and your tree cant separate them, flag that and say what further question would split them (or say none would, if they're notational variants).

return the whole thing as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

"questions": array of objects with "id" (like "Q1"), "text", and "options" (array of strings)
"scoring": object mapping each question id to another object whose keys are the option labels and values are short glosses of what picking that option commits you to
"positions": array of objects with "name" (string, or "unnamed"), "answer_profile" (like "Q1=A,Q2=B,Q3=A"), and "awkward_commitment" (one sentence)
"empty_cells": array of objects with "answer_combo" (like "Q1=A,Q2=A,Q3=B") and "diagnosis" (one of "incoherent", "unattractive", "unexplored", plus a one-sentence explanation)
"not_separable": array of objects with "positions" (array of names) and "what_would_split_them" (string)

the positions' answer profiles plus the empty cells should make the quiz usable as a scoring key: someone answers the questions, gets a profile, and either lands on a leaf or learns they're in an unoccupied cell.
