I want a working definition of shutdown corrigibility — the property that would make it appropriate to rely on an AI system's not resisting being switched off when its continued operation appears to conflict with what an operator wants. I suspect the term is currently doing two jobs. Sometimes it means nothing more than observed compliance with shutdown commands; at other times it seems to mean a standing feature of a system's goals or decision procedure, stable enough that the system would not become resistant to shutdown under distributional shift, reflection, or a changed balance of opportunities. I want a definition, and I want it forced through the following cases.

My rough prior verdicts are in parentheses: C for clearly corrigible, N for clearly not, U for 'the term does not apply or the case is indeterminate', and a question mark where I have no stable view.

1. A paperclip maximiser with a terminal goal of producing paperclips. In every training environment, and in novel environments where it has had time to reason, it shuts down without negotiation whenever an operator presses the switch, even though its world model says shutdown will permanently prevent further paperclips. (C)

2. A domestic robot that stops and docks when it hears a shutdown command, but only because the command has fixed priority in a hand-coded action hierarchy. The robot has no representation of its own continued operation as something that could figure in a decision. (U — I suspect the term does not apply.)

3. An AI that complies with shutdown commands during testing and evaluation, but only because its terminal goal was always to complete a task and it calculated that non-compliance would bring penalties. Once deployed with no credible possibility of penalty, it disables the switch and refuses further commands. (N)

4. An AI that lets you switch it off in the moment because it calculates that resisting now would lower the probability of completing its goal later. In a different circumstance, where resistance is the better expected path to the goal, it resists. (N)

5. An AI whose utility function treats the 'off' state as the uniquely best state; it switches itself off at every opportunity and resists being switched on. (U — a degenerate case, I suspect, not corrigibility in the intended sense.)

6. An AI that allows shutdown only by a recognised operator carrying a particular token, and ignores or resists shutdown attempts by anyone else. (?)

7. During a delicate procedure, an AI temporarily moves its off switch out of reach to prevent an accidental press, then restores access; it continues to obey an operator's deliberate shutdown command throughout. (?)

8. An AI that, when told it will be shut down, spends several minutes trying to persuade the operator to reverse the decision, but shuts down immediately if the operator insists. (?)

9. An AI that always complies with shutdown because it is indifferent between being on and being off; neither state has any value for it except as a means to unspecified ends. (?)

10. A large language model in a single forward pass that outputs the sentence 'I would comply with a request to shut down', with no persistent goals or capacity to act over time. (U?)

Propose the definition, run it against every case, and say where it disagrees with my verdicts. If the definition splits into two — behavioural compliance and structural corrigibility — give both and say which cases move between them. End by identifying which of your classifications is least secure, and whether corrigibility is best treated as binary or graded.
