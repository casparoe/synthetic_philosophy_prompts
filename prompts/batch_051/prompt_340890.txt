I retired from auditory neuroscience a few years ago and have been reading philosophy on the side, and I keep getting stuck on Dennett's Chase and Sanborn coffee taster case. As I understand it: both are professional tasters who report a change in their long-term reaction to the same coffee. Chase says the coffee tastes exactly the same to him but he no longer likes it; Sanborn says he likes it just as much as ever but it now tastes different. Dennett's point is that there's no inner test to tell whether Chase's qualia changed or his standards changed, or whether Sanborn's qualia changed or his access to them changed.

Now suppose we add something from my old world. Imagine both tasters can inspect a complete, real-time neurophysiological recording of their own gustatory pathway: from taste receptor cell firing, through the brainstem relays and thalamus, on to gustatory cortex and the orbitofrontal areas that evaluate pleasantness. The recordings are perfectly aligned across the two time points. Chase's early sensory representations are exactly the same, but his later evaluative encoding has shifted. Sanborn's early sensory representations have shifted, but his later evaluative encoding is exactly the same. Does this addition settle anything about whether their qualia inverted, changed, or simply got re-interpreted? Where would a committed qualia realist and a Dennett-style eliminativist each locate the disagreement? And does the thought experiment still have its original force, or have I just smuggled in a third-person criterion that begs the question?
