I keep having this argument with my brother and neither of us can get out of it. He says a moral question is like asking for directions in an unfamiliar city: you find a trustworthy person, you take their word for it, and that's that. I say if you just take someone's word on a moral matter you might end up with the right answer but you don't really have it — you can't explain it, push back on it, or notice when it would steer you wrong. We just go in circles, so I want to make it concrete instead of winning on style. Design the best test you can actually run (or a thought experiment tight enough to count) that would separate 'right moral view, adopted because a reliable source said so' from 'right moral view, actually understood.' What result would count for each side, and what would even a clean result still leave unsettled about whether understanding can be handed over by testimony? I dont care who wins, I just want to know what evidence could even bear on it.
