I'm a PhD student working on colour and the knowledge argument, and there's a variant I keep coming back to that I haven't seen anyone spell out.

Keep Mary's room as it usually is: Mary knows all the physical facts about colour vision, has never seen colour herself, the lot. Now add a brain interface. It doesn't just reproduce ordinary colour vision; it can drive her chromatically tuned areas into states no normal trichromat has ever been in — not merely reddish-green or bluish-yellow (the usual 'forbidden' combinations from opponent processing), but genuinely novel activity patterns, ones whose corresponding experience no human has had and for which there is no ordinary colour word at all. Mary understands the device physically: she knows exactly which populations fire, in which sequence, at which frequencies. She switches it on and has an experience she could not of predicted from the inside.

Question: does she gain something new when the machine fires, and does the knowledge argument do the same work here as in the original?

I keep flip-flopping. In the original Mary learns what red is like, and that seems to matter because there was already a whole human practice of seeing red she was excluded from; her new knowledge slots into a pre-existing phenomenal kind. In my variant the experience is new to everyone, so there is no shared kind waiting for her. If she gains anything, it looks more like discovering that this physical state feels like that, rather than learning what others had meant. But maybe that's a distinction without a difference: the physicalist can just say she gains an ability or a new guise on an old fact here too.

So run the standard replies — ability, acquaintance, old fact under a new guise, eliminativism about qualia — on this version properly. Does the variant make the knowledge argument stronger, weaker, or just move it somewhere? And try to isolate which feature is doing the work: the novelty for Mary, the novelty for all humans, or the absence of a colour word. If it's the novelty-for-everyone that does the work, what should a physicalist say about an experience that has never occurred before but is, by stipulation, fully specified in physical terms in advance?
