Suppose a city with a fixed supply of experience-machine pods adopts the following allocation scheme. Each resident receives a nontransferable weekly allowance of hours; before any session they must file an anticipation statement identifying a standing desire the session is intended to serve and predicting how their non-machine self will assess the session a year later. The statements and later follow-up surveys are posted in an anonymized public database, and residents whose predictions repeatedly miss lose priority for scarce slots. Assess this scheme as a mechanism rather than as a moral ideal. Walk through what it incentivizes for users, for the administrators who review the anticipation statements, and for the friends and family who can read the public record. Where does it get gamed first, and what second-order effects follow? Then say how a hedonist, a desire-satisfaction theorist, and an objective-list theorist about well-being would each evaluate the equilibrium, and whether the mechanism would in practice enact one of those theories over the others.
