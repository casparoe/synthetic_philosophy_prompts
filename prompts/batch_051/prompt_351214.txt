So my dad just retired after 34 years at the Fed and I'm giving the toast at his retirement dinner. Hes spent my whole life going on about how Keynes's essay "Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren" is the only piece of economics he actually believes: growth is just for solving the economic problem, and after that we finally face the real, permanent problem of how to live well. His favorite bit is Keynes's split between absolute needs (the ones we have no matter what anyone else has) and relative needs (the ones that only exist because we're comparing ourselves to somebody else). And here's the punchline: the man worked 60-hour weeks chasing promotions for three decades, and now, one month into retirement, he's started a consulting practice and joined two boards.

The dinner will be maybe a third economists who know the essay cold and will catch any sloppiness, and the rest family and neighbors who mostly think Keynes is just the deficit-spending guy. I want this toast to run about five minutes, to be warm and funny, and to be built around the absolute/relative needs distinction rather than the tired line about Keynes predicting we'd all be working a 15-hour week. I'd like it to tease him gently for turning retirement into another round of relative-needs status games, but in a way that lands as love and respect, and without making Keynes sound like a naive utopian or making the non-economists feel like they've stumbled into a seminar. Can you write the actual speech?
