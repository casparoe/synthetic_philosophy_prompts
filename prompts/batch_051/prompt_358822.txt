Thomson's violinist argument grants, for the sake of argument, that a fetus is a person and then tries to show that abortion is still permissible because no person may use someone else's body to stay alive: you may unplug the violinist even if he dies. A common way to defend this is to insist the right at stake is a right to stop the bodily use, not a right to kill as such — the death is downstream of the unplugging. Now suppose safe, cheap artificial wombs mean a fetus at, say, twelve weeks can be transferred and carried to term with no extra burden or risk to the pregnant person than a standard abortion, and with no continuing legal obligations after transfer. Does the violinist argument still justify fetal death in that world, or does it only justify extraction and transfer?
