I'm a high school student helping my history teacher grade short-answer responses, and I'd like a careful second opinion on one of them. Below are the question and a student's answer.

Question: Who started the Cold War, and is "who started it" the right question to ask? Discuss how the main schools of historiography have assigned responsibility, and evaluate whether the question itself is well formed.

Student's answer: Historians have answered the question of who started the Cold War in roughly three waves. The traditionalist school, dominant in the late 1940s and 1950s, blamed the Soviet Union, treating its consolidation of Eastern Europe and its support for communist movements as an expansionist threat that the United States had to contain. The revisionist school reversed this picture, arguing that the United States was the aggressor: American economic and strategic interests drove the confrontation, and programs like the Marshall Plan, together with the atomic bomb, were used to divide Europe and encircle the USSR. Post-revisionism later corrected both schools and concluded that the two superpowers were equally to blame, and that the conflict was essentially inevitable once the wartime alliance lost its common enemy. In the end, "who started it" remains the right question, because a conflict of this scale must have had a responsible party; historians who treat the question as misguided are really just avoiding the need to assign blame.
