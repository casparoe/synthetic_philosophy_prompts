Yesterday I spent a long evening arguing with a friend about whether a highly capable artificial intelligence — say, a system that manages assets, negotiates routine contracts, and can cause real damage when it goes wrong — could ever be recognized as a legal person in the way a corporation is. My friend's position was that legal personhood is for human beings, or at least for entities with human beings standing behind them in the right way. I replied that corporate personhood already severs the link between legal personhood and being human, and that the analogy does more work than he was willing to admit. Afterward I tried to write out my side as cleanly as I could:

(1) A legal person is simply an entity that a legal system elects to treat as a single bearer of rights and duties for the system's own practical purposes.

(2) The law treats a corporation as a legal person for precisely such practical purposes — so that a business can hold property, enter contracts, and be sued as one unit — and not because the corporation has a mind, a body, or human interests of its own.

(3) The only work being done in the corporate case is that the corporation provides a stable point to which rights and liabilities can be attached and through which legal acts can be routed.

(4) A suitably structured AI system with identifiable human operators provides an equally stable point in those respects: assets can be set aside and administered for it, contracts can be concluded on its behalf, and liability for what the system does can be channeled to that set-aside pool rather than to the operators personally.

(5) Therefore, whatever justifies legal personhood for a corporation justifies legal personhood for such an AI system.

I keep half-suspecting that the weak spot is in step (3) or step (4) — that I have slid something in about what does the justificatory work, or about what it means to be a stable point — but I cannot make the objection precise. Is the argument valid? If it is not, which step fails first, and under what reading of it? If it is valid, is the conclusion strong enough to carry the weight I put on it in the argument?
