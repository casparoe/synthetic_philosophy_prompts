I've been turning over an idea about physics and I cant decide whether it's obvious or deep, so I'd like someone to spell out its implications for a layperson. From the popular stuff I've read, a "final theory" would be one that covers all the forces and particles and leaves nothing for physics left to explain. But the thing that nags me is that no experiment could ever announce that a theory is final — all an experiment can do is fail to refute it. So it seems possible that physicists could one day actually hold the final theory, and still be in exactly the same position as some earlier scientist whose theory was about to be overturned: no outward sign that the job is done. What are the most important implications of this — that a final theory could be reached without anyone being able to know they'd reached it — for how a non-scientist should hear confident claims that physics is nearly finished, and for whether "getting closer to the complete explanation" is the right way to picture what physics is doing? And if the implication is just that we should keep some doubt about every theory, is that the ordinary scientific humility or is there something stronger hiding in it?
