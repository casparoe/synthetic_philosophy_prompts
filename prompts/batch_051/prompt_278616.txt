Everyone says Frege overthrew Aristotelian logic and built the predicate logic we use now, but I keep failing to see the substantive break. When I actually test it, every valid syllogism comes out valid in first-order logic and every invalid one comes out invalid — Aristotle doesn't seem refuted so much as absorbed. The changes people usually name feel cosmetic: bound variables, a cleaner notation, and the ability to handle relations like 'x loves y'. Couldn't you just keep the whole 'All S is P' subject–predicate setup and bolt on a few extra rules for relations? What did Frege actually change, other then the notation?
