I help run a DAO that makes maybe a dozen real decisions a year — treasury grants, protocol parameter changes, hack-recovery payouts. I keep hearing about futarchy: the idea that you vote on broad goals, then let conditional prediction markets pick which policy actually gets us there. I want a screening checklist I can run in an afternoon, without an economist on call, to decide whether a specific decision should go through a futarchy-style market instead of a token vote. Give me the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure stops with a flat no, and where it hands off to a person because the question is one of values rather than facts. Be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure. Then run it on three candidate decisions: funding a bug bounty, changing our token's issuance rate, and an airdrop to compensate users who lost funds in an exploit — and include at least one case where the checklist tells me no even though I'd want it to say yes. Keep the line sharp between what's settled about futarchy and what's genuinely contested among people who actually work on this; don't present a contested position as the consensus.
