I am in my first term on the council of a mid-sized city, and next week I have to cast the deciding vote on next year's allocation of our small solidarity fund. Until now, the fund has quietly supported modest projects abroad. This year, a delegation from our sister city in a much poorer country has asked us to help pay for a water-treatment plant; our own public-health staff have concluded that the project would prevent far more death and illness per dollar than any of the domestic programs we currently underfund. At the same time, a local coalition is demanding that the whole fund be redirected to expanding a dental clinic for uninsured residents of our city. In caucus, a colleague I respect put the majority view bluntly: we were elected by our voters, and the people of this city have a special claim on us simply because they are ours; their needs come first, full stop. I cannot accept that as stated, because it seems to imply that we should never spend a cent abroad even when the same money would do vastly more good. But I am also uneasy with the opposite thought, that our own residents are just people among people and that it would be just as defensible to spend all of our resources on strangers on the other side of the world.

I am asking for help thinking through the question underneath the vote, not for a reading list and not for generic advice about following my conscience. What, if anything, could make it true that fellow citizens have a special claim on shared resources simply because they are one's own, and where does such a reason run out? How should I weigh the formal fact that my office exists to represent residents of this city against the fact that the foreign project would save more lives per dollar? If you were to build the strongest case for each side and then identify the exact point at which each case fails or overreaches, what would that look like? And is there a defensible position between our own first, always and no one's needs count more than anyone else's, or is every middle position just an unstable compromise?
