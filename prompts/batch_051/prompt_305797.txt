My friend is finishing a doctorate on the concept of death, and she has a set of settled verdicts about particular cases that she has asked me to keep consistent. She is firm that a man whose heart stopped during surgery and could not be restarted was dead by the time the team ended resuscitation, even though some of his tissues were still viable; that a child pulled from an icy river with no pulse and no breathing was not dead, because the cold had merely suspended things and rewarming might succeed (it did); that a patient in a permanent vegetative state, with no detectable cortical activity but a working brainstem, a steady heartbeat and unassisted breathing, is alive; that a patient with locked-in syndrome, fully conscious though unable to move or speak, breathing without assistance and with a steady heartbeat, is obviously alive; and that a patient whose heart was stopped for two hours during an operation while a heart-lung machine circulated and oxygenated his blood, and who later woke and recovered, was alive throughout.

She is now trying to decide what to say about a case she has so far refused to pronounce on. A young woman in intensive care has suffered total infarction of the entire brain, brainstem included. She has no cranial nerve reflexes, no respiratory effort, and a scan shows no intracranial blood flow. At the same time her heart beats strongly, her blood pressure is stable, her wounds heal, and the ventilator is doing her breathing. I would like to know what consistency with her earlier verdicts requires her to say.

Please answer in exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. The items should be: (1) the two or three candidate rules that fit all of her settled verdicts; (2) what each live rule says about the new case; (3) which of her earlier cases is the closest analogue and how it bears on the new one; (4) your best prediction of what she will say, with the reason for preferring the rule behind it; and (5) a further case that would cleanly separate any rules still live. Use British English throughout.
