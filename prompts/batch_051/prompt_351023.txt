I keep trying to get my head around when it's okay to reproduce propaganda in order to criticize it, and my gut is pulling me different ways on different cases. Here's roughly where I land:

- A professor shows a two-minute clip from a Nazi propaganda film in a lecture on film technique, sound off, running commentary: fine.
- A friend posts an actual Proud Boys recruitment video on his feed with the caption "look how gross this is": not fine; he's basically doing their distribution for them.
- A colleague plays a deceptively edited political video in a media-literacy class, pausing to point out the cuts: fine.
- A news site embeds a real beheading video on autoplay so readers "get what we mean": not fine.
- I quote-tweet a clip of an elected official comparing immigrants to vermin at a rally, with a long thread on the historical echoes: fine, partly because he's a public official and the thread does real work.
- Someone I know retweets a Great Replacement meme with the comment "what is wrong with people": not fine, even though the intent is clearly critical. It felt like it did the meme's work.
- An advocacy group makes a YouTube video that recreates a politician's attack ad against trans people, nearly shot-for-shot, with commentary over it, to expose the ad's tricks: I go back and forth. It's more substantive than the retweet cases, but it still puts the images back in circulation, and a trans kid could end up seeing the ad without the commentary.
- A researcher pastes a long excerpt from a misogynist manifesto into the body of a published paper, when a summary would have done: not fine.
- I share a screenshot of a libelous anti-vax post on my Instagram story with a point-by-point debunk overlaid on the image: fine.

Is there a principle that gets all (or nearly all) of these, or do I have to give one or two up? What does your best principle say about the one I'm stuck on, and about a related case I've been avoiding: a podcast that plays the full audio of a mass shooter's manifesto-style video, framed as "we have to look at this squarely"? I'd rather have a principle that's a little ugly and complicated than one that's clean but obviously wrong, but tell me honestly which of my verdicts it can't save.

And whatever you give, lead with the bottom line in the first sentence and then the reasoning, no preamble, no restating the question.
