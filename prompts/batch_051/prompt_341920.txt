I am designing an exam question for a seminar on Kant's moral philosophy, and I want something that pushes students beyond exegesis into genuinely counterfactual reconstruction. The seminar has covered Kant's view that we have no direct duties to animals: animals are not self-conscious rational beings, so they are not ends in themselves; our duties concerning them are indirect duties to ourselves, chiefly because cruelty toward animals dulls our capacity to feel with their suffering and thereby weakens a disposition that serves our moral relations to other human beings. Kant treats even kindness and gratitude toward animals (an old horse, a loyal dog) as, strictly speaking, owed to ourselves rather than to the animal. I would like you to do the kind of work I hope my best students will attempt: a disciplined prediction of how Kant would have judged three developments he could not have foreseen, if he had been given accurate empirical information about each.

The three developments are: (1) industrial animal agriculture, in which vast numbers of sentient animals are raised in conditions of routine suffering and killed after short lives, for food that is no longer necessary for human survival; (2) cultivated meat, produced from animal cells in bioreactors, so that meat can be eaten without killing or directly harming any sentient animal; and (3) xenotransplantation, in which genetically modified pigs are bred to supply organs for transplantation into human beings, sparing human patients but treating the pig as a kind of living spare-parts source. For each case, give your best reconstruction of the verdict Kant's considered position would have yielded, with the argument he would most plausibly have given. Then tell me where the system is genuinely underdetermined: for each case, what further premise (about the empirical effects on our moral dispositions, about what counts as cruelty, about whether routine instrumental use of animal life is itself corrupting) would have to be supplied before a Kantian answer becomes determinate, and which way the conclusion would go under the different defensible ways of supplying it. This is a reconstruction exercise, not a literature review, so please do not present reading lists or citations; focus entirely on the philosophical prediction and its internal tensions.
