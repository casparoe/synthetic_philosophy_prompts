Last week I spent an evening arguing with a close friend about whether the global skeptical scenarios familiar from Descartes—an evil demon, a lifelong dream—give me any reason to reduce my confidence in ordinary empirical beliefs, such as my belief that a scheduled meeting will actually occur. He maintained that consistency requires the doubt to leak: if I grant that I cannot rule the scenario out, then I should be at least slightly less confident in each particular empirical claim. I resisted, and I have since codified my position as a standing rule for handling doubt. The rule is this: I will revise a first-order belief only in response to a doubt that is statable from within some method I already treat as working—perception, memory, a particular instrument, a named informant. A doubt gets quarantined when it is global, meaning it would undermine all of those methods at once and would equally undermine the reasoning by which the doubt was generated; the evil demon and the dream are the paradigm cases. I permit quarantined doubts their full force at the level of philosophical reflection. There I will entertain them, grant that I cannot refute them, and even grant that they expose a familiar circularity—the problem of the criterion—in any attempt to validate my basic methods except by using them. But when I return to ordinary belief and action, my stored credences go back to exactly where they were, as if the doubt had never been raised. My friend insists this is motivated belief management, a way to purchase the appearance of rigor without paying its price; I reply that it is something like Humean naturalism, the recognition that everyday confidence is not something philosophical argument can reach, and that this is not a defect. I would like the rule itself audited. Is my quarantine rule rationally defensible? If not, what is the defensible version of it, and how would I tell, from the inside, whether I am running the defensible version or merely protecting my beliefs from any challenge general enough to be unanswerable?
