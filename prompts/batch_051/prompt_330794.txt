I have just come away from a long and unresolved argument with a friend about pain, and I want to set my own position out in a form that can be audited. She kept pressing me on pain asymbolia, and I found myself asserting things that I still believe one by one, but that I am no longer sure hold together as a set. Below are the commitments as I would now formulate them; please go through them and tell me where the strain is.

1. Pain is not a neutral sensory signal that we happen to dislike. To be in pain is, in part, for the experience to feel bad. A state that registered tissue damage, or even demanded attention, but carried no negative feeling at all would be an alarm, not a pain.

2. I take the pain-asymbolia reports at face value. In this rare condition, people describe a noxious stimulus as sharp, intense, and located, and they will call it painful, while showing no distress, no withdrawal, and no desire to escape. They are not faking, not confused, and not speaking metaphorically.

3. These cases show a real dissociation: the sensory-discriminative aspects of pain—where it is, how intense it is, whether it burns or stabs—can remain intact while the affective-motivational aspect—the badness, the urge to stop it—is stripped away.

4. The urge to stop a pain is not a separate reaction that the pain merely causes; the wanting-it-gone is built into the pain itself.

5. The badness of pain is exhausted by its felt unpleasantness. Pain is not bad because of tissue damage, danger, or the way it disrupts plans; it is bad solely because of how it feels.

6. A subject's sincere first-person report is decisive about whether she is in pain. If someone reports that an experience hurts and is intense, but adds that she does not mind it, then she is in pain; the fact that it does not matter to her is a fact about her experience, not a defect in her report.

7. Pain is an ordinary concept, and ordinary concepts are not automatically authoritative about the boundaries of a natural kind. A scientific taxonomy may discover that some states the folk classify as pain are not pain.

I would like an audit of this set. Tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which are only apparently so. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, what would have to give to resolve it, and what the cheapest resolution would cost. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set coherent. Please take the commitments as stated and do not resolve the strains by quietly redefining the terms.
