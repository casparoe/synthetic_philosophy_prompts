Spinoza maintains both that every state of a finite mind follows from the divine nature with the same necessity as a truth of geometry, and that the highest blessedness available to us consists in the intellectual love of God, an affect that arises from the third kind of knowledge. I find it hard to hold these two claims together. If the order of my ideas is already fixed by necessity, then whether I ever pass from confused ideas to adequate ideas—and so whether I ever attain that love—would seem to be settled independently of anything I do now. But then the Ethics, which presents itself as a curative discipline meant to lead a reader from bondage to blessedness, would be superfluous: no discipline could make the difference between attaining and failing to attain the intellectual love of God. Does Spinoza have the resources to dissolve this worry, and if so, what is wrong with my reasoning?
