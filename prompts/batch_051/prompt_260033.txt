I spent my career in physics and metrology, so I'm used to standards changing and watching how fast the world actually follows. Here's a prompt I keep chewing on. Suppose that in 2030 the major international standards bodies issue a voluntary recomendation that the civil calendar switch to a new epoch: year 0 is set at 1945, months and days unchanged, and as of 2040 the old year numbering is deprecated but not banned. I don't want an argument over whether 1945 deserves to be year zero; I want a reasoned prediction of what happens over the next fifty years. Which institutions convert quickly and which drag their feet or quietly run two systems forever? Work through the mechanisms — legal instruments that cite dates, school textbooks, software that already counts seconds from 1970, historians' periodization, national prestige, coordination between calendars — and tell me which of your predictions are robust regardless of how hard the recommendation is pushed, and which depend on enforcement or luck. Also: would your prediction differ much if the epoch were 1969 instead of 1945? If so, why, and what would that show about what actualy makes an epoch a good one?
