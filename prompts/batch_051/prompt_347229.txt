I'm a novelist, and theres a distinction I keep wanting in my work but I don't know whether anybody has a name for it. In my current novel I have two characters who both betray an original cause, but the betrayals feel like completely different kinds of failure, and I want to know how different they really are.

The first character starts as a sincere campaigner for, say, fixing a local housing crisis. But over the course of the book she gets more and more preoccupied with becoming the kind of person people listen to — the platform, the allies, the base of support, being in the room where things are decided. At first all of that really is for the cause. By the end, though, protecting and growing the influence has become the actual project; the housing crisis is mostly a thing she talks about in order to keep the influence. The original goal has been hollowed out by the means.

The second character never chases power at all. She stays quiet and sincere — the head of a small charity, say. But over thirty years the thing she is working for slowly drifts. Each step is small and defensible: a compromise with a funder, a new ally who shifts the priorities, a bit of practical wisdom that makes the original goal look naive. None of it is a betrayal she could point to. At the end she's serving something she would once have opposed, but she'd insist it's the same cause, just grown up.

I've seen both of these in fiction and in real institutions, and I've felt both in myself. So my question is: is there an established name for this pair of failure modes, or for the contrast between them? I'm not after a bare label. I want to know whether these are really two species of one thing or two different things. The second could be re-described as the first — maybe the drift only happens because somebody quietly gained influence and steered it. And the first could be re-described as the second — a persons values drift until accruing influence is the only value left. So where exactly is the boundary? Can one happen without the other? If there are existing concepts near this, I'd like to know what they are, how what I'm describing differs from each of them, and which fits best; if there isnt an established thing, could you name and define it so the definition marks the edges?

Oh, and could you write your answer in British English?
