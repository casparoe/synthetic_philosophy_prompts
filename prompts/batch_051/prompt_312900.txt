For a feature I am writing on Zhuangzi's butterfly dream and dream scepticism, I have ended up with a distinction and a small principle that I would like stress-tested before I put either into print.

The distinction is between 'bare' and 'rival' dream doubt. Bare doubt is the thought that this experience might be a dream, full stop, with no particular alternative waking life specified. Rival doubt is the thought that I might in fact be some other being — a butterfly, say — dreaming my present life, so that there is a concrete alternative state of affairs about which I could be wrong. My suspicion is that only rival doubt is genuinely unsettling; bare doubt is idle because it offers no alternative to reason towards.

The principle is what I call the 'return test'. A state deserves to be treated as waking to the extent that, from inside it, I can recall, locate and causally explain the state that would undermine it, while the undermining state cannot return the favour. In the received passage, Zhuang Zhou wakes and can remember the butterfly and account for it as a dream; the butterfly, so far as the text tells us, has no reciprocal grasp of Zhuang Zhou. The two states are therefore not on a par, and the dream does not really supply a rival standpoint.

I would like to know whether this is coherent, whether it merely restates something familiar under a worse name, and whether the return test survives the obvious hard cases: a dream in which I seem to remember my waking life and can tell a causal story about it; a false awakening in which I dream that I have woken and checked; a lucid dream; and a waking state that cannot recall the dream it supposedly explains. Is the test circular because it depends on memory, which is precisely what dreams can counterfeit? And does my claim that bare doubt is idle rest on the dubious idea that a sceptical possibility must offer a concrete alternative before it can matter? I would welcome the three strongest objections and an honest verdict on whether any of them is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable.
