I've been in a reading group on physicalism for months, and we keep stalling at the same point before we even get to consciousness or mental causation. Carl Hempel, in his response to Goodman's Ways of Worldmaking, put the dilemma roughly like this: if 'physical' means the entities and properties countenanced by current physics, then physicalism is almost certainly false, since current physics is incomplete and has been revised again and again; but if it means the entities and properties of some future, completed physics, then the thesis is too vague to assess, because nobody knows what that future physics will include. The group has now split into three camps. Some say define it by current physics anyway and bite the bullet that physicalism is probably false. Some say define it by future physics, but they cant say what that rules out. Others say we should drop the label 'physicalism' and just argue about what exists. My working theory is that the disagreement isn't really about the world at all, but about what philosophers want the word 'physical' to do—demarcation, honour, or something else. That feels right but also a bit glib. What kind of disagreement is this actually, and what would count as progress? Please answer in British English, and give a diagnosis rather than another survey of the physicalism literature.
