Question: "Explain the distinction between killing and letting die. Is this distinction morally significant? Does it show that active euthanasia is morally worse than passive euthanasia?"

Student's answer: "The distinction between killing and letting die is the difference between actively causing a death and merely allowing a death to happen that one could have prevented. For example, giving a patient a lethal injection is killing, whereas not starting CPR on a patient with a DNR order is letting die. Many people think killing is worse, which is why active euthanasia is illegal in most places while passive euthanasia is widely accepted. James Rachels attacked this asymmetry. In his bathtub example, Smith drowns his young cousin to inherit money, while Jones stands by and lets the cousin drown for the same reason. Rachels thought the two men were equally bad, so he concluded that killing and letting die are always morally equivalent. Since there is no moral difference between the two, he argued that active euthanasia should be accepted wherever passive euthanasia is accepted. The underlying reason the distinction seems important is that killing involves intending a death, while letting die only involves foreseeing it, so really the debate is about the doctrine of double effect."
