I've been co-running a small worker co-op and there's a recurring fight I can't get a handle on. A couple of members will block a proposal or take money out of the shared fund, and when the rest of us ask them to walk through the reasoning, they say "I don't owe you an explanation." Not "I need time to sort out my thoughts" or "this is really between me and the person I'm accountable to" — just the general slogan, said like it's self-evident. Some of us read it as a refusal to be accountable; they read it as a boundary. I want to know what that sentence could actually be claiming, because I'm pretty sure it's doing different work in different mouths.

Lay out the distinct precise theses "I don't owe you an explanation" might express. For each one, spell out what "owe" means there (enforceable duty? moral requirement? virtue? role obligation? something else?), what counts as an "explanation" (access to my reasons? a causal story? a public justification? a translation into terms the other person accepts?), and who the "you" is (the whole group? an affected party? an institution? a stranger?). Then say for each reading whether it's a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or just false, and which reading would actually justify using the slogan to shut down a request for reasons inside a consensus-run group whose decisions bind everyone in it. I'm also curios about two variants: whether the claim changes strength when the "you" is an institution rather than a co-member, and whether a duty to be legible might be asymetric — heavier for people with more informal power or for people who lean on the group's trust. Finally, say how confident you are in the verdicts you give and single out the one point you're least sure of.
