I am preparing a seminar handout on Goodman's Fact, Fiction, and Forecast, and I keep getting pulled between two readings of his claim that the difference between projectible predicates such as green and non-projectible predicates such as grue is a matter of entrenchment: that the former predicates have a history of being used in successful inductions by our linguistic community, while the latter do not. One reading says entrenchment is a descriptive fact about our inductive habits: it explains which predictions speakers in fact make, and perhaps which ones feel natural to them. The other reading says entrenchment is a normative criterion: it explains which hypotheses we are justified in projecting, not merely which ones we are disposed to project. A cognitive scientist colleague tells me that if I am making a claim about actual inductive practice, the claim should be experimentally tractable. She proposes a study in which participants first observe a sample from a novel domain, are taught two artificial predicates that both fit the sample exactly, but are then shown that one predicate has a recorded history of having been used successfully by prior experimenters on earlier, structurally similar samples, while the other has no such history. The participants are then asked to make predictions about novel cases. She says that a preference for the historically embedded predicate would amount to evidence for an entrenchment mechanism in inductive projection. But I am not sure what such a result would actually establish. What is the strongest version of that experiment, or of some other observation, that could bear on the claim that entrenchment rather than some formal or natural-kind property is doing the work in distinguishing lawlike from accidental generalizations? Say precisely which result would favor the descriptive-entrenchment reading and which would favor a deflationary reading on which entrenchment is just a proxy for something else, such as perceived reliability or naturalness. And then say what even a decisive behavioral result could not settle: in particular, whether entrenchment answers the justificatory question about why some projections are rational and others are not, or merely answers the psychological question about why we make the projections we do.
