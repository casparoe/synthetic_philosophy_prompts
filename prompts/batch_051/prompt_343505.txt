Last night a friend and I argued for three hours about psychiatric classification, and by the end we had nearly converged on a document but never actually wrote it down. He is a classification realist: he holds that categories like major depressive disorder and ADHD are, however imperfectly, attempts to discover real kinds, and that better neurobiology and genetics will increasingly make them track joints in nature. I lean toward the interactive-kind view associated with Hacking's looping effects: classifying people changes them, because the label changes how they describe themselves, how institutions respond to them, and which behavior gets noticed; the kind then absorbs those changes, and the cycle repeats. So the category partly constitutes its object rather than merely tracking it. Neither of us is an eliminativist about mental disorder, and neither of us thinks the two pictures are strictly exclusive, yet we kept parting ways on a small number of sub-questions.

Draft the joint statement we should have produced: something both of us could sign. It should contain four parts. First, a numbered Agreed Claims section: substantive claims both camps can sign, worded so neither side would wince — including claims about whether looping actually happens, about what biological findings would and would not show, and about the difference between discovering a kind and shaping one. Second, a Residual Disagreements table: for each point where we still differ, a Realist formulation and a Looping formulation, each stated in wording its own side would accept and neither being a caricature. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would genuinely move at least one of us. I suspect these include whether looping is universal or merely local; whether a category's instability across time and culture undermines realism about it or is compatible with an imperfectly discovered kind; whether a robust biomarker found after decades of looping shows the loop was tracking something real all along or only that the loop has stabilized; and whether the looping view is committed to denying any underlying state independent of classification, or only to denying that our classifications can remain purely descriptive. Fourth, for each crux, a concrete case, an imagined longitudinal design, or an argument both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance and a note on what would count as retreat for that side. If you conclude that part of our disagreement is merely verbal, say exactly which part and why. Keep the whole document under 1,000 words.
