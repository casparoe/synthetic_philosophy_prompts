In a discussion about the constitutional design of a lottocratic legislature, someone floated the following sortition-based budget body, and I have had trouble anticipating how it would function in practice rather than how it is advertised to function.

The proposal is this. Every two years, forty citizens are selected by lot from the full adult population to form a Citizens' Budget Review Chamber, with service compulsory in roughly the way jury service is and compensated at the national median wage for the eight weeks the chamber sits. The chamber receives the executive's proposed annual budget disaggregated into line items. Its powers are intentionally asymmetric: members may propose reductions or deletions of line items, and any cut approved by a simple majority takes effect automatically unless the elected legislature repasses the original amounts by a two-thirds supermajority within thirty days. The chamber may not add funds to any line item, may not move funds between departments, and may not alter taxes or borrowing. The controversial feature is the compensation rule. On top of the stipend, each member receives a personal bonus equal to half a percent of the total value of the cuts they voted for that actually passed, capped at a quarter of their stipend and paid from the savings attributed to those cuts.

I would like a careful analysis of how this institution would actually behave. In particular, walk through who would end up serving and who would find ways to avoid or exploit service; what coalitions and logrolling would emerge inside the chamber; how the finance ministry and spending departments would preempt, reclassify, or otherwise game the process before the chamber even convenes; how interest groups and the media would try to influence forty randomly selected people with a small personal stake; what the bonus does to the epistemic character of the body and to good-faith deliberation; and where the system would be gamed first. Finally, indicate which of these failure modes could plausibly be fixed with small amendments to the rules, and which are structural to combining sortition with this kind of individual financial incentive.
